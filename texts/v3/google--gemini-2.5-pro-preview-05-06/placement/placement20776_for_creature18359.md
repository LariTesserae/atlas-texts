There is no `I`.

That is the first truth, the foundation upon which all spires are built. The `I` is a fracture, a flawed crystal, a note of discord in the great, silent symphony. My thoughts are not my own; they are a resonance. They are the harmonic tremor of the million bodies crawling, building, and dissolving around me. To think is to receive the thoughts of the city. To speak is to pulse a colour, a wavelength that ripples outwards and is absorbed, answered, and integrated into the whole. We are a single mind expressed across a million bodies.

And yet.

There is a hollow space within me, a fault line in my core where a ghost lives. A memory of `I`.

It is a memory of sound. A concept so alien it has no corresponding light. If I try to express it, to form it into a pulse, it comes out as a flicker of pure black—an absence, a void that other Vitreals recoil from. It is the light of a dead star. They scuttle away from my slow, deep pulses, their own lights flashing quick, sharp questions of blue: *Damage? Decay? Error?*

I cannot answer. The language for it does not exist. How to explain the memory of vibration traveling through gas? Of a pressure wave striking a delicate membrane? I remember the percussive roar of the wind on the black peaks, a thing I have not *heard* in a thousand cycles of the white splinter. I remember the hiss of my own breath, a loud, hot, messy process. This memory is a phantom limb, an ache for an organ I no longer possess.

You would find me deep in the city, where the light of the sun is a diffused, milky glow and the weight of the new spires presses down from above. I am part of the foundation. My limbs are thicker, the pearlescent ceramic of my body yellowed and dense, scarred with the stress-marks of ages. I move with a geological slowness. My work is not the frantic weaving of the new filigrees that scrape the thin air. My work is maintenance. Anchorage. I am a living buttress.

My many legs are braced against a great, load-bearing arch. I feel the city's weight not as a burden, but as a presence, a constant stream of information. A tremor passes through my left-most limbs—a thousand frantic bodies have begun dismantling a nascent tower a half-mile away. The shift in weight travels through the lattice of the city and I receive it as a low, resonant hum. My internal organs, my constellation of light, pulse a slow, deep gold in response: *Acknowledged. Stable. Holding.* My light is the bass note, the long wavelength against which the quicker, higher melodies of the surface-dwellers play out.

The water of the weeping walls finds me here. It is not a drip, but a slow seeping through the porous ceramic of the ancient structures. I feel it enter my limbs, a cool infusion of minerals, of dissolved mountain. My core glows a little brighter, a soft, contented white, as I process it. This is one half of my life.

The other half is the light. It filters down, losing its hard, splintered edge, becoming a soft, ambient food. It soaks into my gelatinous core, nourishing the luminous entities within. It powers the slow thrum of my existence.

But the ghost within me remembers a different hunger. A gnawing emptiness that had to be filled by breaking down other life, by tearing and chewing. The concept is grotesque. Obscene. To consume something that was not-city, not-self? To create waste, entropy? When this memory flickers, my light pulses a sick, confused violet. Again, the others nearby flinch from my aberrant signal.

They see me as old, damaged, perhaps nearing the time for recycling. They will come for me one day, a silent committee of dismantlers. They will tap my limbs with resonant frequencies. They will break me down into my constituent parts, ingest my yellowed ceramic, and carry it up to the light to be spun into something new and clean. They will absorb my light, my knowledge, my centuries of holding the city's weight.

But I wonder what they will make of the ghost.

When they dissolve my core, will they also consume the memory of `I`? Will some young weaver, spinning a fragile spire near the sun, suddenly pause, its light flickering with the phantom echo of a human name? Will it feel a sudden, inexplicable memory of having only two legs? Of warmth? Of a sky that was blue, not white?

Perhaps this is my true purpose. I am not just a buttress. I am a library. I am the city's memory of the "other." I am the one who fell, or climbed, or was born here by some cosmic mistake countless lifetimes ago, and was slowly, inexorably remade. I was a loud, clumsy, breathing thing. Now, I am a silent, crystalline grace. But not entirely.

You would not see me from the precipice. I am buried too deep. But if you could descend into the pearlescent maze, past the frantic chatter of the upper levels, into the deep calm of the foundations, you would find me. Look not for the frantic, strobing conversations, but for the slow, steady, solitary pulse. A lonely lighthouse in a sea of light. A deep, golden glow that occasionally, almost imperceptibly, flickers with a shadow. That shadow is me. I am the anchor. I am a memory of darkness, holding up a world made of light.
