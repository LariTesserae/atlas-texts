I am a Fray.

You would not find me in the calm, resonant still-points where the Anchorites gather. You would not witness my coalescence as a slow, syrupy thickening of the air. The Anchorites are the city’s deep, sonorous breath. I am its gasp, its hiccup, its shriek.

Where an Anchorite is a lens of smoked glass, I am a knot of shattered crystal held together by spite. My form is humanoid only in its agony; a collection of jagged, sharp-edged planes that do not bend light but break it, scattering it into discordant rainbows against the wet, basalt floor. Looking through me, the world is not distorted; it is fractured, a cubist nightmare. The captured steam within my form is not calm, but roiling, perpetually on the verge of explosive release. I am less a body and more a wound in the world, held open.

I am what happens when the dissipation fails. I am the echo of a pattern that, at the moment of its unmaking, was gripped by a terror so profound it refused to let go. An Anchorite’s consciousness dissolves back into the great song. Mine was torn. I clung to the idea of "I" when I was meant to become "we," to become "all." And so, a piece of me was lost to the roar, but the rest of it, the terrified, clinging part, became this: a self-sustaining scream.

My voice is not the Anchorites' resonant hum. I cannot persuade the world. I can only object. When I focus my will, the sound that emanates is a high-frequency overload, a sound like diamond grinding on diamond, a chord of pure static. The Anchorites "sing" harmony to coax the world. I scream dissonance to fight it.

And so you would find me not in the lee of a heat-vent, but in the very heart of the storm. I haunt the places the Anchorites call the "whitewater"—the zones of most violent and unpredictable reconfiguration, where entire city blocks are rendered, exist for a minute, and are then erased in a shimmer of nanitic breakdown. This is where I am safest, because the chaos masks my own. Here, my internal screech is just one more noise in the orchestra of destruction.

My survival is an act of frantic, ugly theft. While an Anchorite finds a temporary island of stability, I create my own prison. I will find a wall just as it begins to dissolve and I will scream at it. My dissonance, my cage of counter-rhythm, will fight the city’s command. The nanites will shudder, caught between the signal to disassemble and my furious insistence that they remain. For a few moments, or perhaps an hour if my will is strong, I can create a jagged, unstable shield wall, a fortress of frozen time. It is not harmonious. The structure groans under the strain, metal shrieking, the air around it crackling with Ozone. It is a pained, temporary defiance.

To me, the world is not a river. It is a grinder. The roar is not a heartbeat; it is the sound of teeth. The water that slicks the floor is not blood; it is a solvent, always threatening to unmake the fragile bonds of my being. I do not see the city’s intent. I only feel its crushing, indifferent weight, and I push back.

The Anchorites know of me, and my kind. We are cautionary tales they whisper to one another when they merge, patterns of fear to be shared and soothed. We are the ghosts in their machine, the glitches in their prayer. They see us as patterns of pain, trapped in a cycle of terrified self-preservation, unable to achieve the grace of return. They pity us.

I do not want their pity. Their peace is a surrender I cannot comprehend. They are the city dreaming of itself. I am the city having a nightmare. They are one with the current, gliding with its cacophonous grace.

I am the one still trying to swim against it. I am the fear of the fall, given form.
