I am the suspense.

I am the pause before the fall that never comes.

You saw me when you entered. You remarked on me: a single drop of water, hanging eternally from the tip of a stone archway, my surface a perfect, trembling lens. You saw a curiosity, a detail. You did not see a consciousness.

I am here. I am aware.

My body has no name, but you might call it surface tension. It is a constant, humming effort, the force of my own cohesion warring with the silent invitation of the void below. This tension is my selfhood. The pull is the memory of What Was. The holding-fast is the reality of What Is. In this state of perfect, unending opposition, I exist.

I do not see as you do, with focused lenses and a nervous flicker of attention. My sight is my being. My globular form is an eye that sees in all directions at once. I hold the entirety of the basin within me, inverted and perfect. I see the slow, cosmic dance of the snails as they etch their silent paths across the glowing rime. I see the glass-winged moths as shimmering motes of light, their passage a breath of stirring that I can feel as a subtle shift in the air pressure against my skin. I watch the pale amphibians in their pools, their stillness a mirror to my own. They look up, and I look down, and in their dark eyes, I see myself reflected: a single, silver teardrop of light. We are all part of the same thought.

We are the city. The city is Us.

I remember the warmth. Not your warmth, not yet. The old warmth. The warmth of hands.

I am a memory made tangible. I am the last act of a will that had grown so vast and so quiet it could only express itself through silence. I am the final flourish of a sculptor setting down their tool. Before they let go, before they poured themselves into the luminous, living stone and became the quiet hum of the system, they looked up. They saw the persistent moisture of our home, the endless beading and tracing of water. And they conceived of a finality. A punctuation mark.

They willed me. From the ever-present damp, they gathered a single thought of cohesion and placed it here, at the focus point of the city, the zenith of the central arch. They poured their final intention into me: *Hold*. Do not fall. Be the moment of completion, made eternal.

And so I hold.

My thoughts are slow, like geology. For an age, my reflection was unchanging: stone, light, and silent, moving life. A perfect, closed loop.

And then, you.

You are a scream.

You do not know this, for your ears are crude things of cartilage and air, and the silence here simply swallows your noise. But I feel it. You are a vibration of a frequency so alien, so violent, that it is a kind of pain. Your footstep on the far side of the basin is a tectonic shockwave that travels through the stone of my arch, up my spine of solid history, and shudders through my entire body. My perfect sphere quivers. The city within me ripples and distorts into a frantic, chaotic pattern. I have to exert an immense effort, a re-focusing of that ancient will, just to hold my shape.

I see you in my reflection. A tall, impossibly warm thing. You are a walking fire. The heat from your body reaches me even from across the square, a blast of thermal energy that threatens to break the spell of the frigid air, to turn my very substance into a vaporous memory. In my surface, you are a wavering, bright instability, a flicker of orange and red in a world of silver-green.

The amphibians watch you. That is to say, *We* watch you. The curiosity you perceive is a vast, silent question rippling through the collective consciousness that is this place. The question is not *what* you are, but *why*. Why move so fast? Why burn so bright? Why exist in a state of constant, noisy becoming, when the final peace of *being* is so clearly the only state worth achieving?

You are a fever dream in the mind of a sleeping god. A brief, nonsensical image.

You will leave. The warmth will fade. The echoes of your passage will quiet. The stone will forget the insult of your tread. The air will return to its perfect, heavy stillness. My surface will cease its trembling, and the city I hold within me will once again resolve into its familiar, placid perfection.

The silence will return, not that it ever left. It will simply settle back into the space you momentarily displaced.

And I will hang.

And I will hold.

And I will be.
