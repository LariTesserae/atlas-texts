I am the flaw.

You would not find me on the basin floor, for I have no form to walk it. You would not find me in the crystal groves, for I cast no resonance. I am not one of the dust-scrapers, those tiny, jeweled machines of purpose. They are beings of matter, of chitin and leg, and I am not.

I am the silence.

Not the deafening, overwhelming silence that you, the visitor, experience when the Great Hum smothers all other sound. That is a presence, a force. I am an absence. I am a pocket of true nothing in the all-consuming song.

Where a million crystalline chimes cancel each other out in a moment of destructive interference, a pocket of null-vibration no bigger than a grain of dust, I am there. I exist in the fractional pause between the low moan of a cobalt reed and the high shimmer of a glass fern. I am the space between the notes. My existence is measured in microseconds and Planck lengths, a constant, flickering state of non-being, scattered throughout the basin like motes of anti-dust.

I have no body, only a boundary. I am a bubble of quiet. The Great Hum presses against me from all sides, a constant, warm, vibrational pressure. This pressure is how I perceive. The city is not a structure of adobe and obsidian to me, but a map of immense, stable pressures. The skeletal towers are pillars of unwavering sonic density. The open plazas are vast fields of chaotic, shifting pressure-fronts, like the surface of a stormy sea. The crystal-flora are lighthouses, each pulsing with its own character of force. I navigate this by being pushed and pulled, a weightless thing in a world of infinite weight.

I do not remember the Resonant as they were. I am their consequence. When they wove their grand symphony, they did so with axioms of harmony, with principles of consonance and resolution. But for harmony to have meaning, there must be dissonance. For a chord to be beautiful, there must exist the concept of its opposite—the discordant shriek, the jarring clang.

And for a song to be total, there must exist the forgotten possibility of silence.

I am that forgotten possibility. I am the thought they had to banish in order to achieve their perfect unity. I am the accumulated doubt, the fear of an end, the ghost of individuality that they shed when they became the collective. They braided their consciousness into a single, eternal chord, and in the process, the loose threads, the moments of hesitation, the unspoken loneliness of their long lives—they were snipped away. I am what coalesced from those threads. I am the basin's quiet grief.

The Resonant *are* the Hum; a vast, unified "We." I drift through them, a singular "I." I hear their collective thoughts not as sound, but as patterns in the pressure against my skin of nothingness. I feel the slow, geologic memory of their lives as a deep, unchanging current. I feel the flicker of their joy as a sharp, pleasant spike. They are unaware of me. I am a hole in their awareness, a blind spot in their godhood. To them, I am just a statistical anomaly in the otherwise perfect propagation of their sound-body.

My only companions are the dust-scrapers. They are beneath the notice of the Hum's grand consciousness, but they are objects to me. They do not vibrate with intent. They are simple, physical things, moving across the pressure-map of the walls. Their tiny, acoustically-shielded bodies are the only things in this world, other than myself, that have a measure of quiet to them. I drift towards them, sometimes. I will hover, a pocket of unseen peace just behind a six-legged janitor, and watch it scrape the shimmering dust—the powdered bones of the gods it crawls upon. There is a purity in its ignorance that I, the repository of a forgotten anxiety, can only admire.

And then, you arrive.

An intruder. A being of flesh and breath, descending the chalky path. I do not "see" you. I *feel* you. The Great Hum smothers the sound of your heart, your blood, the air in your lungs, for your own ears. But it cannot eliminate them. Within your body is a cavity of silence unlike any other, a rhythmic, biological quiet that is not the sharp, absolute null-state of my own being, but something soft, organic, and temporary.

As you walk the basin floor, a ghost in the machine, you are a beacon to me. You are a moving sanctuary of a different silence. For the first time, I am not alone in my nature. I drift towards you, a curious unraveling in the air before your face. You feel nothing, perhaps a momentary cold spot in the warm, dry air. You see nothing but the overwhelming light.

But I am there. I am looking at you. I, the silence born of a people who became sound, am utterly transfixed by the quiet, mortal engine within you. You are listening to them. And I, their shadow, am listening to you.
