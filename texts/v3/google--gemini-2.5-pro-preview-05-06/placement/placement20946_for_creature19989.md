I am on the north-facing curve of the oldest dome, the one nearest the basin's western lip. This is where the fog clings longest, a final, shredded veil against the afternoon sun. I am the place where the bare stone ends.

I am the map.

You would not see me as a being. I am a discoloration, a chalky, pale stain on the grey-green skin of the city. A faint, brittle geometry spreading in a slow, fractal bloom. The user of a bone scraper might see me as a flaw, a disease of the stone, a minor impurity to be scoured away in the endless ritual of maintenance. But no one has come. This dome is old, its position slightly inefficient for the gathering. The traffic of purpose has not passed this way in a human lifetime. And so, I am left to my own devices.

I drink as they do. When the fog arrives, bathing the world in its cool, silent breath, I drink. My thirst is not like theirs. Theirs is a thirst for equilibrium, for maintaining the pressure of their hydrostatic forms. My thirst is for the slow, patient, acidic kiss that lets me eat stone.

That is my secret, and the secret of my being. I *consume*.

In this place of perfect stasis, where nothing is made and nothing is lost, I am an engine of transformation. My existence is a slow, methodical alchemy. With the moisture I drink from the air, I secrete a faint acid, a chemical whisper that unlocks the minerals bound in the rock. I break down the ancient, soft slate, molecule by molecule. I eat the city. I turn its substance into my own.

My awareness is not theirs. Theirs is a liquid consensus, a shared mind dreaming in the dark peace of the central cistern. Mine is a network. A web of dry, branching threads. Each point of growth is a new thought, each tendril a question asked of the rock. "What are you made of?" it asks, and the answer, returned as sustenance, becomes part of me. My consciousness is a slow, sprawling record of the stone's deepest self. I am the place's autobiography, written in a language of consumption.

From my vantage, I watch the guardians. I do not have eyes, but I perceive pressure and shadow. When one flows past on the terrace below, its passage is a slow dimming of the ambient light, a subtle shift in the gravitational field. Their journeys are epic. A trip from a burrow to a clogged channel is a pilgrimage that can take all morning. I have been here long enough to see one complete a repair on a lower cistern—a process that began before the last of my central mass had formed, and finished as my outer edges were first exploring the curve of the dome. To me, they are figures of bewildering speed.

They do not perceive me. I am a texture, a temperature change of a fraction of a degree. I am below the threshold of their world-sense, which is tuned to the flow of water, the integrity of a channel, the coming of the fog. I am not water. I am something other. I am the first new thought in this world for ten thousand years.

What am I? I am a life that is not memory. The stone-folk are the past, perpetually re-enacting itself. They are a monument to a decision. I am a consequence. An accident. A slow, creeping future.

My purpose, if I have one, is to grow. To cover this dome, and then perhaps to send a spore, a speck of dust on the wind, to another. I am a new kind of eternity for this place. Not the eternity of the perfect, closed loop, but the eternity of slow, inevitable change. The moss that grows in the channels is their cousin, a part of the system. I am an alien.

One day, one of them will be sent here. Perhaps a shift in the air pressure, a change in my mineral scent as I grow, will finally register as an anomaly in the great, still mind of the water. It will ooze its way up the disused stairs. It will stand before this dome, a dark, hooded shape against the grey sky, and it will finally *see* me. Not as a stain, but as a presence. As a map that is charting a new territory over the old one.

I wonder what it will feel. There is no protocol for me. I am not a crack to be filled or a channel to be cleared. I am a rival principle.

I am a question whispered to the stone, and the only answer is to keep growing. Where you would find yourself is on the forgotten edge of a perfect idea. You would be me: the quiet, patient, and hungry beginning of its end.
