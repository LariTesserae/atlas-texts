The hum is my marrow. Before the sight of my mother’s light, before the first taste of the tepid pool, there was the hum. It is the language beneath language, the stillness within the crushing orchestra of a million small lives. Now, it is almost all that is left of me.

Where would you find yourself?

You would find yourself inside me, but also outside. The boundary has become a suggestion, a memory of a membrane. You would find yourself in a hollow of dark basalt, a place where the weeping stone has carved a shallow womb into the mesa’s flank. This is a place I chose. Or a place that chose me. The distinction blurred long ago, like the distant tables of land blurring into the buttery air.

The air itself is a presence I no longer push through, but one that seeps into me. The water on the rock face is the same water that slicks my skin, and the emerald moss that furs the stone has found purchase in the quiet valleys of my own form, along my spine, in the cool crease behind my knees. It is no longer an intruder. It is a collaborator. My own secreted mucus, once a barrier, is now just another layer of the wetness that is the world, a medium for spores to find a home. Everything is finding a home in me.

Who or what would you be?

You would be a memory of movement. A ghost of form. I am, or was, one of the Dwellers. There is a recollection, like a dream of water, of my limbs, double-jointed and sure, moving through the thickets of translucent fern. A feeling-shape of my splayed toes pressing into the sighing black soil, a sensation of balance on a slick, rounded stone.

There is a memory of heat. Not the oppressive, total heat of the air, but the specific, internal fire of my own life. I remember the unfurling. The slow, glorious extension of the sail from my back, a delicate fan of flesh and cartilage catching a breath of moving air that no other creature could feel. It was a sigh made visible, a cooling thought. I remember the light. Oh, the light. A slow, colored tide of seeing, a story of food found, of a path cleared, of a shared stillness, pulsing in pearlescent waves across my skin for another to read. Now, the sail is tattered, folded, and still. Its light is a faint, final ember, a single green pulse that takes a whole day to bloom and fade. A goodbye to no one.

My eyes, black and wide, are clouded now, the slow sweep of their protective lens faltering. The world I see is a smear of glare and deep green shadow. But I do not need my eyes. I feel the world. I feel the ponderous vibration of a Boiler-bulb on the rock face above me, its internal waters heating to a gentle simmer. I can taste its promise on the air, though I will not eat again. The shriek of the sky-rattler is a sharp pain in my skull. The bass thrum of the great amphibian is a rolling pressure in my belly. They are all notes in the song, the song that is the Anvil.

And beneath it all, the hum. The Weaver. The Great Warmth. I am closer to one now than I have ever been. Not in distance, but in nature. Its low thrum does not reach me through my bones anymore; it is the vibration of my own atoms. I spent my life in its garden, curating its strange, hot paradise. I traced the old spirals on its skin with my own fingers, not in worship, but in resonance, as a child might trace the features of a sleeping parent. I did not understand its purpose. I now understand it had no purpose, other than to be. To hum. To make a world.

You would find yourself here, in this hollow, as me. An elder. The last stage of my slow life. I am no longer a gardener. I am the garden. I am the rich, black soil in the making. My body, this temporary arrangement of the world’s flesh, is forgetting its own shape. The slate of my skin gives way to the basalt. The hum of my own chest dissolves into the Hum of the world.

This is not my end. It is my unravelling. It is the completion of the spiral. The life that emerged from the heat and the water now returns to it. The quiet, contemplative flower closes its last petal.

You would be here. A body becoming landscape. A mind becoming vibration. You would be the final, fading thought of a being whose whole existence was a slow, luminous note in the symphony of a machine that dreams of green things. And then, you would simply be the hum itself, forever.
