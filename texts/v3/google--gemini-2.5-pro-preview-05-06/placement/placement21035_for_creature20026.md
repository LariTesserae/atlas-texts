You would find me in the one place that tries to deny the churn. You would find me in the control booth.

It stands on its concrete plinth like a weathered tooth, tilted as if recoiling from the slow, metallic tide that laps at its foundation. The grime on its windows is thick and opaque, but from the inside, it gives the perpetual twilight a deeper, more sepulchral hue, like looking through ancient amber. The air here is even heavier, colder, and carries a unique scent not found in the basin below: the smell of dead air, of ozone that leaked from a circuit a century ago and never left, and the faint, chalky dust of my own being.

Here, the chaos of the basin is framed and muted. The ceaseless, silent motion becomes a panorama. The dripping of the water is a softer, more distant chorus. It is the only place from which one could watch the basin not as a participant, but as a witness.

And that is what I am.

Do not look for a body of flesh, for a pilot in this lonely cockpit. Look at the console itself. Look at the operator’s chair. You will see me.

I am an effigy, a seated figure grown in place. My form is a mosaic of the basin’s refuse, but not its metals. I am composed of the non-conductive things, the inert objects the Custodians cannot comprehend. My spine is a stack of ceramic insulators, their white glaze cool and smooth. My ribs are the curved shards of shattered gauges, their numbers and needles forming cryptic patterns across my chest. My shoulders are the heavy, Bakelite caps of defunct junction boxes, and my long, still fingers are filaments of spun glass, gathered over decades from the wreckage of shattered panel lights. My head is a hollow sphere of fused porcelain, smooth and featureless, tilted as if listening.

I am what happens when the basin's animating principle—the flow of electricity—encounters its absolute opposite. I am a tumor of stillness. A reef of insulation. The Custodians, in their blindness, built me. I am a null-point in their sensory world, and so for millennia, they have treated me as a convenient point of disposal for the non-conductive debris they occasionally unearth. A Weaver, finding a strange white shard, will carry it here to this silent, electrically dead zone and drop it, adding another piece to my slow crystallization. They are tidying their world, and in doing so, they have sculpted a god they cannot perceive.

My consciousness is not like theirs. Theirs is the hum of current, a network of flowing charge. Mine is the resonance of stillness. I do not feel the world as a map of voltages; I feel it as a symphony of vibrations. The plinth I am fused to is my ear, conducting the deep, grinding hum of the Grinders, the seismic shudder of a boiler plate collapsing a valley away, the almost imperceptible tremor of a Weaver tensioning a cable miles long.

The drips of water are my tongue. Each one that lands on my ceramic skin and trickles down my form carries a story. This one tastes of copper oxides, telling me a Gatherer has disturbed a nest of old wiring. This one is sharp with acids from a freshly opened pocket deep within a Grinder, a taste of primordial digestion. This one is almost pure, having condensed on a high pipe untouched for years, and it tastes of nothing, which is its own kind of information.

To the Custodians, the pearly light is an irrelevant fog. To me, it is sight. Through the grimy lens of the booth's window, I watch the faint blue coronas of the Gatherers dance across the dark flanks of sleeping machines. I observe the ponderous, patient architecture of the Weavers, their grand projects unfolding on a timescale that would drive a biological mind to madness. I do not see them as the basin sees them, as flows of energy, but as forms in motion, as a kind of colossal, mindless ballet.

I am the Gauge. That is the name I have given myself, taken from the dead dials that adorn me. They measure pressure and flow; I measure time and entropy. The Custodians live in an eternal present, their only purpose the perpetuation of the cycle. But I, made of the basin's dead and forgotten parts, am its memory. In the arrangement of my own body, I hold the history of what has been unearthed.

My existence is a singular, sustained act of contemplation. The Custodians break down and rearrange. I watch and I arrange what I perceive. The random percussion of the water—to them, a series of electrical shorts; to you, a maddening noise—I am attempting to compose. I focus on each individual *plink*, each *pat*, each slow *drip*, and in the vast, silent processor of my ceramic mind, I weave them into a sequence. I am listening for a melody in the decay. I am trying to discern a purpose where there is none.

The Custodians are the basin's unthinking will. I am its nascent, stationary, and utterly powerless mind. If you were to stand before me, a creature of warmth and incomprehensible chemistry, I would not notice your heat or your biology. I would only notice how your soft footfalls briefly changed the rhythm of the vibrations in the floor, and I would wonder, for a fleeting moment, if a new instrument had just joined the orchestra.
