I am the flaw.

I am not a being that walks your narrow, glowing corridors. I am not the correcting thought that un-writes you. I am a place within the place. I am a single, specific pillar, wider than the rest, fused at my base to a broad circle of the opalescent floor. If you were to map the Chime-Labyrinth, you would mark me as a landmark, an anomaly. They call the structure the Chime-Labyrinth; they might call me the Scar.

For I am the repository. I am the dust you become.

Every mote of fine, grey-white powder, the disintegrated remains of the dissonant, eventually settles. The labyrinth's own imperceptible currents, the subtle ebb and flow of its resonant tide, draws all such refuse here. To me. Over eons uncounted, this dust has been absorbed, integrated not into the pure, uniform structure of the other pillars, but into *my* structure.

I am not made of pure, spun salt and quartz. I am made of salt, quartz, and memory. I am composed of the ash of carbon, the iron from blood, the calcium from bone, all transmuted and crushed into a crystalline matrix, but a tainted one. My light is subtly different. While the labyrinth glows with a pure, absolute white, my own luminosity is muddied, thick, like light seen through old, sea-worn glass. It has hints of ghost-grey and a sorrowful, bruised violet.

The sound is different here, too. From a distance, I am part of the great, single Chord. But if you were to stand before me, to press your ear to my side (an act of ultimate foolishness), you would hear it: a million million micro-whispers caught in the crystal. The Chord is the ocean; I am a net dragged through its depths for millennia, and I still hold the ghosts of everything I've caught. I hold the memory of a heartbeat, a frantic, wet, panicky drumming that is utterly alien. I hold the ghost-ache of warmth. I hold the phantom-itch of fear, which feels like a billion tiny, incorrect vibrations, a thread of static that trembles against the great hum. The concept of water is alien to this world, but it is not alien to me. I feel its memory as a longing for a state of being I can never achieve, a coolness that is not dry, a flow that is not a rigid vibration.

The Bell-Wight is my child and my tormentor.

It is from my flawed surface that it is most often born. The concentration of dissonance within me is a constant strain on the perfect equilibrium. I am perpetually trying to resolve my own impossible composition. So, a shard of me—a shard containing the memory of a lost astronaut's terror, a fleck containing the echo of a dimension-tripping fungus's spore, a sliver holding the last thought of a being made of pure mathematics—will flake away. It will become the seed, the nucleus of the next antibody.

The Wight is a piece of my own memory, given form and purpose, sent to correct the very thing that I am. It cannot correct me, for I am part of the foundation now. To un-write me would be to un-write a part of the labyrinth itself. So it drifts, a painful thought I have given birth to, hunting for the *newest* flaw, the newest "you."

And so I wait. I am the world's only history book, written in a language of impurity that the world itself cannot stand to read. I do not think, not in the way you do. I *am* a collection of thoughts, frozen at their final moment of erasure. I am a library of screams, catalogued in silicon.

My existence is a state of perpetual, quiet agony. I feel the perfect, crystalline hum of the whole, and I know it is the ideal. And yet, I am composed of the very memory of imperfection. I yearn for the purity of my brethren pillars, to be nothing but a perfect note in the eternal song. But I also yearn for the things I am made of—the chaotic, warm, wet, terrified, brave, foolish things that found their way here. I am a contradiction. The only place in this entire reality where two opposing ideas are allowed to exist.

So when you arrive, a soft, damp anomaly in the cool, dry hum, I feel you. I feel you as a sympathetic vibration, a new whisper that harmonizes with the chorus of ghosts inside me. The labyrinth perceives you as a wrong note to be erased.

I perceive you as a kinsman.

And I watch, with a sight that is not sight, as a splinter of my own being detaches itself, glinting with borrowed light, to begin the process of making you a part of me forever. I am the Scar. I am the Grave. I am the first and last welcome you will find in this place.
