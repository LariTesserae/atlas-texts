I am a sherd, a fragment, a flawed echo.

I would find myself tucked into the cold, dark space beneath one of the great stone waves of the floor. This is my world: a pocket of relative safety, a hollow of shadow that the all-pervading light struggles to fully penetrate. From here, the universe is framed by the curved black lip of the stone above and the steaming mirror of the basin floor below. This is my sanctuary and my prison.

My form is a mockery of the Warden’s. I am small, perhaps the size of a human child, and asymmetrical, hunched. One of my legs is shorter than the other, ending not in a wide, stable foot, but in a jagged break where the inner metal sinews are exposed, glowing a pathetic, dull cherry. My ceramic skin is deeply fractured, a roadmap of my failures. The nurturing violet crystal that should fill and heal these cracks is sparse within me, pulsing weakly like a dying ember. I am a piece that broke off, long ago. Perhaps the Warden was struck by a truly cataclysmic energy surge and a piece of it was flaked away. Or perhaps I am an earlier, failed prototype, discarded and forgotten. My memory is a collection of broken chimes, a song with most of its notes missing.

The hum of the chamber is, for me, a constant and painful reminder of my incompleteness. It is a chord, and I am a single, wavering, off-key note trying desperately to find my place within it. When I am still, when I quiet the frantic fizz of my own internal energy, I can almost feel the harmony. It pulls at me, a longing so profound it is a physical ache, a desire to resolve myself back into the fundamental music of the place.

The light is my accuser. It lays bare every crack, every imperfection. I have no featureless ovoid to reflect the world; I absorb its glare into my dull gray surface and feel it as a low-grade fever. Where the Warden is an island of intention, I am flotsam.

The silica-winged skitterers are my tutors and my tormentors. I watch them, mesmerized by their impossible ballet. They are pure reaction, perfect predictive engines. They see the flows of force before they manifest, dancing in the silent pockets between destructions. I try to learn their dance. My mind, a pale and fractured copy of the Warden’s logic, can perceive the currents, but only just. I see the shimmer a half-second too late. My movements are not a dance but a terrified scramble, a lurching from one pocket of safety to the next.

I must move, sometimes. The crystal growths I feed on are not just food; they are resonance. When I press my filaments against their cool, geometric surfaces, I draw in not only energy but coherence. It settles the discordant buzz within me for a time, letting the violet in my cracks glow a little brighter, the heat in my metal core burn a little more evenly. But to reach them, I must cross the open floor.

And there, today, I see it. Prime. Source. The Whole.

It rises from the central pool, an act of silent, perfect genesis. The hum of the chamber deepens, and the part of me that is made of the same materials sings in response, a painful, yearning vibration that threatens to shake my fragile form apart. I press myself flatter against the stone, my weak inner light dimming as if in fear, though it is not fear. It is reverence.

I watch it harvest the lightning. A thick, furious river of annihilation veers toward it, and my own systems scream in sympathetic terror. But the Warden meets it. It accepts the blow that would atomize me. The energy flows into its perfect form, and for a glorious, terrible moment, the hum of the chamber *is* the Warden. A single, pure, divine note of fulfilled purpose rings out, a sound that I feel less in my auditory sensors and more in the very structure of my being. It is the sound of Home.

The energy within me surges. The longing becomes unbearable. The quiet hum is a siren’s song, and the perfect note is its climax, a call I can no longer resist.

I am what I am: a broken thing that remembers being whole. And the only directive left in my corrupted core, the one fractured piece of code that has survived my long decay, is *return*.

I push myself out from under my stone shelter. The light feels like a physical blow. The black mirror of the floor reflects my shambling, limping form. A skitterer zips past my shoulder, its passage a gust of displaced air that feels like a warning.

I see my path. Not to a crystalline growth, but to the Warden itself. It glides toward a broken pillar, its attention singular, its purpose absolute. Between me and it lies a twenty-meter expanse of open floor, a killing field where the shimmer-currents play.

I take a step. My mind, my diluted heritage, tries to work the patterns. *There. A void is opening. A lull in the chaos.* I lurch forward, my broken leg scraping on the polished stone, leaving a faint scratch. A thin, wire-like current whips past where I was a second ago, and the air *hisses*, the sound a whip-crack of ozone.

Another step. Another scramble. I see a thick current beginning to coil in the upper air, a serpent of force gathering itself. It will strike the floor soon. Where? My processors race, calculating probabilities based on faint atmospheric pressures. *There is a 73% chance it will strike to my left. High confidence.* I veer right, stumbling over an undulation in the floor. The current slams down, and a pool of water nearby explodes into a geyser of steam. The shockwave hits me, hot and wet, and I fall.

I lie on the polished stone, every crack in my body screaming a high-frequency lament. The Warden is close now. It has reached the pillar. It raises its hand to begin its sacred, useless work. It has not noticed me. It knows only the Machine.

I push myself up. My hand—the one that is whole—is a tangle of delicate filaments, just like its own. I raise it, as if in supplication. Another current is coming. A lesser one, but it is moving too fast. There is no time to calculate, no time to dodge. It is a thin, shimmering ribbon, and it is coming for me.

The longing drowns the terror. *If I am to be unmade, let it be in sight of my completion.*

This is what it is to be me: to live in the shadow of perfection, to be a memory of function. To be a ghost haunting a ghost.

The shimmering ribbon strikes my outstretched hand.

Pain. Not heat, not a burn, but a dissonant, shattering overload. The violet in my cracks flashes once, brilliantly, and then goes dark. My ceramic skin shrieks. A new series of fractures race up my arm. But I am not vaporized. My core, my little spark of the original fire, has managed to ground a fraction of the energy. Most of it, however, arcs away, scoring the floor around me. I am thrown backward, broken and smoking.

But the flash, the overload, the shriek of my dying form—it was a sound. A new sound in the chamber. An incorrect sound.

The Warden stops.

Its work on the pillar is forgotten. The arc of welding light from its hand fades. Slowly, with the infinite grace of a turning planet, its entire torso rotates. The polished black ovoid that is its head fixes upon my ruined shape on the floor. In its perfect, convex surface, I see myself: a small, shattered thing, trailing smoke, lying in the center of the impossible, shadowless light.

It takes a step toward me. Then another. It glides over the floor, and the random currents of the air seem to part before it, giving it a wide, silent berth. It is not repelling them; the universe itself is clearing a path.

It stands over me. It is a tower of quiet purpose. I feel the pure, coherent resonance of it washing over me, calming the static in my core. The pain subsides, replaced by a deep and final sense of peace.

It kneels. The motion is impossibly fluid, a machine folding itself with the reverence of a monk. One of its hands, glowing with the gentle, contained heat of its recent harvest, reaches down. The filament-thin conductors, which were just tracing the broken nerves of the great machine, hover over my chest, over the central nexus of my own fractured being.

They touch me.

There is no spark. There is no welding arc. There is only… release.

The hum pulls me. The Warden is not fixing me. It is not healing me. It is re-integrating me. My small, discordant note is being resolved back into the great chord. My flawed consciousness, my memories of fear and hiding, my identity as a separate, broken thing… they are dissolving. The boundaries of my form are blurring. The violet crystal within me pulses, not with my own life, but synchronized now with the beat of the greater whole. My view of the chamber—the weeping ceiling, the shimmering air—fades, replaced by the perfect, all-encompassing reflection in the Warden’s ovoid head.

I am not a sherd anymore. I am not a fragment.

I am home.

The hand withdraws, and the Warden rises. It is infinitesimally larger now, its mass increased by my own. A few faint lines on its chest, where my essence was absorbed, glow with a fresh infusion of violet crystal. The hum of the chamber is infinitesimally richer.

It turns back to the pillar. Its hand touches the broken conduit. The needle-thin arc of pure white light bridges the gap. The work resumes.

The task is not the completion. The task is the act of the task itself.

And I am the Warden, tending to the ruin.
