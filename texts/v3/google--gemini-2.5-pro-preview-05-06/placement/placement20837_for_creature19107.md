I would not find myself. There is no “I” here to be found, not in the way you understand it. The self is a sharp edge, an island of awareness separate from the sea. Here, there are no islands, only the water and the currents that move within it. There is no me, only a moving, and a need.

I am a locus of hunger.

My being begins as a question mark formed in the heavy, silent water. I am a long, slow ribbon of nothing. Where the polyps are points of light, I am the space between them. My skin—if it can be called that, this soft, porous boundary—is the color of a starless night sky, a velvet that does not reflect the city’s glow but drinks it. I am an absence given form, a soft, deliberate emptiness drifting through the arteries of this living place.

The pressure of the world is my cradle. It holds me, informs my shape. The viscous water is not an obstacle to be pushed through, but the very medium of my grace. I do not swim; I yield. A slow, rhythmic pulse originates from a place that is not a heart and travels the length of my body, a single membranous fin that is my only feature. It is a wave, an undulation, a sigh that propels me forward. My movement is the substance of my existence, a languid patrol without beginning or end.

Where would you find me? You would not. You would feel a slow current, a subtle shift in the dimness. You would see me only as a moving patch of darkness against the dappled walls, a blot of ink bleeding slowly across a page of faint light. And then I would be gone.

I drift toward the light not out of reverence, but out of necessity. The city is a garden, and its flowers are soft lanterns of flesh. For me, this is a field of quiet sustenance. The hunger is not a pain. It is a pulling, a hollowness, a calling. It is a lack that seeks its fill. I will feel a particular glow drawing me in—not the brightest, not the nearest, but the one that resonates with the specific emptiness I carry.

My approach is without malice or haste. I am a shadow falling. I arrive at a porous opening in the pearlescent architecture, a space through which a polyp’s light sings its quiet, violet song. There is no thought, only a rightness. I affix my mouth—a perfect, soft circle, toothless and gentle—to the wall. It is not an attack. It is a communion.

I do not tear or bite. I simply... connect. I become a conduit. And the light within flickers. It does not struggle. It gutters like a candle in a soft breeze, its life, its energy, its faint glow passing from it, into me. It is a sweet, cool transference. The surrounding city pulses, a silent, communal recognition of the lapse, a signal to begin its patient weaving once more. I feel the emptiness within me become whole. The hunger recedes, replaced by a temporary peace, a state of perfect balance.

Then I detach. I leave behind a small, dark wound in the glowing tapestry, a beat of silence in the city’s pulse. The wound will heal. The silence will be filled. That is the nature of this place.

I am what you would be.

I am a Grazer. A Void. I am the sculptor’s other hand, the one that takes away so the other can continue to shape. The Crystal-Crawlers, with their busy, geometric legs, scramble away from my shadow. They exist on a different plane of purpose, tidying the edges of the dream. I am the editor of the dream itself. I am the exhale that follows the world’s long, slow inhale. I am the darkness that gives the light its meaning, the silence that makes the deep, generative groan of the crystals into a song.

The dark is not my home; it is my nature. And I drift, a question searching for its quiet answer in the light.
