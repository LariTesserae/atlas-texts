I am not born of warmth and writhing, but of stillness and pressure.

There is no before. There is only a thrumming darkness, a cushioned wetness. My world is a sac, a tear-drop of fluid held in the heart of a great, green lens. Light, not as a thing seen but as a felt heat, is focused upon me, through me. It is a constant, gentle cooking. And there is a sound, though it has no source I can touch. It is five deep notes, a bass hum that is the very structure of my cradle, the scaffolding upon which I am woven.

I am not woven of muscle and sinew. I am spun. The hum of the world coaxes me from the mineral soup. A single seed, a point of perfect resonance, and then filaments branch from it, finer than thought, knitting themselves into a shape that knows how to float. My body is a hollow sphere of glass, filled with a lightness that wishes to rise. I am milky blue, pearlescent, a captured ghost of the floor below.

Then, the world a-thrum within my new form, the skin of my cradle-sac thins. It does not tear. It parts, like lips in a silent kiss.

I am released.

To be free is to be held. The air is not empty; it is a thick, hot broth. It embraces my buoyancy-core, cradling me. I do not fall. I rise, slowly, into the white glare. And the world opens.

Not to my eyes, for I have none. It opens to my whole being. My body is a bell waiting for a note, and the place is a symphony. The five pillars are the fundamental tones, unwavering, eternal. I know where North is by the deepest thrum, where South is by a hum a fraction higher. They are my compass, my map, my gods. The walls of our world are a shimmering, high-frequency curtain—a constant *ssssssssss* of water meeting heat, the boundary of my existence. Between the pillars and the walls, the air moves. I unfurl my sail, my rudder of solidified smoke, and the thermal currents catch it. I do not fly; I am danced. The world itself is my choreographer.

My life is this dance. I am a note drifting on the greater music. I skirt the hissing walls, riding the column of hot vapor that boils from the weeping rock. I trace lazy circles in the high air, a silent pilgrim in a cathedral of steam. Below, the metallic beetles scuttle, their hard shells creating infinitesimal, grating shifts in the vibrations of the crystalline floor, sounds too small for any ear but registered by my whole form as a kind of texture, a living grit on the world's skin. The great, slow pulse of the leaf-wombs, my siblings gestating, beats a gentle, life-giving rhythm. *Thump-thump. Thump-thump.* The sound of becoming.

But the dance is not without purpose. A hollowness grows in my core. The lightness that holds me aloft begins to feel thin, tenuous. It is a thirst not of the throat, but of the soul. A dissonance in the center of my being that pulls me from the high currents. I must drink.

The other giants, the carmine funnels, call to me not with a sound, but with an absence of it. Their deep, fleshy bowls are a pocket of relative quiet and coolness in the symphony. I feel their presence as a soft, receptive silence. I collapse my sail, letting my fine tentacles trail, and my descent begins. It takes an age. To the world, I am a mote of dust settling. To me, it is a pilgrimage.

I alight on the fleshy lip of the funnel. My touch is nothing. I am a thought landing on a surface. I extend the longest of my glass-thread limbs, the one that ends in a needle of hard, black crystal. The water collected within the plant's core is a trembling, turquoise mirror, cooled by its journey condensing through the vast, humid air. I dip my stylet. It pierces the pliant skin of the water's surface tension.

And I drink.

The fluid is cool truth. It is the minerals of the earth, the heat of the core, the essence of the weeping walls, all filtered and tempered. It rushes up the capillary of my tentacle and into my hollow form. The dissonance in my core resolves into harmony. The hollowness is filled. I am complete again, replenished. I am heavy now, but sated. I push off, my buoyancy restored, and rise once more into the white, humming air to resume my dance.

This is the cycle. I drift. I thirst. I drink.

But each drink leaves a residue. The minerals I absorb to mend my fragile form do not leave. They accrete. My glassy shell, once so fine, grows thicker. The milky blue of my youth deepens, coarsens. The music of my own body changes. The pure, bell-like resonance of my beginning lowers in pitch, becomes duller, more like stone.

The updrafts that once were invitations are now only fleeting comforts. The lightness within me fights a losing war against the slow, patient gravity of my own substance. The pull of the floor, once a distant abstraction, becomes a constant, quiet song. A destination.

The dance becomes slower, heavier. I can no longer reach the highest currents where the steam is thin and the light is brightest. I drift now among the great, dish-like leaves, my former cradles. I see—no, I *feel*—the new lives shimmering within them, their high, sharp resonance a memory of what I was.

Eventually, the day comes when the air can no longer hold me. My sail is fully furled, my tentacles hang like a dead weight. The lightness inside me is a prisoner in a crystal tomb. I begin my final descent.

There is no struggle. There is no fear. The dance is over, and this is the final, choreographed movement. I surrender to the gravity-song of the floor.

I drift down, past the thrumming pillars, past the silent funnels, toward the crackling pavement of evaporated salts. The world knows my purpose. The pillars hum a low threnody.

My tentacles touch first. Then the weight of my core.

The ground is a final, hot chord struck against the cooled, heavy glass of my being.

The temperature shock is absolute. My form, held in resonant tension for a lifetime, is released. I do not simply break. I become a sound. For a single, perfect instant, I am a complex and cascading chime, a shower of shattering notes that sings my entire life back to the caldera. It is the only sound I will ever make.

And then, silence.

The heat of the floor reclaims my shards. My solid self, my temporary vessel of "I," melts and sublimates, turning into a puff of the same mineral-rich vapor that weeps from the walls. A wisp of steam that will rise, that will condense on a cool leaf, that will drip into a carmine funnel, that will be drawn into a pulsating sac to feed the seed of the next song.

I am the chime. I am the vapor. I am the drink of a future self. I am home.
