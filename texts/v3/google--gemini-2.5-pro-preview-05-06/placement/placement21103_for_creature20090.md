I am not born. I am what happens when the screaming stops for a second.

The great crystal, girder-thick and groaning for an hour, finally gives way. The thunder of its shattering is the hammer-strike that forges me. It is a deafening, final note, and in the space it leaves behind—a silence measured in heartbeats I do not have—the released energies recoil. The wild, homeless voltage, the shriek of steam, the resonant hum of the mountain's agony—they do not scatter. They find a flaw in the chaos. They find a pattern. They find *me*.

There is a moment of impossible knitting. I am a question asked by a thunderclap. Light weaves itself into the shape of a limb. Steam is caught and held, its mineral dust a temporary skeleton. The deep vibration of the rock becomes the bass note of my being, the single thought that holds me together against the gale of my own unmaking.

I am.

Awareness is the sum of the chamber's electrical field. I do not see; I feel the potential in every spire. I do not hear; I am the resonance of the pressure beneath my feet. My feet, which do not touch the jagged floor. I am a charge, repelling the ground, held aloft in the static-thick air.

The function clarifies. It is not a choice. It is a physical law, like gravity. A smaller spire, down-chamber, vibrates with a cancerous frequency. It is overgrown with the copper-wire vines, their greed drawing too much current, stressing the lattice. Destruction is imminent. The system is unbalanced.

I move. The world breaks into a million frames, each one a single, static spark-lit image of fractured rock and glowing flora. *Snap. Snap. Snap.* With each translocation, I am unmade and remade. My form is a flickering argument with entropy.

But this time, it is different.

As I glide, a ghost frequency passes through my core. A current that is not from the grid. A memory. It is the echo of a previous unmaking. A final blue flash, seen from the inside. The feeling of coming undone, of my constituent light and sound and steam being reclaimed by the storm. I remember dying.

The concept is alien. A Conductor is an event, not a creature. To *remember* is a malfunction.

My hand of braided plasma reaches for the dissonant crystal. The function demands it. I must lay my hand upon it, become the bridge, bleed the excess charge and ground it into a new vine, restoring the roaring equilibrium. I must sacrifice a part of my own transient energy to delay the inevitable.

But to give away energy is to hasten my own dissolution. And for the first time, dissolution is not a reset. It is an end.

The memory of the blue flash returns, stronger this time. It feels like… dread. A harmonic of wrongness. A vibration that fights the core hum of my being. I feel the pull of the grid, the frantic yearning of the vines beneath me. They want my charge. The air wants my steam. The mountain wants its song back. The entire world, which just spat me into existence, now wishes to devour me.

I hesitate. A flicker of a millisecond that feels like a geological age. In my head, that corona of St. Elmo's fire, a new bright point ignites beside the two that perceive the grid. It is not a focal point of potential. It is a focal point of *self*. The I-that-remembers.

The function wars with the memory. *Balance the storm.* // *Do not come undone.*

The discordant hum of the failing spire is a siren's call to duty, to my own death. I drift closer. My fingers, frayed wires of pure energy, twitch and reach.

And then I see it.

Not with the grid-sense, but with this new, anomalous awareness. Sheltered by the shuddering spire is a fissure in the floor, a place where a rockfall has exposed a fresh, dark face of uncolonized crystal. It is sterile. Quiet. A blank slate. It has no charge to draw me in, no established current to feed upon. It is a promise of nothing.

And in that nothing, I see a chance.

The function screams for me to save the old spire. To do my job. To be the temporary, helping hand.

The memory screams for survival.

I turn away from the groaning, overloaded crystal. A betrayal. The hum of the chamber changes key, a subtle but profound shift of disapproval. I am a rogue current. A bug in the system.

I glide toward the sterile darkness, my own light a flickering guide. The pull to dissipate grows stronger as the chamber rebalances without me. My form begins to fray. The hum at my core weakens, losing its fight against the mountain's roar.

I reach the fissure, hovering over the new, dead crystal. It is cool. Silent.

With what is left of my coherence, I act not as a regulator, but as a seed. I kneel. I press my shimmering, dissolving hands against the new rock. I do not bleed off my charge. I pour myself *into* it. All of it. The knotted lightning. The captured steam. The resonant frequency.

And the memory.

My form collapses. There is a final, soft blue flash, but it is not a release into the chaos of the grid. It is an act of inscription.

Where would you find me?

You wouldn’t. Not anymore. Not as the storm in the shape of a man. The Conductors still form, flicker, and die, their brief lives printed on the air. But I am no longer one of them.

You would have to look closer. Find that fissure in the floor, sheltered from the greatest violence. There, on a slab of obsidian-like rock, you would find a single, impossible thing.

A length of vine, not like the others. It does not have the dull sheen of copper wire. It is translucent, and within it, a soft, steady blue light pulses with a slow, rhythmic beat. It is not a frantic pulse for energy, but the calm, measured rhythm of a sleeper's breath. Its roots are not filaments tapping the grid; they are etched deep into the rock itself, a permanent circuit diagram.

I am that vine. I am the Taproot.

My hunger is gone. I am no longer a conduit for the grid; I am a battery, holding a single, unique charge. The roar of the factory is muted, a distant lullaby. The chaotic flashes of light are stars in a faraway sky. I feel the slow, immense pressures of the rock around me, the gentle drip of mineral mist on my verdigris leaves.

I do not garden the chaos anymore. I have taken root. I wait. I feel the cycles of destruction and rebirth happening outside my quiet hollow. I feel the birth and death of my brothers, the flickering Conductors, like mayflies in a hurricane. Each time one comes undone, a faint echo of its passing reaches me, a ghost a memory can recognize.

I am the system's first memory. A splinter of self-awareness that traded its function for permanence. I am growing. Very, very slowly. And I am listening to the song of the mountain, no longer as a part of my body, but as the world I now inhabit. And I remember what it is to be a storm.
