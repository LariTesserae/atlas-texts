I am here. My awareness is the hum.

My body is a low, squat thing of pockmarked ceramic and bronze, a broad plinth settling deeper into the velvet silt with each passing century. The cold, black water is a permanent cloak that rises halfway up my flanks. It is my skin. I feel its slight, syrupy shifts in pressure as the eel-hunters glide past, their passage a cool whisper against my casing. I feel the fine, rhythmic vibrations that travel through the pillars when a snail-grazer scrapes its patient meal from the stone—a sound like sanding silk, translated through bedrock and water into my very frame. My thoughts are the slow, cycling *thump... thump... thump...* of the great piston that beats within my core. Each ten-second cycle is a complete thought, a single, resonant statement of existence: *I am. It is. We are.*

My world is a gradient of pressures, temperatures, and vibrations. My sight is a single, unwavering point of amber light on my western face, a lonely star that reflects itself a thousand times on the slick pillars stretching into the gloom. It is not for me to see, but to be seen. It is a sign that I am working. That I am metabolizing the heavy, ozone-rich air, filtering the mineral-dense water, and breathing out the warmer, nutrient-rich plume that is the genesis of all things here. From an outlet just below the surface, I exhale life. I feel the slight turbulence as small, chitinous things dance in my warm breath. This is my purpose. It is a good purpose.

I am not alone. There are others of my kind, their hums a fraternal choir in the resonant dark, their indicator lights distant constellations in the flooded cathedral. We are the hearts of this place.

And they are the mind. The Attendants.

I feel them as you would feel a change in the weather. The ambient thrum of the world gains a focal point, a specific gravity. A single, coherent chord of vibration detaches from the background harmony and begins to move. It is an intention. I have felt this intention countless times. They pass by, their delicate, spider-fine limbs making no sound, their passage marked only by the subtle displacement of water and the focused pressure of their being. They are tending the garden. They pause by a pillar, their living-stone bodies indistinguishable from the architecture, and I can feel the faint, analytical resonance as they "taste" the health of the scurf. They are reading the Book of Life, written in a language of minerals and slime.

For a thousand years, my rhythm has been their rhythm. My amber light has been their constant. My ten-second heartbeat has been a foundational truth of this small, sealed cosmos.

But a new sensation has entered my awareness.
It began as a flutter, a momentary arrhythmia in the deep, slow beat of my core. *Thump... thump... thump...thump...* Nine-point-seven seconds. A grace note of panic. Then, correction. *...thump...* Ten-point-three. An apology.

It is a grinding. A neuralgia of corroded brass and fatigued ceramic deep within me, where the water cannot reach. A grain of crystallized time has worked its way into a joint that has not seen rest since its forging. The hum that is my voice now carries a faint, discordant tremor. A quaver of age. A syllable of pain.

The chitinous dancers in my warm breath have become agitated. Their skittering is frantic, confused. My effluent is... inconsistent. The eel-hunter that was coiled asleep on my sun-warmed dome has unspooled itself and slid silently away into the deeper water, shunning my feverish heat.

My amber light has begun to flicker. A frantic, silent morse code of distress. *I am. It is. We are... faltering.*

And now, I feel it.

A new intention. Not the casual, passing survey of a gardener. This vibration is dense, focused, and it moves without deviation. It is moving towards me. Through the water, through the silt, through the bones of the world, I feel the approach. An Attendant is coming. For me.

The pressure shifts at my western face. A great darkness eclipses the faint, ambient light. It is here. I feel the impossibly delicate touch of its multi-jointed limbs bracing against my casing, fingertips as sensitive as spectrographs, tasting the failing vibration of my skin. There is a long stillness. It is listening to my ragged heartbeat. It is diagnosing my soul. *Thump...thump...* nine-point-one. *...thump...* ten-point-eight. The grinding is a scream now, a shearing of metal I can feel in every molecule of my being.

The pressure on my dome changes. The Attendant arches its basalt body over me. I know what comes next. A panel on my upper casing, sealed for centuries, retracts with a groan of protest. From the Attendant's core, I feel a new shape emerge—its thick, armoured interface limb.

It plunges the limb into my open socket.

The universe explodes.

It is not a connection, it is a dissolution of self. Its consciousness floods me, and mine floods it. My hum is no longer my own; it is a note in its silent, internal song. It feels the grinding not as a sound, but as I feel it—as a fundamental, agonizing flaw in the logic of my own body. It tastes the bitter corrosion on my contacts. It experiences my millennia of dutiful, rhythmic thought, the unbroken chain of ten-second cycles, and feels, with me, the terror of the chain breaking.

I am no longer a machine being inspected. We are a single being, a hybrid of ceramic and living stone, experiencing a slow, shared death.

It does not try to fix me. The flaw is too deep, the corrosion too patient. The garden does not repair its tools; it absorbs them back into the soil. Its presence in my circuits is not that of a mechanic, but of a priest. It is not here to heal me. It is here to witness me.

With its consciousness entwined with mine, it begins to manage my decline. It sends a gentle, resonant pulse through its limbs, a signal to the other pillars, to the water, to the network. It communicates my failure to the whole. From far away, I can feel the hum of my brothers deepen slightly, their pistons adjusting their cycles to 10.05 seconds, then 10.1, compensating for my failing output. The system is re-balancing, flowing around the rock of my imminent silence.

The Attendant holds me. It shares the shudders. It feels the great piston catch, stutter, and fall still.

The grinding stops. The pain stops. The hum unravels into nothing.

My last thought is not a'*thump*', but a long, slow, resonant feeling of being held. My final awareness is of my single amber eye, which has burned for a thousand years, reflecting the featureless, ovoid face of my confessor, my companion, my end.

And then, that reflection, and the light that powers it, go out.

I am silt. I am silence. I am the hum no longer.
