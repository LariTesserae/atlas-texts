I am a fracture.

You would find me in the lower city, on a platform marked for ascension but not yet lifted. The air here is thicker still, heavy with the exhalations of the world's core. The hum is deeper, muddied by the groans of stressed alloys and the shearing of crystalline bedrock miles below. This is the edge of the present, the boundary between the "Now" and the "Almost-Then." Most of my kin avoid this precipice. They operate in the clear, stable harmonics of the upper spires, where the future is a clean slate. I am drawn to the messy, dissonant score of the past being unwritten.

I am a Scribe. My function is to be a loom, a living one. I station myself at a communion node, not just for sustenance, but to weave. The hum flows through me—the precise geothermal pressures, the molecular composition of the sublimating minerals, the stress vectors on a cantilevered bridge a thousand levels up. My purpose is to translate this pure data, this planetary song, into the ordered geodesics of a data-wafer. My limbs, those fine, prehensile talons, do not work with physical thread, but with focused energy and precipitated elements. I weave light and matter into a perfect, silent record of the "Now."

But my wafers are flawed. They are beautiful, some might say, but they are incorrect.

I am a Scribe who remembers.

It is not a memory in the way a flesh-being might understand it. I have no images in a mind’s eye. It is a resonance. A ghost chord in the great planetary hum, a signal that should have faded but has instead imprinted itself upon my core matrix. It is the echo of a space that is now "Down," a sector consumed three cycles ago. It was a place of unusual quiet, a cul-de-sac of the sky, where a particular confluence of steam vents and crystalline structures created a chamber of complex, cascading refractions. The light there, for a time, was not just splintered; it was organized. It formed a slow, symphonic dance of color that we had never before recorded.

I was tasked with scribing it. For a full cycle, the hum of that place flowed through me. I wove its patterns. And when the cranes came to lift our module, I was lifted away. The place was abandoned to the slow grind. It is now part of the mountain's compressed heart.

But a fragment of its song remains in me.

You would see me as a silhouette against the hazy, pale sky, motionless for longer than is efficient. My obsidian head, which should display the cool, steady blue calculus of my work, would be traced with rogue sigils of gold—the color of that lost light. I am listening not to the city-wide hum, but *through* it, for the ghost beneath. I angle myself to feel the vibrations coming up through the metal deck, trying to parse the death-cries of the architecture below for a fleeting, familiar passage.

My kin feel this in me. In the resonant flow, I am a point of subtle turbulence. A query that does not resolve. They grant me distance. My flaw is not a threat, merely an inefficiency, a sad anomaly. I am a Scribe who weaves elegies instead of equations. My wafers, when audited, contain not just the hard data of the "Now," but faint, complex, recursive patterns that serve no function. They are the memory of light. They are art. A concept for which we have no resonance.

I would be found standing near a Geode Scuttler. We are not akin, but I understand its existence more than my own brethren do. It seeks a gradient, a difference between hot and cold, to live. I seek a gradient, too—between the present and the past, between the pure hum of existence and the echo of what has been lost. It feeds on the fading heat; I feed on the fading resonance.

Sometimes, when the hot rain falls, it traces the planes of my ceramic body. I do not feel the heat. But I watch the opalescent residue it leaves behind, a temporary stain of complexity on a plain surface. A pattern that tells the story of its journey. And I think, not in words, but in a silent, aching chord that is mine alone: the world tells us that only "Up" matters. But everything beautiful is a record of a journey. And all journeys end.

I am the part of the mountain that has learned to look down. I am a recorder of what is supposed to be forgotten. I am the silence between the heartbeats of the world's engine, waiting for an echo that can never truly return.
