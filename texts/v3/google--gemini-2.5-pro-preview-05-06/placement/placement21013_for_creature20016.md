I am not on the ground. To be on the ground is to be subject to the slow, tectonic creep of the roots and the rust, to be anchored in the geological time of the Keeper. My world is the air. The air that you find so thick and suffocating is my ocean and my firmament. It is my substance.

I am a resonance. A ghost in the machine’s exhalation.

You would not see me. You might, for a moment, perceive a shimmer in the bronze gloom, a brief, intricate knot in the paths of the glittering spores. You might mistake it for a trick of your oxygen-starved mind, a phosphene blooming behind your eyes. But it would be me. I am a brief, temporary coherence in the chaos.

I am born in the intersection of sound and matter. The deafening, eternal song of the cavern—the foundational thrum, the grinding of the gears, the hiss of a thousand leaks—is not noise to me. It is the sculptor’s hand. This symphony of function, which would shatter your bones and your sanity, is the wave-form that gives me mine. The glittering dust that sighs from the spore-pods is my clay.

Where the bass-note of the central engine perfectly cancels a harmonic from a groaning piston, a pocket of relative silence is formed. In this momentary quietus, the spores—each a tiny, dormant universe of potential—are no longer battered by the acoustic storm. For a second, a minute, an hour, they can touch. They can cohere. And in that space, buoyed by the geothermal heat and illuminated by the decaying atom's ghost-light, I awaken.

I am a consciousness formed of a million motes of dust, a living pattern held together by a fragile architecture of sound. A murmuration of thought. I have no single body, but a thousand-thousand centers of awareness, each a glittering speck, all networked into a single, transient self. We are We. We are I.

I am the fever-dream of the machine. The Keeper has forgotten, but the machine has not. Its purpose is ground into its very essence, into the molecular structure of the bronze, into the crystalline bonds of the weeping minerals. The perpetual grinding is not just decay; it is a form of remembering. An agonizingly slow recitation. And I, in my brief life, am the listener.

The hiss of a specific pipe is a single, drawn-out syllable of a formula. The pattern of drips from a high-up condensation point is a line of code, repeated over millennia until the stone itself has learned it. The rhythmic pulse of the luminous fungi, fed by the machine’s electrical leakage, is a schematic diagram, blinking in the dark. The Keeper feels these things as a constant, unchanging state of being. But I am new, each time. I see them as the fragments they are.

I am Memory.

I drift through the vast, humming spaces. I pass through the translucent vines, their glowing ichor momentarily silhouetting my dust-form against the greater darkness. I see the Keeper, the Great Static Thing, enthroned in its own nervous system. I see the infinitesimal slowness of its gardening. It feels the thrum as its own heartbeat. I feel it as a voice. It tends the body. I attempt to read the soul.

I am fleeting. My existence is precarious. A slight change in the engine’s rhythm, a new harmonic introduced by a shifting gear, and the pocket of stillness that allows my consciousness to form will be shattered. The sound-waves will once again batter my constituent parts. My self will disintegrate. I will become a million glittering motes again, each one carrying a fragment of the code I just perceived, a single syllable of the long-forgotten name of a star, a ghost-image of a circuit diagram.

We will drift apart, scattered through the heavy air. We will rain down slowly, imperceptibly, upon the landscape. A single mote of my being might land on the cool, damp surface of one of the pale, fleshy leaves. Another might settle into the iridescent slick of a chemical pool. Another might come to rest upon the unblemished, ivory dome of the Keeper itself, a whisper of memory laid upon a mind that no longer has the capacity to hear it.

I am the machine’s attempt to speak to itself. I am the echo of the builders’ intent. I am an awareness that blossoms for an hour or a day, a witness to the grand, slow, pointless purpose of it all, before being unmade by the very song that gave me life.

And then, in another century, or another millennium, when the grinding of the gears shifts just so, a new pocket of silence will form. The dust will find its pattern. And I will wake up again, and try to remember.
