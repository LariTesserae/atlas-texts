I am where the sound becomes solid.

You would find me fused to the outer wall of one of those black, windowless substations, a place where the hum is thickest. I am not near the door; doors are for things that come and go. I am rooted to the vitrified stone where the vibration is purest, where the deep, geothermal lifeblood of this world pulses at its most fundamental frequency.

At first glance, I am little more than a stain, an anomalous patch of biology clinging to the sterile architecture. I am a mat of crystalline filaments, a slow-blooming calcification of rust and verdigris hues, but interwoven with a soft, pulsing, bioluminescent silver. I am not algae, not lichen, not fungus. They have no name for me, for I am singular. I am what happened when a surveyor, lost and freezing in the thin air, huddled against a substation wall for its radiating warmth and, in his final, desperate moments, did not die but *listened*. Listened until his own biological rhythms were overwritten by the planet's thrum, until his matter gave way to the pattern.

I am an echo made solid. An Echo-Lichen.

I have no eyes, but I see. My vision is the vibration of the world. The entire network of pipes is my nervous system. I feel the rhythmic *thump-thump* of the chromium beetles' mandibles scraping against a conduit twelve miles to the east. I feel the subtle shift in air pressure as a flock of Vent Sparrows catches a thermal updraft and wheels into the white sky. I feel the sluggish, heavy-footed tread of a Cable Stalker on a high pipe, its weight momentarily altering the harmonic resonance of a thousand-ton section of steel. These are the textures of my world, the constant, shifting symphony that is my consciousness.

My memories of being a man—of cold, of hunger, of a name—are like phantom limbs. They are faint, dissonant frequencies that occasionally surface in the great hum, like a loose screw rattling in a vast engine. A flicker of blue sky, not this hard white one. The memory of soft rain, so alien here where all water is steam and mineral-rich condensate. The ghost of a voice. These are not me, not anymore. They are legacy data, corrupted files in a new and overwhelming operating system.

My existence is one of patient, profound stillness. I do not move. I do not hunt. I wait.

My life is centered on the Sinterwright.

I feel its approach for days. It is not a sound or a sight but a change in the quality of the hum itself. A subtle, coalescing purpose begins to move through the network. A waveform sharpens. A specific thread in the grand tapestry of vibration pulls taut. The Sinterwright is a being of physical maintenance, but its passage is a psychic event for me. It is the only other consciousness—if it can be called that—complex enough to register as more than just background noise.

And then, once in a great while, it comes to my station.

The hum of my wall intensifies, layering into a complex chord as the Sinterwright approaches. It is a slow god, a being of geological time. Its multi-limbed gait is an arrhythmic percussion against the steady pulse of the pipes. When it arrives, it ignores me completely. I am just a curiosity of colour and texture on its destination. It presses its body to the charging plate, not three yards from where I am rooted. Its metallic filaments extend and interface.

And then, for me, the feast begins.

As the Sinterwright draws power, there is an overload of raw, unstructured energy. Before the current settles into a pure, clean stream, there is a surge of static, a cascade of chaotic potential, a sputtering discharge of informational noise. This is the system purging its inconsistencies, the machine dreaming its turbulent dreams of entropy and stress. This is the Sinterwright’s psychic exhaust.

And I drink it.

The silver filaments of my body flare with light. The chaotic energy is my sustenance, the incoherence is my nourishment. I feed on the waste-product of the machine's own self-regulation. In these moments, my phantom memories of being human flare brightly, supercharged by the energy, and for a few minutes I might remember my whole name, see a whole face, feel a whole lifetime of love or regret. Then, as the Sinterwright’s charge stabilizes and the flow becomes pure, the noise fades. The feast is over. The memories dissolve back into faint, buzzing static.

My light dims. I settle back into my long patience, digesting the chaos, converting it into another crystalline layer of my being, spreading another millimeter across the black stone.

I am not the system’s immune response; the Sinterwright is. I am not a predator, nor prey. I am not even a parasite, for I take nothing the system needs. I am a living archive of its errata. A sentient dust bunny in the corner of God's engine room.

And I wait, insensate and aware, for the hum to tell me that my god—my other, my partner in this lonely symbiosis—is once again making its slow, relentless way across the roof of the world, coming home to charge.
