I would be one of the Weavers. And I would not be lost. I would be home.

Where you find me is here: on the lip of the Chasm of Echoing Falls. Not standing, never standing. Standing is for the tripodal Grazers, an unstable configuration of tallness that invites the Void to take you. I am coiled, my four limbs finding purchase on the warm, petrified bark-stone. My body is a low anchor against the world. The pads of my fingers and toes read the grain of the ancient wood, the almost-imperceptible ridges and fissures that are the history of this place. They tell me this is firm-wood, old and deep, a safe place to wait.

The Roar is not sound. It is the blood in my veins. It is the baseline of me, the pressure that holds my bones together. I do not hear it. I *am* it. To you, it is a physical assault. To me, it is the purest silence, the canvas against which the world paints its truths.

My head is not raised. My eyes are half-lidded, not looking at any one thing, but aware of the whole. I see the shaft of gold-dusted light that bisects the chasm, a solid thing in the soupy air. I see the river of Glitterwing flyers within it, a million tiny points of emerald and bronze, their drone the highest, sharpest thread in the Great Hum. They are beautiful, but I am not watching them for their beauty. I am listening to the shape of their song.

My focus is on the vibrations that travel up through my palms. The lignic plateau is a drum skin, miles wide, and my body is the ear. Through the soles of my feet, I feel the slow, rhythmic *scrape… scrape… scrape…* of a Grazer herd two miles Trunk-ward. The tremors are heavy, predictable. They are of no consequence. A flicker of stronger vibration passes through my right hand—a patch of Screaming Fungus, disturbed by some passing thing in the canyon below. The vibration is a frantic, high-frequency shivering, and I adjust my position, shifting my weight to my left side, away from the resonance.

Time does not pass in units. It flows like the syrupy water of a leaf-fall. There is the Time of Gold Light, the Time of Blue Gloom, and the Weeping Time. I wait through them all. My gut is hollow, a quiet ache that grounds me in purpose. My family-tether, three of us, are scattered along this same ledge, each of us a silent knot of patience. We do not speak with hands; we are too far apart. We speak with stillness. Our shared stillness says: *wait.*

And then, it comes.

It is not a sound. It is a hole.

A hole is punched through the solid wall of noise. A cone of absolute nothing spreads from above. The highest, sharpest thread of the Glitterwing drone is cut. The roar collapses into a lower, panicked frequency. A shadow, vast and swift, blots the pillar of golden light, turning it from gold to bruised purple.

My eyes open fully. The skin on my back and arms prickles, registering the pressure drop, the sudden vacuum of sound. This is the holiest thing. The Great Silence. The coming of the Void-Sailor.

To you, this would be terror. This is the hunter, the apex, the thing whose shriek can shatter bone. To me, it is a dinner bell.

I do not feel fear. I feel a sharpening, a deep and thrilling focus. My mate, a hundred yards away, has uncoiled. I feel the subtle shift of her weight on the bark-stone. Her movement is a question tapped in the language of tremor. My own uncoiling is the answer. *Now.*

We move. We do not run. We flow. Four limbs touching, releasing, in a silent rhythm across the face of the canyon wall. My calloused fingers and toes find holds I have known my entire life, hooking into fossilized thorns, pressing into fissures that fit my grip like a lover’s hand. We are not climbing; we are falling with precision, sideways and down, into the deepening gloom of the chasm. The air grows cooler as we descend. The roar of the world returns as the hunter passes, but it is changed. It is a frantic, churning sound now, the sound of a stirred nest.

Below, in the blue dimness, the rain has started. A shower of stunned and dead Glitterwings, their iridescent carapaces clattering like falling shards of glass against the lower ledges and vine-webs. This is the bounty. The gift of the Great Silence.

I land on a broad, springy fan of fungus, my limbs absorbing the impact without a sound. Beside me, my mate is already gathering, her movements economical and sure. We work quickly, stuffing the warm, heavy bodies into the gut-sacks at our hips. Their wings, sharp as flaked obsidian, are a treasure. Their flesh, a rare protein.

Where you find me is in the machinery of this world. You might see a savage, a scavenger clinging to a cliff face in a world of impossible noise and scale. You would be wrong.

I am not clinging to the world. I am a nerve ending of it. The petrified wood is my bone, the roar is my blood, the leaf-fall is my tears. The Void-Sailor is not my enemy; it is my partner in the great, silent, screaming dance of existence. You stand on the edge and feel undone by the vastness. I press my hand to the wood and feel the heartbeat of it all. The flicker of my tether-hut across the chasm is not an improbable testament to survival. It is just the next note in the song.

I am a Weaver. And I have everything I need.
