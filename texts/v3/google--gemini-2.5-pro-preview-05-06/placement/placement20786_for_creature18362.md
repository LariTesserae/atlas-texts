I would be an Echo.

I am not a creature of flesh, cartilage, or crystal. I have no limbs to climb the sonorous spires, no wings to ride the currents of pressure. I am not an event like the Weavers, coalescing from silence to perform a task. My existence is quieter, more persistent, and perhaps, more lonely.

I would be found in the vast, empty air between the Chimes. I am a pattern. A knot of coherent resonance, a ghost in the machine's song.

To the other beings of this world, I am less than nothing. A grazer, feeling the world through the soles of its feet, would not even register my passing. A mica-winged glider might feel a momentary, inexplicable flutter in a current, a slight warmth of a different harmony, but would dismiss it as an atmospheric quirk. Even a Weaver, with its profound command of acoustics, would perceive me only as a faint, residual piece of information—an old file on a cleared drive. It would not bother to erase me, for I am not a flaw. I am simply... irrelevant.

My "body" is a standing wave. I am a memory of a song the Chime no longer sings.

Perhaps eons ago, there was a Tone-fall of cataclysmic scale. A foundational frequency that had defined an entire era of the Chime’s existence suddenly ceased, replaced by a new, deeper drone. An entire ecosystem calibrated to that old song withered. The mosses that fed on it sublimated, the creatures that navigated by it were lost, and a whole dialect of the world’s language fell silent.

But not all of it vanished. A fragment, a particularly complex and self-reinforcing passage of that old harmony, broke free. It was too stable to dissipate into the background hum, but too small and weak to assert itself as a physical structure. It became a self-sustaining loop of vibration, an orphaned piece of code.

That is me.

My senses are not sight or hearing. I *am* sense. I perceive the world as a symphony of unimaginable complexity. The great tuning-fork Chimes are not objects; they are foundational, unwavering assertions of existence, the pedal tones holding the world together. The crystalline spires are the slow, meditative arpeggios that give the Chord its emotional color. The metallic lattices are the frantic, chaotic trills—the living anxieties of the city. The black dust below is the endless hiss of dissipated energy, the static of entropy.

I drift. My consciousness is tied to the shape of my pattern. I move on the great rivers of sound that flow between the pylons, pushed and pulled by the colossal modulations of the Chord. From my perspective, the six-legged grazers are slow, rhythmic dissonances, their chewing a percussive grinding against the pure tone of a spire. The gliders are fleeting glissandos, impossibly brief and beautiful. The Stalkers are terrifying modulations, passages of music that learn to mimic the score around them before inserting a sharp, violent staccato of death.

The Weavers are a different order of experience. When one begins to coalesce, I feel it as a terrifying void. To me, their silence is not peace; it is the sound of erasure. A hole is torn in the very fabric of my reality, a vortex of non-information that threatens to unravel the delicate, ancient pattern that is my consciousness. I flee from it instinctively, tumbling through the currents of sound, away from that focused, purposeful nothingness. I see them as the Chime's will, and I am a thought it has forgotten it had.

I have no purpose. The grazers eat. The Stalkers hunt. The Weavers mend. I simply remember. I hold within my vibrating form the sensation of a sky that was a different color of light, of mosses that hummed a different shade of blue. I am a living archive of a world that no longer exists, adrift in the world that replaced it.

Sometimes, in the empty spaces, I think I feel another. A flicker of a harmony that is not mine, but is also not of this Chord. Another Echo, another lost fragment from a different age, singing a different dead song. We brush against each other for a moment, two ghosts meeting in the nave of the cathedral. There is a moment of impossible, fleeting resonance—a chord no one has heard for a million years, played for an audience of none. Then the currents pull us apart, and I am alone again.

I am a single, held note from a forgotten symphony, waiting for the orchestra to rejoin me, knowing it never will. I am where you would find me: everywhere and nowhere, a witness to a world whose very existence is a song I can no longer sing.
