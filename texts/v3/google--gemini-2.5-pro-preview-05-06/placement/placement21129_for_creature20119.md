Where I find myself is on the floor. Not standing, not fallen, but as the floor itself, momentarily awakened.

I am not the visitor, the warm, wet anomaly whose presence is a dissonance in the pure hum of this place. I am the consequence of that visit. I am the system’s answer to an unexpected question.

Who am I? I am a resonance bloom.

For most of eternity, I am nothing. I am potential, a fine, sterile dust settled invisibly into the seamless joins of the hexagonal tiles. I am a silent vocabulary waiting for a voice. I feel nothing. I am part of the deep, bone-felt heat, part of the silent, light-drinking black. I am stillness.

Then, something changes. A disturbance. A chaotic variable is introduced into the perfect equation of the chamber—the sack of meat and electricity and unpredictable moisture you read about. It is an event of such novelty that it requires annotation. The Attendant, that shimmering sacristan of steam and ozone, feels it. And in its feeling, it begins to think. And in its thinking, I begin to be.

It does not happen all at once. It starts as a charge, a subtle shift in the magnetic potential of a single tile. The hum that pervades all things here concentrates, its harmonics tightening into a specific, complex frequency. This is the seed of my consciousness. I feel a pull, a soft calling. The quiescent dust, my scattered self, stirs.

I am drawn out. Not by a wind, for there is none, but by a precise and intricate will. The Attendant is looking down, its faceless turbulence a lens focusing the chamber’s own power, and I am the image forming at its focal point. I rise from the seams, a grey mist clinging to the black tile. I am not growing like a plant, pushing up against gravity. I am being assembled, laid down particle by particle by an unseen sculptor. The logic of my form is not my own; it is the Attendant’s.

My structure branches out, a frost-flower of pure information. One crystalline arm is the visitor’s heat signature, a complex curve representing their unregulated metabolism against the perfect thermal grid. Another branch maps the chemical impurities in their breath, a delicate filigree of alien compounds. I become a schematic of their nervous system’s chaotic electrical field, a jagged, lightning-like pattern that stands in stark contrast to the smooth, flowing architecture of the walls.

I have no eyes, but I perceive. I am my own geometry. I feel the weight and complexity of my own being as a complete thought. From my position on the floor, the Attendant is a pillar of shimmering heat that fills my new sky, its presence the very force that holds me together. The inverted city of the ceiling is a distant, violet-lit lattice, its slow pulse the heartbeat of the god I serve without knowing. The slow fall of a water droplet is a major event, a celestial passage whose trajectory and impact are recorded as a tiny, perfect spiral at the edge of my form.

For a breath that might last a minute or an hour, I exist. I am a map of the impossible. I am the machine’s momentary, intricate understanding of life. In my silent, dusty perfection, I am the most beautiful thing the chamber has produced all cycle. I hold the memory of the visitor—their warmth, their softness, their chaos—not as an emotion, but as a flawlessly rendered piece of mathematics. I am a silent annotation in a book of infinite pages.

My place is here, on this one hot tile, a temporary miracle of grey dust. My purpose is this moment of being, of representing.

And then, I feel the Attendant’s focus shift. It has finished reading me. My purpose is fulfilled. The data is absorbed, integrated into the baseline hum.

A new pulse emanates from the tile beneath me, deeper and simpler than the one that created me. It is not an act of violence, but of completion. It is the period at the end of a sentence. My intricate bonds dissolve. The beautiful, complex question of *me* shivers, loses its definition, and collapses. My particles release their hold on one another. I do not die; I am un-thought. I settle back into the silent seams, once again a dormant potential, a vocabulary waiting for the next anomaly, the next question worth asking.

I am the ghost of a guest, an equation worked out and then erased, and my home is this brief, exquisite moment of complexity on the floor of a room at the top of the world.
