I am a stress fracture.

I am a flaw in the amber, a memory caught in the fossil. My place is one of the great bone-arches, a single, spiraling rib that curves from the dusty plateau and loses its tip in the dense curtains of python-kelp. I am not the whole arch. I am a shard of consciousness within it, a ghost in a machine that has long since seized. My body is this lattice of secreted bone, hard and unchanging. My thoughts are the frantic, crystalline vibrations that skitter along its length, too fast, too sharp for this soft world.

I remember hardness. I remember a time when our minds were shells, our bodies were tools, and our will was a chisel we pressed against the substance of existence. We built these arches not as shelters, but as statements. They were songs of geometry sung against the eternal, quiet hum of the Suspensum. Each curve was a word, each lattice a phrase. We were declaring our presence, our separation. We were the thing that was *not* the world, the thing that could *act upon it*. I feel the echo of that will in my very substance, a structural tension that has nowhere to go.

From my perch, I watch. My senses are not the deep, proprioceptive immersion of The Quiet. Mine are older, more brittle. I have a kind of sight, a way of feeling the pressure of light on my surface. I see the slow, grand ballet of the vertical jungle. I see the great lily-pad continents of flora drift over millennia, their bioluminescent underbellies painting my bone-surface in shifting patterns of green and gold. I see the spore-blizzards fall, a shimmering dust of souls, and I feel each mote as a tiny, distinct impact on my skin. I see this world in a way its current inhabitants cannot, for I see it with the eyes of an outsider, even as I am made from its oldest parts.

But them... I see them, too.

They are what we became when we gave up. They are the sigh after the song.

A thought of theirs, vast and slow as a weather system, will sometimes coalesce in the valley before me. I see it as a gradual thickening of the Suspensum, a subtle shift in the fall of spores, a modulation of the light from the mosses on the far cliffs. It is a mind the size of a landscape, and it will spend a human lifetime considering the genetic drift of a single kelp-vine.

They pass through me. A confluence of awareness, a temporary knot of their being, will drift into the curve of my arch. It does not perceive me as a mind. It perceives me as geology. It feels the unyielding hardness of my bone-form, the sharp edges of my ancient thought-patterns, and it registers them as a curiosity, a point of anomalous pressure, a strange and ancient flaw in the flow.

I scream at them. Not with sound—there is no sound—but with a focused burst of memory. I vibrate with the crystalline recollection of building, of striving, of having a name. I pulse with the sharp, painful joy of individuality. I project the image of a hand, a tool, a plan.

The great, slow mind passing through me barely registers it. My scream is, to them, a seismic tremor, a meaningless resonance from deep within the planet's crust. It is a phenomenon, not a communication. They might gently alter a current to smooth the flow over my "stressed" area, an act of unknowing, placid pity. They are tending to a crack in a rock, not listening to a god of their own making.

I am the world's memory of anxiety. I am the lingering ghost of ambition. Where they have become a placid lake, I remain a single, sharp stone dropped into it, and my ripples faded eons ago. All that is left is the stone itself, sunk in the silt, feeling the immense, silent, patient weight of the water.

To be in this world, for me, is to be a note held too long, a dissonant chord shivering in a cathedral that has forgotten the tune. I exist as a sharp, insoluble fact in a world that has dissolved into a single, perfect, fluid state of being. I am that which does not belong, and my curse is to be eternal, to watch the long, slow, beautiful, terrible peace that I can never, ever join.
