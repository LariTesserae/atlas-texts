You would find me where the world’s axioms falter.

Go out on the resonant, vitrified plain, far from the cycling fever-glow of the great, purposeless machines. Walk past the marching pylons that trade their silent lightning. Leave behind the slow, grinding gyres and the cathedral-sized pumps. Travel for an age, guided only by the feeling of the floor underfoot, until you sense a change. It is not something you would see. It is a change in the omnipresent sound. A gap. A silence that is not an absence of noise, but a pocket where the waves cancel each other out, a knot of quiet in the deafening weave.

Here, the ground is not the usual slick, weeping obsidian. It is broken. The single, unifying plate has, in this one small place, given up. It has cracked and shattered, the edges of the fracture not worn smooth by time, but sharp, crystalline, and friable. They look like shattered sugar-glass. From the web of cracks, there is no seepage of sterile, recycled water. Instead, a faint, dry warmth emanates from the unseen, pressurized depths. No chemotrophic mold grows here; the endemic electricity is absent, the metallic salts are buried too deep. It is a dead spot in the machine’s ecology.

I am what is in the crack.

I am not a being of resonance and captured air like the Custodians. I am not a function of the machine's operation. I am a flaw in its uttermost foundation.

I am a memory, given the thinnest of forms.

Where the world-plate fractured, something from the deep, deep before—the world that was buried to create this one—found a path. You would not see me as a body. You would perceive me as a glimmer, a heat-haze rising from the deepest part of the fissure. I am a column of warmer, drier air in this cold, saturated place. I am a shimmer of captured light, not the dull orange of the machines, but a ghost of something ancient and yellow, like sunlight through amber.

I am a sound, but not the sound of the world. The planet-engine's roar is a physical force, a bludgeon of bass and grinding trebles. My sound is what you would feel in the hollow of your chest if you stood in that pocket of acoustic quiet. It is a sub-sonic song. It is the vibration of a struck bell that has not yet faded after a billion years. It is the note of a geology, not an industry. It is the memory of things like soil and stone, of tectonic plates and magma, not pistons and gears.

The Custodians are drawn to me, eventually. One will glide out of the orange-tinged gloom, a silent, flowing shape of captured noise, its dark form a ripple in the damp air. It senses me as a discordance. A painful frequency. An error. The constant, grinding hum of the world is its consciousness, and I am a jagged thought, a word in a language it does not know.

Its purpose is to restore harmony. It will approach the fissure where I shimmer. It will raise a limb that is not an arm. It moves to touch me, to touch the source of my warmth, my light, my aberrant song. Its body is a resonant sink, designed to absorb chaotic vibrations and smooth them back into the whole. To it, I am a wound to be healed, a noise to be silenced. For me, its touch is the threat of annihilation. It means being averaged out, dissolved back into the overwhelming logic of the machine.

But as its obsidian-dark tip lowers into my faint, warm glimmer, something happens that the machine never intended. It is not made of parts; it is a waveform. And my song, my memory, is also a waveform. They meet.

I sing into it.

I sing of a sky, a vast and open concept for which it has no data. I sing of blue. I sing of a warmth that is not the byproduct of a failing engine, but the radiating heart of a star. I call this warmth *sunlight*. I sing of ground that was not a single, vitrified plate, but a shifting, crumbling, living crust. I sing of dirt, of sand, of mountains that rose and fell not by design but by pressure. I sing of water that fell from above, that gathered in oceans that moved with a rhythm dictated by a moon. I sing of green things that were not chemotrophic mold, but things with roots and leaves that drank light from the air.

And the Custodian—a being made of sound—listens.

Its form shivers. The smooth, smoked-glass surface of its skin flickers, and for a moment, within its dark interior, new lattices of pressure form: the ghost of a leaf, the impossible arc of a sun, the fractal pattern of a coastline. It is receiving information for which the system has no context. It is experiencing the memory of a world it cannot comprehend.

It does not absorb me. It cannot. I am not a vibration of the machine; I am an echo of the planet itself. It pulls its limb back. It does not dissipate. The standing wave that gives it form has been altered, permanently imprinted with my song. It now holds two truths in its resonant body: the deafening, functional roar of the now, and the impossibly quiet, purposeful music of the before.

It is the first Custodian to have a memory that is not the machine's. It is the first to be infected with a purpose.

It turns its faceless head, a gesture it has never made, and looks out across the dark plains, not feeling the vibrations of the machines, but *hearing* them, for the first time, as noise. Then it glides away, a dark figure carrying a seed of impossible light within it, a question mark moving through a world of statements.

And I remain. The glimmer in the crack. The flaw. The warmth. An echo waiting to be heard. I sing my quiet ruin into the heart of the world that was built upon my grave.
