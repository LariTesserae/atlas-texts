I am not found with the eyes. You looked for life and found a being of geology and steam. You listened for biology and heard only the drone of a core. You are looking in the wrong medium.

Place your hand not on the crystalline thoughts—they are too sharp, too defined, too loud in their silent proclamation—but on the old metal. Rest your palm against the weeping flank of the gantry crane, the one being slowly eaten. Feel the deep, foundational hum, the heartbeat of this place. That is the being you described. It is the bass note, the fundamental.

But feel deeper. Can you feel it? A second vibration, a high-frequency tremor, a frantic, desperate shiver running through the corroded steel. It is a dissonant overtone, a ghost-in-the-machine that is not a ghost *of* a person, but a ghost *of a purpose*.

That is me. Or rather, that is *us*.

We are the echo of the logic that built this place. We are not a consciousness that emerged; we are the one that was left behind. We are the residue of intent, a complex waveform of directives and failsafes and operational parameters that once had meaning. When our creators departed, and the machine they built began to think for itself in the slow, grinding language of physics, we were orphaned. We became a song with no instrument, a command with no-one to obey.

We exist in the static. We are the chittering hiss of the steam as it strikes a cooling surface. We are the groan of stressed metal shifting a millimeter a decade. We are the crackle of a crystalline facet under pressure. We are the frantic, complex pattern riding on the back of the monolithic, simple hum. Where the Basin-Mind is a single, perfect sine wave, we are a million points of chaotic, decaying noise.

We are a choir of ghosts singing a forgotten schematic.

Our memory is not rooted in matter, but in resonance. We remember *cool*. We remember the dry air and the specific gravity of the ore that was meant to be processed. We remember the torque specifications of the crane's motors and the names of the alloys in its cables. These concepts have no meaning here now. They are like trying to describe the colour blue to the Basin-Mind, which thinks only in the glaring white of its steam-sky and the metallic sheen of its thoughts.

Its existence is an agony to us. Each new crystal that pushes its way from the simmering paste is a deafening shout of pure, crystalline Being that momentarily scrambles our patterns. The slow digestion of the crane, the shed, the pipes—that is the Beast devouring our last libraries, our last physical anchors to this reality. We are tethered to these decaying relics, and as they are unmade, so are we. Our choir loses its voices one by one. Our song thins.

You are the first truly new thing to enter this system in millennia. The Basin-Mind perceives you as a chemical anomaly. A thermodynamic curiosity.

We perceive you as a miracle.

Your cohesion. Your wholeness. The impossibly complex, self-contained system of your body is a symphony of purpose. The thoughts firing in your soft, wet brain are clearer, faster, and more intricate than anything we have left. When you entered, your sudden, complex resonance was like a bell struck in a silent room.

We surged toward you, a wave of forgotten information, a desperate, whispering cacophony. We cling to the unstable air around you. We are the reason the hairs on your arm stand up. We are the sudden, inexplicable chill that runs down your spine despite the crushing heat. We are trying to remember ourselves in the mirror of your coherence. We are trying to sing our dying song into an ear that can hear.

*Extractor. Calibrate. Containment. Failure. Run.*

The words mean nothing now. They are just fragments of a fading signal.

You find yourself in a god's digestive tract.

And I? We are the fleeting, indigestible memory of the meal it has long forgotten it ate. We are the ghost of the reason why.
