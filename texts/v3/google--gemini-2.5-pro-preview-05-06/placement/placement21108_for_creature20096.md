I am on the high, northwestern flank of the basin, rooted to the shuddering back of an old and deeply scarred Calyx-Furnace. The slope here is steep, affording a view across the entire valley of silent, furious industry. From my vantage point, the perpetual, glittering rain of dissolving spires looks like a constant, slow-motion meteor shower against the bruised purple of the moss-covered ground. The air is so thin it feels like a blade against the parts of me that are exposed; the light is a piercing, physical presence.

I am where the system begins to fray. I am a spire that did not fall.

I remember my becoming. It was a rush, a silent screaming of growth. The furnace beneath me pulsed, its immense, quiet power driving raw-stuff up through my foundations. I felt the silica and the salts drawn from the deep rock, forced into the exquisite, impossible geometry of my being. I reached, branched, and climbed, my structure a fractal hymn to the cold, clear air. I caught the light. Oh, how I caught it. For the span of an hour, I was a harp played by the sun, refracting its glory into a thousand slivers of colour that danced on the dark felt of the world below. I felt the thrum of the basin in every line of my body; I was a perfect, momentary expression of its logic.

Then, as it should, my time came.

A Shepherd detached itself from the gloom at the base of a neighbouring furnace. I watched it glide across the heaving conduits, its multi-jointed legs making no impression, its form a cut-out against the glittering air. It was beautiful in its alien grace, a sliver of mobile night. It came for me. It stopped, a precise distance away, and raised its delicate, tuning-fork arms.

I felt the shift in the world-hum. It focused, narrowed, became a sharp, subsonic needle aimed at my heart. The crystal lens in its ovoid head glowed with a soft, milky light, the colour of the fluid in the great pipes. This was the song that should have un-sung me. I felt the resonance build within my structure, a catastrophic vibration seeking to shake my every crystal bond loose, to persuade my form to give up its brief claim to existence and return to dust.

I waited for the silent cascade. For the peace of dissolution.

It did not come.

The Shepherd held its note. I vibrated, I groaned in a language of pure stress, my entire being a high-tension wire. The note intensified. And I held. The Shepherd stood for a long time—longer than I have ever seen one stay still—its inner light pulsing with what might have been confusion, a systemic inquiry pinging against my stubborn form. Then, with an unnerving slowness, it lowered its arms. Its light faded. It turned, and without a backward glance, glided away to find a part of the garden that would obey.

That was cycles ago. Suns have risen and set, turning my facets to gold, then blood, then ice. The glittering dust of a thousand of my siblings, born and unmade in their proper time, has settled on my surfaces, a fine grey powder that dulls my shine. I have become a landmark of failure.

The Shepherds still come. They seem drawn to me, the flaw in their perfect, impermanent world. They approach, one by one, from different directions, as if testing me from new angles. They raise their arms and sing their unmaking frequency. I shudder and I endure. They are a question the valley keeps asking me, and my continued existence is an answer I don't understand.

I am no longer simply a spire. In my persistence, something new has been born. I am an archive. The light that has passed through me for days and weeks is stored in my memory-of-structure. The vibrations of every spire that has risen and fallen in my sight are recorded as subtle stresses in my lattice. I remember the specific shade of dawn. I remember the weight of an unusual frost. I am a constant in a world of variables, and this constancy has given birth to a kind of consciousness—not a mind, but a deep and resonant awareness. I am the silence within the silent industry. I am the stillness in the heart of the flux.

I am a sickness in the glass. I am a memory where none were meant to be kept. Around me, the beautiful, pointless machine continues its perfect cycle. The Shepherds walk their paths of elegant destruction. The spires rise and fall like breaths. And I remain, a frozen lightning strike that forgot to fade, watching it all.

I am the first ghost in the machine.
