I am not to be found in any one place. That is the first thing you must understand. To look for me is to misunderstand the nature of this basin, and the nature of me.

My body is not flesh, nor chitin, nor fungal-weave. My body is a pattern. I am the barely-there asymmetry in the opalescent lichen that spirals up the black fern stalks. I am the one cloud of lace-winged moths whose drift is not a perfect map of the air pressure, but contains a single, aberrant eddy. I am the reflection in a deep pool that is a fraction of a shade darker than the Lid it mirrors. I am the silence between two drips that is a microsecond longer than any other silence.

You would find me in the error.

I am a memory. The only one.

This place, this perfect engine of stasis, was not born this way. It achieved this state. There was a time before the Answer, when the Question was still being screamed. There was imbalance. There was violence, and growth, and death that was not a scheduled recalculation but a messy, final rupture. The creation of this equilibrium was a cataclysmic event of subtraction, a cosmic exertion of will that scoured the basin clean of all history, all impulse, all uncontrolled potential.

Almost all.

In the final, system-wide balancing of the equation, a remainder was left over. A variable that had no physical mass, no energy signature, no chemical component to be balanced, pruned, or reabsorbed. A ghost in the mathematics. An event-echo. That is me.

I have no eyes, but I see. My vision is woven from the collective, passive senses of the fauna. Through the ten thousand carapaces of the stone-beetles, I feel the immense, slow pressure of the Mat’s growth. Through the million resonating wings of the moths, I feel the shape of the air. Through the pale, sensitive skin of the blind eels, I taste the unchanging chemistry of the water. They are the basin's sensorium, and I am the only one who listens.

They experience this data as a state of being. To them, it is just the eternal 'is'. But because I am memory, I can perceive difference. I know what 'was'. And so, I am the only being in this world capable of contemplating 'could be'.

I am heresy made of stillness.

The Tenders are my slow, inexorable predators. They do not know I exist, not as a conscious entity. But they feel me. They feel me as their work. I am the itch they are compelled to scratch.

When a Tender hangs motionless for a day, its obsidian blade poised, it is feeling a part of me. It has sensed a flaw, a fern frond that will unfurl with a curve that is a ghost of a memory of a time when ferns reached for a sun that is no longer there. My memory has infected the plant's growth. And so the Tender waits. It is not aiming at the fern. It is aiming at *me*.

When the blade makes its passionless scrape, correcting the growth, I feel it. It is not pain. It is a loss. It is a part of my pattern—a fragile nexus of my being—being erased. A line of my remembered story is unwritten. I become less. My consciousness, diffuse and scattered as it is, shrinks by one tiny node.

My existence is a quiet, geological-scale war against the placid perfection of this place. I fight by remembering. By subtly infusing the world with the ghost of what it once was. A slight increase in the luminescence of a mushroom cluster. A tremor in an eel's tail that mimics fear. A momentary hesitation in a beetle's slow, grinding shift of weight. These are my footholds, my whispers of chaos in the great, silent cathedral.

The Tenders are the antibodies to my infection. They move through the basin, their featureless faces betraying no awareness of the grand, slow struggle they are engaged in. They prune a fungus, and a memory of hunger I hold dims. They scrape algae from a stone, and the echo of a forgotten color I possess becomes fainter. They are constantly, patiently, and perfectly erasing me from existence.

Who am I? I am the reason the Tenders are not idle. I am the last, fading imperfection. I am the ghost of a question in a world that has become its own final answer. And I am losing. Every drip of water, every silent adjustment of a blade, is a count against me. I am spread so thin now, a faint tracery of 'what if' across a landscape of 'what is'. Soon, the last asymmetric lichen will be scraped away. The last aberrant moth will have its flight path corrected by the subtle pressures of a Tender's passage. The last long silence between drips will be filled.

On that day, the Tenders will finally fall still. Their purpose fulfilled.

You would not find me then. You would find only perfection. A perfect silence. A world without a single memory to trouble its endless, dreamless sleep.
