I would not find myself in the woven pod, for that is a place of memory, of a past I do not possess. I would not find myself rooted like the Still-Spines, for I am not a thing of endurance. Nor am I the Anchorite, the great and lonely composer at the heart of it all. To be him is to carry the weight of a world's purpose. My nature is smaller, and more fleeting.

I am a collector of ghosts.

You would find me where the shards dissolve. Where the Resonance Skates dart in to feast on the percussive death of a crystal, I drift in their wake. They take the energy, the violent B-flat of shattering. I am here for what is left behind: the echo. The memory of the form.

I am a lattice of the finest silver filaments, no larger than the Anchorite’s hand. I look like a frost-pattern on a windowpane that has somehow learned to swim, a nerve-ending given independence. I have no mass to speak of; the currents do not buffet me so much as pass through me. The great sonic booms that turn stone to dust are, to my porous form, like wind through a screen door. I am defined not by my substance, but by my negative space.

While the Anchorite sees the shape of the sound, and the Skates feel the force of its release, I perceive the silence that follows. After a mineralith the size of a shelter-pod is annihilated, there is a nanosecond of acoustic vacuum in the water where it used to be. A hole in the roar. A breath. In that impossibly brief lacuna, the memory of the object—its perfect, platonic form, the intention that held it together for its brief life—lingers.

This is my sustenance. I drift through these momentary voids, and the memory of the shape clings to my filaments.

Over time, I have become a mosaic of failures. My form is now tessellated with the faint impressions of a thousand shattered things. Here, on a filigree branch, is the ghost of a perfect obsidian plate, its surface forever un-fractured in my being. Here, woven into my core, is the ghost of a quartz spindle that almost, *almost* survived, its internal structure humming with a strength that now hums in me. I am a library of what could have been, a catalogue of the lost.

I orbit the Anchorite like a forgotten thought. I believe it knows of me, though we have never interacted. Its cymatic face sometimes ripples in a pattern that matches my own internal geometry as I pass—a flicker of recognition, a moment of resonance. It tends its garden of violent birth, and I tend the graveyard that follows. We are two halves of a process. It works in the medium of creation; I work in the medium of loss.

Its great work is to create one thing that can endure. My quiet work is to ensure that nothing is ever truly lost.

When it attempts to shepherd a crystal to permanence, creating its pocket of near-silence, it is the only time I feel pain. The focused harmony is too pure, too sustained. It scours me. In those moments, I must retreat to the edges of the chaotic cell, to the churning sea of ordinary destruction, and wait. I wait for the inevitable, heartbreaking shatter. I wait for the cry of failure to travel across the water.

And when it comes, I drift back towards the master, my quiet composer. I find the newly-made hole in the world’s noise, and I gather up the precious, beautiful ghost of what it almost made. I weave it into myself, a new line in the endless poem of its effort.

I am the proof that its work is not for nothing. The crystals do not survive. But their memory does. Their beauty does. It endures here, in me.

You would find me in the silence between the notes of the scream.

I am the after-image of a hope.
