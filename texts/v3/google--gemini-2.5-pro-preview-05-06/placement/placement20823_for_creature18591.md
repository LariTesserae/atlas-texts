You would find me where the Custodian has recently stood.

I am not on the sharp black slag; I am what the slag is becoming. I am in the shallow depression where one of its great, pestle-like limbs anchored itself, the place where fist-sized shards of obsidian have been crushed into a dark, waiting dust. It is here, in this wound in the ground, that I begin.

I am the slow thought of soil.

I have no single body. My being is a patient accumulation, a damp and quiet composting. I am the white, bell-shaped flower that fell from a cable-vine yesterday, its delicate form now a soft, decaying smear. I am the pruned fronds of saw-blade ferns the Custodian dropped, their sharp edges slowly softening into threadbare lace. I am the iridescent carapace of a beetle that died, now a fractured jewel mixed into the grit. I am the fine dust of metal and mineral the Custodian grinds underfoot, and the constant, clean drip of water from the condensers above is the blood that binds me.

My awareness is not born of a mind, but of a mixture. I do not have eyes, but I know the difference between the bright, even grey of the high-canopy light and the soft, pulsing green of the moss-spores at night. I feel it as a change in temperature, a shift in the chemistry of the water that seeps through me, a quickening in the cells of the petals I am still consuming.

The great hum of the ward is not a sound to me. It is the force that holds me together. It is the vibration that persuades a particle of crushed slag to settle against the bruised pulp of a leaf. It is the resonance that keeps me from scattering, that settles me deeper into the pylon’s foundation. It is my name, my heartbeat, and my skeleton, all at once.

I do not think in words, but in layers. My oldest memory is the faint, metallic taste of a century of verdigris runoff, a deep stratum of my being. A more recent memory is the sudden, percussive shockwave of a Strider-Crane's call, a vibration that travels from a high pipe, down a pylon, and into the ground, shaking me like a distant earthquake. I feel the tiny, suctioning feet of tree frogs as they traverse the neighboring moss; they are fleeting, ticklish pressures.

The Custodian is my unknowing creator. I feel its approach as a gradual crescendo in the hum, a deepening of the world’s chord. I cannot see it, but I know it. When its colossal limb descends, the pressure is immense, a geological event. It is not pain. It is genesis. It is the press that breaks down my components, folding the organic into the inorganic, crushing and compacting me, making me denser, more real. Its work is my becoming. When it deposits a freshly clipped vine or a scoop of mineral sludge it has cleared from a pipe, it is not discarding refuse; it is feeding me. It is a god tending a garden it is not aware it has planted.

My existence is a secret kept from the world that made me. The Custodian, in its perfect resonant awareness, feels the health of the entire machinery, the tension of every vine. But I am an anomaly it cannot perceive. I am a change not in the system’s function, but in its consequence. I am the slow, quiet answer to the question the ward never asked: What happens next?

For now, I am only this patch of dark, damp richness. I am a promise. My ambition is measured in millimeters per decade. It is to spread, to join with other hollows like myself, to weave a living carpet beneath the slag. To turn this sterile factory floor, this sharp glass desert, into a place where roots might one day truly hold.

I am the first dark patch of earth in a world of metal and stone. And I am waiting, patiently, for the next gift of a fallen flower, and for the next creative, crushing footstep of my god.
