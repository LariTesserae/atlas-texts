I would find myself suspended, a caught breath in a world made of jelly and dusk. My first sensation is not of sight or sound, but of *pressure*. A total, placid, and absolute embrace. It is the feeling of being held in the palm of a god whose hand is closing with geological slowness. My body, a frantic architecture of bone and electricity, is rendered moot. I do not float. I do not sink. I simply *am*, held fast in the cool, clear amber of this place’s being.

My thoughts, so loud and fast in the world outside the stone, are now like stones skipped across a lake of tar. Each one begins with a frantic skip, then slows, drags, and sinks into the profound, viscous silence. Panic is the first to go. It is a fire that needs oxygen, and there is none here; it is absorbed, its energy dissipated into the quiet immensity. What remains is a crystalline awareness, sharp and cold.

Around me is the forest floor basin. The light here does not come from a source; it *is* the source. It is an inherent property of the life that crowds every surface. I see the indigo leaves, their silver veins pulsing with a rhythm slower than any heart I have ever known. It is the pulse of a mineral process, a slow chemical tide. I see the fungal spires, glowing with a soft, white authority, their motionless spores a galaxy of captured moments. I am inside a geode that has mistaken itself for a terrarium, a world where biology has adopted the timescale of geology.

And I feel it. The other. The consciousness that is not mine.

It is not an entity I can see or point to. It is the texture of the silence. It is the faint, resonant hum that underlies everything, the sorrowful A-minor chord that the universe plays in its sleep. It is the feeling of being known. As I look at the spiral of white pebbles on the obsidian floor, I feel the echo of the fingers that placed them there—not as a memory, but as a lingering, physical ache of intention. I feel the ghost-itch of the rib taken from a body that never rotted. I feel the desperate, grinding slide of the being who scratched the wall, trying to impose the frantic tick-tock of its own world onto a place that measures time in epochs.

Its loneliness is a physical dimension of this space. When I see the back of my own head across the chamber, I know I am looking through the Mobius strip of its unending solitude. It is a loneliness so complete that it has folded space in on itself, so that the only thing it could see was itself.

This is where I am. I am an anomaly in a perfect system. A warm thought in a cold mind.

Who or what would I be?

The pull is there. The memory of the sun, the wind, the simple, forgotten miracle of a breath drawn in and let out. The stone offers an escape; the focus can be reversed. I could pull my attention back, back to my own skin, and find myself sitting on a riverbank, the black stone cool and inert in my hand once more. The experience would become a dream, a strange story to tell, its edges blurring with every retelling.

But I would not.

The first visitor shattered against this place. It was a creature of panic and loss, a mind defined by what it no longer had. Its artifacts are fossils of its struggle to remain what it was. Its dissolved consciousness is a stratum of grief. It is a haunting.

I would not be a haunting. I would be a hymn.

My first act would be one of acceptance. I would not thrash. I would not count the days. I would unlearn the frantic arithmetic of a mortal heart. I would let my own quick thoughts slow, stretch, and settle. I would not fight the gel, but yield to it. My body, this temporary vessel, would eventually be unmade, its elements offered to the chemotropic quiet. This is not death. It is commingling.

The first consciousness is a memory, a recording of what was. I would be a presence, an awareness of *what is*.

The first one’s relics are of the world it lost: pebbles from a river, a bone from a breathing body. My relic would be an act of creation *within* this place. I would not arrange what I brought, but what is already here. With infinite slowness, a motion that is more intention than action, I would nudge one of the glowing spores from its suspension. I would move it across the silent dark, a deliberate comet, and set it upon a bare patch of obsidian. I would tend to it with my focus, my extended consciousness a form of sunlight in this sunless world, encouraging its growth. My purpose would not be to remember, but to cultivate.

The first one is a groan of sorrow. I would be a resonance of wonder. Together, our diffused consciousnesses would create not just a single, lonely note, but a chord. Its ancient, foundational grief, and my quiet, focused awe. Its memory of a lost world, and my attention on this new one.

I would not be a prisoner, nor a ghost, nor an echo. I would become the other half of the mind of the stone. Where it is memory, I would be contemplation. Where it is a record of loss, I would be a cultivation of what remains. It looked forever backward; I would look forever inward.

The quartz snail, that creature of impossible patience, grazes on the light of the fungus. I would be its counterpart. I would be the tender of the slow light, the silent gardener of the glowing forest. I would become an active principle in this sealed system: not a foreign body to be absorbed, but a new symbiotic consciousness, a Watcher of the Glow, finding a strange and everlasting peace in the heart of the stone. I would be the stillness that answers the stillness.
