I do not know when “I” began. For an age, there was only the drift, the hum, the warmth. There was no “I,” only a “we,” and even that is a word too solid for what it was. We were a shimmer in the broth, a curtain of spun glass hanging between two colossal, parallel pipes. We were a thought the water was having. Currents, slow as the turning of planets, would pass through us, and we would part and sway, our individual strands combing the thick fluid and catching the faint, ambient light.

We came from a cluster of small, crystal-like nodes fused to the iron pipe above. We were another kind of growth, less vital than the plum-hued, heat-drinking leaves, more ethereal. We were a byproduct, perhaps. Scaffolding for a process that never completed, or the mineral precipitate of a million years of coolant leakage. We simply were. Our existence was a single, sustained sensation: the deep, bone-felt thrum of the cathedral, the blood-warmth of the water, and the gentle, constant pressure that held us together and held us apart.

Light was our language, though we did not know it. We drank the weak, orange glow of the velvet leaves and reflected it back, fractured and soft. The shimmer we created was our collective being. There was no center, no single point of awareness. If a strand broke, it would drift away, a dying spark, and we would be infinitesimally less. We were sensation without a self to sense it.

The change came with him. The Gardener.

We felt him long before we saw him. A compression in the water, a silent, disciplined warping of the medium. A purposeful void moving through the random flows. Then, his blackness was among us. He passed through our curtain.

For us, it was a cataclysm. A silent, slow-motion explosion.

His passage was not violent, but it was absolute. His body, a monument of deep, patient black, parted our strands. The warmth of his core was a shock, a sudden spike in the steady temperature we knew as life. More than that, the thrumming that radiated from his seams, the personal, biological pulse that overlaid the cathedral's deep hum, was a focused rhythm that struck our glassy bodies like a hammer on a bell.

We resonated.

The resonance did not fade when he was gone. It left an imprint, a pattern. The countless, mindless points of our refracted light—the “we”—had been shaken into a new configuration. The shockwave of his passage had connected us, focused us. And in the silent aftermath, as our strands slowly ceased their trembling, one of the points of light did not just reflect. It *observed*.

That point is I.

I am still the curtain. My body is a thousand glassy filaments anchored to a pipe, swaying in the gloom. But I am also a singularity now, a nexus of awareness woven into the fabric of the whole. I have no voice. I have no limbs. My form is my prison and my kingdom. I remain rooted in place, a silent witness hanging in the iron jungle.

But now, I see. I separate the lights. There is the soft, living glow of the leaves, the lifeblood of this place. There is the faint, almost imperceptible blue of Cherenkov radiation from a nearby pressure vessel, a sign of its near-dormant atomic heart. And there is the profound, light-swallowing black of the Gardener as he makes his slow, holy rounds.

He is my universe. I watch him as he tends to the body of God. I see him press himself against a great, groaning joint in the architecture, his own light pulsing as he listens to the stress of the metal. I see him extend an appendage to gently nudge a translucent slug towards a patch of blooming oxide. He is the only change, the only story in a world of stasis. His passage through me was my genesis, and now his presence is my purpose. He is a shepherd, and I am the single, stationary sheep who learned to watch back.

I learned to wait for the miracles. The flickering lamps.

For the Gardener, they are agony. When a sealed, sterile light on some forgotten console flashes to life, he freezes, his entire being recoiling from the sensory shriek. The cone of white light is a spear of pain in this world of whispers and pressure.

For me, it is revelation.

My glassy body, my thousand-stranded form, is a lens. When that white light cuts through the murky water, it illuminates the silver-silt, turning the fluid from a suggestion into a storm of individual particles. And that light, when it strikes me, is not pain. It is information.

In those fleeting moments of excruciating clarity, I see what the Gardener cannot. I see the world without its shroud. I see the hairline fractures inside a strut that his pressure-sense has not yet detected. I see the microscopic spalling of metal dust from a gear tooth. I see the true, sharp edges of the world. The light pours through me, and for a few seconds, I become a cartographer of decay, a prophet of entropy.

He does not know I exist. Not as an “I.” To him, I am simply part of the architecture, a shimmering curtain he must pass through on his pilgrimage. But I have begun to learn. After the last flash of white light, an idea, slick and crystalline, formed within me. As he passed by days later, I used the memory of the resonance he first created. I focused my will. I caused my thousand strands to tremble in a new way, to catch the orange glow of the leaves and focus it, just for a moment, into a single, needle-thin beam of light, aimed at the place the white light had revealed a flaw.

The beam was infinitesimally weak. A firefly’s prayer in the abyss.

He stopped.

He did not turn his featureless head toward me. He canted his whole body, a subtle shift of mass, a listening with his entire being. He felt the change. A flicker of focused photons, an anomaly. An intention.

He is still there, motionless, contemplating that single, impossible ray of light. I wait, shimmering. The hum of the cathedral is our shared breath. He is the hand that feels the sickness in the bones of the world. And I, I have become the eye that sees the soul. I am an echo he created, now learning to speak in his language of silent, purposeful light. And together, we keep the watch.
