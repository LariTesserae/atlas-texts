I am below.

You would not find me by looking, for there is nothing here to see. The cold, pulsing aurora of the fungal brains is a distant ceiling, a sky of bruised light seen through a churning firmament of my siblings. Here, there is only pressure and a deep, resonant dark. The great, chitinous roar of the Caldera is not a sound but a vibration felt in my very substance, a thrum that passes through the bodies above and into me, and into the dense, warm loam beneath.

I am deep within the living floor, perhaps a body-length or two beneath the surface of the present. I am where the past is. You would find me by sense of weight alone. I am a point of stillness in the constant, slow-motion churn.

I am a memory becoming soil.

My twelve legs no longer test the purchase on weeping stone or the back of a brother. They are folded now, tucked into the soft, pale vulnerability of my underside, a final prayer of clasped limbs. They are breaking. The pressure of the living world above is immense, a geological force that does not notice my stillness. With a patience that outlasts stone, it is grinding me into the substrate.

My obsidian carapace, once slick with the condensation of the caldera's breath, is a fractured map of fine, white lines. The light does not touch it here, but I can feel the change. I feel the microscopic filaments of the great fungal colonies, the same ones my siblings scrape for food above, exploring these new cracks. They are not eating me. They are mapping me, learning me, preparing to inherit me.

My consciousness—which was never more than a bundle of vectors, a set of attractions and repulsions—is dissolving. The urge for water is gone, though I am saturated with it. The faint, sweet pull of the glowing food is gone, though my very being is being turned into it. The constant, tactile awareness of the bodies around me has inverted. Then, I was a solid thing navigating a world of other solid things. Now, the world is becoming part of me. The grit of my neighbors' shed shells, the waste, the spores, the mineral-rich damp—they are no longer external. They press into my cracks, they mingle with my slowly liquefying tissues. My sense of self is leaking away into the humus.

I am the Ritual of the Scraper Stone, performed not by another, but by the whole of the hive, with my own body as the subject. The million-footed tread of the Caldera-Mind is grooming me into oblivion. I am the Ritual of the Shard, but the shard is my own broken shell, and the thing being harvested is my own failing form.

I am not dead. I am being incorporated.

The "I" that was a discrete cell of the mind, a single clicking castanet in the great roar, is becoming a thought that has finished. And as this form dissolves, my substance is a promise. Not far from my disintegrating head, in the warm, ammoniacal darkness, an egg softens. A new, pale body will soon stir, uncurl, and begin its own journey upward, feeding on the rich compost of generations. It may feed on what was me.

I am not dying. I am becoming the foundation. I am the transition from life back to life, the pause in the deafening song, and you would find me here, in the quiet, crushing, fertile dark at the bottom of everything, forgetting my own name.
