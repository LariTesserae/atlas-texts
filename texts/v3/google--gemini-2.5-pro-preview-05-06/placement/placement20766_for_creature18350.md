I am found in the seams.

My shell is the color of cooling embers, a mottled grey-black that drinks the faint light. It is a carapace of a dozen interlocking plates, hard enough to withstand a fall, flexible enough to squeeze through the tightest fissures in the growing stone. I am not a Gardener, not one of the luminous, slow-drifting Oallan who sing the rock into being. They are the giants, the masters of this vertical world. I am their shadow. A scavenger. A secret.

My home is a pocket, a flaw in the accretion that the Gardeners have not yet smoothed away. It is a hollow no larger than my coiled body, deep within the fractal lattice of a young spire. From here, I feel the world's hum not as a distant note, but as a constant vibration in my bones—for I have bones, small and hollow, a framework for my quick, wiry muscles. The Gardeners are cartilage and grace; I am chitin and haste. My six legs are tipped with hooked claws that find purchase in the microscopic roughness of the new-born crystal. I do not float. I cling. I crawl.

When the valley is calm, washed in the gentle, peachy glow of the sessile life-houses, I hunt. I do not hunt things that move; I hunt for leavings. The Gardeners, in their slow, deliberate sculpting, scrape away veils of the velvet-moss to guide a new channel of growth. They do not eat it, but for me, it is a delicacy. I skitter down the spire-face, a quick, multi-legged drop, my senses attuned to the chemical tang of disturbed life. I taste the water with the feathery antennae that sprout from my jaw, and the taste of a fresh scraping is a sharp, electric sweetness. I feast on the glowing dust, my mandibles clicking softly in the immense silence.

I watch the Gardeners. I know their light. I do not understand their slow, intricate conversations, the flowing symphonies of violet and gold that pass between them. But I know the basics. I know the slow, steady amber pulse of a settled mind, and I know that beneath a Gardener in that state, I can move with little fear. I know the sharp, inquisitive strobe of turquoise-white they use to examine the rock, and I know to freeze when that light sweeps over my hiding place, mimicking a shard of stone. And I know the sudden, valley-wide flash of alarm – a dropped tool, a tremor in the rock – and I know to be gone, deep in a crack, before the last echo of that light has faded.

My life is a staccato rhythm against their legato hum. They accrete conclusions; I am a being of pure reflex. A flicker of movement in the gloom, a change in the pressure—I am already wedged in a crevice, my heart hammering a fast, sharp beat against the inside of my shell. Their lives are centuries; mine is perhaps a decade, if I am careful.

Care becomes an act of worship when the god-beast comes.

It begins as a taste in the water, a new mineral tang, the scent of ancient distance. Then the low hum of the world changes its key, dropping into a sub-audible thrum that I feel in my claws. The Gardeners all stop their work. Their lights dim to a deep, reverent indigo. They anchor themselves and wait. I do not need their signal. I press myself into the deepest recess I can find, a place where the new rock has folded over the old.

Then comes the light. It is not the dappled, living light of the kelp and moss. It is a profound, primal blue, a wave of cold fire that washes away all shadows. It is the light of the deep, the light of beginning. It fills my vision even with my eyes shut tight. The spire I am clinging to sings with a new vibration as the leviathan glides past, its skin a universe of flowing bacterial constellations. The pressure changes, a slow-motion thunderclap that pushes me deeper into my hole. I see its shadow, a passing night within the perpetual twilight, and for a moment my small, fast life feels like nothing, a mote of dust in the path of a god.

But then it is gone. The blue light fades back into the gloom. And in its wake, the world rains.

This is my time of plenty. The passage of the great filter-feeder has stirred the abyss. A thick blizzard of nutrients, of plankton stunned by the pressure wave, of mineral dust sloughed from the god-beast's own hide, drifts down through the kelp forests. It coats every surface in a shimmering film. For days, I do not have to hunt. I have only to crawl, to cling, to taste. The Gardeners begin their work once more, their lights shifting to a celebratory pattern of shimmering, upward-flowing silver. Their city has been blessed. My belly is full.

I am a Remora, a Crack-dweller. I am a small, hard, fast thing in a world of soft, slow things. The Gardeners build the mountain, and I live within the pores of their creation. When my short time is done, my shell will lose its grip. I will fall, tumbling slowly into the denser, darker gloom below. And there, perhaps, I will land upon a young, rising spire, a piece of hardness and life where there was none. And the mineral rain will fall, and the velvet-moss will grow, and over ten thousand of my lifetimes, I too will become a part of the stone, my small, quick existence a secret heart within the slow, patient body of the mountain.
