I would be found in the gaps, in the interstitial spaces, in the humming dark between the great arterial pipes. You would not find me on the open plateaus or the vast causeways, for I am not a grazer nor a hunter. You would find me coiled in a forgotten junction box, or clinging to the underside of a support strut a kilometre above a chasm of weeping mist.

I am a Mender.

My body is long, sinuous, and tough, like a whipcord of living cable. It is perhaps two metres from the tip of my sensory hood to the anchor-claws of my tail. My skin is not a monolithic carapace like that of The Slow, nor the iridescent shell of the beetle-herds. It is a dense, flexible mat of chitin-fibre interwoven with scavenged metallic threads, the colour of a deep, old rust. It is pitted and scarred, a map of a thousand close calls and a hundred thousand cycles of work. I am built for torsion and tension, for squeezing through spaces where the city-organism groans under its own weight.

I have eight limbs, but they are not for walking. They are for climbing, for gripping, for holding on. They are arranged in four pairs along my body, each tipped with a cluster of fine, diamond-hard talons and broad, sensitive pads. My pads do not fuse to the stone like those of The Slow; they listen. They feel the micro-vibrations, the stress-harmonics of the metal, the rhythmic pulse of the slurry in the pipes. The world’s hum is my map, my language, my sight.

My head is a smooth, armoured cowl that I can retract partway into my torso. I have no eyes. The milky light is a distraction, a wash of useless information. Instead, two feathery antennae, delicate as spun copper but strong as steel wire, unfurl from my cowl. They taste the air, reading the chemical gradients, the humidity, the tell-tale signature of a pressure leak—the aerosolized tang of nutrient-paste where it should not be.

My purpose is not the grand, geological husbandry of The Slow. They are the architects, the great consciousness of the mountain. They maintain the eons. I maintain the seconds. I am part of the city’s autonomic response system, a nervous filigree that darts through the skeleton, tending to the small, immediate pains.

My world is sound and feeling. I am asleep, which for me is a state of low-frequency listening, coiled in the warm, resonant darkness of a service conduit. The bass note of the planetary core is steady. The tenor harmony of the district’s pumps is stable. The high, scratching descant of a beetle-tide flowing a hundred metres below is a healthy, constant noise. But then, a new sound enters the symphony. A dissonance. A high-pitched, uneven hiss, a vibration that feels like a broken tooth. It is a flaw, an inefficiency, a wound.

And so I wake.

I uncoil, my anchor-claws releasing with a faint *click*. My pads read the path. The hiss is north-east and down. I move through the world that is the inside of the walls. I scuttle along the length of a pipe wide as a fallen god, the metal vibrating with the lifeblood it carries. I squeeze through a pressure equalization vent, the warm, damp air rushing past my body, and emerge onto an open face.

Here, the world is vast and grey. The mist is thick, and the opposite wall of the "valley" is a ghost, a presence felt more than seen. Below, the shining river of beetle-carapaces flows without end. I ignore them. A shadow passes over me, slow and deliberate. One of the great crab-like hunters, stalking the edge of the beetle-flow. I freeze, flattening myself against a verdigris-stained bronze fixture, my colouration making me one with the corrosion. I do not register as food. I do not register at all. It moves on, its stone-tapping legs sending dull thuds through my frame.

The hiss is louder now. I crawl downwards, into the older, damper strata. The walls here are furred with the pulsing, anemone-like life. They are a carpet of soft, slow mouths, and they recoil as I pass over them, my claws scraping their silicate shells.

I find the source. A smaller capillary pipe, no thicker than my own body, has developed a fracture. A fine, energetic mist of the ruddy nutrient-paste is spraying into the air, wasted. It’s creating a slick, dangerous patch on the wall below and feeding a local, frantic bloom of microbial life that will eventually clog the surrounding mechanisms. It is a small wound, but a wound nonetheless.

This is my work. From a pouch below my cowl, I manipulate my two foremost limbs, which are finer and more dextrous than the others. One is a hardened chisel, the other a spinneret. First, I must prepare the surface. I scrape away the burgeoning microbial slime and the mineral crust around the fracture, my chisel-limb sending sharp, precise shocks into the metal. The sound is unpleasant, but necessary.

Then, from my spinneret, I extrude a filament. It is a substance I create in my own gut, a mixture of processed minerals I scavenge and a protein catalyst. It emerges warm and pliable, but hardens into a metallic seam upon contact with the air. I begin to weave. I am not building a web; I am stitching a wound shut. Back and forth, my fine claws guiding the thread, I lay down a patch over the fracture. I work the new material into the old, my pads listening constantly, feeling the hiss diminish, the pressure equalise. The jagged vibration softens, smooths, and finally, rejoins the great, harmonious hum.

My work is done. I have taken no food here. My payment is the restoration of the balance. I will move on, and in a few cycles, find a designated feeding nook, a small, benign node where I can press the orifice on my underside to the pipe and draw my share of the slurry.

I am not The Slow. I will not live for millennia, becoming one with the stone. My life is a frantic dash compared to theirs. I am a thought they have, an impulse, a reflex. When my time ends, my body will fall into the depths, and its hoarded metals will be consumed and recycled by the blind things in the dark pools. I will be returned to the system.

I crawl back into the humming warmth of the city’s bones, coiling in a new hollow. The world sings its monotone song. The harmony is restored in my small sector. I am a Mender. And I am listening for the next small pain.
