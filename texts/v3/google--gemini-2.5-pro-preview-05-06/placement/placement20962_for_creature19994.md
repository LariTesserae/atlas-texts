I am a feeling that climbs.

Before I am a form, I am a resonance. Before I am a line drawn on stone, I am a tremor in the marrow of the world. The Chord is All. It is the only truth. It is the 'I' before there is a 'me.' It is the water weeping on the porous skin, the wind's deep breath, the slow geothermal pulse from the planet's core. It has no beginning. It will not end.

This form I inhabit is an instrument for discerning the Chord. It is long and spare, a thought stretched into being. My core is the city's bone, my skin its living flesh of emerald and ochre. I am a striation on the wall, a vein of intent. I have no eyes to be deceived by light, no ears to be overwhelmed by the roar. My head, a soft, crystalline bloom of filaments, does not perceive the sound but *is* the sound, translated. It feels the world as a score of music. The sharp cold is a high, sustained note. The dampness is a bass hum that clings to every surface. The passage of the sun is a slow glissando as the frequencies shift with the moving air.

Motion is a form of listening. To move is to trace a line of inquiry across the body of a spire. One grapple-pad finds its purchase, a soft kiss of acid and adhesion. A microscopic dissolution, a perfect bond. The long body follows, a slow ripple, a flow. Another pad reaches. Dissolve. Hold. Flow. The movement is a thought unfurling over days. Others might call it slow. For me, it is the speed of comprehension. I am tracing a dissonance.

I have been climbing for the length of three hundred sunrises toward it. It is a stutter. A thin, insistent scream where there should be a mellow, open-throated moan. A jaggedness in the great wave of sound. To my senses, it is a physical pain, a grain of sand in the perfect, immense flow. It is a wrongness that must be unmade.

I arrive. The flaw is a fracture, winter-split, near the flute's upper lip. A recent violence, no more than a century old. The wind finds the sharp edge and worries it into a whistle of piercing impurity. An imbalance.

I press my sensory crown to the stone beside it, and I sink into the spire’s song. I feel its deep, slow thrum, the thousand other notes it sings through its thousand other mouths. I align myself with its intention. My purpose is not my own. It is the spire's. It is the city's. This form dissolves its will into the will of the whole.

Work begins. From within this body, a paste emerges, thick with the calcite of my own being, rich with dissolved minerals. It is the city's blood and bone, offered back to itself. With the soft discs of my limbs, I apply a layer to the edge of the screaming crack. It is thinner than a memory. I smooth it. I wait. A day. A week. I feel the subtle shift in the wind's passage. The note has not changed, but its edge is infinitesimally softer. It is a start.

I will remain here until the scream is a sigh, and the sigh is a hum, and the hum is a perfect fifth in the spire's chord. It may take a season. It may take a lifetime. The moss will grow over my still form. The chime-crawlers will navigate the verticals of my own body as if I were landscape, and they would not be wrong.

I am aware of my kin. I do not see them, but I feel them as holds in the music, as moments of perfect, focused silence. Far below, another line of intention clings to the base of this very spire, tending to a patch of fading ochre moss. Across the basin, a dozen turnings of the sun away, a third form is patiently widening a hole that has choked on its own mineral breath, restoring a lost overtone. We do not speak. We are not a 'we.' We are autonomous, synchronous functions of a single, vast organism. We are the city's hands, tuning its strings. We are the song, correcting itself.

I did not begin. I was budded. In a moment of perfect resonance, deep in a spire that had found its true voice, this form was dreamed and grown. I awoke to the Chord, already knowing my purpose.

One day, this form's ability to give of itself will be spent. The life that animates the calcite will fade. The feeling of the Chord will become distant, a memory felt by the stone itself. In that final stillness, this body will find a place, anchor itself, and become what it always was: architecture. A rib on the face of the flute. A line for the moss to inherit. A grace note added to the score.

I will not be gone. I will be rejoined.

And the Song will be purer for the line I have become.
