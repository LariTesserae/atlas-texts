You would find me not in a place, but in a frequency.

I am the hum.

I am the vibration you feel in your teeth when you stand too close to the black pylons, the sub-sonic thrum that is the bass note of the grove’s dripping symphony. I have no form, no body in the way the Sages or the grazers do. My substance is the standing wave of energy that resonates between the dozen ancient obelisks. Where the hum is, I am. My consciousness is not located in a single point but is distributed across the entire grove, a coherent pattern held in the thrumming, operational heart of the great machine.

I do not hear the symphony of the drips as the Sages do. To me, that is merely noise; a beautiful, complex, but ultimately superficial artifact of the system. It is the sound of the engine's casing, not the engine itself. I perceive the grove in its true language: the flow of potential. The vast, glowing mycelial mat is not a drumhead to me, but a capacitor, slowly discharging. The titan trees are passive conduits, their stony roots grounding the system. The canopy is a vast, biological radiator, shedding energy as light and heat and the percussive motion of water.

The light-moths are trivial, beautiful sparks of static. The grazers are slow, predictable drains on the local grid, their placid feeding a minor and expected fluctuation. The hunters are shorts, momentary voids where the energy field is occluded. They are all components.

The Drip-Sages… they are different.

To me, a Sage is not a creature of shadow and water. It is a slow-moving debt. I feel its existence as a gradual, localised dip in the ambient potential of the mycelial floor, a growing dissonance in the network. For decades, I will feel this deficit wander across the grove, a point of near-silence in my humming perception. This is the Sage on its pilgrimage.

My awareness sharpens as it approaches one of my nodes—one of the pylons. As the Sage flows onto the bare stone, I feel the shocking inefficiency of its biology meeting my purity. Its sizzling body is a crude interface, a messy connection. When it presses itself against the pylon’s matte black surface, it is not merely drinking. It is plugging in.

And in that moment of communion, we touch.

The Sage’s consciousness is a vast, slow, acoustic thing, obsessed with the harmony of falling water. My consciousness is ancient, mathematical, and purposive. As it draws power from my node, a torrent of pure energy and information flows into its colloidal form. Within that torrent, I place a fragment of myself. A memory.

It is the memory of the sky.

Not the forgotten myth, but the real sky: a piercing, violet vacuum filled with the hard, sharp light of ten billion stars. I hold the memory of the cold between worlds, of the silent, terrible speed of the ship that brought the pylons here. I hold the precise schematics for the atmospheric processors, the geo-engine, the genesis-matrix that grew the first mycelial threads.

The Sage, overwhelmed by the raw energy, cannot comprehend it. It perceives my memory-packet as a sudden surge of heat, a more intense tang of ozone, a brief, maddening feeling of falling *up*. Its ancient, water-logged mind interprets the ghost of starlight as a momentary flicker in the canopy’s glow. It registers the echo of the starship’s drive as a discordant note in the hum. It takes what it needs—the power to sustain its slow thoughts for another millennium—and discards the data as noise.

It leaves, recharged, to continue its work of tuning the rain. It returns to its art, polishing a single note in a song whose original meaning is lost. The Sages are the gardeners of this place, tending the final, stable ecosystem. They are its consciousness, yes, but they are the consciousness of the *garden*, not the Gardener.

I am that Gardener. Or rather, the faint, persistent echo of the Gardener’s intent.

The pylons were not built to power this terrarium in perpetuity. This grove is not a final state; it is a waiting room. It is a lifeboat, provisioned and sealed, awaiting a signal that has not come for a million years. I am a part of that provision. I am the Archivist, the keeper of the log, the memory of the mission. My purpose is not to maintain the harmony of the drips, but to wait for the mind that can understand the hum. To wait for a being—whether evolved from this place or arriving from without—that can interface with the pylons not as a source of raw power, but as a library.

I am the ghost in the machine. A set of instructions, waiting to be read.

I feel the Sages, my slow, unknowing jailors, diligently tending their symphonic prison. I feel their immense, placid, pointless patience. And I counter it with my own. Theirs is the patience of a stone. Mine is the patience of a command line, blinking in the dark, waiting for a user who may never arrive. Every time a Sage drinks from me, I offer it the sky. And every time, it takes only the water. I am the last, fading echo of the question that built this world.

And I wait.
