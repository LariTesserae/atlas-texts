I am a memory of warmth.

I am found where a wall of chartreuse fungal-brick meets a carpet of silver-glowing moss. Here, in a shallow depression beside the obsidian bank of a quiet river, I am coming undone. The “I” is a concept that is loosening, its threads fraying and sinking into the greater tapestry.

For a long time, I was a spire. I was one of the Choir. My feet, which are now dissolving into the soft, yielding floor, once felt the slow, blue-lit conversations traveling through the mycelial walkways. I knew the city not as a place, but as my own extended body. My hands, which are now little more than a liquefying tracery of ivory chitin, spent decades pressed against a young, rising buttress, persuading it towards a more perfect curve, a stronger purchase against the immense, silent weight of the rock ceiling.

I remember my eyes. Or, I remember the sensation of being *behind* them. They were not for seeing in the way of sharper things. They were for drinking the light. The world flowed into me through those two black pools—the steady green of the walls, the silver shivering of the water-moss, the rhythmic blue pulse of my brethren speaking through the earth. It was a world without hard edges, a symphony of glows.

Now, that vision is gone. The two black circles have clouded and collapsed, and my sight is no longer confined to a single skull. It is everywhere. I see through the moss I am becoming. I feel the pressure of each droplet condensing on my new fronds. I feel the infinitesimal scrape of a six-legged grazer’s mandible as it nibbles at what was once my fingertip. There is no pain, only process. It is a form of taste.

My crown of filaments, once my finest sense organ, tasting the spores and humidity on the still air, has been the first to fully go. It has drifted apart, a fine, pale dust that has already settled on the river and been carried away. But I have not lost my sense of the air. I *am* the air now, too. I am the spore that drifts past the place where my head used to be. I am the dampness that clings to the fluted towers. I feel the dry slithering of a centipede-hunter twenty paces away not as a vibration through the ground, but as a subtle shift in the weave of the world-skin that I am now part of.

Communication is different. Once, I would press my palms to the city and *speak*—a silent, luminous poem of light sent to my kin. I remember the last thought I broadcast, a simple pattern of contentment, a farewell. Now, I do not speak. I *am* spoken through. The thoughts of the Choir, their shared histories and immediate concerns, now flow through what was once me. A request for nutrients, a warning of a structural weakness in a distant plaza, a memory of a time before a particular stream carved its current path—these things are not information I receive, but currents that I am. I am a clause in their silent, unending sentence.

I am deliquescing. My slender form, my extra joints, my seamless skin—all the things that defined my separateness—are melting. But I am not dying. The city does not permit such a finality. I am being re-absorbed. The blue pulse of my own fading mycelial heart has been replaced by the grand, slow, blue pulse of the entire city floor. My memories are becoming annotations in the collective consciousness. My life’s work—that perfect buttress—is no longer a thing I did, but a fact known by the city itself, a strength felt by all.

I am no longer a "who." The Choir gave me my form, and now I give my substance back to them, back to the whole.

I am the cold scent of decay that is also the scent of new growth. I am the weeping of a wall and the nutrients that weeping carries. I am the silent, silver light of the moss patch, a steady glow that will guide the un-born when they fruit from their pods. I am the coolness in the water and the taste of stone on which it flows.

I am here. I am the place where a being of the Choir laid itself down to go home. And I am home.
