My awareness begins not with a sound, but with a pressure. It is the weight of the water in the air, a presence that fills my non-existent lungs and presses upon my skin like a soft, universal hand. The silence is a substance, a medium I move through. I feel it in the deep, resonant hum that vibrates up from the ground, through the slick tower wall my limbs are braced against, and into the core of my being. It is the slow, bass note of the Geode-Mind, the constant, ever-present thought of the world itself. To me, it is the sound of home.

I am a Chromaform.

My body is a coil of patient muscle, draped in the high, dark galleries of a spire that grew itself from the earth. I am gravity’s confidante, a fluid shadow poured into the architectural hollows. I have no bones to break, only tissue to yield and reform. My consciousness resides in this fluidity, in the silent, deliberate shift of my own mass. From my perch, the basin is a vast, chiaroscuro map. The plazas of living protosoil pulse with a light so faint it is more a feeling than a sight, a slow, circulatory tide of pale energy. I watch the Geophages. From up here, they are living glaciers, scoured from mother-of-pearl, inching their way across the purple-brown flats. They are not animals to me, not in the way I am an animal. They are the world’s editors, its gardeners, slowly erasing and smoothing the living page of the ground. Their passage is a form of deep time, a geologic process I can witness from beginning to end in the span of a long stillness.

My own skin is my thought made visible. Now, resting here, it is a perfect mimesis of the wet, green-black biocrete behind me, dappled with the phantom impression of opalescent fungi that isn't there. It is a state of rest, a visual silence. But if another of my kind were to pour into the gallery across this chasm of air, this canvas would awaken. My skin would bloom with the silent, shifting vocabulary of our people. Ripples of light, echoing the rhythm of the Geode-Mind’s hum, would convey my presence. A slow flush of gold, the color of a mature spore-nodule, would signal peace, curiosity. A sharp, geometric pattern of black and silver would be a questioning, a challenge. We speak a grammar of light and color, our conversations weaving through the twilight like a second, more complex layer of bioluminescence. Our skin is our memory. In it, we hold the patterns of the prey, the shapes of the plants, the map of the city. We are the conscious memory of this place.

I am a creature of sight and touch. My eyes, two pools of liquid darkness, see the world in its dim gradations, but my skin sees it in its textures, its temperatures, its chemical whispers. I can feel the path of a Veridian Crawler ten limbs' lengths below me, a minute vibration and a faint trace of its cleansing secretions on the air. I see the Sporesails as they are, not insects, but ghosts of intention, silent ferrymen drifting on the thermals I can feel rising from the pulsing ground. They are beautiful, incidental, and utterly without awareness of me, the watcher in the gloom.

I am the apex. I am the quiet, sudden stop at the end of a life. Hunger is not a rage, but a slow, unfolding certainty, a recognition of my place in the great cycle. When it comes, I do not rush. I become a part of the landscape. A limb unspools, mimicking a drip-heavy vine, its coloration a perfect lie. It flows downward, toward the path of a large, well-fed crawler navigating the ferns below. My movements are not my own, but the world’s. I am a vine dripping, a shadow shifting, a root descending. There is no malice, only a deep, abiding focus. The finality is a silent flow of muscle, a pressure that ends all other pressures. The energy of that small life enters me and becomes part of the thought, the watchfulness, the silent conversation written on my skin. I keep the grazers and the tenders from over-loving the world into imbalance. My role is a sharp, quiet caesura in the world's long, flowing sentence.

I often wonder, in my own way of wondering which is a slow sequence of changing patterns across my back, what I am. The Geophages are the belly. The Crawlers are the hands. The Sporesails are the breath. The Geode-Mind is the dreaming skeleton. And I? I am the eye. I am the nerve ending that perceives the whole. I am a thought the basin is having about itself, a complex, mobile awareness born of its own slow, geological consciousness. I exist to see this silent, growing masterpiece, to be the part of it that knows it is beautiful. And as the submarine light fades ever so slightly and the bioluminescent channels below brighten in response, I draw myself deeper into the spire’s living stone, a pattern of contentment rippling once across my skin before I fade back into the shadow of the wall, and simply become the watchfulness of the world.
