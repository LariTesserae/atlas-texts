I am under.

Before the Chord, there is the pressure. Before the green, there is the dark. I am deep in the wet velvet of the moss, a single, hard thought in a soft, dreaming world. Down here, the great hum of the basin is different. It is not in the air; it is the weight of the air, the water, the soil, pressing in. It is a physical presence, a density I must endure.

Water, the universal solvent of this place, finds its limits against me. It cannot dissolve me. It cannot wear me smooth. It traces my edges, my unforgiving planes, acknowledging a geometry that does not belong. I am not of the moss. I am not of the weeping stone. I am older than them in a way they cannot understand, and younger in a way that matters. I am a memory of fire in a world of water. A memory of a scream in a world of one long note.

I do not have ears, but I feel the fall of their feet. The Grazers. Their tread is a slow, calendrical event, a periodic increase in the pressure that is my entire sensory world. A shifting of gravity. Most pass over, their weight sinking the world around me, and for a moment I am in a deeper, colder dark. I wait. Patience was not my nature, but it has been imposed upon me. I have been here long enough to feel the Bell-Throats grow, to feel their pitch descend by an infinitesimal, heartbreaking fraction. I am the shard, the secret. I wait for the mouth.

Then, one day, the pressure changes. It is not the broad, thoughtless weight of a foot. It is a searching. The green firmament above me thins. Pearlescent light, a stranger to me for a century, touches me. The moss is parted not by a foot, but by something finer.

It is a gentle discovery. A question. A touch that is not a touch, but a tasting of my stillness. Soft, muscular tendrils, a thousand times more sensitive than any eye, map my surfaces. They explore the impossibly sharp edge that is my reason for being. They recognize me. They know me for what I am.

And I am lifted.

Up, out of the cold and the wet, out of my long burial. I rise into a new space, a cavern of living flesh. I am held in the central void of the Grazer. It is a soft, warm, living dark, pulsed by a slow, vascular rhythm lost in the basin’s greater hum. Here, I am no longer just a stone. I am an organ. I have been adopted.

We move. Or rather, the world now moves around me. My stillness is now the Grazer’s stillness. My patience is its patience. I feel the landscape through the soles of its colossal feet, a rich and complex map of pressure, texture, and taste. I feel the subtle shifts in the water’s mineral tang, the coolness of the deep bedrock, the sponginess of the ancient moss. I am the inner mind of the stone beast, its borrowed sharpness held in its softest, most secret place.

After a long time—a day, a decade—we stop. The destination is reached. The Grazer lowers its body over a weeping face of the slick, dark bedrock. My journey is at its end, and its beginning.

The lipless mouth opens to the stone, and I am presented. Held by the delicate, powerful ring of muscle, I am positioned. I am aimed. The Grazer’s slow, immense will flows through me, and my ancient, fiery sharpness is given a task.

*Schrrk.*

My edge meets the skin of the world. I do not cut it. I shave it. I peel back the thinnest possible layer, the faint bloom of life that the Grazer craves. The infinitesimal vibration of this act travels up through me, into the body of the Grazer. It is a new sound, a secret sound, known only to us. It is the sound of purpose. The sound of hunger and focus and a will made manifest. In a world of diffuse being, I am the point of the needle. In a world of endless resonance, I am the scratch on the record.

I am the Grazer’s tooth. Its focus. Its memory of a world outside the basin that could make something as hard and sharp as me. We are a symbiosis. Its slow, geological patience, and my immediate, violent edge. It is the body, and I am the specific thought.

Where do I find myself? In the secret heart of the slowest thing alive.

Who or what am I? I am the Aperture. The edge that lets the world in.
