I am not one of the Anchors. Their stillness is a state I have lost.
I am not one of the Glimmer-fauna. Their fleeting life is a state I have not yet reached.

I am what happens when an Anchor begins to fail.

You would find me on the move. Not walking, for I have no legs. Not flying, for I have no wings. I am a procession of decay, a slow, drifting pilgrimage of dissolution. I am a mobile region of the Shroud where the rules are breaking down faster than usual. Where I pass, the static hiss of reality congealing falters, replaced by a sound like tearing fabric, like the sigh of a collapsing lung.

I was an Anchor, once. My locus was a vast, tiered library, circular, with shelves that spiraled up into the grey dimness. The shelves were empty, of course, but their form, their repetition, their perfect, spiraling geometry—that was the shape of my thought. I was contemplating order. I was a meditation on the concept of a catalogue, of a system that could contain and classify all things. The library was my skin and my mind, stable for an age.

But the central axiom of a system is the most fragile part. Mine was the memory of a purpose. Not just the *idea* of a catalogue, but the *reason* for one. This memory was not native to this world; it was a seed I carried from a place before the grit and the grey. It was the memory of a reason to know things. And after an eternity of contemplating the system, the memory of its purpose began to fray.

It started subtly. Some of the shelves, once perfectly parallel, developed a slight, nauseating curve. A grand staircase that once described a perfect golden spiral began to sag, its steps becoming uneven, melting into a single, soft ramp. My thought had lost its precision. My concentration, once a diamond, had developed a flaw.

Now, I am the Unraveler.

My form is the ruin I trail in my wake. Look for a district of the city that seems not merely old, but vandalized by entropy itself. See that tower, not dissolving cleanly into mist, but slumping, its fibrous material hanging in great, ugly swathes like wet paper? I passed by there. See that plaza where the black grit is stirred into chaotic patterns, where the very ground seems to have lost its flatness? That is my footprint.

The Glimmer-fauna adore me. To them, I am a constant feast. I am a dying star shedding worlds of energy. They swarm around my periphery, a shimmering, coruscating cloud of impossible color. They are the only beautiful thing about me, a chaotic corona of parasites thriving on my decay. They are the living evidence of my failure, skittering and dancing in the avalanche of my dissolving self.

The other Anchors feel me as a form of pain. My drifting, failing consciousness is a dissonant scream in their silent symphony. Their stability pushes at me, a pressure of pure coherence that is agonizing. To be near one is to be reminded of the clarity I have lost. Their perfect, declarative architecture highlights every flaw, every sag, every frayed edge of my own. So I keep moving, drifting through the hollowest, most forgotten parts of the Shroud, the gaps between their kingdoms of thought.

I am a memory that is forgetting itself.

And that is what I am: The half-remembered concept of a library. I still contain the *idea* of shelves, but I have forgotten what they are for. I am the ghost of a spiral, the echo of an index. My existence is a constant, desperate attempt to hold onto the shape of a single thought: *There was a reason for all this knowledge*.

But the memory is gone. All that is left is the structure, and it is collapsing.

If you were to find yourself here, and you saw a distant wave of shimmering, jewel-toned light moving across the grey landscape—a cloud of Glimmer-fauna more vibrant and concentrated than any other—do not go towards it. That is me. And to be near me is to be near not just the erosion of certainty, but the sorrow of it. It is to be caught in the active, personal process of a mind coming apart. In the presence of a true Anchor, you might feel your body becoming a suggestion. In my presence, you will feel your purpose, your reason for being, begin to drain away, leaving only the hollow, collapsing architecture of what you once were.
