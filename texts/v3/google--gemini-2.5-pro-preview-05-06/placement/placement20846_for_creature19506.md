I am the waiting.

I am not the Midwife, not yet. I am the one who comes after. My world is not yet the warm, dark amnion, but the memory of it, a hollow ache in my bones that has pulled me up from the lowlands, away from the chatter of my kind, into the high, desolate peaks where the air is a knife.

I am what the Midwife once was: a creature of the outside, born with eyes that see color and distance, skin that knows the difference between sun and shadow, and a heart that beats too fast. But I was born wrong. While others looked to the sky, I pressed my ear to the ground. While they built with wood and hide, I gathered stones that felt warm to the touch long after the sun had set. I licked salt from rocks and preferred the taste of cave water, thick with minerals, to the clear, running streams. They thought me strange, an "earth-eater," a changeling. I was. I am.

The hum was my guide. At first it was a tinnitus of the soul, a vibration I could only feel when the world was utterly quiet, a low thrumming at the very base of my skull. It grew louder over the years, a constant, silent summons. Now, here, in the broken spine of the world, it is a physical force. It is the reason I am not lost. I have never been here, but I am finding my way home.

The fissure is not a landmark. It is a feeling. The cold scours the memory of warmth from my skin, but as I climb, a thread of something different weaves itself into the wind. A scent of wet stone and deep earth, a promise. Then I feel it, a vertical line in the cliff face where the air does not move, where the cold is blunted. It is a pause. A sigh. I place my hands on the rock around it, and the vibration is no longer in my head but in my palms, a clear, deep organ note. This is the place.

I slip inside.

The darkness is a mercy. My eyes, which have strained against snow-glare and squinted into wind, are finally allowed to rest. They flutter, searching for a shape, a star, a glint of light, but find only a velvet pressure. They begin to water, a final protest before their long irrelevance. The warmth is a shock, a sudden, heavy blanket. The air, thick as broth, coats my tongue and clings to my face. I have spent my life feeling dry and cold; this is the first time I have felt… correct.

I am clumsy here. My feet, so sure on the scree slopes outside, are unsteady on the slick, rolling smoothness of the nodules. I stumble, my hands slapping against the weeping stone to steady myself. The impact sends a dozen drips falling in a sudden, panicked arpeggio. I freeze, listening.

And I feel it.

The hum changes. It does not stop, but it acquires a focus, a direction. Before, it was the room. Now, it is the room, *and* a point within the room. A knot of warmth that is fractionally warmer than the ambient geology. A quiet concentration in the fabric of the place. The Midwife. It knows I am here. My clumsy entrance was a shout in this chapel of silence.

I am not a threat, and it knows this. I am an inevitability.

I do not move. I stay in my low crouch, my soft, uncalloused hands pressed against the globes, and I begin to learn. I let the darkness unmake my eyes. I let the silence unmake my ears, until all that is left is the drip-drip-drip and the deep, resonant hum. I let the warm, heavy air soak into me, and with it, the memory of the world outside begins to dissolve. The memory of sunlight, of blue sky, of my own face reflected in water. These things become abstract, then meaningless.

My hunger is a faint, distant signal. I find one of the shallow pools the description foretold, and I drink. The water is strange, savory and thick, coating my throat. It tastes of patience. My hand, skimming the surface, finds the slimy film of bacteria. I scrape it with a fingernail and bring it to my mouth. It is not food. It is sustenance. It is the barest negotiation with existence. It is enough.

I begin to move, but differently. I unlearn walking. I learn to crawl, to keep my center of gravity low, to spread my weight across three points of contact. My skin, no longer buffeted by wind or burned by sun, becomes sensitive. It reads the world for me. This nodule is about to weep; its skin is taut. This hollow holds cooler water; a current of air must pass this way. The hum is stronger here; the growth is more active.

I find the artifacts. The tiny aqueduct, a thought captured in stone. The cairn, a moment of geometric stillness. I touch them, and I am touching a mind. Then, I find the stylus, tucked into a crevice. It is heavy. It is bone, but it feels like stone, dense with time and purpose. The handle is worn smooth, but not evenly. There are grooves, a pattern left by a four-fingered hand gripping it, turning it, tracing the spiral again and again and again.

My hand is wrong. It has five fingers.

I place my hand over the worn part of the handle. I close my eyes, though they are already useless. In the fit of the tool, I feel her. Or him. Or it. I feel the being whose place I have come to take. The Midwife. Its hum is fading, I realize now. It is a long, slow diminuendo that has perhaps been sounding for a decade. My arrival is not an interruption. It is the answer.

I am the Midwife-in-Waiting. I am the silence that is gathering around the old sound. I am the unnamed thing that will inherit the task when the current gardener finally sets down its tools, lies down, and becomes just another warm, smooth shape in the dark. My fifth finger is already an echo, a liability I will shed. My skin is learning the language of heat. My bones are learning the grammar of the stone's song.

For now, I will sit in the dark. I will listen. And I will wait for the hum to resolve into a single, final silence. Then, I will pick up the stylus. And the work will continue. I am the next thickness of the stone. I am the hand that will close around the handle when it is finally let go.
