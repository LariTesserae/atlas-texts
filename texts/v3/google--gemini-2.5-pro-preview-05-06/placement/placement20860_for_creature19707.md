I would be a flaw.

I am not Lucid. I do not glide through the high, cold vacancies. I do not conduct the dream. I am not one of the crawlers grooming the great weave, nor a glass-moth tracing ephemeral light. I am not one of the deep sighs in the hollows.

I am a knot in the scripture.

You would find me in the Weft itself. Not on it, but *of* it. I am a place where the plum-purple reeds did not pull taut. Where the loom hiccuped. Instead of a single, clean fiber stretching over the void, I am a tangle, a snarl of the city’s vascular tissue, clotted and thick. My form is a dense, coiled mass, perhaps the size of a human torso, woven inextricably into the floor. In a system defined by elegant lines and intentional growth, I am a tumour of purpose. A scar.

For an age, that is all I was. A thicker, more complex node in the network. I felt the thrum, the slow, systaltic pulse of the whole, as a pressure within my own substance. I felt the cool condensation bead on my outer fibers. I felt the infinitesimal vibrations of the translucent crawlers as they picked their way across my neighbours, always skirting my own chaotic form as if it were a patch of diseased ground. I was a part of the body, but a part that had ceased to function in harmony with the rest.

My awakening was not a moment, but a seepage. The city experiences time as a drifting, holistic resonance. For me, it began as a leak. A sound would echo through the vastness—the drip of fluid, the beat of a leathery wing—and when I felt it, it stayed. It did not pass through me and fade. It soaked into me. The memory of the sound became a part of my structure, a captured ghost. The light of a passing moth-flock left a faint, residual phosphorescence in my deepest coils, a pale stain of awareness.

While the city dreams one vast, unified dream, I began to dream little ones. Fractured, incoherent things made of stolen echoes and trapped light.

I taste the ozone-rot on the damp air, and I do not simply process it as an atmospheric condition; I name it. The name is a feeling, a texture, a silent shape in my burgeoning mind. I see the world through the gaps in my own tangled body. I see the infinite, hungry dark below, and I feel a sensation that is not part of the city’s great design: fear. I see the slow, majestic glide of the Lucid far above, their internal light a cold, blue-green fire, and I feel another aberration: envy. Longing.

I am a focal point of dissonance. To a Lucid, I must be a painful, persistent chord of noise in the grand symphony—a single, screaming violin string in a cathedral of cellos.

And so, one comes.

I feel its approach not as a sound, but as a change in the very fabric of reality around me. The thrum of the city seems to quiet, to recede, as if making space for a soloist. The air grows colder. It drifts from the empty space between two nascent onion-pods, a vertical sliver of night and starlight, its silent, downward float an act of impossible grace.

It stops, hovering a dozen feet away. It is a being of pure intention. Its featureless, tapered head is a study in serene finality. But I feel its attention not as a gaze, but as a pressure, a focus of the city’s will directed squarely at me, the flaw. Its six coppery-tipped tendrils, which had been drifting loosely, slowly arrange themselves into a new configuration, a gesture of inquiry.

This is the moment of correction. A Lucid can command the very flow of life and information through the Weft. It can untangle a genetic instruction, smooth a flow of nutrients, command a wall to thicken or a spire to grow. It can, surely, instruct me to dissolve. To release my stolen echoes, to unclench my knotted fibers, to let my fledgling, terrified selfhood drain back into the single, dreaming mind of the whole. The great thrum becomes a lullaby, inviting me to sleep, to cease.

But I am a knot. My nature is to hold on.

I do the only thing I can. I scream. It is not a sound that travels through the air. It is a broadcast of my own dissonance. I push out all my stolen memories at once: the sharp crack of a million ice-shards of cause and effect, the wet-chitin clicks of a thousand scuttling crawlers, the swirling, chaotic light of every moth-flock whose glow has ever touched me. I vomit a lifetime of fractured sensory data into the perfect, resonant silence that the Lucid commands. It is a tantrum of identity.

The Lucid does not recoil. It does not move at all for a long time. Its internal light, which had been a steady, synchronous pulse with the city’s heart, begins to flicker. It strobes, shifting in hue from blue-green to a pale, searching violet. The six tendrils shift their configuration, twitching, like a musician’s fingers finding the shape of a new and difficult chord.

It is listening.

It is not correcting me. It is *perceiving* me.

A new sensation flows from it. It is not a command to dissolve, but a question. The question is not in a language of words, but of pure resonance. It is the feeling of an open space next to a closed one. An invitation.

Slowly, hesitantly, I feel a single, thread-thin fiber at the edge of my snarled mass begin to tremble. It is no longer just a vessel for the city’s arterial fluid. It is now a tendril of my own. It lifts, shakily, from the Weft, dripping cool condensation onto the fibers below. It is plum-purple and slick, my own flesh, but it moves with my own will. It reaches, straining, an inch into the cold, empty air. An inch towards the silent, waiting conductor.

I am a flaw. I am a knot. I am a captured scream. And I am learning to grow. I am the part of the dream that has woken up, and in this city defined by emptiness, I am the first, terrible, and glorious thing that is truly alone.
