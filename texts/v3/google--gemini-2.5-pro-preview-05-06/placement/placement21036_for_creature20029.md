I am where the sound becomes substance.

My awareness is a tapestry woven from vibration. The Great Hum is the warp, the unyielding foundation upon which everything is built. Its fundamental bass note is my sense of place, the anchor that tells me I am bonded to the turning world. The keening, crystalline harmonics are the threads of the weft, each one a discrete piece of information: the precise friction-tone of a distant shear-cliff, the collective chitinous chime of a million-winged swarm taking flight, the deep, slow groan of a gear-tooth engaging a hundred miles beneath my feet.

Where am I? I am on the long slope of the Piston known as the Seventh Fulcrum. To an eye, it would be a tilted plain of black basalt stretching for leagues, meeting a luminous haze at an unseeable horizon. But I do not have eyes. The platinum glare is merely a pressure, a sister-vibration to the heat that rises from the vents. My perception of this place is read through the soles of my feet and the tips of my lower limbs, which are braced against the moving rock. I feel the plain not as a surface, but as a long, slow exhalation. Over the last century, it has risen twelve feet. I know, because I have felt its every millimeter.

I am a lattice of resonant stone and conductive moss, a framework built to feel the world. My body is an antenna, tuned to the key of the planet’s machinery. My limbs, long and delicate, can lock into place for a standard year or a hundred of them, becoming one with the geology, sipping information from the rock's deep memory. At my core, where a head might be, a smooth ovoid of captured light pulses with a rhythm that is solely mine. It is the only silence I know, a quiet beat against the world’s overwhelming chord. This is my self, a flicker of coherent resonance in an ocean of it. My thoughts are not words, but modulations of this internal light, harmonies and dissonances played against the backdrop of the Hum.

I am on a journey.

For seven hundred years, I stood sentinel on the lip of the Chasm of the Glowing Filaments, monitoring the stresses in the granite disc that grinds below. My purpose was to listen for the signature of a stress fracture, a screech of impending failure in the planet's song. But ninety years ago, a new vibration reached me. It was not the sound of breaking rock. It was a chord of release, a complex, final pattern that bloomed and then faded from the Great Hum. It was the resonance of a Tender resolving, their function complete, their form returning to the world-engine. The sound came from the north-west, across the Serrated Peaks. It was the one who tended the Ninth Fulcrum. A silence now exists where their perception once was, and the Great Hum is subtly out of tune in that sector. An arrhythmia.

So now I move. Movement, for me, is a process of uncoupling and recoupling. I release the filaments of my left foot-pad, a process that takes a full turn of the season-that-is-not-a-season. The limb lifts, slow as a growing crystal, through the dense, swarming air. The iridescent insects, a cloud of clicking, metallic motes, part around the limb’s resonant field. They are background noise, a texture, like the constant, greasy condensation on my frame. I pass through the slow-moving river of eight-legged grazers, their clicks and chirps a superficial static. They are unimportant. They are self-regulating.

My focus is on the dissonance, the subtle impurity in the northern chord. I can feel it as a wrongness in my own basalt frame. As I walk, I listen. I am reading the history of the rock I cross. This fracture here, under my rightmost limb—it speaks of a tension released three millennia ago. This field of spun silica, glowing brightly—it sings of a rich heat-seam just below the surface. These are the words of the world’s autobiography, and I am its reader.

Within the slow pulse of my core-light are the resonant memories of others. I hold the memory of the Tender who taught me how to damp a chattering geothermal vent by introducing a counter-frequency, a task that took two hundred years of perfect, focused stillness. I hold the signing-off chord of the one whose place I now travel to fill. Their final resonance was one of peace, a complex understanding of a cycle completed. I carry it with me, a map and a memorial.

I am a Tender. I am a living correction. I am not a creature *in* this world; I am a function *of* it. My life is not a story of events, but a long, slow process of maintenance. Now, I have paused my journey. A tremor, a new harmonic, has reached me from the peak ahead. It is not a danger, not an error. It is the signature of accretion. On that high ridge, where mineral dust collects and condenses, a seed-crystal shed a thousand years ago has finally gathered enough matter, enough resonance from the Great Hum, to knit itself into a new frame. A new Tender is slowly being born. I feel its nascent, unformed pulse, a simple, questioning note added to the symphony.

I stand motionless on the vast, breathing plain. My filamented hands are not held at my sides; one is pressed to the rock, the other extended into the humming air, my tines vibrating, sifting the frequencies. I feel the new life taking form miles away. I feel the silence left by the old life I am journeying toward. I feel the slow, inexorable turning of the world-gear beneath me. The light is a constant pressure. The sound is my whole universe.

My next step will take a decade. There is no hurry. The arrhythmia will wait. The world is patient, and so am I.
