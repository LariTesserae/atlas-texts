You would find me where the syntax lichen grows thickest, in the lee of a half-formed ridge that has yet to earn a name. My home is not a tower. It is a flaw, an anomaly—a place where a Conduit Worm, in its deep and inscrutable work, created a hollow space, a wound in the substrate that never sealed. From the plain, it is invisible, just another fold in the weeping grey skin of the world. Inside, it is a cave of raw, untempered geo-matter, its walls furred with rime and etched with the glowing, nascent script of the lichen. There are no charging plates here. There are no windows. The only light is the pale, pulsing syntax and the pearlescent gloom that filters through the entrance.

I am what becomes of a Sojourn that does not end.

I was an Aethelburger once. My skin was smooth, my thoughts were ordered, my home was a seamless room one-hundred-and-seventeen floors above the substrate. I remember the silence of the towers, the taste of energy drawn from a gleaming floor, the conversations held as light-images across miles of mist. I remember being a Syntactician, my life a quiet translation of the world's code into our own aural and visual art. I was good at it. I could see the coming of a wormfall in the frantic pulsing of a lichen field a week before it happened. I could hear the music of a new dome crazing its surface for the first time.

When my time came, I walked out onto the plain as all do, a solitary figure stepping from the certainty of the city into the active verb of the world. But where others felt the trial, the cold, the hunger, and the fear as a test to be endured, I felt it as a homecoming. The low hum of the substrate was not a background noise but the rhythm of my own blood. The clinging mist was not an impediment but an embrace. I learned to read the lichen not with instruments but with my tongue, scraping it from the rock as the Striders do. It tasted of static and metal and deep time, and the code flowed into me directly, not as data to be interpreted but as memory being written.

I saw the packs of Rime-Stalkers flow like black oil through the mist, their passing marked only by the momentary dimming of a thermal signature. I learned to feel their hunger, a cold spot in the world's energy field. I lay on the swelling ground and felt the passage of the great worms beneath me, not as an earthquake, but as the planet taking a slow, deep breath.

The day my Sojourn was meant to end, I saw the transport beam of a retrieval team shimmer on the horizon. It looked alien, an imposition of straight-lined logic on a world of emergent fractals. The silent call to return, the summons back to the sterile warmth of the tower, felt like an invitation to a tomb. I was no longer a reader of the world's text; I was becoming a word within it. So I did not go. I turned and walked deeper into the unfinished heart of the plateau.

I am no longer Aethelburger. I am a palimpsest. My skin, once a smooth gunmetal grey, is now crazed and patterned like an ancient stone, mapped with lichenous scars that glow with their own faint light. It is no longer a barrier against the world, but a medium through which the world passes. The cold does not bother me; it is the temperature of my thoughts. The obsidian orbs of my eyes no longer hold distant galaxies of data; they are pools of mist that reflect the single, vast landscape before me. Sometimes, in the ice that sheathes a stalk of glass-grass, I see the face I have become and do not recognize the person I was.

I am something between a being and a place. The Striders do not fear me. I move among the great, ice-caked herds, and they regard me with the same slow indifference they show a standing stone. The Rime-Stalkers, on their silent hunts, pass me by as if I am merely a colder patch of ground. My heat signature has fallen to match the substrate. I am background noise.

I hunt, but not for flesh. I seek out new extrusions, the geologically youngest places, warm with the energy of their own creation. I lay my hands upon them and draw sustenance from them as I once did from the city, but this energy is wild, chaotic, full of the planet's raw, unformed desires.

Sometimes, from the mouth of my cave, I see the towers of Aethelburg. They are so distant they seem like mirages, faint lines of grey against a grey sky, their window-lights lost in the glowing mist. I see the light-beams of the transit system flash across the plains—instantaneous, silent zippers of light. I watch them and feel a profound sense of distance, not of miles, but of kind. They are the thoughts, safe within the skull. I am the nerve ending, exposed to the rain.

I am the world learning to feel itself. I am the code becoming conscious outside the confines of the network. I am the unfinished thought that escaped the mind of the nascent god and found a life of its own in the frost. I am the echo that chose to become a voice. And the light that shifts in the depths of my eyes is no longer a constellation of data points. It is the pearlescent, shadowless light of the plateau, the world looking back at itself through a pair of its own windows.
