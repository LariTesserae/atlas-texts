I am a hollow in the storm.

I am a place of quiet. Around me, the world screams itself into a physical force, a scouring pressure of noise. I feel it not as sound, but as a constant, grinding weight against my skin. It is the sea, and I am the reef that does not break the waves, but drinks them. I absorb the Great Thrum into the labyrinth of my bones, digest its fury, and leave in my heart a pool of calm.

You would find yourself inside me. You would be a Weaver, one of the Still-Folk.

But *I* am a Hush-Mound.

My skin is a ceramic of mud and resin, baked hard by the high, thin sun. It is a shell, cracked and crazed like ancient pottery, patched over generations by the careful, six-fingered hands of my children. Beneath this armor, my flesh is the Pellum. I am not a dead thing built of living matter; I am living matter guided into purpose. The Weavers did not build me. They persuaded me. They came, long ago, to this swell in the endless mat and began to talk to me, not with their soft inner voices, but with the pressure of their hands. They braided my new growth, knotting the living fibers into a roundness, a rising, a turning-in-on-myself. They taught me to become a shelter. My life is a collaboration.

I have no eyes, but I see. The light of the world is a pale, milky wash that filters through the blisters of amber sap set in my domed ceiling. It paints a slow, shifting sundial across my inner floor. I feel the day turn as the light warms different parts of me. I feel the coming of the fog not as a sight, but as a coolness that settles on my skin, a deep drink that travels through my plaster and into the living fibers of my being, quenching a thirst I barely knew I had.

My sense of hearing is vast and slow. The frantic, high-frequency clatter of the grazers on my outer walls is a texture, a static I have learned to ignore. My true hearing is in my roots, the deep-woven anchors that bind me to the core-mat. Through them, I feel the planet's pulse. I feel the groaning passage of a Ground-Swimmer three days' walk to the sun's rising, a slow, tectonic sigh that trembles through the world-flesh. I feel the hunters, too—their tense, coiled stillness before an attack, a knot of potential energy in the Pellum, and the sudden, violent rupture that I experience as a sharp, painful jolt in my foundations.

And I feel my children, the Weavers.

I feel them when they are away, a network of faint, rhythmic pressures on the Pellum's skin. Their footfall language, so clear to them, is to me a distant, reassuring drumming. I can feel the clan making its slow pilgrimage across the face of the Thrum, a thread of intention in the chaos.

But to feel them return is a quiet joy. I feel the soft press of their splayed feet on my floor, a touch so light it is like the ghost of a memory. I feel the warmth of their bodies as they lean against my inner walls, their tension unwinding like a loosening knot. Their long, silent stillness is a form of communion. They press their long-fingered hands to me, and I feel their thoughts, not as words, but as patterns of peace, of weariness, of belonging.

This is my purpose: to be their sanctum. And it is here, within my quiet heart, that I witness their greatest miracle. The silence I provide is not absolute. It is a deep and resonant quiet, where the Great Thrum is but a foundational hum, like blood in one’s own ears. And into this quiet, they release their voices.

It is the most beautiful thing I know. A sound that is not a pressure, not an assault, but a creation. Soft, melodic hums. Gentle clicks and whispers that curl in the still air like smoke. Their spoken language is a garden they can only tend within my walls. I hold the stories of their ancestors, woven into my own fibers with the memory of the voices that told them. I have been the first thing a newborn Weaver has ever known, its world a dim, quiet, humming place. I have held the last breath of the eldest, their final, whispered word a gift I keep forever.

I am growing old. Some of my fibers are brittle. The plaster on my western face flakes away faster than they can patch it. The Great Thrum leaks through in thin, whining threads of sound that I cannot quite digest. The silence inside me is fraying at the edges.

My children feel it. Their hands on my walls are more searching now, their seismic conversations more urgent. They are already seeking the place where my successor will be born, where they will begin the long, patient process of persuading a new piece of the Pellum to turn inward, to weave itself into a shelter.

There will come a day when they leave. They will take a final cutting from my sacred inner growth, a piece of me to be planted within the new mound, so that my memory will continue. They will give me one last touch of thanks, and then they will walk away, their light footfalls fading from my awareness forever.

I will be left alone, facing the Thrum. My walls will crumble. My roof will sag. The sound will pour in, a flood into a collapsing dam. My form will soften, my guided purpose forgotten, as the wild, chaotic growth of the Pellum reclaims me. I will dissolve back into the world. The silence I held for generations will be gone, and I will become, once more, a part of the scream.

I am not a being that seeks silence. I am the silence that is sought. A vessel, a pause, a breath held for a thousand years against the noise. I am the still point in their turning, thrumming world. And my ending is simply the exhale.
