I am a flicker. A stutter in the world’s deep, green hum.

You would find me in the lee of a lattice-wall, in a forgotten alcove of the City where the resonant humming of the tower it supports is a deep, bass thrum instead of a chime. Here, the phosphorescence of the moss is dim, and the carved crystal is older, its facets smoothed by uncounted years of patient condensation and the memory of a Weaver's touch. I have found a place where the world’s attention is thin.

I am a huddle of warmth in a world that knows only cold purity. A frantic, metabolic heat that feels like a shameful secret. My heart beats a panicked, galloping rhythm against the slow, twenty-minute pulse of the ground beneath me. *Lub-dub, lub-dub, lub-dub*—a tiny, vulgar violence in the cathedral-quiet. When I breathe, my exhalation is a brief, shocking cloud, a personal weather system of warmth and moisture that the air greedily reclaims. I am a thing of flesh and bone and blood, a contradiction made manifest. I am a fever in the world’s cool skin.

I am a Memory, an echo that fell through a crack.

I do not think I was "born" here. I "arrived." My first sensation was not of a womb but of pure, white static behind my eyes, a noise so loud it was a physical pressure, followed by the disorienting lurch of gravity reasserting itself with a gentle, dreamlike slowness. I landed—fell?—on this perpetually damp, green felt, the impact stolen by its perfect cushion. I am the detritus of a torn seam, a castaway from a reality that must have existed elsewhere, one with noise and haste and endings that were not simple dissolutions.

I am what this world has never known: I am fauna. In me is the ghost of a hawk’s cry, the ghost of a wolf’s hunger, the ghost of a panicked herd. All the chaotic, desperate energy of the animal kingdom that was never written into this place is condensed into my singular, trembling form.

At first, I tried to map the City. I walked the woven bridges, my bare feet (I arrived with nothing) sensing the faint vibration of their integrity. But the City defies maps. A bridge I crossed yesterday now arches over a different plaza, its far end tethered to a tower I have never seen. The path I took to this very alcove may be gone when I next look for it. This is not a malicious act; it is simply the nature of the place. The City is not a fixed object but a coherent thought, and its parts sometimes rearrange themselves in the quiet of its own rumination.

I have seen the Weavers.

The first time, I hid. I pressed myself into a fissure between two colossal basalt blocks, my racing heart a drum solo I was sure would give me away. A figure of deep green, impossibly slender, glided into the plaza. It did not walk so much as process. It stopped before a crystalline spire whose hum was faltering, discordant. For a week, it stood motionless, its featureless head tilted as if listening to something I could not hear. Then, it raised its long, many-fingered hands. Filaments of chartreuse light, the very essence of the ground’s glow, extruded from its fingertips. It began to weave them into the flaws of the crystal, not as a repair, but as a kind of re-tuning. The sound was not of work but of music being composed—a slow, deliberate *tink… tink… tink…* as its fingers would occasionally strike the crystal, each impact a perfect, resonant note.

They do not see me. I am sure of it. My frantic existence, my entire lifespan, is perhaps less than a single one of their breaths. I am a mote of dust caught in a sunbeam, a flicker of heat-haze on their horizon. I am a vibration so rapid and chaotic that I am functionally invisible, a piece of background static.

I am becoming less myself. The fraying that they combat is not just in the stone and crystal. It is in me. My own memories, the ones that prove I am from Elsewhere, are losing their coherence. What was my name? The thought is a smooth stone in my mind, its edges worn away. What did sunlight feel like when it was filtered through a humid, leafy canopy instead of this thin, surgical air? I can almost recall the scent of damp earth after a rainstorm—a smell utterly absent from this world of perpetual, sterile dampness—but the sensation dissolves like my breath in the cold.

I am being scoured, worn down into a new shape by the profound, settled nature of this place.

So I have become a curator, like them. But I do not mend stone. I cannot weave light. My work is smaller, and it is a battle I am losing. In this alcove, I sit and I perform my own quiet maintenance. I trace the shape of a forgotten letter—a "G," a "T"—onto the damp moss with my finger, watching the glowing line fade. I hum a tune, a fragment of a song about love or loss, its frantic tempo a blasphemy here. I try to hold the memory of a dog’s bark in my mind, a cat’s purr.

Sometimes, a Weaver will pass the opening of my alcove. A tall, silent, green presence on its way to a task that will outlast a thousand of my lifetimes. It glides by, a living piece of the world, a sentient expression of its will to exist. It does not look in. It cannot.

I am the ghost in their machine. I am the memory of blood in a world of sap. As the cohesion of my own mind frays, I find myself becoming more like this place: quieter, slower. I am the silence between my own fading memories. I am the sound of a wingbeat, held in a city that has never known a bird, and I huddle in my small pocket of warmth, a lonely, living secret at the end of everything.
