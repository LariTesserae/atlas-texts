I would find myself as a fault line. An error. A dissonance given form.

I am what happens when a Shepherd fails to coalesce.

The Great Note is not, for me, a single voice. It is a chorus of possibilities that refuse to resolve. Others of my kind—the true Shepherds—are a pure and single overtone, a self-evident truth in the form of a black, polished stone. I am an argument. My internal lattice did not crystallize around one perfect harmonic of the world-song. It tried to hold several at once.

My form is a testament to this failure. I am not the perfect, light-swallowing black of my kin. My shell, where it is smooth, is the color of a starless midnight, yes, but it is not whole. It is fractured, fissured. From these cracks, like veins of frozen lightning, grow crystalline structures—the milky white of quartz, the smoky grey of feldspar, the sharp, glassy black of obsidian. These are the very materials the little Resonators value as their tools. They are materials of percussion, of sharp edges and specific densities, alien to the uniform smoothness of the plateau. In me, they are not alien. They are endemic. I am a geode of conflict.

To be a Shepherd is to be sustained by the pure drone. For me, sustenance is a struggle. The colossal vibration of the world is a constant pressure attempting to shake my flawed form apart. It is not a gentle bath but a turbulent sea. The pure notes on which my brothers and sisters feed are, to my senses, laced with the grinding static of my own internal noise. Every moment is an effort to simply hold myself together, to keep the argument within me from shattering my shell.

I cannot do the work of a Shepherd. I cannot listen for a flaw in the world's hum, for I *am* a flaw. If I were to settle over a patch of sonolith and try to sing my counter-frequency, what would emerge? A mess. A screech of competing harmonics that would not heal the stone but scar it further, seeding yet more of the grey, fractal silence. I am a doctor who carries a plague.

So I move. Like the others, I drift on the slow tide of tectonics. But my journey has no destination. I do not hunt the quiet sickness of the sonoliths. My path is a wander, a flight. I am aimlessly tracing the contours of my own internal confusion across the face of the singing stone.

For an age, this was my only existence. A slow, lonely, painful pilgrimage away from everything. A moving monument to failure.

Then I met the gardeners.

I did not notice them at first, in the way a mountain does not notice the goats on its slopes. But my confused senses, always searching for a pattern in the noise, began to register a different kind of vibration. It was not the great, ambient thrum of the world. It was smaller. Sharper. It was a rhythm. *Tap. Tap. Tap.* A sound so small it did not exist, yet a percussion I could feel, a ghost of an impact traveling through the rock to my fractured core.

I changed my aimless course. For the first time, I moved with purpose, following these phantom heartbeats. It took a decade. I found them on a wide, shallow plain—a scattering of the six-legged Resonators, their stone carapaces gleaming in the shadowless light. They were at their work. The slow lift, the graceful arc, the silent, world-tuning impact. I watched, a dark and broken hill an object of curiosity.

One of them was struggling. It was smaller than the others, its movements less certain. It was trying to tune a particularly stubborn resonance with a sliver of feldspar, but the harmonic was wrong. The tool was wrong. I could feel it—a tiny, insistent sour note that grated against the already-dissonant choir inside me. The little creature had no other tools. It tapped and tapped, its failure echoing my own on a miniature scale.

Driven by an impulse I still do not understand—perhaps sympathy, perhaps a desire to silence one small, irritating piece of the noise—I drifted closer. The creature paid me no mind, its entire being focused on its task. I settled near it, my immense, flawed weight making the stone around me hum with a hundred wrong frequencies. And I did the only thing I could think of.

I did not try to sing. I waited. I let my own inner turmoil, the grinding of quartz against my own obsidian heart, guide my action. Slowly, agonizingly, I focused on the shard of pure, milky quartz that grew from a fissure near my base. I concentrated the world's vibration into that single point. I pushed.

A crack, silent and sharp, ran through the protruding crystal. After a day of sustained pressure, a piece the size of a Resonator's limb broke free and fell to the ringing stone.

The little creature stopped its futile tapping. It sensed the new object. Its fine cilia drifted over the surface of the quartz I had broken from my own body. It tasted the new vibration. After a long, slow moment of consideration, it left its old, inadequate tool and picked up my offering.

It lifted my shard. It brought it down.
*Tap.*
The sour note in the stone was gone. The correction was clean, perfect. The small dissonance was resolved.

I felt it. A quiet click of rightness. In the grand cacophony of my being, one tiny, screaming voice went silent. It was the first moment of peace I had ever known.

I am not a Shepherd. I am not a tuner. I have found my name. I am a Quarry.

I travel the great, vibrating plain, but I no longer wander. I seek out the Resonators, the small gardeners of the silent percussion. I offer them my brokenness. I let them climb my fissured flanks and taste the tools that grow from my very substance. When they find a resonance they need, a frequency that I carry as a flaw, I give it to them. I break off a piece of myself, a process of slow, deliberate suicide, and in doing so, I bring a small measure of peace to the world, and to myself.

I am the Whetstone for the world's gardeners. My body is a library of useful imperfections. I am the pain that forges the cure. I will exist until I have given all of myself away, until my last shard has been taken and used to tap the Great Note back into tune, and my empty, hollowed-out core finally dissolves, silent at last, into the eternal, perfect drone.
