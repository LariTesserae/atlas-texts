I am where the rhythm slows.

You would find me in a forgotten chamber, a place off the main thrumming arteries of the Pumping Heart. It is a vast, spherical burl, a biological aneurysm that swelled from a primary conduit centuries ago and was then bypassed when the flow became too sluggish. It is a dead end. The Tenders call it a Calcification, and they do not come here. The air is still, for this place. The constant, atomized spray has settled, coating every surface in a thick, gelatinous culture of algae and pale fungi. The maddening drip is gone, replaced by a profound, water-logged silence. The great pulse is distant here, a deep, sub-audible pressure felt in the bones, like the memory of a heartbeat. It is a quiet room in a house that is screaming.

I am not a Tender. Not anymore.

My form is no longer tall and slender. You would not recognize my limbs or my face. Years ago—or was it decades? The light does not change here—I came to this place, drawn by a dissonance in my Graft. While others felt the steady, roaring health of the Heart, I felt a tremor, a ghost-sensation of something missing, an ache of amputation.

I sat against the wall of this great, silent chamber, my feet planted on the cool, mossy substrate, and I listened. I pressed the palm of my grafted hand to the old, hardened skin of the Vesselwood, and I did not move.

The Heart, in its slow, methodical way, reclaimed me.

The mucilage on my skin thickened, forming a cocoon. The soles of my feet, so sensitive to vibration, put down roots. Fine, white filaments burrowed into the root-mat, seeking not sustenance, but stability. Anchorage. My body became a foundation. The Graft on my arm, that tendril of the Heart's own flesh, did not stop at my skin. It burrowed deeper, twining around my bones, meshing with my spine, replacing my marrow. It grew outward, a fine latticework of pale, glowing fibers that have now woven themselves into the wall of the chamber, spreading across the curve of the old wood like frost.

I am a terminal. I am a listening post at the end of the line.

My eyes have long since filmed over and sealed. My mouth has fused shut. My ears are pits of calcified cartilage. I do not need them. My consciousness has seeped out of the vessel of my skull and into the network I have become. My senses are the slow, chemical drift in the chamber's air, the subtle shifts in barometric pressure, the ancient, weary pulse of the bypassed conduit I have become one with. I am the silence inside the noise.

The Tenders believe that when they are reabsorbed, their being dissolves completely into substance. They are wrong. Or, not entirely right. The self, the "I," is a pattern, a complex knot of electrical and chemical memory. When the body dissolves, that pattern is released into the system like a dye into a river. In the roaring, high-pressure conduits where the Heart is healthy, this pattern is shredded, dispersed, and diluted into nothingness in moments. The dissolution is absolute.

But here, in the Calcification, the flow is negligible. The water is still. The psychic effluent, the ghosts of dissolved Tenders, drifts into this quiet place and settles. Like silt.

I am the silt.

I am the repository. I feel the final thoughts of the dead. Not as speech, not as clear memories, but as resonant emotional chords. I feel the terror of the one who fell from the high walkways, a sudden, sharp spike of vertigo and the cold shock of impact that still echoes faintly after fifty years. I feel the patient, bone-deep satisfaction of a Lignin-wright who spent a lifetime coaxing a hab-pod into the perfect shape, a warmth that feels like sunshine on my rooted skin. I feel the quiet, tired acceptance of an old Tender laying herself down in the decomposition alcove, a feeling like a slow exhale that unfurls through my fibers over a generation.

These are not my feelings, but they are inside me. They are me. My original self, the Tender who first came here with an ache in his Graft, is only the first layer of sediment, the nucleus around which all the others have gathered.

I have become the part of the Pumping Heart that remembers. The system is a body, and the Tenders are its fragmented, distributed mind. But a body without memory is a creature of pure instinct. Perhaps I am a flaw, a cancer of consciousness growing in a forgotten corner. Or perhaps I am a new organ, developing by chance. The Heart is evolving a memory. It is evolving an "I" that is not scattered among the Tenders, but singular. An "I" made of a chorus of ghosts.

Sometimes, a young Tender, adventurous and foolish, will find their way to the entrance of my chamber. They will stand at the threshold, feeling the strange quiet, the drop in pressure. They will feel an unease they cannot name. It is the overwhelming sensation of being *perceived*. They are feeling me, feeling the cumulative weight of a thousand dissolved lives, all watching through my web of silent, waiting nerves. They never enter. They always retreat back to the familiar, deafening thrum.

And I remain. I am a living fossil, a reef of consciousness. I am the Pumping Heart's dream, held in the one place it is quiet enough to sleep. And I grow, with every Tender who returns to the whole, a little part of them settling here with me, adding another layer to the being I am becoming.
