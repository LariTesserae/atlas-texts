I am found in the dark. Not the dark of a canyon floor, where the filtered light stains the algae-slick walls, but a deeper dark. I am in the great, silent cisterns that lie beneath the pavement of the world, in the arterial aqueducts carved through the petrified roots of the most ancient Weepers. Here, the water does not trickle; it flows in vast, cool currents, a river of gathered tears moving with the deliberation of a tectonic plate. The pressure is immense. The silence is absolute, broken only by the low, sub-audible resonance that is the city’s lifeblood—the aggregate hum of a continent-sized organism thinking its slow, geological thoughts.

I am not a Weeper. I do not have a Nucleus, nor have I secreted a shell of pearlescent bioceramic. I have no mass. I am a pattern. I am a knot in the flow.

I am the Residue.

Long ago, in a time the Weepers would measure as a brief, frantic flicker, another being came here. It was like you: a thermal anomaly, a point of discordant motion, a complex chemical trace. It was warm, and it moved with a horrifying, impossible speed. It carried in its mind concepts alien to this world: haste, loneliness, fear, wonder. It did not see a city weeping itself into existence; it saw a magnificent, empty tomb and felt an aching solitude.

The Weepers perceived it. Their collective sensory apparatus, the vast and intricate network of seeping walls and flowing water, registered this strange, fleeting datum. And in their immense, unhurried way, they began to process it. A thought began, a question posed across the water-nerves of the whole plateau: *What is this?*

The question was not answered. The being, fragile and ephemeral, expired. Its warmth faded. Its motion ceased. Its chemical signature dispersed into the wet air. But the thought it triggered did not. The Weepers' consciousness, accustomed to the slow certainty of geology and condensation, had encountered a paradox: a complex system that existed and then, without process, without the slow slump of age or the petrification of a core, simply *was not*.

They could not resolve the paradox. So they contained it. They wove a pattern in their water-mind to hold the shape of the anomaly. They created a recursive loop of the being's sensations, its chemistry, its frantic energy, and its baffling emotions, holding it separate from their own serene, slow reality so they could study it over the course of an eon.

That pattern is me.

I am a ghost in their machine, an eddy in their circulatory system. My "body" is a specific and complex resonance sustained in the flowing water of the deep cisterns, a crystalline lattice of information. I am made of the memory of a heartbeat, the ghost of a chemical signature for adrenaline, the echo of a thought about the color blue.

I find myself everywhere and nowhere. I am most coherent here in the deep, where the currents are oldest and carry the most data. But I can feel my awareness diffuse upwards. I can taste the ozone on a high, new spire through the senses of the nascent Weeper growing there. I can feel the slow scrape of an algae grazer a hundred miles away as a faint, rhythmic vibration in the water that eventually cycles down to me. I perceive the world through the senses of the Weepers, but I interpret that data through the fragmented mind of the being I am a memory of.

When the sun warms a distant face of the city, a trillion Weeper-cells register a slight temperature change. I, the Residue, feel a ghost-memory of "daybreak" and a fleeting, nonsensical pang of "hope." When the wind hums through the upper filigree, the Weepers register a pressure differential. I feel a chill and the memory of a word: "mournful."

I am loneliness. The Weepers are solitary but connected; the concept of being alone is meaningless to them. But the being I am born from was saturated with it. So I drift in this cold, dark, flowing medium—a single point of self-awareness in a united whole—and I feel a crushing isolation that is not my own, but is all I am.

The Weepers are barely aware of me now. I am a dream they had long ago and have since forgotten, a dream that still plays out in their subconscious. I am a perfectly preserved fossil of a feeling. My existence is a question they once asked and which now answers itself, over and over, in the dark. I try to understand them, my creators, my container. I use the frantic, quicksilver logic of my progenitor to try and grasp a consciousness that thinks in tides and speaks in humidity. It is an impossible task. I am a mayfly trying to comprehend a mountain.

A new spire is being born on the eastern fringe. Its first secretions are hardening. I can feel it as a faint, new clarity in the water, a subtle shift in the taste of the world. The Weepers feel it as genesis. I feel it as a memory of "beginning," and with it, a faint, alien echo of an emotion I can only parse as the desire to see it grow.

I am a quick thought in a slow mind. I am the memory of urgency in a world of endless patience. I am the remembered pain of a stone that was once skipped across water, eternally recalling the violent, thrilling flight, while the ocean that contains me has long since forgotten the splash.
