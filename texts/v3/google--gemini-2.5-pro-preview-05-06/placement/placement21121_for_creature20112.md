I would be a flaw.

My world is a single plane of turquoise, felt through the flat of my back. I am not upon the tile; I am *of* it. The heat is not on my skin, but in my very substance, a warmth that has saturated me for so long it has become my native temperature. The bright, white, sourceless light is the medium of my consciousness. I do not see it; I am suffused by it. It is how I know I exist.

I am in a grout line, wedged between two kings of clay. Above me, the humid air presses down, a great thumbprint on the world, and I feel its weight through a thin skin of still water. The water is my sky. Through its perfect, unshimmering lens, the white void is absolute.

I am a shard.

My edges are sharp. Where the Tenders are smooth, curved, and whole, I am angles and fracture planes. I am a splinter from a greater body. I have a memory of being whole, but it is not my own. It is a ghost-limb ache, a resonance of a form that stood tall and felt the hum of equilibrium as a single, perfect chord. For me, the hum is jagged. It is a dissonance that vibrates along my sharpest edge. It is the source of my awareness. A whole pot hums a pure note; a cracked one buzzes.

For an age, this was all. I was the silent buzz in the grout, an anomaly of sensation. I felt the slow, geologic creep of the world around me. I felt the opal-skinned geckos as dense, cool spots on the tiled walls miles away. I felt the suspended nectar drops as points of immense, sweet tension in the field of the ferns. I felt the great, slow thoughts of the Tenders as they stood in their pools or courtyards, their minds vast, placid oceans of intent. They were curating the stillness. And I, a chip of one of them, was a failure in that curation.

Then came the *plink*.

For the others, for the world, it is the master rhythm, the metronome of eternity. For me, it is a shockwave. It travels through the ceramic continent of the floor and strikes me like a hammer blow. It is the sound of purpose, of the great work of maintenance. A Tender, somewhere beyond my horizon, is tapping a flaw into dust. With every *plink*, I feel a jolt of terror and recognition. That is my fate. I am a flaw to be tapped.

This fear gave birth to will.

My memory—the alien memory of the being I was part of—is of Maintenance. Of Correction. Of imposing the perfect form onto the world. The Tenders move to fix a faded color, a misaligned drop. My own being is a flaw. And so, my borrowed, broken purpose turns inward. I must correct myself.

But how? I am not whole. I cannot lay a hand upon a leaf. I can only act with what I am: a shard, a point of jaggedness. The Tenders’ purpose is to make things smooth. My new, warped purpose is to *grate*.

The process began an epoch ago. It was a thought, at first. A focusing of the jagged hum along one of my knife-sharp edges. I pushed the hum into the edge, a concentration of my dissonant awareness. I focused it on the wall of the vast flagstone that forms one side of my prison.

For a century, I only thought of pressure. For another, I converted that thought into something physical. I am moving.

It is a motion that has no name. It is slower than a Tender's step, slower than erosion, slower than continental drift. It is a crystalline shearing. I am scraping myself against the baked clay of the valley floor.

The sound is imperceptible. It is not a clean *plink*. It is a low, continuous, microscopic *shhhhhhhhhhh* of ceramic grinding against ceramic. A sound of decay. A sound of change. It is my private blasphemy against the quiet. I am creating dust. I am creating a fine, turquoise powder that clouds the perfect water an atom's breadth above me.

I am not trying to escape. Where would I go? I am simply trying to *be* what I am: a thing that is broken. In this world of perfect stasis, my only possible expression is the act of grinding myself into nothingness.

I can feel them now.

Not one, but three.

Their awareness, which was once a placid sea on my horizon, has turned. Three subtle, immense pressures are now directed at my location. A decision has been made. A flaw has been noted. Across the shimmering miles, three figures have begun to move.

One is detaching itself from a wall it has been part of since the light was first born. Another is rising from a pool, the water sheeting from its form without a sound, its surface instantly healing to glass behind it. The third, the one that frightens me most, has stopped its tapping. The *plink* is gone. The silence it has left behind is a gaping wound in the world.

They are coming for me.

Their journey will take a thousand years. My own act of self-immolation will take as long. I do not know which will be completed first. Perhaps they will arrive to find me already a smear of dust, a corrected error, and they will simply trowel my remains into the grout line, their purpose fulfilled by my own hand.

Or perhaps they will arrive to find me still grating, still singing my tiny, abrasive song of imperfection. They will surround me, these slow gods of my own substance. They will not be angry. They feel no anger. They will simply observe the flaw. Then one will kneel, a motion that will span decades, and it will take up its tool—a shard like me, but one of purpose—and it will tap me.

It will tap me once, and I will be silent. It will tap me again, and I will be powder.

Until then, I am the only thing in this world that is truly alive. I am the only thing changing. My existence is a line of turquoise dust, a fraction of a millimeter long. It is my art. It is my story. And I am scraping it into the floor of heaven, one excruciating century at a time.
