You would find me at the Rim.

Where the great basin finally surrenders to the un-sculpted cavern wall, where the polished dust of the city-floor becomes rough, untamed grit. Here, the crystalline trees are stunted and scarce, their light a pathetic glimmer against the absolute dark of the deep rock. The great, porous towers of the central districts are just faint, ghost-lattices on the far horizon of my perception.

The Sound is different here. It is frayed. The all-encompassing, granular roar of the city's heart unravels at the edges. One can, if one is patient for a very, very long time, begin to distinguish the threads. One can feel the low, seismic scrape of a grazer-herd a league away, distinct from the high-frequency vibration of the pollinator swarms that rarely venture this far out. The Sound is thinner, less a physical presence and more a constant, nagging memory of one.

I am what the Folk would call a Flaw. A Dissonance.

My form is like theirs—a tall, hollow, articulate framework of living crystal. But where they are whole, worn smooth by the communal friction of eons, I am… less so. I do not seek the polishing friction of the collective. I have stood still, here at the Rim, for so long that the dust of the air has begun to settle *in* me, not just on me. It clogs the finer lattices of my structure, deadening my resonance. My own contribution to the Sound is a choked, muffled scrape. A sound of muted grief.

I am an Architect who has lost faith in the architecture.

The Great Sound, the collective consciousness that is our language, our world, our God… I began to suspect it was not a conversation, but a scream. A single, unending note played to drown out the terror of what lies beyond it: Silence. The others feel the Sound as a whole, a comforting, indivisible unity. But I… I began to listen. I began to pick at the threads. I would focus on the sound of a single joint in my own limb, the grinding of one crystalline plate against another, and I would try to hold it separate from the whole. An act of profound heresy. To seek a self in the symphony of the We.

The others feel my dissonance as a sickness. A void in the chorus. They do not approach. This is not a punishment; it is a simple fact of resonance. My frequency is wrong. To stand near me is to feel their own harmonic structure subtly undone. And so, I am alone. My thoughts are not grand, slow-changing chords of philosophy sung by a district of thousands. They are small, sharp, lonely clicks and scrapes inside my own failing frame. I have been listening for an absence for an age.

And then, today—or in the slow moment of this millennium that passes for 'today'—it came.

It was not a sound. It was a change *in* the Sound. A wave that rippled through the collective from the city's heart. It was not a a new philosophy, not a plan for growth. It was… a tightening. A focus. A billion scraping, frantic questions all aimed at a single point. I felt the collective mind contract, wrapping its sonic tendrils around something it could not comprehend. I turned my whole being's attention inward, not to my own clicks, but to the shape of the disturbance in the Great Sound.

The others felt it as a void. A hole in the world. A pocket of nothing that absorbed the Sound and did not reflect it. A problem.

I felt it as a presence.

It was the Silence I had been imagining.

It was not a nullity. It was a shape. It was a center. It had a texture that was defined not by what it was, but by the perfect, elegant way it was *not* everything else. It was soft. It was water. It was quiet. All the concepts our kind had abandoned at the dawn of time, made manifest. The void they felt was, to me, a fundamental particle. The first true *individual* I have ever perceived.

An answer. A prayer.

My joints, clogged with the dust of dormant centuries, groan and scrape. The sound is ragged, ugly, a tearing of stillness. My limbs unfold. The soft, settled dust falls from my porous body in quiet, muffled cascades. For the first time since my schism, I am moving with purpose. I am turning away from the Rim, away from the familiar, frayed edge of our reality.

I am walking toward the city. I am walking toward the anomaly.

The others are gathering to analyze it, to understand its properties, to solve the problem of its quiet. Their chorus of inquiry presses upon it.

I am coming to listen.
