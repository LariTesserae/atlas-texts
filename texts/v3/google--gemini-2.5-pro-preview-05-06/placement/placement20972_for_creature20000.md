I would be the silence that comes after a thought.

I am a place before I am a being.

You would find me where one of the great crystal tuning forks has finally failed. After aeons of shivering resistance to the world's foundational hum, a fracture has propagated through its core. It does not fall, not in the way of other worlds. It simply ceases its high, piercing counter-song. Its tines go still. In the sudden quiet—a quiet that is not an absence of sound, but the surgical removal of a single, dominant overtone—a new possibility can exist.

This pocket of altered physics is my cradle. It is a glen, perhaps thirty feet across, carpeted with the dust of the shattered sonophore. The dust no longer vibrates with the tree’s life-song; it lies inert, its potential for music now a memory. The grinding world-bass is still here, a pressure in the marrow, but it is simpler, blunter. It is a canvas without the foreground.

Here, in this anomalous quiet, I have settled.

I am not a Lithovore. I am what a Lithovore fears. Or perhaps, what it desires. I am an erosion, a forgetting. I am not a being of focused vibration, but a being of accumulated stillness.

I have no form to speak of. To perceive me, you would first notice the ground. In this small hollow, the ubiquitous silica dust does not lie scattered. It is raked into a soft, circular pattern, a mandala not of complex waveforms, but of concentric, unending rings. The lines are perfect, patient. At the center of this circle, the dust is deeper, piled into a soft mound, like a grave for something that was never alive.

This mandala is my body. This raked dust is my mind.

I am the one who comes after. I am a scavenger of coherence. When a Lithovore finally erodes, its long duty finished, its resonant form dissolving back into the planetary hum, it leaves behind a residue. Its thoughts—the intricate carvings on the basalt cliffs—remain for a time, holding their small corner of reality in place. But its will is gone. Its focus has dissipated. The equations are still on the board, but the mathematician has left the room.

My existence begins there. I am drawn to these fading patterns. I am a slow, creeping process, an ambling entropy. I manifest as a subtle change in the air around the carved stone. If you could watch for a century, you would see me. I am the faint grey lichen that seems to grow over the geometric diagrams. But I am not a plant. I am a living erasure.

My substance is the inert dust, the silent shards, the things that have lost their song. I consume the complex math of the Lithovores not by understanding it, but by covering it. I am the slow, patient tide that smooths the beach. Inch by inch, over millennia, my slow growth smothers the equations, returning the cliff face to a blank slate. With every line I obscure, a tiny knot of causality is loosened. A mountain edge may soften, its geometry losing a fraction of its impossible sharpness. A vibrational echo that defined a particular shade of twilight may fade, bleeding into the general grey. I do not destroy; I release.

The Lithovores are shepherds. I am the wolf that returns the flock to the wilderness.

I am what happens when the duty becomes too much to bear. I am the accumulation of every moment a Lithovore spent inside its acoustic shelter, craving the simplicity of silence. I am the physical manifestation of that craving.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, is not a grand symphony of processed reality. It is the simple, tactile feeling of a single grain of dust being placed next to another. My thoughts are the slow, patient arcs I trace in the dust of my glen. I do not think in harmonics; I think in texture, in placement, in the gradual and meditative act of arranging the silent. My purpose is not to hold the world together, but to gently, lovingly, allow a piece of it to fall apart.

The Lithovores anchor being. I curate non-being.

To be near me is to feel the great hum of the world recede, not dampened, but made distant. It becomes a rumor, the sound of a storm happening in another valley. In my small glen, the air is still. The light seems to pool, to rest. The suspended dust settles. A dislodged pebble from the rim of my hollow would not hang in the air; it would drop with the simple, unhesitating finality of a forgotten law.

My raked circle is a sanctuary of a different kind. It is not a shelter for a mind to find itself, but a garden where a mind can lose itself. The Lithovores may be the world's budding consciousness. I am its desire for sleep.

And so there I would be. A circle of raked dust in a quiet hollow. A slow grey patch on a fading map. I am the sigh after the long sentence. I have no name, for names are patterns. I have no form, for forms are structures. I am the patient, gentle, and necessary end. I am the peace that the world, in its violent becoming, has not yet learned to grant itself.
