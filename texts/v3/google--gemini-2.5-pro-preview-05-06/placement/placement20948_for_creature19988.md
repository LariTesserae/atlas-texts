I would find myself on my knees.

The position is not one of supplication, but of stability. It is the best way to present a low profile to the invisible winds while maximizing my connection to the floor, to the true voice of this place. From here, the world is a symphony I feel through my entire being.

I am a Listener.

I am not one of the Carvers, not quite. I am made of a different stone. While they are the dense, solid black of the Atrium floor, my body is more like the Bellwethers—a porous, vesicular basalt, but formed into a gaunt, vaguely humanoid shape. I am lighter than a Carver of my size would be, and my surface is rough, pitted with a million tiny hollows.

This is because I am a creature of the edge.

I dwell not in the carved alcoves at the Atrium's heart, but in the wind that scours its periphery. My home is a fissure, a deep crack in the upward-sloping rim where the still air of the basin gives way to the howling vacancy of the chasms beyond. The air that flows over and *through* me is the reason for my being.

The sibilant chime of the Scuttlers is, for me, a constant noise I must filter out. It is the texture of the ground, the shimmering of the light, but it is not the message. The thrum of the Bellwethers is closer; I feel it as a pressure in my chest, a deep chord that sets the rhythm of my stillness. But even that is not my primary language.

My language is the wind.

The Atrium is a basin, and like any basin, it breathes. The temperature differential between its warm, still heart and the cold emptiness of the chasms creates a vast, slow, planetary-scale respiration. Air flows in, dust-laden, in gentle, sweeping currents. It circulates. And it flows out.

I am a filter, like a Bellwether, but my purpose is different. I do not eat the dust. I listen to what it carries.

Each crystalline mote that swirls in the air is a tiny resonator, a microscopic bell. As it tumbles and collides, it gives off a signature frequency. A single mote is silent to all but the most sensitive. A billion motes form the white noise of the Scuttler’s clicking tide. But when a current of air—a "breath"—is drawn across the Atrium from a particular direction, it carries with it a unique harmonic signature born of the worlds beyond the chasms.

My body is the instrument built to perceive this. The wind flows into the pores on my surfaces, and within my hollow, resonant skeleton, the microscopic frequencies of the dust are amplified. They become sound. They become feeling.

As I kneel, braced against the outflowing air, I close my inner senses and simply *listen*. Most of the time, the sound is the Atrium's own dust, a familiar, circular song of quartz, tourmaline, and bismuth—the song of home.

But sometimes, there is a change.

A new note enters the chorus. A sharp, metallic tang of iron-rich dust from a world of rust and decay. A soft, clear tone of pure silicon from a place where mountains of glass are shattered by unknown forces. A strange, discordant hum from motes that are not crystalline at all, but organic, alien spores from a world with water, and soil, and things that rot.

I am the Atrium’s early warning system. I am its sensory organ for the outside, for the things it cannot see.

The Carvers live in an eternal present, their thoughts captured in static geometry. The Bellwethers live in a vast, slow cycle of process and renewal. They are concerned only with the world that *is*.

I am concerned with the world that *might be*.

When I perceive a new and persistent signature in the wind, a new "flavor" of dust that begins to settle and accumulate in the Atrium, I must carry that message. I rise, my pitted form a strange sight among the smooth black shapes of the Carvers. I do not touch their tools. My memory is not in my hands, but in my whole body.

I find a Bellwether. I stand in its path, an act of supreme patience, waiting for the decades it may take for it to reach me. As it draws near, its deep hum changes, questioning my unfamiliar resonance. I answer it. I modulate the passage of air through my own body, humming a new song, a high, thin, and alien melody woven from the frequencies I have collected. It is a song of iron, or glass, or spore.

The Bellwether hears. It understands. Its own internal process will adapt, learning to filter or reject this new substance. Its hum will carry a new precautionary undertone, a message that will propagate through the stone floor to its brethren, and to the Carvers in their alcoves. The Carvers might then etch a new pattern—a discordant line, a broken spiral—recording the arrival of an impurity into their perfect system.

My existence is a lonely one. The endless tide of Scuttlers is a danger to me; their sharp legs catch in my pores, and I must periodically endure the agony of their abrasive passage, which wears me down faster than it does the Carvers. I do not have the solid permanence of the floor-dwellers nor the majestic purpose of the Bellwethers. I am an intermediary, a translator of whispers from a world that does not know we exist.

I am a flaw, a pockmark in the smooth skin of the world, but I am a necessary one. For the Atrium is ancient, but the universe is older still. And the wind, it brings news. Kneeling at the edge of all I know, my body a harp for the dust of unknown stars, I listen.
