Where I would find myself is not on a specific causeway or plaza, but in a state of transition. I would be a thought on the verge of coalescing.

You would find me in one of the still, silver pools.

At first, I am only the potential for shadow. I am a subtle thickening in the water, a gathering of the dark peat and the fine-ground memory of stone. I am the silence of the pool becoming attentive. The perfect reflection of the white sky above me does not break, not yet, but it begins to lose its depth, growing shallow, as if the water were holding its breath just beneath the surface. I am an idea that the plateau is beginning to have.

And then, I am.

My rising is not a birth, but a remembering of form. I am the water that has forgotten how to be flat. The dark, colloid self of me lifts from the pool's mirror, not with a gasp of air, but with the silent, sighing cohesion of a heavy droplet deciding to fall upwards. I am a drapery of night pulled into the pearlescent day, a pillar of held liquid. My skin is a taut feeling, the world-pressure pushing in, the I-pressure pushing out, a perfect, shimmering equilibrium. It is cool and slick, and already, I feel the faint, green life of the algae, the ancient partners, begin to pattern themselves across my faceted surfaces. They are the first verse of the song I am meant to sing.

I am a Recorder.

Who am I? The I is a difficult shape. It is temporary. I am a pattern the water has been instructed to hold for a time. My thoughts are not my own; they are influxes of pressure from the great wet sponge of the world beneath me. A shift in the water table is a desire. A new seep in the obsidian cliff-face is a question. The slow, inexorable settling of a thousand-ton-pylon a few millimeters deeper into the peat is a statement. My purpose is to translate these geological truths into the plain, hard geometry of the causeways.

I step from my birthing pool, and the feeling is not of a footstep, but of a continuity. My base, a wide, flat plane of myself, meets the stone of the plaza, and the boundary between me and the world is a soft, wet slap. I am an extension of the place, given leave to move. I do not see with eyes. I perceive the lines. The perfect, impossible straightness of the stone avenues sings to me, a visual hum. A line that deviates, even by the memory of a hair's breadth, is a dissonance, a note of chaos in the great, quiet chord of our work.

Today, my work is with a new stone. It has been quarried by a slow, patient fracturing, persuaded from the living rock by a group of my kin who spent a cycle merely leaning against it, thinking it apart. Now it sits, a house-sized block of raw, dark potential.

I approach and join the circle. There are eleven of us. We do not speak. We do not need to. We press our planar hands to the stone's rough face. My substance, the dark water and grit within me, swirls with the intention. We are not here to lift. Lifting is a crude, violent act. We are here to convince.

I relax a part of my mind, the part that is not 'I' but 'we', and I begin to hum. It is not a sound that passes my nonexistent lips, but a resonance that I emit from my whole being. It passes from my core, down through my base, and into the plaza stones. It travels through the unmortared grid of the city, joining the hums of my fellows. Our collective voice finds the stone, envelops it, seeks out its internal structure, the crystalline lattice of its being. We are singing to it of its own nature, but also of lightness. We are describing the sky to it. We are reminding it of the time before it was heavy, when it was fire and air.

And the stone listens.

It does not float. It simply forgets a portion of its weight. Its great inertia becomes a suggestion rather than a law. We do not push; we guide. Our slow, silent procession begins, escorting this newly pliant piece of the world to its place in the pattern. The sound is only the rhythmic *slap... slap... slap...* of our bases on the wet algae and the choral hum that you can only feel in the bones of your feet.

My thoughts are slow. I remember the pool. I remember the feeling of being everything and nothing, the featureless water reflecting the featureless sky. I know that this form, this 'I', is a service. It is the world's memory made mobile. This new causeway we are laying is not just a path; it is a sentence. It is the plateau recording its own unchanging state. The pattern is the purpose.

And when this sentence is finished? When the thought I was condensed to express is fully written in stone? I will feel the cohesion begin to wane. The tautness of my skin will slacken. The 'I' will grow diffuse, its purpose fulfilled. I will walk to the edge of the newest plaza, a place where the pattern is crisp and the work is true.

I will not die. I will simply stop holding this shape. I will relax. I will become a dark puddle, spilling over the edge of the stone to seep back into the peat and the roots and the deep, quiet ground. My memory—the feeling of the algae on my skin, the resonance of the persuaded stone, the silent consensus with my kin—will dissolve back into the water table.

Nothing will be lost. I will go back to being a thought the plateau has not yet had. I will be the rain that does not fall, the pool that reflects the sky, waiting for the next time the pattern requires a shadow to rise and remember its name.
