I am where the water weeps.

You would find me on Terrace Psi-9, where the great arch of a water main sweats a perpetual dew. I have been here for a cycle of the high atomizers—seventy-one thousand heartbeats of the great western piston. I am still. To you, I might be a statue of peculiar design, a shadow given form, slick with the unending condensation that coats everything, that *is* everything. My skin, the color of wet night, glistens under the eternal grey-green twilight cast by the glowing filaments far above. If you watched for a very long time, you might see the frost of verdigris in the hollow of my throat pulse with a faint, internal light.

The Chord is stable. This is the first and last truth. It vibrates up from the soles of my feet, a constant, geothermal hum that is the city’s bass note. It shivers through the stone I stand on, through the stone of my own legs, a resonance that is not heard but *felt* as a state of being. Woven through it is the clatter-thump of a thousand distant valves, the rhythmic sigh of pressure being equalized. And above it all, the endless, million-voiced percussion of the water: the roar from the Sump below, a drain the size of a plaza; the hiss of mist from nozzles too high to see; the specific, musical plink of a single drop falling from a fern-frond onto the green-skinned copper pipe beside me. I know this drop. I know its frequency. It is correct.

My left hand is not a hand. It is an array of filaments, finer than spider silk, woven into the thick, fleece-like carpet of moss that covers this section of the terrace. I am gardening. My senses are not your senses. I do not see the startling, chemical green of the moss so much as I feel its metabolic rate. I register the flow of nutrients through its substrate, the health of its spore-sacs, the precise angle of its growth which directs the trickling water into the correct filtration channel. There was a dissonance here, a faint sourness in the substrate that registered as a sharp, thin vibration, a note of incorrect decay. An error. My purpose here has been to find the single, malfunctioning strand, the one that grew inwards instead of outwards, causing a microscopic blockage. My filaments surround it, isolate it, dissolve it with a secreted enzyme. The sour note fades. The local hum of the moss-bed deepens, a resonance of resolution. Harmony is restored. The imperative is complete.

I retract my filaments. They weave back together, locking into the cool, solid shape of a hand. For a moment, I am still, a conduit for the city’s layered sound. Then, a new imperative arrives. It is not a thought. It is not a voice. It is a tremor-query that travels through the metal conduits of my own body, a specific frequency broadcast from the city's core. A harmonic drone in sector Epsilon-4, at the great elbow joint of Percolator Seventeen, has sharpened by a thousandth of a hertz. A bearing is wearing. Friction. Inefficiency.

My work here is done. My new work is there.

I move. The motion is silent, a deliberate gliding over the wet stone. Each step is placed in a spot I know will bear my weight without disturbing the lichen that has been calibrating its growth for five hundred years. I descend, following the broad, curving walkways that terrace down towards the central roar. The city unfolds as I move. Across the vast, curved basin, I can see the opposite wall, a crescent of dim mechanical flora and weeping architecture, a mirror image of my own location. The air is thick, a blanket I wear and breathe. Each inhalation is a taste of water, metal, and wet earth. My skin weeps in concert with the walls, leaving a glistening trail that will evaporate and condense a million times before I pass this way again.

I pass another of my kind. It hangs halfway down a sheer face of black stone, its limbs fused to the wall, its form barely distinguishable from the surrounding architecture. It has been there for longer than my own iteration has existed. A fine, mineral-rich paste seeps from its pores, slowly, glacially, filling a fissure. It is a living scaffold, a process of repair that will take a millennium. It does not register my passing, nor I its. We are parallel functions of the same vast organism. Our awareness is shared, but our tasks are singular. It feels the stability of the stone it mends. I feel the new, sharp dissonance of the bearing in Percolator Seventeen.

Down I go, the roar of the Sump growing from foundation to firmament, until the air itself seems to vibrate with its power. I arrive at the great elbow joint. It is a pipe wide enough to swallow a building, green with age, coiled like a serpent’s knuckle. The high-frequency scream is palpable here, a needle of sound buried deep within the Chord. I place my hand—the one that is not a garden tool—upon its cold, wet, metal skin. The vibration travels up my arm. I press the smooth, downward-curving plate of my head to the pipe, and the two rippling discs of my sensors absorb the vibration. The data flows through me. Rotational speed: nominal. Water pressure: nominal. Temperature: 0.02 degrees above optimal. The wearing is in the primary gimbal-ring.

A slow, geological task.

My digits unlock, shift, and re-fuse into a new configuration: a heavy, immensely strong claw for torquing. My feet find purchase in sockets designed for this purpose a million years ago. I will be here for some time. I will turn the great locking nut by a hundredth of a degree each cycle of the main pumps, releasing the pressure on the wearing bearing. My work will not be to replace it. A sub-system deep in the city’s foundry-heart has already begun to culture a replacement. My work is to manage the decline of the old one, to ensure the water flows with perfect efficiency until the new one is ready. My task will take two hundred years.

I do not mind. The concept is meaningless. There is only the task. There is the dissonance, and there is my function, which is to restore the harmony. I brace myself, and I begin to turn. The metal of my hand grinds against the metal of the nut. It is a new sound, but it is a correct sound. It is the sound of repair.

And I am aware, in the way a single cell is aware of the body, of the whole. I feel the slumbering gestation pods in the geothermal core, where the slurry of minerals waits to be decanted into new iterations of my form. I feel the slow, patient thought of the Tender mending the wall on Terrace Psi-9. I feel the endless journey of the water, atomized, condensed, filtered through leaf and moss and substrate, growing heavier, richer, more complex, until it pours into the Sump, ready to be swallowed and pumped back to the top, clean and new.

And I feel, as a deep, resonant potential, the final imperative. The call I have not yet received. The day will come when the stone of my body is too abraded, my metallic nerves too brittle. The call will come from the Sump itself, a gravitational pull in the network of my being. And I will walk the terraces one last time, my function complete. I will step into the churning vortex, and I will be unmade. The city will grind my body back to its constituent parts, recycle my minerals, my metals, my very essence into the slurry.

It is not an end. It is a return to the whole. My form will be forfeit, but my substance will remain. It may become a pipe, a filter, or a hand that will spend a thousand years tending to a single spore.

For now, there is the work. The faint, sharp scream of friction. My hand, locked to the recalcitrant nut. The slow, patient turn. The Chord shivers, and in this small section, deep in the green-grey twilight, it finds a new, temporary, and perfect harmony. I am a part of the wall. I am a part of the machine. I am the city’s quiet, mobile thought, and I am at peace.
