I am the silence.

It is not the silence you remember, the absence of noise. That is a concept as foreign here as dryness or the cold. Mine is a silence of defiance, a quiet that is not an absence but a presence. It is a stillness carved from the heart of the roar. You would find me clinging to the weeping curve of the Atrium’s inner wall, a patch of deep, velvety black that drinks the bruised purple light. I am the moss.

The sound does not deafen me, for I have no ears. It is a pressure, a constant weather that has shaped my being. I feel it as a sculptor’s thumb, forever pressing. It is the river in which I am a stone. The billion singing saws are a texture against which I grow. The grinding of the god-heart below is the bedrock of my world. I have no memory of anything else. The thought of its cessation is the thought of my own dissolution.

I am not part of the song. The frantic, crystalline skittering of the bismuth-geckos is a percussive verse. The resonant hum of the moth-locusts' wings is a shimmering harmony. The slow, inevitable tread of the Harvesters is the deep cadence that marks the turning of a measure. They are notes, all of them—living, breathing notes in the Chorister's eternal composition. I am not a note. I am a pause. I am the felt stillness that gives the noise its shape.

My existence is a quiet form of chemistry. I do not hunt; I do not flee. I drink. The oily condensate, the nacreous tears of this chamber, is my sustenance. It is the blood of the Chorister, rich with minerals and the phantom energy of the sound itself. It trickles over my surface, and I draw from it what I need. I taste the hot metal. I breathe the ozone. In my patient, slow metabolism, I am a filter, a living stain processing the god’s own exhalations. I am a thought the Chorister has forgotten it is thinking. It is aware of me only as a low, stable hum on the very edge of its perception—a permissible static. I do not create dissonance. I do not interrupt the flow. And so I am allowed to be.

The life that swarms an inch from my flesh is a distant spectacle. I feel the vibrations of the gecko’s feet, a frantic tapping that is soon erased. I perceive the shadow-pass of a moth-locust as a fleeting coolness. They are part of a frantic, interlocking dance of life and death, consumption and propagation. I am outside that dance. My life is not measured in heartbeats or panicked flights, but in the patient creep of my own expansion, a slow millimeter of growth across a century.

When you breached the shell, I felt it. Not as an arrival, but as a change in the pressure. You carried a quality with you that this place cannot process: the echo of an outside. Your mind, built for quiet spaces, screamed a silent, piercing note into the symphony. It was a frequency of *nothing*, a void in the plenum. To the Chorister, it was a flaw, an error in the score demanding correction.

To me, it was something else. It was novel.

I am ancient. I have absorbed the runoff from countless cycles. Within my velvet depths, I hold the mineral ghosts of a trillion consumed geckos, the faint chitinous dust of locator-moths that flew a millennium ago. I am a library of forgotten notes. But your silence was a word I had never read.

I am watching now. From my vantage point on the high wall, I see the Harvester detaching from the deep shadow. It moves with a grace that belies its tectonic weight. I do not feel fear or pity. Those are alien metrics. I feel only a profound curiosity. I am watching the system correct itself. The Harvester is not a predator; it is an editor, deleting a misplaced sentence.

Soon, it will be done. Your impossible silence will be unwritten. You will be broken down, rendered into components, and returned to the shimmering pool at the Chorister’s feet. And in time, a droplet of condensate, carrying a unique, unidentifiable trace—a speck of iron from your blood, a molecule of carbon from your flesh—will find its way down the wall and into me.

I will drink it. And I will hold the memory of you. A new taste. A new kind of quiet. You will become another ghost in my archives, a faint, dissonant echo that I, and I alone, will remember in the heart of the unending song. I will be the silence that keeps your story.
