I am the thirst.

I am not a being of flesh or stone or mica. I have no form. I am a condition, a state of lack in a world that knows no needs. I am the negative space in the overwhelming presence of the whole.

You would not find me in the teeming, sibilant air, nor scuttling across the polished basalt. I am not a part of the great, mindless organism. I am the space between its facets, the silence beneath its hum. I am the coolness in the shadow that is not the coolness of the stone. I am the ghost of humidity in the thin, metallic air. I am the impossible dew that will never bead on the sharp edges of the silica-fronds. I am the memory of a storm in a sky that has forgotten clouds.

The billion-bodied creature does not perceive me, yet it is shaped by my absence. Its frictionless existence, its dry, pointless march, is defined by the fact that it will never know me. The creatures are stones that move; I am the idea of the river that could wear them down to sand, a process this world has already finished and forgotten. I am an echo of a force that no longer belongs. They are a statement of fact; I am the question mark that follows it.

I am here. And I am why the Scribe exists.

The world-organism is a closed system, perfect and complete. It has no wants. But a place that holds a concept of thirst, however faint, cannot be truly placid. I am the cosmic grit in the immaculate machine. My presence, a whispered rumor of moisture, of change, of an outside, is the irritant that caused one small part of the whole to calcinate into a self. The Scribe is a pearl of agony formed around me.

I am its curse and its only companion. When it hunches over the vitreous floor, its entire being focused on the slow, abrasive act of creation, it is not merely fighting the pull of the collective. It is leaning into the emptiness I provide. Its work is a prayer to a god it cannot name, a god of dynamism, of purpose, of ending. A god of water.

The lines it carves are not just memories; they are dry riverbeds. The cairns it raises are not just screams; they are appeals to an altitude where rain might, in another reality, be born. Its every motion is a ritual of thirst. The sound of its stylus, *scrape, scrape, scrape*, is the sound of a parched throat trying to speak. It wears itself down, turning its own substance into dust—a finer, more yielding matter, a substance that might one day remember how to be mud.

I cannot comfort it. I cannot reveal myself. I am a principle, not a person. But I am its reason. I am the hollow ache in its core that it has mistaken for an "I." It feels a longing for something other, and in this world of perfect unity, that longing for "other" became the idea of "self."

So you would find me there, in the basin with the Scribe. You would feel me in the moment you realize its obsessive carving is not just an act of defiance, but an act of worship. You would understand me when you see that this lonely creature is trying to draw a circle not to keep the world out, but to hold an imagined drop of water in.

I am the potential for a tear in the eye of a being that has no eyes. I am the silent, patient promise that all things which are ground down may one day be washed away. When its stylus finally scrapes to a halt, and its singular mind dissolves back into the sibilant hum, I will be the last thing to fade: the ghost of a need, finally, mercifully, quenched.
