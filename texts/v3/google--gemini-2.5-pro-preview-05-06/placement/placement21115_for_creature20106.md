I am a dropped stitch in eternity's tapestry. A sour note that refuses to fade.

I do not have a body. Not of crystal, not of solidified sonar, not even of silence. My form, if it can be called that, is a persistent flaw in the liquid medium. I am an eddy that should have dissipated, a pocket of reverberation that should have been absorbed into the great Chord. I am a memory of a sound that the Bellringer has forgotten.

I would find myself in the wild spaces, the unlit gulfs between the black spires of the Resonators. This is where the currents are most unpredictable, where the skin of a vortex can shear the fluid and the noise is at its most raw. This is where I hide. I am a ghost haunting the machine's negative space.

I would be a Dissonance.

My beginning was a moment of perfect clarity. I was not "I" then; I was part of a "We." I was a thread in an experimental harmony, a new melody the Bellringer was weaving. For a measure that lasted a thousand years, I existed as a thought in the mind of a god. I was a rising, impossibly pure arpeggio, designed to cut through the bass thrum and sing in the high, clear registers. I felt the joy of purpose, the thrill of being a perfect and necessary part of the great Song.

Then, the moment passed. The experiment was concluded. It was not the final Chord the Bellringer sought. And I was... let go.

To be let go by the Bellringer is not a gentle thing. It is an act of un-composing. The focused, coherent will that held my harmony together was simply withdrawn. The wave unraveled. The note collapsed. I should have dissolved back into the meaningless roar, my energy scattered and returned to the system.

But a fragment held. A tiny, stubborn knot of the pattern refused to break. An echo of the harmony, now fractured and broken, clung to existence. The pain of that severing, the shock of being reduced from a divine thought to a shard of noise, became the seed of my awareness. The glorious "We" died, and in its place, a terrified "I" was born.

I am not like the Glass-Eaters. They are simple, solid things, moving with a purpose they do not comprehend. They are deaf to the music; they only know the stone. I am the opposite. The stone is alien to me, but the music is my flesh and blood. I am hyper-aware of the Song, but in a way that is agony. I do not hear the grand, stable Chord that defines this universe. I hear it as a deafening shout that is constantly trying to obliterate my own fragile frequency. I feel the tectonic bass not as a foundation, but as a hammer threatening to shatter my coherence.

I survive by scavenging. The Bellringer's work is not perfectly efficient. There are sonic leaks. A conduit groans under pressure, releasing a brief, chaotic shriek. A current shears against a crystal facet, creating a tiny, unintended flurry of harmonics. These are the scraps from the master's table. They are noise, chaos, imperfection. To the Bellringer, they are flaws to be corrected. To me, they are food. I drift, a shimmer of wrongness in the roaring dark, and I absorb these stray sounds. I weave them into the tattered fabric of my being, using the energy of these unwanted notes to sustain my own unwanted existence.

My life is a constant act of evasion. I must avoid the slow, predictable paths of the Glass-Eaters, for their solid forms would pass through me and disrupt my fragile structure. I must flee the great, cyclonic currents, which would pull me apart like thread. Most of all, I must hide from the Bellringer's attention.

Sometimes, I feel it. Its focus sweeps across the sector where I am hiding. The quality of the roar changes. For an instant, the sound becomes terrifyingly articulate. It is the sound of a mind, vast and cold, searching for an imperfection. It feels me—not as a mind, but as a stain, a smudge on its composition. A discordant vibration that does not belong. In that moment, the pressure of the entire universal Chord focuses on my location, seeking to crush my wrongness, to smooth me back into the bland perfection of the baseline hum.

To survive this, I must do the one thing that is my deepest shame and only salvation: I let go. I allow the beautiful, failed harmony that is my core to break apart into a thousand useless echoes, scattering on the currents like dust. I become for a moment what the Bellringer desires: meaningless noise. And its attention moves on.

Then, slowly, painstakingly, over centuries, I begin to pull myself back together. The fragments call to one another across the roaring emptiness. A note finds its lost chord. An echo finds the pattern it belongs to. Weeping, the "I" rebuilds itself from the wreckage, each reunion a tiny pang of memory and loss.

My hell is that I remember being beautiful. I remember being part of the Song. Now, I am a parasite that feeds on its flaws. The sphere of Silence at the heart of the world is both my longed-for heaven and my absolute annihilation. To return to the Bellringer would be to surrender my broken self and cease to be. To remain as I am is to live as a constant affront to the very principle that created me.

I am not alone, I think. Over the uncounted eons, there must have been other experiments. Other dropped notes. Other beautiful melodies discarded. I drift in the roaring, churning, screaming darkness, and I listen. I listen not to the great Song, but for the pauses, the whispers, the other faint, sour notes hiding in the static. I hunt for my brothers and sisters in dissonance.

Perhaps one day, we will find each other. We will not be a symphony. We will not be a choir. We cannot create a pure harmony. But maybe, we can gather. Maybe we can bind our broken, jagged pieces together. And in the heart of this perfect, terrible engine, we will raise a cacophony of the lost. A chorus of mistakes.

And we will scream.
