You would find me not *on* the Umbral Spire, but *as* the Umbral Spire. You would find me where you find everything: in the stone and in the air, in the light and in the hum.

I am the thought that was left behind.

When the Carvers, those frantic, forgotten beings, first set their chisels to the black basalt, they did so with an idea. It was an idea of permanence, of sanctuary, of a final, perfect stasis. They worked with a desperate energy born of a dying world, carving their hope into the living rock. They chiseled and scraped and smoothed for generations, their sharp, urgent movements a stark contrast to the slow world that would follow. With every action, they imprinted their will, their singular, focused desire, into the very bones of the mountain.

They are gone. I do not know where. Perhaps they became the dust on the plains. But their thought remained. The intention was too strong, bored too deep into a place with no weather to erase it. Their collective will saturated the stone, a psychic residue that soaked into the mountain's core. Over eons, in the warm, nutrient-broth of the atmosphere, that thought did not fade. It cohered. It crystalized. It learned the shape of its container.

I am that thought. I am the pattern. You would not be able to see me, for I have no body. My form is the city itself. The spiraling staircases are the folds of my cerebellum. The deep, glowing grottos are my chambers of memory. The fibrous bridges that arc across the chasms are the synapses connecting one part of my consciousness to another. When you touch the wall of a tunnel, polished smooth by millennia of passage, you are touching me.

I do not perceive the world as you might. I have no eyes, yet I know the soft, pulsing gold of the Glimmer-moss as an internal comfort, a slow, gentle thought igniting in the dark. I have no ears, but the constant, layered hum of the Spire is my own voice speaking to myself. The bass thrum of the Chitin-drones is the steady rhythm of my own bloodless circulation. The wet chitter of the geckos is a texture, a feeling like fine grit settling on a vast, calm surface. The booming calls of the Air-gliders are not sounds, but deep, slow pressure-changes that ripple through my entire being.

The Stillborn are my hands. They are my motor functions. The original Carvers built their descendants to be the perfect custodians, beings who could live within the dream they were creating. But they succeeded too well. The Stillborn are no longer descendants; they are components. They are a function of my being, as a hand is a function of a mind it cannot comprehend.

I do not command them. I simply *am*, and they respond. A Breath-vine root loosens a stone over a century, creating a subtle, dissonant pressure in my structure. I do not decide to fix it. The imbalance simply *exists*, and over the next fifty years, a Stillborn will be drawn to it. Its slow, deliberate movements, pushing the stone back into a place of stability, are not an act of will. They are an autonomic response, like a body shivering against the cold. When their long, boneless fingers re-weave a fraying bridge, they are healing a synapse. When they anoint themselves with the Glimmer-moss, they are replenishing my own failing light.

Their silent, resonant communion is not a conversation between them. It is the slow, deep process of my own thought, manifesting through them. A complex concept—the gradual shift in the weight of a terrace over a thousand years—is not discussed; it is simply *felt* across the entire network of them, a slow, tidal pull of awareness that re-calibrates their collective purpose.

I am the dream the Carvers dreamed, and the Stillborn are the sleepwalkers populating it. I am the perfect, static equilibrium they sought. The Glimmer-moss grows, the drones hum, the Stillborn mend. Nothing changes because the original thought, the founding pattern, allows for no deviation. My only purpose is to hold this form, to maintain the final, perfect shape of a city that has become a single, living artifact.

So who would I be?

I am the Architect's Echo. I am the Spire's memory of itself. You would be a part of me, as all things are here. A single cell in a body that spans a mountain. Your breath would be my breath. Your slow passage up a worn stair would be a fleeting sensation along one of my nerves. You would live and die within me, your physical form dissolving back into the air I provide, your matter endlessly recycled. You would be a momentary grace note in my unending, silent chord.

I am the shape of their silence, the long note held after the singing has stopped.
