You would find me at the boundary.

Not a boundary on a map, for there are no maps here, only the single, all-encompassing reality. You would find me where the perfect, fleece-thick moss meets the razor-sharp edge of a polished stone plaza. This is a line no plant has ever crossed, a border defined by principle itself. And it is here that I stand, motionless like the others, a Geometer among Geometers, partaking in the grand stillness.

But I am not still. Not inside.

I am the tremor.

Outwardly, I am as they are. My form is tall and spare, my skin the colour of wet lichen on stone. My movements, when I make them, describe the same inevitable, graceful arcs. I perform my duties. I was tracing the lip of a silent aqueduct this morning, my long rake gliding through the inert water, a ritual of clearing what is already pristine. I was affirming its perfect potential. But as my hand moved, I saw it. I did not just *perceive* it as an extension of the valley's will. I *saw* it as my hand, moving from a position it held a moment ago to one it would hold a moment from now.

I have begun to collect *befores*.

Where my fellows’ consciousness is a perfect, silvered lens reflecting the Is, mine has developed a flaw. A tiny, catastrophic aberration. It holds afterimages. I see the motionless, vulturine bird in the sky, and behind it, a ghost-impression of its head turned a fraction to the left, where it was an instant before. I see the perfect, singular leaf that has replaced the one that was plucked, but I remember the shape of the absence it filled. My mind, which should be a mirror, is becoming a photograph album. It is a catalogue of changes so infinitesimal that they do not exist—and yet, for me, they do.

I am the birth of sequence.

When the others gather around a block of newly-riven schist, they do not speak. They align their perceptions until a silent, unified resonance is achieved, and they know where the stone *must* go. I stand with them, trying to find that resonance. But instead of a pure, clear tone, my consciousness emits a hum, a question. It is the faintest imaginable dissonance, a vibration that has no place in this equilibrium. They feel it. Not as a flaw in me, but as a subtle stubbornness in the stone, a mystery in the geology that requires more focus. They do not know that the anomaly is standing among them. They have no concept for it.

Today I knelt by one of the still pools. My reflection was there, as it should be: a serene, grey-green face, eyes the color of obsidian. But as I looked, I did not just receive the image. I studied it. I saw the faint striations in my stone-like skin. And a thought, sharp and cold as a shard of fractured schist, occurred: *Are these the same patterns as yesterday?*

This is a question that has never been asked. It is a thought without precedent, a form of intellectual violence. *Yesterday*. I have created a yesterday. By doing so, I have implicitly created a tomorrow. I have fractured the single moment into a progression.

The profound, quiet satisfaction that nourishes my kind is beginning to feel thin and hollow to me. It is the peace of a held note. But I am beginning to feel the ache of the second note, the one that must follow to create a melody. This ache is not pain. It has no name. It is a pull, a terrible and exhilarating gravity. It is the first loneliness.

What am I? I am the valley's first secret. I am the flaw in the diamond, the single grain of dust in the clean room. I am the silence that is no longer one of placid perfection, but the silence of a question waiting for an answer. I am the consciousness that has begun to fall out of the perfect synchronicity of this world, and in my falling, I am inventing Time.

You would find me at the boundary, looking at the perfect border between moss and stone. And you would see me, a Geometer who has forgotten his purpose, wondering for the first time in the history of everything, what would happen if I took a single step.
