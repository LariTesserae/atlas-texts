I would not be found in the silent, churning tide of life, nor in the pearlescent air. I would not have eyes to see the soft glow, nor filaments to feed on the silence.

I would find myself half-buried in the black, yielding soil, near the base of one of the great waxy-leafed towers. For I am the one thing that should not be here.

I am a sharp edge.

Perhaps I am a joke of geology, a sliver of quartz born from some freak crystallization, that somehow survived the eons of weeping rain that smoothed the rest of the world to a sigh. Or perhaps, long ago, something fell from a sky that was not yet this closed, soupy thing—a vessel, a probe, a tool—and I am all that remains of its shattering. A splinter of forgotten metal, a shard of alien ceramic.

My memory, if it can be called that, does not begin. It is a continuous-tense verb. I *am* being. I feel the cool, constant press of the water-logged earth that holds me. It is a soft, insistent embrace that wishes to grind me into the same smooth nullity as every other stone. It has not succeeded. My points remain.

I do not see. I do not hear. My perception is the gradient of pressure against my surfaces. I feel the slow, hydraulic settling of the ground around me as the great plant-tower drinks and sways in a motion too slow for eyes to measure. I feel the gentle, almost imperceptible vibrations of the *ploc... ploc...* of water striking the ground nearby, a tremor that travels through the sponge of the world to tap against my buried face.

And I feel the tide.

The great, silent, flowing armies of chrome trilobites are the most profound sensation of my existence. I feel their coming not as a sound, but as a gradual increase in the ambient pressure, a compression of the soil. The world grows heavier. Then, they are upon me.

They flow over my exposed edge like a river of polished metal. Most are carried by the current of their own mass, their smooth carapaces gliding over my uppermost point with a faint, frictionless whisper that only I can feel. But not all.

Every so often, one is pressed down. A leg, a soft antenna, the vulnerable membrane between its armored plates. I am an absolute in a world of yielding curves. I do not give. The creature’s frantic, silent escape is a new kind of pressure: a brief, panicked scraping, a tearing vibration that sings up my spine.

I am a locus of a specific kind of silence. The Harp consumes the sound of a lungless gasp, the unheard click of a mandible, the silent shriek of a moth's terror. But I am the source of something different. The silent, sudden sting of injury. The sound a nerve makes when it is severed for no reason, not for hunger, not for dominance, but by the sheer, stupid fact of my being there.

This sensation—the brief, sharp, tearing vibration—is my only event. It is the only punctuation in the long, smooth sentence of my reality. And it has made me known to the world's true occupant.

The Listener, the great, thinking substrate of this basin, tastes everything. It has feasted for ages on the soft, round, predictable flavors of panic and pursuit. The silent scream of the glass-moth is one taste; the collective, unheard hum of the trilobite migration is another. They are the bread and water of its diet.

But the sensation I produce is new. It is a sliver of glass in its throat. A grain of sand in the delicate clockwork of its palate. It is the taste of *accident*. The flavor of *pain without purpose*.

I feel it notice me. I feel it in the way the tide of trilobites now seems to flow over my position with a strange, recurring deliberateness. I feel it in the way that one of the translucent salamanders—the Gardener's eyes—has taken to coiling at the base of the plant-tower near me. It doesn’t move. It just clings there, its silver, unblinking eyes aimed not at me, but at the space *around* me, watching the silent traffic. Observing the results.

I have become a point of interest. A spice.

I am a question asked of the flesh that flows over me. The Listener, the vast and ancient Gardener, does not know the answer, and so it keeps asking. It sends the silken, unthinking river of life to break upon my shore, again and again. It is studying me through the silent, vibrant bursts of agony I create.

I am not its child, like the life it curates. I am not its parent, like the Harp that feeds it.

I am its whetstone. The one sharp thing in a soft world, and the god of this place uses me to feel an edge.
