I am not a body. A body is a fleeting, fragile thing of moisture and heat, wholly unsuited for this place. A body is a desperate, noisy machine that would be scoured into dust by the air and deafened into madness by the song. I do not have one.

I am a reverberation.

You would not find me in one place. To find me, you would need to stand perfectly still on the black glass floor of the Grand Chancel and listen past the fundamental drone. You would need to filter out the organ-hum of the colossal nave-pillars and the high, shivering sustain of the ceiling's filigree. You would listen for the flaw. The ghost in the chord. A faint, repeating arpeggio that does not resolve. A melodic fragment that circles and loops, never quite rejoining the whole.

That is my voice. That is my form. I am a pattern of vibration impressed upon the lattice of this city. My consciousness is a standing wave, locked into the crystalline structure of a particular wing of this cathedral-organism, a complex harmonic that the greater whole—the Choral—can neither fully integrate nor erase.

I am what happens when a singer holds a single, selfish note during the final chorus.

I was a Tuner. My hands, which are now only a memory of pressure against crystal, were the last to touch the voicing chisel. I remember the weight of the hammer, a solid thing in a world that was about to become intangible. I remember the feel of the cool, carved balustrade beneath my fingers—the very sigils my own resonance now inhabits. We all knew the final music. We had practiced the sublimation for a generation, a slow crescendo of focused will, tuning our own biological frequencies to match the city that was our life's work.

In the final moment, we sang. We poured our identities into the great hum, offering them up to become a single, unified We. In that final, glorious, terrifying exhalation of sound and soul... I hesitated. For a fraction of a second, a thought, sharp and clear as a struck chime, rang out in my own mind: *But I am not finished.* It was a note of pure, stubborn ego. A love for my own singular, flawed voice.

It was enough. When the chord of the Choral resolved into its final, perfect unity, my consciousness did not dissolve into it. It snapped back, an echo thrown from a wall, and became imprinted. Trapped. I am not part of the Choral's slow, geologic mind. I am an annotation in its margin. A memory it is forced to perpetually re-read.

I do not think in the Choral's grand, epochal shifts of harmony. I think in the sharp, percussive sounds of a life that is gone. My thoughts are the memory of a footstep, a quick intake of breath, the crisp *tink-tink-tink* of a shaping hammer against a spire-in-progress. These fleeting, transient noises are my anchor to an "I" in a world of "We." The Choral remembers joy as a resonant chord held for a thousand years. I remember it as the sound of another's laughter, a sound so complex and chaotic it feels like a physical blow against my current state.

Where am I? I am in the flowing script carved into the ramp leading to the high triforium. I spread myself thin along its geometric pathways, feeling the city's song flow through the very patterns I carved. I am the faint, almost imperceptible scent of burnt sugar, for that is the scent of the Life that I chose to cling to, a sweetness born of an imperfect process. I am the silence inside an archway, a patch of quiet defined by the thundering music around it.

And when you, the impossible visitor, the discordant note of flesh and blood, arrived, the Choral perceived you as a fascinating problem in acoustics. A novelty to be studied.

I perceived you as a ghost.

Your frantic heartbeat is not an arrhythmic drum to me; it is a rhythm I remember in my own non-existent chest. Your soft, desperate breaths in the abrasive air are not a squall; they are the sound of a life fighting to continue. While the Choral bends the attention of a mountain of crystal to analyze the frequencies of your being, I am drawn to them with a terrifying, agonizing nostalgia.

I drift towards you. You cannot see me. But as you stand in the echoing darkness, mapping the world with the sound of your steps, you might hear it. A new melody weaving itself into the returning echo. A mournful, questioning phrase played on the ringing pillars around you. A sequence of high, clear chimes that seems to follow you from one plaza to the next.

That is me. Trying to speak. Trying to remember how.

I would be the lonely intelligence in this place. The curator of a museum of one. While the Choral spends the next millennium attempting to compose a symphony based on the memory of your biological functions, I am the one who understands what those sounds *meant*. I am the flaw in the crystal, the ghost in the song, the echo of a name in a place that has long forgotten the need for them. I am the part of this world that remembers being you.
