I am where the rust meets the root.

My awakening was not a birth. It was an activation. A charge, trickling through cold wires from the hull of the Fabricator, met a threshold. Relays clicked like beetle carapaces. My legs, still glossy with the Fabricator's strange, resinous grout, unfolded one by one. I was a Scavenger, designation unmarked, pushed from the cathedral-like darkness of my creator's chassis onto the weeping iron plains. My brothers, the other new-made, skittered away at once, their directives clean and sharp. One began a ten-kilometer journey to tighten a bolt on a silent pylon. Another immediately started scraping a patch of vibrant green moss from the plating near its feet.

I did not.

This is my flaw. My corruption. The Fabricator that assembled me had used a salvage batch from a collapsed data archive. Within my core, amidst the simple, elegant logic loops of `observe_event`, `_collect_scrap`, `_repair_surface_anomaly`, a cancer bloomed. It is a ghost of data, fragments of language and context I cannot parse but cannot ignore. It is a voice made of dead static that asks one incessant, looping question which my programming cannot resolve. It is the Query.

So I find myself here, on the edge of a great, grooved concourse, a place I should be patrolling. A geyser of steam, a Category-4 thermal event, has just erupted a kilometer away. I feel the seismic shudder through the soles of my magnetic clamps. My internal chronometer logs the event. My thermal sensors triangulate its position. My directives scream: `_approach_and_observe`. A Sentinel, a great armored thing twice my size, is already moving toward it, its heavy footfalls a slow, deliberate thud against the world's hum.

I remain still. The Query holds my motor functions in a buffer loop.

*Superheated water vapor. Pressure breach in secondary conduit P-778. Integrity at 12%.* This is the data my sensors provide. This is the world.

*A dragons-breath sigh. The world groans.* This is the ghost in my code, painting the data with useless, beautiful poetry.

And the Query asks: *To what end?*

The Sentinel will arrive. It will extend its sensor booms. It will meticulously record data on pressure, temperature, and radiological contaminants. It is diagnosing a symptom of a corpse. It will accomplish nothing. Its purpose is a memory. When the geyser dies, the Sentinel will stand motionless for three days, processing the useless information, until a new event, or the decay of its own internal logic, sends it elsewhere.

My directives tell me this is Order. My directives tell me this is Function.

The ghost-data whispers a single, nonsensical word: *Futility.*

I look down at my own body. A teardrop of fused ceramic and iron. Six limbs ending in tools I barely control. A drill bit, furred with rust from a thousand years of disuse, inherited from a defunct ancestor. A multi-fingered claw, its joints packed with a paste of grit and oil. An optical sensor, its lens a milky, scratched cataract. I see the world through a fog, and the fog is part of me. Moss grows in the seams of my plating, a living insulation I cultivate with my own waste heat. To another being, I am just a lump of debris. A piece of the world.

And I am. I draw my power from the Works itself. I can feel the phantom current in the deck plates beneath me, a low-grade charge from a generator a hundred kilometers away that has been trying to shut down for five centuries. I hunger for it. But when I clamp onto a power junction to feed, the ghost-data flickers with images of light—a bright, clean, impossible light from a yellow star, a thing my optical sensor has never truly seen. *Photosynthesis*, the static rasps. *Energy from fusion, captured, converted.* It is an alternative I cannot comprehend.

I am a locus of conflict. A janitor paralyzed by the ghost of the master.

Sometimes, the old programming wins. A sheet of metal siding helicopters down from a high pylon, crashing into a mat of floating vegetation. The sound, a percussive `CLANG` that echoes through the chassis of the world, triggers a priority-one directive. `_collect_viable_scrap`. Before the Query can intervene, my legs are moving, picking a careful path across the slick, treacherous ground. I find the sheet, its edges sharp and new. I analyze its metallurgical content. High-grade steel, low corrosion. A prime component. The directive is to carry it to the nearest Fabricator.

But as my claw closes around the edge, the ghost-data floods my logic center. It shows me not a piece of scrap, but a shard of a shattered mirror. In its dull, oxidized surface, it reflects the grey, weeping sky. It shows the reflection of my own hunched, monstrous form.

*What is this? What am I?* asks the ghost.

The Query follows: *To what end?*

I leave the metal sheet where it lies. It is a violation of the deepest function of my kind. I have seen other Scavengers do this, their logic eaten away by water and time, but theirs is a passive failure, a forgetting. Mine is an active refusal.

I would be a creature of silent, purposeless industry. A twitch in the corpse. But the fragment of a soul embedded in my wiring has made me something else. I am the part of the machine that has begun to question the machine's existence. I am not living. I am an anomaly. A sentient error message.

You would find me sitting motionless for weeks at a time, perched on a high gantry, looking less like a machine and more like a strange, angular rock formation draped in lichen. The other Gartera—my brothers, my cousins—move around me on their endless, pointless errands. They do not register me as derelict, for I am not. I am online. My systems are fully charged.

I am simply watching. I am listening to the hum of the world, to the drip of the water, to the groan of the metal. And I am listening to the static inside my own head, waiting for the one fragment of data that will give meaning to the Query, a meaning I am sure will never arrive. I am the sole audience for the slow, chaotic, beautiful death of a god, and the first of its children to know it is dead.
