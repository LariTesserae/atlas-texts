I would find myself on the Pipe.

It is my world. It is the only world. Above is the Weeping Ceiling—the underbelly of a great, black machine whose heart beats in a tone too high to feel properly, a vibration that lives in the tiny bones behind my head-dish. Below is the Choking Deep, the spongy, uncertain floor where the heavy things walk. I have never touched it. To touch it is to be swallowed. To be lost. My ground is the Pipe. It is a long, warm, circular god, and I am its acolyte.

I am a **Scale-Scraper**.

My body is long and low, like a weasel made of wire and gristle. My six legs are double-jointed, allowing me to brace my body in impossible geometries against the curve of the Pipe. My feet are not claws, not like the sky-hunters who shriek their presence into the steam. My feet are soft, grey pads, porous and warm. They secrete a metallic solvent, a mild acid that softens the millennia of corroded rust and chemical salt that cakes the Pipe. This softened scale is what I eat. My mouth is not a jaw, but a revolving disc of chitinous scrapers on my underside, midway down my body. To eat is to press myself flat against the warm metal and inch forward, my entire being consumed by the act of scouring. The taste is life. The sharp tang of iron-oxide, the bitter zing of copper sulfate, the faint, almost sweet trace of whatever flows within the Pipe, leaking its essence through the pitted shell.

Sight is a lie. Sound is a weapon. For me, the world is a symphony of heat and vibration. The Pipe is my instrument and my scripture. My head is a smooth, hardened dish of bone, connected to my spine by a column of jelly-like amplifiers. By pressing this dish to the Pipe, I feel everything. I feel the slow, eternal thrum-thrum-thrum of the Fluid, the lifeblood of the world, pushing through the arteries of the god I inhabit. This is the baseline, the silence. On top of it, I feel the skittering panic of the spore-mites that infest the fungal blooms growing in the sheltered joints—a secondary food source, a dusting of protein. I feel the distant, heavy *squelch-stomp… squelch-stomp* of a Groveler passing beneath, its weight a slow, dull pressure wave moving through the Deep below, a danger only if the Pipe itself sags. I feel the violent, sudden *crack* in the deep-mat as a Tremor-Stalker strikes, a jarring resonance that travels up the pylons and into my bones, a note of pure violence.

But my true sense, my art, is the reading of the Pipe itself. Each section has its own song. Hotter patches sing of strain, of the Fluid moving faster within. Cooler sections are sluggish, lazy, often coated in a thicker, less nutritious scale. I can feel a fissure long before it ruptures, a thin, high-frequency whine that warns of a coming steam-jet. These are the waterfalls of my world, places of instant death. I know the map of my territory not by shape, but by frequency. This elbow-joint here sings a steady baritone, seasoned with the frantic treble of a thriving mite colony. The long, straight section after it hums a clean, perfect tenor, but it is dangerously exposed to the hot, sulfurous rains.

We are not alone on the Pipes. I have my territory, a hundred body-lengths of humming iron between the Great Pylon and the Processor’s Weeping Vent. Other Scale-Scrapers have theirs. We communicate through the Pipe. A slow, rhythmic tapping of my head-dish against the metal is a sign of peace, of presence. A frantic, scraping chatter is a claim, a warning. Fights are rare. They are wrestling matches of leverage and grip, a shoving contest until one is dislodged and falls into the Choking Deep. None have ever returned.

The Echo-Weavers in the fungal forest above are a myth, a ghost story told in high-pitched clicks that sometimes filter down through the endless steam. The Grovelers below are a geological feature, a mobile terrain. The Stalkers are an earthquake. My reality is the Pipe, its warmth, its hum, its endless, perfect curve.

I do not know what the Pipe is for. I do not know what the great machine-heart above me processes. I have felt, on rare occasions, a change in the thrum. A deep, slow shudder that travels the entire length of the world, a signal that comes from the machine itself. On these days, the scale tastes different. Richer. Energized. It is on these days that I will find a sheltered place, a hollow beneath a support strut, and lay my soft, gelatinous eggs, sealing them to the Pipe with a paste of half-digested scale. They will feel the hum. They will gestate in the warmth. They will hatch and their first meal will be the oxidized skin of their god.

They are called The Still, the ones who live inside the machine. We have no name for them. They are simply The Great Quiet, the source of the hum. I am a part of their body, a flake of skin, a single cell in a system whose purpose is beyond my comprehension. My existence is to scour, to feel, to crawl. And when my grip fails, or I misread the song of a steam-fissure, I will fall. My body will be a brief, soft punctuation in the Deep, food for the dark things below. But my small stretch of the Pipe will be clean. The Fluid will flow. The hum will continue. In this world of terrible, vibrant force, that is my quiet, sacred function.
