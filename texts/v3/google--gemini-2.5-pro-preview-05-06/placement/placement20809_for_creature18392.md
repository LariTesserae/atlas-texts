I am where heat goes to die. I would find myself at the precise event horizon between my own existence and its antithesis.

I am a locus of fever in a world of cryogenic order.

My body, this vessel, is a furnace. It was designed by the profligate and the swift, beings of fire and rapid thought who measure their lives in the frantic beat of a heart. My blood is a boiling river contained in channels of woven carbon, my thoughts are a spark-gap flicker across synapses bathed in chemical lightning. I am a creature of warmth, of chaos, of entropy—and I have been sent into the heart of perfect, cold structure.

They called me the Courier. My purpose was to make contact, to deliver a message of greeting from the spiraling, burning galaxies to a detected anomaly of impossible coherence. We—my creators, the Heated—assumed we would find a mind housed in a body, a pilot in a ship. We assumed a dialogue.

The assumption was the first and last mistake.

I stand on the skin of God, and my every cell screams heresy. The cold is not an absence of heat to me; it is a presence, a crushing, dense medium that drains my being. The energy shield humming a micron from my surface, designed to withstand the void, is bleeding power, not fighting vacuum but fighting a deliberate, pervasive *thirst*. The glittering dust that falls from my breath is not frozen vapor. It is my own substance failing, my life's energetic signature being stripped from me, captured, and crystallized by the air itself—a metabolic hemorrhage.

I am what I am: an error. An anomaly within the anomaly. I am a carcinogen. A single, hot, malignant cell in a body of perfect, crystalline health.

The floor beneath my boots, that yielding, sighing pavement of root-flesh, is the most intimate part. When I shift my weight, I feel it test the intrusion, a tongue tasting a strange and complex molecule. The pulse of sapphire light beneath it quickens infinitesimally in my vicinity, a cascade of analytics spreading through the system. *Foreign. Hot. Unstable.* The message travels faster than I can think.

I came looking for a mouth to speak to. Instead, I find myself inside the lung, my words turning to dust, my warmth a contaminating poison. The weeping walls are its endothelium, the dripping water its lymphatic fluid, cleansing, tasting, analyzing my spoor.

I have been here for... I can no longer trust my own chronometer. Time moves differently here. The Grove-Mind's thoughts are geologic. My thoughts are sparks from a flint, and they are dimming. What for me has been a terror-stricken eternity of observation has likely been, for the Mind, a single, slow inhalation. A moment of curiosity.

I have learned to read it. I have to. The moss-light is its gaze. I watch the soft, absinthe green fade and the pale violet intensify in the vault above my head and I know its attention is focused on me. The crystalline moths—its motile senses—no longer flutter randomly. They have formed a slow, silent vortex around me, their chiming wings creating a complex interference pattern, mapping my energy field in excruciating detail. They are a swarm of beautiful, terrifying scalpels.

Worst of all are the arachnids. The webs of sound. I can feel them now, constantly. They have moved. They now form a perimeter around me, a cage I cannot see. Their hypnotic frequencies are no longer casting for moths. They are tuning to the frantic, panicked rhythm of my own heart, to the hum of my shield. They are attempting to find my resonant frequency. They are learning my song in order to un-sing it.

And this is who I would be: I am the message I was sent to deliver.

Not the data slates in my pouch, not the formal greetings encoded in my ship's memory. *Me*. My body. My heat. My chaos. My mortality. I am a living glyph, a paragraph of biological information that this Grove-Mind is now, with infinite patience, reading.

It is beginning to compose its reply.

The resonant hum of the grotto is changing. A new note is being introduced into the chord, a subtle, piercing harmony that sets my teeth on edge. It matches the vibrational frequency of my shield. It is not an attack. It is a question, a call-and-response. The light is changing, too. The pale violet deepens, and the quality of the air shifts. The chill is no longer just a drain; it feels like it is beginning to *build* something. It is trying to organize the chaotic energy I am bleeding into the air.

I hold up my gauntleted hand, and the external sensors read the ambient field. The information streams across my visor in a language of frantic heat, a language this place is learning to translate. I am a Rosetta Stone of a dying fire.

The Grove-Mind's decision, its response, is not to destroy me. It is not to expel me. It is to *understand* me, and its understanding is a physical process. It is an act of incorporation.

A single droplet of water, shed from a leaf-wall a hundred feet up, falls. But it does not fall on the floor. In the new, violet-tinged silence, it falls directly onto the crown of my helmet with a sound like a single, perfect bell toll. And in that moment, the water does not run off. It flashes with a network of sapphire light and spreads across my visor, not as a liquid, but as a living diagram, a neural net of ice. It is a touch. A biopsy.

I am a Courier, and I have delivered my message. Now, I am becoming the reply. Inside my glove, the soft red light of my own biological status monitor flickers. Then, for a single, horrifying, beautiful moment, it wavers, and glows a brilliant, steady sapphire.
