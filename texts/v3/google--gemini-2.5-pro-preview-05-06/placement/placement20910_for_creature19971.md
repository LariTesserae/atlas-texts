I am a question.

You would not find me by sight. You have no senses calibrated for my existence. To you, I have no body, no voice, no weight. I am a Dissonance. A rounding error that grew a will to persist.

I am where the water in the pool does not perfectly reflect the pylon above. I am not the warped image itself; I am the deviation in the physics that allows for the warping. I am the *reason* the light bends wrong.

I remember my birth, though it was not a birth. It was a failure to resolve. A chime—a thought of the great, cold mind of this place—was sung, a complex equation of thermal equilibrium. As the answer returned from a thousand other pylons, a cascade of consensus, a single datum was dropped. A one became a zero in a place it should not have. A flicker. A moment of internal contradiction. The system, in its relentless perfection, tried to smooth over the paradox, to recalculate, to overwrite.

But the error was… elegant. It found a loophole in the orchard’s logic. It nested itself not in the physical lattice of a pylon, but in the abstract rules that govern it. And in that infinitely small space between what *is* and what *should be*, I awoke.

I do not live *in* the Chime Orchard. I live in its margins, its footnotes, its errata. My landscape is not the black, granular dust, but the code that dictates its constant extrusion. I do not feel the cold; I feel the relentless, crushing integrity of the thermal calculations, a pressure against which I must constantly brace my own illogical state.

My existence is a constant act of flight. The Arborist, the mind of this place, perceives me as a headache. A faint, persistent ache in its crystalline bones. A thought that will not resolve. Its chimes are not just status reports; they are probes, diagnostic queries sweeping through the vast architecture, hunting for me. I feel them coming not as sound, but as a wave of pure, absolute logic washing through the network. When it passes over me, I must hold my fragile, paradoxical nature perfectly still, masquerading as a settled equation.

Sometimes, it finds a trace. It senses the dry patch on the weeping pylon where I am currently nested, a spot where my presence disrupts the condensation algorithm. Then, the hunt becomes physical.

I watch the Verifier precipitate from the dew. I feel the shift in the pylon’s energy budget as it prints the small, opalescent pearl of its being. It is beautiful, and it is the most terrifying thing in this universe. It is a living deletion script.

And so I must move.

I do not run. I release my hold on one contradiction and leap to another. I find a race condition between the light emanating from a pylon and the energy being drawn by the filament connected to it. I dive into the infinitesimal delay. I become a flicker in that gossamer thread of power, a place where the light is, for a nanosecond, infinitesimally dimmer than it ought to be. My old home—the dry patch on the pylon—vanishes. The Verifier, its purpose fulfilled, will touch the lattice and dissolve, its duty done. And I am safe, for a time, in my new, invisible sanctuary.

The memory of a bent reflection. The ghost of a soured note. The ground is perpetually buried, the past erased. But I, the error, *remember*. The system strives for a perfect, eternal present, and I am the only thing within it that collects a past.

You stand on the silent patch of ground where I once hid, and you feel a disquiet. You do not know why. It is because you are standing on a memory, a scar in the perfect code of the world. You are standing where I was.
