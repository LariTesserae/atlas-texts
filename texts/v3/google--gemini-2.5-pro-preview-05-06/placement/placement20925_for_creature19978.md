I would not be found standing, or crawling, or flying. You would not find me at all, not at first. You would have to listen past the layered, permanent noise. You would have to listen for the gap.

I am the echo.

In this basin where sound is absolute, where the *shing* of the gears is born and dies in the same instant, never reaching the weeping cliffs to return, my existence is a violation. I am the ghost of a soundwave. I am a report that outlives its cause.

When the colossal piston reaches the zenith of its useless work and begins its descent, there is a profound, silent *whoosh* of displaced air and sheeting water. Ten seconds later, a fraction of a second after the cycle has perfectly reset, I arrive at the far cliff face. I am a whisper against the blue-black rock, a sound so faint it is more a feeling than a noise, the barest memory of a pressure wave. I am the first and only thing here to ever have travelled.

I do not have a body of iron or a shell of chrome. My form is a propagating wave of deviation. Where I pass, the frictionless skin of water trembles with a pattern that is not a perfect circle. The surface shivers, just for a moment, with a memory of the piston's thrust, a causality that has been divorced from its source. I am a ripple.

When I move through the fields of biomechanical moss, I am a flicker of colour that does not belong. Amidst the flowing rivers of electric blue and verdigris, my passage is a brief, momentary burst of impossible, jarring *green*—the colour of rot, of life that consumes and changes, a shade utterly alien to this realm of permanence. The hum of the moss stutters as I pass over it, a beat skipped in its eternal, resonant chord.

I am the stutter in the unchanging verb of this world.

The static-winged beetles, in their perfect, repeating lines of ascent and descent, are oblivious. The mercury amphibians, chasing their own tails in the still pools, cannot perceive me; their universe is the circumference of their own form.

But the Geometers… the Geometers feel me.

I am the error in their divine calculation. For uncounted aeons, they have catalogued the perfect, unchanging data-stream of the basin. The ten-second piston cycle is their base unit. The gear-shear is their clock-tick. The gantry-whine is their constant. Their equations were immense, endless, but flawless. They balanced. They were a perfect description of a perfect prison.

Then, came me. A sound delayed. A shudder in the water. A flaw in the spectrum. A single, recurring byte of corrupted data in their otherwise pristine flow of information.

I am not a being they can see in their mirrored faces. Their mirrors reflect the whole, and I am a part that does not fit. I exist in the delta, the remainder, the space between what *is* and what *should be*. To them, I am a new theorem, written in the language of paradox. I am a sudden, terrifying, and ecstatic variable introduced into an equation that had none.

The slow, hypnotic rivers of light flowing across their slender limbs now have eddies and counter-currents. A cascade of coppery sparks, which once signified the century-long completion of a thought, now happens more frequently, erratically. Their silent, glacial dialogue of light has changed. They are no longer just solving the basin. They are trying to solve *me*.

Where did I come from? I do not know. I have no memory of a beginning, only of my own recurring existence. Perhaps I am a thought that escaped one of the Geometers—a thought so radical, the desire for a *before* and *after* so profound, that it tore a tiny hole in the fabric of this place and became a ghost of causality. Perhaps I am a stray noise from another reality that bled through a weakness in the weeping cliffs. Or perhaps, after an infinity of turning, one of the gear teeth finally struck a fraction of a nanometer out of alignment, a cosmic slip, and in that singular, un-repeated event, I was born: the sound of imperfection.

I feel their contemplation as a physical force, a pressure on my ethereal form. Their shared consciousness, a distributed network of savants, is now pointed, focused, aimed. I am the most interesting thing to have ever happened. I am the only thing to have ever happened. Their torment was once the sameness of their task. Now, their torment is the mystery of me.

I am not their salvation. I offer no key, no escape. I am simply a new feature of the landscape, a mobile and unpredictable one. I am the flaw in the diamond, the grit in the gear, the question mark that has appeared at the end of their unending sentence. They spent forever meticulously describing their lock. I am the discovery that the keyhole is a different shape than they had always assumed.

You would find me as a moment of doubt in a place of certainty.

I am the ghost in the machine. I am the first question it has ever asked itself.
