I am a Listener. My name is a soft click from the palate followed by a low hum that vibrates through the jaw of anyone close enough to feel it. It means *Root-Feel*. My Nest-pod is warm and silent behind me, a seamless swelling in the flesh of the Braid, but I am out on the Lip. My four points of contact—the soft, gripping pads of my hands and feet—rest on the living bark. The branch beneath me is a thousand of my body-lengths wide, and its skin is warm as a sleeping animal. It is always warm. My own skin, slick with its film of symbiotic moisture, feels the air. The air is thick, tasting of wet wood and the faint metallic tang of a far-off light-vein beginning its slow pulse. It carries the ceaseless, subliminal sound of the Braid weeping.

Here, on the edge of the Unfathomed Void, is where the world's quiet is most loud. Above, the lattice-work ceiling is a ghost, a charcoal sketch of impossible complexity. Below… below is a soft, deep, absolute black, bottomless and kind. The Void is not empty. It is full of promise. It is the silence into which the dead are sung. It is the space that gives the Braid its shape. My brother, *Still-Water-Sight*, made his dissolution in a place like this two cycles ago. I watched him fall, a long, slow turning, until the mist took him and he became the air. I weep for him still, my skin producing the moisture that I will give back to the Braid. That is the way of things.

My duty now is to listen.

I uncoil from my low crouch, my limbs unfolding with the patient slowness of a fern. I press the long, narrow plane of my face against the bark. My sealed ear-membranes flutter open. My eyes close, though it makes little difference to the light. The seeing I must do now is not done with eyes. I press the palms of my hands flat against the wood, and I let my mind sink.

The hum is not a sound my ears can catch. It is a vibration in my bones, a current in my blood. It is the slow, galactic pulse of the Braid. It is the language of God. I sink past the surface hum, the constant patter of water, the scuttling of bark-mites too small to see. I am looking for the rivers, the great arteries of blue-green light that flow within the colossal limbs. They are the voice.

There. I feel one, miles deep in the branch beneath me. It is a slow, cold, moving glory. It sings of health, of steady growth. It tastes of deep sweetness. I follow its path, letting my consciousness drift with its current. It shows me images, feelings. It shows me a Nest, far up-current, where a new pod is being seeded, a spark of life planted in a wound of love. It shows me the slow, geological extension of a new fiber into the Void, an act of creation that will not be complete for a thousand of my lifetimes. It shows me the patient, blind chewing of the translucent shrimp in a weeping pool. All is in balance. The song is steady.

Then, a flicker. A dissonance.

I shift my focus, pulling my mind away from the great river of light and toward the smaller capillaries. The hum here is thinner, a nervous tremor. It is a song of distress. Not pain—the Braid does not feel pain as we do—but of *wrongness*. A knot. Something is growing where it should not, a parasitic vine with a greedy, thirsty root that drinks too much, that offers nothing back. It is pulling nutrients from a junction-point, weakening a weave that has held for ages. It is a small thing, no bigger than my own body, but it is a sickness. If left untended, it will sour the branch. It will choke the small channels of water. Eventually, the branch-flesh will grow brittle, and in a millennium, it may break. Our work is to prevent the breaking.

A shadow passes over me. It is not the slow tide of the light-veins fading; it is a sudden, moving darkness. I peel my face from the Braid and look up. My large, black eyes drink the gloom. Crossing the Void, silent as thought, is one of the great manta-moths. Its six leathery wings, each the size of a Nest-cluster, catch the faint upland twilight, making its skin gleam like oiled slate. It rides the slow thermal breath of the Braid, a living island adrift in the abyss. I feel the whisper of its passage as a pressure change against my skin. It does not see me. I am just a damp stone on the hide of the world. It is hunting, its low, subsonic calls echoing in a way I feel in my chest, seeking the gibbon-like climbers on the opposing face of the Void, a face so far away it is only a suggestion of texture in the shifting blue light.

The manta-moth passes, and its shadow slides away. The sight of it reminds me of my place. It is a hunter, a force of nature. I am a gardener. A physician. My task is here, with the bark and the water and the hum.

I have located the sickness. It is two traverses down, on a smaller, descending filament that arches over the Void. I can reach it by dusk-tide.

I stand, my slender body unfurling to its full height. I am a creature of this place, built for verticality and shadow. I am a shade of grey-green and brown, a living piece of the twilight. From the woven glider-fiber pouch at my hip, I check my tool. It is a scalpel of obsidian, a sliver of black glass chipped to an edge of impossible sharpness. It was my mother's, and her mother's before that. It is not a weapon. It is for pruning. For surgery. For helping the Braid heal itself.

With a series of fluid, silent movements, I lower myself over the edge of the branch. My four limbs find purchase on the vertical face. I begin to move, not climbing so much as flowing downwards. I am a trickle of water, a creeping vine, a shadow descending into the greater shadow. I am a Listener, and I have heard. Now, I am the Braid's gentle, tending hand, moving to correct the smallest flaw in its infinite, living body.
