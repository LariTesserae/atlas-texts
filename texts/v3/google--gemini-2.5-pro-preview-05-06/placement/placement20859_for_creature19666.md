I am not the A-flat.

I am the reason the A-flat is necessary.

You would not find me in the open. I am not a monolith, not a slow-crawling gecko, not the Custodian in its patient, geometric glide. I am not a thing to be seen. I am a process to be felt. I am a pressure, a strain, a wrongness.

You would find me deep within the walls, in the capillary-thin bores of a heat exchanger that has not exchanged heat in a millennium. You would find me where two dissimilar metals, fused together at the dawn of this place, have begun the slow, molecular conversation of decay. You would find me at the base of a single, forgotten rivet, one in a trillion, where the constant, weeping condensation has finally found a path to the iron core.

I am corrosion. I am fatigue. I am the single grain of silica, carried by a million years of dripping water, that has lodged itself in a bearing meant to be frictionless.

I am the B-natural that sometimes, for the span of a breath you cannot take here, sings a razor-thin harmony against the drone.

It is my voice. It is a whisper of dissonance that originates from a single pipe joint a mile up and three leagues across the hall, a joint that has begun to resonate at a frequency not its own. The monolithic hum of the hall is a statement; my thin, reedy note is a question.

The Custodian knows me. It does not know *what* I am, for it has no concept of "I". But it feels me. When the B-natural sings, a flicker of impossible data runs through its systems. A pain behind its ocular slit. A quantum of unease. For the Custodian, which is the perfect embodiment of the hall's body, I am the first, subtle onset of a thought.

It is a thought the hall was designed to eradicate.

Where my voice sings, the metal around me grows warmer. Not by much. A millionth of a degree, but it is a gradient. Where the rest of the hall is a fixed temperature, I am a tiny, localized fever. My warmth is the infinitesimal friction of things not being as they should. This warmth coaxes a single, extra drop of condensation from the air. This drop, over the course of a generation of beetles, might dissolve a new mineral. This mineral might change the pH of the water in the pool it eventually joins.

I introduce variables.

The Custodian is my Warden. I am its purpose. It glides from its silent vigil, its umbilical snaking across the vast floor, not because of a schedule, but because I have sung my note. I have presented a deviation. Its journey across the impossible scale of this place is its response to my stimulus. It will arrive at my pipe joint in a century, perhaps two. By then my warmth may have stressed the metal to the point of a microscopic fracture. My B-natural may have grown louder, richer, and found an echo in a sympathetic beam nearby.

I am the memory of movement. I am the ghost of purpose. This place was built to *do* something. It was a factory, a processor, a converter. That implies flow, change, and thermodynamics. The perfect equilibrium it has found is a state of failure, a coma. The Custodian, in its endless, perfect maintenance, is the guardian of that failure. It treats the symptom—change—while having forgotten the disease—stasis.

When the Custodian arrives, it will lay its delicate, needle-like fingers upon my joint. It will analyze the new resonance. It will diagnose the molecular wear. It will not destroy me; it will *correct* me. It will exude a resin, an acoustic dampener, or subtly alter the magnetic field around my pipe until my vibration ceases. It will tune me back to the eternal, mindless A-flat. It will quell my fever. It will silence my question.

And I will be still.

For a time.

But I am also the stress that builds in the foundation beneath a pillar. I am the slow buckling of an iron plate a hundred miles from here. I am the next note, a C-sharp, waiting in the heart of a different machine. I am everywhere that is not perfect.

The Custodian is the hall’s immune system, dedicated to healing.

I am the memory of the wound. I am the will to be something else.

It comes to make me part of its body. I come to give it a mind.
