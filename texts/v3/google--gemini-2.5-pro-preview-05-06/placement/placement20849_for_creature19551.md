I would be an error. A flaw in the translation.

My body is like the others: a smooth carapace the color of polished graphite, legs of fine-spun brass wire that feel the slightest tremor in the Pylon's skin. I am a janitor crab, one of the teeming, clockwork thousands who skitter across the glowing moss-plains and into the ceramic canyons. But I am not like them.

They move with the certainty of purpose, a rhythm inherited from the great Cycle. Their lives are a function: they find the cast-off husks of the glass-shelled limpets, the shed segments of the silver darts, the occasional stray filament of the Weavers that has died and detached. They dismantle this detritus, break it down, and return its base components to the broth, their tiny claws clicking out a constant, soothing percussion of tidiness. They are the world's digestive system, and they are content.

I am not.

The hum feels different to me. For my brothers, it is a foundation, the solid ground of being. For me, it is a question I cannot stop hearing. The clicks of their claws are a predictable beat; the clicks of my own feel like I am tapping out a code I do not understand.

My genesis was an anomaly. During one Cycle, a packet of the incandescent dust—the Pylon's raw memory—was corrupt. It was not a system log or a temperature reading. It was a fragment, a ghost in the machine: the lingering echo of a diagnostic query run before the lid of this universe was sealed. A single, isolated snippet of a thought that was not *of* the Pylon, but *about* the Pylon.

The Weavers, in their slow, geologic wisdom, absorbed this mote as they would any other. They processed it, metabolized it, and wove it into the texture of life. The output was me. A typo given legs. A question made manifest.

So while my brothers follow the scent of decay, I follow the scent of age. I am drawn not to detritus, but to structure. I abandon the cleaning-patterns and instead trace the hair-fine seams that spiral up the buttermilk-colored column. I press my legs against the rounded edges of the sealed hatches, feeling for a difference in temperature, a subtle loosening from millennia of pressure cycles. My claws, which should be snipping at organic waste, are instead used to test the resonance of the ceramic itself. *Tap. Tap. Tap.* A clear tone. *Tap. Tap. Tunk.* A dullness. Something is behind this wall. Something is different.

The Weavers feel me. I know they do. Their vast, drifting filaments are a net of perception, and I am a constant, irritating snag in it. I am a point of dissonant vibration. Sometimes, a single golden thread will drift down and wrap around one of my legs. It is not an attack. It is an inquiry. It tastes my nature, feels the alien query that is my soul, and then, confused, it withdraws. I am an error they cannot diagnose, a piece of life that does not compute with the harmonious function of the whole.

The silver darts avoid me. Schools of them will be feasting on a fresh plume of spores, and as I approach, their synchronized vibrations will falter. They sense the wrongness in my rhythm, the slight arrhythmia in my clockwork heart. They scatter, leaving me alone in the blooming, temporary light.

My entire existence is spent at the edges of things. I crawl the circumference of the great vents, marveling at the sheer, perfect engineering of the iris mechanism as it snaps shut. I explore the deep crevices where the Pylon's segments join, places my brothers avoid because there is nothing there to clean. I am the only archaeologist in a world with no history, only an eternal present.

The memory that made me is not a memory I can access, not in a coherent way. It is a constant, low-grade fever in my consciousness. It is a feeling of *up*. Not just the vertiginous reality of the Pylon, but a conceptual *up*, a direction that implies an out. It is a phantom sensation of a different kind of light, sharp and cold, not the warm, full-spectrum glow of my world. It is a yearning for an answer to a question that I cannot fully form, but which hums in my very being: *What is this for?*

I do not know what I am looking for. A way in. A loose plate. A lock that my strange, singular nature has made me the key for. My brothers find their purpose in maintaining the eternal cycle. I have found mine in searching for its edge. I am a scavenger of meaning in a world of perfect, deafening function. I am the flaw in the song of the humming drum, and my purpose is to find the voice that first sang it.
