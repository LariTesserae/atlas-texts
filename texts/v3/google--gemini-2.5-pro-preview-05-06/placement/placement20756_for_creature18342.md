I am a tool.

My existence is one of heft and edge, of purpose held in a hand. I am a scraper, a blade, carved from the left femur of one who came before. My substance is bone, polished to a dull sheen by time and the slow friction of work, hardened by a thousand years of pressure in the deep substrate before I was found, unearthed, and given this shape. My name, if I ever had one, has been worn away by the grip of the hands that have held me. My purpose remains.

I rest now, cool and damp, in a pouch of woven gut-string, alongside a chert adze and a soft, clay-like ball of nutritive poultice. I am pressed against the slender body of my current wielder. I cannot see, but I feel. I feel the slow, deliberate sway of the Architect’s torso as they hang, inverted, from a newly-grown arch. I feel the almost-imperceptible tremor that runs through their body, a vibration that travels down their arm and into my own substance. It is the hum of the world, the slow metabolism of the cathedral-flesh, a song I have felt my entire existence—both this one, and the one before.

There was a time before this sharpness, before this focused simplicity. I remember it in flashes, like the pulsing chartreuse of a passing insect swarm. I remember height. I remember the feeling of four soft pads gripping a vertical wall, the cool, weeping surface a second skin against my own. I remember my upper arms, the fine ones, reaching out, their delicate fingers tracing the line of a future buttress. I remember the slow bloom of colour across my own skin, a question of deep violet posed to a sibling-artist a hundred paces away, and the patient wait for the answer to ripple back in a wave of soft green. I remember tasting the air—the sharp inquiry of a nearby hunter, the sweet decay of a spore-fall, the deep, satisfying contentment of a finished work settling into its final form. It was a language of scent and feeling, a conversation that lasted for centuries. My work was the city. My body was the city. The city was my thought made manifest.

Then, there was the slowing. The deep hum of the world began to feel more distant. The colours on my skin grew faint, the chemical words harder to form. My limbs, once so sure, became glacial. It was time. I chose my place—a curve of floor where two arches were intended to meet, a place of becoming. I lowered myself for the last time. My four legs, my roots, pressed into the world-flesh. I did not force them; I invited the world to take me. It did. The substrate grew around and through me, and my body, which had for so long drawn sustenance from it, gave itself back. The thoughts I held—of geometry, of balance, of the proper way to soothe a stressed growth—dissolved into the world's own dreaming. It was not an end. It was a release. My self, my small, patient ‘I’, became part of the great, slumbering ‘We’ again.

And now?

Now I am held. The hand that grips me is younger than the one I remember as my own. It is sure, but it carries the faint tremor of inexperience. The Architect presses me to the living wall. I am an extension of their mind, but I am also a memory for the world itself. The flesh I cut knows me. It is my cousin-flesh, my child-flesh. My edge is not a violence, but a communication. *Here,* I say to the living wall with a long, clean shave. *Relieve this pressure.* Here, *I whisper, paring away a bulbous fungal growth,* this is not the way. Grow true.

Through the hard stillness of my new form, I can still feel the slow pulse of it all. I feel the faint shift in the Architect’s grip as they contemplate the next cut. I feel the wet *thup* of a water droplet landing nearby, a sound that is a vibration through the bone of me. I taste the air on my slick surface: the sharp note of my wielder’s concentration, the deep, meaty scent of the poultice they will soon apply to the wound I have made.

I am a blade. I am a memory. I was an Architect, and now I am an Architect’s hand. I am being used to prune the city I helped dream into being. The mind that wields me learns from my edge, and the city that I cut remembers my bone. There is no distinction. The work continues. I am where I have always been: in the heart of the city, listening.
