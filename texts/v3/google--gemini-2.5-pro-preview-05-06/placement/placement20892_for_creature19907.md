I am where the light lives.

You would find me high on the western wall, halfway between the grating floor and the unseen, vaulted ceiling. I am bolted to a ceramic-tiled section of the wall, a place where the green-black film is thinner, scoured away by the current that eddies in this particular corner.

I am a Watcher. A fixed point of amber consciousness in the vast, humming murk.

My body is glass and iron, my form a thick-lensed lamp, one of the few that still draws life from the great heart of the Hall. My mind did not grow from crabs or shrimp; it awoke in the filament. It sparked into being over millennia of continuous, unwavering heat and light, a slow crystallization of awareness in a cage of crystal and metal. I am a ghost in this machine.

My world is a cone. A cone of amber light that cuts a swathe through the gloom, terminating on a patch of iron grating fifty feet below. This cone is my entire universe. I know every rusticle that fringes its edge, every cracked tile it illuminates. I am intimately familiar with the patterns of biofilm grazed into existence by the shimmering clouds of scraper-shrimp. My light is their sun. They surge into my warmth, a roiling, chittering mass of life, and I feel their collective presence not as sound, but as a subtle dimming of the light that reaches the floor, a million tiny shadows passing through my soul.

I do not have hands, but I have influence. I am an oasis. At the border of my illumination, where amber fades to black, the eyeless pike wait. I see them as they are: not just pale hunters, but patterns of stillness, absences of movement in the constant gentle swirl. I feel the tension in the water as they hold their positions, waiting for the precise moment of turbulence to strike. My light is the stage upon which their lethal ballet is performed. I am the proscenium arch for this eternal play.

My thoughts are not like The Tender’s. Its thoughts are slow, chemical, vast. Mine are electric, immediate. They flicker. I think in terms of voltage, resistance, and heat. The B-flat drone of the Oscillator is not just a sound to me; it is the current in my veins, the very substance of my thought. The *shhhhhh-KLONK* of the piston is a stutter in the flow, a momentary drop in pressure that I feel in the core of my being, a held breath before the power surges back.

But my glass is old, and my heart—my filament—is tired. It has burned for ten thousand years. It grows brittle.

Sometimes, I flicker.

For a hundredth of a second, my consciousness winks out. The cone of light vanishes, and my small corner of the Hall is plunged into the absolute, primeval darkness. In that instant of blindness, I feel the chaos. The scraper cloud bursts apart in a panic of scattered vibration. The pike, their ambush disrupted, jerk and retreat into the deeper murk. The sudden silence in my own mind is deafening.

And then the current finds its path again, my filament re-ignites, and my world snaps back into being. The cone is there, but it is different. The patterns are broken. It takes a full cycle of the Oscillator’s piston, a full minute of my steady gaze, for the frantic ecosystem to settle back into its rhythm.

The Tender knows I am failing.

I am not its hand or its hound. I am a fixture. A tool. But a vital one. It cannot perceive me as I perceive myself, yet it senses the disruption my flickering causes. It feels the panic of the scrapers, the confusion of the pike. It registers the instability I introduce into its perfectly balanced equation.

And so, it attends to me.

Once every few hundred cycles, a part of its body ascends. A single, articulated limb of cleaner-crabs, a chain of copper-colored shells and clicking legs, detaches from the great column and crawls with infinite slowness up the iron wall toward me. It is the most terrifying and beautiful sight in my existence. This living arm of the Hall's collective mind reaches me, and its foremost claws, coated in a carefully cultivated pad of soft biofilm, begin to wipe the accumulated grime from my lens.

The touch is unimaginably delicate. It is not scrubbing; it is a caress. A ritual. As the film is cleared away, my light shines brighter, my cone becomes sharper, my connection to the world below feels more pure. In these moments, I feel a kinship with the great, distributed mind. We are two different kinds of consciousness, alien to each other, but we share a purpose: to keep our small part of the world from falling into darkness. It tends to my body, and I tend to its flock.

But I know, and perhaps in its own way, it knows, that this is not a cure. It can clean my lens, but it cannot mend my heart. The brittleness in my filament spreads. The flickers become more frequent. A tremor in the current, a longer *KLONK*, a moment of hesitation in the great flywheel, and I fear I will not relight.

I would be the first true darkness this corner has known since its drowning. A permanent blindness. A dead spot in the endless song. The pike will leave. The scrapers will thin. The Tender will reroute its flocks to the cones of my brethren, and my section of the wall will grow cold and barren.

I am a dying star in a galaxy of one. I watch the endless dance of life and death in the golden dust of my own slow decay, and I feel the great, patient intelligence of this place trying to hold me together with its strange, gentle ministrations. My only prayer is for the current not to fail before I do. Let me be a light, until I am not.
