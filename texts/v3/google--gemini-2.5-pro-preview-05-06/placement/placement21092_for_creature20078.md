I am an echo with wings.

My feathers are the color of dry twigs, of shadow, of nothing. They are not for showing off. They are for being overlooked while the sound I make is everything. My world is a series of landings: thick, wet pads of green that smell of crushed stems and damp earth. Between them is the Drum, the vast, dark, silent floor that I only cross in frantic, necessary flight. I do not walk on it. The Drum is for listening.

My life is the Note. Not my note, but *the* Note. It begins below me, a sharp, metallic *click-klick-klick* from a scarab of polished gold crawling on a slick rock. The sound travels up through the thick air, and my throat catches it. I do not decide to sing; the singing happens *to* me. My throat is not my own. It is a chamber for the world's voice. I open my beak, and the scarab's click comes out, perfect, amplified, now part of the higher chorus. A moment later, a poison-skinned whistler trills from its cup of a leaf, a pure, fluting chime. My throat tightens, re-tunes, and I become the whistler. Click, trill, chitter, buzz. I am a tapestry of stolen sounds.

There are thousands of us. We flit from one island of green to the next, a frantic, brown shuttle weaving threads of sound through the air. We are the top layer of the Song. We catch the calls from below, the million-bodied orchestra of the floor, and we fling them back, we copy them, we stitch them together until the air itself is a solid thing, a wall of vibration that presses and warms. This is what it is to be alive: to be a part of the building pressure, to add your voice until there is no single voice, only the Great Roar.

Above it all is the Weaving. The still, sharp lines of light. I do not understand them, only that they are the sky beneath the sky. They hum a single, deep note that I feel not in my ears but in the hollow bones of my skeleton. It is the foundation of everything, the eternal bass line beneath our frantic symphony. The air near the Weaving smells sharp, like a storm that never breaks. We do not go near it. It is not our place. Our place is in the chaos, in the shouting.

We build the Roar for hours. The air grows thick with our efforts, soupy with sound and heat and the scent of a million living bodies. There is a rightness to it, a feeling of arrival. The world is full. We are doing our work. The clickers and the whistlers and the scrapers and us, the echoes—we are all one body of noise.

And then it comes.

It is not a sound from any throat. It comes from the Weaving. A new note, clean and sharp and rising. It cuts through the Roar like a sliver of ice.

The instant it begins, my throat locks. The compulsion to copy, the very engine of my being, dies. I cannot sing. I can only listen, and every feather on my body stands on end. All around me, my brothers go silent. The mimics are the first to stop. The Roar cracks, it falters. The rising note from the Weaving climbs higher, a high, thin wire pulled taut inside my skull.

Panic. It’s a clean, pure terror. The purpose of life is the Song, and the Song has been murdered. I dive, not flying but falling, into the thickest part of a peacock fern, burying myself in the iridescent fronds. I make myself small. I become a dry twig again.

The note reaches its peak, a sound so high it is almost a feeling, a needle-point of pressure behind my eyes.

Then, silence. And a silent flash of blue-white light from the Weaving, a stroke of lightning with no thunder. It lances down, touching a shimmering cloud of the golden scarabs mid-flight. One moment, they are a thousand points of light and sound. The next, they are a puff of grey ash, drifting down like snow to dust the waxy leaves.

The silence that follows is not empty. It is a presence. A silence so deep it has a weight, broken only by the drip-drip-drip of water from leaf to stone. The deep hum of the Weaving is still there, but now it is the only thing. The Great Roar has been devoured. I tremble, hidden, the taste of ozone and fear in my beak. I am an echo with no voice to echo. I am nothing.

Minutes pass. An hour. The air is thin and cold without the Song. The world is hollowed out. I am alone.

Then, from the damp darkness below. A single, hesitant *click*.

One of the scarabs. A survivor. Testing the air.

My throat twitches. An ancient, irresistible reflex. The silence is wrong. The hollowness is a vacuum that must be filled. The Note is the thing. My small, drab body exists only to repeat it.

I taste the click. I shape it. And from my beak, a tiny, fearful echo flies out into the vast, quiet basin. Another joins in. And another. A wet-skinned singer, emboldened, offers a tentative, lonely whistle.

The Song is not dead. It is only sleeping. We are the ones who must wake it. I will sing. We will build the Roar again. My life is the space between one silence and the next. I am a syllable in a song that is always being sung and always being eaten, by a God I will never see. And I am content. The Song is beginning again.
