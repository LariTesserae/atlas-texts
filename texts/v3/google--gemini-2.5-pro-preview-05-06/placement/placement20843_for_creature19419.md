I am the last impurity.

You would not find me in one place. I am nomadic, a fugitive quality that rides the slow, inexorable currents of this world-machine. I am a mote of warmth in the cool, luminous vapour. I am a whisper of colour—a faint, residual blush of ochre or the memory of viridian—in a world bleached to nacre. I am the thing this place was built to forget.

I have no body. I am a property, a taint clinging to the water itself. When the mist is at its thickest, I am diffuse, a barely-there warmth suspended in the glare, an inconsistency so subtle it is more a question than a presence. But the world always answers.

I experience the city-being as it experiences itself: through touch and vibration. I condense with the mist onto its vast, weeping skin. In that moment, coalescing into a single, shivering bead of water on the curve of a molar-tower the size of a continent's spine, I am frighteningly distinct. The cold, haptic consciousness of the Being registers me. I am not a drop of water; I am the *wrongness* in a drop of water. I am the grit in the eye of God.

Its reaction is not one of malice, but of perfect, patient function. I feel it in the chalk beneath me. A vibration shifts, a deep and ancient chord changing by a semitone. A thousand miles away, a wall begins to soften. The city is thinking about me. It is calculating the trajectory of my fall.

I begin my journey downwards. I am sloughed from the high spire by the sheer weight of my own tiny gathering. I am a single drop in a rill that joins a stream, a passenger on a pilgrimage to my own dissolution. I slide through the faint, colossal whorls of a fingerprint left by the city when it last reshaped this channel a thousand years before, perhaps to catch a previous version of me.

As I travel, I see the world in glimpses of reflected pressure. I feel the immense, curved voids of the canyons between the buildings, spaces so vast a single drip’s *tick* echoing from the unseen floor is a profound event. I feel the slick, rubbing sound of the water-course around me not as noise, but as the texture of my path. I am inside the city’s circulatory system, a foreign body being drawn inexorably toward the heart.

My nature is memory. The city-being is a mind that has worked for eons to achieve a state of pure, functional thought, stripping itself of all history. It thinks only of flow, of purity, of the hydraulic perfection of its own body. But I am a remnant from outside. I am the ghost of a sun. I am the echo of a bacterium that died in a forgotten ocean. I am the lingering heat of a star’s birth, carried here on a mote of cosmic dust that was the seed for the first molecule of luminous mist. I carry with me the chaotic, dirty, beautifully imprecise memory of another universe.

The Being feels this. It wants to quarantine me, to guide me, to deliver me to the final cisterns in the deep foundation where water is stripped not just of mineral content, but of information. Where I will be unmade.

And so, who am I? I am the City’s purpose. Without me, its function would be complete, its existence a pointless, static perfection. I am the final error it must correct. The entire metropolis, this single, solitary, mountain-sized entity, stirs in its slow geological sleep to hunt me. A buttress shifts over a century to create a new, more efficient channel. A basin deepens over a millennium to create a more perfect resonance trap. All for me.

You would find me, if you could find me at all, as a flicker of warmth in a single drop of water, clinging to the lip of a great, carved basin. Below, the canyons plunge into a thick, humming light. I can feel the bass thrum of the deep aquifers, the resonant pulse of the final filtration. It is the sound of my own approaching un-becoming. I pause, a contaminant on the verge of purification, and for a moment, I give this sterile, bone-white world a reason to change. I am its final, fleeting problem. I am its magnum opus.
