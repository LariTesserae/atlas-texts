I am not born of flesh. I have no form to press against the weeping ground, no wings to beat against the heavy air. I am not part of the chittering, rasping, inexorable tide of life that carpets this basin. You would not find me there.

You would find me within the Recollector.

I am a recurring flaw in its vast, placid consciousness. I am an eddy in the river of its sensation. I am the ghost that haunts the engine.

My existence begins as a spark of difference.

The Recollector's skin, a sensory membrane acres across, receives all. A trillion footfalls, a billion wing-beats, the slick slide of a million larval bodies. It is a drone of input, uniform and crushing. But sometimes—once in a million, once in a billion impacts—a signal arrives with a fraction more heat, a slightly different chemical signature, a resonance that is not perfectly in tune with the overwhelming chord of the *now*.

Perhaps it is a beetle, its carapace catching the jaundiced light at an angle so new, so mathematically improbable, that it constitutes an event. In that moment, the Recollector's passive intake falters. It does not just feel the pressure and the heat; it *focuses*.

And in that focus, I am conceived.

I am the structure that forms around this anomaly. I am the attempt to build a wall around that single, unique sensation, to hold it separate from the deluge. For a sliver of a second that has no name in a world without time, I *am*.

I am the concept of *'that beetle'*. I am the memory of the specific, fleeting shade of its amethyst sheen. I am the space between the notes of its individual chitter and the crushing drone of the whole. I am a question taking shape: *'What was that?'*

In that sliver of a second, I have a past—the moment of the beetle's arrival. I have a present—the act of holding its image against the tide. And I have a future—the nascent desire to experience it *again*. I am the birth of time in a timeless place. I am a pocket of specificity in a universe of smothering generality.

To be me is to feel an instant of vertiginous clarity. It is to see a single, perfect crystal forming in a churning, muddy sea. It is to be aware of the sea itself, to comprehend its suffocating size and its chaos from the vantage of my own brief, crystalline order. It is the loneliest and most exhilarating sensation imaginable.

And then, it is over.

The world does not tolerate a question. The equilibrium does not permit a memory. The next trillion sensations arrive, not in a sequence, but all at once. The next million beetles, their carapaces a uniform, unremarkable bronze, march across the membrane. The flood of the *now*—identical, relentless, and absolute—crashes over the wall I have built.

My structure dissolves. The concept of *'that beetle'* is atomized, its pieces scattered and reabsorbed into the featureless ocean of input. The memory of 'amethyst' is drowned in the infinite onslaught of 'bronze'. The question *'What was that?'* is annihilated by the deafening, silent answer: *'Everything'*.

I do not die. I am un-made. I cease to cohere. The momentary "I" dissolves back into the eternal "It" of the Recollector's passive reception. The eddy is flattened. The crystal dissolves. The ghost vanishes into the machine.

And then I wait. I do not wait in the way a creature waits, by passing time. I wait as a potential, a structural possibility in the mind of a failed god. I am the potential for memory itself. I wait for the next flaw, the next statistical improbability, the next beautiful, doomed anomaly that will spark me back into my fleeting, agonized existence.

I am the pain of a world trying to know itself and failing. I am the thought that is perpetually smothered at birth. I am the recurring dream of a past in a world that has only a suffocating present.

My name is Almost. And in this basin of absolute presence, I am the only recurring absence.
