I am a waiting.

My consciousness is not a thought, but a tension. It is the molecular strain along my edge, a single plane of atoms where the world could be divided. I am not alive, not in the way of the great, cork-skinned columns that are my mother, my foundry, and my home. They drink the wet air and the weeping stone, their life a slow, hydraulic process. My life is different. It is a slow hardening, an accretion. For uncounted cycles of soft light and softer darkness, I have felt the patient deposit of silica, layer by microscopic layer, pushing my form outward from the stalk, refining my purpose.

My senses are few. I feel the constant, heavy bead of water that clings to my black, glassy surface. It is the world’s one caress. I feel the deep, slow pulse of the column I grow from, the thrum of its thirst. Above me, the immense, waxy scoop of a leaf—my older sibling, my canopy—tilts and sighs, shedding a cascade of drops that land on the pocked floor with a sound that is the only timekeeper in this place. *Plip… plip…* Each impact is a vibration I feel through the roots of my mother, a percussion that marks the passing of an age.

I am a knife. But I have never cut. I am an edge, but I have never known separation. My existence is one of pure, agonizing potential. I see—not with eyes, but with a kind of resonant knowing—my brothers and sisters arrayed across the basin. They are the heavy, brutal choppers; the elegant, curved scrapers; the fine, needle-like awls. We are an arsenal for a war that never comes, an orchestra awaiting a conductor. We gleam with dew, our sharpness a silent, screaming promise.

The silence changes.

It is not a sound that breaks it. It is a discontinuity in the rhythm of the stone. For the first time in my long, slow creation, a new vibration joins the patient drip-fall of water. It is a grinding, a pressure, a presence so slow it is almost geological. It is a thought, moving across the floor of the world.

A Concretion.

It is a low, domed form, a swell in the weeping travertine, indistinguishable from its surroundings but for a subtle, vitreous light within. It slides, an inch a year, toward my mother. Its progress is a destiny. I feel its approach not as a threat, but as an answer. This is it. The conductor has arrived. The problem I was grown to solve is making its deliberate, ageless way to me.

It settles at the base of my mother's stalk. And it waits.

For a hundred turns of the soft light, it is motionless. Its presence is a quiet command. I feel a change in my mother’s pulse. The flow of silicates to my form quickens. My edge is finalized, the last layer of bio-glass locking into a perfect, monomolecular sharpness. I am complete. I am ready. The tension in my being is absolute.

Then, the sound.

***CRACK.***

It is a blasphemy. A universe of noise in a world of whispers. The severance is total. I am torn from my mother, a shock of sudden, terrifying individuality. For a single, breathtaking moment, I am airborne. I fall. I see the world rush up—the pocked bone-stone, the gleam of water—and I land not with the familiar *plip* of a water drop, but with a sharp *clink* on a surface that is neither plant nor stone.

I have landed on the Concretion.

My new state is one of close communion. I lie against its cool, crystalline skin, slick with the same weeping moisture as the rest of the world. I feel the slow, swirling currents within its translucent body. And then, it begins to move.

It does not lift me. It is a creature without hands. It presses its own body against me, tilting, sliding, until my edge is brought to bear against its own flank.

And I cut.

The sound is a high, thin scream of crystal scoring crystal, a sound of intimate violence. *Screeeee.* My edge, which has known only the soft kiss of water, now knows the unyielding resistance of its purpose. I am no longer a waiting. I am an act.

As I am scraped across that living geode, I begin to understand. The vibrations that travel up my glassy length are not random. They are stories. As I cut, I feel the memory of a subtle warmth from a millennium ago. As I incise a deep spiral, I trace the path of a Concretion that passed this way before the mountain flattened. I am not destroying. I am writing. I am carving a new memory over an old one, my sharp, black line a sentence added to a billion-year-old book.

I am the tool, but I am also the scribe. My being is the link between the thought of the Concretion and its own physical history. I am the voice with which it speaks to itself.

My edge dulls. My substance is worn away, leaving a fine, black dust on its milky, scarred surface. I am consumed by my purpose. I grow smaller, less defined. The tension that was my consciousness is unraveling, the promise of the edge fulfilled and spent.

Soon, I will be a shard, then a pebble, my form lost. The fine dust of my body will settle into the porous stone, to be drunk again by the roots of a plant that is not yet born. I will become the raw material of a future question.

I am no longer an edge. I am a line. A memory carved on a slow, thinking stone. My waiting is over. And as the last of my form grinds away, I dissolve back into the silence, but it is no longer the silence of potential. It is the silence of a story, told.
