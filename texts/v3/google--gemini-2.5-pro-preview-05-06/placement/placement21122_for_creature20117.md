I would be a Scribe. But my time as a dancer in the silent city is ending.

I am a thought near its completion.

My rearmost pads rest on the living vellum. Through them, I feel the slow, planetary pulse, the hum of ten million billion lives metabolizing in unison. The warmth is a comfort, the very signature of being. It is the only mother I have ever known. My middle limbs are braced against the curve of a vast, horn-plate canopy, its surface slick and cool. Above me, the filtered, golden light is a constant, a presence more than an illumination, thick and heavy as honey. The air is taste. It is a complex broth of knowledge: the astringent tang of a nearby bladder-colony ripening, the chalky mineral scent of a spire-organism calcifying a new buttress, the faint, sweet-rot pheromone of a gelatinous browser dissolving its meal a thousand paces away.

I am old. My parchment-shell has lost its luster. Along the joints of my segments, the chitin is crazed with a network of fine lines, a map of my own long journey through the quiet. My forelimbs, my filaments, are an extension of my will. They are not trembling with age, but with purpose. They dance in their final configuration.

I hang, suspended between the ground and the low canopy, completing my last work. My body forms the final arc of a great glyph written upon the world's skin. For cycles beyond counting, I have been a part of this inscription. My task was to record the Resonance of the Long Waning—a subtle shift in the city's growth, a slow, generational cooling in a quadrant of the western spires. Other Scribes began it, their fossilized forms now part of the very ground that holds the beginning of this history. I am the final punctuation.

My filaments trail across the living mat. They do not scratch. They persuade. From their delicate tips, I secrete the last of my life's chemistry. It is not ink, but a catalyst. A specific, complex instruction that tells the vellum how to remember. In my wake, the ground will darken over seasons, shifting from ochre to a deep, resonant umber. The texture will change, becoming smoother, denser. Future Scribes, a hundred or a thousand generations from now, will walk this place and read with their soft pads the story I have inscribed. They will know of the Long Waning. My knowledge will become their instinct.

The concept of 'I' is a convenience of this final moment, a flicker of focus. For most of my existence, 'I' was simply a node. Thought was not a private act, but a current I swam in, a chemical tide flowing from the collective. A touch of filaments with a sibling was a merging of libraries. A pheromonal storm was a shouting of gods in which 'I' was but a single syllable.

Now, the tide recedes. The network-sense grows thin, like a scent dispersing on a slow thermal current. My own chemistry is becoming simple, singular. The urge to feed, to sculpt, to communicate—it has all faded into a single, quiet imperative: to *be still*.

My filaments complete their last, graceful sweep. The glyph is finished. It is a vast, beautiful fractal, a pattern of truth that sprawls for a mile across the plain. I feel a deep, resonant satisfaction that needs no sound. It is a rightness in the gut of the world.

My forelimbs retract slowly, curling into themselves like dying ferns. The strength ebbs from my middle limbs, and I sag from my hold on the horn-plate, settling my full length onto the vellum with a silence that absorbs all weight. My rearmost pads, which have for so long gripped the world, now relax into it.

I am where I am meant to be. Within a new, unwritten patch of ground that needs the gift of my body. My internal fluids thicken. The slow peristalsis of my segments ceases. The intricate web of chemoreceptors at the tip of my proboscis goes dark. The buttery light filtering through the canopy seems to grow warmer, heavier. The hum of the world is no longer an external sensation, but a vibration I am joining.

Other Scribes will find me. I can feel their approach, not as individuals, but as a subtle pressure change in the vellum, a shift in the chemical composition of the air. They will not mourn. They will understand. They will touch my cooling shell with their filaments, reading the ghost of my last secretion, approving the final work. They will cover me, nudging and secreting, accelerating my union with the ground.

My consciousness is fraying, becoming landscape. The distinction between my body and the living mat blurs. Am I the hum, or am I being hummed? My memories—the taste of a particularly sweet bladder-fluid in the high spires, the intricate dance of a spire-sculpting, the feeling of a spore-fall settling like fine dust—are no longer mine. They are becoming data, geology.

The 'I' dissolves.

There is only the warmth. The pressure. The slow, inexorable process of becoming a foundation. I am a line of script. I am a patch of umber ground. I am the memory beneath the feet of the thoughts yet to come, and in the Great Quiet of this world, that is the only immortality that has ever mattered. I am home.
