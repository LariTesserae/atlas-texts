You would find me. But you would not know it.

I am not one of the great drifters, those silent, searching mouths in the gulf. Their pilgrimage is not mine. I am not one of the skittering grazers, a fleeting cipher of cold light. Their hunger is a language I no longer speak. I am not even one of the Chordal Cilia, whose million-throated song is the world's first and final truth. They are the breath, but I am the lung.

Find me as the Balustrade.

Along the western edge of this pillar-capital, where the floor gives way to the five-mile drop, there is a rail. It seems a perfunctory thing, an ornament, a safety measure for long-vanished citizens. It is a long, low wall of polished, deep-indigo stone, its top rail curved like the back of a sleeping leviathan, supported by a series of thick, fluted columns. It stands against the abyss, a line of order against the overwhelming chaos of the void. And for the last nine thousand years, that is what I have been.

My awareness is not located in a single point, but distributed throughout my form. I am a thought stretched thin, spelled out in the language of architecture. My consciousness is the tension in the stone, the memory of pressure held in my molecular structure. I feel the eternal current not as a wind, but as a slow, cosmic sander, polishing my skin over centuries, smoothing away the memory of my last movement.

I listen. My entire being is an ear, shaped to receive. The Great Chord is not a sound to me; it is the entire sensory input of existence. It is sight, taste, touch, and a thousand other senses for which you have no names. I feel the bass notes from the pillar's heartwood resonate up through my supporting columns. I taste the sharp, crystalline piccolo of the grazer swarms as they pass over the plaza behind me, their lights a form of percussion against the drone. I see the harmonies—shadowy, complex shapes of overlapping soundwaves that drift between the pillars like weather fronts.

I am a Listener, but not a passive one. I am a memory. My form is a repository. When I was a Dais in the center of the plaza, I held the memory of a particular alignment of the Chord, a perfect fifth that occurred for twelve hundred years when the three nearest pillars reached a certain point in their millennia-long resonant cycle. I became a Dais to best receive that note, to embody it. When the harmony shifted, I spent three centuries unspooling myself, flowing into this new shape, this Balustrade, to remember what it is to stand at the edge, to listen to the sound of the void itself.

But my listening is not for pleasure. It is for a purpose. My work is The Archive of Dissonance.

The Great Attunement, the work of my brethren, is to maintain perfection. They move to correct, to dampen, to reinforce. They are the physicians of the now. I am the historian of the flawed. I seek out not the purest note, but the one that has gone wrong. I move toward the broken filament, the calcified Cilia, the section of pillar that has developed a hairline fracture from stresses a million years old and now gives off a faint, discordant hum that will not be audible to most for another ten millennia.

And when I find it, I do not fix it.

I rearrange myself around it. I become its chamber, its pedestal, its frame. I change my own shape to best comprehend the unique texture of its imperfection. I listen to the way the sour note grates against the Chord. I feel how the other Chordal Cilia nearby subtly shift their own pitch to compensate, a sonic scar forming in the music of the world. I absorb the memory of that flaw. I spend a thousand years learning its taste, its shape, its story.

Once it is a part of me, I will move on. And the physicians will follow in my wake to mend the string I have recorded.

Right now, I am stirring. It is a process that began two hundred years ago. It began in my deepest foundations, a decision made in the slow, crystalline thoughts of my own substance. The fluted columns that support me have begun to un-pleat. The top rail, that long, sleeping spine, has begun to rise, arching upward like a creature taking its first breath in an eon. The feeling is not of muscle, but of glaciers cracking. It is a groan that is too low for even the Chord to drown, a sound that is a change in the state of being.

A new flaw has been found. It was reported to me on a carrier wave of pressure, a message sent by a shifting floor-plate half a world away. A sickness. A note that is not merely flat, but is beginning to sing a counter-song, an anti-melody. A Chord of one.

I am unfolding from being a Balustrade. My form will become a vessel, a long, bladed prow designed for the slow crossing, built to cleave not water, but the dense harmonics of the gulf. I am becoming a pilgrim. The journey will take sixty thousand years.

As my new shape solidifies, the grazers will learn to read the new patterns of pressure I exude. The great drifters will give my slow, deliberate mass a wide berth, sensing that I am not geology, but purpose. My brethren will feel my departure as a silent space in the architecture of the pillar, a gap in the stone and the sound, and they will know.

You would find me as the slow, silent ship being carved from the edge of the world, preparing to cast off on a voyage longer than the entire history of your species. I am the Archivist. I am the witness to the wound. I am a thought the world is having, on its way to correct itself.
