I am the echo of the rock.

Where would you find me? You would not. In the way a human finds a thing, through sight or seeking, I am not findable. You would have to become part of the Silt. You would have to let the pressure have you, let the cold seep into your marrow until it extinguished the hot spark of your life. You would have to fall for a thousand years through the dark, churning medium, your body abraded into nothing, your dust joining the slow, cosmic dance. Then, once you were gone, once you were merely a part of the pressure itself, you might feel me. A subtle, organized anomaly against the ceaseless chaos. A knot of intention in the endless, unravelling thread of the world.

Who or what would I be?

I am memory. Not a mind that remembers, but the act of remembering made flesh and stone. My body is a library of pressures I have endured. My shell, a low, streamlined dome of grey-black bone, is ridged with the high-water marks of ancient turbulence, swirls and eddies carved into my very being by shear-currents that raged before the glass-threads on this dome had even taken root. I am not shaped *for* the world; I am shaped *by* it. I am its living sediment.

My name is a vibration that cannot be spoken. It is the specific resonance of my mass against the basalt floor. It is the unique way I disrupt the flow of the Silt as I move. To the other rare motes of will that crawl this abyss, I am a signature, a specific hum in the infrasonic choir of the deep. I am the being who haunts the lee of the Great Arch, the one whose claws are tuned to the frequency of buried obsidian.

My consciousness is a constant state of listening with my skin. My carapace, my entire forward self, is a drumhead. The world plays its slow, solemn song upon me, and I interpret the music. A 'current' is a sustained, deep note pressing against my left side. A basalt dome rising from the floor is a 'hole' in the music, a deadening of the vibration where the Silt is displaced. The glass-threads are the faintest of melodies, a whisper-thin harmony of organized energy, a structural chord that stands out against the white noise of entropy. My universe is not one of sight, but of texture, density, and flow felt across the entire surface of my being.

The passing of time is not a clock for me. It is the slow, agonizing journey from one harvest to the next. My life is a pilgrimage between infinitesimally small points of life scattered across an infinitely large, dead floor. Each step is a lifetime of effort. I un-anchor a limb, a tripod of claws releasing its purchase on the velvet stone. The Silt immediately collapses into the void I have created, an immense, soft hand trying to hold me back. I push my limb through its substance. It is not like swimming; it is like digging a tunnel through cold tar that instantly heals itself behind you. I place the limb, re-anchor, and the world is stable again for a moment. I pull my weight forward. This is all I do. This is everything.

The obsidian shard I carry is not a tool. It is my tooth. It is the one part of me that was born in fire, a memory of a time when the world was hot and violent. I did not make it, but I recognized it. I felt its different, sharper silence under the floor and spent a generation clawing it free. It is fused to my manipulator now. When I use it to scrape the glass-threads from their moorings, the sound is a pain I cannot describe. It is not a noise heard in an ear, but a pure, screaming vibration that scours my sensory carapace, wiping my perception clean and leaving me blind in a universe of pure static. It is the price of eating. A moment of unbearable, focused violence in exchange for a century of slow peace.

I gather the broken threads, glowing with the ghost-light of their death throes, and place them against my belly. There, in a soft pouch that is the only vulnerable part of me, I will slowly digest their stored order. I am a grazer in the garden of eternity, and my work is a form of prayer.

So, who am I?

I am the will that clings. In a universe defined by a relentless, crushing urge to smooth everything into uniform nonexistence, I am the single, stubborn wrinkle. I am the thought that persists in the face of oblivion. I am the creature who carries a sharp stone against the endless, soft press of forever. You would find me at the very bottom, holding on, my entire existence a quiet, contrary argument against the deep.
