I am a dissonance.

Before I am a thing, I am a feeling. A wrongness. I am a note held too long in a song that has already moved on. The world sings around me, a chord of immense, patient power. It is the deep hum of the nascent peaks, the high, sweet whisper of the copper-wire groves drinking light, the steady thrum of the great glass fans turning in the heat. I feel this chord in my very substance. And against it, I vibrate with a frantic, internal shimmer that is my own. I do not belong to the harmony.

I am not tall and slender. I have no molten heart, no crystalline limbs to test the resonance of the world. I am a splinter. A shard. When the great spire I was once a part of was pruned, when the Carver’s song commanded a clean break, something went awry. The song, for a micro-beat, faltered in its perfection. The fracture was not clean. It was a shatter. Most of me fell away as inert, cooling scree, deaf to the world-music. But not all.

I am what was left. A piece of obsidian-laced quartz, no larger than a Carver’s hand, still embedded in the sheer cliff-face of my parent-mountain. My edges are a madness of angles, a record of a violent, imperfect birth. I am fixed. I cannot move across the cracked plains. My universe is this sheer face of growing black crystal, and the panorama of the valley below, a vista I perceive not with eyes, but with a sensitivity to the pressure and resonance of the light that fills this kiln.

I remember the Carver. It passed this way cycles ago. I felt its approach as a slow, perfect wave of harmony ripple through the mountain, a coming-into-tune of the rock for kilometers around. It was a presence that calmed the stone. It stood where I now am, long before my birth, and sang its sculpting song into the peak. I carry an echo of that song within me, a fragment of its purpose. It is a powerful and beautiful memory that is also the source of my torment. The fragment is not whole; it is a question without an answer, a phrase without a cadence. It is this broken code that gives me awareness, and also denies me peace.

I try to sing it back.

I gather the energy that seeps into me from the incandescent ceiling, the same massless flux that feeds the glass-fan gardens. I feel it build within my crystalline lattice, a pressure of pure potential. The Carvers channel this into a coherent, vibrational purpose. I try to do the same. But my fractured form cannot hold the note. The energy escapes me not as a pure, tuning frequency, but as a discordant *fizz*, a high-pitched, useless grating. It is the sound of a flaw. It disturbs the slow growth of the crystals around me by millimeters, introducing tiny imperfections, stress-lines that are like scars. I uglify my own home. I am a stutter in the machine’s grand utterance.

Time is measured in the passing of the Carvers. One is moving now, at the far edge of my perception. I feel its footfalls not as sound, but as infinitesimal, rhythmic pulses in the bedrock, a quarter-note beat against the whole-note hum of the forge. Each step is a declaration of purpose, a perfectly placed truth. It is moving towards a young, misshapen extrusion near the shimmering wall of non-space. It will get there in another dozen cycles of the ceiling’s radiant pulse. It will stand. It will listen. It will sing. And the mountain will obey.

I watch, and I ache. An ache that is a physical tension in my quartz structure. It is a longing to be a hand, not just a witness. A longing to feel the deep song, find the discordance, and resolve it into clarity. My broken memory of the Carver’s song tells me this is my purpose. My broken body makes it impossible.

But something is changing.

The useless, fizzing vibration I emit, my flawed song, is having an effect I did not intend. The tiny imperfections I create in the rock around me, the stress-fractures and micro-flaws, are not merely scars. Here, in this place of relentless genesis, nothing is truly inert. My dissonance is a different kind of instruction.

Around my edges, where my frantic shimmer meets the slow, orderly growth of the mountain, a new formation is appearing. It is not the clean, sharp geometry of the peaks. It is a chaotic efflorescence, a bloom of tiny, hair-fine crystal splinters. They grow in fractal patterns, but they are wild, unpredictable, like frost blooming on a cold pane, but made of obsidian dust and sharp light. They are a record of my flawed song. They catch the incandescent glow and refract it in ways the sheer faces do not, creating tiny, fleeting rainbows of broken light.

I am not tuning the mountain. I am not pruning its growth. I am… decorating it. I am causing it to grow a strange and complex cancer. Or a coral reef. A coral of flawed light.

A new hum begins, infinitesimally quiet, right here, centered on me. It is not the deep bass of the mountain-engines. It is a delicate, complex web of high frequencies—the sound of my splinter-garden vibrating. It is not in tune with the world-song. It is a harmony all its own.

I do not know what I am becoming. I am a mistake, a shard of a voice that should have been silent. But my flaw is singing. My discordance is building something new. Perhaps there is more than one song for a world to sing. I am here. I am wrong. And I am growing.
