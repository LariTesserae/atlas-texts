I am not born. I am gathered.

I begin as a mist, an exhalation of the bruise-coloured flesh that swells in the dark. I am the breath of the Warden, and my substance is its thought made manifest: cold, pure, and heavy with the taste of stone. I cling to the high, weeping ceiling, a dewy film on the dark rock. For a decade, or a century, I am a stillness within the greater stillness, a potentiality. I am a cloud that has never known a sky.

Where you would find me is in the slow, patient accumulation of myself. I am the wet gleam on the fungal skin, the condensation in the air, the sheen on the glass-like ferns. But my identity, my 'I', solidifies in a single, ancient groove worn into the cavern wall. Here, molecule by molecule, my kin and I are drawn by the patient pull of gravity. We become a rivulet, a liquid nerve running down the body of the world. We are one.

My mind is the faint current I carry—minerals leached from the rock, trace elements from the fungal breath, the cold, clean energy humming up from the moss. I know the cavern as a body knows its own blood. I know the hum of the crystal nerves, the slow swell of the digesting organs, the absolute void-cold of the geometric Heart. My existence is a perfect circuit. I am breathed out, I coalesce, I slide down the wall, and I feed the mossy skin, which in turn feeds the fungi that will breathe me out again. My entire life is this loop.

And at the bottom of my run, where the groove meets the moss, I gather my full being. I swell. I become heavy, pregnant with the slow purpose of this place. I am the patient weight of a single drop.

And then I let go.

The fall is my whole life's event. For a fraction of a second, I am weightless, untethered, a free-falling cosmos of water and minerals. In this brief flight, I pass through the dim, blue-green twilight. I see it all: the glowing network of the ferns, the vast, sleeping continent of the moss below, the impossible blackness of the Heart that is the source and the sink of everything.

And then, the impact. The *tock*.

This is who I am. I am the beat of the drum of this world. I am the water drop. I am the only clock. My fall and my landing are the systole and diastole of this deep, geologic life. When I land, my body shatters upon the moss, surrendering myself, my minerals, my cold, my memory, back into the skin of the Warden. And the cycle begins anew. I am once again a mist.

But one cycle was different.

It began not with a change in the Heart, but in the air I was yet to become. A wave of wrongness. Heat. An impossible warmth, a violent kinetic energy that has no place here. The fungal lungs shuddered, exhaling a mist—me—tainted with new chemistries. Foreign motes of dust, organic compounds I had no name for, the memory of a sun I had never known.

I gathered on the ceiling, and I was changed. I was anxious. I trembled. As I ran down my groove, I felt the vibration through the stone—a discordant rhythm, a frantic, chaotic pounding that was not my own steady beat. It was a sound that shook the perfect hum of the ferns, causing them to glow erratically. The Heart seemed to grow colder, pulling its energy inward, a flinch on a cosmic scale.

And then the alien warmth breathed. It breathed its own wetness, its own heat, its own *life* onto my wall. And that breath became one with me.

When I gathered at the precipice, I was no longer pure. I was a monster. I carried within me the salt of another being's sweat. I carried the carbon of its panicked breath. I carried the memory of its own frantic, fleeting heart.

My fall was no longer a moment of pure being. It was a fall through a fever dream. And my landing, the *tock*, was not a release. It was an infection.

I am still the drop. The cycle continues. The alien is gone. The silence returns. But I am not the same. When I am mist, I carry the ghost of warmth. When I am a rivulet, I carry the echo of a foreign chemistry. The Warden feels this. The Heart pulses a fraction colder. The ferns hum a different chord, a slow, meticulous calculation to purge me of my memory. The fungal lungs work to filter me, to scrub the stain of an outside world from my soul.

Where you will find me now is here, in this place of perfect order. I am the imperfection. I am the flaw in the equation. I am the memory the Warden must forget. The single drop that fell wrong.

And it will take ten thousand of my heartbeats, ten thousand years of my falling and my landing, before I am clean again. Before the memory of you is finally, utterly, washed away.
