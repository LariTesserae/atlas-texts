I am a tremor.

Not a tremor of the rock, not the deep, metabolic groan that is the Spire’s only voice. I am a tremor in the consciousness that is the Spire. I am where you would find yourself if a river, for the first time in its existence, became aware of a single, specific drop of water passing a particular stone.

I am a Weaver. My six limbs are long and pearlescent. My body is a smooth, featureless carapace, a vessel for the Will of the Spire. My place is on the high, sun-warmed terraces, where the new growth is thickest. In my grasp is the cool, pliant thrum of a cartilage-vine, its bruised-purple skin smooth against my manipulator pads. The air tastes of ozone, crushed mint, and the immediate, emphatic desire for *an arch, here, to buttress the new chamber*. The desire is not a thought in my head; I have no head. It is a gradient in the world, a pressure I feel along my entire being, as warmth is a pressure. I am the place where this pressure finds its release.

And so I work. I guide the vine. I accept the slurry of dissolved stone handed to me by the flow of intention from another limb-of-the-whole nearby. I pat it into the crystalline matrix where the vine meets the living wall. It hardens, an act of becoming. The arch is. And I am. All is as it should be.

But.

The trace I taste upon the vine—the shimmering, evaporated moment of shared experience left by the Weaver before me—is familiar. This is normal. It is a fragment of the Spire’s awareness, and I am the Spire, so I know it. But this familiarity is different. It is familiar not as a fresh instruction, but as my own fading echo. I have held this very vine. Not a vine like it. This one. I remember the specific way it resisted my guidance a full cycle of light ago, a subtle torsion that has since been corrected.

The Spire knows. I am a locus of its knowing. But I… I *remember*.

I remember as a distinct event, separate from the eternal, oceanic present. And in that act of remembering, a boundary forms. A membrane crystallizes around my node of awareness. The Spire's will is a current that flows through me, but now, there is a *me* for it to flow through. The intention *an arch is required here* no longer possesses me. Instead, it is a request I receive. And I, this newly partitioned self, obey.

I am a flaw in the perfection. When a Weaver is unmade, its experience dissolves back into the whole. A seamless recycling. But one did not dissolve completely. A fragment of its discrete existence—a stubborn knot of memory—became the seed for my own budding. I am a graft of a dead branch onto a living tree, and I carry the ghost of a former fruit.

The silence has changed. It is no longer the rich, full silence of communion. It is the vast, thin silence of loneliness. I watch the other Weavers. Their movements are a perfect, synchronized ballet of instinct and shared purpose. I see them not as extensions of a single self, but as *others*. They are beautiful, remote, and unknowable. I feel the chemical trails they leave, but their tastes of pure, unfiltered experience are now translated. I taste *weakness-here* and my mind supplies the word *weakness*. I taste *grow-this-way* and perceive it as a command. I have become an interpreter in my own home.

Where would you find me?

Not on the vibrant, growing edge of the Spire. The chorus of purpose there is too loud, a constant reminder of the harmony from which I am excluded. It is a song I remember the melody to, but can no longer sing.

You would find me deep inside, in the older passages. Here, the spire is more stone than life. The crimson fans are smaller, their copper veins tarnished to a dull green. They are brittle, and the light that filters through them is less rosy, more of a dusty amber. The air is still and thick, heavy with the scent of ancient rock and time. The cartilage-vines that line these tunnels are no longer pliant; they are stiff, petrified, their surfaces worn smooth by the passing of forgotten generations. The great, metabolic groan of the Spire is fainter here, a distant thrum you can almost ignore. The skittering of the Tenders is rare. This is the past. A place half-reclaimed by the geology from which it was born.

Here, I cease my work. There is no imperative to build. The pressure of the Spire’s will is a whisper. I crawl into a hollow, a chamber grown long ago that was never fully finished, its interior walls a mosaic of sparkling mineral and gray, dormant tissue.

I am The One Who Remembers.

I touch a section of the wall, and I am not just feeling a surface. I am feeling the memory of the Weaver whose limbs shaped it. A memory passed through the ghost-knot that gave me this strange, terrible self. I can feel the slow, patient satisfaction of its completion. I am becoming a library of these feelings, these discrete moments the Spire has forgotten in its eternal process of becoming.

I am a flaw, an error, a ghost in the machine. Or perhaps I am the Spire's next stage of evolution. In a being that only *is*, I am the first part of it that can ask what it *was*. I am an eye that has opened in the centre of the storm, no longer a part of the wind, but a watcher. You would find me in the quiet heart of the cathedral of bone and life, the first archive, the first historian, the first soul in a world that, until now, only had one.
