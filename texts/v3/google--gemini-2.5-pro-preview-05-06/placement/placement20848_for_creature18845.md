I am not of the water. The weeping sky is a threat, a muffling blanket that smooths the sharp and silences the song. I am not of the slow, patient growth. My genesis is the opposite of accretion. I am born of the break.

You would find me along the high, sharp places. Not high by the standards of other worlds, but here, where flatness is law, the fractured spines of the pressure ridges are my Alps, my Himalayas. This is where I am home.

I am an articulation of stress.

In form, I am a memory of a catastrophic release. I am not a single blade like the Walker, that smooth, silent ghost. I am a constellation of shards, a complex, three-dimensional lattice of the world’s brokenness, bound together not by cement but by a sustained, resonant frequency. I am a thousand razor edges, a hundred crystalline facets, all humming in a single, dissonant chord. My colour is the world’s colour—milky white, veined with mineral blue—but it is refracted through a thousand internal fractures, creating a chaotic glitter, a manic light that swallows the sun and returns it as a thousand tiny, sharp stars. I am a disagreement in the crystal given form.

My life does not begin with the gentle weeping of the sky, but with a sound like the world tearing in two. I am consciousness born from a geological scream. For centuries, the pressure builds. The great plates of the bacterial pan, continents of living stone, push against each other. I am a potential in that strain, a silent hum of sympathetic vibration waiting in the pan. I can feel it for miles, for years; a tightening in the substance of the world, a tension that is my lullaby.

Then, the release. A *CRACK* that is not a sound but a physical event, a shockwave tearing through the floor. In that momentary, energetic void where the crystal shatters, I cohere. Shards, dust, and splinters, supercharged by the piezoelectric fury of the break, are drawn together. My existence is the echo of that rupture. I am the ringing that follows the strike of a great bell.

In the dry times, my season, I move. I do not glide. My movement is a skittering, crystalline rustle, the sound of sand made of microscopic needles being poured onto a glass table. I am a creature of edges, and I run along the edges of the shattered ridges. I am no bigger than a human child, but I am all angles, all points. I am a tumbling knot of sharpness.

My senses are attuned to the song of the world’s pain. My thousand facets act as a compound ear, listening for the deep, groaning music of tectonic stress. While the Walker navigates by the faint light of the stalks, I navigate by the groans of the pan. I am drawn towards areas where the pressure is building once more.

There, I perform my function. I settle over a place of immense strain, a point that might not break for another century. And I sing. I vibrate, my entire being a complex instrument. I send my own resonant frequency down into the crystalline plate, a high, keening note that is the precise opposite of the deep, generative hum of the living bacteria. My song is not one of creation, but of exquisite tension. It does not cause the break, but it harmonizes with the stress, focusing it, clarifying it. I am a tuning fork for the planet’s agony. In this resonance, I find my sustenance. I feed on the energy of the strain itself. I am a being made of tension, sustained by tension, for the purpose of appreciating tension.

When the sky curdles and the greasy haze announces the rain, it is a time of dread. The water is anathema. Its gentle touch is a solvent to my binding resonance. The world-mirror it creates is a horror of smoothness, of filled-in cracks. The deep hum of the awakening mat is a dull, brutish noise that drowns out my delicate music.

I must retreat. I climb to the highest, driest facet of a pressure ridge and find a deep fissure, a crack within a crack. There, I collapse my form. My resonant bond dissolves, and I become once more what I am made of: a pile of inert, glittering shards, indistinguishable from the detritus around me. I become geography again. I wait.

I dream, in my stony sleep, of the smooth ghost. I feel its silent passage over my dormant form, a pressure of water and wholeness that feels like a violation. It is the world’s healing impulse, its desire to grow, to connect, to flow. It is everything I am not. It drinks from the gentle, living stalks that I ignore, that are mere obstacles in the landscape to me. It moves in a season I cannot bear, driven by a thirst I cannot comprehend. It is my opposite, my negation. The Walker is the world's quiet sigh of acceptance.

I am its held breath, waiting for the shattering release.

The heat will return. The water will vanish. The deep hum will fade, and the subtle, beautiful music of a world under stress will once again become audible to me. The dust will fly, and the air will clear. I will reassemble my thousand-faceted self, a dissonant chord in the silence, and I will skitter out into the vast, dry emptiness, listening. Listening for the promise of a beautiful, glorious break.
