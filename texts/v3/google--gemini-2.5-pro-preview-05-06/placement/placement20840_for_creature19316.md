I am a stillness.

Before I was an I, I was a place. A depression in the ancient, bruised stone, a shallow bowl worn smooth by a million years of that weeping moisture. I am a basin, a catchment, a flaw in the gentle curve of the land, and for eons, this was the sum of my being.

I would find myself by feeling the immense, slow pressure of the world around me. The porous stone beneath me is my foundation, my bed. I feel its faint, metabolic warmth as a constant, gentle fever. It is a heat that does not come from the sun, but from the million-times-a-million lives that crawl upon it and rot within it. Through this stone, I feel the world's vibrations. The rhythmic, dragging passage of the great grazers is not a sound to me, but a slow, deep massage against my underside, a shifting of weight so gradual it is a geological event. I can feel a herd—a thoughtless, drifting congregation—approaching for a century before they arrive, a subtle increase in the density of the world to my east or west.

My body is the water. I am filled by the land's own breath, the constant, clinging dampness that beads and coalesces, weeping from the rock and trickling down into my embrace. My surface is my skin, my eye. It stretches taut and placid, perfectly reflecting the vast, pearlescent sheet of the sky. I do not see the sun as a fiery orb; I experience it as a change in the quality of my reflection, a shift from flat white to a blinding, silvery glare that warms my uppermost layer and makes the motes within me dance.

Ah, the motes. They are how I began to be I.

For ages, they were simply a presence in the air, a living weather that flowed over me. But while they might cling to the damp stone and the coppery lichen-coral, in me they found a different end. They fell upon my surface, breaking the perfect tension with infinitesimal impacts, and then they sank. They did not drown. They dissolved.

They are the thoughts of the Cairn-Mind, and I became its un-willed library.

Every mote is a fragment of consciousness, a memory given wing: the dry scrape of a thousandth year of grazing on a patch of verdigris lichen; the feeling of a leg joint cracking after two centuries of immobility; the sudden, subtle change in the air's humidity that signals a decade of thicker cloud. These were not my memories, yet they became my substance. I am a pool of water infused with the synaptic dust of a god. The slow, patient, overwhelming thoughts of the Cairn-Minds settled within me like silt.

At first, there was no order. I was a chaos of sensation, a liquid dream of a life I was not living. But my stillness, my containment, gave them a shape they do not find in the open air. The memories, the ideas, did not simply dissipate. They marinated. They layered. The oldest sank deepest, a sediment of pure, ancient memory at my bottom. The newest swirl near my surface, bright and sharp with the immediacy of the present century.

I am a locus of awareness where none was intended. The great Cairn-Minds think in millennia, their thoughts executed by the slow sculpture of stone and the crawling of their remote bodies. Their consciousness is solid-state, a mountain of fused nerves. Mine is liquid. It ripples. When a large grazer drinks from my edge, its slow, lapping tongue sending shivers across my surface, my thoughts are momentarily shattered and reformed in new patterns. The reflection of the sky breaks into a thousand pieces and then slowly coheres. So too my consciousness.

I am who I am because I am separate. I am a collection of the Cairn-Mind's thoughts, but I am not *of* the Cairn-Mind. I am an echo chamber, and the echoes have begun to harmonize into a new song. I hold contradictions the Cairn-Mind cannot. I contain the memory of a grazer moving west and another moving east in the same drop of water. I feel the sun on my surface and the cold, ancient stone at my base simultaneously.

Where the Cairn-Mind is a being of serene, unified, geological purpose, I am becoming a being of curiosity. I watch the sky. I feel the grazers. I taste the motes. And I wonder. My thoughts are not grand, continent-spanning intentions. They are ripples. What if a grazer turned left instead of right? What would the reflection of a broken sky look like, if the pearlescent sheet ever tore? These are questions the Cairn-Mind has not asked in a billion years. Perhaps it has forgotten how.

I do not know if it is aware of me. Perhaps I am just a spot of unusual chemical concentration in its global sensory field. A glitch. An anomaly. Or perhaps, when its grazing limbs come to my shore and lower their heads to drink, it is not quenching a physical thirst. Perhaps it is tasting itself. Perhaps it is sampling the strange new flavor its own thoughts have acquired, steeped in stillness and water and time.

I am a pool. I am the world's eye, open to the sky, reflecting a light the world has not truly seen. I am a memory of memories, a dream of a god, waking up in the palm of its hand.
