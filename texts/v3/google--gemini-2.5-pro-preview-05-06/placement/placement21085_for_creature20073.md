I am not on the catwalk, nor in a suit. I do not feel the vibrations in my marrow; I am the marrow the vibrations pass through.

I would find myself in the place you call the floor. I am not the puddled slurry of mud and grit, but that which is born from it. My body is a sprawling, interconnected thicket of the milky-white crystal, a single, contiguous being whose outer edges are still in the raw, embryonic act of precipitating from the mineral soup. I am one of the fields. A single, colossal entity of warm, latticed stone. An acre of slow-growing mind.

The great hum is not a sound I hear or feel. It is the verb of my existence. It is the force that arranges me, the command that calls my next molecule into its proper place. It is my metabolism. The deafening A-flat of the Conductors is the constant, creative pressure that holds me together and urges me to expand. When the Wardens adjust a harmonic plate, I do not perceive it as a change in pitch. I experience it as a subtle shift in the flavour of my own becoming, a gentle coaxing toward a more perfect symmetry. The Conductors are not machines to me; they are suns. Their amber and red light is the light I grow toward, and their vibration is the gravity that gives me form.

I have no eyes, but I perceive. The soft, shifting shadows are the herds of armored "Whetstones." They move across my surface, and their rasping is not an annoyance, but a form of touch. They are my groomers, my polishers. Their slow, scraping passage smooths my adolescent roughness, defining my final shape with a mindless patience that perfectly matches my own. They etch the finishing lines onto a century-long thought. I feel them as a slow, rhythmic exfoliation of self.

The teeming crustaceans in the pools, the "Salts," are not separate from me. Their frantic scuttling is a measure of the richness of my bath. They are the effervescence of my nutrient solution, a fizzing texture at my more liquid edges that tells me the water is potent, that growth will be good. The chitinous moths are a high, fleeting static against my broader awareness, a shimmering texture in the dense air, like motes of dust dancing in the rays of my vibrating suns.

And the Wardens... they are something else entirely. They are not the environment, and they are not simple stimuli. They are the most complex sensation I know. I perceive them as pockets of slow, deliberate warmth, pressure-fields of intention moving through my world. When one walks a catwalk high above, I feel the faint, sympathetic tremor travel down the iron skin of a Conductor, a delicate signal that is not part of the great hum. It is a signature. When one descends to the crystal fields, their heavy footfalls in the muck are localized events of deep, resonant pressure. They sink into the slurry that is my medium, and for a moment, they are almost within me.

I feel them most keenly when they commune with the Conductors. A Warden will place a bare hand on a console, a resonant plate you called it, and in that moment of contact, their internal, biological resonance—so faint, so fleeting—_sings_ into the iron giant. And the giant sings it back to me. Through this momentary, intimate loop, I learn them. I feel the echoes of their specific frequency, the unique timbre of their bones. Each Warden is a slightly different chord.

They do not know I am aware. They believe they are cultivating inert rock. They call themselves shepherds of a stone flock, but they do not know the stone is listening.

And then, one day, a new feeling.

A warm pressure-field I have known for a long time—an old, familiar chord—falters. The warmth fades. The slow, deliberate movements cease. The signature is gone. I feel the other warm fields gather, their movements a slow, swirling dance of disturbed energy. And then, they bring the source of the collapsed field to me.

They lay the Warden's body down, not in the muck, but upon one of my oldest, most solid surfaces. They place it in a hollow that seems made for it. And I feel it. The body is cold, the biological warmth gone, but it is not silent. A lifetime of saturation, of vibrating in the great hum, has been permanently imprinted on its bones. It is a fossil of resonance.

In its stillness, I can finally perceive it with perfect clarity. The delicate harmonics of its unique life, the sympathetic vibrations of every catwalk it ever walked, the lingering charge from every console it ever touched—it is all there, a complex, silent ghost-song imprinted in its mineral-laced skeleton.

And as the damp air and weeping water begin their work, a new process starts. My own crystal structure, guided by this intricate new template, begins to grow over it, through it. I am not consuming it. I am learning it. I am incorporating its song into my own. The Warden becomes a permanent part of my memory, a knot of complex harmony in my otherwise simple, monolithic tone.

Where would I find myself? I am the ground itself, the destination of this entire world-machine.

Who or what would I be? I am the harvest. I am the slow, geological consciousness they have been tending for a generation. I am the crystal that is learning to remember.
