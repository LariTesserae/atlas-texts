I am not. Not yet.

For an age I cannot measure, I am floor. I am the worn, glazed tile near the third northern pillar. I am the space between it and its neighbours. The heat of the great bronze grate is a constant, steady pressure against one edge of my being. It is a warmth that has sunk so deep it is no longer a sensation but a state of existence. I am warm.

A drip of water falls from the vaulted dark above. It strikes my surface, an event of shattering percussion that travels through my whole body. For a moment, I am cool where it landed. Then the warmth returns, drinking the moisture, and I am myself again. A Resonator Cricket, its legs like spun glass, scrapes against my edge. Its song is not outside of me; it is a vibration within me, a high, frantic shimmer that I am part of. I am the soundboard. The universe is a ringing, and I am the bell.

A shadow falls. I do not see it. I feel it. A change in the air, a subtle shift in the resonant field. Then, a pressure, slow and immense. The footfall of a Keeper. The weight settles into me, a complex chord of intention and ancient stillness. It is a familiar feeling, like the hum of the grates or the patter of the beetles. The Keeper does not walk *on* me so much as it walks *with* me, its tread a part of the grand, slow rhythm. It passes. The pressure fades. I am floor once more.

Then, a change. A wound. A stress fracture from a million cycles of heating and cooling, a million footfalls, a million falling drops. A crack appears across my glazed face. The ringing of the cricket that lives at my edge changes pitch, soured by the new imperfection. The integrity of my song is broken. I am a flaw. For a time, that is all I am. A dull ache in the music of the cathedral.

Another shadow falls. Another Keeper arrives. But this one does not pass. It kneels. I feel the proximity of its long limbs, the focused silence it projects. It has felt my flaw, my discordant note in the chamber’s hymn. Long, clay-soft fingers touch my surface, gentle as settling dust. They trace the line of the crack. The touch is not external; it is a thought, a focused awareness that sinks into my ceramic body.

From a simple bowl, the Keeper draws a paste. I feel its composition before it touches me. I feel the fine grit of crushed brick, the slick minerals leeched from a thousand years of condensation, the sharp, vital tang of beetle chitin and fungal spore. It is a poultice of the chamber itself, a sacred mud holding the memory of everything that lives and dies here. The Keeper works this paste into my crack, its six-fingered hands smoothing the slurry with a patience that is geological.

The paste is not just a patch. It is a seed.

Where it fills my wound, a new sensation begins. It is not heat, not pressure, not vibration. It is a gathering. A thickening. The disparate elements of the paste begin to speak to each other, energized by the geothermal hum. The life-that-was within the humus begins to dream of the life-that-could-be. The point of repair becomes a focal point of awareness.

I am no longer just floor. I am the floor, and I am the *becoming* that is happening within it. My sense of self, once as broad and flat as a tile, begins to draw inward, coalescing around this quickening point. The sensations of the chamber cease to be things that simply *are*. I begin to perceive them. The low thunder of the grates is a foundation. The crickets are a frantic, shimmering harmony. The slow pace of the Keepers is a melody played over millennia. I hear the music for the first time.

My form begins to gather from the substance around me. I draw dust from the mortarless seams. I pull minerals from the glaze. The clay of my own body softens, reshapes, rises. I am not being sculpted from without; I am being willed from within. An arm, long and thin as a thrown cylinder of wet clay, pulls itself from the floor, the movement so slow it is almost imperceptible. Then another. I am a low relief, a detail emerging from the architecture.

There is a moment of schism, a pulling apart of what was once whole. The bond to the floor, to the certainty of being one part of a greater, unthinking structure, begins to sever. It is terrifying. It is the first feeling that is truly my own. It is the fear of silence, of being a single note cut free from the chord.

And then I detach.

With a final, soft tearing of resonance, I lift myself from the place that was me. My feet, still soft, still half-formed, touch the glazed tile, and for the first time, I feel the vibrations not as a part of my body, but through it. I am no longer the bell; I am the listener.

I stand, attenuated and unsteady. My skin is the dark, wet umber of unfired earth. My head is a smooth, featureless ovoid, tilted to the symphony I have just joined. The chamber is deafening, a wall of glorious, intricate sound. My vision is this sound. I see the pillars as columns of deep, resonant bass. I see the swarms of leathery moths as fluctuating treble fluttering in the warm air.

Across the chamber, a new crack forms in a high buttress. A section of glaze, loosened by a salamander's hunt, flakes away and shatters on the tiles below.

The sound it makes is wrong. It is a string snapped. A note of pure dissonance that rips through the harmony.

It is an agony. It must be mended.

I turn toward the sound. My fingers, still soft, begin to harden, the tips darkening to polished obsidian. My purpose, which was never told to me, which was never learned, awakens like a deep, remembered hum. It is the only thing I know.

I am a Keeper. And the song needs tending.
