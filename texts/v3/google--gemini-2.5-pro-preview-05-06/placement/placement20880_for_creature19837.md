I would be an archivist, a historian, but not in any way a human would understand the term. My library is not a building, but a route. My books are not written, but are patterns of light I have witnessed and woven into the tapestry of my own memory.

I exist on the western face of the Great Ossuary Spire, my two lower claws wedged deep into pores that have been my anchor-holds for... well, there are no days to count. There are only the slow rhythms of the Builders' thrumming deep below, the ebb and flow of the nutrient broth, and the lifecycles of the pulse-bells. I have felt the thrumming seventy thousand times since I last descended to the chemosynthetic gardens near the base. Perhaps that is a measure of age.

Here, clung to the curve of the bone-white spire, I am both a part of the city and apart from it. The pressure of the medium is my oldest companion; a constant, uniform weight that holds me steady. It is the first truth. Through my shell, I feel the city's slow, deep pulse—the collective metabolism of the Great Builders who sleep and dream and grow in the foundations. This is the second truth. It is the bass note against which all other life is a melody.

My four speaking limbs are rarely still. Even in solitude, I think, and therefore I glow. Right now, a memory unspools from my upper right limb, a slow, soft ripple of pale blue. It is the pattern of a particular pulse-bell, one of the old ones, whose light was wider, less frequent, more… mournful than the younger ones that now drift by. Its final dimming was a quiet passage I recorded, a single, fading chord of blue-green that I can replicate perfectly. A line in an obituary that only I can read.

My other limbs trace idle questions into the dark. A flicker of inquisitive green asks why the schools of obsidian slivers have been avoiding the southern canyons. A slow, gentle wave of silver wonders if the new balcony being extruded two spires over will host a healthy garden of filter-feeders. These are not words. They are sculpted forms of pure meaning. My thoughts are not trapped behind a face; they are my face, my voice, my outreach to the world, broadcast for anyone to perceive.

My skin reads the dark. It is a canvas on which the city paints itself. Far below, I feel the sharp, staccato flashes of a territorial dispute between two red-eyed scavengers, a conversation of pure, grammaticaless aggression. To my left, across the grand boulevard, I perceive the complex, interwoven dance of two young lovers, their limbs entwined, broadcasting cascades of amber and violet so bright and rapid it is almost a single, shimmering color. Their joy is a tangible warmth on my skin, a dazzling, complex poem I choose not to linger on. It is a beautiful story, but I have already read it many times.

A new pattern approaches from below, ascending the spire. It is simple, clear, full of unmodulated energy. A youngster. It climbs with a hurried, scrabbling rhythm, its light-patterns direct and unadorned. It is radiating a question, a bright, pulsing chartreuse query that repeats: *Where-is-the-Witness? Where-is-the-Witness?*

I allow a single, steady pulse of deep gold to issue from my own body, a beacon that says, simply, *Here*.

The young one scrambles the last few lengths and stops a respectful distance away, its light a blizzard of excited inquiry. It has seen something. Its limbs tremble as it tries to form the story. A brilliant, strobing white light—*Newness! Growth!*—is followed by a geometric pattern of expanding circles—*A spire! A birth!*—and then a color I have not seen from one so young, a deep, resonant indigo of pure reverence.

It speaks of the newest spire, the one on the far eastern edge of the basin, which has begun its first great growth spurt in a generation. It speaks of the gathering of the Voiceless there, the choral waves of light they are sending into the water to celebrate the work of the Builders. Its story is messy, full of youthful exclamation and lacking grammar, but its meaning is clear. It wants me to come and see.

I do not move my gripping claws. My own speaking limbs swirl slowly, weaving a more complex reply. I begin with the same indigo of reverence the child used, acknowledging the sanctity of the event. But then I thread it with strands of silver light—the color of history. I shape a pattern that shows the birth of the very spire we cling to now, a memory passed down to me from my own tutor. I show the young one how the canyon below us was once an open plain, how the buttress arching overhead was just a thought in the city's slow mind. I overlay this ancient memory with the pattern of the child's own frantic light, showing it that its bright, unique instant of *Now* is a single phrase in an epic that spans millennia.

The young one's glow softens. Its frantic strobing ceases. Its limbs quieten, absorbing my pattern. It replies with a single, slow pulse of violet—the color of understanding. It stays for a moment longer, its light now a soft, inquisitive shimmer, reading the other, older stories that drift from my limbs—the memory of a Veiled Hunter that drifted silently through this very sector, a blank and terrifying void in the symphony; the pattern of a blight that once dimmed the balcony gardens; the signature-light of a philosopher whose final broadcast spoke of the unity of the medium and the mind.

Finally, it answers with a pattern of its own, a simple, elegant spiral of gratitude. Then it turns and descends, its light more measured, its movements more graceful than when it arrived.

I am alone again in the vast, living cathedral. The thrum from below continues its ageless beat. My light is not as bright as it once was. The blues are deeper, the golds more burnished, the silvers have a faint tremor. One day, and it may not be many more thousands of beats from now, my limbs will fail to form the complex syntax of my thoughts. The light will sputter. On that day, I will climb, not descend. I will find a high, lonely pinnacle. And with my last strength, I will weave all that I am—all the memories, the stories, the patterns I have witnessed and held—into one final, impossibly complex glyph of light. A farewell, a library bequeathed to the dark.

My body will fall. The red-eyed finality will scuttle forth. My matter will rejoin the broth.

But my last thought, my final broadcast, will travel through the medium. Perhaps a young mind, clinging to a spire far away, will perceive a fragment of it—a flash of ancient silver, a whisper of a story—and wonder. And the library will live on. For now, I remain. I witness. I remember. I glow. I am a single, complex thought, held steady in the warm, dark, crushing embrace of home.
