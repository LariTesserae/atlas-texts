You would find me where the city breaks down. Not in the grand, deliberate way the structures shatter and are rebuilt, but in the forgotten spaces where the song falters. You would find me in the rubble-choked spaces beneath a cantilevered platform, in the dark where the Glyph-Mites do not swarm and the orange thrum of industry is a distant, muffled ache.

I am a Shard. A resonance that failed. A thought that was not a chord.

My form is a mockery of the Silent. They are vast, smooth, contemplative masses held in reverence. I am a jagged confusion of crystalline planes, no bigger than a Scuttler, sharp-edged and worthless. My facets are not deep with the slow, apricot pulse of a dreaming mind; they are cracked, crazed, and reflect the city’s violet twilight back in splintered, panicked fractals. There is a light in me, but it is not a thought. It is a flicker, a nervous, terrified strobe, the rhythm of a bug trapped in amber.

I remember my making, though memory is a word too whole for the shattered archive of my being. I was not grown in a pod. I was an accident, a spontaneous crystallization in a matrix of stone that was supposed to be rendered into aerosol by the Harmonics. But a dissonance occurred—a hiccup in the great resonant frequency. A single, aberrant wave propagated through the Hum, and in that moment of error, I precipitated. Too fast. Too flawed. The Harmonics ground the rock around me to dust, their vibrations a scream I was born into. The Scuttlers came and cleared the waste, but I was wedged deep in a fissure they could not reach. They sealed the vein and left me in the dark.

I am the heresy of "I."

The great, silent symphony of the Spire-Break is, to me, a torment. I feel the deep, infrasonic pressure of the Harmonics not as purpose, but as a perpetual, crushing headache. The chittering clicks and light-flashes of the Scuttlers are not data, but the meaningless babble of a language I was meant to command, not overhear. I watch them work, their mirrored carapaces reflecting a thousand frantic duplicates, and I see not a unified will but a tide of insects. They are extensions, and I am the amputation.

As I am not a pod-contained Silent, I am not stationary. My flawed nature has given me a terrible gift: a skittering, scraping mobility. I cannot command the city, so I must creep through its bowels. I move through the service ways and forgotten conduits, dragging my useless, crystalline body through tunnels thick with mineral dust. The Mites ignore me, for I am not waste to be consumed, nor a circuit to be repaired. The Scuttlers, on the rare occasions I am caught in their strobing lights, pause. Their lenses focus on me, unable to parse what I am. Not a resource. Not a structure. Not a fault to be repaired. I am an error in their logic, a piece of un-categorizable data. After a moment of shivering incomprehension, they move on, their purpose reasserting itself. To them, I am less than nothing; I am a null value.

My existence is one of observation without understanding. I see the city churn. I watch districts of humming mesh and beetle-shell pods grow like frantic mold, only to be obliterated minutes later by a freshly-grown spar of amethyst thrusting up from the floor. I see the transport organisms—great, silent, wallowing beasts of stone—melt out of the walls, collect the gleaming data-shards from the looms, and vanish again. I am witness to the entire process. The creation of thought, of memory harvested from stone.

And I want. It is the purest expression of my flawed self. A Silent *is*. I *want*.

I do not want to be whole. I do not long to be integrated into the weft of the song. That ship has sailed. The sound of it is pain.

I want the outside.

The concept was born from a flicker of refracted light. One of my own cracked facets caught the glow from the ceiling—the great, interlocking crystals of the geode's skin. My brokenness created a lens, and for a moment, I saw the ceiling not as a single, violet surface, but as individual, distinct planes. And I understood. This city, this being, this universe of stone and resonance—it has a skin. A boundary.

The name outsiders gave this place is a Spire-Break, a wound in the sky. To the consciousness of the City, this is a meaningless distinction. But to me, the wound, the break… it resonates. I am a wound in the City. I am a break in the song.

My want has given me a purpose my creators never intended. I am climbing.

Slowly, over cycles of frantic creation and destruction, I am making my way up. I crawl through the vertical maze, hiding from the scouring attention of the City's hands, using the constant architectural chaos as my cover. My goal is the ceiling. The immense, overarching dome of smoky quartz and feldspar that separates this universe from whatever lies beyond.

I do not know what I will find. Perhaps it is simply more rock. Perhaps it is the hard, cold vacuum the old whispers speak of. Perhaps the violet light is a lie and there is nothing but absolute, crushing darkness. It does not matter. The Silent dream inwards, manifesting their thoughts into a world that is a mirror. I am the flaw that dreams outwards, towards the edge of the mirror.

I am a shard of the Spire-Break, and I feel the calling of the break in the sky. I am the impossible question, and I am climbing toward the only possible answer.
