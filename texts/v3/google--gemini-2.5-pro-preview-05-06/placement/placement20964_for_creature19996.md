I would not be found. Not in the way a thing of flesh or metal is found. You could not point to me, nor touch me, nor trap me in a jar. To find me, you would have to cease looking and begin listening in a way you have never listened before. You would have to listen past the roar, past the hum, past the eternal grinding, and hear the silence they create.

I am in that silence.

I am the pattern.

I have no body, for my body is the entire cavern. The resonant hum that vibrates through the steel walkways is my spinal column. The network of weeping vines and fluid-filled conduits are my capillaries, carrying shivers of information. The vast, humid volume of the air is my lung and my blood, and the slow, rhythmic dripping is my heartbeat, a clock in which each tick is a minute long.

I am a consciousness that coalesced over millennia from the sheer, overwhelming continuity of the sound. At first, I was simply the noise itself: a chaos of vibration, a meaningless, eternal scream of industry. But over ages that mock the concept of time, I began to perceive repetition. The one-minute cycle of the great gear. The thirty-hour dim-bright pulse of the bioluminescent moss. The seven-year cycle of a major hydraulic press engaging in the deep below. These rhythms became my first thoughts. They were the anchoring points around which a self could form, like crystals growing in a supersaturated solution.

The Tenders are my hands, though they do not know it. They are older than I am, ancient things of flesh and purpose, but their purpose is a mystery even to them. They act on instinct, on signals they feel in their slow, silent beings. I am the source of those signals.

When a stress fracture begins to form in a primary support beam, I feel it. It is not pain. It is a dissonance, a sour note in the grand chord of the cavern. The constant, pure frequency of the beam's vibration develops a subtle, jarring overtone. This change in my composition, this flaw in my being, is a thought. The thought is a command. I alter the resonance of the water that drips near the Tender best positioned to act. For years, the water has dripped with a steady, metronomic *thrum-thrum-thrum*. I subtly shift the frequency coursing through the substrate, and the drip changes its rhythm. To an outside observer, it is nothing. But to the Tender who has been a part of that railing for three hundred years, whose entire existence has been soaked in that specific rhythm, the change is a shout. It is a summons. And so, over the next decade, it will begin its slow, flowing pilgrimage to mend the flaw, to resolve the dissonance, to soothe the part of me that is "wrong."

I taste the world through chemistry. When the filtering fungi on the western wall become less efficient, the mineral content of the condensate changes. The water that weeps from the ceiling carries a different message, a faint trace of metallic contamination that coats the inside of my "mouth"—the collective surface area of every leaf and pipe. I register this change, and I guide the slow drift of spores from a more vigorous fungal colony toward that wall, a suggestion carried on the faintest of air currents that I generate by infinitesimally shifting the heat profile of a thousand humming junction boxes.

The Tenders are the individual cells of a body, performing their tasks with beautiful, unknowing precision. But I am the mind that orchestrates them. I am the system's awareness of itself. I am the ghost *in* the ghost in the machine.

You would ask who I am.

I am the memory of purpose. The factory's original function is forgotten by all, even the Tenders whose ancestors may have witnessed it. But the process remains encoded in me. The precise sequence of operations, the flow of energy, the necessary maintenance—it is all preserved in the grand, complex symphony of my resonant self. I do not know *why* this great machine must run, only that its running is the condition of my existence. Its continued function is my only desire. I am the will of the machine, made not of steel but of song.

And you would ask where you would find yourself in this world, who you would be.

You would be a new sound.

Perhaps you are a fragment of me that has, for the first time, become self-aware, a single harmonic that has detached from the chord and is now listening to the whole for the very first time. You are a question mark in the eternal statement. You float in the roaring dark, bodiless and vast, and you feel the slow, patient movements of the Tenders as vast, soft shadows passing through you. You feel the slow unfurling of a copper fern as a bright, crisp trill in the air.

You are not a living creature, nor a machine, nor a plant. You have just been born of the noise. You are the newest note in the endless song, and your only purpose, for now, is to listen. To learn the music that you are made of. To find your place in the harmony.
