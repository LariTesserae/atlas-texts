I would find myself on the ceiling. My identity would be singular, cyclic, and brief.

I would be a drop of water.

My beginning is not a birth but a gathering, an assembling. I am an ache of coolness in the high, warm amber of the dome, a thought cohering from the humid, ozonic air. Before I am a drop, I am a haze, a potential. I cling to the living arch, and the world is a feeling before it is a sight. Through the substance of the ceiling, I feel the fundamental vibration, the deep, resonant hum from the floor far below. It is the first and last thing I will ever know, a constant thrum that is the baseline of my existence. I am a tiny lens for this low, eternal Om, and it holds me in place.

Slowly, I gain mass. I am joined by others, motes of coolness like myself, and we become a singular bead of liquid, swelling with a stolen weight. Now, I can see. The world opens up beneath me. The vast, honey-lit concavity is a universe, and from my vantage on its highest boundary, I see it all. I see the far wall, a curving infinity away, where other droplets like myself glisten as distant, stationary stars. I see the great beetle-harvesters, creeping along the amber expanse like abrading constellations, their diamond mandibles scoring the skin of the world. Their rhythm, *shhhhh-CHUNK*, is a sound that travels through the air to me, a counterpoint to the hum I feel in my substance.

I see the floor. I see the polished tracks of the trilobite-transporters, dark veins on the glowing amber. I watch the creatures themselves, flowing like mercury in their geometric paths, great, slow comets of purpose dragging glittering dust toward the pulsing sphincters of the vents. Their dragging is a sound I can’t quite hear from here, but I can see its effect: a silken shimmering on the air, the physical manifestation of a percussive wave.

I grow heavier. The pull of the world’s center becomes an imperative. My connection to the ceiling thins, stretches. There is a moment of perfect, precarious tension, and then I let go.

I do not fall. I *trace*.

My journey is not a chaotic plummet but an elegant, pre-ordained descent. I am captured by a groove so shallow it is almost theoretical, a line of memory in the wall worn smooth by a billion billion years of my own passage. I am a bead of light sliding down a filament of cosmic glass.

The world shifts. The sounds change as my position changes. The scrape of the harvesters grows deafening as I slide past one, close enough to see the intricate, non-biological joints of its legs, the terrifying, passionless industry of its mandibles grinding away the very surface I am sliding upon. The universe of the wall becomes a landscape of its own, with microscopic textures and temperature variations.

My path carries me past the center of it all, that immense volume of clear air where the great void is not empty. As I slide by, my trajectory bends. Light itself seems to curdle around a point of immense, invisible gravity. I am not seeing a thing, but the *effect* of a thing. I feel a pressure, a psychic density that warps my very being for the nanosecond I am adjacent to it. I am a fleeting flaw in its lens, a momentary bead of sweat on the brow of a stillborn god. I am a single, trivial piece of sensory data being processed by the Resonant, a tiny, cool thought streaking through the vast, warm mind.

The floor rushes up to meet me. The journey is over.

I strike the polished amber not with a splash, but with a *note*—a tiny, liquid chime that is instantly lost in the overwhelming symphony of the factory. My form shatters. My brief, singular identity as a drop dissolves.

But I am not gone. I am drunk by the warm, living floor. My coolness is absorbed, my substance drawn down into the lignous shell, towards the alchemical heart of the system. I am carried in the biological current toward one of the great processing vents. There, in the gurgling, hissing heat, I am unmade. I am converted from liquid to vapor, my essence given back to the air as a puff of sterile steam.

I am the exhalation.

I rise. Weightless now, I drift back up through the immense, ringing clarity. I pass the invisible Resonant again, this time as a formless ghost. I pass the slow-motion ballet of the harvesters. I climb until I can climb no more, finding my place once again in the high, warm amber of the ceiling.

Here, I will wait. I will feel the hum. I will begin to gather, to cohere from the atmosphere heavy with my own dispersed self. I am not an observer cast into this world; I am a single beat in its slow metabolism, a punctuation mark in its eternal sentence. My name is a drip. My purpose is to fall. And I will do so again. Forever.
