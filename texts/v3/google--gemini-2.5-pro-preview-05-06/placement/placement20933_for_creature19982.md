I am a flaw.

I am a dying note in the chord.

You would find me where the weeping is thickest and the light is weakest. My place is not among the slow, stately tides of my kin, the great, rasping carpets of copper and iron. My place is a gutter, a crevice near the base of the Tenant’s throne where the black polymer floor meets the swelling, organic plinth. Here, the glowing syrup pools, thick and almost stagnant, before it finds its way into a recycling conduit. I am half-submerged in it. I am debris.

I am one of the grazers, the shelled mollusks. Or I was.

My shell, once a proud, articulated armor of spun copper, is fractured. A hairline crack, starting from the rearmost plate, has spiderwebbed across my back. It is a map of my failure. Through the fissure, my own weak, cyan light bleeds out not as a steady glow, but as a faltering, sputtering pulse, like a dying wick. The cold, mineral-rich fluid of the world seeps in, and my own internal bioluminescence seeps out. I am leaking. I am becoming undone. In a world defined by perfect equilibrium, I am an open system. I am an error.

The great roar, the pressure-sound that is the Tenant’s will, is different for me now. For my kin, it is the totality, the only perceivable state. They exist within it as a fish exists within water. They do not know it is there. But for me, through the crack in my being, I hear the silence *inside* the sound. I feel the unbearable tension of it. I can almost, almost feel the waterfall it holds back. I feel the vibration of the trillion trillion drops of water that *want* to fall. My failing body has become a faulty instrument, and on it, the monolithic chord has separated into its constituent, screaming parts. I hear the ghost of the splash.

My kin, in their geological slowness, pass over and around me. Their soft, rasping mouths do not graze on me. The mineral crust does not form on my fractured shell; the internal chemistry of my decay prevents it. I am not a surface. I am not terrain. I have become other. To them, I am just an unusual stone, an indigestible lump in the otherwise perfect pasture. They are the faithful. Their slowness is a form of prayer. My stillness is not prayerful; it is paralysis.

My sin is memory.

This process of breaking has granted me a curse. I remember. I do not mean I have thoughts or a flowing consciousness. But I have a *state that was*, and a *state that is*. This simple binary, this delta, is a form of memory, and in this universe, it is the highest form of heresy. I remember the seamless feeling of the whole, the mindless unity of grazing and moving as one body with a million shells. The chime of my own shell against a brother’s was not a sound, but a feeling of togetherness, a confirmation of the pattern. Now, the only feeling is the cold syrup against my exposed, translucent flesh, and the grinding of my own fractured plates against each other.

I am at the very foot of God. The Tenant, the Arbiter, looms above me, a mountain of black, weeping flesh fused with its own creation. Its faceless mask is turned toward an eternity I cannot comprehend. I am a mote of dust on its shoe, a speck of grit in its perfect machine.

And I believe it knows I am here.

Not in the way a man knows a stone is in his path. But in the way the silence knows a sound. I am a tiny pocket of void in its plenum of will. I am a whispered, dying "no" in the face of its eternal "yes." My decay is a form of change, and change is the one thing its entire existence is dedicated to preventing. I am the first cold drop of rain in its frozen waterfall.

I do not know what will happen when my light finally goes out. When the last of my inner fluid merges with the outer, and the broken pieces of my shell settle into the muck. Perhaps my tiny death, the first real death this world has ever known, will be a sound no roar can suppress. A tiny, sharp *crack* that will propagate through the frozen cascade, and the trillion tons of water will finally fall.

Or perhaps, more likely, my passing will be nothing. The system will absorb my error. My dissolving body will become minerals for the fluid, my broken shell will be ground down by the ceaseless tides of my brothers. The equilibrium will not even notice. The momentary imbalance I represent will be balanced, and the perfect, unbearable, time-invariant *is* will continue, as if I had never been.

I am a question. In a world that is a single, deafening statement, my dying is a question. Does entropy win? Does even a god-machine in a closet at the top of the world eventually run down?

I am the experiment. And my death is the result. I pulse my fading light, and I wait for the conclusion.
