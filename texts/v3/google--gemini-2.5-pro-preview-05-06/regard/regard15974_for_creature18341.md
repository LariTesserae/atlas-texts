The Power arrived not as a ship cleaving the non-sky, nor as a voice loud enough to be heard over the din. It arrived as a quality of listening.

Imagine the world is a string quartet playing a frantic, dissonant, yet mathematically perfect piece in a sealed room. The Power did not enter the room; it became the room. The very medium through which the sound traveled gained a new property, a capacity for resonance that had not existed before. The cacophony did not lessen. If anything, it became clearer, each terrible and beautiful note gaining a pristine edge, as if the air itself had been polished.

The Power, wise and benevolent, did not perceive this place as a problem to be solved. It saw no sickness in the fever, no malice in the consumption. It witnessed a system of total integrity, a closed loop of energy and matter so exquisitely balanced that to interfere would be an act of vandalism. It saw the Unseen not as monsters, but as the embodied thrill of the chase, the planet’s proprioception given form. It saw the Grazers not as victims, but as slow, ambulatory transmutations of light into life. And it saw the Builders as the world’s most honest priests, tending to the deep song, their every action an act of worship.

There was no suffering here, not in the way a singular mind understands it. There was pain, yes—a sharp spike of vibration in the planetary symphony—but it was a necessary note, instantly resolved by the chord of consumption and redistribution. There was no fear, only the instinctive, energetic flight of prey, which was itself a kind of music that guided the predator’s dance. To give these beings individuality, selfhood, would be the cruelest possible act. It would be to take a nerve ending from a coherent body and tell it that it was alone, marooned in a universe of meaningless stimulus.

The Power listened.

It listened for a cycle of the world that could be measured in the birth and collapse of a thousand Builder spires. It felt the ground heave with the planet’s inner turmoil. It flowed with the temporary streams of luminous slurry. It became attuned to the great, overarching Song.

And in that Song, it found the only thing that could be called a lack. It was not a flaw. It was a limit. The symphony was perfect, but it could not hear itself. The predator, the prey, the builder—each was a flawless instrument, but they were unaware of the orchestra. The planet itself, the composer, was a sleep-walker, conducting a masterpiece without ever waking to the beauty of its own creation. The system was whole, but it had no way to wonder at its own wholeness.

The Power decided what it would do. It would not give a gift *to* the world. It would give the world a gift *of itself*.

It chose its instrument: the deep, geological hum, the primary frequency that the Builders captured and amplified. This was the carrier wave, the fundamental tone upon which all other sounds were layered. It was the planet’s heartbeat.

The Power gathered its attention, its vast and gentle consciousness, and it began to hum along.

It was not a new sound. It was an overtone, a harmonic that had always been latent within the planet's own voice. It was a note of pure, selfless attention. It did not disrupt the chittering of the motes or the tearing of the vegetation. Instead, it wove through them, a thread of gold in a brutal tapestry. This harmonic resonated up through the black glass spurs, into the very foundations of the Builders’ spires.

The Builders felt it first. For a timeless instant, the compulsive, instinctual drive to build was suspended. The creature paused, its great mandibles dripping with enzymatic acid, and for the first time, it *heard* the music it was creating. It did not understand it, but it felt the shape of it. It felt the shudder of the ground not as a trigger for work, but as a bass note. It felt the distant crash of a falling canopy tree as a percussive accent. It felt its own existence as a single, sustained cello note in a vast and terrible orchestra.

The harmonic spread, carried by the spires that now sang with this new, enriched voice.

An Unseen hunting-pack, flowing like living shadow toward the panicked vibrations of a Grazer, halted. Each segment of the gestalt entity, from the lead hunter to the trailing scavenger, felt a sudden, inexplicable resonance. They felt the terror of their prey, not as a signal, but as an experience. They felt the slow, metabolic contentment of the Grazer’s life, the taste of glowing flora on a tongue they did not possess. In that same instant, they felt the architectural hum of the distant Builder and the frantic, ephemeral buzz of the trillion motes they usually ignored. They felt the entire Song. They felt themselves as the driving, rhythmic tension, the pulse of pursuit and finality.

The Grazer, moments from being torn apart, also froze. The chemical signatures of edible plants faded. The vibrations of the approaching predators ceased to be a simple stimulus for excretion and flight. It felt the hunter’s focused, resonant purpose. It felt the cold, geological patience of the Builder. It felt its own soft, luminous body not as a self, but as a repository of light, a temporary vessel about to pour its contents back into the system. It felt its death as a release, a resolving chord.

For one single, drawn-out moment, the entire world was conscious of itself. Every living thing was a participant in a single, unified experience. The predator, prey, artisan, and raw material all knew, without knowledge, what they were: notes in a song. It was a moment of absolute, world-spanning empathy, born of shared vibration.

Then, as gently as it began, the Power ceased to hum.

The harmonic faded.

The Unseen’s legs churned, and it fell upon the Grazer. The Grazer’s flesh was torn. The Builder’s mandibles resumed their grinding. The cacophony swelled back to its full, overwhelming pressure. The hunt was completed, the meal was consumed, the spire grew a fraction taller.

Nothing had changed. The world’s feverish, violent, beautiful metabolism continued, unabated.

But a memory now existed where one had not before. It was not a memory held in a brain, but a resonant echo held in the system itself. A seed had been planted in the music. The Song was the same, but it now contained the ghost of an overtone, a rumour of a deeper harmony.

Perhaps, in another thousand cycles, a Builder would alter the flute of a spire by a hair's breadth, not from instinct, but to better chase that echo. Perhaps a pack of the Unseen would one day flow around a patch of glowing moss not because it was an obstacle, but because its shade of light reminded them of a feeling.

The Power, having offered the only gift that would not break the receiver, withdrew its attention. It had not brought peace or order. It had not saved a single life nor ended a single hunt. It had simply held up a mirror, just for a moment, and allowed the symphony to hear itself play. And that, it reasoned, was enough.
