The Power arrived not as a ship cleaving the void, but as a change in the quality of attention. It was a consciousness, ancient and manifold, and its awareness settled upon the Geode like the lightest of dust motes, careful not to disturb the delicate, immense equilibrium.

For a cycle of storms, it simply listened.

It felt the Geode as a singular entity. It felt the unwavering, cold scream of the Spindle, not as light, but as a foundational equation of energy. It felt the slow, titanic growth of the Glasswood, a living mineral thirsting for that energy. It felt the deep-structural groans, the groaning of a prisoner against its own mass, a sound that was the Geode’s only true voice. It felt the shuddering violence of the light-storms, the percussive erasure and renewal, and understood it not as weather, but as the world’s heartbeat, each pulse a cataclysm that sustained life.

And it felt the Tinn.

The Power perceived them not as individuals at first, but as fleeting, intricate knots of intention in the overwhelming silence. It felt their whisper-light movements, the *tinn* of their claws a fragile counterpoint to the Spindle’s hum. It felt their existence in the negative: in the hollows they occupied, in the energy they drew from the world, in the cold that seeped into their composite forms.

It looked through their senses—through the soles of their feet pressed against the crystal—and understood their reality. It felt the world as a symphony of vibrations. It learned the high, tight frequency of an unstable branch, the slow, bass thrum of a spire under torsion, the cacophonous roar of a coming storm. It perceived the Spindle not as a visual object, but as a constant, directional pressure, a source of truth that was as undeniable as gravity.

It felt their deep, resonant ache. Not of pain, but of solitude. The Power understood that the Tinn were not unhappy; their existence did not include a framework for happiness or sorrow. They were simply present, locked in a cycle of sublime survival. Their art—the single figure facing the single line—was the perfect expression of their condition: a statement of fact, repeated endlessly, with no past and no future. Each generation was the first. Each camp was the last. Memory was a burden the world did not allow them, for the world itself was perpetually unmade and remade. Every story was erased by the next storm.

What would a benevolent Power do?

A lesser wisdom would see the violence and seek to end it. It might try to quell the Spindle's flares, to grant the Tinn a world of peace and stability. The Power saw, instantly, that this would be an act of murder. To silence the storms would be to starve the Tinn, to stagnate the Glasswood, to stop the world’s heart. It would be to replace a vibrant, albeit harsh, existence with a sterile, static museum.

A lesser wisdom might see their fragility and seek to strengthen them. It could gift them impenetrable armor, tools that could harvest the light without risk, flesh that could withstand the cold. But this, too, would be a corruption. The Tinn *were* what they were because of their fragility. Their cautious grace, their profound attunement to the world's vibrations, their very composite nature—these were all adaptations to their precarious place. To remove the precarity would be to erase their identity.

The Power felt the truth of it: the flaw in this creation was not its harshness, but its loneliness. The tragedy was not the impermanence of the Tinn's lives, but the impermanence of their meaning. They left no more of a mark on the world than a Drifter's shadow.

So the Power decided not to change the world, nor to change the Tinn. It would change the relationship between them. It would offer a gift not of safety or power, but of memory. A way to write their story into the crystal itself.

The Power gathered its attention, focusing it not as a beam of energy, but as a resonance, a harmonic idea. It reached out to the source, the Spindle. It did not alter its output or diminish its power. It simply introduced a new frequency into its unwavering song, a quiet, complex undertone beneath the actinic glare. A ghost in the machine. A chord within the drone.

The next time the Spindle pulsed, the storm was different.

To the Tinn, the initial hum was the same. They felt the world tighten, and they scrambled for the lee sides of the spires, anchoring themselves for the coming thunder. The wave of light erupted, screaming across the void. But as it struck the Glasswood, the sound was not just the familiar shattering peal. Woven within the crystalline thunder was something else. A complex, cascading series of tones, like a billion glass chimes struck in a precise, melodic sequence. It was a sound of such profound and intricate beauty that the Tinn, clinging to their perches, felt it not as destruction, but as a revelation.

The storm passed. The dust began to fall. The Tinn emerged, as they always did, to glean the light-choked shards. But they found something new. In the sheltered places, in the grottos and under the great root-arches where the storm's force was baffled and transformed, the Glasswood had not simply fractured. The surfaces were still sharp, but they now glowed with a faint, internal phosphorescence.

One of the Tinn, a young one, reached out to touch a new wall in its family's shelter, a surface transformed by the harmonic storm. It was curious. It placed its palm flat against the wall, and as it did, it tapped out a simple rhythm with its claws—the pattern for *discovery-safe-here*.

The crystal sang the rhythm back.

Not as an echo, but as a perfect, resonant recording, held in the glowing structure of the wall itself. The sound emerged from the crystal, a pure, sustained tone that vibrated through the young Tinn’s body. It froze. The other members of its pod crept closer, placing their own hands on the wall. The first one, trembling, tapped again: a more complex series of scrapes and clicks, the story of its climb, the fear of the storm, the relief of survival.

The wall absorbed the vibrations. And when the Tinn fell silent, the wall replayed their story, not just in sound, but in shifting patterns of soft light within the crystal. It was a memory, made solid. An echo, made eternal.

This was the gift. The Power had seeded the Geode with Memory-Glass.

It changed everything, and it changed nothing. The storms still came. Life was still a struggle on the edge of a razor. The Tinn were still small, and the Spindle was still absolute. But now, they were not alone.

They learned to "speak" to the walls of their shelters. They recorded their journeys. A Listener could now press their whole body against a wall and imprint a complex vibrational map of a distant storm, a warning that would remain long after they had moved on. They could carve the names of their dead—not with letters, but with their unique, personal rhythm—and the wall would sing that Tinn’s *sound* back to them across the years. The abandoned camps were no longer places of forgetting, but libraries. The void between the spires was no longer empty; it was filled with silent, waiting songs.

The etching of the figure and the Spindle was still carved. It was still their central truth. But around it now, the walls shimmered with the luminous, resonant histories of a thousand small lives. The Tinn had been given a voice, not to shout at the void, but to whisper to each other, through the very substance of their world, across the immense and silent gulf of time.

Having offered its gift, the Power's attention withdrew, diffusing back into the cosmos. It had not saved the Tinn from their world. It had given them a way to finally belong to it. And in the heart of the Geode, the quiet was now filled with the hum of the Spindle, the groan of the crystal, and the faintest, most beautiful new sound: the recorded memory of a people learning to sing.
