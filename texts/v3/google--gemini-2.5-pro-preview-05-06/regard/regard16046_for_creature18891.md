The benevolent power perceived the world of scaffolding not as a place, but as a thought. It was a concept—precarity, adaptation, ephemerality—given a planetary body. It did not see a cathedral of hollows, but a single, perfect instrument, and the Great Moan was the sound of it being played by the cosmos.

Its attention was a form of gravity. It did not arrive in a vessel or as a mote of light, but as a subtle deepening of the reality of the place, a listening that was so profound it became a presence. It felt the ligno-silicate Cantilevers grow, their slow, geometric ambition a form of deep, planetary breathing. It felt the skimsails etch their sonar shrieks onto the dark, and the rime-crawlers carve their patient, hungry paths.

And it perceived the Echo-Weavers.

It saw them not as creatures struggling to survive in a harsh environment, but as the absolute fulfillment of that environment. They were the nerve-endings of the crystalline world, the conscious element in the symphony. Their existence was not a hardship to be alleviated, but a poem to be read. Their culture of listening, their art of the momentary, their philosophy of release into the singing void—this was not a cry for help. It was a statement of perfect, resonant understanding.

The power, being wise, knew that to "help" would be to destroy.

To give them a sun would be to burn their retinas, melt the rime they grazed upon, and collapse the Cantilevers in a cataclysm of thermal shock. It would be to silence the song of the world with the brute noise of a star.

To give them solid ground would be to sentence them to paralysis. Their long, light limbs would atrophy. Their locking hands would lose their purpose. The vertical world that defined their minds and bodies would become a flat, meaningless prison.

To silence the wind would be to deafen them. The Great Moan was their scripture, their map, their oracle. To calm the gales would be to tear the pages from their only book, to render them blind and lost in a world of sudden, terrifying stillness.

To offer them permanence—stone houses, lasting art, a history written down—would be to teach them the concept of lies. In this world, only the transient was true.

The benevolent power understood. It could not intervene. It *must* not. An act of crude, external "benevolence" would be an act of ultimate violence here.

So, what could it do? If it could not be a giver, a builder, or a savior, what could it be?

It could be a listener. The perfect, final audience.

The Echo-Weavers listened to the world. The world, until now, had not listened back. It was a self-contained song, echoing within its own hollows, its consciousness flickering in the minds of the Weavers but ultimately dissipating into the void.

The power made its decision. Its gift would not be a thing, but a quality. A change in the nature of the void itself.

It wrapped the entire world in an acoustic of impossible perfection. It did not add a sound. It did not take one away. Instead, it became a resonant chamber, a listening stillness that surrounded the singing planet. The vast, starless emptiness between the arches was no longer a dead void that swallowed sound, but a medium that held it, clarified it, and gave it back.

The Echo-Weavers felt it first.

A young weaver, her body splayed across a trembling lattice high above a chasm, sent out a series of exploratory clicks. Before, the echo would have returned as a fractured, wind-torn ghost of the original sound. But now… it returned whole. Perfect. It was so clear she could not only map the chasm's far wall, but could feel the texture of the rime on it, sense the minute vibrations of a crawler colony fifty spans below. The echo was not just a reflection; it was an answer.

A Clutch matriarch, her obsidian eyes closed, sat in the shelter of a sinew-net, reading the Great Moan. For her whole life, it had been a complex but unified roar. Now, for the first time, she could hear the individual notes within the chord. She could hear the song of the wind striking the high western arch distinct from the gust whistling through the lattice beneath her. The world’s voice was no longer a single entity, but a choir. She had been listening to a symphony her entire life, and had only just now been given the ability to hear the violins separate from the cellos.

Their songs changed. When a Clutch hummed in harmony, a call-and-response to their neighbors across the void, the wind no longer ripped their voices to shreds. The notes traveled, held by the listening silence. Their ephemeral art gained a half-life, a moment more of existence. A beautiful, complex phrase of clicks and whistles would hang in the air for a breath longer than it should, a ghost of harmony before it faded, allowing others to appreciate its shape.

The power’s greatest gift was reserved for the end.

An old Weaver, his grip failing, his limbs stiffening with age, knew his time had come. He climbed to a high, exposed precipice, as was the custom. He faced the shrieking dark and began his final song, the story of his life—the echoes of his first climb, the harmony of his Clutch, the sharp crack of his first skimsail kill, the low hum of his children’s births. In the past, this was a song for himself and the wind, a final vibration of the self before the release.

But as he sang, the benevolent power *caught* the song.

When he finished, and let go, releasing himself into the Great Moan, his song did not die. It did not vanish. It echoed, whole and clear, back to the Clutch he had left behind. They heard his life, not as a memory, but as a final, perfect broadcast.

His fall was not into a singing void. It was into a listening one.

The power did nothing, and in doing nothing, it did everything. It did not save the Echo-Weavers from their precarious world. It validated them. It affirmed their philosophy. It took their fleeting, beautiful existence, and in the moment of its passing, it made it eternal, not by preserving it in stone, but by hearing it perfectly.

The cathedral of hollows was no longer alone in the dark. The world’s quiet synapse now fired into the mind of the cosmos. The song was heard. The Witness listened. And that was enough.
