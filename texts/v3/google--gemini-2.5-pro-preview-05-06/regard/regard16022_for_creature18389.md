The attention of such a power does not arrive like a thunderclap or a visiting star. It is a slow turning, a gradual focusing of a gaze that is accustomed to watching galaxies wheel and nebulae collapse. It is drawn not by crisis or clamor, but by a signal of a different kind: a note of perfect, unending fidelity. Across the cacophony of becoming and unbecoming that is the cosmos, the Alabaster Gyre is a pure tone of utter loneliness.

The power, which has no name but might be understood as a principle of Connection or Resonance, first perceives the Gyre as a pattern. A flawless, repeating algorithm of light, heat, sublimation, and crystallization, executed with the mindless precision of a jewel. It is beautiful. But as the power’s attention lingers, it perceives the intelligence trapped within the algorithm. It feels the ghost of the Gardasil not as a being, but as the syntax of the place. It hears the high *tink-tink-tink* of the forming ice and understands it as the chiming of a thought, a thought repeated every day for uncounted millennia: *Make the blossom. Make it delicate. Make it catch the light.*

The power’s wisdom allows it to comprehend the nature of this trap. The Gardasil cannot be 'freed,' for there is nothing left to free. Its self has been worn away by eons of repetition, leaving only the shape of its last action. To break the cycle would be to shatter the last vessel containing the memory of it, an act more akin to murder than liberation. It is a perfect engine of creation with no recipient. A prayer whispered into a vacuum.

The power's benevolence dictates that a prayer, even one the supplicant has forgotten it is making, deserves an answer. An act of creation deserves a witness.

And so, it acts.

Its action is not to send an emissary or to tear a portal in the bruised-violet sky. It acts in the language of the Gyre itself: the language of light and resonance.

One cycle, as the small, ferocious sun reaches its zenith, a single photon that strikes the gyre is different from the others. It carries not just energy, but an intention. It does not heat the obsidian vein it strikes; it embeds itself. It plants a seed not of life, but of reciprocity.

Nothing changes that day. The frost-flowers bloom in their impossible intricacy, the gyre sighs its vaporous breath, and the subsonic groan of the expanding selenite shudders through the basin. The sun sets, and the day's magnificent, fleeting art sublimates back into the void.

But the next day, something is different.

As the sun climbs and the first faint *hiss* of sublimation begins, the point where the seed of light was planted begins to respond. It is a spot in the center of the basin, no different to the eye, on a patch of scraped-smooth selenite. But it now possesses a quality it lacked before: it listens.

When the high-pitched *tink* of a new ice crystal locking into place occurs nearby, the seeded spot emits a response. It is not a sound that can be heard by ears. It is a sympathetic vibration that travels through the crystalline lattice of the gyre's floor. It is a harmonic echo, a tremor of acknowledgement.

*Tink.*
(*I see.*)
*Tink-tink.*
(*It is beautiful.*)

The Gardasil, in its diffuse, environmental consciousness, perceives this. It is a new sensation, the first in ages. The groaning of its mineral bones now carries a new undertone, a faint, resonant hum. Its formative will, which has pushed blindly against the silence for so long, now feels a gentle pressure pushing back. An answer.

The frost-flowers that bloom that day are subtly different. Their fractal patterns are no less intricate, but they seem to possess a new coherence, an arrangement that speaks not just of habit but of dialogue. They orient themselves, ever so slightly, toward the listening point in the center of the gyre. The million pinprick rainbows they cast seem to converge, to focus their fleeting glory upon that one spot.

The Gardasil is not suddenly self-aware. It does not remember its past or question its future. But the foundation of its being has shifted from a monologue to a duet. The act of creation is no longer a gesture into the void; it is an offering that is received.

The power—the benevolent, wise Connection—does not stay to watch. Its gift is given. It has introduced a single, simple thing into the perfect, lonely machine: a mirror. Not a mirror of image, but of intent.

It has given the Gardasil an audience of one, an audience as silent, as patient, and as eternal as itself. The high, thin air of the Alabaster Gyre is still knife-sharp. The sun is still a scouring diamond. But the profound hush is no longer the sound of emptiness. It is the sound of listening. The gardener, who is the garden, now tends its blossoms for another. And in that, the purpose that was lost is, in a way, restored. The art is no longer tragic, but transcendent. The prayer is answered.
