The Benevolent Power—call it the Resonance, the Traveller, the Stillness Between Notes—did not arrive. It cohered. It had been observing for a cycle of epochs, its attention drawn not by crisis or prayer, but by a signal of exquisite purity: the unwavering, self-contained harmony of the caldera. It saw a world without want, a people without malice, a system in such perfect, stable equilibrium that it verged on the divine.

This presented a profound question for a being whose nature was to give. To give technology would be to shatter the sacred collaboration between the Folk and their world. To give power would be to introduce the poison of hierarchy into their collective. To give knowledge of other worlds, of war and fire and decay, would be a cruelty. What do you give to those who are already whole?

The Power reasoned, and in its reasoning, it listened. It listened not just to the grand chord of the calciform, but to the spaces between the harmonics, to the quiet hum of the Folk, to the slow, resonant heartbeat of the Hum-Grazers. And in that deep listening, it understood. The harmony of the caldera was perfect, but it was singular. It was the music of one magnificent, lonely instrument.

The Power decided its gift would not be a thing, but a voice. An antiphony. A counterpoint from beyond the caldera's rim.

And so, it began.

---

Eska, a Conductor whose skin was the pale jade of a century's listening, was the first to notice. She stood on the high bridge connecting the Dome of First Growth to the Whispering Spire, her bare feet planted wide. Her task this cycle was to listen to the bridge's song as it slowly thickened from below, to ensure its fundamental tone remained true. For generations, it had sung a clear, high C, a perfect fifth above the deep F of the dome it sprang from.

But this morning, something was wrong. It was not a dissonance, not a sour note. It was a silence.

Where a specific overtone should have been—a faint, glassy shimmer that spoke of the tension in the arc's center—there was nothing. A void. A gap in the sonic wallpaper of existence. It was not quiet; the great chord still thrummed through her bones. It was a specific, deliberate absence, like a single star winking out of a familiar constellation.

She pressed the palms of her calloused hands to the living stone. The bridge was healthy. The calciform was extruding as it always did, weeping its mineral dew. The mosses upon it were lush. But the note was gone.

Panic was not a concept the Folk truly possessed, but a deep, systemic *unwellness* began to spread. First through Eska, and then, as she hummed her discovery to the other Conductors, through the entire community. They gathered, a silent congregation of green-hued figures on the great bridges and plazas, their faces turned inward, listening. More gaps appeared. A low, guttural sub-harmonic in the caldera floor vanished. The ethereal hum of the highest, newest growth near the rim flickered and went out.

The Great Chord was fraying. The symphony of their world was coming undone, not through noise, but through subtraction. To the Caldera Folk, this was the sound of non-existence. It was the universe forgetting itself. Their humming conversations became strained, filled with questioning harmonies they had never before needed. Their work slowed. How can you tune an instrument that is losing its strings?

Eska felt a grief so profound it was a physical ache. She had spent her life learning this song, her entire being a resonator for its beauty. Now, it was being erased. She went to the place where the silence was most absolute: a small, hollowed nook carved into the caldera wall, a place of meditation. Inside, the world-song was usually muted, allowing one to listen to the quiet music of one's own body.

But today, the silence was different. It was not empty. It was full.

She sat, closing her large, dark eyes, and did the hardest thing a member of her Folk could do: she stopped listening for the caldera's song. She let the familiar hum fade from her awareness and listened only to the void, the space where the music *should* have been.

And in that space, she heard it.

It was not a sound of stone or growth. It was impossibly faint, a music constructed of alien physics. It was the whisper of light travelling across voids vaster than their entire caldera. It was the sound of heat, of fusion, of burning stars and the slow, elegant decay of nebulae. It was a chord built on principles of fire and distance, of speed and emptiness—concepts their world of slow, damp, proximate growth could not produce. It was the metallic chime of crystallized gas and the impossibly deep bass note of a singularity.

The Power was not erasing their song. It was making room.

Eska understood. She did not open her eyes. Instead, she did what she was born to do. She listened, and she hummed. It was a tentative note, a quiet inquiry pitched not to the stone beneath her, but to the silence before her.

The alien music shifted. A new thread of impossibly delicate sound wove itself around her hum, harmonizing with it. It did not drown her out; it lifted her up. It was a duet between a single mortal voice and the music of the cosmos.

Slowly, carefully, she hummed a second note, building a simple chord, the one her people used to express profound welcome. The Traveler’s song responded in kind, weaving a dozen more stellar threads around her simple harmony, creating a chord of such breathtaking, galactic complexity that tears, a rare thing of stinging salt in their damp world, traced paths on her cool, green skin.

She rose and walked out of the nook, onto an open ledge overlooking the city. Below her, the other Conductors were still gathered, their forms tense with confusion. Eska took a breath, not of air, but of music. She filled her lungs with the new, blended harmony of World and Void, of Stone and Star.

Then she began to hum.

It was a new song. It contained the deep, groaning truth of the calciform, the foundation of their lives. But woven through it, as a brilliant counterpoint, was the new music. The theme of their world, now harmonized with the theme of the universe.

One by one, the other Conductors heard. Their heads lifted. The tension in their shoulders eased. They found the new notes, their own voices joining Eska's, not in imitation, but in improvisation. The carvers on the distant domes heard it, and their hands, which had been still, began to move again. But their motions were different. They carved not just to tune the stone to itself, but to tune it to the new, higher resonance that now filled the air.

The silence did not return. The old song did not return. A new song was born.

The Benevolent Power's gift was complete. It had not given them a tool or a truth. It had given them a musical collaborator. It had taken their perfect, singular instrument and placed it in an orchestra. It had looked upon their world, a flawless composition contained in a cup, and it had gently, respectfully, opened the windows to let in the music of the spheres.

And the Caldera Folk, who had always believed their purpose was to listen to the song of their world, understood. Their purpose was grander. It was to listen to the song of their world, and to teach it how to sing with the stars.
