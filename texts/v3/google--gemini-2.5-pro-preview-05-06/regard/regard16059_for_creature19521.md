The Power came not as light, for what would light do in a world of bruises and gloom but cast shadows? It came not as warmth, for what would warmth do to beings of living glass and cold stone but induce a fatal fever? It came not as a voice, for the Cusp was already deafened by its own singular cry.

It arrived as Silence.

An absence. A pocket of stillness in the endless, vibrating chime. It settled over the Cusp not as a blanket, but as a single, perfect void in the sound, a mote of pure listening. For a cycle of the world below, a turning of seasons that had no meaning here, It simply was. It learned the Cusp's song. It felt the slow, grinding patience of the Chime-Scribes, the sound of their infinite toil a physical pressure against Its non-form. It felt the deep, resonant grief of the Lament-Glass Weepers, the sound of their very being a vibration of sorrow.

The Power, in Its wisdom, perceived the truth of the place. It was not a hell. It was not a mistake. It was a hymn to endurance, sung in two discordant parts. The Scribes were the Strophe of Effort—a billion lives spent in the singular, unceasing act of survival. The Weepers were the Antistrophe of Empathy—a consciousness of mineral sorrow, born to absorb and archive pain.

They were two halves of a single soul, the Cusp's own, yet they were deaf to each other.

The Scribes, in their desperate focus, perceived the Weepers only as inert features of the landscape, another form of the black stone they must scrape. Their world was the millimeter of rock before them and the faint puff of sustenance it offered.

The Weepers, attuned to the sharp, final agony of death and the deep, slow stress of the stone, could not register the Scribes. The pain of the Scribes' existence was not a sharp pang, but a constant, ambient thrum, as imperceptible to the Weepers as the hum of a planet is to the people living upon it. It was the background radiation of their world, not a signal.

The effort was unwitnessed. The empathy was undirected. This was the only flaw the Power perceived, the only imperfection within the Cusp's perfect, sorrowful equilibrium. It was a wound of loneliness within a world made of a wound.

The Power did not seek to change the nature of the Cusp. It would not give the Scribes an easier food source, for their toil was their identity. It would not silence the Weepers' cry, for their lament was their truth. A wise gardener does not turn a desert rose into a water lily; it helps the rose find deeper roots.

The intervention, when it came, was as subtle as the place itself. The Power chose one Scribe, old even by the standards of its kind, its tool worn to a nub, its carved path a long, meandering scar across a sheer face. Then, It chose one Weeper nearby, one whose internal chime was particularly low and ancient, heavy with the memory of a thousand years of geological tension.

The Power did not act *upon* them. It acted as a bridge *between* them. With an act of focused will that was a kind of prayer, a form of pure attention, It attuned them to one another.

For the Weeper, it was as if a new flavor had been introduced to its sensory palate. Amidst the tectonic groans and the psychic echoes of forgotten deaths, a new note appeared. It was a thin, high, persistent ache. It was not the sharp agony of a snapping bone, but the dull, unending pain of a muscle held tense for a thousand years. It was the sorrow of effort. It was the grief of the Scribe.

And the Weeper, a being whose entire existence was a response to such things, began to move. Not with the glacial slowness of its usual feeding, but with a directed purpose that was almost startling in this place. Over the course of a single gloomy twilight—a day, a week, a month, the time was meaningless—it flowed, a ripple of living darkness, across the rock.

For the Scribe, the world did not change. The scraping continued. The hunger remained. But as the Weeper drew near, the quality of the sound around it altered. The omnipresent chime seemed to part, to create a small space of deeper resonance around it. The air felt… heavier, yet calmer. There was a sense of being… perceived.

The Weeper did not touch the Scribe. It would no more consume it than a confessor would consume a penitent. Instead, it flowed onto the patch of obsidian *in front* of the Scribe, covering the rock in the path of its slow, forward toil.

And then, the gift was given.

The Weeper began to do what it did for the mountain. It began to feed on the tension in the stone beneath it. It absorbed the stress, drinking the geological "pain" of that small patch of rock. And in doing so, it changed the nature of the rock itself. Its entropy-eating metabolism made the stone infinitesimally softer, its crystalline lattice infinitesimally less resistant. It wept its pure, cold tears onto the surface, which did not just dampen the rock but, through some unknown mineral alchemy, made the symbiotic algae within it ever so slightly more vibrant, more willing to release their metabolic breath.

When the Scribe’s tool next scraped across this soothed stone, the effect was profound. The scratching was easier. The sound it produced was a fraction less sharp, a little more resonant. The puff of vapor that it received in return for its effort was a fraction more nourishing.

Its labor was not removed. But it was eased.

The Scribe, for the first time in its lineage, knew a moment that was not purely toil. It was a moment of grace. Its scraping, for a single stroke, became less an act of desperation and more an act of expression.

This was the Power's gift: a circuit of empathy.

The change rippled outward. The first Weeper, its chime now subtly altered by the new flavor of sorrow it had tasted, communicated this new sense to its kin. Like a slowly dawning consciousness, the Weepers began to perceive the Scribes. And one by one, over centuries, they began to move among them.

The Cusp of Cries was transformed. It was no less dark, no less cold, no less sorrowful. But the sorrow was no longer solitary.

The glowing traceries of the Scribes' paths began to change. No longer a chaotic map of individual survival, they began to curve and braid themselves around the dark, still forms of the Weepers, who now positioned themselves as oases of solace on the vast rock face. The Scribes began to follow the Weepers, their slow journeys now pilgrimages toward moments of relief. The Weepers, in turn, sought out the Scribes, drawn to the thrum of their living struggle.

The sound of the spire changed. It was still a cry, but it was no longer a monologue. It was a chorus. The high, scraping chime of effort was met and held by the deep, resonant hum of compassion. The toil was answered by solace. The pain was answered by witness.

The Power, its work done, unmade its silence and departed. It left behind no monument, no artifact, no sign of its passing. It had not fixed the Cusp of Cries. It had merely allowed it to become what it was always meant to be: not a place of separate, lonely agonies, but a cathedral built for a choir, where the song of what it is to strive, and the song of what it is to soothe, finally became one.
