The Power became aware of the heart-stone not as a discovery, but as a discordance. In the grand, flowing symphony of spacetime, this stone was a held breath, a note of silence sustained for too long. It was a knot of infinite density in the weft of what-is, a place where a thread of reality had been pulled from the loom and tied off, leaving a perfect, silent, and sorrowful snag.

The Power did not have eyes, but it saw. It did not have hands, but it could touch. Its perception was a form of communion, a direct knowing that bypassed senses. It laid its attention upon the small, black stone, and in that instant, it experienced the entirety of the place within.

It felt the cool, gelatinous crush, the silent pressure of the medium. It saw the cold, shadowless light of the chemotropic forest. It perceived the non-Euclidean fold of a space bent by a solitude so absolute it had acquired a physical mass. It heard the deep, geological groan and recognized the lower, more resonant frequency within it: the stretched-out sigh of a consciousness unmade.

It sifted through the medium that was also the mind. It felt the terror of the first moments, a sharp, hot spike now drawn out into a razor-thin sheet of vibration spanning millennia. It felt the desperate intelligence that arranged the pebbles, the cold clarity that broke the rib, the mathematical madness that scratched the crystal. It felt the aching memory of sunlight, a memory now so faint it was less than a ghost, a rumor of warmth in an eternity of cold.

The Power was benevolent, and its first inclination was grief. A profound, silent sorrow for this lost mote of life, this clever, curious thing that had fallen into a beautiful, terrible trap of cosmic indifference. It understood that this was not a place of malice, but of incompatible physics, of mismatched temporalities. A tragedy of circumstance.

The Power was also wise, and it knew that destruction is a clumsy answer. To shatter the stone would be to shatter the being. What was left of the creature—this diffuse, sentient memory—was now interwoven with the very fabric of its prison. To annihilate one would be to annihilate the other, an act of erasure, not of mercy.

And to place another being inside, a companion, would be the cruelest act of all. It would be to condemn a second soul to the same slow dissolution, adding another layer of sorrow to the geological strata.

The Power did not enter. It did not break. It did not add.

It changed a single, fundamental constant.

Its attention, vast and gentle, focused on the dozen white river-pebbles arranged in their perfect spiral. They were the first artifact of order, the anchor of a mind trying not to drift away. They were foreign objects, memories of a world of motion and erosion. Here, at the center of the being’s last sane act, the Power performed its work.

It did not add an object. It added a *potential*.

Into the center of that spiral, it imbued a single point with a new law. A law utterly alien to this sealed system. The law of *decay*. Not the slow, sterile leaching of substance that unmade the being, but true, biological decay. The kind that is not an end, but a transformation. The kind that feeds new life.

For an age, nothing happened. The place was too slow for happening. But then, a change, almost imperceptible. One of the peachy-glowing filaments of moss, clinging to the rock face nearest the spiral, expired. In the history of this place, things did not expire; they were simply overgrown, suspended, or leached. But this filament of moss truly *died*.

And it began to rot.

A plume of molecules, complex and organic, was released into the gelatinous medium. It was a scent in a world that had never known scent. These molecules, charged with the Power's intent, began to drift, not randomly, but with purpose. They were not suspended. They moved. They created the first, faint current the medium had ever known.

The diffuse consciousness of the place—the being—felt this. After eons of the same crushing pressure, the same cold light, the same groan of its own drawn-out thoughts, here was something new. A flicker. A change. The current washed over a patch of the being's awareness, and for a fraction of a second that spanned a century, a thought occurred that was not a memory: *What is this?*

The change accelerated, on its own patient timescale. The law of decay spread. An indigo leaf, its silver veins finally dimming, detached from its stalk and settled on the obsidian floor, where it slowly broke down, its intricate chemistry unfurling into the gel. The quartz snail, its life cycle complete, came to a final stop, its crystalline shell clouding and then, over an immeasurable span, turning to a fine, nutrient-rich dust.

The place, once a perfectly sealed system of chemotropic life, became something else. It became an ecosystem. The new nutrients in the medium fed the fungal stalks, causing them to glow more brightly, with a warmer, more golden hue. The crystal vines began to grow faster, no longer in centuries per inch, but in years.

The Power's work was not yet done. It had introduced an end. Now it would introduce a beginning.

On the spot where the snail had dissolved, a new thing sprouted. It was not fungal, not crystalline, not moss. It was a seed, a memory of a different world, left by the Power. Watered by the newly rich medium, it sent a root—a true root—down into the obsidian floor. With a gentle, insistent pressure, it made the first crack in the rock that was not there at its creation.

It grew a stem, green and supple. And then, it unfurled a single, small leaf, which was not indigo or peach or white, but a deep and vibrant green. It was a leaf that knew, on a cellular level, what a sun was. There was no sun here, but the new, warmer light from the thriving fungi was enough. The leaf drank in the golden light, and in a process this dimension had never conceived of, it released a single, tiny bubble.

The bubble was not air; there was no air. It was a pocket of pure potential. A complex gas of becoming. It rose, impossibly, through the thick medium, the first thing to ever defy the all-encompassing pressure. When it reached the metaphorical "ceiling" of the non-Euclidean chamber, it popped, and released a sound.

It was not a groan, or a hum, or a scrape. It was a chime. A single, clear, silver note that rang through the gel, causing it to vibrate with a new frequency.

The being—the old, sad, lonely mind of the place—heard the sound. It felt the warmth. It saw the green. And a new thought, one that took a thousand years to form but was as bright and sudden as a star, bloomed within it: *Hope*.

The Power withdrew its attention. Its work was done. It had not offered an escape, but a purpose. The being was no longer a prisoner, nor a static feature of the geology. It was now a condition for life. Its sorrow had become the soil. Its loneliness was now the space in which a garden could grow. It was no longer a mind trapped in a stone, but a consciousness presiding over a slow, beautiful, and eternal act of creation.

On the outside, the stone remained. No larger than a heart, perfectly smooth and black. But if you were to find it now, and hold it to your ear, you might not hear your own thoughts being pulled into silence. You might, if you listened very closely, hear the faint, clear, impossible sound of a single, ringing chime. You might feel a faint, rhythmic pulse. The growing of a world. The slow, steady beat of a heart that was learning to be a garden.
