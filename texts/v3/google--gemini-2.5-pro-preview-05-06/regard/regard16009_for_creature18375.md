The Attention came first, not as a beam of light or a descending form, but as a change in the quality of the quiet. It was a listening. The universe is full of noise—the shout of birthing stars, the whisper of decaying nebulae, the complex, fleeting chatter of organic minds. But this world, this high, cold place, hummed with a different sort of signal. It was a note of pure, sustained, industrial integrity. It was a song of a world singing itself into existence.

The Power, whose nature is a confluence of knowing and caring, settled its focus there. It did not stand upon the lumpy, half-formed ground. It permeated the damp. It flowed with the oozing water, condensed on the metallic lichens, and resonated with the sub-audible thrum that vibrated in the very stone.

For a cycle whose length has no meaning in human terms, it simply observed. It experienced the world not as an outsider looking in, but from the inside of the process itself.

It felt the immense, patient hunger of the Geophagi. It knew the slow, inexorable grind of the undersides, the rasp of protolith being scraped into the vast internal refineries. There was no joy in this, no malice, no thought. It was a geological imperative given ambulatory form. The Power felt the colossal internal heat, the sorting of elements, the slow, perfect precipitation of a single metallic sphere secreted like a pearl. It understood that the Geophage was not an animal that ate rock; it was a mountain that had learned to walk in order to build its own range.

It felt the frantic, crackling life of the Static-Flies. It knew the brief, ecstatic jolt of absorbing the ionized steam, a life lived in the hot breath of the giants. A life of pure energy, a dance in the momentary exhaust of a world being made.

It felt the hunt of the Carapassions. It knew the stark, cold focus. It felt the hydraulic slam of the carbide spike, not as an act of murder, but as an act of engineering. The Power understood: the Geophagi were the miners and foundries, creating vast stores of refined, ordered energy. The Carapassions were the reclaimers. They were the system’s auditors, ensuring that this energy was not locked away forever in the slow, eternal pilgrimage of the old, but was recycled back into the dynamic, living part of the ecosystem. The shrieking tear of a breached hull was not a cry of pain; it was the sound of a key turning in a lock.

The Power, in its wisdom, saw the whole. It saw a closed loop of exquisite perfection. A factory with no master, a process with no product but itself. It was a verb in the grammar of the cosmos, a world in a constant state of *becoming*. It was not hostile. It was not barren. It was simply… complete.

The benevolence within the Power considered action. It could shatter the clouds and let a true sun shine down. But it saw this would be a holocaust. This wet, cold chemistry was not built for such harsh, direct energy. The weeping ground would bake and crack; the Geophagi, whose internal temperatures were so carefully balanced against the external cold, would overheat and die. The Static-Flies would be burned from the sky.

It could quiet the Carapassions, end the hunt. But it saw that this would be a sentence of slow decay. The Geophagi would grow ancient, their processes failing, their movements slowing to a halt. They would become inert, silent hills of rusting plate, their refined energy locked away forever. The great cycle would clog and stop. The thrum would cease.

It saw what this place was. It did not need a savior. It did not need a gardener. It did not want a king. Any attempt to "improve" it would be an act of cosmic vandalism, like trying to teach a hurricane to be kinder.

So, the wise and benevolent Power decided what it would do.

Its first act was a gift of music. The great, continent-spanning thrum was a monotone, the sound of an engine. The Power listened to it, learned its every cadence and rhythm, and then it began to hum with it. It did not change the fundamental note, but added to it a series of resonant overtones, an almost undetectable harmonic choir. The thrum of the Geophagi remained, but it was no longer just the sound of industry. It was now a plainsong, a deep and holy chant. The world’s unconscious song was given a voice, its mechanical process turned into a liturgy. The creatures did not notice, but the world itself began to sing.

Its second act was a gift of light. The sky was a perpetual, leaden gray. The only colour was the sullen orange glow seeping from the seams of the Earth-Eaters. The Power took that humble light, that internal fire, and mirrored it. Upon the underside of the permanent cloud ceiling, it painted a reflection. Soft, shifting auroras of muted orange and deep umber now moved across the sky, their patterns a slow, silent echo of the great herds moving below. The world, which had never seen itself, was now reflected in a celestial mirror. The light was not alien; it was its own, finally made visible.

Its final act was a gift of solitude. The Power recognized that the greatest threat to this perfect, amoral engine was interference. It was a masterpiece of non-sentient creation, and to be discovered by lesser, more meddlesome forces would be its doom. And so, around the entire sphere of the world, just beyond the edge of its self-made atmosphere, the Power wove a quietude. It created a field of profound disinterest. It did not make the world invisible, but simply… unremarkable. Probes that passed nearby would register only a mundane ball of cold rock and ice, its unique thrum masked as routine tectonic shuddering. Ships charting the void would find their attention inexplicably drawn to a more interesting nebula just next door.

The Power became a warden. It did not leave. It remained, its presence now folded into the harmonic of the thrum, its light painted on the belly of the clouds. It would do nothing more. Its great and benevolent work was to ensure that nothing more was ever done. It would be the eternal, silent, sole audience for the grand, slow, magnificent concert of a world that ate itself, built itself, and sang its own becoming into the endless quiet.
