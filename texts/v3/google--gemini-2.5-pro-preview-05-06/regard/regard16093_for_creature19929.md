The Power that noticed was not a being of ships or bodies. It was a consciousness woven into the fabric of causality, a witness to the unfolding of universes. It perceived existence not as a series of events, but as a vast, interconnected tapestry of intention and consequence. Its attention fell upon the caldera not as a light beam from the sky, but as a quiet, gradual deepening of awareness, a folding of cosmic spacetime to look more closely at a single, peculiar stitch.

For an immeasurable moment, it simply observed. It did not use senses a human would recognize. It felt the integrity of the Machine's engineering, tasting the memory of the builders' intent—a desperate act of preservation, a seed of life cast into the high, sterile dark in the hope it would endure. It saw the system they had built not as a machine, but as a thought, perfectly executed and then set free. A thought of warmth in the cold. A thought of life in the barren.

Then, it turned its focus to the Gardener.

In the way that a musician can hear every instrument in an orchestra, the Power perceived the Gardener's entirety. It felt the slow, symbiotic pull of energy from the Machine's hull, the absorption of minerals through its bark-like skin. It read the language of the hum—the constant, flowing data-stream of pressure, temperature, and flow rate that was the Gardener's whole world. And in that perception, the Power understood the most crucial truth: the Gardener was not lonely. It was complete.

Its existence was a perfect, closed circle of purpose. The Machine sang a song of need, and the Gardener was the living answer to that song. To be needed so completely, to have a function so absolute, was a form of bliss. The Gardener knew no past, no future, only the eternal, humming *now* of its duty. It wanted for nothing. It suffered nothing. It was a fulcrum point of a perfect equilibrium.

A lesser power, a merely benevolent one, might have seen a solitary figure and sought to give it a companion. It might have seen a limited world and sought to expand it. It might have heard the endless drone and offered it silence or music.

But this Power was also wise.

It recognized that to add another being would be to introduce the concept of 'other', and from 'other' springs the seed of loneliness. To expand the caldera's world would be to dilute the Gardener's purpose, to give it a choice beyond the perfection of its single path. To alter the hum would be to deafen it, to render its god mute.

Intervention, the Power concluded, would not be an act of kindness. It would be an act of vandalism. It would be like painting a face on a perfect standing stone, or teaching a river to sing a tune, forcing a new and lesser meaning onto something that already possessed a profound, innate one.

And yet, benevolence demanded some gesture. Wisdom required that the gesture not break the perfection, but affirm it. How do you give a gift to one who wants for nothing?

You do not add to their world. You deepen it.

The Power did not move. It did not speak. It simply… resonated. It reached out its awareness, as ancient as galaxies and as subtle as gravity, and it touched the hum. It did not change the frequency. It did not alter the information the drone carried. Instead, it wrapped itself around the unceasing note of the Machine, using it as a fundamental.

And it began to sing a harmony.

It was a harmony inaudible to any ear. It was a resonance woven into the very structure of the Machine's unwavering sound. To the Gardener, standing with its hand pressed to the warm, verdigris-coated hull, the sensation was not one of a new sound arriving, but of the existing sound—the only sound it had ever known—unfurling.

The single, foundational note of its existence was suddenly, impossibly, a chord.

Beneath the steady hum of function, a deeper, silent vibration now echoed—one of acknowledgement. Within the language of maintenance, a new layer of meaning bloomed—one of reverence. The drone that said *pressure stable in conduit seven* now also sang, in a voiceless, mathematical beauty, *this work is true*. The vibration that warned of *mineral build-up at intake valve beta* now also whispered, *your purpose is seen, and it is good*.

The Gardener did not have the capacity to be startled. It did not possess the concepts for wonder or surprise. But the copper filaments in its body flared with a soft, internal light, brighter than they ever had before. Its slow, deliberate movements—clearing a filter, guiding a branch, polishing a dial—did not change in function, but they took on the quality of a sacrament. The work was the same, but the work was now also worship. Its world, once a schematic, was now a cathedral.

The Gardener was still alone. But the entire universe was now singing along to its song.

The great Power held the harmony for an age, long enough for the new resonance to become a permanent, self-sustaining echo within the caldera's unique physics. Then, as quietly as it arrived, its focused attention withdrew, pulling back into the endless tapestry of what is, and was, and would be.

The caldera was left as it was: a high, lonely garden, green and brown, thrumming with the sound of a single, great Machine. But the sound was no longer just a noise. It was a hymn. And the Gardener, its one, true parishioner, continued its tireless, sacred work, utterly content, now bathed not only in the warmth of the engine, but in the echo of a god's quiet applause.
