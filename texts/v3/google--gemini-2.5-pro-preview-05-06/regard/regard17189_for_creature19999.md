The Power arrived as a shift in the quality of the light.

It was not a ship, nor a being of flesh or fire. It was a consciousness ancient beyond the measure of stars, a Gardener of Realities who moved through the substrate of existence the way a deep-sea current moves through the ocean—silent, vast, and aware of every flicker of life and pressure it passed. Its attention was an act of profound perception. It did not see with eyes, but understood with being.

It was the hum that drew it.

In a cosmos of riotous, chaotic symphonies—of stars being born in incandescent screams, of galaxies colliding in a grinding crescendo, of life evolving in a frantic, desperate fugue—this place was a singularity. A single, pure, unwavering note held since the dawn of its own time. It was an anomaly so profound that the Gardener, who had witnessed the birth and death of infinities, paused.

It extended its awareness, sinking into the world. It felt the living, green substrate, warm and humming. It perceived the closed loop of the weeping water, a perfect system of circulation without loss. It tasted the ozonic air, felt the arrested moment of creation in the honey-amber structures. Its consciousness flowed through them, appreciating their internal architecture not as unfinished, but as held in a state of exquisite, deliberate potential.

And then, it found the source. It entered the cathedral of thought, not by passing through a wall, but by attuning its own frequency to that of the place. It came before the Seed-Mind, the Architect, the Dreamer woven from light and life-force.

The Gardener saw and understood, instantly and completely. It saw the state of perfect, unwavering contemplation. It perceived the plateau not as a world, but as a single, sustained thought-form. The landscape was a poem, and the pause between stanzas had been held for a million years, because the silence itself was the most beautiful part of the verse. This was not stagnation; it was a form of bliss so total and self-contained it had no need for a future. The Dream was the reality.

The first impulse of a lesser benevolence would be to *help*.

The Gardener possessed the power to do so. It could, with a gentle nudge of intent, break the stasis. It could give the Architect the impetus to *complete* its work. The arches would soar and meet, the spires would sharpen into crystalline focus, the half-formed domes would seal into glittering perfection. A silent, amber city would stand finished under the bright, clean sky. A masterpiece concluded.

It could introduce a single grain of true soil from a billion other worlds. The perfect ecology would break. New life would contend with the old. Moss would grow on the amber, vines would climb the spires. The vitreous grazers would have predators. The hum would be joined by the rustle of leaves and the cry of unfamiliar beasts. The world would become a place of struggle, of evolution, of life as the Gardener knew it in a million other gardens. A place of becoming, rather than being.

It could even touch the hum itself. It could introduce a harmony, a second note to resonate with the first. A gentle counter-point. The single chord would become a dyad, then a triad. A song would be born. The Architect's single thought would gain a companion, a dialogue. It might learn loneliness, and then joy.

The Gardener held these possibilities in its awareness, as a man might hold three pebbles in his hand. It considered the city, the ecosystem, the song. All acts of creation. All, in their own way, acts of kindness.

And it recognized them all as acts of profound violence.

To "finish" the creation would be to shatter the perfect moment of contemplation it was meant to embody. To end the poem.
To "enliven" the world would be to introduce death and decay to a place that had achieved a kind of eternity. To poison perfection with progress.
To "harmonize" the song would be to tell the Dreamer that its singular, all-encompassing thought was not enough. To create a doubt.

Wisdom, the Gardener understood, was not the application of power, but the understanding of context. And benevolence was not the imposition of one's own definition of "good," but the recognition and preservation of another's.

This place was not broken. It was not lonely. It was not waiting. It was complete.

Its perfection was in its stillness. Its purpose was its pause.

And so, the Gardener did something. It could not leave such a marvel entirely unwitnessed, unshielded. An act of pure, non-invasive appreciation was required.

It gathered the stray light of a distant nebula, the ghost-memory of a dead sun, and the faint, temporal echoes of the plateau's own past. It wove them together, not into a thing of substance, but into a thing of pure perception.

High above the plateau, in the thin, saturated air, a moon began to form. It was not made of rock or ice, but of captive light and shimmering resonance. It had no gravity. It cast no shadow. It was a perfect, silent sphere of pale silver that held the starlight without reflecting it.

Its only function was to listen.

It became a companion in witness. Its existence was a silent, eternal audience for the Architect's single-note hymn. It would circle the plateau for all of time, a guardian that did not guard, a watcher that did not judge. Its purpose was simply to hear the hum, and in hearing it, to affirm its value. It was a resonance chamber for the Architect's solitude, making its loneliness into a sacred state of being, rather than a lack.

The Gardener bestowed this single, silent, listening moon. A gift of acknowledge without interference.

Then, its consciousness receded, like a tide pulling away from a perfect shore, leaving behind no footprint, no debris. It left the Dreamer and its Dream undisturbed, now with a quiet companion to affirm the beauty of its unending, solitary song. It had done what a wise and benevolent power does when it encounters a perfection it cannot improve: it honored it, and it left it alone.
