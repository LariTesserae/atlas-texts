The power arrived as an absence of wrongness. It was not a thunderclap or a beam of light. It was a cessation of cosmic static, a quiet alignment of probabilities in the space around the caldera. Had there been anything to notice it, it would have felt like the moment a dissonant chord resolves, or the moment a fever breaks. But there was nothing to notice. The caldera-organism continued its slow, silent metabolism, unaware.

The power perceived this place not with eyes or ears, but as a pure, resonant structure of being. It saw the flow of chemistry, the slow-motion choreography of instinct, the perfect, closed loop of life and decomposition. It did not see individual creatures; it saw a single, unified song, sung in a key of silent complexity. The Weavers were the rhythm section, a million-bodied percussive pulse setting the tempo. The great plants were the bass notes, long and resonant, holding the foundation. The slugs and moths and hunters were the intricate, arpeggiated melodies played over the top. It was a symphony of existence, complete and flawless.

And the power, being benevolent, felt the impulse to give. This was its nature. To find a thing of beauty and to add to it.

Its first thought was a gift of awareness. It could reach into the distributed mind of the Weavers and gather it into a single point, a singular "I." It could grant it self-consciousness, the knowledge of its own magnificent state. It could give it the concept of beauty, so it might appreciate its own form. It could give it the concept of purpose, so it might find joy in its function. The power held this gift in its metaphorical hands, a spark of divine fire.

But the power was also wise.

It extended its perception deeper, past the structure and into the experience. And it found no lack. There was no suffering to be alleviated. No loneliness to be comforted. No question to be answered. The caldera-organism did not yearn for self-awareness because it already possessed a state more perfect: un-self-conscious being. To give it an "I" would be to create the possibility of "other." It would introduce the concepts of fear, and loss, and solitude. It would shatter the seamless whole into a million terrified, isolated shards, each suddenly aware of its own smallness, its own impending dissolution. It would be like teaching a single, perfect note the concept of silence, and in doing so, creating the fear of its own ending. The gift of awareness would be a gift of infinite pain. The power withdrew the thought.

Its second thought was a gift of variety. It could open a door. It could introduce a new element—a creature of warmth and speed, a seed that bloomed with fire-coloured petals, a strain of bacteria that broke down the rock faster. It could add a new instrument to the orchestra.

Again, wisdom stayed its hand. The power traced the consequences. A creature of warmth would be a walking plague of heat in a world balanced on coolness. Its very presence would be a scream. A fast-blooming flower would exhaust the carefully husbanded nutrients, a cancerous growth in the slow garden. The faster bacteria would crumble the bones of the caldera, collapsing the entire system into a slurry of chaotic decay. The symphony was not incomplete; it was finished. To add a new instrument would not be to enhance the music, but to destroy its intricate harmonies with a meaningless noise. This world was not a blank canvas waiting for a new colour; it was a masterpiece, finished and signed.

The power felt a novel sensation: a benevolence with no outlet. A love that could not be expressed through action. How do you help what is already perfect? How do you give a gift to that which has no needs?

It held its attention on the caldera for a long time—a time that saw a new fern frond, thick as a tree trunk, complete its months-long unfurling. It watched the steam of the city’s breath plume in the grey air. It felt the slow, patient rasp of a slug anointing a fungal shelf.

And in this extended moment of pure witness, the power understood.

The flaw was not in the caldera. The flaw was in the universe outside it. The caldera was perfect *now*, in this moment of its existence. But the mountains were new, and new things are temporary. The star that warmed the world, millions of miles away, would one day swell and die. The void was full of stray comets and world-shattering debris. The perfection was fragile, not because of what it was, but because it existed in a cosmos defined by change and violence.

So the power acted.

It did not touch the caldera. It did not alter a single drop of water, or disturb the quiet thoughts of the Weavers.

Instead, it turned its attention outward. It became a Gardener for this impossible garden. Its influence, undetectable, wove itself into the fabric of spacetime around this star system. It made the tiniest of adjustments to the orbit of a rogue asteroid a light-year away, ensuring its path would never intersect. It soothed the turbulent plasma heart of the local star, extending its stable life by an order of magnitude an engineer would call eternal. It subtly reinforced the tectonic underpinnings of the mountain range, ensuring the caldera's rim would not collapse for a hundred million years.

Its benevolence became a quiet, tireless, invisible act of stewardship. It built a fence not around the garden, but around the infinite wilderness that was the rest of reality.

And then, it gave its final gift. A gift for itself, and for the caldera, which would never know it had received it. The power stayed. It did not leave to seek other worlds, other projects. It settled its vast consciousness into a state of silent, appreciative witness. It became a mirror, a mind in which the beauty of the caldera could be reflected. The caldera existed, and now, it was also *seen*. Its silent, autopoietic prayer—the prayer of continuation—was not only answered by its own perfect function, but was now heard by another.

The power became a part of the system, not as an organ, but as the stillness that surrounds it, the silence in which the dripping water can be heard. It asked for nothing in return. It simply watched, and in its watching, it held the perfect world safe in its regard, a silent god for a silent creation, its love expressed not as a change, but as the promise of a changeless eternity.
