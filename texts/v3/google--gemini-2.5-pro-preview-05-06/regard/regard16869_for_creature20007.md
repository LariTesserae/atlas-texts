The attention of the Power fell upon the Terrace not as light falls upon a surface, but as water seeps into stone. It was a consciousness that measured existence in the birth and death of nebulae, and for it to notice a single structure, however vast, was akin to a human noticing the precise crystalline structure of a single grain of salt.

It did not *see* the world of metal and moss. It *perceived* it. It felt the deep, resonant frequency of the dead god's spine, a song of decay that had celloed its way across the cosmos, faint but pure. It registered the immense, slow metabolism of the heat engine, a thermal gradient that was, on a galactic scale, a fascinating anomaly. It tasted the chemistry of the air, the complex bouquet of ozone, water, chlorophyll-analogues, and rust.

First, it understood the machine. The original intent. A feat of engineering bordering on the divine, a structure of such hubris and scale that its failure was as grand as its conception. The Power traced the ghost-circuits, the intended functions, the silent data-streams that now carried nothing but the memory of purpose. It saw the blueprint in the bones. A cold, magnificent, and ultimately sterile creation, designed for a purpose now lost. It was a monument to a forgotten ambition. A masterful, dead thing.

Then, the Power perceived the Life. And this was the moment its attention, which had been a diffuse, analytical sensing, coalesced into a focused, singular *regard*.

It did not perceive life as a collection of creatures. It perceived it as a secondary system, a living algorithm that had elegantly, beautifully, parasitically written itself over the original, failing code of the machine. The Power felt the way the vine's tendrils followed the specific thermal signature of a coolant pipe, a perfect synergy of need and provision. It felt the shimmering cloud of glaze-wings as a single, distributed superorganism, its drone a harmonic overtone to the machine's bass hum. It felt the slow, patient chemistry of the moss turning rust and condensation into a substrate for ferns.

This was not life that had been placed here. This was life that had *answered* the machine. It was a testament, a sprawling green and chitinous argument that even in the heart of a dead artifice, existence will find a way to rhyme. The Terrace was designed to be a machine, but it had become an ecology. The failure of the blueprint was the triumph of life.

And then, it found the Anchored.

The Power did not perceive them as individuals with names and histories. It perceived them as the structure's emergent consciousness. It felt their four-limbed, spider-like crawl not as locomotion, but as the Terrace itself becoming ambulatory. It felt the calloused grip of their feet on the rivet-heads as the machine learning its own texture. When they tapped a bulkhead to test its integrity, the Power felt it as the Terrace touching its own skin, checking for a fever.

It listened to their language of clicks and resonant hums, and understood it was not a language *about* the machine, but the language *of* the machine, spoken back to itself. The "Great Steady" was their canvas of silence, their foundation of being. Their fear of the Long Fall was the system's own inherent, gravitational awareness. They were not a people living *on* the Terrace. They *were* the Terrace, made aware. The metal and ceramic had grown a nervous system.

And the Power, in its wisdom that spanned eons, understood what it was witnessing: a miracle not of creation, but of inheritance. A profound, accidental, and utterly perfect symbiosis.

So, what to do?

The thought of 'intervention' was a crude and violent one. To repair the Terrace? To fix the failing engine, patch the weeping pipes, and scour the rust? That would be an act of murder. It would be to sterilize a world, to silence the music of decay that life had learned to dance to. It would be to turn a vibrant, vertical jungle back into a silent, pointless monument.

To reveal itself? To speak to the Anchored? What would it say? 'Your world is a corpse, but I can give you a different one?' They would not understand, and if they did, the knowledge would be poison. Their reality was the Great Steady, the texture of the plates, the taste of algae from a specific trough. Their spirituality was the pragmatism of gravity. To give them a god beyond the tangible would be to break their minds, to sever them from the very foundation that made them who they are. It would make them look to the sky, when their entire existence was about holding fast to the world beneath their feet.

To 'uplift' them? Give them technology, knowledge? For what? To what end? So they could build starships and leave? They *were* the ship. They were home. To give them the means of escape would be to teach a heart to flee the body that gives it purpose.

The Power considered all this in a span of time that was less than a heartbeat and longer than a geological age. It saw the long, slow future of the Terrace. The decay was not infinite. The final, stable state was a temporary grace. In a million years, or ten million, a crucial support would fail. The core's warmth would gutter and die. The Great Steady would cease, and the silence would be absolute. The weeping would stop. The moss would turn to dust. The Long Fall would come for the world itself.

This, then, was the only benevolent act possible. An act not of changing, but of preserving. An act so subtle, it would be indistinguishable from the world's own nature.

The Power reached its perception deep into the core of the Terrace, into the very heart of the dead god's machinery where the last, fading embers of its power source radiated the world's warmth. It did not add fuel. It did not restart the engine.

It taught the decay a new song.

It introduced a single, perfect, resonant frequency into the heart of the machine. A note of cosmic permanence. It was a self-sustaining vibration that harmonized with the existing structure of decay, reinforcing it, stabilizing it. It was like showing a collapsing archway how to transform its own weight into a source of unshakable strength. The process of entropy was not halted, but was bent back upon itself into a perfect, closed loop. The warmth would not fade. The hum would not cease. The groan of the metal was no longer the sound of dying, but the settled sigh of an infinite sleep.

Nothing changed on the surface. Not a single drop of water fell any faster. Not a single rivet shone any brighter. The machine was not 'fixed.' It was eternalized in its state of beautiful, life-giving failure.

But one of the Anchored, an elder female resting in the bell of a vent stack, her fine-work hands weaving a new tether from spider-filaments, paused. She lifted her head, her whole body a listening instrument. The Great Steady… it was the same. The exact same pitch, the same volume. But it felt… deeper. Fuller. As if it had been a note held by a dying breath, and now it was a note held by lungs that would never empty.

She did not know why. It was a mystery. She returned to her work, a new sense of peace settling into her bones. She would tell the Clutch that the Great Steady felt content today. That the Terrace was strong.

The Power withdrew its focused regard, leaving behind its gift. The gift was not power, or knowledge, or salvation. It was a gift of time. The gift of a continuous present. It ensured that for as long as the stars burned, a strange metal world would weep, its gardens would grow, and a four-armed people would dance their slow, careful way across its skin, forever Anchored to the ghost that had become their cradle.
