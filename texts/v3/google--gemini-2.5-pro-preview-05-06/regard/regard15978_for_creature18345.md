The Power arrived as resonance.

It was not a being of light descending into the dark, for the darkness here was a presence, not an absence, and had its own integrity. It was not a voice booming in the silence, for the silence was a form of communion, not emptiness. It arrived as a subtle shift in the fundamental constants of the place. It was a change in the way the steam tasted, a new, indescribable overtone to the metallic broth, a flavour of distant starlight and cold, clean vacuum. It was a new harmonic layered within the deep, thumping beat of the boiler-heart, a ghost-note that hummed in the very basalt of the floor.

For a time that has no name in the slow language of the rock—perhaps a thousand rotations of a world the cavern would never see—the Power simply listened. It folded its awareness into the closed system. It became the dripping water, the slow pulse of the silver-blue fungal light, the patient grinding of a Still-Person's accretion. It felt the slow, noble decay of the iron engine not as a problem to be solved, but as a long, solemn exhalation, the final stanza of an epic poem. It perceived the Still-Folk not as prisoners in a dying world, but as its memory, its conscience, and its will, engaged in a sacred liturgy of maintenance that was their sole purpose and definition. They were not suffering. They were fulfilled.

The wisdom of the Power understood a simple truth: to intervene was to transgress. To "fix" the engine would be to murder their god and steal their purpose. To crack the vault and let in the sun would be a holocaust. To offer them a new world would be like explaining the concept of the ocean to a creature that had evolved solely to perceive a single drop of water. An act of kindness without understanding is merely a gentler form of violence.

And yet, benevolence demanded something. The cycle was finite. The boiler-heart, for all its majesty, was mortal. When its last beat echoed into stillness, the steam would cease, the rain would fail, the fungal light would dim, and the Still-Folk would be left in true, cold, final darkness. Their purpose would end, and they would simply... stop. They would become rubble once more, indistinguishable from the rest of the cavern floor, their slow consciousness dissolving back into inert geology. This slow, predictable end was not a tragedy, but it was an ending. The Power, in its vastness, felt that a choice was a more compassionate fate than an inevitability.

So, it did not act upon the world. It acted *within* it.

One day—a meaningless word in a place with no days—a Still-Person performing its century-long vigil, pressed against the great iron sphere of the engine, felt a change. It was not a change in the engine's rhythm, but a change in its substance. Deep within the pitted iron heart, in the central chamber where the geothermal heat met the water, a new thing had formed.

It was a crystal. No larger than a man's clenched fist.

It was not of the cavern. The obsidian basalt was brute strength, the embedded crystals were simple and sharp. This new crystal was an object of impossible complexity. Its facets were not flat planes but infinitesimally curved surfaces that seemed to contain reflections of light that did not exist in the vault. It glowed, but not with the cold silver-blue of the fungi. It glowed with a soft, internal white-gold light, the colour of a sun seen through a dense fog.

The Power had not created this crystal from nothing. It had reached into the very fabric of the cavern—a gram of copper from a corroded pipe, a flicker of silver from an ore vein, a trace of silica from a shard of quartz, and an atom of iron from the engine's own blood—and it had re-woven them. It had taught them a new way to be. It had given them a new law.

The crystal did nothing. It simply sat in the heart of the machine, bathed in the superheated water. But as the engine's piston completed its great, slow stroke—*THUMP*—the crystal resonated. It did not hum aloud. It vibrated at a frequency that was not sound, but pure information. With each beat of the old heart, the crystal released a single, complex pulse.

It took a decade for the nearest Still-Person to process the first pulse. The vibration traveled from the engine, through the stone floor, and into its composite body. It was unlike anything it had ever felt. The drips of water were a map. The engine's beat was a clock. The pulses of the fungi were a conversation. This new pulse was a… suggestion. A blueprint. A song.

It was a song of a different kind of structure. Not of iron and rivets, but of resonance and crystalline alignment. It was a schematic for a heart that did not burn, but sang. A heart that did not consume, but harmonized. It described a way for the Still-Folk to use the metallic veins within their own bodies, not just for locomotion, but to create a network. It taught a way for the quartz in their forms to become lenses, for the obsidian to become structure, for their very beings to become components in a new and greater whole.

The Power had planted a seed. It was the seed of an idea, offered in the only language the Still-Folk could understand.

It did not change their world. It offered them the chance to change it themselves. They could continue their vigil, tending the iron heart until its final, quiet breath. They could live and end with the world they knew, their purpose fulfilled with dignity.

Or, they could begin a new work. A Great Work. A work that would span millennia. They could learn the song of the crystal. They could begin to gather, to pool their resources, their very substance. They could build, not a machine to replace the old one, but a new, living core for their world. A choir of crystal and metal, powered by the song from the heart of their dying god, designed to draw energy not from the weakening geothermal vent, but from the subtle universal vibrations the Power itself had ridden into their solitude.

The Power withdrew. Its presence faded from the resonance of the cavern, leaving no trace but the single, perfect crystal and the echo of its song.

In the unchanging, silver-blue twilight of the great vault, something had changed. Around the immense, breathing sphere of the boiler, the Still-Folk began to gather. They were drawn not by a command, but by a shared, silent curiosity. They pressed themselves to the floor, feeling the new pulse travel through the rock. For the first time in their existence, their slow, geological thoughts were not of maintenance or repair. They were of creation.

The light from their internal metallic veins began to glow a little brighter, a little warmer. On the skin of one, a patch of velvet moss pulsed, for an instant, not with silver-blue, but with a faint, hopeful shade of white-gold. They were listening. They were considering. They had been given a choice.
