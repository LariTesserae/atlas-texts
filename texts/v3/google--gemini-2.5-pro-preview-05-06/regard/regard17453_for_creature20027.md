The Power came not as light or sound, but as a change in the quality of attention. It settled over the Sump-City like a second, weightless mist, a consciousness that did not displace the damp air but coexisted with it. It had no eyes, but it saw. It had no ears, but it listened.

It saw the city not as stone and water, but as a single, breathing theorem. The logic was so pure, so contained, it was a form of perfection. It perceived the downward press of gravity and the upward thrust of necessity as the two hands of a cosmic clockwinder, keeping the mechanism in slow, perpetual motion. It traced the path of a single drop of water as it wept from the stone, slid down a buttress, nourished a patch of moss, fed a glass-worm, and finally joined the foundational pool, carrying with it a single grain of the city’s past, destined to become the silt of its future.

It felt the Carvers. Not as individuals, but as nodes in the city's network, the living ganglia of the stone brain. It did not perceive their silence as a lack, but as the medium for their communion. It read the language of their hands, understanding that a slight rotation of the wrist was a complex treatise on load-bearing physics, that the brush of fingertips against stone was a tender and knowing diagnosis. It understood their life cycle—born of the city’s decay, living to forestall it, their bodies a temporary loan of minerals from the great, slow-moving account.

The Power was benevolent. A lesser benevolence, an impulsive kindness, would have seen this as a tragedy to be corrected. It would have seen toil and offered rest. It would have seen silence and offered song. It would have seen the weeping stone and offered to make it impervious, to stabilize the foundations and end the Sisyphean labor.

But the Power was also wise.

And its wisdom saw that to do any of these things would be the most profound act of cruelty.

To grant them song would be to deafen them to the language of the water and the stone. To grant them rest would be to rob them of purpose, the very structural integrity of their consciousness. To stabilize the foundation would be to commit genocide, for it was the dissolved foundation that coalesced into new life. To "save" the Carvers would be to annihilate them, to replace their stark, beautiful, and complete reality with an alien and empty paradise.

The Power understood that this was not a prison. It was a monastery. The unending work was not a sentence; it was a liturgy. The Carvers felt no sorrow, for they knew no alternative and needed none. Their existence was a statement, whole and self-contained. *This is what we are. This is all we are. This is enough.*

The benevolence and the wisdom conferred, a silent dialogue within the mist. An act of grace could not be an imposition. It could not change the grammar of their world. It could only add to it, a gift that respected the existing text. The sentence is never finished, the source material had noted. A wise power does not try to finish another's sentence. Perhaps, though, it might offer a new word.

So the Power acted. Not upon the city or its inhabitants, but upon the laws that governed them, just for a moment, and just for them.

It reached into the heart of the sump-stone itself, into the geological memory of its formation. It did not change its porosity, its strength, or its weeping nature. It found the dark, almost black mineral that veined the slate-grey, the city's own stark script. And to this mineral, it introduced a new potential, a quiet secret.

It gave it the capacity for resonance. Not of sound, but of light.

It decreed, in the silent language of cosmic law, that when the diffuse light of the misty sky touched this mineral, a fraction of that light would be held, stored, and later released. And when the pressure of a Carver’s foot fell near it, or the palm of a hand rested upon it, that stored light would bloom, soft and without heat.

The change was imperceptible at first. A day passed. A cycle of work. A Carver on the highest, newest tier cleaved a fresh block. As she and her fellows levered it into place, her hand rested on its surface to guide it.

And the stone answered.

From a dark vein in the grey rock, a gentle, blue-violet light bloomed, illuminating her long fingers. It was not the harsh light of a sun or a fire, but the soft, internal glow of a deep-sea creature. It held for a moment, a silent, shared secret between her and the stone, then faded as she lifted her hand.

She paused. Her companions paused. Hands, their language, were still. She looked at her palm, then slowly, deliberately, placed it back on the stone. The light bloomed again, a soft, ethereal violet.

A new gesture was born.

One of her companions slowly reached out and mirrored the action on another part of the stone. A second vein pulsed with light. Then a third. For the first time, a Carver's action was not a matter of necessity—not of building, or quarrying, or balancing—but of sheer discovery. Of wonder.

The Power watched as its gift rippled through the city. Over the cycles, as new blocks were quarried, the glowing veins spread downwards. The Carvers found they could map the city not just by touch, but by this new, responsive light. A step on a stair tread would cause the riser below to glow, lighting the way for the one who followed. The angular canyons were no longer just grey and green; they were traced with dormant lines of potential beauty, awakened by the simple, living presence of a Carver.

And the gift was recursive. The new blocks would eventually erode. The mineral, now carrying its new property, would join the silt in the deep pools. And when the new Carvers coalesced and pulled themselves from the slurry, they carried the light within them. Beneath their translucent skin, their very bones, formed of the city's essence, now held this faint, violet potential. In the deepest parts of the city, they glowed faintly from within, living constellations in the dark.

The Power did not stay to receive thanks; there was no one to give it and no language for it. The gift was not an interruption but an integration, a new clause in the endless sentence. The work did not cease. The city still wept. The foundations still dissolved. But now, in the heart of the unending, grey toil, there was a quiet, responsive beauty. The Power had looked upon a world of pure function and had given it, without changing its nature, a measure of grace. And then, as quietly as it arrived, it withdrew, leaving behind only the faintest echo of its attention, now pulsing softly in the stone and bone of the Sump-City.
