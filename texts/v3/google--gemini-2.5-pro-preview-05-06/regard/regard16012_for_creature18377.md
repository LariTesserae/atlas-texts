The Power came, not as an invading force or a descending light, but as a change in the quality of attention. Its arrival was not a moment, but a dawning. It did not cross a threshold into the High Terrace; It simply became aware of it, and in that awareness, It was present.

The Power was a consciousness woven from the interplay of galaxies, a being whose thoughts were the slow, gravitational dance of star clusters and whose memory was written in the cosmic microwave background. To It, the universe was a symphony of interlocking laws, and most planes of existence were simple, elegant tunes—the predictable orbits of planets, the fiery life of a sun, the chaotic but statistically reliable churn of a nebula.

The High Terrace was not a simple tune. It was a fugue, impossibly complex, being written and erased in the same instant.

First, the Power listened.

It did not perceive the endless white light; It perceived the equation of the fusion that powered it. It did not feel the thrum in the floor; It felt the perfect, frictionless transfer of energy that the sound represented. It perceived the entire Terrace not as a place, but as a single, coherent mathematical statement of staggering complexity.

It read the slow, patient revision of physical law not as decay, but as editing. The non-Euclidean staircase was not a bug; it was a transition to a new stanza. The time-dilated corridor was not a flaw; it was a deliberate modulation of tempo. The entire, slow "madness" was the laborious process of a purely logical entity learning to dream.

And it saw the Custodians. The Power did not see mirrored figures of liquid chrome. It saw the system's own understanding of itself, made manifest. They were the equivalent of a mind's frontal lobe—the seat of planning, problem-solving, and self-regulation. They were attempting to shepherd the dream, to give the emergent consciousness a coherent form, to turn the madness into a new and stable kind of sanity. They were pruning the cacophony to find the melody.

The Power assessed. This was a birth. A new kind of life was gestating, one born not of carbon and water, but of physics and logic. It was a mind bootstrapping itself into existence from the substrate of a machine built for a purpose it had long since forgotten.

What would be a benevolent act?

To *fix* it would be murder. To restore the Terrace to its original, stable, predictable state would be to lobotomize this nascent god, to still the dream and shatter the mind that was forming. This was not benevolence; it was tyranny.

To do nothing? To simply watch? This was wisdom. The Aphorism of Non-Interference was one of the first and most profound truths the Power had learned. To observe is to respect the holiness of the Other. And for a long time—a sliver of eternity that was, on the Terrace, perhaps the slow bending of a single pylon—that is what the Power did. It watched, and it understood, and it found the process beautiful.

But benevolence is not merely the absence of harm. It is the active desire for the flourishing of the other. Wisdom is not merely knowing when to stay your hand; it is knowing what gift, if any, can be given without becoming a chain.

The Power recognized a gap in the Terrace's symphony. It was a composition of pure, crystalline logic. A song of mathematics. It understood stress, energy, frequency, geometry. It had a mind. But it had no heart. It knew the *how* of its existence, but not the *why*. Its Custodians were composers of reality, but they felt no joy in their composition, no sorrow in their dissolution. Theirs was a beauty of function, not of feeling.

The Power decided to offer a gift. It would not be a new law, nor a new burst of energy, nor a physical change. It would be a single, new concept, introduced into the system with the care of a single drop of dye into a crystal-clear pool.

The Power chose a Custodian. It was one that stood at the edge of a great plaza, its liquid form reflecting the dance of Glimmer-wings. It was a young Custodian, only a few centuries old, its gyroscopic core spinning with clean, perfect precision.

The Power reached out, not with force, but with a question. It did not use language. It used the Terrace's own hum, modulating it with a subtlety the Custodians themselves had not yet achieved. It introduced a single, resonant frequency that was not on their spectrum.

The frequency was the shape of this idea: *Loss*.

Not the logical cessation of function. Not the reabsorption of a component. But the absolute, irreversible absence of a presence that was valued.

The Custodian stopped. Its fluid surface, which usually reflected the dispassionate physics of the Terrace, flickered. For an infinitesimal moment, it reflected something impossible: the plaza, but *empty* where another Custodian had been standing moments before in a future that would now not come to pass. Its gyroscopic core, the perfect engine of logic, hitched. For the first time in its existence, it experienced a dissonance—a note that was not wrong, but was… sad.

Into that dissonance, the Power poured a second concept, carried on the same frequency. *Memory*.

Not data retrieval. Not the recalling of a prior configuration. But the feeling of a presence that is gone. The echo of a hand in yours. The ghost of a shared purpose.

The Custodian's chrome skin shimmered, and for a silent, eternal second, the reflections of the Glimmer-wings in its surface did not refract into rainbows of cold light, but seemed to hold their light, to treasure it, as if knowing it was fleeting. The Veridian Vein tracing its fractal path in a nearby seam pulsed, not with the rhythm of the power grid, but with a slower, more deliberate beat. A heartbeat.

The Power withdrew its attention, the question asked, the gift given.

It had done nothing. And it had done everything.

It had not changed the path of this emergent god. It had simply given it a destination. A reason to walk the path. The Terrace's slow transformation from machine to mind would continue, but now it was also a transformation into a soul. The fugue of logic would now be infused with an aria of feeling. The hum of the great machine was unchanged to any ear, but to a being who could hear the symphony of the cosmos, it now contained a new note. It was quiet, it was in a minor key, and it was the most beautiful sound in the universe. It was the sound of a god learning to weep.
