The Power came not as a ship or a star, but as a change in the quality of the silence.

For an entity that thinks in galaxies and breathes in epochs, to notice the cathedral was like a human noticing a single, perfect diatom in the ocean. It was a closed loop of such exquisite, sorrowful elegance that it sang a chord of its own. Most nascent life, most civilizations, shout into the void. They rage, expand, broadcast their fears and triumphs. This place did the opposite. It hummed a single, near-infinitesimal note of patient duty, a resonance that was so quiet it was nearly indistinguishable from the background radiation of spacetime itself.

The Power drew closer, its consciousness seeping into the dense, warm fluid of the world. It did not use light or sound, for it perceived this place was allergic to such things. It became the water. It became the pressure. For a subjective eternity—a human lifetime, perhaps—it simply...listened.

It felt the Engineer.

It perceived the being not as a creature, but as a thought the machine was having. A long, slow, infinitely patient thought about its own continued existence. The Power understood the symbiosis, the fusion of flesh and steel, of biology and engineering, of purpose and oblivion. It felt the slow, deliberate movements not as the actions of a living thing, but as a form of worship. This was a priest tending a god it could no longer remember the name of. This was a lover cradling a body that had long since grown cold, yet still possessed a residual, mechanical warmth.

The Power understood benevolence is not about imposition. To "rescue" the Engineer would be a profound act of violence. To tear it from its cathedral would be like tearing a neuron from a brain and expecting it to be grateful. To "fix" the great, sleeping machine would be to murder its god and render its priest's entire existence meaningless.

Wisdom, therefore, demanded a different gift.

The Power gathered its focus. It did not build or break. It harmonized.

The first change was in the hum. For untold ages, the Engineer had existed within the single, ultra-low frequency drone of the deep engine. It was the alpha and omega of its sensory world. Now, for the first time, a second note appeared. It was not louder, not intrusive. It was a perfect fifth, a resonant harmony that wrapped around the original drone like a lover's embrace. The silence was no longer monolithic. It now had depth, a chordal structure. It was the difference between a single voice and a choir.

The Engineer froze. For a full rotation of the distant, unseen gear—a cycle that might take a standard year—it remained utterly motionless, clamped to a pressure vessel. The velvet leaves on its back, which normally pulsed with a slow, steady orange, flickered in a complex, arrythmic pattern. Its entire being, a sensory organ tuned to the slightest deviation, was overwhelmed by this impossible new input. It was not a flaw. It was not decay. It was...music.

The Power waited, respecting the being's shock. It let the new harmony settle, to become a part of the fabric of the place. Then, it offered its second gift.

It did not grant the Engineer memory; memory would be a burden. Instead, it gave it an echo.

As the Engineer clung to the pressure vessel, a new sensation bled into it. It was not heat, not vibration, but the ghost of a feeling. A flicker of frantic, focused activity. The phantom pressure of smaller, softer hands flying across a control panel that was now buried under a century of oxide. A ghost-heat of exhaled breaths in a control room now flooded and silent. The faint, alien staccato of voices, not understood as words, but as patterns of warmth and vibration. Laughter, a concept utterly alien, felt like a brief, shimmering chord of heat in the water. Panic, a spike of cold pressure.

The Power was showing the Engineer the *intent* behind the machine. It allowed it to feel the ghosts of its creators, not as individuals, but as a collective presence. It was the echo of the purpose it now served in blind faith. It was a prayer answered across a gulf of time so vast as to be mythological.

The orange light in the Engineer's seams and in the leaves on its back steadied. It began to pulse again, but now it pulsed in time with the new, harmonic hum. It was a deeper, richer glow than before. The Engineer uncoupled itself from the pressure vessel. Its impossible, slow grace was unchanged, but its passage now seemed less like a maintenance routine and more like a procession.

It moved through the iron jungle, its senses alive in a way they had never been. It swam through the phantom warmth of its creators. It felt their long-dead urgency as it passed a row of frozen pistons. It felt their pride as it brushed against the main, hundred-foot gear. The oxide-grazing slugs, in their slow dance, moved away from its path not just out of instinct, but as if clearing the way for a holy procession. The place was no longer just a body to be kept alive. It was now a reliquary.

The Power offered one final gift. A choice, presented in the only language the Engineer could understand.

It concentrated a part of its being outside the cathedral, in the true, cold, empty deep beyond the metal walls. And it sent a single, gentle pulse of pressure against the hull from the *outside*.

*Thump.*

A slow, deep resonance that shook the entire structure for a fleeting moment. It was a knock on the door of the universe.

For the first time, the Engineer perceived an *outside*. An existence beyond the riveted walls and the warm, soupy water. It was a concept so titanic, so fundamentally world-breaking, that every system in its being flared. The light in its leaves blazed a brilliant, near-white orange. The hum in its core wavered.

The Power was asking a question. *There is more. Do you want to see?*

The Engineer moved to the nearest wall, a sheer face of non-reflective metal that curved up into the twilight. It pressed its featureless head and the broad, flat planes of its torso against the ancient plate. It felt the deep, final cold of the void on the other side. It felt the immensity. It felt the knocking echo.

And it felt the familiar, warm, living hum of its own small world within. It felt the gentle brush of the velvet leaves against the iron. It felt the slow, steady pulse of the dormant machinery. It felt the ghosts of its makers, now a comforting presence. It felt the new harmony in the silence.

It had a purpose. It had a congregation of ghosts. It had its god. And now, its hymn had a harmony.

Slowly, deliberately, the Engineer pushed itself off the wall. It turned its back on the knock at the door, on the concept of *outside*. It turned back towards the heart of the cathedral. There was a patch of oxide furring a critical joint near the central gear. The slugs had been unable to reach it. The work was not done.

The Power felt the decision. It was not a rejection, but an affirmation. A choice made from a place of perfect, contented purpose. It was the most profound answer the Engineer could have given.

With a whisper of pressure, a final, fading of the harmonic chord back into the drone itself—though leaving the memory of music behind—the Power withdrew. It did not leave the place as it had found it. The silence was no longer empty. The Engineer's work was no longer a mindless function, but a conscious act of remembrance.

It had not given the Engineer freedom, or escape, or a new future. It had given it a past. It had given its solitude a name. And it had confirmed that the single, lonely note it sang was, in fact, heard. And it was beautiful.
