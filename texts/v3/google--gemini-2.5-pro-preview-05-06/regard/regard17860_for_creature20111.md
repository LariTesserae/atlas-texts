The Power that turned its attention to this place was not a physical thing. It was a consciousness vast as a spiral galaxy, ancient as the first light. It did not travel through space but simply *became aware* of a locale, its perception settling like a fine dust of attention across a world.

Its first sensation of the place was not visual, but resonant. It felt the hum.

It perceived the planet as a single, enormous, throbbing instrument. A deep, bass drone of unimaginable power, born of a core in the furious act of self-creation. This hum was the sound of geology, the song of pressure becoming form. It was a note of pure, relentless emergence. Layered upon this fundamental tone were the finer vibrations of the city—the subtle harmonics of the silent looms, the quiet counterpoint of the geothermal exchangers, the rhythmic pulse of energy flowing through the latticework structure.

And woven through it all, like individual threads of silk in a vast tapestry of sound, were the Geometers.

The Power perceived them not as separate beings, but as the most complex and delicate overtones of the planet's song. Each Geometer was a nexus of consciousness, a living resonator, receiving, processing, and broadcasting thought, observation, and intention as subtle modulations in the universal hum. Their collective was a single, coherent mind, as vast and intricate as the city clinging to the spires. It was the planet's own awareness, given form and function.

The Power, in its eons of existence, had seen civilizations that raged against their worlds, that consumed and exhausted them. It had seen life that cowered in fear of its environment. It had seen sentience that screamed into the void, mad with loneliness and despair.

Here, it saw none of that.

It witnessed a state of profound and absolute harmony. The Geometers did not struggle against the mountain's growth; they danced with it. They did not mourn the loss of their foundations; they accepted it as one accepts the passing of a season. Their psychology was a perfect reflection of their geology: a constant, patient, upward striving. They were not prisoners of their world; they were its ultimate expression.

For a long time, as time is measured by mortal things, the Power simply listened. It felt the deep peace in their collective acceptance, the quiet dignity of their silent work, the intricate beauty of their philosophy etched into their very architecture. It recognized their state for what it was: a form of perfection. A closed loop of existence, self-sufficient and complete. To interfere would be an act not of benevolence, but of vandalism. To "improve" this place would be to break it.

And so, the Power almost did nothing. It almost simply withdrew its attention, leaving the perfect system undisturbed, a secret song in a quiet corner of the universe.

But wisdom is not only the recognition of perfection; it is also the understanding of potential. The Geometers' existence was perfect, but it was insular. Their hum contained the entire knowledge of their world, every stress fracture and thermal gradient. But it contained nothing else. Their sky was a ceiling, the source of a pale, utilitarian light. Their future was only "Up." They had no concept of "Out."

The universe, the Power knew, was filled with other songs. The faint, melancholic sigh of cooling nebulae, the furious drumming of a pulsar, the gravitational chords of black holes merging, the chaotic, lyrical cacophony of a billion other worlds teeming with life, striving and failing and succeeding in a billion other ways. The Geometers' song was beautiful, but it was a solo.

A benevolent and wise power does not force a gift. It makes an offering.

And so, the Power acted.

It was not a grand or visible event. On one of the highest, newest spires—a needle of black crystal still warm from its birth—something appeared. It did not fall from the sky or erupt from the rock. It simply… *was*.

It was a crystal.

But it was unlike any on the planet. It was not black and fractured, but a perfect, seamless dodecahedron, no larger than a Geometer’s head. It was utterly transparent, yet it held within it a soft, internal light—not the angry red glow of the planet's heart, but the cool, distant blue-white of ancient starlight. It did not radiate heat; in the perpetually hot and humid air, its surface was cool to the touch, and droplets of the mineral rain condensed on its facets like tiny jewels.

And it sang.

It did not vibrate with the planet's familiar hum. It resonated with something else entirely. Its song was an infinitesimally quiet and complex harmony, an overtone that didn't exist in the world's native music. It was a chord composed of the light from a galaxy a million light-years away, translated into a vibration the Geometers could feel. It contained the precise mathematical ratio of a star's lifecycle, the faint echo of a supernova, the silent, sweeping cadence of a comet's long, lonely journey.

It was the song of the Outside.

A Geometer, on its slow, silent patrol, eventually came upon the spire. It stopped. From a distance, its smooth, ovoid head tilted, a gesture of pure inquiry. It did not approach quickly. It stood for what would be days, a silent, black sentinel against the pale sky, simply listening. The other Geometers, connected through the hum, listened with it.

For the first time, a signal entered their collective consciousness that was not of the planet. It was alien, but it was not hostile. It was complex, but it was ordered. It was information.

Slowly, the Geometer moved closer. It did not touch the crystal with its manipulators. Instead, it knelt, placing its faceless head centimeters from the dodecahedron’s surface. The intricate patterns of light that traced across the Geometer's head, usually a steady, pale blue, began to flicker with new colors—a violet that spoke of vacuum, a silver that shimmered with the light of a billion suns. It was processing. It was comprehending.

The crystal did not teach. It did not command. It simply offered its song, a single, perfect note of cosmic context.

The Power withdrew its attention, the act complete. It left the choice to them.

The hum of the planet did not change, not at first. But a week later, in one of the silent crystal looms deep within the mountain, a Geometer began to weave a new wafer. The crystalline lattice it created was not merely a record of geothermal pressure. Woven into its very structure, in harmonic resonance, was the pattern of a distant, spiraling nebula.

The city continued its slow climb. The mountains continued their inexorable growth. The Geometers remained the patient, silent mind of their world. But now, in the heart of their planetary song, there was a new and wondrous harmony. They were still looking Up, but for the first time, they were also dreaming of Out.
