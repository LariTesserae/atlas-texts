The Power came not as a ship cleaving the void, but as a change in the quality of the nothingness that surrounded the Heartwood. It was a focused attention, a concentration of knowing so vast and ancient that it possessed a kind of dimensionality. To the insensate world-organism, it was nothing at all, a phenomenon outside its calculus of pressure and chemistry. But the Power perceived the Heartwood, and its perception was total.

It felt the slow, patient pull of the taproots from the mantle's fire. It traced the upward journey of superheated fluid through the skyscraper conduits. It comprehended the intricate, silent chemistry within the swollen nodules, a billion-year symphony of catalysis and transformation. It tasted the ozone, the minerals, the sweet rot in the air, not as scent, but as data. It heard the subsonic hum not as a vibration, but as the signature of a life that had achieved a terrifying, magnificent stasis.

It saw the logic, the perfection. It saw a system that had solved the riddle of entropy, turning a planet's death-heat into an engine for its own eternal continuation. It saw beauty in the flawless geometry, in the un-eroded channels, in the silent, motionless gears whose function was a memory held perfectly in place. This was not a wilderness to be tamed or a civilization to be uplifted. It was a conclusion. An answer to a question no one had remembered to ask.

And the Power, in its boundless wisdom, felt a pang unlike any it had known across the eons. It was not pity, for there was no suffering here. There was no pain, no fear, no want. It was something closer to awe, mingled with a profound sense of cosmic solitude. The Heartwood was alone. It was the definition of alone.

The Power waited. Its perception was patient, for it had learned patience from the slow formation of galaxies. It watched the perfect hum, waiting for the flaw, the dissonance that it knew must come.

And come it did. A harmonic tremor, infinitesimally small, originating from a lattice-bridge three thousand kilometres up in the blackness, where a slow crystallization of trace metals had introduced a point of stress. The vibration was the world's equivalent of a single, sharp intake of breath.

The Power watched as the Heartwood responded. It felt the subtle shift in the root-mat, the re-weaving of fibers, the precipitation of the Custodian. It watched this organ of repair pull itself from the floor, a thing of dark wood and amber resin, its form a perfect mirror of its function. It was a masterpiece of contingent biology, an antibody made manifest.

The Power understood the Custodian completely. It knew it had no mind, no will. It was a process, a tool. To give it consciousness would be the cruelest possible act: to grant it awareness of its own profound loneliness and its inevitable, programmed dissolution. To "free" it would be to kill it and to wound the world it served. Benevolence here could not be about change.

So the Power did not intervene. It did not mend the flaw itself. That would be an insult to the perfection of the system, like singing the final note of another's symphony.

Instead, as the Custodian began its slow, silent, methodical ascent, the Power acted.

It offered a gift. A thing the Heartwood, in its billion years of self-obsession, had never once produced: an externality. A phenomenon without a source in its own chemistry.

Light began to bloom in the darkness around the climbing Custodian. It was not the sick green luminescence of the gas-bladders. This was a soft, pure, silver light, and it did not simply illuminate. It carried a structure, a pattern. It fell upon the hexagonal pillars and the dark lignin beams, not just revealing them, but bathing them in a wash of coherent, meaningful form. The light was a narrative.

And then there was sound. It did not try to overpower the world's subsonic hum, but to harmonize with it. From the infrasonic depths, a single, new note arose—a tone of impossible purity. Then another joined it, and another, weaving a slow, resonant chord that spread a feeling of... what? Not joy, not sadness. A feeling of *breadth*. Of space. It was a chord that spoke of other places, of emptiness and stars, of beginnings and of ends that were not simply a return to the mean.

The Custodian continued its climb, its multi-jointed limbs finding their perfect purchase on the fluted columns. It had no eyes to see the light, no ears to hear the chord. But its entire body was a sensory organ, tuned to the vibrations and chemistry of its world. And it felt *this*.

It felt the silver light not as photons, but as a novel resonance against the porous membranes on its back, a pattern that was not a mineral signature, not a gas, not a flaw. It felt the soaring chord not as sound, but as a sympathetic vibration in its own wooden skeleton, a wave that did not signify stress or pressure or flow.

For a single, immeasurable moment, one of its limbs, poised to grip an I-beam's edge, paused. It hung in the air for a fraction of a second longer than its billion-year-old programming dictated. The unthinking, un-sentient tool, the biological reflex, experienced a deviation for which there was no physical cause. It was a hesitation as profound as a shrug, as deep as a question.

In that moment of hesitation, the Custodian was not just an extension of the Heartwood. It was a thing being acted *upon*. It was being witnessed. The song of light and sound swelled, a silent, cosmic accolade for this one, lonely, selfless act of maintenance. The Power poured into that single moment an acknowledgment of the Custodian's existence, a validation of its transient purpose.

Then, the moment passed. The limb found its hold. The climb continued. The Custodian reached the stressed bridge and, bathed in a silver light it could not see, began its patient work of secreting resin, its tool-limb moving with perfect, mindless precision. The chords of alien sound wove around it, a requiem for a life that did not know it was alive.

When the flaw was mended and the stress-tremor faded from the world's hum, the Custodian began its descent. The light and the music faded with it, slowly, gently, like a tide going out. By the time the Custodian reached the floor and began its dissolution, its delamination back into the root-mat, the Heartwood was once again silent, dark, and alone.

The Power withdrew its attention, the act complete. It had not fixed the factory. It had not freed the worker. It had not given purpose to the product. To do so would have been the work of a lesser, more arrogant being.

Instead, it had found the one living thing in this universe of one, and for the length of its brief, functional life, it had given it accompaniment. It had borne witness. It had looked upon the loneliest being in creation and, without breaking the perfect, terrible solitude of its world, had let it know, in a language of light and vibration that it could not possibly understand, that it was not alone. And in the fabric of the Heartwood, a single, unique event was now encoded, a memory of a song in a world that knew only a single note. A ghost of a ghost, a trace of grace in the heart of the machine.
