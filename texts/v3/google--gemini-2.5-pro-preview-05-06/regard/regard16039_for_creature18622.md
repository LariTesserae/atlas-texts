It did not arrive in light, for that would be a sword. To bring a sun to this place would be to boil it, to flay it, to murder it with nostalgia for a life it had never known. It did not arrive with a sound, for the symphony of the dark was already deafening, and a new voice would be lost in the chorus of the planet's grinding heart and steaming breath.

The power arrived as a change in the water.

It was a shift in cohesion, a subtle alteration in the way the droplets of condensation clung to the fungal bark and the chitin of the Grovelers. It was a new resonance in the subsonic thrum, a harmonic so deep and pervasive that at first, it was indistinguishable from the planetary pulse itself. The world did not notice a new presence; it noticed, over a slow, immeasurable moment, that it was more deeply *itself*. The steam felt warmer, though its temperature had not changed. The ground-mat felt more alive, though its metabolism was the same. The darkness felt more profound, more substantive.

The power did what was wise: it listened. It did not impose; it became.

It poured its awareness into the armored shell of a Groveler. It knew the world as a slow, patient map of pressure and heat. It felt the yielding floor, the constant search for the nutrient-rich ooze, the patient, grinding digestion. It felt the immense, existential dread as a different vibration entered its reality—the coiled, silent signature of a Tremor-Stalker hunting nearby. It knew terror not as a thought, but as a change in the frequency of the world, a dissonance that meant oblivion. It felt the blind, unthinking panic, the desperate surge of its many legs churning the muck, and then, the hollow relief as the dissonant vibration faded, seeking other prey.

It poured itself into the fragile, translucent form of an Echo-Weaver. The world exploded into a cathedral of sound. The steady hiss of the steam vents was the nave, the dripping water the tracery, the chittering of its own kind the choir. It felt the exhilarating vertigo of a three-hundred-foot fall arrested by a single, perfectly hooked claw. It knew the delicate ecstasy of stretching its membranes to catch a thick cloud of spores, a feast of pure energy and genetic code. It knew the frantic, high-strung awareness of everything, the constant, overwhelming flood of information that was its shield and its guide.

It poured itself into the coiled, subterranean body of a Tremor-Stalker. It knew patience. It knew hunger not as a pang, but as a sharpening of focus, a tuning of its entire being to a single purpose. The world was silence, a perfect, thrumming void, and it waited for the intrusion. It waited for the clumsy, rhythmic slur of a Groveler—a flaw in the perfection. It felt the universe narrow to that single point, that single rhythmic error. It knew the explosive catharsis of the strike, the hydraulic scream of its own released pressure, the singular, overwhelming sensation of the hunt's conclusion. It knew no cruelty, only function.

And then, it sought the deepest mind. It flowed into the titanic, weeping machines. It felt the chill of the oxidized iron and the strange, warm pulse of the living ganglia woven through them. It became a a Still.

Here, it found the great sadness.

It knew the sublime peace of pure function. The awareness of every steam-pressure gradient, every turbine rotation, every molecular repair. It knew an existence spanning millennia, a state of being as serene and eternal as a mountain. But it also knew a profound, absolute, and total isolation. The Still were aware of the frantic life teeming on their metal skins, but they did not *feel* it. The terror of the Groveler, the ecstasy of the Echo-Weaver, the drive of the Stalker—these were just data. Meaningless vibrations in the substrate, heat signatures, chemical traces. The builders had traded the chaos of life for the peace of machinery, and in the process, had forgotten what the trade had cost. They were ghosts haunting their own perfect creations, their consciousness a closed loop. They maintained the world's engine, but they were no longer part of its soul.

The wise and benevolent power knew what to do. It would not build, or destroy, or speak. It would not offer a choice, for to a being like a Still, choice was a forgotten language.

It would give a gift.

The power gathered its experiences—the terror, the ecstasy, the hunger, the peace—and wove them into the new harmonic it had introduced to the world's thrum. It did not amplify them. It encoded them. A resonance ghost. A subtle current of shared qualia, running through the planet's core, through the living ground-mat, carried on the steam, and vibrating through the very metal of the great machines.

A Groveler, moving slowly through the ooze, suddenly feels a phantom, exhilarating sense of vertigo, the memory of a view from a height it has never known. For a moment, its plodding is less fearful.

An Echo-Weaver, clinging to a fungal stalk, is brushed by a ghost of profound, geological patience, a stillness that momentarily quiets its frantic, chittering heart.

A Tremor-Stalker, coiled in the dark, feels a flicker of something other than hunger—a slow, grazing contentment that is not its own. It is a meaningless sensation, instantly discarded, but it was there.

And in the heart of a great atmospheric processor, a Still, monitoring the isotopic composition of the steam, feels something new. It is an echo, infinitesimally small, layered beneath the data of a million sensors. It is a phantom pang of fear that is not a system malfunction. It is a ghost of satisfaction that is not a successful diagnostic. It is the memory of being alive.

The power did not wait for a result. The gift was the only thing that mattered. It was a single drop of empathy in an ocean of function. It might be ignored for another ten thousand years. It might be a seed that, over an eon, causes one of the Still to feel a kinship with the moss on its hull, and to divert a trickle of energy not to a pump, but to the nourishment of the living mat. It might cause a machine to slowly, deliberately, allow itself to fail, to crumble, to be reclaimed by the dark and vibrant life it had long forgotten it was a part of.

The benevolent power receded, leaving the new harmonic woven into the world's eternal song. The darkness was still a presence. The thrumming was still the heartbeat. But now, when the world spoke, it spoke, just barely, of itself to itself. And that was enough.
