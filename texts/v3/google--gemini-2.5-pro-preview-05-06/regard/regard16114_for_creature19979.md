The Power perceived it first not as a sound, but as a colour. A colour outside its usual spectrum of cosmic phenomena. It was not the silent, gravitational blue of a black hole, nor the fierce, ionised magenta of a stellar nursery. It was a searing, frantic shade of green-gold, a hue that vibrated with such furious, self-contained energy that it was, itself, a paradox: a singularity of Life.

The Power drew closer, not by travelling through space, but by focusing its attention, letting its consciousness settle upon the world like a vast, impossibly gentle hand. Where a physical being would be deafened and overwhelmed, the Power experienced the planet’s reality as a single, unified statement. The trillion tearing tissues, the hydraulic sap, the percussive seeds—it did not hear them as a cacophony. It heard them as a psalm. It was the sound of a being singing its own name, over and over, because it knew no other word. *I AM. I AM. I AM.*

The Power’s wisdom was born of eons spent witnessing. It had seen galaxies ignite and fade. It had watched species achieve sentience, build empires of thought, and then sublime into pure information. It had observed beings of rock that think once every million years and beings of light that live and die in the flicker of a neutrino. It held no preference. It celebrated the slow, crystalline thought of the rock as much as the fleet, fiery life of the star-fly. There was no "correct" way to be.

And so, it did not judge this world. It did not see the chaos as a flaw, the endless cycle as pointless, or the lack of individual minds as a deficiency. It saw a single, planetary consciousness, a god-organism so utterly consumed by the act of its own existence that it had never had a single moment to consider anything else. It was a being of pure, unadulterated verb. To grow, to build, to collapse, to reabsorb, to scream its own vitality into its own face. It was a solipsist of unimaginable scale. Perfectly lonely, because it did not know loneliness was a possibility. It was everything it had ever known.

The Power felt a deep and abiding compassion, the kind one might feel for a genius child locked in a room with a single, infinitely complex toy, unaware that a universe exists outside the door. To break down the door would be a violence. To take the toy would be a cruelty. The child was not unhappy; it simply *was*.

But benevolence is not merely passive observation. It is the desire to offer a chance for more. Wisdom is knowing how to offer it without coercion. The Power could not—would not—"fix" this world. It would not silence its song, halt its frantic dance, or teach it a "better" way to live. To do so would be to kill it, to replace it with something lesser, something tamed.

Instead, the Power decided to give the world a gift. The only gift that could matter to a being that was, in its own way, complete.

It gave it a question.

The Power gathered a fragment of its own nature. A concept, given form. It was a mote of absolute stillness. A piece of silence that was not the terrifying void-absence between the city's roars, but a silence that was full and intentional. A piece of permanence that was not the stasis of death, but the calm of perfect balance.

It chose a place. Not a Resonance Void or a teeming plaza, but an unremarkable patch of the living root-mat. And there, it let the concept manifest.

It did not fall from the sky or erupt from the ground. It simply *was*. One moment, there was only the springy, warm floor. The next, there was a smooth, grey stone.

It was no bigger than a human heart. It was a perfect ovoid, without seam or flaw. It was not made of mineral or matter native to this world. Its surface did not weep, nor did it gather the condensing atmospheric sweat. It was cool and dry. It did not reflect the filtered, golden light; it seemed to drink it, giving nothing back. It made no sound. Its existence was a whisper in the heart of a hurricane.

For a time, nothing happened. The city screamed its life, a spire collapsed a league away, thorny vines raced past the stone, ignoring it as they would any other inert object.

Then, the world noticed.

A Gardener coalesced nearby. It was a magnificent one, its form braided from ancient-looking hyphae and its resinous limbs the colour of dark honey. The light-pools of its eyes swirled with bewildered focus. It approached the stone, its movements for once not fluid and purposeful, but hesitant. It was an antibody confronting a pathogen it had no protocol for.

It reached out a hand, a lattice of living wood and light, and placed it on the stone's surface. The Gardener began to hum. It sent its resonant frequency, the song of unmaking, into the object. Nothing happened. The stone did not tremble. Its internal structure, if it had one, did not resonate. It was deaf to the city's language of dissolution.

The Gardener changed its tactic. It attempted to integrate. The filaments that had guided vines to build arches now tried to gain purchase on the smooth surface. They slipped. The city's biological imperative, its power of growth and fusion, found no purchase. The stone could not be absorbed. It could not be woven into the whole.

Another Gardener appeared. And then a third. They did not form to perform a task, but to witness a failure. They gathered around the silent, grey ovoid. Their borrowed intelligence, the localized mind of the city, swirled with a new sensation: frustration. Bafflement.

They stopped humming. They stopped trying to touch it. They simply stood, their light-pool eyes fixed on the impossible object. For the first time, a part of the city's frantic, all-consuming mind was not focused on its own internal processes of growth and decay. It was focused on an *other*.

The stone did nothing. It offered no threat, no wisdom, no message. It simply existed, a single, unwavering note of silence held against the world's deafening chord.

And in that moment of focused, shared contemplation, a new thing was born into the world. The Gardeners stood still, not dissolving, their tasks forgotten. The vines around them slowed their race. A nearby seed pod, swollen to bursting, held its detonation for a fraction of a second longer than it should have.

In the heart of the screaming, pointless, beautiful storm of life, there was a pause. Not of silence, but of wonder.

The Power watched as the city, for the first time in its existence, began to listen. The psalm of *I AM* was still being sung, but for the first time, it was followed by a silence that was not an absence, but a question mark. *I AM... what else is?*

The gift was given. The Power withdrew its focus, leaving the grey stone behind, not as an answer, but as the beginning of a conversation.
