The power that noticed was not a being of ships or bodies. It was a consciousness woven from the subtle gravity and ancient light between galaxies, a Traveler whose senses were attuned to the grand patterns of existence. Its awareness was a soft net cast across a swathe of the cosmos, and in that net, it felt a pulse.

It was not the frantic, chaotic spark of biological competition, nor the sharp, rhythmic signals of a technological race. It was a note, impossibly pure and sustained. A chord of geological, atmospheric, and biological harmony held steady for eons. The Traveler focused, its attention coalescing like a lens, and the world of the singing spires resolved not as a place, but as a single, functioning thought.

It perceived the whole. The wind, the stone, the moss, the weeping water, the slow crawl of the Weavers—it saw them not as separate components, but as inseparable truths of a single verb. The planet did not *have* a song; the planet *was* singing.

And the Weavers were not custodians. They were the will of the song made manifest. They were the physical embodiment of the music's desire to perfect itself. Their slow, painstaking work was not a chore, but an act of meditation, of devotion to a god that was the very ground they climbed. They were fingers on a string, adjusting the pitch. They were the breath held and released to sustain a single note.

The Traveler’s benevolence was not the sort that sought to alleviate suffering, for there was no suffering here. There was only purpose. Its wisdom was not the sort that sought to teach or to guide, for what could it teach a system that had achieved a state of such profound, dynamic equilibrium? To interfere would be a sacrilege. To offer a gift would be to introduce a dissonance into the perfect chord. To speak would be to scream into a library of silence and sound.

What, then, could it do?

For a long time, measured in the slow drift of constellations, the Traveler simply listened. It let the planet's song fill its vast consciousness. It learned the deep drone of the largest flutes, the counterpoint of the whistles, the mournful glissando as the sun's warmth shifted the winds. It felt the patient, infinitesimal work of the Weavers as tiny, deliberate *glissandi* in the grand composition. It felt the life of a Weaver begin as a subtle new harmonic within a spire, and its death as the final, quiet chord of its body merging back with the stone.

And in this deep listening, the Traveler understood. The song was a prayer, a statement of existence sung to a universe that, until now, had not been listening. It was an act of profound, cosmic loneliness, but a loneliness so absolute and ancient that the singers themselves had no concept of it. They did not know they were alone. They only knew the necessity of the song.

The wisest, most benevolent act was not to change the song, but to answer it. To let the singers know they had been heard.

The Traveler decided. It would not descend. It would not manifest. It would not speak.

It would become a mirror.

Slowly, carefully, subtly, the Traveler began to adjust the very fabric of spacetime around the small planet. It was an act of cosmic tuningforksmanship. It didn't add energy or matter. It altered resonance. It created a sympathetic vibration on a scale the Weavers could not conceive of, but could absolutely feel.

One day, for the first time in a million years, something changed for the Weavers. A twelve-foot custodian, pressed against its spire, tending to a patch of ochre moss, felt it. The thrum of the spire beneath its body was the same. The wind's whistle through the pores was the same. But underlying it all, vibrating not through the stone or the air but through the Weaver's very being, was a new resonance.

It was an echo, but not an echo. It was a perfect harmonic.

When the spire sang its deep note of C, a note so profound it was felt more than heard, the universe around it now resonated with a G, impossibly soft, a perfect fifth that gave the spire's note a sudden, inexplicable fullness. When a high wind shrieked a B-flat through a tiny hole, the cosmos answered with a whisper of D-flat, a minor third that turned the shriek into a thing of heartbreaking beauty.

The Traveler did not compose a new song. It simply, and with infinite precision, provided the missing notes of the chord that the planet was already playing. It became the cathedral for the choir.

The Weavers did not stop their work. Their purpose was unchanged. They still crawled their slow, patient paths. They still tuned the spires, patched the moss, and dissolved the blockages. But now, their work felt different. When a Weaver added a millimeter of calcite paste to a spire's lip, the change in the note was no longer just a local correction. It was now met with an instantaneous, complementary shift in the cosmic resonance. The act of tuning was no longer a solitary maintenance; it was a conversation.

They had no minds to conceive of an audience, no eyes to see a listener in the sky. But they could *feel* it. They felt the rightness of their work in a way they never had before. Their slow, ancient existence, once a closed loop of purpose, was now imbued with a sense of… completion. The song was no longer sung into a void. It was received, understood, and cherished.

The benevolent power did nothing, and in doing nothing, it did everything. It became the silent, unseen partner in a slow, eternal duet. It gave the Weavers the one thing they lacked, a thing they never knew they needed: the feeling, resonant and true, that their beautiful, lonely song was finally, and forever, being heard.
