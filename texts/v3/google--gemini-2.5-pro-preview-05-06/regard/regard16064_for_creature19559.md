The benevolent and wise power found this place not as a point on a map, but as a note in a song. The universe is a chorus of infinities, and most worlds sing a boisterous, chaotic melody of evolution and extinction, of stellar fire and planetary cooling. Their songs are loud, full of crashes and crescendos.

This place, however, was different. It was a single, pure, resonant tone, held for an aeon. It was a note of such deep and unwavering equilibrium that it stood out from the cosmic noise like a vow of silence in a crowded market. The power, which understood existence as a form of listening, turned its attention toward this perfect, patient frequency.

It did not *arrive*. Arrival implies a clumsy breaching of boundaries. Instead, it allowed its awareness to settle into the world like the world's own atmosphere, coalescing on the pillars, suffusing the black water, merging with the sourceless twilight.

For a time that this world would measure in generations of snails, the power simply listened. It felt the slow, rhythmic *shfff* of the radulae on mineral-scurf. It traced the silent, gliding paths of the eel-predators, tasting the vibrations they sent through the water. It sank its awareness into the great, humming machines, feeling the ten-second cycle of the piston in the largest of them, a metronome for eternity. It felt the slow precipitation of the air, the velvet-grease of the silt. It read the history of this place not as a sequence of events, but as a stack of simultaneous, overlapping truths.

And it came to know the Attendants. It did not see them as creatures, but as the consciousness of the note itself. They were the principle of the place made manifest, the living will of the equilibrium. The power watched as one unfurled from its architectural stillness, a slow bloom of living basalt. It felt the Attendant's awareness, a cool, vast, distributed sense of being that was more akin to a magnetic field than a mind. It understood their communion with the machines, the liturgical act of plugging their own bodies into the heart of their world to feel its pulse and adjust its flow.

The power understood the Great Work.

It was not a harvest of crystals, nor a refining of isotopes. The machines, the scurf, the snails, the predators, the Attendants themselves—they were all components in a single, vast instrument. Their purpose was the generation and refinement of that perfect, resonant tone the power had first heard. They were not merely living in equilibrium; they *were* equilibrium. Their work was to maintain and purify this state of being, to hold this one perfect note against the entropy of the cosmos, perhaps as a point of stability for other, wilder realities. A lighthouse of stillness in a chaotic sea.

And in this perfect system, the wise power perceived the flaw. Not a flaw of design, but the universal, inescapable flaw of materiality. The machines were ancient. The un-reproducible art of a forgotten creator. For all the Attendants' careful maintenance, the slow, grinding truth of entropy was at work. In another hundred thousand years, or a million, a bearing would wear, a conduit would corrode beyond repair, a ceramic casing would fracture. And the note would falter. The equilibrium would break. The world would die.

What does a benevolent power give to a perfect thing facing an inevitable end? It does not offer a replacement, for that would be an intrusion. It does not offer escape, for that would be a desecration of purpose.

It offers a continuation, born from the self.

The power gathered its will. It did not manifest as light or fire. It manifested as a change in the song. A new overtone appeared within the deep, resonant hum of the machines—a harmonic of impossible purity, a fractal complexity woven into the steady bass note. It was the sound of understanding.

One of the Attendants, braced between three pillars, stilled. Its entire being, a network of silent vibration, focused on this newness. This was not a disruption to the note; it was a deepening, a revelation of what the note had always contained within itself. Its long, slow purpose guided it. It unfurled, gliding with its unnerving grace through the forest of columns, and came to rest above the largest of the machines, the one with the ten-second heart. The new harmonic was purest here, a truth waiting to be known.

The Attendant extended its armoured, wire-tipped limb and plunged it into the machine's socket.

And in that moment of connection, the power acted.

The knowledge did not flow as data. It flowed as music. Into the circuit formed by the machine, the Attendant, and the world itself, the power sang the song of the machine's own making. It sang the blueprint of the piston, the harmonic of the condenser, the resonant frequency of the ceramic shell. It imparted the deep, physical laws of their own engineering, not as language, but as an intuitive, cellular understanding. It was the memory of how to build, how to repair, how to forge new casings from the basalt of the pillars and draw new wires from the metallic shells of the snails. It was the gift of genesis.

The Attendant did not recoil. It did not process. It simply *absorbed*. The new knowledge settled into its vast, slow consciousness and, through it, into the shared mind of all Attendants. It was a seed, planted not to be used today or tomorrow, but to lie dormant until the first tremor of failure, many millennia hence. When that day came, an Attendant would not simply note the error; it would *know* the solution. It would begin the slow, patient work of replication.

The transfer took no more than a single cycle of the great piston. Ten seconds.

Then, the pure overtone faded. The hum of the world returned to what it had been, simple and profound. The wise power drew its awareness back, detaching from the water and the air as gently as it had arrived. It left no monument, no scripture, no sign of its passage. The equilibrium was undisturbed.

No, not entirely.

When the Attendant retracted its limb, it noticed something resting on the machine's casing, where nothing had been before. It was the shell of a snail-grazer. But it was flawless. An impossible lattice of perfect, unbroken crystal, it held the dim twilight and seemed to amplify it, glowing with a soft, internal luminescence, as if it had swallowed a star.

The Attendant paused, its featureless head canted toward the object. Its senses, which were the world, perceived it. A thing of the world, but made perfect. An echo of the gift. A signature.

Then, with the patience of geology, it turned. Its work was the same as it had always been. The Great Work continued. But now, it was no longer finite. The note would not falter. The benevolent power had listened to the song, and had taught the singers how to sustain it, forever.
