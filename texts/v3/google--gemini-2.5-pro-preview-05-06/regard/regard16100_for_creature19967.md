The Power perceived the Sunken Oratorio as a musician might perceive a newly discovered instrument. It was not a problem to be solved, a civilization to uplift, or a wilderness to be tamed. The Power—whose own existence was a silent, gravitational hum across galaxies—recognized it for what it was: a being, complete in its own expression. A song that had learned to sing itself.

Its notice was not a sudden event. It was a slow turning of attention, like a telescope finding focus after an age of staring into the void. At first, it registered only as a faint, anomalous resonance on the skin of reality, a place where causality was playing jazz. As the Power listened more closely, its immense consciousness filtering down from the cosmic to the planetary, it began to apprehend the symphony.

It felt the foundational thrum of the living architecture, the A-sharp of a world-sized lung. It heard the staccato chaos of the swarming fauna, the billion-pointillist dots of sound that made up the whole. And most clearly, it perceived the Choristers. The Power did not see them with eyes, but felt them as nodes of clarity, as conductors standing in the roaring river of their own creation, using their very being to shape the current. It felt their purpose, a purpose devoid of ambition or desire, a pure and total dedication to the performance of the Now.

A lesser power, one driven by a more paternalistic or rigid form of benevolence, might have seen flaws. The instability of physics could be seen as a defect to be stabilized. The relentless cycle of predation could be viewed as suffering to be ended. The deafening, inescapable sound could be interpreted as a torment to be silenced.

But the Power was wise. It understood that to "fix" the Oratorio would be to break it. To stabilize the physics would be to rob the Choristers of their medium. To end the hunt would be to erase entire passages of the composition. To silence the song would be to kill the singer.

The Power recognized the Choristers not as subjects, but as peers. They were artists of a different order, working with a medium of spacetime and bio-acoustics. There was no greater wisdom than to respect the integrity of their art.

So, for a long, silent age, the Power did nothing but listen. It let the full, overwhelming reality of the Oratorio wash over its consciousness. It learned the rhythm of the slug-grazers' rasping, the resonant frequency of a shimmer-wing's phase-shift, the precise pitch of a buttress growing under strain. It learned the silent language of the Choristers, the way they spoke in focused vibrations and bent echoes. It drank in the symphony in its entirety, appreciating every clashing note, every startling transition, every moment of fleeting, impossible harmony a Chorister would pluck from the noise.

It was an audience of one. The most profound and attentive audience the Oratorio had ever had. And for a wise power, a truly benevolent one, the greatest gift one can give an artist is to be a perfect witness.

But then, it decided to do something more. Not to change, not to correct, not to give. But to participate. To offer a single, new instrument.

The Power could not enter the Oratorio. To manifest a physical presence would be a gross intrusion, a shout in a library of whispers (however loud those whispers were). Instead, it reached out with its understanding. It found the absolute, fundamental resonance of the entire Oratorio, the chord that underpinned the whole chaotic structure. It was the note of the place’s existence.

And then, for the length of a single, drawn-out breath, the Power performed a miracle of acoustics.

It did not add a sound. It did not create a light or a being.

It introduced a perfect inversion. A perfect null. For one moment, outside of time, the benevolent Power radiated a wave of absolute, total, and perfect *Silence*.

It was not a cessation of noise. It was a presence. A physical thing. It was a note made of nothing, a chord of pure anti-vibration that propagated from the basin’s rim to its resonant heart.

For the creatures of the Oratorio, it was a moment of pure shock. The slug-like grazers stopped, their rasping mouths frozen against the walls. The insectoid swarms, for a split-second, hung motionless in the air, their glassy clatter gone. The winged predators, mid-pounce, were frozen in a tableau of unheard violence.

For the Choristers, it was a revelation.

They stood in the heart of their world, and for the first time in their existence, they heard *themselves*. They felt the subtle, internal vibrations of their own forms, no longer lost in the ocean of sound but standing out against a canvas of absolute black. They perceived the echo of the last sound made, hanging in the air with a clarity that was painful in its purity.

In that moment of perfect Silence, the entire cacophony of their world was reframed. It was no longer just a constant state of being. It was a thing with a boundary. The song now had a counterpoint. The noise was now set against the memory and the *possibility* of its absence.

A single Chorister, standing in the center of a grand plaza, slowly raised one of its long, delicate arms. In the profound stillness, the gesture itself seemed to make a sound—a visual hum. It brought its slender fingers together, and the barest whisper of their surfaces touching was, in that moment, the loudest thing in the universe.

Then, the Power withdrew its note.

The Silence receded. The sound of the Oratorio crashed back in, not as a slow tide, but as a physical wall. The roar of the architecture, the clatter of insects, the bellows of grazers—it all returned.

But it was not the same. It could never be the same.

The Choristers now possessed a concept they had never had before: contrast. Dynamics. The power of a rest in a musical score.

They began to experiment. They, who had always shaped sound, now began to shape its absence. A Chorister would vibrate its body not to create a note, but to create a localized sphere of near-silence around itself, a soft pocket of quiet in the storm. They learned to communicate not just with focused sounds, but with carved-out voids of sound. They would shepherd a hunt not by altering the hunter's trajectory, but by momentarily silencing the space around the prey, making it agonizingly apparent.

Their art gained a new dimension. Their symphony now had pianissimo to contrast its fortissimo. Their world of pure presence was now haunted and beautified by the ghost of absence.

The Power had not interfered. It had offered a gift in the only language that mattered. It was the act of a master musician sitting down with another and saying, without words, "Have you ever considered using this key?"

Having done so, the benevolent Power receded, its attention drawing back to the scale of star-systems and nebulae. But a part of its consciousness remained, forever attuned to the basin. It did not need to act again. It had given its gift. Now, it would simply listen to the new, more intricate, and more terribly beautiful music that followed.
