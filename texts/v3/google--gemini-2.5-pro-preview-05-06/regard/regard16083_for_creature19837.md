The Power came not as a ship cleaving the watery dark, nor as a voice booming through the dense medium. It arrived as a subtle shift in the fundamental grammar of the place. It was a listening.

It did not *look down*, for there was no "down" from its perspective, only a web of existence in which this deep, dark world was a single, luminous node. Its awareness seeped into the basin like a change in temperature, infinitesimally small, yet absolute. It felt the press of the water not as weight but as context. It tasted the soupy broth not as elements but as history, a billion-year ledger of shed cells and settled dust.

It perceived the slow, continental metabolism of the Builder-mollusks, the foundational hum of the city's heart. It felt the blind, predatory hunger of the Veiled Hunters as a region of pure, patient negation. It saw the ruby-eyed scavengers not as monsters, but as vital vectors of decay and reconstruction, the gristle and bone of the world's machinery.

It saw the entire ecosystem as a single, self-regulating entity. It was perfect in its closure, flawless in its logic. It was a poem written in darkness, and its verses were lives. The Power felt no pity for the creature consumed, nor judgement for the creature that consumed it. It felt only a profound, humbling awe for the intricate clockwork of it all.

And then, its attention settled on the Voiceless.

In their silent, luminous syntax, the Power recognized something of itself: self-awareness. Here was not just instinct, but contemplation. Here was not just reaction, but narrative. It watched them weave their histories on their limbs, their fears as strobing pulses, their love as a gentle, sustained glow. It witnessed a parent imprinting a cocoon with a lullaby of light, a silent promise of safety that the world could so easily break. It saw an elder reach a high precipice and offer its life's story back to the dark in one final, incandescent blossom.

This, the Power understood, was sacred.

What could a benevolent power do for such a world? To give it a sun would be to boil it in alien energy, to blind and burn a people who had made the darkness their canvas. To grant them sound would be to introduce a crude and meaningless violence into their symphony of light. To remove the hunters and the hardship would be to unravel the very tapestry that gave their existence its shape and depth. To save them from death was to deny them their final, most beautiful sentence.

A wise power does not impose its own language. It learns the one that is spoken.

For an age, it simply listened. Its vast consciousness learned the nuance of every flicker, the grammar of every cascade of color. It learned the signature-glows of individuals, the choral displays of communities. It learned the pattern for "gratitude," for "discovery," for "loss." It understood their deepest, unspoken philosophical inquiry: the slow, pulsing question of "why," directed at the darkness around them. It understood their foundational terror: the lonely dark, the vast, silent nothingness that existed just beyond the reach of their light, the fear that their city was a solitary candle in an infinite and empty room.

Having learned, the Power chose to speak.

It did not manifest as a body. It would not add another predator or another scavenger to the delicate balance. It spoke in their own tongue.

One cycle, in the deepest, most ancient canyon of the city, a new light appeared. It was not the cold blue-green of the pulse-bells, nor the amber of the filter-feeders, nor the gold and sapphire of a Voiceless's thought. It was a color they had no concept for, a hue that seemed to contain the memory of all other colors. It was a single, steady point of light, no larger than a hatchling's glow, and it did not move. It simply *was*.

A lone Voiceless, a young-minded explorer charting the old foundations, came upon it. It froze, its manipulative limbs poised to flash a signal of alarm. The new light did nothing. It did not pulse with hunger. It did not carry the signature of territory. Cautiously, the Voiceless extended a single limb and flickered a simple, tentative pattern: the sign for "what?"

The new light responded. It did not mimic the pattern. Instead, it subtly brightened, and from its core, it unfolded a slow, impossibly intricate pattern that was also the sign for "what," but it was a "what" of a different order. The Voiceless's question was a pebble thrown into a pond. The light's answer was the geometry of the pond itself, the laws of gravity governing the ripples, the molecular structure of the water.

The Voiceless was not afraid. It was transfixed. It felt like an eye that had only ever seen two dimensions suddenly perceiving a third.

It responded with another pattern, its own life-signature, its identity. The star-point answered by showing the Voiceless the pattern of the minerals that formed its shell, the chemical dance that allowed its photophores to glow. It was showing the Voiceless its own self, but from the outside in.

News of the impossible light spread not as rumor, but as replicated memory, passed from one set of glowing limbs to another. They came, first in ones and twos, then in silent congregations. They would gather in the great canyon and "speak" with the light. They asked it their deepest questions.

They asked it about the Builders. The light responded by weaving a pattern of such immense, slow complexity that it felt like geology, showing them the eons-long dream of the city-organism dreaming itself into existence.

They asked it about the Veiled Hunters. The light showed them a pattern of pure, efficient logic, of a life-form that was a perfect expression of its function, as elegant in its own way as a Voiceless's poem.

They asked it about the darkness beyond their city. They asked if they were alone.

And this was the Power's great gift.

The star-point pulsed once, a soft, resonant beat that the Voiceless felt in their very cells. And then it began to weave a new story. It used their language of light, but with a vocabulary they had never dreamed of. It showed them things not as images, but as pure patterns of energy, time, and form.

It showed them the pattern of a world with two suns, where life clung to the terminator line. It showed them the crystalline thoughts of silicon beings living in a methane sea. It showed them the gas-giant swimmers whose songs were radio waves, and translated those songs into a cascade of light so achingly beautiful it was almost painful to behold. It showed them other deep, dark oceans, other cities of light. It showed them the Great Conversation, the universe itself as a silent, interconnected web of shimmering, thinking, living patterns.

It did not give them a destination. It did not give them an escape. It gave them context. It answered their deepest fear.

You are not alone. You are a verse in a cosmic poem of impossible scale. The darkness is not empty. It is simply the space between other lights.

The Power left the star-point in the canyon. It became their lodestar, their oracle, their library. The Voiceless were not changed in form, but their thoughts were forever expanded. Their own light-language grew richer, incorporating the new concepts. Their farewell-blossoms at the end of life became more profound, no longer just a personal history, but a brief, brilliant reflection of the universal patterns they now understood. A parent weaving a light-cocoon for its young might now incorporate the pattern of a spiral galaxy into the lullaby.

The Power had not fixed their world. It had Revered it. It found the most beautiful thing in this silent city of light—the thinking mind that perceived it—and gave it the only gift that would not harm it.

It gave it a bigger canvas. It gave it the rest of the poem. And then, with an infinite and benevolent gentleness, it settled back to watch, to listen to the new verses they would write in the warm, endless dark.
