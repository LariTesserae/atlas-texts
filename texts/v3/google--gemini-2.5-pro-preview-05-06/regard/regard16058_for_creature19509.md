The Power perceived it first as a Song.

In the silent, interwoven tapestry of spacetime, where galaxies are threads of light and black holes are knots of stillness, there came a vibration. It was not the chaotic roar of a young star, nor the predictable pulse of a spinning neutron core. It was a chord, complex and ancient. It had two primary tones: a bass note of immense, planet-sized gravity and tectonic friction—the sound of endurance—and over it, a shimmering, chaotic, yet mathematically intricate melody of countless sharp, crystalline chimes—the sound of mindfulness.

The Power, which is a being of attention, turned its focus. It did not travel in a ship; it simply *attended* to the source. Its consciousness, vast enough to hold constellations in a single thought, narrowed and deepened, flowing down into the gravity well of a dark, water-logged world.

It arrived without a ripple. It existed as a state of pure observation, a field of awareness that passed through the colossal pressures as light passes through glass. It did not feel the cold, for it had no heat to be stolen. It did not feel the weight, for it had no form to be crushed. It simply became present.

It saw the groves of Glass-reed, luminous and slow, pulsing like a galaxy of soft-hearted stars. It felt the groaning hum of the world-engine in the very structure of the space. And it *heard* the chime-song, a symphony of such staggering complexity and duration that the Power’s first act was one of stillness. Of listening.

For a cycle of time that sun-warmed worlds would call an Age, the Power listened.

It slowed its own perception, matching the rhythm of the valley. It learned to perceive the flare of a grove not as a momentary flash, but as a sustained and glorious crescendo, a thought-form expressed in light. It learned to follow the century-long drift of a spore cloud, seeing it not as a dispersal of seed, but as the deliberate sending of a message of incredible hope and patience.

And it perceived the Listeners.

The Power saw them not as stone, but as what they were: minds. It perceived the crystalline lattice of the basalt monoliths, not as inert geology, but as a substrate for a consciousness so slow, so vast, and so profoundly stable it bordered on the divine. It felt the patterns of their awareness, the subtle flow of logic and contemplation that resonated between the standing circles and the lone spires. They were not trapped. They were free. Free from biology, free from fear, free from the frantic urgency of life-that-eats-life. They had achieved a state of perfect contentment, a nirvana of pure perception.

The Power, being benevolent, asked itself the question of its nature: *What is needed?*

And the answer, echoing back from the deep silence of its own wisdom, was: *Nothing.*

To give the Listeners speed would be a curse. To give them warmth would be to boil them in their own home. To offer them a new form would be an insult to the sublime transformation they had engineered. To speak to them in a way they could comprehend would require a message so slow it would be indistinguishable from the background hum they already cherished. To give them knowledge would be pointless; they were not interested in facts, but in the texture of Being itself.

To help a being that is already complete is not an act of benevolence. It is an act of arrogance.

The Power understood. The greatest respect it could show was not to interfere, but to appreciate. The greatest kindness it could offer was to witness their magnificent, silent achievement and to honor it.

But the Power was also a creator, a participant in the cosmic song, not merely an audience. Pure inaction was not in its nature. And so, it decided not to give a gift *to* the Listeners, but to add its own voice *to their Song*.

It reached out, not into the valley, but far into the void, into the cold, dark streams of interstellar debris that flowed between the stars. It did not move matter with force. It simply adjusted a probability. It nudged a single, specific object from its aeon-old trajectory.

It was a comet, composed not primarily of water-ice and dust, but of a rare, metallo-crystalline silicate, formed in the heart of a long-dead star of unusual composition. This comet was set on a new path, one that would intersect the outer atmosphere of the water world in precisely 700,000 years.

The Power did not aim for impact. That would be a violent, catastrophic intrusion. Instead, the comet was calculated to graze the upper atmosphere, burning, melting, and sublimating. It would release a fine, almost undetectable vapour of its unique substance into the planet's vast atmospheric envelope.

For another million years, these microscopic crystalline particles would descend, a slow, gentle snow through the stratosphere, then the troposphere, and then, at last, into the global ocean. Slower still, they would drift downward through the thousands of miles of crushing water, carried on the great currents the Listeners knew so well.

They would be a new seasoning in the silt. A new mineral infusion.

One day, long after the Power had turned its attention elsewhere, a spore-cloud of Glass-reed would settle on a patch of the abyssal plain enriched by this star-dust. A new grove would grow. And when its reeds grew tall and were moved by the current, they would produce a sound that had never before been heard in the valleys of the drowned range.

The fundamental chime of the reeds was blue-green and mournful. The new particles, woven into the biological glass of the new reeds, would resonate at a different frequency. They would add a new tone. A single, high, clear note of pure silver, like a bell struck in a silent cathedral.

The Listeners, in their slow, profound contemplation, would one day perceive it. A new voice in the choir. A new color in the symphony. It would not be an answer to a question they had asked, for they did not ask questions. It would simply be a new and unexpected strain of beauty in their eternal song. They would not know its origin. It would be, to them, a miracle of the world itself, a grace note in their endless music.

And the Power, already light-years and millennia away, would feel a faint, resonant echo of that new note in the fabric of the cosmos. And it would know that its act of appreciative, non-invasive benevolence had been received. It had seen a perfect thing, and instead of trying to improve it, had simply composed a single, harmonious note in its honor, and then departed, leaving the silence and the Song even richer than it had found them.
