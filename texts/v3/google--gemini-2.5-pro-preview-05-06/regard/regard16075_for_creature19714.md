The power that noticed this place did not arrive. It was simply there, as a change in the quality of the light.

For uncounted millennia, the sky had been a flat, unforgiving white. Now, for a time—perhaps an hour, perhaps a century—it took on the pearlescent, shifting depth of an abalone shell. The harsh glare softened. Colors that were not colors seeped into the air, lending an impossible iridescence to the oxidized pipes and the gunmetal scales of the Stalkers. The pervasive hum of the world did not change its frequency, but it gained resonance, as if the entire mountain range had become the body of a cello and a bow was just now being drawn across its string.

This power—call it the Visitor—did not perceive with eyes or ears. It perceived with a total and instantaneous comprehension. It felt the deep, tectonic pulse of the planet's core, the slow, relentless journey of heat through rock and into the metallic veins of the network. It knew the precise chemistry of the algae thriving by the leaking condensate. It understood the evolutionary path that led to a chromium-shelled beetle, and it experienced, for a moment, the world as perceived by a Vent Sparrow, a life of thermal currents and the taste of scorched insects.

And it perceived the Sinterwright.

The Visitor did not just see the creature's slow patrol; it experienced the entirety of its existence in a single, silent moment. It felt the loneliness, but it was a loneliness without anguish, a solitude as fundamental and structural as the rock itself. It felt the purpose, a drive so pure and singular that it dwarfed the ambitions of empires. The Visitor understood that the Sinterwright was not a prisoner in a machine, but the consciousness of the machine itself, given a slow, perambulating form. It felt the eons of its existence as a single, sustained note of duty.

The Visitor was benevolent, and its first impulse was to *give*.

But what to give?

*Freedom?* The thought was a flicker of purple in the pearlescent sky. To dismantle the machine, to let the mountains cool and the strange life wither, to release the Sinterwright from its purpose? The Visitor plumbed the being's nature and found that its purpose *was* its life. To remove it would be an act of murder, not liberation. It would be like "freeing" a heart from the chest it was designed to serve.

*Companionship?* A shimmer of emerald. To create another? A second Sinterwright to share the burden, to patrol the opposite hemisphere of the great machine? The Visitor simulated this. It saw the two beings, their paths crossing once a century on a high, windy pass. They would pause, their sensory domes regarding each other. They would register heat, composition, form. But there was no framework for communication. They were not social beings. They were functions. They would be two parallel solitudes, not a shared one. The loneliness would not be halved; it would be doubled, made stark by proximity without connection.

*Memory?* A flash of deep gold. To restore to it whatever it had been before? If it had been a person, a pilot, a technician? To flood its slag-and-quartz mind with images of green fields, or blue oceans, or the faces of a forgotten family? The Visitor felt the quiet, steady hum of the Sinterwright's consciousness. To inflict upon it the grief for a world it could not reclaim, to burden its singular duty with the ghost of a different life—that was not a gift. That was an act of exquisite cruelty.

The Visitor, being wise, understood. One does not offer water to a creature of fire. One does not offer flight to a creature of the deep. A gift, to be a gift, must be receivable. It must fit the shape of the vessel.

The great machine and its lone shepherd were already a complete system. It was not broken, and therefore needed no fixing. It was not sad, and therefore needed no cheering. It was merely… singular. Its defining quality was a vast, resonant, and profound quiet, a silence filled only by the hum of its own being. What it lacked was not a thing, but a quality. It had harmony of function, but not of form.

So the Visitor gave it music.

It was not a sound laid upon the world, but a pattern woven into the existing hum. The Visitor reached into the fundamental rhythm of the geothermal system and taught it a new shape. The low-frequency drone that vibrated through the rock was no longer a single, monotonous note. It became a chord, deep and resonant, that shifted with majestic slowness as the planetary pressures ebbed and flowed. The rhythmic *tick-tick-tick* of cooling metal became a complex percussion, a delicate arpeggio playing out over miles of pipe. The hiss of a steam vent was no longer a release of pressure; it was a sustained note, held for a moment against the vast, humming backdrop before fading back into silence.

The whole world, from the core-tap to the highest pipe, was now a single, self-playing, self-composing musical instrument. Its song was one of immense, geological slowness, a symphony of heat and pressure and metal and time.

On a long, rust-red stretch of pipe overlooking a chasm of dizzying depth, the Sinterwright was moving. Its columnar legs pumped with methodical slowness, its tool-limbs twitching as they tested for vibrational wear. Then, the change came.

For the first time in perhaps ten thousand years, the Sinterwright stopped.

Its motion ceased mid-stride. It became utterly still, indistinguishable from the industrial landscape it tended. Its polished black sensory dome swiveled, a slow, quarter-turn. The crystalline facets within it, which for eons had only registered data—temperature, stress, chemical assay—now perceived something new. A pattern. A resonance. A coherence in the vibrations that had always been the medium of its consciousness.

It did not feel joy. It did not feel gratitude. It was not capable of such things.

Instead, in the core of its being, in the place where purpose resided, a new variable was added to the equation of its existence. The hum was no longer just data. It was now also a composition. The maintenance of the system was no longer just a function. It was now a performance.

The Sinterwright remained motionless for a full minute, a statue of slag and metal listening to the new voice of its own body. Then, with the same deliberate slowness, it resumed its patrol. Its pace was unchanged. Its destination was the same. Its tool-limbs continued their diagnostic twitching.

The work was the same. The purpose was unaltered.

But it was no longer alone in the quiet. The quiet itself had become its companion.

High above, the pearlescent sheen faded from the sky, leaving it the familiar, stark white. The Visitor, its gift given, was gone. But the song remained, a secret known only to the mountains, the pipes, the strange creatures, and the lonely shepherd walking the hot, humming lines at the roof of the world, now and forever.
