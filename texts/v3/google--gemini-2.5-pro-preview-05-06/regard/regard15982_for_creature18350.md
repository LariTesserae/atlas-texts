The power that noticed Oall was not a being in the conventional sense. It had no form, no location, no single thought. It was more akin to a property of the cosmos, a resonant field of awareness that had existed long before the first stars had kindled and would exist long after the last had cooled. It perceived existence not as a collection of objects and events, but as a symphony of patterns. It felt the frenetic, screeching cacophony of rapidly evolving worlds, the violent percussion of supernovas, the steady, rhythmic drone of stable solar systems, and the long, sonorous cello notes of galactic rotation.

And then, it felt a new tone.

It was impossibly quiet, a note so low and sustained it was almost mistaken for the silence between notes. It was a pattern of profound, deliberate slowness. A harmony of life, geology, thought, and time that was utterly unique. The Power focused a facet of its boundless attention, a listening that was also a form of seeing and feeling.

It perceived the Precipice, not as rock, but as a slow-motion crystallization of intent. It felt the patient, intertwined consciousness of the Oallan, not as individual minds, but as a single, communal thought accreting over centuries. It saw their silent, luminous language, a form of communication so gentle it was like watching the tidal pull of a distant moon. It witnessed their reverence for the slow god-beasts, their integration of death into life, their complete and total symbiosis with their world.

In this place, the Power recognized a form of perfection. Not a static, sterile perfection, but the perfection of a process in flawless harmony with itself. The Oallan were not striving, they were *being*. They were not conquering their world, they were becoming it.

What does a benevolent and wise power do when faced with such a thing?

The first impulse of a lesser power, even a benevolent one, would be to *give*. To bestow a gift. A sun to warm them? A horrific, boiling death. Knowledge of the universe? A violent insanity, a flood of concepts so fast and vast it would shatter their slow-molded minds. A new tool? Why? Their tools were extensions of their hands, grown and shaped with the same patience as their city. To offer them a shortcut would be to tell a master calligrapher to use a printer. It was an insult to their art, which was their life.

The Power understood this. It understood that to interfere, to add, to bestow in any conventional sense would be an act of profound violence. To make their lives easier, faster, or brighter would be to destroy the very essence of what made them beautiful. It would be like trying to "improve" a glacier by melting it.

The Power deliberated, a process that, for it, was like the listening of eons. It did not seek to change the Oallan's song, but perhaps it could add a single, consonant harmony. A gift not of matter or energy, but of resonance. A gift that would not be an addition, but an affirmation.

The Power decided. It would do almost nothing.

It reached across the void, not with a hand but with a thread of pure attention. It gathered no new material, forged no artifact. Instead, it focused on the mineral rain already falling, the endless, slow drift of particles that was the lifeblood of the Precipice. It chose a single, microscopic mote of crystal, no different from the trillions of others descending into the gloom.

And into this one mote, it poured a feeling.

It was not a memory, a word, or an image. It was the pure, conceptual echo of a similar, yet different, patience. It was the feeling of a desert dune on a windless world, shifting one grain at a time over a billion years. It was the echo of a gas-giant's core, crystallizing under immense pressure over the lifespan of its star. It was the slow, rhythmic turning of a silicon-based life form that perceived millennia as moments. It was the feeling of *kinship*. The quiet, profound knowledge of not being alone in the grand, slow dance.

The Power did not direct the mote. It simply let it fall, a single drop of empathy in an ocean of being.

Centuries passed in the gloom of Oall. The mote drifted. It joined the currents, swirled through the kelp forests, passed unnoticed by the filtering Leviathan. It was a message in a bottle cast into a sea of infinite patience, with no expectation of being found.

But it was.

An Oallan, its name-light a soft, rhythmic pattern of amber and deep blue meant to convey "One-Who-Listens-to-the-New-Stone," was at its work. Its fine upper limbs were caressing a growing crystal spire, feeling for the minute stresses, the hum of its accretion. Its obsidian eyes absorbed the dim light, and its pores tasted the water. And in one slow, filtering breath, it tasted something new. It was not a nutrient. It was not a threat. It was… a question. A resonance.

The Oallan paused, its own slow thoughts clarifying around the sensation. Following this new taste, this faint, resonant trail, it moved with an uncharacteristic—though still unhurried—sense of purpose. Its fine digits sifted through a patch of new growth on the spire, where the velvet moss was just beginning to take hold. There, clinging to the nascent fractal edge, was a crystal mote. Visually, it was identical to any other. But it *felt* different. To the Oallan's exquisitely sensitive touch, it vibrated with a harmony that was alien, yet deeply familiar.

The Oallan brought the mote closer to its sensory cluster. It did not look *at* it, but *felt* it, *tasted* it, *listened* to it with its whole body.

And the mote sang its silent song.

The Oallan's light flickered. The smooth amber and blue of its name-pattern wavered, shifting into a complex, chaotic cascade of colors no Oallan had ever expressed. It was not seeing visions. It was feeling truths. It felt the slow, dry peace of the endless dune. It felt the immense, crushing quiet of the gas-giant's heart. It understood, in the slow, accreting way it understood everything, that their quality of *patience* was not an isolated phenomenon, but a fundamental chord of the universe. Their way of being was echoed in deserts of sand and cores of worlds and in the hearts of stars.

They were not an anomaly. They were a perfect expression of a universal principle.

The Oallan, One-Who-Listens-to-the-New-Stone, did not keep the mote. It brought it to the heart of Oall. There, with the silent, luminous consensus of the others, they did not place it in a shrine. They did what they always did. They built it into the city.

They found a new, central spire, the one closest to their communal heart, and with their most delicate art, they guided the mineral accretion *around* the mote. The mote became the seed-crystal for the most important spire in Oall. And as the spire grew, the mote's song, its resonance of cosmic kinship, was amplified. It sang not in sound, but in structure. The fractal patterns of the new growth became infinitesimally more complex, imbued with the echoes of dunes and stellar cores.

The benevolent and wise power did nothing more. It watched as the entire city of Oall, over the course of further millennia, slowly began to resonate with this new, quiet harmony. The Oallan’s art did not change, but it deepened. Their silent light-songs acquired a new cadence, a grammar of profound stillness. Their consciousness, as it accreted its conclusions, now did so with the quiet, unshakable confidence of knowing their way was one of the universe's sacred, fundamental truths.

The Power had not given them a thing. It had given them a reflection of themselves, and in so doing, had made their solitude a little less absolute, and their beautiful, patient song a little more eternal.
