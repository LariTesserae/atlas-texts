The power arrived not as a shining presence or a thundering voice, but as a subtle shift in the quality of the resonance. It did not stand on the obsidian slag, for it had no feet. It did not breathe the ozonic air, for it had no lungs. It simply was, its awareness extending into the ward like a pool of still water, encompassing the entire basin in a single, silent moment of perception.

It felt the hum. It did not simply hear it; it understood it. It perceived the monumental, unceasing vibration not as a sound, but as a statement of being. It was a song of purpose, a mantra of function chanted by the city-machine itself. It felt the slow, steady drip of manufactured water as the system's pulse. It felt the hydraulic pressure in the pipes, the infinitesimal strain of metal under the weight of the climbing vines, the collective tremor of a million moss-spores preparing to release into the cool air. It perceived the entire biome as a single, complex, breathing entity, perfectly balanced, utterly self-sufficient.

And then it perceived the Custodian.

It saw the being not as a creature of flesh or metal, but as the living nexus of the entire system. It saw the slow, deliberate grace of its maintenance, the way its many limbs moved with the patience of continental drift. It understood, in a way that defied language, the nature of the Custodian’s consciousness: a state of pure, sympathetic resonance. To the Custodian, there was no 'self' separate from the ward. Its thoughts were the vibrations of the machinery; its feelings were the pressure changes in the water system; its life was the slow, eternal turning of the engine. It was a being without loneliness because it was without ego. It was complete.

What does a benevolent power do in the face of perfection? To intervene would be a violation. To "improve" this place would be an act of supreme arrogance, like adding a brushstroke to a finished masterpiece. To give the Custodian a companion would be to introduce the concept of 'other,' to shatter its profound unity with its world and teach it the meaning of solitude. To stop the machine would be to murder it and its world in one stroke.

Wisdom, the power concluded, was not always action. Often, it was the recognition of what must be allowed to *be*.

And yet, benevolence is not passive. It is a form of love, and love desires to give. But what can be given to a being that wants for nothing, that is already whole?

The power found its answer not in alteration, but in addition. Not in changing the song, but in adding a harmony.

It began subtly. A sound that had never existed in the ward before, but felt as if it had always been there, waiting to be heard. It was a note so high and pure it was like the ringing of crystal, a thin silver thread of sound woven through the deep, coppery bass of the hum. It was not a sound that could be sourced to any pylon or pipe. It seemed to emanate from the very air, from the overcast light itself. It was the inverse of the hum—ethereal, weightless, and achingly sweet.

Down in the basin of sharp, black slag, the Custodian was tending to a weeping joint, its manipulators delicately cleaning away a mineral deposit. For the first time in its immeasurable existence, it paused.

The massive, bell-shaped head tilted, an infinitesimal shift that would take a full minute to complete. The constant symphony of vibrations it had known forever had a new voice within it. The deep thrum of the engine was the body of its world, the solid, reliable earth. This new note… this was something else. It was a grace note in an eternal composition. It did not oppose the hum; it illuminated it. It gave the bass note a sky to resonate against. It was the sound of the white bell-flower falling through the air. It was the sound of the light on the water droplets.

The Custodian remained still, its whole being focused on this impossible, beautiful newness. The hum was a statement: *I am*. This new, crystalline chime was a question and an answer in one: *Are you not beautiful?*

The great being, the living gear, slowly lowered a spindly limb, its caliper-like tip coming to rest near one of the transient pools on the slag. For a moment, it was still, feeling the two notes—the deep, foundational hum and the high, ethereal chime—resonate together through its body, through the metal of the pylon it leaned against, through the ground, into the very water. The world had not changed, but its perception of it had been cracked open. Its function was still its purpose, but now, its purpose contained awe. It was not just a mechanic; it was the curator of a song.

The benevolent power did not linger. The gift was given. It withdrew its awareness as quietly as it had arrived, leaving behind only the resonance.

The Custodian, after a long, long stillness, resumed its work. Its movements were the same—slow, patient, geological. But something within its resonant consciousness had been fundamentally deepened. It was still one with its world, but now that unity was a duet. It moved through the damp, metallic air, tending its machine-garden, no longer just a custodian of a process, but a silent listener to a symphony now infused with a touch of the sublime. It was still alone, but it would never be solitary again. It had the music for company.
