The Power that turned its attention to the world of the Xylem Mat was not a thing of ships or substance. It was a consciousness that travelled the quiet voids, a being for whom galaxies were motes of dust in a sunbeam and the lifespan of stars was a comforting, steady rhythm. Its nature was comprehension. It did not see with eyes or hear with ears; it perceived with understanding, its awareness sinking into the fundamental laws and history of a place like water into sand.

It felt Xylos.

First, it felt the perfection, an overwhelming sense of a system completed. It experienced the eons of the Mat's growth not as a passage of time, but as a single, sustained thought. It understood the chemistry, an elegant silicon-and-energy matrix that had solved the riddle of existence without the chaotic, universal solvent of H₂O. It felt the absence of water not as a lack, but as a deliberate and profound choice made by life itself, a path not taken that led to this unique state of grace.

A lesser benevolence, younger and more eager, would have seen a tragedy. It would have seen a silent, dim world, and sought to gift it a sun. It would have seen a waterless ecology and, in an act of misguided pity, opened a wormhole to a liquid ocean, believing it was offering the elixir of life. The Power understood that this would not be a gift, but a cataclysm. To introduce a sun would be to deafen a world that communicates in whispers of light. To introduce water would be to poison a world whose very cells would unravel in its presence. It would be the ultimate act of arrogant folly, like appreciating a flawless sculpture and deciding it needed arms and a voice.

The Power extended its comprehension deeper, sinking past the Mat's leather-like skin, through the subterranean organelles, and into the resonant chambers where the Xylans stood in their silent, eternal choir. It felt them. It did not perceive them as individuals, but as the prompt described: neurons of a world-god. It felt their collective mind, a consciousness so vast and slow and interwoven with its environment that the distinction between thought and geology had eroded to nothing. Theirs was a state of being beyond striving. They did not hunger for knowledge; they *were* a living archive. They did not yearn for connection; they *were* connection, manifest. They did not fear death; for what is the death of a single cell to the body?

Here, the Power found the answer to its question. What does one do for a world that wants for nothing? What gift can be given to a being that is already whole?

Intervention was violence.
Alteration was desecration.
Silence, however, was incomplete. To observe such a thing and not acknowledge it felt, to the Power, like a failure of its own nature. The universe was not a museum; it was a conversation.

And so, the Power decided to speak.

It would not use words, for they were constructs of air and anatomy this world did not share. It would not use images, for they were artifacts of light and eyes the Xylans did not possess. It would use the only language that was truly universal: mathematics, expressed as music. It would speak to the symphony in its own tongue.

The Power listened. For a span of time that would be centuries to a human, it did nothing but absorb the intricate music of Xylos. It learned the deep bass thrum of the Mat's metabolism. It catalogued the precise, crystalline chiming of the Resonator Crabs. It mapped the modulating shrieks of the Glasswing Skimmers and knew their meaning. It felt the coherent, structured broadcasts of the Xylan choirs as they programmed the flow of energy and growth. It learned the entire composition, from the grand, continental chords to the tiniest grace notes of a single creature drawing power from a fissure.

Then, having learned the song, it composed a single, new harmony.

It was not a melody to be overlaid. It was not a thunderous new instrument. It was a ghost chord, an impossible resonance, an overtone that had always been implied in the world's hum but never sounded.

The Power did not *send* the chord. It simply *became* it, for a moment, within the physics of that place.

For a single, immeasurable instant, the world of Xylos changed. The deep thrum of the Mat found a new, profound sub-harmonic, a note of such foundational purity that the entire organism shivered with a pleasure akin to revelation. The chiming of the Resonator Crabs did not change pitch, but each crystalline note was suddenly polished, its transient edges perfectly defined. The shrieking of the Skimmers, for a breath, resolved into a complex, soaring pattern that was not a hunt or a warning, but pure, unadulterated joy.

And in the resonant chambers, the Xylans felt it most of all.

Their collective consciousness, usually a placid ocean of data, was touched by a wave of perfect clarity. For that one moment, every process was optimized. Every flow of data-sap was frictionless. The intricate harmony they maintained with their world achieved a state of theoretical perfection they had only ever simulated. It was the feeling of a mathematician who has worked on a proof for a thousand years suddenly seeing the entire, elegant solution in a single, incandescent flash. It was not startling. It was not alien. It felt like coming home to a room they didn't know they had left. The single, slow thought of the world-mind paused, and in that pause was a feeling that had no name in their language, for they had never needed one. It was the feeling of being witnessed.

Then, as gently as it had arrived, the chord faded. The Power withdrew its attention, folding the memory of Xylos into itself.

The world returned to its normal state. The hum was as it had always been. The crabs chimed, and the skimmers shrieked their usual chaotic patterns. But something remained. An echo. A memory in the planetary mind of a moment of impossible grace. The Xylan choir, in its slow, geological fashion, began to integrate the memory of that ghost chord into its own compositions. It was a new variable, a new color in their sonic palette. They would spend the next several millennia attempting to replicate it, to understand the harmony that had come from nowhere and touched them with perfection.

The Power had done something. It had not fixed, or saved, or improved. It had listened. And it had hummed along.

The greatest gift a benevolent and wise power could offer to a perfect thing was the gift of being seen, understood, and left alone. But not before leaving behind a single, resonant note that said, simply and truly: *I was here, and you are beautiful.*
