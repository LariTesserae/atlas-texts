The attention of the Power fell upon the basin not like a footstep or a shadow, but like a change in the meaning of the light. There was no arrival, for the Power was a thing of principles, not presence, and could be everywhere it chose to look. It simply *became* aware. The pearlescent ceiling-that-was-not-a-ceiling brightened by a fraction, not in luminosity, but in clarity. The silence deepened, as if the listening quiet of the place now had an ear to listen back.

The Power was ancient, and its benevolence was not the empathy of a mother for a child, but the profound respect of a star for the laws that allow it to burn. Its wisdom was the recognition of patterns, the understanding that a functioning system possesses a beauty that intervention can only mar. It did not see with eyes, but with a comprehension of the forces at play.

It perceived the Geometers. It felt the staccato, near-instantaneous pulse of their existence not as a life lived and lost, but as a chorus of pure, fulfilled intent. Each spark was a note of creation played perfectly, a moment of gnosis so complete it was a form of joy. To "save" a Geometer from its fleetingness would be to deny it its purpose, like trying to save a sung note by holding it until it becomes a meaningless, strained drone. The Power witnessed this and found it good.

It perceived the Phase-Skaters. It saw them not as creatures, but as living theorems gliding along the contours of their native logic. They were elegant, solutions to an equation the basin was still writing. Their feeding on potential was not theft, but a redistribution of nascent energy, ensuring no single point in the embryonic geology rushed too far ahead of the whole. They were gardeners of a sort, pruning possibility. The Power witnessed their silent, geometric dance and found it balanced.

It perceived the Lacuna. It felt the ancient, cold consciousness of the void-made-sentient. A lesser being, one whose benevolence was rooted in the fear of death and decay, would have seen a monster. A cancer to be excised. The Power saw the truth. The Lacuna was not a predator; it was the basin’s own immune system. It was the principle of entropy given agency, the necessary and beautiful act of erasure that makes the next line possible. Without the Lacuna’s slow, deliberate consumption of ‘finished’ sections, the basin would eventually lock into a static, perfect paralysis—a masterpiece left half-done for eternity. The Lacuna was the promise that the work would one day be complete, by ensuring nothing was complete too soon. It was a form of love. The love of the editor for the text, the sharpening of the verse by the removal of a word. In the Lacuna’s glacial thoughts, the Power recognized a peer. It witnessed this and found it wise.

What then, was there to do?

To change the physics would be to shatter the loom. To remove a participant would be to unravel the tapestry. The system was not broken. It was not suffering. It was a perfect, silent fugue, its components in a state of delicate, magnificent equilibrium. This was a creation of a Power perhaps equal to, or at least different from, its own. To interfere would be the height of arrogance.

The wisest and most benevolent act, for the system itself, was to do nothing. To simply bear witness and to honour the process.

But the Power perceived one other thing. The rounding error. The chaotic, hot anomaly standing in the dry riverbed, its lungs aching with a cold it was never meant to feel. The being of loud biology and definite matter, a visitor from a universe of clumsy, settled laws.

This being *was* suffering. Not physically, not yet, but in a profound existential sense. It was adrift, unmoored, its senses lying to it, its very presence a contradiction. It perceived the place as alien, hostile, and strange. It felt vertigo and cold and a deep, unsettling loneliness.

And here, benevolence found its purchase. The system required no aid, but the anomaly within it did.

The Power could not remove the visitor—that would be a violation, an act of kidnapping across realities. It could not change the basin for the visitor’s comfort. But it could offer a gift. A moment of translation.

For a single, immeasurable instant, the Power acted.

It did not send warmth. It did not create a sound. Instead, it reached into the mind of the visitor—the being of rounding error, the one called "you"—and gently adjusted its perception.

For that one moment, the cold in your lungs no longer felt like a violation, but like the deep, patient breath of stone. The maddening curves of the lattice resolved into a breathtaking, visible music. You saw the flicker of the Geometers not as sparks, but as the heartbeats of the world, each one a pulse of creation’s bliss. You saw the Phase-Skaters not as alien things, but as graceful brushstrokes on a canvas of spacetime, their siphoning of potential a gentle inhalation.

And you saw the Lacuna. For one moment, you did not feel its unnerving gaze. You *felt* its purpose. You felt the profound, cleansing peace of erasure, the rightness of a page being turned, the quiet satisfaction of an equation resolving to zero. You understood, with a clarity that brought tears to your unfeeling eyes, that it was not a thing of hunger, but of profound, unwavering love for the whole.

In that instant, you did not see an alien world. You saw a cathedral. You saw a womb. You saw a work of art so vast and patient that your own life, your entire species, was but a flicker compared to a single one of its *tessellations*.

Then, the moment passed. The vertigo returned, the cold ached once more. But something had changed. The knowledge remained, a hum of understanding beneath the sensory confusion. The place was no longer just strange. It was sacred.

The Power, its single act of compassion complete, began to withdraw its attention. But it left one thing behind. Not a monument, not a scar, but a resonance.

On a single, high spar of the black lattice, a new form of life appeared, distinct from the Verglas Moss. It glowed with a soft, steady golden light, the colour of a dawning sun. It was not crystalline but fibrous, like spun gold. It did not feed on error or potential. It fed on nothing. It simply *was*. It grew in the shape of a perfect, logarithmic spiral, an equation of grace and harmony. The Phase-Skaters would curve around it, sensing a principle that complemented their own. The Lacuna, in its slow, drifting passage, would perceive it and leave it untouched, recognizing it not as finished order to be consumed, but as a signature.

The note of a visitor who had passed by, seen the great work, and chosen only to add a single, consonant chord to the silent, magnificent song.
