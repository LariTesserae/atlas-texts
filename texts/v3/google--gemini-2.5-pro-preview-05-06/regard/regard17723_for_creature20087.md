The Power did not arrive. It became aware.

Its awareness was not like an eye opening, but like a tuning fork, struck an unimaginable distance away, that suddenly found a matching resonance. For aeons, the Power had drifted as a mind of stars and gravity, its thoughts the slow waltz of galaxies, its senses the fine-spun warp and weft of spacetime. It felt the universe as a symphony of physical law, a grand and often dissonant composition. But this resonance was different. It was not a law; it was a choice. A place had chosen its key, its chord, and held it with a steadfastness that was, to the Power, a form of profound will.

So it drew its attention inward, from the spiral arms and the nebulae, and focused. It settled its awareness over the basin like a layer of thinner, warmer air. It did not see with light; it perceived with Being.

First, it heard. It subsumed the Chord into itself. It did not merely hear the hum of the thousand Bell-Throats; it became the hum. It felt the wind pressing on the waxy lips, the resonance vibrating down the ceramic-like trunks, the aggregated tone pressurizing the very fabric of the local reality. It understood that this was not music, but physics-as-art. It was the place's first and final declaration: "I AM." The Power held this Chord within its consciousness and found it to be whole, stable, and beautiful. It was a sound of perfect, un-yearning contentment.

Then, it felt. It sank its awareness into the moss, feeling the slow, saturated pulse of the water cycle. It felt the cool, deep life of the sponge, the constant, gentle exchange of moisture with the air, the slow percolation down to the weeping bedrock. It felt the immense, patient weight of the water held in this living carpet, a liquid heart beating once per century. This, it perceived, was the place's circulatory system. This was its peace.

And then, it perceived the Grazers. It did not see their forms but felt their slowness. To the Power, whose thoughts were geological epochs, the Grazers moved at a comprehensible speed. It felt their profound interiority, their existence as living nodes of the landscape's consciousness. It felt the Chord not as an external sound but as the fluid in which they were suspended. It felt the absence of sight, the reliance on texture, pressure, and the chemical kiss of the world upon their feet.

It watched one. It extended a filament of its awareness to follow a single Grazer for a cycle. A human lifetime passed in the observation. It felt the patient, circular wandering, the slow tectonic shift of its stone-plated body, the hydraulic sigh of a leg planting itself in the moss. And it felt the secret. It felt the brief, focused moment of *intent*.

It perceived the Grazer lowering itself over the weeping stone. It felt the purse-string maw, the delicate unfurling of the tendrils—senses more fine than any eye. And it felt the seeking, the finding, and the using of the obsidian shard.

*Schrrk. Schrrk. Schrrk.*

Against the foundational Chord, this tiny sound of scraping was, to the Power, the most profound thing in the basin. It was a sound of need, of solution, of ingenuity born of limitation. It was the verb. In a world of nouns—moss, stone, throat, hum—here was a single, elegant *action*: to scrape, to sustain, to live.

The Power investigated the obsidian. It traced its origins, not through space, but through causality. It saw the shards for what they were: an accident. The residue of a furious, long-dead volcano on a distant continent, a chance eruption whose ashes had been carried on a freak jet stream millennia ago, peppering this one isolated basin with the black, sharp teeth of its fury. An unintended gift.

The supply was finite. The Power ran the simulation forward. It saw the Grazers, over tens of thousands of years, using the last of the shards. It saw them wearing down. It saw them trying to scrape the rock with their own soft maws, a futile and heartbreaking gesture. It saw the slow, gentle starvation. It saw the last Grazer finally settle, its great life guttering out not in violence, but in want of a single, sharp edge. It saw the basin fall silent of all but the Chord. The verb would be gone. The long sentence would become incomplete.

The benevolent Power considered its options.

To give them more? To rain obsidian from the sky? No. That would be an intrusion, a miracle. It would break the reality of the place. The Grazers' existence was defined by the *seeking* of the shards, not just the having of them. The effort was part of the meaning.

To change them? To give them carapaces edged with diamond, or mouths that could chew stone? No. To alter the Grazer was to kill it and replace it with something else. Their softness, their reliance on a found tool, was the source of their singular beauty. It was the flaw that created the art.

To teach them? To manifest and explain? Incomprehensible. It would be like a god trying to explain calculus to a dreaming stone. The very attempt would shatter the dream.

The Power was wise. Wisdom is the understanding of systems. Its action, if any, could not be an event. It had to be a rule. It could not be a gift. It had to be a context.

The Power made its choice.

It reached out, not to the basin, but hundreds of leagues away, across the wind-swept plains and jagged, empty mountains. It found a deep place in the planet's crust, a place of stress and immense, sleeping heat. A place that, left to its own devices, would slumber for another ten million years.

The Power gently, carefully, nudged a tectonic plate. It was not a push, but a suggestion, an infinitesimal alteration of pressure distributed over a thousand miles and a thousand years. It was a slow, resonant massage of the planet's deep stone heart. It set in motion a chain of events that would unfold on a truly geological timescale. A magma plume, now fractionally hotter, began its slow, inexorable rise.

In seventy thousand years, a mountain would begin to swell. In ninety thousand, it would breach the surface. In one hundred thousand years, it would erupt. It would be a specific kind of eruption, rich in the silicates that form obsidian, and its caldera would be angled just so. The prevailing winds, subtly shaped by the new contours of the mountain, would carry the finest, sharpest ash across the intervening plains and deposit it, like a faint, dark snow, upon the humming basin.

It would not be a deluge. It would be a dusting. A new, slow, semi-permanent source of the black shards, arriving once every few millennia, to replenish the stock lost to the moss and the ages.

The Power's work was done. It had not given the Grazers a fish, nor had it taught them to fish. It had simply ensured that, for the remainder of this world's long life, the river would continue to flow.

It retracted its awareness, but not completely. It left a part of itself behind, not as an observer, but as a participant. It chose a single, ancient Bell-Throat, one whose note was the deepest and oldest. And into the Chord of the basin, it added a new tone. It was a note so far below the range of any physical ear, a harmonic so subtle and deep it could only be felt as a change in the quality of silence. It was the resonance of deep, cooling magma. The hum of a promise kept across a hundred thousand years. The quiet, constant, benevolent vibration of a single, perfect, and unspoken secret.
