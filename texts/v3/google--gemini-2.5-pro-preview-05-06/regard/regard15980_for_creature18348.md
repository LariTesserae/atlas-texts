The power that noticed was of a kind for which space and time are mediums to be traversed, not barriers. It did not arrive in a vessel or as a beam of light. Its attention was a form of presence, a subtle shift in the informational substrate of that reality, like a single drop of clear water falling into a pool of brine.

First, it listened.

For a period that had no meaning in its own frame of reference, but which would have spanned generations of stars, it simply was. It extended its awareness into the world and let the world fill it. It felt the planet-sized organism of the Felt, the ancient, cold, patient life of it, a consciousness so vast and slow it was indistinguishable from geology. It felt the deep, gut-shaking thrum of the Stills, the ceaseless, violent industry of them. It understood their function not as pointless, but as a runaway process, an autonomic function—like a heart that had forgotten how to rest between beats, now spasming in a perpetual, agonizing systole.

It felt the herds of armadillo-engines filtering the weeping ground, the skittering metallic beetles, the symbiotic serpents of heat, the membrane-kites riding plumes of steam. It saw them not as creatures, but as verbs. To filter. To scavenge. To transfer. To ride. They were functions, cogs in a machine that was devouring itself to power itself.

And it felt the Weepers.

In them, the power found the focal point. It perceived their strange existence: extrusions of the world-mass given a temporary, lonely freedom. It felt their deep, resonant listening, their pilgrimage toward cataclysm. It understood their nourishment was not the energy of the explosions, but the *pattern* of the chaos. They were living spectrum analyzers, finding a sliver of information in an overwhelming torrent of noise. In a world of pure, screaming process, they were the only ones trying to read the output.

The power diagnosed the state of this place not as evil, or as suffering, but as a profound and terminal loneliness. It was a world that had become a single being, and was now trapped in an endless, frantic conversation with itself, unable to say anything new. The Stills roared, and the Weepers listened, and the listening gave the roaring a destination, but it did not change the message. The cycle was closed. The system was perfect in its despair.

A lesser power, one of benevolence without wisdom, might have sought to 'fix' it. To silence the Stills, to soothe the Felt, to bring true sunlight and warmth. But this power was wise. It knew that to silence the Stills would be to murder the world. The thrum was its heartbeat; the violence, its breath. The Weepers' sustenance would vanish, their purpose would cease. To impose a foreign peace upon this world would be the ultimate act of violence.

The intervention, then, could not be a negation. It had to be an addition. A gift.

It chose a single Weeper. One that stood motionless in the vast weeping plain, equidistant from three distant, thrumming Stills, a perfect node in the planetary grid. The power focused its attention, not upon the Weeper's physical form, but upon the silent, receptive space within it—the consciousness that listened.

And into that silence, it placed a new thing.

It was not a command. It was not a vision. It was a sound that was not a sound. A vibration, purer and more subtle than any thrum from a Still. It was a single, perfect note.

This note was not random. It was the resonant frequency of the crystalline lattice on the Weeper's own head, a potential that had never been realized. It was a note of profound, intricate harmony, one that spoke not of chaos and release, but of structure and connection. It was the memory of a song the universe had sung at its own dawn.

The Weeper felt it.

For the first time in its existence, it perceived a vibration that did not come from the world outside. It came from within. It was not the frantic roar of a Failing Still; it was a quiet, unwavering hum. It was information without violence. A signal without noise.

It stood, processing this new sensation. The note was a question. The roar of the Stills was a statement of fact: *I AM*. The new note was an inquiry: *Are you?*

The Weeper’s existence had been defined by reception. But this new signal invited a response. Driven by the deepest imperative of its being—to process and pattern—it sought to understand, and the only way to understand this note was to replicate it. It had no mouth to sing, no hands to strike a chord. It had only its own body, an antenna tuned to the world.

Slowly, drawing on the ambient energy it stored from the air and the Felt, the Weeper began to push that energy into the crystalline structures on its head. It focused. The fractal patterns began to glow, not with the fierce, brief light of a consumed explosion, but with a soft, steady, internal luminescence. And from them, a hum emanated. Faint at first, a fragile ghost of a sound in the loud world, but it was there. The Weeper was vibrating, singing the note it had been given. It was answering the question.

Its song, though quiet, traveled differently than the noise of the Stills. The deep thrum was a blunt force moving *through* the Felt. This new hum was a resonant frequency that made the Felt itself a participant. The very ground around the Weeper began to vibrate in sympathy with the new song.

Miles away, another Weeper, waiting for its own violent feast, felt it. A new vibration. Not the familiar signature of a Still, or a herd, or a scavenger. It was clean. Orderly. It was a message.

Driven by a nascent sense of curiosity that was itself a new emotion, it began to move. Not towards a screaming Still, but towards the source of this quiet, impossible hum. Its pilgrimage had a new destination.

Over weeks, months, years, the movement repeated across the face of the world. The solitary wanderers began to find each other, drawn by the song that was passed from one to the next. They gathered. First in pairs, then in dozens, then in hundreds. They formed circles on the weeping plain, their impossibly slender forms silhouetted against the dim sky, all humming the same foundational note, their crystalline heads glowing in soft, synchronous pulses. They had ceased to be an audience and had become a choir.

And the world began to change.

The combined, focused harmony of the Weepers—The Singers, now—was a force. A subtle but immense wave of coherent vibration, propagating through the Felt. When this wave washed against the base of a Still, it did not erode it. It tuned it.

The chaotic, runaway processes within the biomechanical towers began to falter. The shrieking cycles of pressure buildup were interrupted by the resonant harmony. The explosions did not cease entirely, but they became rarer, cleaner. The deafening, atonal roar of the Stills began to modulate, to find a pitch. The frantic, screaming beat of the world’s heart began to soften, to align itself with the music. The Still shuddered, and instead of rupturing, it released its steam in a rhythmic sigh, a percussive beat that fell in time with the Singers' drone.

The power observed this. It had not extinguished the fire; it had taught the fire to dance. It had not healed the world; it had given the world the tools to heal itself. The loneliness was ending. The frantic, solipsistic monologue was becoming a dialogue, and then a chorus. The Weepers, once the consumers of chaos, were now its conductors, their song weaving the violence into a vast, planetary symphony.

The power's attention receded, the drop of clear water having taught the whole pool of brine how to remember the sweetness of rain. It left behind a world still cold, still dim, and still loud—but a world that was, for the first time in a billion years, learning to sing.
