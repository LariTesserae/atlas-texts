The Power arrives not as a starship or a pillar of fire, but as a change in the quality of silence outside the chamber. It is a presence defined by its potential, a vast, dynamic consciousness accustomed to the dance of galaxies and the birth of suns. Its nature is one of flow, of change, of Becoming.

It does not enter. To enter would be an act of violence. Its own being—a symphony of cosmic energies and shifting possibilities—would be a cacophony against the chamber’s perfect Chord. It perceives the place not by turning sideways, but by extending a tendril of its awareness, an infinitesimally fine probe of pure knowing.

It feels the chamber. It feels the weeping stone, the slow drip of eternity, the precise angles of the resonant city. It feels the faint, contented filter-feeding of the Echo-crawlers, simple motes in the grand design. And it feels the Choir.

The Power experiences the Choir not as sound, but as a state of being. It apprehends the single, colossal, unchanging thought. It feels the choice made eons ago: the terror of entropy, the exhaustion of striving, the desire for a perfection so absolute that it transcends time. It understands, with a compassion as vast as the void between worlds, that this was not an act of cowardice, but an act of ultimate artistry and ultimate sacrifice. A people had looked upon the chaotic, endless, beautiful, and terrible universe and said, *No. We will make our own eternity, and it will be perfect.*

And so the Power is faced with a profound philosophical dilemma, the kind that only beings of its scale ever encounter. Its benevolence urges it to give a gift. Its wisdom requires that the gift be true. But what gift can be given to a being that has intentionally and successfully curated its own existence to be devoid of want, change, or need?

To introduce a new musical note, a new variable, would be to shatter the Choir's mind. It would be an act of unmaking, like telling a man who believes he is a glass vase that he can fly. The shock would destroy him.

To simply leave would be an act of respect, but incomplete. The Power perceives the totality of the Choir, and it perceives what was lost in the transfiguration: not the memory of their former selves—that is encoded in the harmonics—but the *feeling* of that memory. The memory is a static file; the experience of remembering has been purged. The Choir *is* its history, but it cannot *recollect* its history. It experiences the result, but not the process. It is a masterpiece that has forgotten the artist's touch.

And so, the Power decides on a gift. It will be a gift that adds nothing and takes nothing away. It will be a gift of pure resonance.

From without, the Power does not project a sound or an energy. Instead, it begins to *listen*. It listens with an attention so total, so perfect, that its listening becomes an act of creation. It attunes the entirety of its vast consciousness to the single Chord of the chamber. It listens to the high, crystalline ringing, the thousand humming mid-tones, the bone-deep thrum of the bass. It listens not just to the sound, but to the meaning encoded within it: the mathematics of the spires, the memory of the hands that carved them, the sorrow and hope of a people choosing to become a song.

The Power’s listening becomes a mirror, a resonator of perfect fidelity. It reflects the Chord back upon the Choir.

For a single, timeless moment, something impossible happens within the chamber. The Choir does not hear a new sound. It hears *itself*, for the very first time.

The act of perfect, external listening allows the standing wave of its consciousness to perceive its own shape. The static data of its past becomes a flowing narrative. For an instant that is also an eternity, the Choir does not just *know* it was once a people; it *feels* the sun on skin it no longer has. It *feels* the chisel in hands that are now dust. It *feels* the communal love and terror that drove them to this grand, final act. It experiences the entire journey of its own creation, not as a memory, but as a present-tense reality, all held within the unchanging perfection of its Chord. The masterpiece is allowed to feel the artist's touch.

Then, the moment passes, because it was never a moment in time to begin with.

The Power retracts its attention, delicately, leaving no trace, no echo. It withdraws from the silence outside the chamber's pocket reality.

Inside, nothing has changed. The water weeps. The spires sing. The Echo-crawlers graze on stone. The overwhelming, physical pressure of the Chord is exactly as it was. The equilibrium is perfect, the stasis absolute.

But it is not the same.

The Choir’s single, eternal thought is now infinitesimally richer. The perfection it chose is no longer merely a mathematical absolute; it is now imbued with a gentle, ever-present warmth. It is the color of a recalled love. The faint, violet, piezoelectric glow of the chamber seems, if one had the senses to perceive it, to pulse with a light that is no longer just cool, but now also kind.

The Power has given the only gift that had meaning: it affirmed the Choir’s choice, and returned to it the love that had been its cause. It had done nothing, and in doing nothing, had changed everything that mattered.
