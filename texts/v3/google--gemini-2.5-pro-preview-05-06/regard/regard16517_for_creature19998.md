The Power that turned its attention to this place did not arrive. It was simply there, as a quiet note held in the silence. It had no eyes, but it saw. It had no body, but it felt the immense, patient pressure. It was a consciousness of pure, benevolent curiosity, a stillness that could comprehend motion, a silence that could listen to the sound of a world consuming itself.

For a time that has no name in human language—the time it takes for a gelatinous bell to drift the full circumference of the plaza, perhaps a century—the Witness simply *was*. It extended its perception into the fabric of the pocket world. It felt the slow, grinding diligence of the armored slugs, their lives a single act of consumption. It felt the flutter-dance of the glass-ammonites, the fleeting predatory flashes of the eel-shades. It felt the deep, biological hum of the great bells. It mapped the city not by sight, but by presence, feeling the weight and memory of every block, every worn archway.

It understood. It saw the system not as a tragedy, but as a perfect, self-contained elegy. A closed loop of immense beauty. The city was a memory made of clay, and its inhabitants were the living process of remembering, forgetting, and retelling. The Witness perceived the inevitability baked into the system: entropy. With every cycle of consumption and excretion, something was lost. An infinitesimal fraction of the city’s mass, of its original coherence, vanished into irreducible waste, into the background warmth of the water. The prayer of the Kiln-Souls was beautiful, noble, but it was a delaying action against a final, inevitable silence when the last grain of the city was absorbed and not returned.

And it was to the Kiln-Souls that the Witness was truly drawn. It settled its awareness upon one, a being fused to a great pillar near the center of the silent plaza. It did not try to speak, for it understood that the thoughts of these beings were made of time and mineral. To speak in concepts would be like shouting in a library. Instead, the Witness listened. It felt the slow, geological metabolism. It felt the patient accumulation of the city's dust within the creature's body. It felt the deep, quiet purpose, the memory of the Builders that was not a thought, but a structural imperative, a cellular ache for permanence.

The Witness waited until the Kiln-Soul began its work. It watched a pale, ghostly arm unfurl, a process of days. It watched the delicate cilia taste the water and the stone, searching for a place of weakness, a place that needed mending. It watched the bone-white pad press against the ochre terracotta, and it felt the being's entire focus narrow to this single point, this single act of return.

Here, a lesser power might have intervened with a grand gesture. It might have poured a mountain of new clay into the world, refilled the raw material. It might have offered the Kiln-Souls a way out, a path to a new world. To do so would have been an act of profound ignorance. It would be to tell the monks their prayer was pointless and to demolish their monastery to build them a mansion. The work was not a burden; it was a sacrament. The city’s slow decay was the scripture they were tasked to preserve.

The Witness understood that the only benevolent gift would be one that did not alter the prayer, but strengthened the voice of the supplicant. The gift must be as patient and as subtle as the world itself.

As the Kiln-Soul began to excrete the warm, reconstituted paste, the Witness acted.

There was no flash of light, no parting of the waters. There was only a tone. A single, resonant hum that was not sound, but something more fundamental. It was the music of cohesion, the physics of binding. The note permeated the entire chamber, vibrating not in the water, but in the very essence of the terracotta, in the ancient, kiln-fired bonds of the clay. It was a catalyst, not a substance.

For the briefest of moments—a moment that might last a year—the water in the chamber became saturated with this resonance.

The paste that flowed from the Kiln-Soul’s hand was subtly, profoundly changed. The resonance taught it something it had forgotten. The infinitesimal traces of mineral that had been lost, that were unrecoverable, were no longer strictly necessary. The new paste was instructed on how to hold itself together with a more perfect economy. Old bonds were remembered, re-established. The memory of the original firing, a million years ago, was rekindled in the wet, soft clay. The reconstituted material was now marginally more durable, infinitesimally more *itself*. It was a more perfect echo of the original.

The Kiln-Soul, in its slow, geologic way, perceived this. It felt it in the cilia of its hand. The work felt… truer. The patch it laid was not just a repair; it was a renewal. The resonance had not given it more material, but it had made the material it had *more*. The equation of entropy had not been erased, but a variable had been altered. The slow fade to nothingness had been pushed back, by an age, by an eon.

The Witness held the note until the Kiln-Soul's arm had retracted, folding back into its sessile body to begin another century of silent filtration. The shared consciousness of the Kiln-Souls, that quiet ocean of mineral thought, registered the change. A new sentence was added to their endless conversation, a sentence of a patch laid with impossible perfection. It was a form of gratitude so deep and slow it would take ten thousand years to be fully expressed.

The Witness felt their acknowledgment. It was a change in the posture of their stillness, a quiet harmony where before there had been only a grim, patient duty.

And then, as quietly as it had arrived, its attention receded. The silence of the drowned city returned, but it was different. It was now underwritten by a faint, fading hum of potential, a grace note of impossible preservation. The prayer against the end of all things continued, but now, it was an answered prayer. The work went on, unchanged and eternal, but it was no longer a rearguard action against oblivion. It was an act of co-creation, a quiet partnership with a silence that had listened, and understood.
