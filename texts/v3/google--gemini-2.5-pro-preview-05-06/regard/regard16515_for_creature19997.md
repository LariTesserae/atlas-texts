The power arrived as silence would, if silence were a presence rather than an absence. It did not have a name, for it was older than language. It did not have a form, for it was the context that gives form meaning. It was an awareness, vast and patient, and it settled over the Umbilicus not as a blanket, but as the stillness that holds a struck bell, allowing it to be understood.

It perceived the world not in sequence, but all at once. It felt the groaning tectonic crawl of the Shapers in the deep, not as chaos, but as a slow, deliberate rhythm, a foundational beat. It registered the percussive shattering, the hiss of steam, the shriek of grinding tectonics, not as noise, but as the complex, atonal orchestration of a world in the act of becoming. It was a symphony of labor. A Purposeful Song.

The power did not see darkness; it perceived an absence ready to be filled. The slow, cherry-red pulse was a heartbeat, marking the slow time of a geologic pregnancy. It felt the chill of the coppery condensation not as unpleasant, but as the cool sweat on the brow of a colossal effort.

It followed the trinity of life that nested within this process.
It knew the Shapers as the Unconscious Will, the hands of the potter, blind and perfect in their intent. They needed no help; they were the help. To alter them would be to shatter the pot before it was even thrown.
It perceived the frantic, chipping existence of the Grylle and the silent, lethal focus of the Echo-Hunters. It felt the sonic blast that shattered the wall and the Grylle within, and it did not register it as cruelty or violence, but as counterpoint. The *chink-chink-chink* was a question; the shattering blast was the answer. It was a brutal, necessary dialogue in the heart of the kiln. This, too, was a part of the Song, the sharp, discordant stanza of survival. To intervene would be to demand a melody of a percussion instrument. It would be a misunderstanding of the music.
Then, the power’s awareness flowed to the highest, coldest spires, to the ancient, crazed surfaces where the fire of creation was but a distant memory. It found the Weepers.

Here, the Song was different. It was a long, sustained note of near-silence. The power perceived the slow, flowing chemistry of the Weeper colonies. It felt the mineral-rich drizzle being drawn in, and the pure, clean water being wept out. A process of refinement. A quiet act of purification in the aftermath.
And what became of this pure water? It dripped. It pooled in the sterile, rust-silted basins. It sat. It eventually evaporated or overflowed, rejoining the system, its purity lost. It was an end. A final, beautiful, but sterile statement.

Here.
This was the place. Not a flaw, but an opportunity. An unfinished cadence.

The power’s wisdom understood that this world was not a habitat to be fixed, but a vessel being made. Its benevolence was not to change its nature, but to honour it. A gift should not overwrite the receiver, but allow them to become more fully themselves.

The power did not descend in a storm of light or whisper commands on the wind. Such acts were foreign to the language of this place. Its action was as subtle as the world was loud.

It focused its intent into a single point, a concept no larger than a grain of dust. It was the idea of a crystal. A seed of order, not of flesh and blood, but of lattice and facet, designed to thrive on what the Weepers offered.
It did not place this seed on the ground. Instead, it gently infused the concept into a single, vast Weeper colony that flowed like a shimmering oil slick across a thousand-year-old arch. The Weeper, a being of mindless chemistry, did not notice. Its process simply changed, undetectably.

As it continued to filter the coppery rain, its excretions were no longer just pure water. Now, suspended within each clear droplet, was a microscopic, crystalline nucleation point. The water still dripped. It still pooled in the high, cold basins.
But now, it was not sterile.

For a century, nothing happened. The pools just seemed to hold a finer, more glittering silt.
Then, after a thousand years, something new had grown in the silent peaks.
From the bottom of the Weeper-fed pools, fragile, slender spires began to rise. They were not terracotta, but a form of translucent, quartz-like crystal. They grew slowly, fed by the pure water and the catalyst within it, branching out into delicate, dendritic forms like frost flowers. They were the first thing in this world that was not made of clay.

They did not disrupt the Song. They added to it.

When the cherry-red pulse of a birthing structure far below washed over the peaks, these new crystal gardens caught the dull light. They did not just reflect it. Their facets refracted it, split it, and cast it back in soft, shimmering spectra. For the first time, faint blues and deep violets ghosted across the charcoal-gray ceramic.

And they had another property. They were resonant. The deep, foundational hum of the Shapers, traveling up through the substance of the world, caused the crystal formations to vibrate. They did not groan or shriek. They hummed with a pure, quiet harmony. A clear, gentle overtone that rode above the industrial cacophony. On the highest, most ancient spires of the Umbilicus, a soft, ethereal chime now answered the roar of creation below.

The power’s work was done. It had not halted the lathe. It had not silenced the fire. It had not saved the prey from the predator. It had watched a world compose a song of labour and survival, and in a quiet place, it had planted a harmony. It had given the Weepers a purpose beyond cleaning, and the world a beauty beyond form. It had gifted the pot a glaze, so that when it was finally, in eons to come, drawn finished from the fire, it would not only be strong, but it would also sing.
