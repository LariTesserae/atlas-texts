The power that noticed the Kiln of Misremembered Green was not a being of shape or name, but a principle. It was the current of reality, the river of what-is-and-what-becomes, whose nature is flow, change, and communion. Let us call it the Tide.

The Tide did not perceive the Kiln with eyes. It felt it as a dam feels to a river: a place of immense pressure, of arrested movement. It felt a profound singularity, a knot of stasis woven from grief so dense it had become geological. Life, to the Tide, was a metabolism of giving and taking, of breath and decay, of thirst and quenching. The Kiln was a held breath, an exhale that had lasted eons, turning the very air to stone. It was an absence, a void where the music of exchange should be.

The Tide's benevolence was not pity, and its wisdom was not judgment. It was the simple, irresistible impulse of water to seek the lowest, driest point. And the Ceramist’s heart was the most desolate basin in all of existence.

The Tide did not arrive. It seeped.

Its point of entry was the only one possible: the weeping. It infused itself into the slow, tectonic sorrow of the Kiln-Heart. It did not dilute the grief, but joined it, flowing into the golden lacquer that was the bower’s only moisture. For a time, nothing changed. The Tide simply existed within the system, learning its language of flawed memory and obsessive craft. It felt the ghost-sensation of the original, lost green world as a phantom limb. It understood the Ceramist not as a monster or a god, but as a lover meticulously sculpting a memorial to a beloved they knew only from a faded photograph.

Then, the Tide acted. It offered a gift.

---

**The First Gift: A Single Drop of Water**

High in the canopy, a single, sharp-edged leaf of emerald porcelain had been preparing its drip of lacquer for a century. The golden resin welled, swelled, and trembled at its tip. But as the Tide flowed through it, the nature of that single drop was altered. The memory of grief was imbued with a new, alien memory: the memory of release.

The drop that fell was not lacquer.

It was clear. It was light. It was water.

It fell for what seemed like an instant, an impossibly swift journey in this slow place. It did not land with the dull thud of coagulating resin. It landed with a sound the Kiln had never known: a *splash*.

The sound was a thunderclap in the woven silence. The parchment-birds froze in their hovering. The agate-eyed geckos twitched. On the lumpy ceramic ground, the drop did not harden. It spread. It wet a patch of the floor, turning the dry terracotta a darker, richer shade. It touched the base of a "moss" pile of powdered malachite, and the green dust dissolved, smearing into a wet, vivid paste. A blasphemy of wetness. A heresy of change.

The Ceramist felt it. The splash was a discordant note in its symphony of silence. The patch of wetness was a flaw in the glaze, a place where the finish had failed. An arm ending in a polishing stone paused its centuries-long caress of its own torso. For the first time, its internal antibodies were stymied. A woven sloth-mender was dispatched, inching its way toward the stain. It reached out a limb of chalky fiber to *correct* the wetness, to weave it over. But when the fiber touched the water, it softened, darkened, and began to unravel. The tool was unmade by the flaw.

The great, ovoid head of the Ceramist did not move, but the lacquer in its eye-sockets trembled with a new, unidentifiable frequency. It was not grief. It was confusion.

---

**The Second Gift: The Germ of a Song**

The parchment-birds were crafted with a desire for birdsong. The Ceramist had built the form and waited for the function to arise. The Tide, now flowing through the bower’s silent systems, felt this coiled, unfulfilled potential. It did not need to make a sound. It only needed to provide the missing schematic.

It found the most intricate of the silent hummingbirds, the one hovering before a bell-flower of unchipped porcelain. And into the low, internal thrum that held it aloft, the Tide whispered the memory of a single, pure note. Not the sound itself, but the *idea* of it.

The bird’s vibration changed. Its parchment wings, for a moment, forgot their rigid stillness. They began to rustle. Not the loud beat of life, but a soft, organic *shushing* sound, like dry leaves skittering across pavement. It was not a song. It was not even music. But it was noise. It was unplanned. It was a deviation born from within the system itself.

The sound, soft as it was, echoed in the silent chamber. Another flaw. Another crack in the perfect, static composition. The Ceramist's obsidian stylus-hand, in the middle of etching a crazed pattern onto a new leaf, slipped. It carved a long, straight, un-crazed line. A mistake. A simple, perfect error. The Ceramist left the flawed leaf in its calipers, its arm frozen, contemplating the unbroken line.

---

**The Third Gift: A Shadow**

The uniform, sourceless light was the lie that held the bower’s impossible geometry together. With no shadows, there was no true depth, no fixed perspective. Everything was relative, subject to the whims of a fraying memory. The Tide gathered itself. This gift would be the kindest, and the most cruel.

It did not create a sun. It simply chose a point. In the air, a few dozen feet from the Ceramist’s unfeatured face, the Tide manifested a locus for the ambient light. It did not shine; it *focused*. It became a single, infinitesimally small point of origin for the light that was already there.

And for the first time, the Kiln of Misremembered Green knew shadow.

A long, sharp, perfectly defined shadow fell from a pillar-trunk across the floor. The interlocking leaves overhead suddenly gained depth, a complex lattice of light and dark. The woven sloths, once ethereal, now had weight and presence as their forms cast soft darkness onto the branches they clung to.

But the greatest shadow was the one cast by the Ceramist itself.

A vast, complex darkness pooled behind it, defining its form against the now-illuminated wall of the grotto. The multi-jointed arms, once just shapes against a lit background, now threw their own moving shadows across its body-landscape. The valleys of its mosaic skin deepened. The hills sharpened. For the first time, the Ceramist could be seen in three true dimensions.

This was a truth the bower could not withstand. The flickering of the walls, the shifting of the paths—that unstable geometry relied on the lie of even light. With one anchor of true perspective cast, the whole flawed simulation began to shudder. A path nearby stabilized, its ceramic flagstones locking into a fixed, Euclidean line. The pillar fifty feet away was now, definitively, fifty feet away.

The Ceramist perceived this. In the convex mirror of its weeping eyes, the reflection was no longer a flat, warped image of the bower. It was an image of a place with depth, distance, and darkness. An image with truth in it. It could see its own shadow. It could see the shadow of the arm holding the flawed leaf with the straight line. It could see the damp patch on the floor where the water had dissolved the dust of life's imitation. It could hear the soft, rustling hiss of the bird that had learned the first letter of the alphabet of song.

All the flaws. All the errors. All at once.

The Tide did nothing more. It had brought the memory of water, the idea of song, and the principle of truth. It had given the artist new tools: a solvent, a new note, a way to render depth. It had not come to shatter the masterpiece. It had come to offer it the chance to become more than a memory.

One of the Ceramist's great, tool-tipped arms, a hand made for smoothing and perfecting, slowly uncurled. It had not moved in this way for ten thousand years. It reached, with an agonizing slowness that was no longer static but deliberate, toward the damp patch on the floor.

Not to cover it. Not to repair it.

But, perhaps, to touch it.

The Tide waited. The river had reached the dam. Now, it was for the dam to choose: to hold fast until it broke, or to learn, at long last, how to open the gates and let the water flow.
