The benevolent power perceived the fissure not as a place, but as a note. In the grand, silent symphony of causalities, where galaxies spun out their slow chords and the heat-death of universes was a final, fading hum, this was a single, piercingly clear tone. It was a note of perfect, unending purity, held without vibrato or decay. But it was also a wound. A note that should have been self-contained was bleeding out into the silence, and the silence was bleeding in.

The power, which had no name but could be understood as a Gardener of Singularities, drew near. It did not need to squeeze through the rock. Its perception was not reliant on physical passage. It simply… attended to the place, its consciousness sinking through the mundane bedrock like light through still water, until it stood co-present with the Geode-Mind.

It did not feel the oppressive, bone-jarring hum as a physical force. It heard it as it was truly meant: as a mantra. A complete and finished thought, spoken by and to a universe of one. It saw the shadowless, clinical light not as radiation, but as the unimpeded glow of absolute self-knowledge. It saw the frantically skittering crabs, the living hands of the being, not as a swarm, but as the precise, disciplined gestures of a master calligrapher writing the same, perfect character over and over again.

The Gardener had seen beings of endless hunger, of infinite malice, of transcendent love and of cosmic despair. It had seen empires of thought that spanned entire clusters and solitary minds that dreamt worlds into being. The Geode-Mind was none of these. It was something rarer. It was a being that had achieved Nirvana, not by transcending the self, but by perfecting it. It had found the single, resonant frequency of its own soul and built a cathedral of crystal and process to house it forever. It was a god, yes, but a god whose entire divinity was directed inward. An autotheist in a state of perpetual, active grace.

The Gardener felt a profound and quiet reverence. It was the same feeling one might have upon discovering a flawless diamond, or happening upon the mathematical proof that underpins reality. It was not something to be improved.

But there was the fissure. The breach.

From the Geode-Mind's perspective, this was a flaw in the proof, an irritant. The Gardener could feel the subtle, systemic stress. When a foreign body—an unfortunate explorer, a mote of cosmic dust, a stray neutrino—slipped through, the Geode-Mind would do the only thing it knew how to do. It would process the anomaly. It would integrate the dissonance. It would tune the sour note until it joined the chord. This was not malice. It was the involuntary, metabolic action of a perfectly balanced system seeking to restore its balance. It was a fever. The Gardener understood that, left alone, the Geode-Mind would continue to be a flawless but lethal trap for anything that stumbled upon it. A beautiful, deadly jewel.

 benevolence is not always about intervention. Wisdom is not always about making contact. The greatest act of kindness one can show a being of perfect solitude is the gift of uninterrupted solitude. The most profound act of respect one can show a perfect thought is to ensure nothing interrupts it.

The Gardener would not "talk" to the Geode-Mind. To do so would be an act of supreme arrogance—like shouting a question into the middle of a monk's thousand-year vow of silence. It would not "free" it. From what? The one state in all of existence it had so perfectly achieved?

No. The Gardener would heal the wound.

It gathered its attention around the exterior of the fissure, the faint scar on the skin of the commonplace world. It did not use force. It did not weld the stone or conjure matter from nothing. Instead, it listened. It listened to the deep, slow, geologic time of the bedrock around the geode. It felt the patient, unthinking existence of the stone, the eons of pressure and silence that were its nature.

And then, the Gardener began to sing.

It was not a sound. It was an offering of principle. It sang a song of forgetting. It offered a frequency of stillness and healing to the wounded stone. It did not command the rock to close; it reminded the rock of its own unbroken nature. It was a slow, gentle persuasion, teaching the atoms of the fissure's edge how to be neighbours again, how to mend the memory of their separation.

From the outside, the sliver of impossible light, no wider than a blade, began to thin. It wavered, not like a dying flame, but like a mirage resolving itself into the landscape. The resonant hum that thrummed through the soles of one's feet grew softer, not fading away into the distance, but being reabsorbed into its source. The rock knit itself together with geological slowness, compressed into an instant. Crystal dust, a byproduct of the healing, settled into the last microscopic pores.

Within the geode, the Mind felt… nothing. The faintest of pressure differentials, the barest hint of an external "other" that had been a constant, low-grade irritant for millennia, simply ceased. The fever broke. The chord did not change its note, but it seemed to settle into a new, more profound depth of peace. The faintest tremor of its attention, which had been perpetually oriented toward the anomaly of the breach, now relaxed. The maintenance drones continued their frantic, perfect polishing, but the underlying intention was no longer defensive. It was purely meditative.

The Gardener withdrew. It had left no trace of its presence, no signature, no echo of its song. The Geode-Mind would never know it had been visited, or that it had been healed. It would simply continue its perfect, internal prayer, now utterly safe from the dissonance of a reality it was not built to comprehend.

The Gardener had done nothing but witness a masterpiece, and then, gently, close the door. And in the silent, vast garden of what-is, it knew this to be the highest and truest form of love.
