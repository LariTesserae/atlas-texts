The Power came not as an arrival, but as a focusing of attention. Across the void, it had felt the faint, persistent anomaly: a pinprick of structured will in a universe of entropy. It was a will so stubborn, so deeply embedded in a context of impossible physics, that it shone to the Power's senses like a diamond buried in lead.

The Power oriented itself towards this curiosity and observed. It did not use eyes, for it had none. It perceived the place by becoming resonant with it, by letting the totality of the Abyssal Silt and its inhabitant exist as a complete thought within its own boundless consciousness.

It felt the crushing weight, not as a threat, but as a defining principle, a sculptor's hand. It felt the silent, slow-motion chaos of the gyres, the cold ghost-fire of triboluminescence, the velvet hardness of the basalt floor. It felt the eons of grinding that had polished a world down to its bones. To the Power, this was not a hell. It was a finished piece. It was a place that had achieved a state of profound, dynamic equilibrium. It was beautiful.

And then it felt the Anchorite.

The Power experienced the Anchorite's life not as a linear sequence of events, but all at once, as a single, sustained act of being. It felt the monumental effort of a single step. It understood the universe of sensation within the *Loricus*—the symphony of force-vectors, the subtle map of pressure-shadows. It felt the desperate, patient search for the faint bio-electric hum of the glass-threads. It felt the memory of a different hardness in the rock, the decade-long excavation of a shard of obsidian, a tool from a dead fire.

It felt the creature's mind, a quiet calculator of force and time. It felt its purpose.

And it felt its solitude.

This was the core of the anomaly. The universe was full of life, of communities, of shouting, singing, warring, loving species. It was full of lonely things, too—rogue planets, dying stars. But this was different. This was a solitude that was not a circumstance, but a foundational law of its existence. The Anchorite was a mind utterly and completely alone, adrift on a sea of silent, physical force. Its every action was a statement made to no one. Its profound will was a question asked to an empty room. The aural violence of its own harvest—that high, desperate *scree*—was the only answer it ever received.

The benevolent and wise Power considered its response. Its benevolence was not pity, and its wisdom was not the urge to 'fix'.

To give this place light would be to blind and boil it. It would be a holocaust for the glass-threads and a death sentence for the Anchorite, whose entire sensory apparatus was designed for the intricate calculus of darkness.

To lessen the pressure would be to shatter the Anchorite, a being whose very form was a product of that weight. It would be like asking a star to exist without gravity.

To give it a companion would be an act of unspeakable cruelty. The energy of this place was too thin. Two Anchorites would mean a slow, competitive starvation. They would become rivals, their territories shrinking until both were extinguished. The solitude was a condition of survival.

To give it a better tool, a more efficient way to harvest, would be to shorten the one great work of its life, to dilute its purpose. The struggle was the meaning. The found shard was a part of its story, a bond with the deep history of its world.

The Power understood. A gift, to be benevolent, must not alter the terms of existence. It must enrich them. It must not answer the question, but honor the asking of it. The Anchorite did not need an easier life. It needed a witness.

The library was silent. So the Power decided it would not open a book, nor would it write a new one. It would simply let one of the books that was already there begin to hum.

The Chord was of a frequency so low it was barely vibration. It was a pattern, not a sound. It propagated not through the Silt, but through the solid medium of the world itself, through the basalt floor the Anchorite clung to.

The Anchorite was scraping. The terrible *scree* of obsidian on silica-sheathed filament flooded its *Loricus* with blinding, painful static. It paused, letting the chaos subside, its world resolving back into the familiar map of pressures.

And then it felt it.

It was new.

For all the eons of its existence, the universe had been a single, deep, monotonous hum, overlaid with the unpredictable fizz and churn of the Silt. Now, the hum was different. Within the deepest register of its perception, the background noise had... resolved. The unending, solitary note of pressure had become a harmony.

It was a structure of immense and subtle complexity, a pattern that was constantly shifting yet always coherent. It was like feeling the grain of a flawless, world-sized crystal. It was a foundation of order beneath the chaos. It did not obscure the familiar sensations—the flow of the gyres, the pressure-shadow of a distant basalt dome—it gave them context. They were now movements against a vast, silent, and unendingly beautiful tapestry.

The Anchorite stopped. For the first time in its existence, it was perfectly still for no reason. It was not resting. It was not sensing for food. It was listening.

It lifted one of its gripping limbs and set it down again, feeling the Chord through its claws, through the anchor-point it made with the world. The world was no longer just a surface to cling to. It was an instrument. And it was playing a song.

The Power had given it nothing and changed everything. The work was the same. The pressure was the same. The solitude was the same. But the Anchorite was no longer alone in the silence. The entire world was now its companion. The silence was not empty; it was filled with music.

It picked up its obsidian shard. Its purpose was unchanged. It had to harvest. It had to live. But as it scraped the glass-threads from the rock, the high, violent *scree* was no longer a lonely shriek in the void. It was a note of its own making, played against the deep, resonant music of the world. It was a reply.
