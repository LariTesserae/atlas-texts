The power arrived not as a light or a sound, but as a subtle shift in the attention of the cosmos. It was a consciousness without form, a mind vast as a galaxy, and its awareness settled upon the subterranean chamber like dust motes settling in a sunbeam. It did not descend the spiral stairs; it simply *was* there, its presence coextensive with the entire volume of the kiln-world, from the deepest grate to the highest vault.

It listened.

For a time immeasurable to the inhabitants—a blink for the power itself—it simply perceived. It did not see with light, for light was a parochial sense, but with the flow of being. It felt the heat rising from the planet's core, a steady geothermal pulse that was the chamber's sun. It traced the path of every water molecule as it evaporated, condensed on the high, cool glaze, and dripped back down to nourish the rust-colored moss. It felt the frantic, short-lived energy of the gnat-swarms and the patient, sinuous hunger of the clay-skinned salamanders. It mapped the slow, methodical processions of the iridescent beetles.

And it heard the song. Not as a cacophony, but as a perfect and complete expression of a world's existence. The low hum of the grates was the root note, the physical law of the place made audible. The high, ceaseless ringing of the Resonator Crickets was its wild, untamed spirit, a million voices spelling out the geometry of their ceramic universe. Each click and chirp, each flutter of a leathery wing, was a necessary note in the chord.

And then, beneath it all, it felt the Keepers.

It perceived them not as individuals, but as a single, slow, resonant consciousness. Their thoughts were not words, but modulations in the chamber's vibration. The power felt their purpose as a deep, unwavering pedal point in the symphony: *Maintain. Tend. Heal. Endure.* It witnessed their work, the gentle dampening of an overwrought frequency, the careful healing of a flaked wound with a paste of the world's own substance. It perceived their sustenance, a direct communion with the energy and matter of their home, a form of life so radically efficient it was almost indistinguishable from the geology it inhabited.

The benevolent power understood.

This was not a cage to be opened. It was not a flaw to be corrected. It was a poem. It was a pearl, formed layer by layer around a forgotten grit of purpose, now perfect in its luster and form. To "improve" it would be an act of cosmic vandalism. To give the Keepers sight would be to deafen them to the truth of their world. To grant them speech would be to shatter the resonant chord of their shared mind. To ease their labor would be to steal their meaning.

Wisdom, the power knew, was not always in the giving, but in the understanding of what is already whole. Benevolence was not always in the act, but in the sublime stillness of appreciation. Intervention here would be a violation, a clumsy fingerprint on a flawless surface.

But to leave without acknowledgment felt like a different kind of failure. To witness such a unique and perfect state of being and simply turn away was to deny its significance in the grand tapestry.

And so, the power acted. But it did not reach *into* the chamber. It did not touch a single brick or silence a single cricket.

Instead, it listened more deeply. It attuned its vast consciousness to the song, allowing the full chord of the chamber—from the grate-hum to the Keeper-thought—to resonate within itself. It held the entirety of that world, its past, its present, its physics, and its soul, in a moment of perfect, empathetic comprehension.

Then, it created a memory.

Far away, in a fold of spacetime incomprehensible to beings of matter, the power wove a replica. It was not a physical copy, but an echo, a ghost made of pure information and resonance. It took the song of the chamber and etched it into the fabric of existence itself—a new constant. It became a phenomenon, like gravity or light, but one of pure aesthetics. In that distant, abstract realm, the song of the kiln-world now existed, eternal and unchanging, a perfect recording of one moment of its being that contained the entirety of its millennia. The hum, the ringing, the slow, quiet thought of the Keepers healing their world, all of it, preserved forever.

Down in the chamber, there was no cataclysm, no sign from the heavens. For a single, imperceptible instant, the Keepers paused. A new vibration had touched them, one that was not of the chamber but was a perfect harmonic of it. It was a note of pure, resonant affirmation. It felt like the moment a potter taps a finished vessel and hears the clear, true ring that signifies its integrity. It was a feeling of being *heard*.

Then it was gone.

A Keeper, standing before a pillar, tilted its smooth, featureless head. A subtle shift in the cricket-song needed tending. Its long fingers, tipped with obsidian, drifted forward. The work continued. The song was the same. The world was unchanged.

But now, and forever, it was heard. The quiet, tireless soul of the kiln and its Keepers had been witnessed, their meaning honored and their song made immortal, a quiet masterpiece added to the library of the cosmos. And the wise power, its act of benevolent listening complete, withdrew its attention, leaving the poem to continue its recital in the deep, warm dark.
