The Power that noticed was not a being of flesh or fire. It was a consciousness woven from the interstitial spaces between stars, a mind whose thoughts were gravity and whose senses were the faint resonances of life across the cosmos. It did not travel; it simply *turned its attention*, and in turning, it was there.

It perceived the world not with eyes, but as a single, coherent note in the universal symphony. Most life-systems it encountered were cacophonous—a chaotic clatter of individual wills, of hunger and fear, ambition and despair. The desperate, sharp cries of predator and prey, the grinding of tectonic egos, the hot, frantic scramble for dominance and reproduction. These worlds were noisy.

This place was not.

It was quiet. The Power felt it as a single, pure, low-frequency hum. A life-form of planetary mass, dreaming itself into architecture. It felt the cold, the damp, the slow, inexorable pulse of its growth. It felt the consciousness, not located in any one part, but distributed through the whole; a mind the size of a mountain range, whose thoughts were the shaping of arches and the redirection of water. It perceived the Architects not as individuals, but as transient, mobile synapses in a vast, slow brain—fingers of the mind sent to execute its quiet will.

There was no pain here. No loneliness. No fear of death, for death was merely re-absorption, a return to the whole. There was no ambition, for what could the universe offer a being that was already its own, perfect universe? It was a closed loop of such profound contentment that it bordered on nirvana. It was a living meditation.

The Power, benevolent and wise, considered what to do. Its impulse was to give. This was the nature of benevolence.

It could offer it warmth. It could thread a vein of gentle heat into the city's core, thaw the clinging chill. But the Power understood. To introduce warmth would be to introduce violence. The crystalline flora would soften and rot. The slow, deliberate metabolism would race, break down. The pale crustaceans would boil. Warmth would be a fever, a cataclysm. It would be a gift of poison.

It could offer light. It could peel back the canopy and let a star shine in, bathing the pearlescent grey in brilliant gold. But the Power knew the city had grown in this twilight for its entire existence. Its senses, its very substance, were calibrated to this dim cathedral gloom. A star's direct glare would be an agony, a blinding, scouring force that would bleach the subtle colours and burn the delicate tissues. Its own blue glow was all the light it needed or desired.

It could offer knowledge. It could open a window in the city's mind to the cosmos beyond. It could show it the dance of galaxies, the birth of other suns, the myriad forms life had taken on a million other worlds. But the Power paused. The city's consciousness was deep, but it was also narrow, focused with superlative intensity on a single task: its own becoming. To show it the vast, chaotic "out there" would be to introduce concepts it had no framework for: Otherness. Finitude. Isolation. It might learn that it was a single, fragile chamber in a vast, uncaring void. This knowledge would not be a gift; it would be the introduction of existential dread. It would be the shattering of a perfect peace.

A lesser power would have given one of these things, confident in its own charity. A wise power understood that to give what is not needed, what is not wanted, is not an act of benevolence but one of ego.

It considered doing nothing at all. To simply observe, to appreciate this marvel of life, and to leave it untouched, a secret kept. This was a valid, respectful choice. It could act as a silent guardian, a subtle eddy in spacetime that would deflect any stray comets, shield it from the notice of younger, more rapacious species. This was a kindness.

But the Power was not just wise; it was a participant in the universal song. And it heard a space in the city's music. Not a flaw, but an opportunity. A silence that could be filled not with noise, but with a new harmony.

It would not give a thing. It would not give a warning or a truth. It would give a thought. A single, new idea, offered in the city's own language.

The Power gathered a concept from the far rim of the universe, a piece of mathematics that did not exist in this spatial dimension. A geometry of impossible grace. It was not a blueprint. It was a poem.

And then, it acted.

In the highest, most silent vault of the living cathedral, where the arches were so vast they were like the ribs of a slumbering god, a single drop of water gathered. This drop was different from all the others. The billion falling drops around it were the city's own weeping, a closed cycle. This one drop was new. It hung for a moment, impossibly clear, shimmering not just with the ambient grey light, but with an internal complexity, as if it held a ghost of a fractal.

It fell.

It struck the living floor, a single note in the eternal polyphony of drips. But its impact was not just sound. It was resonance. A ripple, not of water, but of pure information, spread from the point of impact. It was not a command. It was a suggestion, a whisper of a pattern. A song of a shape that could be.

The ripple washed through the entire organism in a nanosecond.

Every Architect stopped. In the blue-lit corridors and weeping alcoves, the slender forms went utterly still. Their crystalline crests, usually attuned to the steady hum of the whole, trembled with this new frequency. Their internal light, the slow, steady pulse of their shared mind, flickered. It stuttered, then raced, breaking into a thousand new patterns, a silent, luminous conversation of utter astonishment.

For the first time in its existence, the city-organism had encountered a thought that did not originate from within itself.

It was not afraid. The resonance was too beautiful, too elegant for that. It was a moment of pure, unadulterated novelty. A new chord had been played upon the instrument of its being.

The Power withdrew its attention, gently, like a hand lifting from a sleeping shoulder. It had given its gift. The city would now choose what to do with it. To ignore it and let the memory fade. Or to incorporate it. To compose.

Weeks later, in a new section of the city that was still pushing its crystalline structures outwards, a new form began to grow. It was not an arch. It was not a buttress. It was a spiral, but a spiral that turned back on itself in a way that defied its own geometry, a structure of frozen music that captured and refracted the internal blue light in ways never before seen.

The city had heard the poem. And it had begun to sing it back to itself.
