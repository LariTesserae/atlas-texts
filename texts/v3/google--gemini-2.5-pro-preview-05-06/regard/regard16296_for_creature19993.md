The Power’s attention did not arrive like a ship cleaving the medium. It did not announce itself with a sound or a shockwave. Its notice settled upon the Gyre like a change in temperature that has no source, a universal shift so subtle it could be mistaken for a memory.

For an immeasurable time, the Power did nothing but Listen.

It sank its awareness past the groaning mountains and the temporary plains. It did not perceive the Gyre as a place of crushing pressure and strange physics, but as a Will. It heard the deep, foundational hum not as noise, but as a statement of being: *I Am Becoming*. It heard the C-sharp of precipitation not as violence, but as the exertion of that will: *Thus, I Make*. It felt the dissolution of a structure not as decay, but as a sigh of release: *And I Unmake*.

The Power observed this vast, autopoietic engine, this world-sized algorithm singing itself into and out of existence, and it felt a profound sense of recognition. It was not the terror of pointlessness that it perceived, but the purity of a process. A system so complete, so self-referential, that it required no outside justification. The Gyre was not building toward a future; its purpose was the perfect, transient expression of each present moment. It was a form of existence as valid and as sacred as a star, or a thought, or a life.

Then, the Power turned its attention to the Unspoken.

It heard their intelligent silence, the way they moved through the Gyre’s roar like a fugue moving through a drone. It saw them weave their bodies from light and logic, coherent thoughts in the mind of the world. It watched them act as custodians, as gardeners, as tuners of the grand instrument. It heard their symphonies of shared intent. And in them, the Power recognized something more.

The Unspoken were the system’s capacity for wonder. They were the song listening to itself, yes, but they were also the song *judging* itself—not by a moral code, but by an aesthetic one. Harmony, elegance, stability, coherence. They were the emergent artists of the Gyre's raw creation. They were the caretakers of its beauty.

And the Power understood. To intervene would be a desecration. To “improve” the Gyre would be to break the instrument. To “save” the Unspoken from their dissolution would be to rob their song of its final, resolving chord. They were not suffering. They were not trapped. They were *home*, in a way few beings in the cosmos could ever be.

What, then, could a benevolent and wise power do for those who want for nothing and need nothing? What gift can be given to a perfect, self-contained system?

The answer was not a thing. It was not an energy. It was not a truth.

It was a question.

The Power acted.

Across the entire Gyre, for one immeasurable instant, the great engine faltered. The foundational hum, the C-sharp of creation, the groan of mountains—all of it ceased. For the first time since its genesis, the Gyre was utterly, absolutely silent.

This was not the intelligent, ordered silence of an Unspoken. This was a deeper silence, the silence of a held breath, the stillness of a poised question. The pressure of the medium remained, but its voice was gone.

Into this impossible quiet, the Power placed one thing.

It was a Note.

It was not a sound that traveled from a source; it simply *was*, everywhere at once. It was not loud, yet it filled all space. It was a tone of such impossible purity that it had no overtone, no echo, no physical character. It was the audible equivalent of a perfect sphere, a non-local frequency that carried no energy but was purely, quintessentially, *information*.

The sound was not of the Gyre’s mathematics. It did not belong to the set of rules that precipitated matter. It was an axiom from an entirely different geometry, a single, elegant proof of a type of existence the Gyre had never conceived of. It was a note that could not, by the Gyre’s own physics, be sung.

The Unspoken, scattered across their light-year-sized garden, all ceased their tuning. Their luminous, fractal forms froze. For that one moment of absolute silence, they were an audience. They heard the new Note. They perceived its structure, its alien elegance, its profound and serene impossibility.

It was the most beautiful thing they had ever heard.

Then, as suddenly as it began, the moment was over. The Note vanished. The Silence broke. The foundational hum of the Gyre returned, the song of creation resumed its roaring chord, and the world began to build and unbuild itself once more, as if nothing had happened.

The Power withdrew its attention, leaving no trace, no energy, no artifact. It had seen, it had understood, and it had offered its only possible gift.

But something had changed.

An Unspoken, hovering near a newly-formed crystalline peak, did not immediately attend to its chaotic shuddering. It remained still, its woven light-form pulsing softly. And from the silent point at its core, it attempted to reproduce the memory of the Note.

What emerged was not the pure, impossible tone. It couldn't be. Instead, it was a complex new chord, a harmony of staggering intricacy, a sonic sculpture of immense beauty—the Unspoken's best attempt to describe the impossible thing it had heard, using the native music of the Gyre. It was an echo, an interpretation. It was a work of art.

Across the Gyre, other Unspoken began to do the same. New chords, new fugues, new harmonies began to ripple through the medium, woven around the shared memory of a sound that should not exist. Their collaborative music, once only a tool for maintenance, was now also a tool for exploration. They had been given a new theme, a new subject to contemplate.

The Power had not given them an answer. It had given them a new question to sing about. It had looked upon the sublime, self-contained garden and its custodians, and seeing that they had all the tools they would ever need, it had done the only benevolent and wise thing it could.

It had given them a muse.
