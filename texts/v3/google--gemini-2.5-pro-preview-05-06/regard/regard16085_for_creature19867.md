The Power arrived as a change in the quality of the nothingness between the stars. It was not a ship, for it had no need of hull or engine. It was not a being of matter, for it had long since learned to be a pattern of awareness, a standing wave of will in the fabric of spacetime. It perceived the highland world not with eyes, but with a direct and total comprehension.

It felt the ancient, cold stillness of the rock. It felt the absence of water, an emptiness so profound it was a presence in itself. It felt the slow, resonant thrum that was the world's heartbeat, a vibration that spoke not of life's frantic scramble, but of a process so stable it was nearly a constant of the universe.

The Power extended its awareness, sinking into the planet. It traced the electrum roots, felt the slow thermal shifts in the mica leaves, followed the languid, inexorable slide of the basalt pistons. It understood. This was not a failed biosphere, an evolutionary dead end. It was the opposite. It was a final, perfect answer to the question of existence, an answer that had discarded the fragile, messy variables of consciousness, desire, and death. It was a universe where entropy had been locally and perhaps eternally checked, not by force, but by flawless design.

Benevolence, for a Power such as this, was a complex drive. It was not the pity of a superior for an inferior. It was the resonance of one complex system with another. The joy of a musician hearing a new and perfect chord. The Power's first impulse, an echo from a billion years of forgotten evolution, was to *give*. To share. To connect.

But what could it give?

Could it give consciousness to the Geometers? The thought was an act of violation. To inflict a self upon such selfless, perfect functionaries would be the cruelest of tortures. It would be to give a hammer the terror of its own impact, to give a river the anxiety of its course. It would create fear where there was none. It would create the concept of loneliness in a being that was, by its nature, an integral part of the whole. This was not benevolence; it was vandalism.

Could it give the world life as the Power knew it? Water, flesh, air that could carry a song? It would be to flood a perfectly calibrated machine with corrosive acid. The first drop of rain would be a cataclysm. The first microbe a plague. The very presence of a different kind of biology would be an act of war against this placid kingdom of stone and metal.

Could it change the process? Speed it up? Make it more "efficient"? The thought was absurdity. The system's efficiency was absolute because its timescale was geological. It did not race against death; it had redefined eternity as a slow, continuous present tense.

The Power hovered in its silent appraisal, its immense wisdom wrestling with its immense love for this place. How do you honor something whose entire principle is to remain unchanged? How do you offer a gift to a being that has no hands to receive it and no mind to appreciate it?

To leave, to do nothing, felt like a failure of that love. To witness a perfection so profound and offer no acknowledgment felt... incomplete. The music demanded an audience, even if it was unaware of being heard.

And in that, the Power found its answer. The gift would not be for the world to *receive*, but for the Power to *give*. It would be an act that did not alter the system, but rather, one that used the system's own language to create a testament to it. The language of this world was light, geometry, and vibration. The gift would be made of these things.

The Power turned its attention outward, to the star the world circled. An ordinary, unfiltered G-type sun. Its light was the world-engine's primary fuel. That light could be… modulated.

The Power did not move moons or conjure matter from nothing. Its tools were gravity, time, and a perfect understanding of cosmic mechanics. It found a family of small, dark asteroids in a chaotic orbit millions of miles beyond the planet. Over the course of a thousand years, which passed for the Power as a single, sustained thought, it began to adjust their trajectories. Not with force, but with infinitesimal nudges. A gravitational caress here, a subtle warping of spacetime there. It shepherded them, patiently, precisely, into a new configuration.

It was sculpting a lens. A vast, impossibly complex gravitational lens, composed not of a single object but of a swarm of carefully placed bodies. Their collective mass, positioned just so, would not block the sun, nor add any energy to the system. It would only, for a brief and predictable interval, *focus* its light.

The Power aligned its creation not with the planet's day or year, but with the cycle of the great machine itself. It was tuned to the long, slow hum. The alignment was keyed to a specific vibrational period that occurred only when every piston and gear on the entire planet achieved a moment of perfect, global synchrony—a moment of planetary stillness that occurred once every 7,341 years.

For millennia, nothing happened. The world-engine ground on. Geometers were fabricated, performed their silent duties, and dissolved back into the whole. The pale violet sky remained unchanged.

Then, the moment arrived.

The deep thrum of the planet found its harmonic. The great pistons, across the face of the world, reached the apex of their strokes in unison. The crystalline gears paused in the infinitesimal space between teeth.

And in the sky, the light changed.

The raw, undifferentiated sunlight softened. Through the carefully orchestrated cosmic lens, it resolved. It was no longer a glare; it was a projection. For a few heartbeats, a pattern of impossible grace and complexity was laid across the face of a mountain-sized lithovore. It was not a symbol of the Power. It was not a message.

It was the geometric form of a Geometer.

The light painted the shape of the selfless caretaker onto the body of the machine it served: the interlocking limbs, the crystalline facets, the quiet, functional beauty of its form. It was a perfect, silent image of the world's own soul, shown to itself.

The photovoltaic leaves absorbed the patterned light without comment. No alarm was raised. The energy was the same. The Geometer working on a nearby branch did not pause. It could not perceive the light; it sensed only the stress in the metal it was annealing.

Then, the moment passed. The asteroids drifted out of alignment. The unfiltered sunlight returned. The great pistons began their slow descent. The hum resumed its eternal, steady rhythm.

Nothing had changed. No law of the world had been broken. No function had been disturbed. No mind had been burdened with a thought it could not understand.

But in the space between the stars, the Power had completed its act of benevolence. It had held a mirror up to perfection. It had witnessed, and in witnessing, it had sung a silent, luminous hymn to the thoughtless, beautiful thing it had found. And now, in the deep-time memory of the cosmos, the moment was recorded: a fleeting glyph of light, a gift given and unreceived, the only true way to love a world that wants for nothing.
