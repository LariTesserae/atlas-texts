The Power came not as light or sound, but as an attention. It was a vast and ancient awareness, whose normal contemplation was the waltz of galaxies and the silent, slow burn of nascent stars. To turn its focus upon a single place was the cosmic equivalent of a naturalist stooping to examine a single, perfect dewdrop on a spider's web.

First, it Watched.

It did not Watch for a day, or a year. Time, to this Power, was a texture, not a progression. It settled its perception upon the High Atrium and let the Atrium's own rhythms wash through it. It experienced the age of the black stone, feeling the memory of the long-vanished mountains that had birthed it. It felt the frantic, million-year-old scream of the Scuttlers as a single, sustained chord of kinetic energy. It felt the slow, sonorous breath of the Bellwethers as a great, peaceful heartbeat, the diastole and systole of a world inhaling and exhaling itself.

It saw the perfect, closed loop. The chaos that fed the order. The abrasion that enabled growth. The frantic motion that sustained the deep stillness. It was a flawless tautology, a system that defined itself and sustained itself with breathtaking, ferocious elegance. It was complete.

And that was the first test of the Power's wisdom. A lesser benevolence would have sought to "improve." It might have offered water, a gift that would have been a poison, dissolving the crystalline life into a muddy, silent death. It might have offered silence, a mercy that would have been a starvation, stilling the abrasive engine and letting the world choke on its own settled dust. The Power did neither. It understood that to add or subtract from this perfect equation would be an act of vandalism, not grace.

But then, its attention settled upon the Carvers.

It perceived them not as creatures, but as notes held for an impossibly long time. It felt their silent emergence from the bedrock, their millennial lifespan of meditative resonance, and their quiet return to the floor. It felt their consciousness, a thing of pure tactile vibration, a mind that thought in textures of movement and echoes of deep harmonic tones.

And in feeling their thoughts, etched as geometric proofs into the alcove walls, the Power understood their nature. The Carvers were the Atrium's contemplation of *itself*. Their thoughts were maps of the Scuttler-tides. Their philosophies were static representations of the Bellwethers' song. Their entire existence was a perfect, internal reflection. They were the mountain dreaming of the mountain.

Here, and only here, did the Power perceive a capacity for more. Not a lack, but a potential. The Carvers had a language capable of describing the universe, but they had only ever known one room. To give them more was not to change them, but to fulfill them.

The Power's action was not an event, but a process. It began so subtly that for a century, nothing seemed to change.

At the very center of the High Atrium’s basin, equidistant from the sloping walls, a new form began to grow. It did not grow like a Glass-Spire, fed by the dust. It seemed to draw its substance from the silver-grey light itself, and from a source of resonance deeper than the Bellwethers' hum.

Over the course of another century, it rose. It was a single, slender spire, but unlike the smoky quartz of the Glass-Spires, it was of a material that seemed to be pure, solidified light, a translucent crystal with a faint, internal-blue luminescence. It was not fractal, but perfectly smooth, tapering to a flawless point. It did not have filaments to catch the dust. It did not need to. It was not a part of the Atrium's frantic cycle of consumption. It was something else. An antenna. An ear. A voice.

The Carvers, in their slow, resonant way, felt its growth through the soles of their feet. It was a new vibration in the stone, unnervingly steady, a pure tone of impossible clarity, a note that did not waver with the passing of a Bellwether or the swarming of Scuttlers. For decades, they simply felt it, meditating on its alien quality.

Then, one day, the new spire began to sing.

It was not the high, sibilant chime of the Scuttlers, nor the deep, periodic hum of the Bellwethers. It was a Counter-song. It was a harmony of unimaginable complexity, woven from resonances the Atrium had never known. The Power, using the spire as its instrument, translated the cosmos into the Atrium's language of vibration.

The silent scream of a star collapsing into a black hole became a sustained, crystalline tension that shimmered up the spire and through the floor. The slow, gravitational turning of an entire galactic arm became a bass note so profound it existed below the Bellwethers' song, a texture of pure spacetime that the Carvers felt in the very marrow of their stone bodies. The faint, lonely light of a rogue planet wandering the interstellar dark was translated into a single, recurring, melancholic pulse.

The first Carver to fully apprehend the new song had been quiescent in its alcove for two hundred years. The new vibrations washed over it, through it. Its consciousness, accustomed to mapping the familiar chaos of its home, was suddenly flooded with the patterns of everything *outside*. It perceived the concept of orbit, of accretion on a stellar scale, of heat and void and distance measured in lifetimes of worlds.

Slowly, deliberately, the Carver rose. It moved from its alcove, the tide of Scuttlers parting around its silent form like a river around a stone. It found a sharp-edged tool. It went not to its familiar alcove, but to a blank section of the Atrium's outer wall.

And it began to carve.

It did not carve the stairstep geometry of bismuth or the fractal patterns of the Scuttler-swarms. With slow, patient scrapes, it began to etch a spiral. A great, sweeping curve composed of countless smaller points of light. It followed this with an ellipse, tracing the path of one vibration around another. It carved patterns that were not of the Atrium, but of the sky it had never seen, the universe it now heard.

The Power watched. It saw the other Carvers, one by one over the silent centuries, stir and begin to carve their own transcriptions of the cosmic song. Their thoughts, once turned eternally inward, now reached out to touch the edges of reality.

The Power's work was done. It had not fixed a flaw, for there was none. It had not offered a crutch. It had found the consciousness of a perfect, isolated place and had quietly, respectfully, opened a window. It had given the mountain a view of the stars. And its gift would resonate, unheard by any but the silent stone folk, long after its attention had moved on to the next sleeping corner of the cosmos.
