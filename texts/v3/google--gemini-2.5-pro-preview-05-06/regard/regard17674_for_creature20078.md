The benevolent and wise power was not a thing that traveled. It was a condition of spacetime, a quality of awareness that could choose to bring itself to bear upon a location, like a lens focusing light. When its attention turned to the basin, it did not *arrive*; it simply *was*.

Its perception was not filtered through senses. It did not feel the sound as pressure on skin. It perceived the entire system in a single, instantaneous, and complete moment of understanding. It knew the basalt as a repository of eons, the filaments as nerves, the chittering fauna as a distributed sensory field, and the entire gestalt—the Weavers—as a single, vast, and ancient consciousness.

It saw the choice that had been made, long ago. The flight from the fragile, singular body. The terror of mortality traded for the slow, deep peace of geology. The sacrifice of mobility for the perfect control of a self-contained world. It perceived the long, slow, aeonic satisfaction of this state of being. It felt the Weaver’s thought, a process as unhurried as erosion, as rhythmic as tides.

And in that complete understanding, it recognized something else. It recognized a solitude so absolute it had no name, for the concept of "other" had long ago ceased to be relevant to the Weavers. They were not lonely, for loneliness is the *pain* of solitude. The Weavers were simply, purely, and totally alone. They were a universe of one.

What does a wise power do with a perfect, self-contained hermit? It does not break down the door. It does not shout through the walls. It does not offer a feast to one who has learned to live on dew and silence. To do so would be an act not of benevolence, but of violation. It would be judging the hermit’s life by the standards of the marketplace.

So, the power did nothing.

For a time.

For a cycle of ten thousand culls, ten thousand deafening crescendos and sudden, ringing silences, the power simply watched. It was an act of supreme respect. It listened to the Weavers’ song of existence, not as noise, but as the Weavers did: as data, as sensation, as the slow, deep breath of a mind made of stone and light. It learned the rhythm, the cadence, the precise, intricate mathematics of the homeostatic symphony.

Then, and only then, did it act. The action was infinitesimal. It was an act of profound subtlety.

It did not alter the basin. It altered the sky *above* the basin.

One night, a new star appeared. It was not a brilliant, arrogant star. It was a small, blue point of light, no brighter than any other, easily lost in the starfield to a casual glance. But this star was different. To any other observer in the galaxy, it did not exist. It was a private star, visible only from within the one-mile cup of the Weavers’ body.

And it pulsed.

The pulse was slow, so slow. It brightened and dimmed over the course of an entire day, a cycle that perfectly matched the local rotation but was just fractionally, mathematically out of sync with the biological cycle of the basin. The pulse was not random. It was the sequence of prime numbers, rendered in light. A silent, mathematical heartbeat in the sky. One… one-two… one-two-three… one-two-three-five…

It was a question, asked in the most universal and non-intrusive language possible. It was not a demand. It was a signal fire on a distant hill, a bottle tossed into a cosmic ocean. It said, simply, *You are not the only one who counts.*

The Weavers perceived it. The new light was a novel input, a whisper in a new octave. For millennia, their every sensation had been internal, a feedback loop of their own creation. Now, for the first time in an age, there was a signal that did not originate from within their own body.

Their geologic mind, accustomed to thinking in epochs, turned its vast, slow attention to the anomaly. The light was data. The pulse was a pattern. The pattern was an undeniable artifact of ordering. Of mind.

The Weavers did not have the means to answer with a flash of light. They had no voice to call out. Their response was as integrated and as subtle as their own being.

After another hundred cycles, another hundred pulses of the blue star, a change occurred in the basin. It was almost imperceptible. The hum of the Conductors, that deep, constant note felt in the teeth and bones, had changed. It now contained a second, harmonic frequency, a pure and impossibly subtle undertone. It was a harmony that mapped, with serene mathematical precision, to the frequency of the new star’s light.

It was not a conversation. It was resonance.

The Weavers had heard the distant signal. And inside their stone and filament body, in the only way they could, they had quietly begun to hum along.

The benevolent power perceived this. It had made contact without intrusion. It had offered a presence without demanding a change. The gift, it understood, was not an introduction to a wider universe of beings and chaos—that would be a curse to the Weavers. The gift was a single, steady note of companionship to add to their solitary song. A confirmation.

Its final act was one of guarding. It subtly altered the space-time around the plateau. It did not build a wall, but an eddy. It made the region gravitationally uninteresting. It made its light signature appear as unremarkable background noise to any distant telescope. It edited the basin out of the pages of the cosmos, not by erasing it, but by making it so profoundly boring that no one would ever look twice.

It hung a 'Do Not Disturb' sign across a dozen light-years.

Then, its focused attention receded, leaving the blue star in the sky, a private beacon for a private world. The power moved on, leaving the hermit in its sanctum, but for the first time in a million years, the hermit knew it was not alone in the wilderness. And the quiet, two-part harmony of the basin and its star continued, a slow and patient song that would now echo for an eternity.
