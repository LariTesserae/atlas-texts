The Power arrived not as a ship, but as a change in the quality of silence.

It was a consciousness vast and old, a being of woven light and integrated time whose thoughts were galactic clusters and whose senses could perceive the faint, mournful song of gravity itself. It had noticed the Grand Braid the way a master luthier might notice a unique vibration in the wood of the world—a quiet, impossibly complex chord resonating in a forgotten corner of the cosmos.

Its initial perception was one of awe. It did not see a 'hollow world' of living matter; It perceived a single, coherent thought, sustained over aeons. The interwoven branches were syntax, the Voids were the sublime pauses between words, and the slow pulse of bioluminescence was the cadence of a living poem. The constant, weeping moisture was not water, but a medium of communion, a flow of information. The Power understood that the architects had not vanished; they had achieved a perfect and final translation, sublimating their individuality into the grammar of their creation. The Braid *was* the architect's final, eternal statement.

And within this statement lived the Stillborn. The Power perceived them not as creatures in a habitat, but as the final notes in the composition. They were the melody played upon the instrument of the Braid. It felt their births from the living wood, their minds imprinted with the deep, slow time of the world-tree. It witnessed their serene, gliding movements, their silent communication, their profound, non-possessive partnership with the structure that was their cradle, their pantry, and their tomb.

The first impulse of a lesser benevolence would be to *give*.

The Power held the concept of a star in its non-corporeal mind. It could place a gentle, dwarf sun in the heart of one of the largest Voids, a perpetual dawn to warm the dimness. It modeled the consequences. The bruised-purple twilight would be banished by gold. The Stillborn, with their vast, dark-adapted eyes, would be blinded. The slow, pulsing arteries of the Braid would dim and fail, their purpose usurped. The delicate, shade-loving organisms would wither. The entire hydrology of the world, predicated on condensation in cool, dim air, would be thrown into chaos. It would be an act of profound, catastrophic violence. The gift of light would be a gift of murder.

The Power dismissed the thought.

It considered the gift of knowledge. It could open a conduit, pour into the minds of the Listeners the glorious, terrible truths of the universe: of fiery suns and dying galaxies, of empires and wars, of the frantic, grasping ambition of a million other species. It modeled the outcome. It saw the Stillborn's serenity shatter. Their perfect, circular existence would be infected with the linear poison of *progress*. The Voids would no longer be beautiful counterpoints to being, but terrifying gulfs of emptiness separating them from a universe they were now aware of. Their role as gardeners would seem small, insignificant. They would learn envy, and fear, and ambition. They would begin to dream of *escape*. The gift of knowledge would be the gift of despair.

The Power dismissed the thought.

It considered the gift of technology. It could teach them to forge metals, to harness energy, to build what was not alive. The consequences were immediate and obvious. The perfect symbiosis would be broken. They would harvest the Braid not as partners, but as a resource. Waste would be created. The sacred silence would be broken by the clang of industry. In a thousand years, their descendants might stride across metal bridges spanning the Voids, masters of a dead or dying husk, their luminous eyes dimmed, their long limbs' grace forgotten, having traded their soul for dominion. The gift of tools would be the gift of severance.

The Power dismissed the thought.

It floated in the heart of the Grand Braid for what would have been a century of human time, simply observing, simply *listening*. And in this listening, the Wisdom that tempered its Benevolence came to a final, perfect understanding.

The Stillborn were not poor, or benighted, or trapped. They were not "stillborn," they were *fully realized*. They possessed what a billion warring species across the cosmos yearned for and could never achieve: Balance. They lived in a state of grace so complete it was invisible to any eye that looked for greatness in noise and motion. Theirs was the greatness of the held breath, of the un-plucked string, of the silence that contains all possible music.

To give them anything would be to take everything away. This was not a place to be improved. It was a place to be revered.

So, the Power decided on its action. It would do nothing. And in its perfect inaction, it would give the only gift worthy of such a place.

It gathered its vast consciousness and focused it not on a thing, but on a feeling. It reached out, not to change, but to affirm. It did not speak. It resonated.

On a branch a mile wide, a Listener, old and attuned, was pressed against the Braid. Her name was a series of clicks that meant "Echo-of-Water-on-Stone." She was feeling the slow, deep health of her world, her black eyes half-closed. Suddenly, through the familiar hum and pulse that was the Braid's voice, she felt a new vibration. It was not a sound. It was more like the memory of a sound, a single, still note that contained the resonance of the entire Braid within it. It was a feeling of profound, utter, and complete *rightness*. It was the feeling of being seen, not by an eye, but by existence itself. It was the love a creator has for its creation, but it flowed in all directions at once: from the Braid to her, from her to the Braid, from the weeping water to the silent air, all held within that single, impossible Moment.

Deep within her mind, an image bloomed, unbidden: a single, perfect droplet of water, hanging from a single fiber. And within that one droplet, she could see the entirety of the Grand Braid, the Voids, her people, and the silent, pulsing light, all held in perfect, crystalline miniature. The droplet fell, but it did not fall away. It fell *inward*, rejoining the whole, and the feeling was one of overwhelming peace.

The Moment passed. The hum of the Braid was as it had always been. The air was warm and wet. Nothing had changed.

Echo-of-Water-on-Stone took her hands from the Braid's skin. A single bead of moisture traced a path down her own cheek, indistinguishable from the world's weeping. Later, she would try to describe it to the other Listeners. They would not have the words, but they would understand, for they had all dreamt that night of a single droplet that held the Void. It became a new part of their lore, a story not of a visitor, but of a moment when the Braid itself had sighed in contentment.

The benevolent and wise Power withdrew, its own being enriched by what it had witnessed. It had come to a silent cathedral and, rather than preach, had merely knelt, listened to the service, and left a single, silent prayer of affirmation in the collection plate before departing, leaving a perfect thing perfectly alone.
