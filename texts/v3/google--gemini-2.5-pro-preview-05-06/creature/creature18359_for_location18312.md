The city is not empty. It is not a ruin, nor a monument. It is a nest, and its makers have never left. They are the architects and the architecture, the city and its citizens, and to see one is to see the other. You have been watching them the entire time.

They are the Vitreals.

At first, you don't perceive them as distinct creatures. You see only motion, a constant, silent turbulence shimming across the pearlescent surfaces. It’s like watching the heat-haze over a desert road, but instead of air, it is solid matter flowing. Then, as your eyes adjust to the relentless light and the impossible scale, you begin to resolve individuals from the mass.

A single Vitreal is about the size of a human torso. Its body is a paradox of hard and soft. The core of it, the seat of its life, is a soft, gelatinous sac, almost entirely transparent. It is a being without a face, without eyes you can recognize, without a mouth. Within this clear, tremulous flesh, you see not organs of blood and viscera, but a constellation of internal structures that pulse with a soft, living light. These are its organs, its nervous system, its soul—luminous nodes that flicker and glow in shifting patterns and hues of soft blue, warm gold, and gentle pink.

This vulnerable, glowing core is supported and carried by a chassis of spindly, articulate limbs. There are many of them, perhaps a dozen or more, jointed like a spider's legs but crafted from the very same biological ceramic as the city itself. They are pearlescent, hard, and sharp-tipped. These limbs are not just for locomotion; they are tools, sensors, and part of the creature's very metabolism. As a Vitreal scuttles across a lattice-work wall, its hard limbs make only the faintest, ticking, glassy sound, a tiny *skrr-tck-tck* that is immediately swallowed by the immense, sound-dampening quiet of the high-altitude air and the sheer scale of the caldera.

They are in a state of constant, silent, communal industry. They are the city's frantic, yet graceful, metabolism. You watch one pause on a high spire. It flexes its abdomen, and from a spinneret near its glowing core, it excretes a fine, viscous filament that hardens instantly in the cold air, becoming another thread of pearlescent ceramic. Before the thread can fall, another Vitreal, clinging to the structure below, reaches up with two of its crystalline legs, catches the filament, and begins to weave it into a growing buttress, its own internal lights pulsing in a steady, rhythmic cadence.

Elsewhere, a group of five have gathered on an older, lower archway, its surface yellowed with age. They are dismantling it. Their hard limbs tap and pry at the structure, not with brute force, but with a kind of resonant frequency, finding the precise points of stress. A shard breaks free. One of the Vitreals takes the piece, smaller than its limb, and draws it to the underside of its gelatinous body. The shard dissolves, absorbed into its being. As it does, the creature's internal lights flare, burning a brilliant white for a moment as it processes the raw material, recycling its own past.

This is how they live. They do not eat. They are living lenses, a species of photovores. The relentless, unfiltered light of the high, white sun is their primary nourishment, focused and processed by their glowing internal organs. The mineral-rich water that weeps from the caldera walls is their second sustenance. They absorb it through their porous ceramic limbs, drawing the dissolved minerals of the ancient rock into their systems to be synthesized into the new material of their city. The entire metropolis is an infinitely complex, self-assembling and disassembling machine for harvesting light and water.

Their world is one without sound, but it is not without language. The city is a storm of silent conversation. As you watch, you begin to grasp the patterns. A sharp, rapid pulse of azure light from a Vitreal on a high tower is a warning or an alarm. An entire section of a wall answers, a hundred individuals flashing gold in unison—an acknowledgement. A slow, gentle, wave-like oscillation of pink and white from one Vitreal to another, as they pass in their frantic scuttling, is a greeting, a moment of contact in the ceaseless flow. The overwhelming brightness of the caldera is not just the sun and the reflective surfaces; it is the reflected glare of the silent, shouting, singing, ceaselessly chattering populace.

To see them is to understand that the distinction between life and environment, between creature and creation, is a clumsy, organic prejudice you brought with you. Here, they are the same thing. The Vitreals are not *in* the city; they *are* the city, in a permanent state of becoming. They live lives of geological patience and insect-like frenzy, their individual moments fleeting, their collective work eternal. You stand at the edge, a being of soft meat and loud breath, and realize you are not just looking at a city of aliens. You are looking at a single, living, crystalline organism a mile wide, and its thoughts are made of light.
