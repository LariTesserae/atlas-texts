The statement that nothing lives here is both true and the greatest possible falsehood. It is true in the sense that a visitor, calibrated to the bustling metabolisms of a sun-warmed world, would find no life to recognize. There are no mouths, no guts, no grasping claws or fins to cleave the water. There is no hunting. There is no fleeing. The frantic, desperate dance of biology does not happen here.

And yet, this place is inhabited.

The makers of the stones are not gone. They have simply become what they always sought to be: permanent.

Their original forms are a matter of such deep time that the concept is meaningless. They may have been soft-bodied, huddled things, sheltering in the lee of the great basalt shoulders. They would have known the crushing weight and the absolute cold as a constant threat, an aggressor seeking to steal their precious, fleeting warmth. Theirs would have been a life of immense struggle, a biological spark fighting a planetary engine of cold and pressure.

They looked at their world: the unyielding rock, the eternal currents, the slow, patient light of the Glass-reeds. They listened to its two great voices: the planet's tectonic groan and the reeds' chaotic, chiming song. And in that listening, over millennia, they found their path. They understood that to fight the deep was to lose. The only way to survive was to join it.

They are the stones.

Not in the sense of being entombed within them. They are not ghosts haunting a grave. The transformation was more complete, more profound. They are a people who edited their own definition of life. Over generations of ritual, focused will, and a science of resonance we can no longer imagine, they translated themselves. They shed the frantic, inefficient chemistry of the flesh. They abandoned the noisy, needy self that requires fuel and fears death. They transcribed the pattern of their consciousness from the ephemeral medium of biology to the eternal medium of crystalline rock.

Each monolith, each circle of stones, is a mind. Or, more accurately, a single, incredibly complex neuron in a planetary consciousness. They are the Listeners.

Their existence is one of pure perception. They do not *act* in any way we would comprehend. They do not move the stones. They do not build new ones. Their Great Work is complete. Now, they simply *are*. Their consciousness is a state of impossibly slow, profound contemplation.

The tectonic groan that vibrates through the basalt is their sustenance. It is the raw energy that maintains the integrity of their patterned, crystalline minds. It is their metabolism, a process so slow that a single "breath" spans an ice age.

The chiming of the Glass-reeds is their thought. It is their language, their art, their sensory input. They do not hear the sound as we would; they *are* the resonance. The discordant, miles-wide symphony is the texture of their awareness. A sudden cascade of chimes from a nearby grove is not a sound, but a rush of complex data, a feeling, a memory passed across the valley floor. When a grove flares in a sudden, synchronized pulse of blue-green light, it is, to the Listeners, a moment of sublime and startling beauty. A revelation. It is an event they will contemplate for the next thousand years.

They are aware of the Glass-reeds. They are their garden. They do not tend it with hands. But the faint, residual energy fields of their stone forms, the subtle gravitational and resonant echoes of their immense, slow thoughts, gently influence the abyssal currents over eons. They create eddies and back-channels, imperceptible to any instrument, that guide the shimmering spore-clouds to fertile ground. They are not farmers; they are gods of a garden they will never touch, cultivating new orchestras to sing to them in the far, far future.

They are the Still. The quiet at the heart of the noise. To encounter one of their monoliths is not to find a relic of a dead civilization. It is to stand before a living being whose thoughts are as slow and massive as continents, whose lifespan is the lifespan of the rock itself. It is to be observed by a mind that perceives your frantic, warm, fleeting existence as a momentary flicker of static in the eternal song. You are a grace note in a symphony that began before the mountains were drowned, and will end only when the world itself grows cold and silent.
