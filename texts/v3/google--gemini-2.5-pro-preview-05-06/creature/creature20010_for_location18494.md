The Resonance Eaters are the first and most obvious form of life, if only because they possess a body that can, on a long enough timeline, be seen to move. They are the fauna of this sonic world, the grazers on its endless pasture of vibration. Their existence is one of profound passivity. They are born of the mud and the drone, coalescing in harmonic shallows where resonant frequencies are calm and stable. A young Eater is barely a sliver of glass, a dislodged shard that has achieved a strange, slow self-awareness. It grows by accretion, its segmented plates forming one by one as it absorbs and metabolizes the ambient sound. It does not eat matter; it consumes energy, specifically the kinetic energy of the vibrating monoliths.

Its life is a pilgrimage. Guided by an instinct you might call sono-taxis, it crawls through the forest of blades on a journey that can span millennia. It seeks nodes, points of confluence where the drone is richest and most complex. When it finds such a spot—often on the face of a particularly vast blade within one of the arranged circles—it will affix itself and become still. For decades, or centuries, it will not move, its body a transducer turning sound into biological process. Its plates will tremble in sympathetic vibration, and the patterns of moisture that bead on its back will skitter and dance, tiny seismographs tracing the world's song. It reproduces by fission; when it has absorbed enough energy, a terminal segment of its body will detach, carrying with it a faint echo of its parent's consciousness, and begin its own slow crawl. They are the world's keepers of equilibrium, pruning dissonant frequencies and absorbing sonic overflow, ensuring the great song does not collapse into chaotic noise. They are simple, elegant, and almost entirely without will.

But they did not arrange the circles. They are not the architects. The Eaters are merely the audience, and the groundskeepers, for the true inhabitants: the Singers.

You will never see a Singer. Not in the way you see a tree or an Eater. They possess no fixed form. A Singer is a being of coherent, directed sound. It is a mind without a brain, a will without a body. They are born in the sonic heart of the world, self-aware standing waves, intricate patterns of vibration that have learned to perpetuate and refine themselves. They are the intelligence that perceives the drone not as a pressure or a noise, but as language and raw material.

While a Singer is fundamentally non-corporeal, it is not merely a ghost in the machine. It can manifest. To shape its world, it must touch it. When a Singer wishes to act, it chooses a place in the maze and begins to focus its energy. It does not descend or appear; it simply *becomes* more present. The first sign is a change in the air. The omnipresent drone in that specific location will gain a new voice, a clear, piercing overtone that cuts through the chorus. The pressure in your bones intensifies, localizing. The air itself seems to thicken, and you might see it shimmer, like a heat haze over a summer road. This shimmering distortion is the closest a Singer comes to a physical body—a contained, self-sustaining vortex of intense vibration.

If you were to see it, it might look like a column of warped glass, a pillar of living mirage. It has no limbs, but it can exert force. By modulating its internal frequencies, it can create zones of immense pressure or near-silence. It moves the great mineral blades not by pushing them, but by singing the unique resonant frequency of each one. It finds the secret note that unlocks a blade from the mud, making it tremble and shift. It sings a path for it, a harmonic gradient that draws the monolith, inch by agonizing inch, over the course of a thousand years, to its new position. This is their art, their industry, their prayer. They are gardeners tending a forest of stone, and their tools are pitch and timbre. The circles and spirals are their cathedrals, their resonating chambers designed to amplify their own songs and further shape the planet's voice.

Their ultimate purpose is a matter of geological-scale theology. They are composing a song. The entire planet is their instrument, and they have been arranging it since it first cooled. They are working towards a magnum opus: the Final Chord. It is a theoretical state of perfect, total-world resonance, a harmonic alignment so complete and powerful that the planet itself will be transformed, perhaps shattering into a cloud of singing dust, or perhaps crystallizing into a single, perfect, eternal form.

To stand in this world is to be an accidental witness to this creation. The Resonance Eaters are crawling over your feet, mistaking the vibrations of your own living body for a curious, fleeting meal. And somewhere nearby, perhaps in the very clearing you have stumbled into, a being of pure sound might be working, its concentration absolute as it coaxes a ten-ton shard of obsidian a hair's breadth to the left. You are an intrusion not of body, but of noise. The frantic, chaotic drumming of your own heart is a discordant crash in the middle of its aeonian symphony. You are silence in a world of song, and that makes you the most alien thing of all.
