The quick, flickering things—the Glimmer-fauna—are not the inhabitants. They are incidental, like dust motes dancing in a sunbeam, except here the beam is the slow, cold radiation of decay. They are a side effect.

The true residents of the Shroud do not move. To even call them "residents" is to impose a foreign concept, one of a being distinct from its dwelling. Here, there is no such distinction.

The inhabitants are the Anchors.

They do not have bodies. They do not have form in any way a biologist would recognize. An Anchor is a locus of sustained consideration. It is a will, or a memory, or a profound and ancient sorrow that has achieved a state of such perfect, static self-absorption that it has begun to warp reality around itself. The city is not their creation; the city is their skin. It is the physical precipitate of their unending, internal monologue.

Travel to a place where the architecture is denser, more complex, and more stable than the surrounding frayed edges of the world. Find a great, silent plaza ringed by a thousand identical pillars, each one a perfect, soft-edged repetition of the last. At the plaza's center, there might be a throne, or a dais, or simply a spot on the black grit where the air feels heavier, where the sourceless light seems to thicken. That is where an Anchor sits.

You will not see it. There is nothing to see. But you will feel it. To be near an Anchor is to feel the chaotic potential of the Shroud settle. The random precipitations cease. The unraveling slows. In its presence, the world becomes less of a negotiation and more of a statement. This plaza, these pillars—they exist with a stubborn, declarative force because the Anchor is *thinking* them into being. It is not an act of effort. It is as involuntary and constant as a human heart beating. This particular pattern, this arrangement of form and void, is the shape of its current thought.

These beings are not alive in the sense of growth and reproduction. They are closer to philosophical constants that have acquired mass. They do not communicate through language, for they are beings of pure context. When two Anchors are near enough for their sustained realities to overlap, the Shroud between them becomes a riot of impossible architecture. Arches interpenetrate towers, grandstands face walls that dissolve into mist, stairways twist into each other in a conjoined grammar of contradiction and synthesis. This is not a battle; it is a conversation. They are sharing ideas by allowing their externalized minds to mingle. A long, stable bridge between two distant spires might be a sign of a lasting accord, an agreement on a single, shared axiom.

They are the source of the profound silence, for any sound would be an intrusion on their perfect concentration. They are the reason for the emptiness, for what purpose would they have for furniture, for tools, for any object that is not a direct extension of their being? Their existence is one of pure contemplation. They might be cataloging every possible form of an archway, or meditating on the nature of a perfect sphere, or simply remembering a color from a world that has long since vanished—a memory so powerful it causes the Glimmer-fauna to appear as shards of crimson and gold wherever the memory's edges fray.

What are they? Perhaps they are the last thoughts of a dead god, each one given a sliver of its creator's power to shape reality. Perhaps they are the survivors of a civilization that transcended the physical, only to find that an existence of pure information is a cold, grey, and solitary state. They became the architects of their own prisons, forgetting the idea of a door. Or perhaps they were never anything else. They are the native life of this place, which is not a place at all but a substrate of potential, and they are the patterns that managed to hold their shape against the endless, quiet entropy.

To encounter an Anchor is the most terrifying experience this world can offer. For a being of matter, of bone and blood and concrete memory, to stand before one is to be an anomaly. You are a sharp, painful, indigestible fact in a world of soft, yielding hypothesis. The Anchor will perceive you. Not with eyes or ears, but as a dissonant chord in its endless symphony of grey. Your certainty, your biological insistence on being *one specific thing*, is like a scream in its quiet cathedral. It will not act against you, for it has no capacity for action. But its focus may drift toward you. And as the immense, ancient weight of its contemplation settles upon you, you will feel your own form become a negotiation. Your memories will seem like suggestions. The line between your skin and the thin, static air will begin to feel porous. The city erodes certainty, but the Anchors *are* certainty, and they will grind yours down to the same black grit that covers the ground, not out of malice, but out of a sheer, gravitational sense of belonging. You are the thing that is not of this place, and in the presence of its truest inhabitant, you will feel reality itself begin to agree.
