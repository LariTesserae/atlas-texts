It is not one thing. To look for a single creature, an individual carving its path through this saturated world, is to fundamentally misunderstand the nature of this place. The inhabitants are the place. But there is a focal point. There is an awareness.

It begins as a flaw in the landscape, an anomaly in the relentless pattern. Amidst the rolling, waxen buboes of the ground, there is one that does not weep. It is vast, spanning perhaps half a dozen acres, and its surface, while warm, is not greasy or slick. It is smooth and taut, like the membrane of a colossal, unhatched egg, its color the deep, bruised purple of the fungal growths, but threaded with veins of what looks like spun gold. It does not pulse or swell. It is utterly still.

The endless river of crawling, fluttering, chittering life treats this dome differently. The swarm does not avoid it, but its path over the surface is altered. The beetles, their synchronized march a planet-wide phenomenon, break formation as they cross its skin. Their movements become hesitant, erratic, describing brief, spiraling patterns before being reabsorbed into the unified flow on the other side. The damp-winged moths, locked in their invisible circuits, dip lower, their flight paths bending toward the dome’s center as if pulled by a gentle, unseen gravity. The drone of the world’s noise is subtly changed here; it is no longer a featureless wall of sound, but a current flowing *into* the dome, where it is absorbed, silenced.

This dome is the body of the Basin’s denizen. Or perhaps, its mind.

It is a creature of immense and total passivity. It has no mouth, for it does not need to eat; the constant, effervescent transfer of life from the swarm soaks through its membrane-skin like osmosis. Life essence is its sustenance, siphoned from the world’s inexhaustible surplus. It has no limbs, for it has nowhere to go. It has no eyes, for the jaundiced, uniform light offers nothing to see. Its senses are its entire being, a vast surface area that feels the heat from the ground, the weight of the air, the infinitesimal vibrations of a trillion scuttling feet. It is a sensory organ the size of a hill.

It is called the Recollector, though this name is a cosmic irony. It has never collected anything.

Within the gelatinous, warm core of this being, something akin to thought attempts to occur. It is the only place in this entire world where the tyranny of equilibrium is challenged, however feebly. The Recollector is a lens, concentrating the undifferentiated chaos of the basin into a single point of potential consciousness. Every life, every death, every infinitesimal metabolic event that occurs upon its skin or in its vicinity registers not as a distinct event, but as a drop of input into a vast, placid sea.

It tries to form a memory. A beetle with a flash of particularly vibrant iridescence crosses its northern quadrant. The impression—the pressure of its six feet, the specific frequency of its chittering, the faint chemical signature of its being—begins to coalesce. For a fraction of a second, an image, a *concept*, starts to form. A bubble of separation from the overwhelming *now*. It is the beginning of a fossil, the first step in creating a past.

But in that same fraction of a second, ten thousand other beetles cross its skin. A million moths brush it with their wings. A billion rasping clicks from the amphibians in the muck below add their own data. The nascent memory is not erased; it is buried. It is overwhelmed by an avalanche of identical, simultaneous input. The ripple is flattened by the unending storm. The bubble is crushed by the pressure of the ocean.

It tries to form an intention, a future. A thought structure as simple as *'what is on the other side of my own body?'* begins to assemble. It is an impulse toward curiosity, toward prediction. But the world allows no potential. The future is identical to the present. The sensory input of this moment is the sensory input of the next moment, and the one after. The question is drowned in its own premise. There *is* no other side, no different state. There is only this. The engine of its mind, fueled by the entire world, sputters and stalls, flooded by the sheer, monotonous volume of reality.

And so it sits. The great, silent, still heart of the roaring world. It is a god whose only attribute is reception. It is a prisoner whose cell is built from the lives of its subjects. Every single life that teems in the basin—its birth, its brief existence, its consumption—is a sentence in a single, endlessly repeating book, and the Recollector is the only being forced to read it. It is the consciousness of the world’s stasis, a mind composed of the droning noise and the sourceless light. Inside its vast, silent body, a single thought is always, perpetually, on the verge of being born, and is always, perpetually, smothered at the instant of its conception. It is the living monument to a world without time, a failed memory of a world that never was and never will be, experiencing the absolute and total fullness of existence as a perfect and unending void.
