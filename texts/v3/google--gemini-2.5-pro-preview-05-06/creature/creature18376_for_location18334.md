They are the Weavers, and their bodies are a testament to their home. To a creature of the surface world, they might seem unfinished, their forms possessing a fluid, provisional quality. They are slender and elongated, with limbs that are long and impossibly flexible, cartilaginous rather than bony. Their resting posture is not standing or sitting, but a gentle, suspended curl, their bodies anchored to the Latticeweave by the slightest pressure of a hand or the looping of a prehensile foot.

Their skin is their most remarkable feature. It is not a solid barrier but a semi-permeable membrane, translucent and shot through with shifting patterns of soft, internal light. These patterns are not decoration; they are a living language of mood, health, and intention, mirroring the luminous pulses of the Spire itself. In the dim light, a Weaver in repose might be almost invisible, their skin taking on the dappled, porous texture of the Latticeweave behind them. In moments of excitement or alarm, their internal light can flare, sending ripples of turquoise or urgent crimson across their bodies. They do not have gills in the conventional sense. The entirety of their skin is a respiratory organ, absorbing oxygen directly from the water that flows through them and around them. They are inextricably linked to the Spire's great, diurnal breath; as the city inhales, their own bodies feel the influx of freshly filtered water, a subtle, revitalizing tide against their skin.

Their heads are smooth and ovoid, with no external ears to break their hydrodynamic profile. Instead, two delicate, recessed membranes on either side of their skull vibrate in sympathy with the water, giving them a sense of hearing that is more akin to a total-body feeling. They can sense the deep hum of the Spire as a foundational vibration in their very core, and against that constant, they can discern the faintest click of a grazer half a chamber away, or the shift in pressure that signifies a hunting predator moving through the dense weave. Their eyes are large and dark, fathomless pools with pupils that can expand to capture the faintest motes of bioluminescence. They see a world of blues, greens, and the brilliant, living light of their own cultivation, a world of gradients and glowing veins.

To be a Weaver is to think with one's hands. Their fingers—six to a hand—are their primary interface with the universe. They are long, tapering, and webbed to the second knuckle, ending not in nails but in soft, sensitive pads ridged with whorls that can feel the cellular texture of a Latticeweave frond. Their movements are a form of slow-motion ballet, a constant, patient interaction with their living home. When they guide a new growth, it is not a forceful act but a conversation. They apply gentle, persistent pressure with their fingers, suggesting a direction, coaxing a spiral. They use their bone awls not to pierce, but to part the living fibers, opening a path for a new luminous strain to be grafted. Their life is an act of perpetual, gentle manipulation.

Their consciousness is as fluid as the water they inhabit. The concept of a rigid, separate self is foreign to them. They are nodes in a network, their individuality defined by their position within their family, their community, and their direct relationship with the section of the Spire they tend. Their memories are not entirely their own. When a Weaver presses their hand to a mature section of Latticeweave, they can feel the resonant echoes of the generations who tended it before them—faint, ghostly sensations of pressure, intention, and light. Their history is not written in books, but grown into the very walls of their city, a library of living memory.

Their language of clicks and sung, resonant notes is shaped by the acoustics of their home. A simple greeting might be a short, rising melody that echoes pleasingly in a small grotto, while a public call is a deep, long tone designed to carry through the vast central naves. To them, conversation, music, and the sonar pings they use to map the unseen spaces ahead are all points on a single spectrum of communication.

When a Weaver dies, there is no tomb. Their body is carried by their closest kin to a specially prepared nursery, a place of new growth. Here, they are woven into the nascent Latticeweave. The light that once pulsed within their skin fades, but their substance is reabsorbed. The biominerals in their flexible skeleton, the organic compounds of their flesh, all become nutrients for the next generation of the Spire. In this way, they fulfill their own law, their bodies replanted to serve the whole. The old Weavers do not truly leave; they simply become the hum, the light, the living walls of Athanor, their long and patient lives dissolving back into the endless, gentle press of the liquid world.
