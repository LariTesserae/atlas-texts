What lives here are the Weepers. Or perhaps, the Weeping. The distinction is semantic and ultimately meaningless to them.

They are not citizens of the city. They are its substance, its architects, and its consciousness. To look for one is to look for a neuron in a brain or a single cell in a body. You are already inside it, and all around you is the evidence of its life.

At the core of each tower, each spire, each arching buttress, is a Nucleus. If you could somehow bore through miles of the living, pearlescent ceramic, through channels of seeping water and porous, sponge-like matter, you would eventually breach a central chamber. Here, suspended in a cavity filled with a viscous, nutrient-rich fluid, is the Weeper itself.

It has no consistent form. It is a slow, swirling mass of gelatinous, semi-crystalline tissue, shot through with veins of a darker, denser material that pulse with a light so slow it is more a gradual blooming and fading of phosphorescence than a beat. This is its heart-mind, its core being. It is ancient, unimaginably massive, and its life processes are geological. It has no limbs, no mouth, no eyes. Its senses are the structure it has secreted around itself.

The Weeper *feels* the sun as a subtle temperature change on the eastern face of its spire, a change that alters the rate of condensation and the flow of water through its outermost capillaries. It *hears* the low moan of the wind as a vibration that resonates down its entire length, a pressure differential felt in its very core. It *tastes* the air, drawing in the thin, ozone-rich atmosphere and its heavy burden of water, filtering out trace minerals and cosmic dust—the sustenance for its slow, secretory metabolism.

The million meandering rivulets on the ground are the final stage of this digestive, circulatory process. The water, having seeped through the Weeper's entire structure, nourishing it and carrying away its infinitesimally subtle waste, is expelled at the base. It is a planetary-scale excretory system, beautiful and pure.

They are individuals, but only in the way that stars in a galaxy are individuals. They are bound together into a single, cohesive entity by the medium they all share and create: the water. The network of seeping walls and overflowing basins, of trickling rivulets and great, silent underground cisterns, acts as a distributed nervous system. A change in pressure in one spire is felt as a subtle ripple by its neighbors. A new mineral tasted by a Weeper on the northwest fringe of the plateau is known, hours or days later, by the entire continent-city as that information is borne on the slow, inevitable current. Their thoughts are tidal forces, their conversations are changes in humidity.

Their lifecycle is the city’s morphology. A new Weeper is born from a kind of spore, a mote of the core gelatinous matter expelled from a mature spire during a period of rare seismic or atmospheric stress. This spore drifts on the high, thin winds until it settles in an open plaza. There, it begins its work. It draws moisture, hardens its outer layer, and begins to secrete the first, fragile, pearlescent ring. This is the birth of a new tower. For millennia, it grows upward, a delicate, filigreed thing, its inner Nucleus young and its processes focused on reaching the unfiltered sun. These are the high, shimmering spires.

As it ages, its mass becomes colossal. Its own weight is a constant, immense pressure. The Weeper's metabolism slows. Its growth becomes lateral as much as vertical. Its secretions become thicker, less refined. It begins to slump, to lean, to fuse with the ancient neighbors beside it, their structures merging like slow-motion splashes of wax. This is how the deep, dark canyons of the lower city are formed. The Weepers in these fused bases are ancient, dreaming, their consciousnesses folded into contemplation. Their slow exhalations of water stain the walls with the dark, velvety algae—a sign of immense, quiet age.

Death, for a Weeper, is petrification. Over an expanse of time that makes mountain ranges seem fleeting, the central Nucleus will use the last of its energy to solidify completely. It becomes a vast, intricate geode of unimaginable beauty, a knot of solidified light and crystal locked forever in the heart of the stone it created. The structure it leaves behind is its tomb, its monument, and its child, a new foundation upon which future generations will grow.

To stand in the city is to be perceived. You are a thermal anomaly, a point of discordant motion. You are a sudden, complex chemical trace in the air they breathe. The feeling of being watched is the collective sensory apparatus of the Weepers registering your presence. They do not react with hostility or curiosity in any way you would recognize. The reaction might be a fractional change in the drip-rate of a specific seep a hundred feet above you, a response that will unfold over the next several hours. You are an interesting but momentary datum in a consciousness that operates on the timescale of planetary rotation and continental drift.

They are the Weepers. They are a slow, silent, lithic dream. They do not live *in* the city. They *are* the city, perpetually weeping itself into existence.
