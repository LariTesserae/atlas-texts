The prompt declares that there is no life here, and in the strict, biological sense, this is true. The beings of this place do not metabolize, reproduce, or die. They do not breathe, for there is no air they would recognize, only the luminous, water-saturated medium. They do not eat, for there is no sustenance but water and chalk. To say they "live" here is a category error. They *are* here. They are the city.

They are the builders, the caretakers, and the structure itself, and the distinction between these roles has long since eroded into irrelevance.

When they are still—which is their state for centuries at a time—they are indistinguishable from the architecture. They *are* the great, sloping walls, the molar-shaped towers, the flared buttresses. Their substance is the same hydrous chalk, that pale, soapstone-like mineral saturated with pure water. A traveler could lay a hand on a weeping wall and be touching the dormant flank of a being whose thoughts move with the speed of geological erosion. They are not *in* the city; they are the city, in repose.

Movement, when it occurs, is an event on a scale that defies observation. It is not an act of locomotion, but of becoming. A desire to reshape a channel, born from a subtle shift in the resonant hum of the deep-world aquifer, will not manifest as a creature walking to a wall with a tool. Instead, a section of wall—perhaps a mile long and a thousand feet high—will begin to soften. Over the course of a decade, its form will lose definition, becoming a slow, colossal slump, a mudslide in arrested motion. From this softening mass, a protuberance will emerge. It is not an arm, but a purposeful extrusion of self, a pseudopod the size of a mountain ridge. With infinite patience, this limb of living chalk will press and smooth a new curve into a canyon wall, smearing its own substance as the building material. The whorls of palms and fingers that cover every surface are not ancient marks left by absent hands; they are the dermal ridges of the city itself, the texture left behind by its most recent act of self-adjustment. The work is "fresh" because the builder has not left; it has simply re-absorbed, its form once again becoming a static, architectural feature, indistinguishable from the whole.

They have no eyes. Their perception is not optical but haptic, and it is total. The entire metropolis is a single, continuous sensory organ. The luminous mist is not something they see *with*, but a medium they feel *through*. Their consciousness is a network of pressure, moisture, and vibration. Every drop that forms on a high surface and falls a mile to strike a resonant basin is a single, clear thought. The trickling of a rill down a thumb-carved groove is a sentence. The deep, bass thrum from the canyons below is not merely sound; it is the slow, collective cognition of the city-being, a distributed mind contemplating the single, overriding imperative of its existence: the water.

Their purpose is the function of the city. They are a passive, patient filtration system given a sluggish, mineral form of life. They are priests to a god of purity, and their liturgy is hydrology. Their thoughts are of flow dynamics, of surface tension, of the slow leaching of imperceptible impurities. They add a new layer of their own chalk-paste being to a wall not for structural integrity, but to subtly alter the condensation pattern of the glowing vapor. They carve a new channel not for transport, but to guide the resulting water towards the unseen cisterns in the world's foundation with even greater perfection.

They are silent, because sound in air is a frivolous, high-frequency thing. Their communication is the resonant pulse of the water moving through their own bodies. They are unseen, because they hide in plain sight, as the ground and the sky. They are without biology, for their substance is stone and their blood is water. They are the grand, passive apparatus, a machine that grew a mind so it could better perform its function. The city was not built *for* them; the city *is* them, a slow, colossal, and solitary being whose only reason for existence is to take the water that is, and transform it into the water that could be. They are the vessel, and they are the potter, eternally shaping and refining themselves for the sacred liquid they were made to hold.
