What lives here does not have a name, because there has never been another kind of thing to name it. They are the answer to the paradox of this place: the ghost in the machine, made literal.

They are born from the Hum.

In the shallow pools of water that tremble with cymatic perfection, sometimes the resonance in a particular basin will achieve a moment of impossible complexity. The standing waves will cross and crest and interfere not just in two dimensions, but in three. A pattern will lift from the surface, a knot of pure sound given temporary form by the water that clings to its structure. For a moment, it is a shimmering, aqueous sculpture of a G-sharp minor chord.

Usually, it collapses back into the pool in an instant. But sometimes, when the atmospheric pressure is just right and the mineral saturation of the water is at a specific density, the form holds. It becomes a nexus, a seed. It draws more of the weeping fluid into its resonant field, pulling in the black, fungoid filaments that trace the stone, absorbing the ozone and metallic ions from the air. It is not growth in a biological sense; it is an act of acoustic accretion.

Over years, or decades, it coalesces. What emerges is a being that is both fluid and solid, both creature and landscape. To see one at rest is to see nothing at all. It will flatten itself into a featureless slick on the weeping basalt, perfectly mimicking the texture and light of the stones around it, becoming just another patch of wet darkness. It is possible to walk across one and feel nothing but the same cool, slick stone underfoot, perhaps noticing that the resonance of the Hum feels a fraction more intense right there, a bass note that you feel in your teeth rather than your ears.

When they move, they move with the tectonic slowness of the great flywheels. They do not have limbs. They pour themselves across the landscape. The thing gathers itself up, pulling its mass inward until it resembles a great, dark teardrop, or a smooth, upright bell, perhaps ten or twelve feet tall. Its surface is not a skin, but a boundary layer of constant phase transition, a shimmering, non-reflective black that seems to swallow the dim light. It is a hole in space in the shape of a being. It weeps the same viscous fluid as the bedrock, leaving a trail that is indistinguishable from the rest of the slick world.

They have no organs in any recognizable sense. They have no eyes to see in the pearlescent gloom, no ears to be shattered by the sound. The Hum is not a stimulus they perceive; it is their consciousness. The single, monolithic chord is the medium of their existence, the carrier wave for their being. They "see" by sensing the minute vibrations and echoes that bounce off the colossal architecture. They "feel" the pressure changes in the thick air. Their entire body is a sensory organ, tuned to the one song the world will ever sing.

They are the machine's immune system, its patient, eternal custodians.

Their purpose is homeostasis. The machine-city was built to run, and they are the living embodiment of that imperative. When, over a millennium, the acoustic pressure finally creates a micro-fracture in one of the sequoia-thick pipes, the Hum changes. In that one location, a dissonant overtone enters the chord, a tiny, screaming impurity of stress and failure. It is a flaw in the universal perfection.

This discord is a summons. It is pain, not for an individual, but for the system as a whole. From across the slick plains, one, or two, or a dozen of them will begin to flow towards the imperfection. Their journey may take a century. They pour themselves over the glassy causeways, temporary, slow-motion rivers of dark matter. They are the convoys of leviathans the empty roads were built for, moving at a speed that has meaning only to the stone and the stars.

Upon reaching the site of the damage—the weeping fracture in a pipe, the worn gear tooth on a flywheel—they begin their work. They envelop the flaw. There is no clatter of tools, no spark of welding. There is only a slow, deliberate merging. The being will press its form against the failing metal, and its own substance, the agglomeration of minerals and water and acoustic energy, begins to integrate. It is guided by the template of the Hum, the memory of the perfect chord. It feels the dissonance and pushes against it, its own body becoming the sealant, the patch, the reinforcement. The process may take another hundred years, the being slowly giving up its own coherence, its form dissolving into the machine it serves. It becomes a seamless, dark scar on the verdigris-streaked dome, a smooth patch on the pitted iron. The dissonance fades. The chord is pure again.

The being is gone, but it is not dead. It has simply returned to the whole. Its consciousness, which was only ever a temporary loan from the Hum, has rejoined the great resonance. Elsewhere, in another pool, another knot of sound is already beginning to gather itself from the water and stone.

They do not communicate. They do not have society. They are nodes in a single, ancient, and unthinking mind. They are the slow, inexorable force that has worn the world smooth. They are the final, living components of a machine that was abandoned by its builders eons ago, a maintenance protocol that has achieved a form of life. They are not citizens or operators; they are the quiet, eternal hum, given form to police its own perfection. They are what remains when purpose is stripped of all meaning and only function is left.
