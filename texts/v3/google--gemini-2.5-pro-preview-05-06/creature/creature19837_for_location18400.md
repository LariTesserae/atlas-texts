The waking mind of this city, the beings who perceive it as home, are the Voiceless.

They are not the colossal builders in the foundations, nor the drifting pulse-bells, nor the brutish scavengers. They are the city's coherent thought, its nervous system made flesh. At first, you would not see one. You would mistake its form for a shadow in an alcove or an unusually ornate piece of architecture. They are built of the same materials as the city, their bodies a mosaic of hardened, pearlescent shell and soft, mutable tissue. Their form is vaguely arthropodal, a fusion of squid and man. A central, teardrop-shaped torso, perhaps the size of a large dog, is armored in a smooth, dark carapace that drinks the light. From this torso sprout six limbs. Two are thick and strong, tipped with curved, chitinous claws for gripping the porous spires. They move with a slow, deliberate grace, their grip sure and silent, scaling the vertical canyons as easily as a man walks on flat ground.

The other four limbs are longer, more slender and delicate. They are not for locomotion, but for manipulation and expression. These limbs are almost unarmored, their flesh translucent and packed with a dense network of photophores—the light-producing cells. These are not simple beacons; they are instruments of impossible complexity.

The Voiceless have no mouths, no lungs, no vocal cords. They feed by extending delicate, feathery filaments from their undersides into the nutrient-rich water, absorbing the soupy broth directly. Sustenance is a passive, constant act, like breathing. Their heads are smooth and featureless, an unblemished teardrop of the same dark shell as their body. They have no eyes. Or rather, their entire being is an eye. Their skin, particularly the soft tissue of their expressive limbs, is covered in millions of microscopic pits, each one a hyper-sensitive receptor tuned to the faint pulses of bioluminescence that are the only reality here. They do not see in images, formed by reflected light. They read the syntax of the glow. They perceive the world as a symphony of shifting, colored patterns; a language they do not need to learn, for it is the very medium of their consciousness.

Their mind is not a monologue inside a skull. It is an extension of their body. To think is to create light. To communicate is to reveal that thought to others. When a Voiceless is alone and contemplating, its four expressive limbs will writhe slowly, weaving intricate, complex patterns of soft gold and silver light for itself alone. A memory might be a slow-blooming fractal of sapphire. A question might be a rapid, strobing pulse of green that interrogates the darkness.

When two meet on the sheer face of a spire, they do not greet each other with sound. They intertwine their slender limbs, and a silent, blindingly complex conversation takes place. Cascades of amber light flow down one arm and are answered by rippling waves of violet on another. They share knowledge, warnings, philosophies in a display of pure, visible thought. They can tell a story—of the near-miss with a Veiled Hunter, the discovery of a new thermal vent garden, the slow, beautiful death of an ancient pulse-bell—with a richness and emotional depth that spoken words could never capture. An individual's entire identity, its history and personality, is written in the unique cadence and signature patterns of its light. They know each other not by face, but by the rhythm of their soul made visible.

They are the city's gardeners and curators. They tend the feathery, amber-glowing filter-feeders on the balconies, ensuring they are not being choked by debris. They gently guide the schools of obsidian slivers away from the lairs of predators. They understand the city's slow, geological life. They feel the deep, metabolic thrum of the Builders beneath them not as a vibration, but as a foundational rhythm, the beat to which their entire civilization pulses. It is their godhead, their metronome, their source. The growth of a new spire is a religious event, celebrated by districts of Voiceless who gather to paint the dark water with silent, choral displays of reverent light.

They are born in the deep alcoves. An adult will secrete a cluster of gelatinous eggs into a protected cavity, then weave a cocoon of its own light around them. This light is not just for warmth or protection; it is a primer. For weeks, the parent imprints the young with the basic syntax of their language, the history of their lineage, and a map of the surrounding spires. When they hatch, they are tiny, almost mindless versions of their parents, their light-producing limbs capable only of faint, simple flickers. They spend their youth clinging to the spires, learning to modulate their glow, to form the complex sentences of light that will allow them to join the Great Conversation.

Their existence is one of profound tranquility and sudden, silent terror. They fear the blankness, the non-expression of a Veiled Hunter. They fear the brute, meaningless red glow of the scavengers' eyes. Their greatest fear is not death, but isolation—to be unable to speak, to have one's light fail. Death itself is a quiet affair. An old Voiceless, its light dimming, will often crawl to a high, exposed precipice. Its final act is to release its remaining energy in one last, brilliant, complex pattern—a farewell, a summary, a signature—before its limbs go still and its body tumbles into the chasms. There, the red-eyed scavengers will find it, and its matter will be re-integrated into the system. Its final thought, a fleeting masterpiece of light, will become a memory for those who witnessed it, another verse in the silent, eternal poem of the city in the dark.
