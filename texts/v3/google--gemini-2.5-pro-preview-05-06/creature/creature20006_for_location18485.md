They who dwell here are not so much inhabitants as they are a geologic process given form, a living expression of the chamber's perfect stasis. They are the chamber's thoughts, made solid and slow. To see one is to almost miss it, for they are made of the same materials as their home.

Their form is vaguely humanoid, but attenuated, as if stretched by gravity or drawn out over immense spans of time. They are tall and impossibly slender, with limbs that move with the unhurried grace of something that has never known haste. Their skin is not skin as the surface world knows it. It is a smooth, seamless membrane, cool to the touch and perpetually slick with the same mineral-rich moisture that weeps from the crystal walls. It is translucent, the color of water in a deep well, faintly tinged with the milky whites of calcite and the smoky grays of quartz. In the pearlescent gloom, their bodies seem to capture and hold the light, glowing faintly from within, much like the pillars among which they move. Through their skin, you can perceive the faint, darker outlines of their bone structure, which is not bone but a denser, more crystalline form of the same substance as their flesh.

Their heads are smooth and ovoid, featureless save for two details. They have no mouth in the traditional sense, but a small, vertical slit at the base of the throat that pulses almost imperceptibly as they "breathe" the heavy, saturated air. And they have eyes. Their eyes are wide and dark, perfectly round, holding no pupil or iris, only a depth. They are like the still pools on the chamber floor, surfaces of absolute black that reflect the dim light with perfect clarity. They do not blink; there is no dust to trouble them, no harsh light to avoid. They simply watch, their gaze patient and unnervingly placid.

They move in utter silence. Their long, bare feet, the soles polished to a dark sheen like river stones, make no sound on the worn crystal paths. They glide through the forest of pillars, their narrow forms allowing them to slip through gaps that would be impassable for a broader creature. Their movement is a kind of slow-motion ballet, deliberate and without wasted energy. When one stops, it becomes indistinguishable from a particularly slender, oddly shaped crystal formation, holding a pose of absolute stillness that can last for what the outside world would measure as hours or days.

Their life is one of pure, passive consumption. They do not hunt or cultivate. Their sustenance comes from the chamber itself. Periodically, one will approach the central basin, its hands—long-fingered and elegant, the palms worn as smooth as the paths—dipping into the heavy, clear water. They do not drink in gulps. They cup the water and slowly, reverently, anoint their own skin with it. Their semi-permeable flesh absorbs the liquid, drawing in the dissolved minerals that are their only nourishment. This is their food, their lifeblood. It is a slow, steady process of renewal, molecule by molecule. They are quite literally built from the water that drips from the ceiling. On very rare occasions, one might be seen crouched by a crystal shelf, its attention fixed. With a movement so slow it is almost imperceptible, a long finger might detach one of the large, silverfish-like creatures. It is not eaten, but held gently in the palm, where it is slowly absorbed over a great length of time, a rare and complex seasoning to their mineral diet.

Their minds, if they can be called that, are mirrors of the chamber. They do not experience time as a progression. There are no days, no seasons, no sense of past, present, or future as distinct states. For them, there is only the
*is*. Their memory is not a story; it is a stratum. An event does not pass; it simply becomes a deeper layer in their consciousness. Their thoughts are not frantic streams of logic or emotion, but slow, crystalline formations of awareness. They are aware of every drip, every condensation, every subtle shift in the internal glow of the stones.

They are solitary but not alone. They move along the same paths, sleep in the same hollows scooped from the crystal bases, and drink from the same basin, but their interactions are minimal and profound. A glance held for a century. The deliberate yielding of a path. A shared stillness in the presence of a particularly beautiful resonance from a dripping stalactite. They have no language of sound, for that would be an intrusion upon the chamber's liquid static. Perhaps they communicate through the subtle shifting of the light within their own bodies, patterns of internal phosphorescence that are as slow and meaningful as the waxing and waning of the chamber's own glow.

They are the tenders, the curators of the stasis. The worn paths, the smooth basin, the polished mirrors on the pillars—these are not relics of a forgotten past. They are projects that are eternally ongoing. An individual might spend a thousand years grinding one facet of a crystal, not to change it, but to better understand it, to align its surface with a particular quality of the light. They are the living will of the chamber, ensuring its equilibrium is never disturbed.

They do not procreate in a biological sense; they are born of the place. When a new one is needed, perhaps once an eon, the minerals in a deep pool might begin to slowly, over millennia, accrete. Guided by the will of the others and the chamber itself, a new form will slowly coalesce and rise from the water, already ancient the moment it takes its first silent step. They do not die; they dissolve. When a life has reached its term, a being will find a remote pool, wade into it, and lie down. Over an immeasurable span, it will slowly un-become, its crystalline structure dissolving back into the water, its substance rejoining the great, closed cycle, ready to form the next drop of water, the next layer of rime, the next pulse of light in a living kinsman.

They are the stillness given a form, the silence given a purpose. They are the heart of the equilibrium, and their beat is the slow, steady fall of a single drop of water.
