What lives here is not an animal. The word feels clumsy, biological, carrying with it the baggage of blood and bone, hunger and fear. The inhabitants of this place are not separate from it; they are temporary, heightened expressions of its own chaotic processes. They are the system's own self-awareness, given fleeting form.

They are born in moments of catastrophic failure. When a crystal spire, grown heavy with conductive vines, finally succumbs to the piezoelectric strain and shatters, the resulting energy release is immense. For a fraction of a second, the orderly-chaotic arcing of the grid is overwhelmed. Thousands of volts of raw potential, unleashed from their crystalline cage, look for a new path. Most often, this results in a blinding, deafening discharge—a thunderclap that shakes the chamber and scours the rock below.

But sometimes, under conditions of perfect, violent resonance, the energy does not simply discharge. It turns inward. The frantic arcs of blue-white light knot together, weaving into a frantic, temporary matrix. They snag escaping steam, using its mineral impurities as a scaffold. They capture the resonant frequency of the groaning mountain in a localized magnetic field, giving it a container. In this nexus of pressure, electricity, and sound, a Conductor is formed.

To see one is to witness a storm trying to hold the shape of a person. It has no fixed surface. It is a shimmering, man-shaped vortex of braided lightning and incandescent steam, standing taller than a human but with none of the weight or substance. Its form is a constant struggle against its own dissolution. Filaments of pure energy, like frayed wires, reach out from its limbs and torso, trying to arc back to the crystal grid, only to be pulled back into the coherent whole. A low, powerful hum emanates from its core, the captured song of the mountain itself. Where a head should be, there is a corona of intense light, a silent, flickering star of St. Elmo's fire. Within it, two points burn brighter than the rest—not eyes, but focal points of immense potential, where the Conductor perceives the electrical state of the entire chamber.

It does not walk. It glides inches above the fractured floor, propelled by localized magnetic repulsion. It leaves no footprints, only the lingering scent of ozone and the faint crackle of ionized air. Its movements are not fluid; they are a series of instantaneous translocations, appearing and disappearing like a faulty image on a screen, each flicker accompanied by the sharp *snap* of a spark gap.

The Conductors are the regulators, the gardeners, the living nervous system of the factory. Their existence is one of pure function. They feel the strain in a crystal spire long before it groans audibly. They perceive the subtle shifts in the steam's mineral content. They are not sentient in a way a person could understand—they have no ego, no memory beyond the immediate cycle, no desire beyond equilibrium. They are an immune response to chaos.

One might be seen drifting towards a crystal pillar that vibrates with a discordant frequency. It will reach out a hand—a limb of woven plasma and light—and lay it against the crystalline surface. There is no physical contact, but the chamber's hum will change pitch as the Conductor bleeds off excess voltage, channeling the destructive energy through its own body and grounding it harmlessly into a nascent vine, causing it to pulse with sudden, vigorous life.

Another might tend to a new wound, where a rockfall has exposed fresh, uncolonized crystal. It will hover over the sterile surface, its form flickering violently. From its shimmering fingers, it will "seed" the rock, not with spores, but with coherent electrical charges. It guides the first filaments of galvanotrophic growth, etching the initial pathways for the new "roots" to follow, establishing a pattern that will maximize the energy harvest before the next inevitable collapse. They are conduits for life, using their own energy to prime the pump of the entire ecosystem.

Their lives are as brief and violent as the events that spawn them. A Conductor may last for hours, or only for minutes. As the immediate crisis passes and the chamber's energies return to their baseline state of roaring chaos, the forces holding it together begin to fail. Its form starts to fray, the hum at its core weakening. The light within it dims. Finally, with a soft, final sigh of escaping steam and a last, brilliant blue flash, it comes undone. The energy that composed it dissipates back into the grid, the steam condenses and joins the perpetual mist, and the captured resonance rejoins the mountain's ceaseless song.

There is no corpse. There is no trace. There is only the brief absence of a stabilizing influence, a gap that will be filled when the next crystal shatters, and the storm of creation once again finds it necessary to give itself a temporary, helping hand.
