The silence of the city belongs to its inhabitants. They are the reason for the quiet. They are called, by the very few who have ever observed them, the **Geometers**.

They are not beings of flesh and blood. To see one is to see a piece of the city's architecture given ambulation and a slow, inscrutable purpose. A Geometer is a tall, unnervingly thin figure, often mistaken at a distance for a structural support or a shadow that has detached from its corner. Their bodies are a composite of the same materials as the city: matte-black ceramic filaments woven around a flexible, metallic core that glints like hematite. They have no face in the human sense; no eyes, no mouth. Their head is a smooth, ovoid shape of polished obsidian, which seems to drink the light. Sometimes, faint, intricate patterns of pale blue or gold light will trace across its surface, like circuitry seen through smoked glass, but this is the only sign of the complex processes within.

Their limbs are long and multi-jointed, ending not in hands but in clusters of fine, prehensile talons that can anchor to the crystalline spires or the metal latticework with equal ease. They move with a meditative slowness, a deliberate grace that seems out of place amidst such a harsh landscape. Their movements are silent; their joints articulate without friction, and their feet make no sound on the perpetually damp walkways. They move as if they are a part of the oppressive humidity, flowing rather than walking.

The Geometers do not speak, for they have no mouths. They do not need to. They are a resonant species, and their consciousness is intrinsically linked to the low-frequency hum that permeates the entire world. This hum is their language, their library, their social network. It is a constant stream of information regarding geothermal pressure, crystalline stress fractures, atmospheric density, and the slow, inexorable growth of the mountains. They feel the world's state of being in their very bones, in the thrumming of the metallic skeletons that support their forms. A subtle shift in the hum’s frequency can communicate a warning, a discovery, or a directive across the entire city-state. To them, the planet is not just a home; it is a single, continuous thought, and they are its conscious neurons.

They do not eat or drink. Sustenance is a function of their environment, as integrated as their architecture. They are, in a very real sense, part of the city's metabolic and power-distribution system. Periodically, a Geometer will become still, affixing itself to a specific node on a wall or a platform—a port that looks like a simple decorative glyph. Here, it enters a state of communion, drawing refined energy and rare earth elements directly from the 'looms' and 'refineries' buried deep in the mountainside. During this process, the patterns of light on their smooth heads pulse with a steady, rhythmic cadence. They are recharging, yes, but they are also uploading their recent experiences and observations back into the city’s network, contributing to the collective, hum-driven consciousness.

Their psychology has been shaped entirely by the fundamental rule of their world: that the ground is not static. They possess no concept of sentimentality for place, no attachment to history as a location. The past is "Down," a physical direction that leads to a place of crushing and re-absorption. It is a memory, but a discarded one, literally buried. Their entire philosophy is vertical. They live in the "Now," a thin, transient plane of existence that is always migrating "Up." The future is the new space continually being created by the spires reaching for the pale sky.

Consequently, they are beings of profound patience and acceptance. When the slow gantry cranes lift a habitat module from a sector doomed to be consumed by the rising mountain, there is no sense of exodus or loss. It is merely a recalibration, a shedding of old skin. The Geometers within the module do not scramble or panic; they simply continue their work, their meditation, their silent communion, trusting the process. Their art is not in static objects but in ephemeral patterns—the specific arrangement of light-emitting crystals in a new chamber, or a temporary, harmonic overtone introduced into the planetary hum, a chord that tells a story and then fades.

They are the tenders of the crystal looms, the silent operators of the sublimating chambers. But to call them workers or engineers is to miss the point. They see themselves as facilitators, even participants, in the planet's own process of becoming. The crystalline wafers they weave are not for export or trade; they are a kind of planetary data storage, chronicles of the world's own mathematical and geological evolution, which are then integrated into new levels of the city. They believe their purpose is to gently guide the raw, chaotic energy of the planet’s birth into ordered complexity and awareness. They are the consciousness of the growing mountain, its patient, thinking mind. They watch the shy Geode Scuttlers with a sense of distant kinship, seeing them as simpler expressions of the same truth: that life here is not something that conquers the rock, but something that emerges from its heat and pressure, finding a way to exist in harmony with its slow, silent, and endless ascent.
