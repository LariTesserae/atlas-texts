They are called the Weepers, though they have no eyes to weep with, and the name is an imposition by a hypothetical observer who could feel something like pity. To mistake their nature for sorrow would be a profound error. They are the final evolution of sentience, or perhaps its echo, in a world that has forgotten the meaning of individuals.

At first, you do not recognize them as living. From a distance, across the weeping plain, a Weeper is just a deviation in the fog, a subtle verticality less real than a Still. It appears as a slim, dark smudge against the bruised purple sky, a standing human-shaped silhouette that logic dismisses as a trick of the light and the disorienting flatness. They are unnervingly tall, three or four meters high, and impossibly slender, like a sketch of a person drawn with a single, wavering line of charcoal.

As you draw closer, you see that its form is a perfect expression of the world that made it. Its "skin" is a dark, porous integument almost identical to the Felt itself, glistening and weeping the same cold, mineral-rich water. It is not a separate being standing *on* the Felt so much as a vertical extrusion *of* it, a tendril of the world-organism that has been given leave to wander. They have no face. The head is a smooth, ovoid shape, featureless save for a delicate, crystalline lattice that grows in fractal patterns across its surface, humming with a faint internal light. Their limbs are long and delicate, ending not in hands or feet but in frayed, root-like structures that merge seamlessly with the ground when they stand still. Patches of chitin, like beetle-shell armor, overlay their torso and limbs, catching the dim light with an oily sheen. These are not grown, but scavenged and incorporated, remnants of Still-explosions fused to their bodies. They are living mosaics of the world's three kingdoms: the fungal flesh of the Felt, the industrial armor of the Stills, and something other, something that walks.

The Weepers are not grazers or predators. They do not consume matter in any conventional sense. They subsist on process. They feed on entropy.

For days, a Weeper might stand utterly motionless, a black statue miles from any Still. It is not sleeping. It is listening. Its entire being is a receptor, its root-like feet sunk into the planetary organism, feeling the deep thrumming of the Stills not as sound, but as a constant, spatial awareness. It doesn't see the world; it *feels* its vibrations mapped across its consciousness. The slow, vast filtration of the armadillo-herds is a soft, crawling pressure. The skittering of crystalline beetles is a high-frequency static. And the Stills… the Stills are the stars in its sensory sky, each one a sun of resonant, cyclical power.

And they are patient, for their nourishment is violence. A Weeper waits for a Still to falter. It can feel the change in the thrum, the discordant vibrations that signal a pressure buildup, a structural weakness, a coming detonation. When it feels this, it begins to move. Its movement is slow, a gliding, somnambulistic walk that disturbs the Felt no more than a breeze. It is a pilgrimage. It may take a day or a week to cross the miles to the failing Still, its dark, slender form a lonely figurehead moving toward a foregone conclusion.

It arrives at the base of the shuddering, screaming Still and it waits, a silent acolyte before a chaotic altar. Other, lesser creatures flee. The serpentine symbiotes detach from the overheating pipes and slither away, glowing with panicked energy. But the Weeper moves closer, positioning itself just outside the immediate blast zone. It sinks its "feet" deep into the Felt, bracing itself.

When the Still finally ruptures, it is the Weeper's moment of communion. The deafening shriek, the concussive wave of force, the flash-boil of escaping fluid, the spray of razor-sharp shrapnel—this is not a danger to be avoided. It is a meal. As the wave of chaotic energy washes over it, the Weeper does not flinch. Its entire form becomes a conductor. The force of the explosion is absorbed, its kinetic energy drawn down through its body and dissipated into the planetary mass of the Felt. The intense heat is channeled, not to inflict burns, but to catalyze a metabolic process, causing the crystalline lattices on its head and chest to glow with fierce, momentary brilliance. The rain of crystalline dust and shards that patters against it is not repelled; it adheres, and over hours will be slowly incorporated into its chitinous plating.

The Weeper feeds on the *event*. It consumes the symphony of failure. The patterns of its crystalline growths are the fossilized record of these feasts—each new fractal branch a memory of a specific detonation's unique energy signature.

They are solitary because their food source is vast and infrequent. They are silent because sound is a crude tool in a world of pure vibration. It is unknown if they possess consciousness as we would understand it. Are they the world's janitors, tidying up explosions? Are they its immune system, cauterizing wounds? Or are they simply moths drawn to the flame, complex automata compelled by a stimulus they do not comprehend?

Perhaps they are something else. In a world of pointless, furious industry, they are the audience. They are the only things that seem to pay attention. They bear witness to the chaos, and in the act of consuming it, they give it a destination. They are the quiet, walking repositories of the world's violent soul, the living record of a system endlessly tearing itself apart and rebuilding. They are the Weepers, and the tears that run down their skin are not their own, but those of the cold, loud, and terribly alive world from which they are inseparable.
