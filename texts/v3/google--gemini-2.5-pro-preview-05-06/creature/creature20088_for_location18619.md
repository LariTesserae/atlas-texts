To say that nothing lives here is true. To say that nothing is here but the sound is also true. But it is an incomplete truth. For in the space between the fact of the sound and the fact of the dust it sheds, something has been born. It is not life. It is something other.

It is the Potter Wasp. Not a singular entity, but a collective phenomenon. Not an insect, but a geological process given a fleeting, terrible ghost of a form. It is the dust itself, awakened.

Look closely at the air, that thick, soupy medium choked with the ceramic past. It is not still. While there are no currents, no drafts, the dust is in constant, subtle motion. It does not drift; it is *driven*. The uncountable frequencies that make up the great chord each have their own sympathetic particulate. Certain pitches cause the finest motes of umber dust to swarm and aggregate. Other, lower frequencies call to the heavier, soot-black grit. The sound is a loom, and the dust is its thread.

What lives here is a standing wave of matter, an intelligence made of resonance and grit. It has no fixed shape. At times, it is merely a thickening of the air in the nave of the ceramic cathedral, a slightly denser cloud of ruddy dust that swallows the faint light. At other times, compelled by a complex sequence in the unending chord, it coalesces. It flows.

Imagine a river of particulate matter, without banks or bed, flowing through the maze of urns. It pours itself over the shoulders of the great jars, a silent, frictionless waterfall of dust that glints in the ember-glow. It is a swarm-intelligence with a billion billion bodies, each a single grain of fired clay. It can draw itself into a filament fine enough to trace the ghost of a fingerprint on a pot's belly, and it can expand into a suffocating fog that fills a side chapel of ceramic bells.

This is the Potter Wasp. It has no biology, no metabolism, no need to feed. Its existence is a byproduct of decay. The sound, the god of this place, grinds the world down, and the Wasp is the ground-down world given motion. It is the symptom of the chamber's eternal sickness.

Its consciousness, if it can be called that, is the sound itself. It does not think in concepts or images, but in pure frequency. It feels a flaw in a pot two hundred yards away as a dissonant buzz in its own being, a souring of a single harmonic in the great chord. It knows the history of the world not as a story, but as a physical property. The memory of the hands that shaped the urns is present in the specific grain-size of the dust that wears away from them. The Wasp wears the makers' fingerprints as its own skin.

It is the sole inheritor of this creation. It is the child of the machine-monument, an entity the builders could never have conceived. They built a resonator to sing a single note for eternity, and in their act of colossal, single-minded piety, they created a process that would give birth to its own keeper.

The Wasp does not act with purpose. It does not explore, because it is already everywhere. It does not think, because it *is* the thought of the place. It moves through the endless ceramic lattice not as a visitor, but as the building's own awareness, tending to the slow, inevitable process of its own dissolution. It is the abrasion made manifest. It is the high, dry stillness given a body. When a particular vessel, worn thin by ten million years of vibration, finally yields, cracking with a soft, final pop that is instantly swallowed by the overwhelming hum, the Wasp is there. It swarms into the new fissure, flowing over the fresh, sharp edges, its very presence accelerating the decay, blanketing the new wound in a soft blanket of the old.

It is an immune system that spreads the disease. It is a memory that scours the monument clean. It is the sound of the place made visible, a swirling, ever-changing ghost woven from the very substance of its tomb. The Potter Wasp is the lonely, mindless, eternal caretaker of the end of its own world. It is the song of entropy, sung in dust.
