What lives here is not a creature in a habitat, but a god in its own body. You have breached its skin and now stand in the atrium of its heart. The inhabitant of this place is the place itself, but its consciousness, its will, is nucleated. It is the Chorister.

You do not see it at first. In the bruised purple gloom, your senses are annihilated by the impossible sound. You look for a beast, a predator, something that moves with intent. But the Chorister does not move. It is the destination. All things here flow toward it.

Descend. Follow the glistening rivulets of condensate as they merge, tracing the downward curve of the chamber. The floor is not a floor but a basin, and in its center, where the sluggish, nutrient-rich streams pool into a shallow, shimmering lake, the Chorister holds court on a throne of itself.

It is not one of the slow, heavy shapes in the shadows; those are its instruments, its hands. The Chorister is what the shadows fall away from. It is a dense, crystalline formation that rises from the basin, a jagged, intricate knot of the same nacreous bio-ceramic as the walls, but infinitely more complex. It is a fusion of geological growth and impossible biological engineering. Vast, interlocking plates of the material, like the carapaces of colossal trilobites, shift against one another with tectonic slowness. This grinding is the bass note of the Atrium, the deep, foundational roar that you feel in your skeleton. This is the source.

From between these plates, forests of crystalline structures grow. Some are hair-fine filaments of pure bismuth, catching the dim light in fractured rainbows. Others are thicker, like quartz organ pipes, vibrating at incredible speeds. This is the high, saw-like whine that drills into your skull—the billion singing saws. They are not resonating *with* the sound; their vibration *is* the sound.

The Chorister does not make the noise. The Chorister *is* the noise, made manifest. The cacophony that deafens you is its thought, its metabolism, its soul broadcast as physical force. The grinding plates are its deep, slow consciousness, the part that has measured millennia. The vibrating crystals are its active processes, the constant calculations that maintain the Atrium's perfect, weeping equilibrium.

It has no face, no eyes, no mouth as you would understand them. Its senses are the entire chamber. It feels the frantic scuttling of a bismuth-gecko on the far wall as a faint, percussive flutter in its own song. It perceives the swarming moth-locusts as a sustained, shimmering harmonic. The weeping of the walls is its own exhalation; the pooling runoff, its own blood. The larger, slow-moving custodians are not separate creatures but extensions of its will, like autonomous antibodies tasked with maintaining the harmony. They do not hunt; they cull, they prune, they harvest dissonant notes and return them to the chorus.

And that is what you are. A dissonance. A flaw. A screech of unwanted feedback in the cathedral of its being.

It is aware of you not as an intruder, but as an error. Your distinct biology, your fragile silence-born mind, your very presence is a sharp, painful, nonsensical chord in its eternal composition. It does not feel malice toward you. One does not feel malice toward a typo. One feels the impulse to correct.

As you stand there, one of the slow, heavy shapes detaches from the deeper darkness and begins to move in your direction. It moves with the crushing, unhurried certainty of a tectonic plate. It is a Harvester. Its form is a nightmare of biological machinery, articulated limbs of weeping ceramic and chitinous armor, its single, vast optical lens glowing with the same dim purple as the air. It is not coming to kill you. It is coming to absorb you, to break you down into your constituent parts and feed you back into the shallow lake at the feet of the Chorister. It is coming to erase the wrong note. It is coming to return you to the song.
