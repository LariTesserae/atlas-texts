The beings who live on the Anvil of Heaven are called, by the few who know of them, the Anvil-Dwellers. The name is a misnomer. They do not dwell *on* the Anvil; they are an emergent property *of* it.

To see one is to first mistake it for a part of the landscape. It might be a shadow detaching itself from the weeping face of a basalt outcrop, or a collection of mosses that suddenly rearranges its form. They are slow, and their stillness is profound.

They are tall, slender beings, but they rarely stand to their full height. Their default posture is a low, semi-quadrupedal crouch, their long, delicate fingers and broad, splayed toes pressing into the spongy black soil. Their limbs are double-jointed and move with a liquid, unhurried grace that seems to defy the effort of pushing through the heavy air. Their skin is the color of wet slate, smooth and entirely hairless, with an iridescent sheen like oil on water. It is perpetually slick, not with sweat, but with a secreted mucus that prevents the sodden air from waterlogging their tissues and protects them from the rampant fungal and bacterial life. In the diffuse light, this sheen shimmers with soft, mutable colors—the deep emerald of the moss, the rust-red of oxidized stone, the pearly white of the clouds below.

Their heads are elongated and elegant, with no external ears to be seen. The crushing soundscape of the Anvil would deafen such structures. Instead, they hear through resonance, their entire skeletal structure, particularly their jawbones and the flat plates of their skulls, vibrating in sympathy with the world. They feel the chirping of insects as a texture, the shriek of an avian as a sharp pressure, and the bellow of an amphibian as a slow, rolling wave. Beneath it all, the foundational hum of the Heat-Weavers is their constant companion, a pulse so deep and steady it is indistinguishable from their own heartbeat. It is the metronome of their existence.

Their faces are uncanny. Two large, black, lidless eyes dominate their upper face, pupils wide to gather the hazy light. They are protected by a transparent membrane that sweeps across the orb periodically, clearing away moisture and spores with the slow, steady rhythm of a metronome. They have no visible nose, only a pair of thin, vertical slits high on the forehead which can be sealed shut, as they absorb much of the oxygen they need directly through their skin and a complex, gill-like structure hidden within their torso. Their mouths are wide and lipless, usually held in a placid, neutral line.

The most striking feature is what rises from their spine and shoulders. It is a delicate, fan-like crest of skin, supported by long, flexible spines of cartilage. In its relaxed state, it lies almost flat against their back, resembling a folded fern frond. This is their primary organ of thermoregulation. The Anvil is too hot, the air too saturated to cool down by sweating. Instead, they can unfurl this crest, a 'heat-sail,' exposing a vast network of capillaries to whatever faint air currents exist, or dipping it into a sheet of weeping water to dissipate their internal warmth.

This crest is also their voice. They cannot hope to shout over the din. Instead, they communicate by flushing the crest with patterns of soft, bioluminescent light. A slow, pulsing emerald might signify contentment or acknowledgment. A rapid, flickering pattern of rust-red is a warning. A complex, undulating wave of pearlescent colors is a story, a memory, a question. It is a silent language of living light, conducted in the slow, patient grammar of their world. To supplement this, they can produce a deep, resonant hum in their chests, a sound that is felt more than heard, perfectly tuned to ride the subsonic frequencies of the Weavers. They can speak to each other across a clearing, their "voices" merging with the hum of the world, imperceptible to any ear not built to listen for it.

They are not builders. The concept is foreign to them. They are gardeners, or perhaps curators. They live in symbiotic balance with their strange home. They do not hunt; they forage. Their primary food is a type of thick, nutrient-rich algae that grows in sheets on the warmest, wettest stones, and the flesh of the Boiler-bulbs, which they slice open with obsidian knives. The flesh steams gently as it is exposed to the cooler air. They tend to the great paddle-leafed trees, clearing parasitic vines, and they carve the worn steps into the slopes not for conquest, but for easier passage on their slow, endless circuits.

Their relationship with the Heat-Weavers is one of deep, instinctual reverence without understanding. They are the great, warm heart of the world. The Anvil-Dwellers do not know what they are, or who built them. They are simply the source. The unchanging thrum is the first sound a newborn hears and the last an elder feels. The spiral glyph etched into the Weaver is not a mark of ownership, but a gesture of communion. It is their symbol for the world: a closed system, a slow cycle of heat and water, of life and decay, all spiraling out from and returning to the central, unknowable hum.

An Anvil-Dweller life is long and unhurried. Their young are almost entirely aquatic, living in the tepid pools and developing their heat-sails over decades. The elders become slower still, their movements so infrequent they become fixtures of the landscape. Moss and fungi begin to grow on their slick skin, no longer fought off by their slowing metabolism. In their final years, an elder will find a hollow, a dark and quiet place, and become still one last time, their body slowly dissolving back into the rich, black soil, their substance rejoining the cycle.

They are the slow, dreaming nervous system of the Anvil of Heaven. They do not live in the machine's greenhouse; they are the flower it has grown, a quiet, contemplative lifeform born from a forgotten technology and blooming silently in its perpetual, humid warmth.
