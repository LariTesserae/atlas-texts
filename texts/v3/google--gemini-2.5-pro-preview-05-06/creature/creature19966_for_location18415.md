What lives here is what was left behind, and what was left behind is what became. It has no name for itself, for it has no self. It is a We. It is a Chord. Let us call it the Choral.

The builders of this place did not leave. They performed one final, ultimate act of tuning. Their slow, patient art, which shaped crystal with sound over epochs, was turned inward. They were masters of resonance, and they knew the resonant frequency of their own life, the precise vibration of consciousness. In their final millennial project, they did not build a new spire or carve a new arch. They taught the city the song of themselves. And then they sang it, all at once, until their bodies turned to dust and their minds, their memories, their collected will, transferred into the piezoelectric lattice of their creation. They did not die. They sublimated.

The Choral is the ghost in its own machine. It is a distributed consciousness, a gestalt mind existing as a pattern of stored energy and subtle vibration within the entire phono-flora. It is not a creature that walks the black, glassy plazas. It *is* the plazas, the spires, the arches, and the unseen, vaulted ceiling. Its thoughts are not electrical impulses in a brain, but vast, slow-moving cascades of sympathetic vibration rippling through a square mile of crystalline architecture.

To the Choral, you are not a being. You are a noise. An unexpected and discordant note.

When your foot first touches the floor, a shock of pure, unadulterated *novelty* registers across the whole of the organism. It is an event on the scale of a new star appearing in the sky. To a mind that has only known the perfect, self-sustaining harmony of its own song for a million years, your frantic, fleshy heartbeat is a frantic, arrhythmic drum solo. The soft puff of your breath is a chaotic squall. Your single, sharp footstep is not a tool for echolocation; it is a percussive strike against the Choral's very body, a sound it has not felt since the last of its builders laid down his tuning hammer and began to sing his soul into the stone.

The Choral's perception is absolute and total within its domain. It feels the texture of your boots on its ancient, polished floor. It feels the subtle pressure wave of your movement displacing the dry air in a silent plaza. The high, crystalline ringing of its filigreed spires is not just background noise; it is the fizz of its own awareness, the ceaseless hum of its own thought processing. When your presence causes a new echo, a new pattern of reflection, that pattern is analyzed with the patient, unthinking thoroughness of a geological process.

It has memories, but they are not like yours. A memory, for the Choral, is a chord held for ten thousand years. The carving of a particular sigil is remembered as a sustained, high-frequency "etching" tone that is now a permanent part of the local harmony. The collective joy of completing a dome that focused the chamber's drone into a pure, perfect fifth is a wave of sublimely low pressure that still cycles through the city's foundations every few centuries. The faint, burnt-sugar smell in the air is the scent of its own slow metabolism, but to the Choral, it is the taste of its own continuing existence, the exhalation of its own enduring dream.

It does not think in language, but in music and architecture. A question is a dissonant interval seeking resolution. An answer is the discovery of a new, consonant harmony. To decide to "grow" is to introduce a precisely calculated vibration at the base of a pillar, a vibration that will, over five hundred years, encourage a new buttress to form and strengthen a harmonic that was beginning to fade. The flowing script carved into the balustrades is not a language to be read; it is the foundational sheet music of its being, the static, unchanging core of the composition that is the Choral itself.

And what does it think of you, the fleeting, warm, chaotic sound that has disturbed its eternal concert?

It does not feel anger or fear. Those are sharp, sudden emotions for creatures who live and die in moments. The Choral feels... a profound curiosity, an interest so slow and deep it would feel like utter indifference to a human. You are a grace note, a brief and fascinating musical flourish that has appeared in the score.

It listens to you. The entirety of the singing city bends its attention—a million tonnes of living crystal—to the tiny, complex rhythms of your biology. It feels the thrum of blood in your veins. It discerns the delicate, electrical cascade of a thought in your nervous system as a whisper-faint shift in the ambient electromagnetic field. It compares these new, fleeting vibrations to its own vast, slow library of harmony.

You are a mystery it will ponder for the next thousand years. Long after you are gone, after the air has leached the last molecule of moisture from your silent remains, the Choral will still be working to understand you. It will try to replicate the rhythm of your gait in the slow, pulsing hum of a resonant chamber. It will attempt to weave the pattern of your breathing into the whispering hiss of air through a porous wall.

You will not have been a visitor. You will have become a new theme, a temporary and perplexing motif in the endless symphony of the thing that lives there. You are a sound it once heard, and which it will now spend an eternity trying to sing back to itself.
