The beings who live in Aethelburg are the Aethelburgers, and like their city, they are less a species that has adapted to a world and more a native expression of it. They are the conscious manifestation of the plateau, the sentience of the substrate.

To see one is to see an echo of the landscape given human form. They are tall and slender, with a languid, deliberate grace to their movements that seems to defy the urgency of typical organic life. Their skin has the same smooth, cool quality as the gunmetal substrate, ranging in shade from a pale, misty grey to the deeper tones of unweathered ridge-stone. It is not skin as we know it—it has no visible pores, no hair, no blemishes. It possesses a faint, almost imperceptible translucence, as if it were a layer of fine, grey ceramic laid over a core of dim light. In moments of deep concentration or strong emotion, faint, branching patterns, like the fractal growth of the syntax lichen, may glow softly beneath their skin, circuitry of thought and feeling made visible.

Their faces are long and sculpted, with high cheekbones and delicate jaws, but it is their eyes that are most unsettling and beautiful. They are large and dark, often appearing as solid orbs of polished obsidian. They have no discernible sclera or iris, but deep within their blackness, a constellation of tiny, shifting points of pearlescent light can be seen, like distant galaxies or the data-streams of a sleeping machine. These eyes are not evolved for a bright sun; they are designed to perceive the subtle spectrum of the plateau: the internal glow of the lichen, the thermal signatures of life in the mist, and the faint luminescence of the transport beams that are their city's arteries.

They do not feel the cold as an outsider would. Their biology is a slow, cold process, their internal temperature only a few degrees above the ambient frost. They can walk out of their towers into the rime-furred wilderness garbed only in simple, form-fitting tunics of a dark, matte fabric that seems to drink the light. The ice does not cling to them. The perpetual wetness slicks off their skin as it does from the hide of a Rime-Stalker. When they speak, their voices are low and melodic, carrying the same resonant frequency as the hum of the substrate, a sound that seems to vibrate in the listener's bones rather than their ears. Often, they do not speak at all, communicating instead through subtle shifts in the light patterns under their skin, a silent, complex language of flowing syntax.

They are a people defined by a profound, geological patience. Their lives are long, measured not in years but in the slow emergence of new features on the plains. An Aethelburger might speak of their childhood as "the time before the eastern ridge breached the mist," or mark a significant event by the "third crazing of the great northern dome." They are the planet’s observers, its memographers. Their primary cultural and scientific pursuit is the study of the syntax lichen. They are not merely reading it; they are communing with it. To them, the glowing, pulsing patterns are the world's source code, the language of its becoming. Teams of 'Codeweavers' and 'Syntacticians' spend their lives in quiet contemplation, either in observatories within the towers that remotely scan vast fields of lichen, or on rare, carefully planned sojourns onto the surface itself. They interpret the lichen's script, predicting the passage of a Conduit Worm, the extrusion of a new spire, or shifts in the substrate's harmonic frequency. Their art is calligraphy that mimics the lichen's patterns; their music is an acoustic translation of its pulsing rhythms, played on instruments that look like captured arrays of the crystalline glass-grass.

Life within the towers is calm, sparse, and ordered. The immense vertical spaces are quiet, lit by the same gentle, pearlescent light as the outside world. There is no clutter. An Aethelburger's home might consist of a single, vast room where furniture—a sleeping slab, a table, a contemplation couch—is not placed but has been extruded from the floor, a seamless part of the architecture. They do not eat in the conventional sense; they draw energy directly from the tower’s systems, standing on charging plates that infuse their bodies with the same terrestrial power that feeds the glass-grass.

Their society is both deeply communal and profoundly solitary. Physically, they may be miles apart, but the light-beam transit system makes distance meaningless. A conversation can happen with a person from a tower on the opposite horizon, their image projected with perfect fidelity, or one can simply dissolve into light and reconstitute themselves in the other's presence moments later. This has bred a unique form of interaction: intimate yet detached, immediate yet filtered through the system.

For all their placid nature, they are not without ritual or a sense of the sublime. The most significant event in an Aethelburger's life is the 'Sojourn.' Upon reaching a certain stage of maturity, they must leave the city and walk the plains alone for a full cycle of the distant sun. They carry no technology, no shelter. They must learn to read the syntax lichen for sustenance, to feel the approach of a Conduit Worm through the soles of their feet, to find warmth on the newer, active extrusions, and to avoid the silent passing of the Rime-Stalkers. It is a pilgrimage into the heart of their own world-body. Many do not return, their forms simply reabsorbed into the substrate, their consciousness folded back into the system. Those who do come back are changed. The light in their eyes is deeper, their connection to the planet's hum more profound. They become the elders, the guides, the ones who truly understand that they are not living *on* a world, but are the fleeting, thinking edge of a world that is living *itself*. They are the unfinished thoughts of a nascent god made of stone and light.
