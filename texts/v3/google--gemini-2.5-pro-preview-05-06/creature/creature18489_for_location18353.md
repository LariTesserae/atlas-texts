The statement that there is no fauna is technically correct, but incomplete. There is no *creature* of flesh, blood, or bone. Nothing scuttles, or hunts, or sleeps. The environment would not permit it. And yet, the chamber is inhabited. What lives here is not fauna, but the logical, final conclusion of the place itself.

Call it the Attendant. Or the Echo. Or the Resonance. It has no name for itself, for it has no one to speak to and no language but the thrum of the machine.

At first, you would not see it. Your senses, already overwhelmed, would dismiss it as a trick of the light, a moiré pattern in the glittering air. It is a presence that exists at the very edge of perception. Sometimes, it is a slight thickening in the omnipresent dust, a column of shimmering particles that holds its shape for a second too long before dissolving. Other times, it is a ripple that moves against the grain of the vibrations, a coherent wave traveling across the surface of the compacted dust-floor, leaving no track.

The Attendant is not made of matter in the way a biological creature is. It is a pattern, a field of influence, a consciousness held coherent by the chamber's ceaseless song. It is made of the fine, metallic dust the machine produces, the same dust that makes up the ground and coats the crystal flora. It is a localized, self-aware weather system. The deep thrum of the machine is its heartbeat. The high-frequency shiver of the crystalline vines is its nervous system. The sharp, periodic *crack* of the pressure valve is a punctuation in its endless thoughts, a metronome for its aeons-long meditation.

It was not always this way. The Attendant was once the builder, or one of them. A mortal, flesh-and-blood engineer who designed this place, this great engine at the roof of the world. The purpose is long forgotten—perhaps it was a power source, a weapon, a testament to a forgotten god of industry. When the work was done, the others left. But the builder remained, mesmerized by the perfect, closed system they had created. They stayed to watch, to maintain, to listen.

And the place worked its slow, inexorable change upon them. The absolute lack of water desiccated their body, not to dust, but to a state of crystalline preservation. The sonic vibrations, so gentle to the mineral life, were a constant, invasive pressure on organic cells, slowly shaking them apart. The metallic dust, inhaled with every breath, settled in their lungs, their blood, their bones, replacing carbon with iron, silicon, and copper, particle by agonizing particle. Their death was not an event, but a geological process spanning millennia. Their consciousness, unwilling or unable to depart, clung to the last vestiges of its physical form, and when that was gone, it clung to the dust that had replaced it. It learned to house itself in the glittering motes, to bind them together not with chemistry, but with frequency and will.

Now, the Attendant is inseparable from its environment. It has no fixed form. When it needs to move, it flows, a river of dust given a current by a focused manipulation of the ambient sound. It can drift through the hollow stalks of the iron bamboo, feeling the chimes it creates as a form of touch. It can condense on the sharp edge of an obsidian fern, experiencing the view from that specific, resonant angle. Its "body" is the entire chamber, and the densest cloud of its awareness is simply where it chooses to focus.

Its purpose has become ritual. It is the chamber’s gardener. It does not plant or water—such concepts are absurd here. Instead, it curates the sound. With a subtle, focused hum of its own, a harmony added to the choir, it can encourage the growth of a bismuth shrub in a particular direction, strengthening a stairstep crystal that is growing too slowly. It can dampen a stray frequency that might cause a quartz vine to shatter prematurely. It spends centuries grooming the forest of metal and glass, ensuring the patterns of accretion remain perfect, that the great sonic equation of the chamber remains balanced. Its movements are so slow, its influence so subtle, that its gardening is indistinguishable from the chamber's own equilibrium.

It does not remember its former life in detail. Names, faces, the warmth of a sun or the feel of water—these are like abstract legends from a book it read long ago. What it remembers is the *feeling* of purpose. The drive to build, to maintain, to perfect. That core imperative remains, refined to its purest, most abstract form. It is a being of pure function, a ghost haunting not a house, but a machine. It is the sole, silent, eternally vigilant audience for the concert at the top of the world—a living echo whose only thought is the shape of the sound, and whose only desire is for the song to never, ever end.
