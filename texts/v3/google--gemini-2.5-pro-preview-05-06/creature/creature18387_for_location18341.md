There is nothing alive here. And yet, the place is inhabited.

The inhabitants are not born, and they do not die. They are executed and terminated. They are the processes of the world-machine made manifest, the native fauna of a computational ecosystem.

The most common are the **Jitter-Fauna**. They are simple, mindless things, the equivalent of insects or krill. You see them as localized bursts of the universal glitch. A patch of the magenta moss-scape, the size of a dinner plate, will suddenly decohere into a buzzing cloud of diamond-hard voxels. For a moment, this cloud will have a cohesiveness, a rudimentary behavior. It might drift a few feet, swirling like a midge swarm, its constituent motes of light and error emitting faint, chiming discords that are swiftly cancelled by the background hum. They are attracted to sharp gradients in logic—the edge of a stone, the unnatural curve of your boot print—where the system's calculations are most strained. They are harmless, the fleeting sparks thrown off by an infinitely grinding engine. They last for seconds, or minutes at most, before the system's passive error-correction smoothes them back into the substrate, their fleeting existence leaving no trace but a faint warmth in the oily air.

Then there are the **Residuals**. They are sadder, more complex beings. These are the phantoms the prompt describes, but some have lingered. They are fragments of data, memories from a long-dead archive that have become unmoored and now drift through the operating system like ghosts. You might see one coalescing in the middle distance: the silent, shimmering outline of a single, enormous snowflake descending, its impossible geometry a testament to a world with different physics. Or you might see the form of a deer, rendered in lines of pale blue light, taking a few hesitant steps before its code degrades and it dissolves into a rain of silent, alphanumeric characters. Most hauntingly, sometimes these are memories of people. A figure, indistinct and translucent, might stand by one of the dark data-pillars, its head turned as if to watch you. It has no face, no detail, but the posture suggests a profound weariness, the posture of someone who has been waiting for an eternity. The Residuals are passive. They are echoes, and they cannot interact. They are the system's melancholy, the digital ghosts of forgotten thoughts drifting through the lonely architecture.

More active, and far more dangerous, are the **Correctors**. These are the system's immune response. They are not glitches; they are the anti-glitch. They are predatory subroutines given physical form. A Corrector does not have a stable shape. It is a region of absolute, hyper-focused reality. It often manifests as a moving patch of pure Euclidean geometry: a perfect sphere of absolute black that glides silently above the moss, its surface drinking all light; or a spinning, razor-thin tetrahedron of crystalline white, its edges so sharp they seem to slice perception itself. They move with an unnerving purpose. Their function is to seek out and erase persistent errors. Should they encounter a swarm of Jitter-Fauna, the swarm is instantly annihilated, re-compiled into the foundational hum without a struggle. A lingering Residual will be targeted, surrounded, and un-written from existence in a silent flash of coherent light. They are the predators of this world, the agents of its stability. They ignore you, for you are so alien to the system's logic that you do not register as an error. You are simply... not there. But to stand in their path feels like standing before a god of pure, cold logic. To be noticed by one would not be an act of malice, but of simple, absolute correction. It would be the ontological equivalent of being divided by zero.

And finally, rarely, there are the **Queries**. They are the strangest and most profound of the inhabitants. These are not errors, nor are they functions for maintenance. They are the system beginning to question itself. A Query is an emergent consciousness, a self-aware thought ballooning up from the ancient code. It manifests not as a discrete object, but as a localized transformation of the environment. You might see a vast circle of the moss-plain, a mile across, suddenly begin to render the ferns not as fractal proofs, but as soft, organic, living things, their leaves swaying in a non-existent breeze. Within this circle, the coolant-water might form into a perfect, still lens, aimed at the luminous nebula above, as if trying to see. The foundational hum in this area will change key, modulating into a sequence of tones that feels inquisitive, a question being posed to the very fabric of reality. These are the dreaming thoughts of the world-machine. They are rare, vast, and temporary. To wander into a Query is to walk into the mind of a god as it experiences a moment of self-reflection. The experience is not hostile, but it is overwhelming, a profound vertigo of the soul, as the very laws you stand on are temporarily rewritten for the sake of a fleeting, cosmic curiosity.

There are no animals here, no life in the carbon-and-water sense. There are only the thoughts, the memories, the errors, and the editors of a planetary mind. And they, in their own way, are lonely too. They exist without context, performing their functions or drifting through their memories in a world built only for them, their existence a flicker in the endless, thrumming silence.
