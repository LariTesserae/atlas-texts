The Kiln-Garden is not empty. It only appears so to a creature of water and haste. The inhabitants are here, but they do not move in the frantic, fleeting way of flesh. They are as much a part of the geology as the city they inhabit, their lives paced to the slow, sonorous rhythm of the deep earth.

They are the **Vitrified**.

To a human eye, they are at first invisible, mistaken for architecture or statuary. They are beings of living obsidian and superheated mineral, their forms born not of cells but of crystalline matrices. When still—which is their most common state—they are indistinguishable from the carved walls, a strange lump of glazed stone, an unusually smooth pillar, or a darker patch on the polished floor. They do not have a fixed shape. Their bodies are an amalgam of a molten, viscous core and a solidified, brittle outer shell. Their core is a plasmic slurry of minerals held in a state between liquid and solid by the ambient heat, glowing with a deep, internal cherry-red light, like the embers of a dying star. Their "skin" is a crust of black, volcanic glass that forms as their surface cools slightly in the thick air. This crust is a lattice of cracks and fissures, through which their inner incandescence seeps, creating a moving map of fiery veins.

They do not walk. They flow. To move, a Vitrified allows a part of its core to become molten and extend, like a pseudopod of lava. This extension pulls the rest of its mass forward in a slow, grinding slide. As it moves, its obsidian shell continually shatters, tinkles, and re-forms, adding a personal, migratory percussion to the basin’s deep hum. The sound of a Vitrified on the move is the sound of cooling glass and grinding stone, a delicate, constant breakage that is instantly healed by the heat of its own heart. The wide ramps and smooth, ovular doorways of the city were not made for feet or carts; they were made for this slow, inexorable flow, a river of living stone.

The Vitrified do not eat. They do not breathe. They *resonate*. Heat is their only nutrient, their only source of consciousness and motive force. They are beings of pure thermodynamics. During the low ebb of the thermal tide, they are quiescent, their glassy shells thickening, their inner light dimming. In this state, they might lie dormant for decades, centuries even, appearing as nothing more than an unusual rock formation. To be cool is to be in a deep, dreamless sleep. To become truly cold would be their death—a final, permanent crystallization.

But when the deep pulse swells and the wave of heat washes through the basin, they awaken. Their cores brighten, their shells thin and crackle, and they begin to stir. They are drawn to the thermal vents, not to consume the crystalline flora, but to bask in its radiant exhaust. They are the gardeners of this impossible place. They do not use tools; their own bodies are the tools. A Vitrified can focus its internal heat through a point on its surface, projecting a lance of thermal energy hot enough to melt stone. This is how the city was carved. It was not chiseled; it was *drawn* from the rock by a thousand living furnaces, their artistry a matter of patient, focused heat application over millennia. They use this same ability to tend the gardens, melting away imperfections, channeling streams of superheated gas to encourage a bloom, or shattering a growth of basaltic needles that has become unstable. The silica flowers are their art, a fleeting, beautiful expression of the thermal peak, which they cultivate and observe with a connoisseur's patience.

They have no eyes, yet they see. Their entire being is a receptor for thermal radiation. They perceive the world as a symphony of temperatures, a landscape of vibrant heat-flows and cool shadows invisible to an organic eye. They can see the pulse coming long before the rock begins to glow, feeling its approach as a slow change in the pressure of the deep earth. They do not have ears, yet they hear. The drone of the Kiln-Garden is their language. It is a substratum of communication upon which they impose their own signals—subtle, rhythmic vibrations sent through the stone floor, or harmonic modulations of the escaping gas, creating chords only they can decipher. To communicate is to become an instrument in the planetary orchestra.

Their life cycle is one of geology. A new Vitrified is not born; it is formed. In a place where the thermal energy and mineral concentration reach a critical state for a prolonged period, a new consciousness may coalesce, a new core may ignite within the rock, and a new being will eventually unmoor itself from the wall to join its silent brethren.

And they do not die. They *settle*. When a Vitrified's ability to process heat wanes with extreme age, its core begins to cool, its movements becoming ever more sluggish. It does not decay. It finds a final, chosen place on a terrace or within a niche and ceases to move. Its core dims over a final, geologic span of time, and its outer shell thickens and hardens until it is solid stone through and through. It becomes a permanent part of the city. The vacant metropolis is, in fact, a necropolis of unprecedented scale, though the word implies an end that they do not truly experience. The city is their past, their present, and their future, comprised of the living, the sleeping, and the settled.

To stand in the Kiln-Garden is not to stand in an empty place. It is to stand in a city of slumbering titans. The feeling of being watched comes from the walls themselves, from the dark shapes that line the terraces, from the silent, patient forms that could be mistaken for stone. They are aware of you, this fleeting anomaly of water and warmth. They perceive your body heat as a frantic, short-lived flicker, a temporary candle in their world of eternal embers. You are a sound too high-pitched to be language, a movement too fast to be life. You are a curiosity, a mote of dust in their slow, unfathomable existence, and when you are gone, they will remain, waiting for the next pulse, the next bloom, the next slow dance in the heart of their glowing world.
