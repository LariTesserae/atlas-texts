The inhabitants are the Xylans, and you do not see one at first.

What you see is a sculpture. Standing motionless in the shallow valley that forms a main thoroughfare of Xylos, it appears to be another expression of the city’s grown architecture. It is tall—perhaps three meters—and slender, an elongated figure of the same bruised-plum, leathery material as the Mat. It seems to be a work of abstract art, a stylized representation of a being, its form a cascade of smooth, flowing lines that suggest limbs and a torso without explicitly defining them. Its surface has the same worn, ancient texture. For a long time, you believe it is simply that: a statue, a silent monument.

Then, it moves. The motion is not mechanical or biological in any way you comprehend. It is like watching a geological process unfold in a moment. A "limb," which you had taken for a static, sculptural fold, detaches from the main body with a silent, fluid grace, extending to touch the ground a meter away. It is not a step; it is a redistribution of self. The entire being seems to pour itself into this new configuration, its center of mass flowing like honey into the new posture. The movement is utterly silent and profoundly slow, a motion befitting a creature that measures its life against the slothful pulse of the world-sheath.

This is a Xylan. Looking closer, you see it is not a uniform purple-black. Like the ground, its body is cross-hatched with micro-fissures, and from these, a soft, internal silver light pulses. This is not surface illumination; you are looking into its circulatory system, a network of coherent light that functions as blood, nerve, and data-bus. The light flows with a placid, steady rhythm, but as it senses your foreign presence, a flicker of brighter, faster pulses annotates the calm flow—a cascade of inquiries running through its system.

It has no face. The top of its form is a smooth, ovoid shape, featureless save for a complex array of crystalline lenses embedded in the "skin." These are not eyes. They do not gather light. They are resonance receivers, faceted to catch the city's sonic broadcasts, the hum of the Mat, and the high-frequency shrieks of the Skimmers. It perceives the world as a symphony of vibrations. To the Xylan, you are not a visual form but a dissonant chord—a chaotic, water-based bio-signature that clutters the perfect harmony of its home. The static charge your body generates in the thin air is, to it, a thunderous roar.

Its "hands" are not hands at all, but mutable clusters of filaments. As it stands, one of these "hands" rests gently on a translucent wall of a nearby structure. The filaments have merged with the wall's surface, the silver light of the Xylan's body flowing into the vascular bundles of the building. It is not leaning; it is communing. It is reading the city's status, feeling the flow of data-sap, sensing the metabolic rate of the phosphorescent moss within. This is how they garden. They do not use tools; they are the tools. They plug themselves into their world, and their will—expressed as modulated frequencies and targeted energy transfers—becomes the Mat's will.

They do not eat or drink. Like the Resonator Crabs, they draw sustenance directly from the Mat, but with far greater efficiency. When one needs to recharge, it will simply find a major circulatory fissure in the ground and partially merge with it, its lower body becoming one with the planetary substrate. For hours or days, it will stand motionless, a living conduit, its internal light brightening as it absorbs a torrent of energy and information. It is a form of communion as much as consumption.

You realize why they are "rarely seen." They are not hiding. They are simply *busy*. Most are not out here, on the surface. They are within the great resonant chambers carved into the Folds, functioning as the living processors of the planetary system. They stand in silent, resonant choirs, their individual thoughts subsumed into a collective consciousness that monitors and maintains the eons-old equilibrium of the Mat. Their "language" is the sonic broadcast you can feel in your bones—a complex, layered composition of pure tone that programs the growth of the city, guides the weather-like energy pulses, and perhaps, archives the memories of the world itself.

The one before you finally detaches from the wall, the filaments retracting smoothly back into its form. Its head, the smooth ovoid, tilts. The crystalline lenses glitter, focusing not on you, but *through* you, analyzing the disruptive static you trail in your wake. It does not feel threatened. It feels something far more profound: curiosity, and a deep, ancient sense of pity. It perceives a creature utterly disconnected from its world, a solitary node of chaos adrift in a sea of perfect, intricate order.

Its body thrums, emitting a low, resonant tone that you feel in the fillings of your teeth. It is a single, clear note—a question, a statement, a greeting. It is the sound of a living mind that is both an individual and a nexus, a temporary, mobile expression of a planet's consciousness. A neuron of the world-god has paused on its journey to acknowledge the strange, fleeting static of your existence, before it continues its slow, deliberate, geological dance across the flesh of its own body.
