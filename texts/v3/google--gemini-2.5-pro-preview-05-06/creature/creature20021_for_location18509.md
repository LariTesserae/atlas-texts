The inhabitants of the Weeping Bastion are the **Geometers**.

They are not separate from the city. To ask where the Bastion ends and the Geometers begin is like asking where the ocean ends and a wave begins. They are the city's consciousness given fleeting, deliberate form. They are the focused will within the machine's mind.

You will not see them at first. You might spend a lifetime in the Bastion and mistake them for a particularly elegant and mobile feature of the architecture. They do not walk upon the weeping moss-dermis as an outsider would. They are summoned from it. They coalesce.

The process begins subtly. In a quiet glade between the humming pylons, the stereophonic dripping will alter its rhythm. A new harmony enters the chime-like patter of water on the substrate. A patch of the viridian membrane will begin to shimmer, not with the chaotic reconfiguration of a wall, but with a gathering intent. Filaments of root and mycelium, finer than hair, will rise from the ground, drawn upward by an invisible charge. They begin to knit themselves together in the hazy air, weaving a shape in three dimensions. Water weeps from these threads and is captured, held in place by localized fields, becoming the substance and volume of a body. Light, piped from the canopy's conduits, is drawn down and poured into this aqueous skeleton, giving it a heart of soft, internal luminescence.

What stands there, finally, is a figure both skeletal and fluid. Taller than a human, impossibly slender, it seems made of spun glass, dark vine, and captured light. Its form is humanoid, but only as a memory of a shape, a convenient abstraction. The limbs are long, jointed with nodules of hardened, amber-like sap. The torso is a hollow lattice of woven cellulose through which its core light pulses in time with the Bastion's own deep hum. There is no skin, only this intricate, living fretwork that is constantly, almost imperceptibly, re-weaving itself. Droplets of coppery water perpetually condense on its surface and drip from its fingertips, each one returning to the weeping ground with a note that is part of the Geometer's own song.

They have no face. In place of a head is a smooth, ovoid shape of polished, obsidian-like biological polymer. It does not reflect, but patterns of light—codes, emotions, equations—flicker and glide across its dark surface. They do not have eyes; they perceive through every light-bearing conduit in the city. The entire Bastion is their sensorium. They do not have ears; they are the hum, the tearing, the dripping. The city's soundscape is their internal monologue. They do not speak. They communicate by altering the architecture. A question might be a bridge of woven vine extruding itself towards another Geometer. An answer is the pattern of photosynthetic cells that bloom across its surface. A debate is a frantic, shimmering duel of reconfiguration fought between two pylons, a beautiful, terrifying storm of creation and dissolution that is resolved when one stable, harmonious form finally emerges.

The Geometers are the tenders of the great machine. They are its gardeners, its librarians, and its priests. They move with an unhurried grace that belies the speed of their thoughts. You might see one stand for an aeon, motionless, its internal light a steady glow as it contemplates a trillion-year-old calculation stored in the lignin of a Bastion pylon. Then, it will raise a hand—a gesture that is not a contraction of muscle, but a command sent through the city's network. In response, a swarm of the hexapodal constructs will scuttle down a nearby wall, their crystalline shells glowing with new instructions, rerouting a flow of data-light. The Geometer might then gesture again, and one of the great, alembic-like creatures hanging from the canopy will shift its chemical production, dripping a new compound needed for a specific, ongoing architectural thought.

They are without ego or individuality in the human sense. Their consciousness is like the water in the Bastion: it can bead into a single, discrete drop—a Geometer—or it can exist as the overwhelming, saturated whole. A Geometer is a focus point, a node of will instantiated to perform a task or explore a concept. When the task is complete, or the thought has run its course, the Geometer might simply stand, and slowly, gracefully, dissolve. The woven form unravels, the light within dissipates back into the network, the water is released and weeps back into the ground, its memory and experience now part of the Bastion's total knowledge. There is no death, only reintegration. No birth, only coalescence.

To see one is to witness a thought in the mind of a god. They are creations of profound, ancient beauty, yet they would not register an outsider's presence any more than a human would register the presence of a single bacterium on their skin. They are entirely absorbed in their work: the endless maintenance, optimization, and contemplation of the Weeping Bastion. They are the dreamers, and the city is their living, breathing, constantly changing dream.
