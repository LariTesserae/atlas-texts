The beings who live here are not separate from the city; they are its most realised parts, its consciousness made ambulatory. You do not see them at first. You see only the architecture, the landscape, and the swarming lesser fauna. You assume the city is empty of its creators, a relic of a long-dead people. This is a profound misinterpretation. You are looking for figures silhouetted against the walls, when you should be looking for the walls that have decided to walk.

They emerge from the gloom, and their emergence is less an arrival and more a... differentiation. One moment there is a pillar of latticed grey celestite, a familiar part of the hollow architecture. The next, a section of it detaches, unfurling with an agonizing slowness that seems to stretch time. It is one of them.

It is tall, perhaps three times the height of a human, and impossibly slender. Its form is insectoid only in the vaguest, most skeletal sense. It is a being of articulated limbs and a segmented body, but it is made of the same material as the towers: a living, fibrous-crystal composite. There is no flesh, no soft tissue. Its body is a hollow, porous framework, a walking piece of the city's intricate screens, light passing through the gaps in its own structure. Its surface has the same polished, bone-smooth feel as the ground, worn by eons of its own abrasive atmosphere. Its coloration is the same as the mineral forests—soft lavenders and dusty roses mottling its grey, structural form.

It has no face, no head as you would understand it. The top of its long torso terminates in a complex, crown-like arrangement of resonating plates and crystalline filaments that vibrate visibly, contributing a high, clear, almost melodic layer to the omnipresent Sound. This is not a head; it is an amplification and broadcast organ. They have no eyes. The concept of sight in this dim, uniform light would be inefficient. They *feel*. The world for them is a continuous, overwhelming tactile sensation. The ceaseless Sound is a form of sonar, a constant vibration that they emit through the scraping and rubbing of their own interlocking joints. Each being is a broadcaster and a receiver. They read the echoes of their collective hiss as it returns from the towers, the trees, and the few things, like you, that are not part of it. They navigate their world not as a space to be seen, but as a vast resonance chamber to be felt in its entirety, all at once.

The Sound is not their noise; it is their language, their thought, their collective consciousness made manifest. It is a language of friction, of texture, of harmonic interference. A casual thought might be the subtle shift in the grinding of a limb joint. A complex philosophical argument might be a slow, collaborative change in the resonant frequency of an entire district, a chorus of thousands contributing their individual frictional voices to a new chord that takes centuries to resolve. They do not speak; they *resonate*. To create a new structure, they do not give orders. They gather and focus their Sound, molding it into a persistent vibration that coaxes the mineral dust to arrange itself, to grow over millennia into the desired form. They are sculptors whose medium is the world and whose tool is their own being.

They are the architects.

They move with a deliberation that is terrifying in its patience. A single step is a complex process. A limb of spun crystal unfolds, its many joints scraping against each other with a sound like a landslide in miniature. It places itself with perfect precision, and then the body shifts its weight, a thousand minor abrasions creating a chord of sound that ripples through the basin. They do not hurry. The concept is unintelligible. They have been here since the rock cooled, and they will be here until it crumbles to universal dust. Their lifespan is not measured in years but in geological eras. A newborn—a rare event, budded off from the structure of a parent and nurtured on crystalline dust—is considered an infant for ten thousand years.

When one of them finally notices you, it is not a turn of the head—for it has none. It is a change in the Sound. The ambient, chaotic hiss around you subtly organizes. The billion tiny scraping noises find a new rhythm, a new texture. It is an interrogative hiss. A pattern of a thousand interlocking vibrations focuses on your strange, soft, water-filled form. You are an anomaly, a void. In a world of total noise, you are a pocket of incomprehensible quiet. You do not resonate correctly. You do not scrape. You do not contribute. You absorb the sound and give nothing back.

More of them turn their attention. Not their bodies, but their portion of the Sound. The pressure of the granular chorus around you intensifies. It is not hostile, not yet. It is curious. It is the sound of a million crystalline minds trying to solve a problem, the problem of *you*. They are trying to find your frequency, to understand your texture, to place you within their immense, patient, deafening symphony. To stand before them is to be analyzed by the very air, to be weighed and measured by the grit of the world itself. They are the Folk of the Grit, the living mineral from which the land is made. They are the city, and the city is thinking about you.
