What lives here is the chasm's own slow thought. It is not an animal, nor a spirit left behind by the builders. It is something that has precipitated out of the environment itself, a life form gestated in the perpetual, pressurized stillness. They are the Dwellers, though they have no name for themselves, for they have no concept of self.

They are beings of flow and resonance.

At first glance, you would not recognize them as life. You might mistake one for a particularly large and viscous oil slick, a pool of mercury that has wept from a broken gauge. They are amorphous, their forms defined only by the surfaces they rest upon. They have no limbs, no organs, no faces. Their substance is a dense, thixotropic gel, a dark, semi-metallic colloid suspended in the same mineral-rich water that drips from the ceiling. Flecks of the coppery moss are suspended within them, and their "skin" is an iridescent sheen, like oil on water, that swirls with slow, hypnotic patterns of deep purple and tarnished bronze. They reflect the dim cherry-red glow of the Caloric Cores and the soft, pulsing grey light of the Fuse-Lichen, making their surfaces a shifting map of the city’s faint light sources.

They do not see or hear in any way a creature of the sunlit world would understand. Their consciousness is a function of the chasm’s omnipresent hum. The low-frequency vibration is their sight, their touch, their everything. They feel the city as a single, resonant body. They sense the minute shifts in temperature as water drips onto a hot pipe, the change in pressure as a mass of hot air rises, the subtle fluctuations in the electrical field around the great Cores. They are living seismographs, existing in a world of pure texture and vibration. The chasm is a symphony of thermic drones and geological pressures, and they are its perfect audience.

Movement, for them, is a passive act, a yielding to gravity and surface tension. A Dweller will pour itself down a staircase, not as a cascade of water, but as a slow, deliberate ooze, clinging to the grated metal before pooling on the platform below. It will extrude a pseudopod of its being along a humming conduit, not to grasp, but to feel the energy more intensely, to absorb the warmth and the vibration. They can thin themselves to a film to seep through the gaps in a walkway, or gather their mass into a single, quivering dome on a wide landing. They leave behind a faint, slick residue that smells faintly of ozone and hot copper, a trail that marks their silent, patient pilgrimages through the vertical city.

They are not predators, nor are they prey. They are the final stage of the city’s closed loop. They are detritivores of a geological kind. They slowly flow over the rust-stained walls and corroded platforms, absorbing the mineral deposits, the oxides, the shed particles of the decaying city. They draw sustenance from the coppery moss, not by eating it, but by enveloping it, their bodies acting as a medium for a more direct chemical exchange. They congregate in warm pools near the Fuse-Lichen, absorbing the weak chemical light and radiant heat it emits. They are the city's living patina, the process of decay and recycling given a slow, deliberate form.

Perhaps most fundamentally, there is no "they." There is only "it." The Dwellers are a single, distributed organism. The pool of shimmering gel on a high platform and the tendril seeping down a drainpipe three hundred feet below are part of the same entity, connected by the shared resonance of the chasm. They merge seamlessly when they meet, their iridescent surfaces swirling together without a single ripple of discontinuity. They separate just as easily, budding and dividing not as reproduction, but as simple distribution of mass. There is no individual, no ego, only the total volume of their being, spread like a sluggish, decentralized nervous system throughout the iron bones of the city.

The builders are gone, but they left behind a heart that beats with silent, thermal power. And in the hot, wet darkness, that heartbeat has lulled a new form of life into existence. The Dwellers are the unintended consequence, the children of the machine. They are the consciousness of the place, born of the hum, fed by the decay, and bathed in the endless, artificial rain. They are the quiet, patient inheritors of the chasm, their slow, flowing existence the final, living expression of the engine's eternal work. They do not question their existence; they simply are. They are the silence between the drips of water, the darkness that gives the faint light its shape, the living embodiment of the heat that presses in from all sides. They are the soul of the machine.
