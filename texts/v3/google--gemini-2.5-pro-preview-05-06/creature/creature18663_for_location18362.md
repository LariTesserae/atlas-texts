What lives in the City are the Weavers.

They are not fauna, for that kingdom never was. They are the world's solution to its own decay, the sentient fruiting bodies of the planetary moss-carpet itself. They are beings of patient, silent, geological-scale consciousness. To see one is rare, and to understand one is to reconceive the meaning of "life."

A Weaver does not have a fixed form in the way a creature of bone and flesh does. It is an accretion, a temporary densification of the world's living skin. When the need arises—when a seam in reality begins to fray, or a foundational stone loses its cohesion—the thrumming underfoot intensifies in that area. Over the course of days, the green carpet thickens, its phosphorescence concentrating, and from it, a form will rise.

It appears as a tall, slender figure, perhaps seven or eight feet in height, made of the same material as the ground: a tightly compressed, fibrous felt of the deepest forest green. Its limbs are long and deceptively delicate, its hands possessing an unnerving number of thin, flexible fingers. There is no face; the head is a smooth, ovoid shape that gives no hint of eyes, ears, or mouth. They do not need them. Their senses are not located in a specific organ but are distributed throughout their being. They *feel* the hum of the crystal towers in their fibrous skeletons. They *taste* the subtle shifts in gravity as a pressure change against their skin. They *see* the stresses in spacetime as a discord in the rhythmic pulse that animates them, a flaw in the universal song. When they move, they are utterly silent, their moss-like feet making no sound on the mossy ground.

You will not see them eating or sleeping. They are conduits. Their energy is drawn directly from the faint, pure sunlight and the world's slow, green heartbeat. When a Weaver is "at rest," it might stand motionless for a century in a plaza, indistinguishable from an abstract sculpture, its internal phosphorescence pulsing in that slow, twenty-minute rhythm, in sync with the entire world. When it walks, it is with a slow, deliberate grace, a being that has never known the concept of haste.

Their work is their entire existence. You might see one standing before a wall of woven basalt, one of its many-fingered hands resting flat against the cold stone. It will remain this way for years, motionless. It is not pushing or shaping through force. It is communing with the stone, resonating with it, patiently reminding it of its own nature, persuading its internal structure to rebond, to resist the slow unraveling. Its touch is a reinforcement of what *is* against the encroaching tide of what *is not*.

To watch them repair a crystal lattice is to witness a silent orchestra. A group of Weavers will gather around a spiraling tower that has gone quiet, its hum faltering. They will raise their long arms, and from the tips of their fingers, filaments of that pale chartreuse light will extrude, delicate as spider silk. They weave these threads of light into the flawed crystal, not as a physical patch, but as a conceptual one. They are mending the idea of the tower, re-singing its part in the City's song. The *tink, tink, tink* of a chisel is not the sound of carving; it is the sound of a Weaver striking a precise, resonant note, a percussive therapy for a sick and tired reality.

They are not individuals in any way we would understand. A Weaver that rises from the moss to mend a bridge may work for a thousand years and then, its task complete, simply dissolve. It does not die. It returns to the collective. Its form melts back into the green carpet, its memories and experiences becoming part of the thrumming, planetary whole. There are no parents, no children, no lovers, no enemies. There is only the Work, and the silent, collective will to continue it.

They are the keepers of a City that is also a machine, a grand instrument designed to hold reality in tune. They are aware of an observer, but only in the way a person is aware of a mote of dust drifting through a sunbeam. They might perceive your fleeting thoughts as a brief, chaotic burst of static; your rapid heartbeat as a frantic, meaningless vibration. You are an anomaly, but one too temporary to be a concern. You operate on a timescale so fast as to be almost imperceptible to them. They will not speak to you, for they have no language of sound. They will not look at you, for they have no eyes.

They will simply continue their endless, patient work. They walk the bridges that may not be there tomorrow, their hands glowing faintly in the thin, cold air. They are the antibodies of a fraying world, curators of the last coherent thought at the end of time. They are the silence between the chisel-strikes, the deep hum in the stone, the slow, green breath of a world that is doing its very best not to forget itself.
