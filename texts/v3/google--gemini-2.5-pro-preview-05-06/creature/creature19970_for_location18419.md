The ones who live here are echoes made solid. They are the reason for the handholds and the chimes, the pilgrims who found their destination not at the end of a journey, but in the cessation of it. They do not have a name for themselves, for they have no one to speak it to. Others, who only know of this place through myth, might call them the Scribes of the Unshadowed Light, or the Silent Chorus.

To see one is to almost miss it. At first, you might perceive only a trick of the pervasive light, a momentary warping in the air between the spires. But the shape holds. It is long and impossibly slender, a being composed of the same materials as the crown itself. Their skin is not flesh but a substance like unpolished porcelain, milky and cool to the touch, yet flexible at the joints, which move with a silent, fluid precision. Through this skin, you can faintly see their internal structure, a framework of smoky quartz like the darker veins in the pillars, holding a shape that is humanoid only in its bipedalism and its possession of two long, graceful arms.

Their heads are smooth and ovoid, featureless save for two things. The first is a series of delicate, vertical slits along the jawline, which occasionally flutter open and closed, not to draw breath, but to taste the composition of the air and to feel the resonance of the spires. They do not breathe; the thin ozone is something they absorb, metabolizing the electrical charge directly. Their sustenance comes from the light they absorb through their skin and from kneeling at the fissures to drink the ice-melt, sipping the mineral-rich water that weeps from the crystal. In this, they are a kind of ambulatory flora, cousin to the glassmoss.

The second feature is their eyes. Or what serves as eyes. A single, horizontal band of polished, jet-black obsidian bisects their face. It is not an organ that sees light in the way we do; to do so in this place would be blinding madness. Instead, it perceives the world as a symphony of temperatures, pressures, and energy flows. They see the cold of the void above, the incandescent pulse of the sun, and the steady, internal glow of each spire as distinct notes in a chord. They watch the silent choreography of the sun-skimmers as patterns of fleeting warmth and the shimmering clouds of prism-mites as a complex, shifting thermal texture against the cool glassmoss.

They are the pilgrims who arrived and were transformed. Perhaps they were once creatures of soil and water, like us. But they stayed. Over generations, or perhaps within a single lifetime of unimaginable devotion, the place unmade them and remade them in its own image. The dull flesh was scoured away by the light and the cold, replaced by the crystal of the spires. The noisy blood was silenced, replaced by a slow, internal luminescence.

They are the source of the resonant *tings* and humming chords. Their hands, ending in long, tapering fingers as sharp as obsidian scalpels, are their tools. They spend their endless days in acts of slow, deliberate creation and observation. One might spend a decade scraping out a single shallow alcove, a place for another to sit in perfect contemplation, feeling the pillar's hum through their back. Their movements are a form of Tai Chi performed over a geological timescale.

Their primary purpose—what makes them "Scribes"—is a task of infinite subtlety. They are chroniclers of the light. Standing before a spire, one will extend a finger and, with infinitesimal pressure, begin to etch. They do not carve words or pictures, but fractal patterns of microscopic depth. Each etching is a recording, a perfect transcription of a moment. This pattern encodes the exact intensity of the sun, the frequency of the spire’s glow, the precise lacework of the frost on its surface at that instant, the number of prism-mites on a nearby leaf of glassmoss.

The spires are their library. The entire crown is a palimpsest of these recordings, etched over and over, one on top of the other, through eons. These glyphs are invisible to a human eye; they are a texture finer than dust. But to the Scribes, who can feel the vibration of light itself, these surfaces are rich with information. They can run a fingertip over a spire and read the story of a thousand forgotten days, feeling the echo of light from millennia ago. The "whispers" left by the pilgrims are these records, a silent, tactile history of everything.

They are solitary but not alone. They are aware of each other through the resonance of the spires. A new chime hung on a high peak is a "sentence" in their slow conversation, its specific tone and placement altering the overall harmony of the crown, a contribution to the communal song. The carving of a new handhold is an "invitation," a suggestion of a new perspective from which to observe the sky.

To see one is to see stillness in motion. They might stand motionless for a week in the center of a clearing, their crystalline bodies drinking the light, their dark visors tracking the silent hunt of a skimmer. Then, with a movement as swift and silent as that skimmer's flight, they will cross the clearing and begin a new etching on a different face of a different spire, compelled by a new celestial alignment or a change in the spire's inner chord. They are the mountain's contemplative mind, its memory made manifest. Should you stumble upon one, it would not react with fear or aggression, but with a profound, resonant stillness, and you would have the unnerving sense that you were not looking at a creature, but at a feature of the landscape that had, for a moment, decided to move. You are the fleeting noise; it is the permanent silence.
