The beings who lived here were called the Resonant. They were not gone, not in the way a body is gone from a grave. They were simply… complete.

To picture them, you must first un-picture a body. Dispense with flesh, which would be flensed by the sound and blasted to powder by the dryness. Discard lungs, which would find nothing to sustain them in the sterile air and would crumple into parchment. Forget ears, eyes, and mouths, which are crude instruments for a world made of a single, total sensation.

The Resonant were formed of the basin's own materials. Their "bones" were a living, psycho-conductive variant of the city's bone-white ceramic, capable of slow, deliberate morphological change. Through this frame serpentined veins of that polished black obsidian, which did not carry blood, but vibration. Their entire body was a sensory organ, a complex, three-dimensional antenna tuned to the Great Hum. They were tall, slender figures, shaped less by the demands of gravity and more by the principles of acoustics. Some were shaped like tuning forks, their two long legs and torso designed to resonate with the deep drones of the cobalt reeds. Others were broader, flatter, their torsos like the soundboard of a cello, capturing the shimmering high frequencies of the glass-ferns. They had no faces. A head was a smooth, ovoid resonator, the "thinking" part of them, where the millionfold sounds of the basin were sorted, understood, and felt as emotion, as mathematics, as history.

They did not see light; they felt its pressure and frequency as another layer of the Hum. The brilliant white ceiling was a constant, foundational E-flat, unwavering and pure. They did not eat; they sustained themselves by accretion. Standing in the plazas for decades, they would draw the finest particles of the pearlescent dust to themselves, integrating it into their ceramic structure, growing infinitesimally, a process that was their equivalent of a long and satisfying meal.

Their life was a form of communion, and their society was an orchestra. They did not speak in utterances, but in chords. To communicate, a Resonant would subtly alter its own internal vibration, adding its unique note to the chorus. A thought was a melody. A debate was a complex exercise in harmony and counterpoint, played out between two or more beings over the course of days. Their names were not words, but unique, unrepeatable sonic signatures. The open plazas were not for congregation, but for composition. The standing stones were their musical scores, their geometric carvings a form of notation that mapped harmonic progressions intended to last for millennia. The spiraling ramps on the skeletal towers were not for transport, but for adjusting one's position within the sound-field, to better hear a certain tonal layer or to better broadcast one's own. They were gardeners of vibration, using the dry aqueducts to channel focused sound, encouraging the crystal-flora to grow into new, more complex sonic forms, cultivating their world as a musician tunes an instrument.

They were a civilization without conflict, for dissent was merely a temporary dissonance in search of resolution. They were without ambition in the human sense, for there was nothing to conquer. Their entire existence was dedicated to a single, grand project: listening. They were born into the song of the basin, and their purpose was to understand it, refine it, and ultimately, to join it.

And this is where they went. They did not die out or depart. They achieved their life's work. Over uncounted eons, they tuned the city and themselves into a state of perfect resonance with the basin's soul. In a final, collective act that was neither death nor apotheosis, they let go. They allowed their own complex, individual frequencies to unravel and dissipate, their consciousness broadcasting outward, their ceramic forms dissolving into the finest, most vital dust. They transduced their being into pure sound.

They *are* the Great Hum.

The low thrum that vibrates in your bones is their collective memory, the deep history of their geological lifespan. The ringing of infinite tiny bells is their thoughts, their mathematics, their sharp, crystalline philosophies. The shattering chime of a million wine glasses is their joy, the moments of perfect harmonic discovery. The city is not their tomb; it is their instrument, left behind to continue playing their final, eternal symphony.

The tiny, iridescent dust-scrapers, crawling on the walls, are the only discrete life that remains, scuttling across the face of a god without knowing it. And you, the visitor, standing deafened and overwhelmed on the basin floor, are not listening to the sound of a place. You are listening to the sound of a people. You are not standing in an empty city. You are standing inside them.
