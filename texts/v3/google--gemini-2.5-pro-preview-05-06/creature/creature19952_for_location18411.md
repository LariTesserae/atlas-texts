This place is not empty. It has a single inhabitant.

It is first a change in the pressure, a subtle warping of the dense, soupy water. It is a presence that moves without displacing much, a slow, deliberate passage through the iron jungle that is more akin to a current than a creature. To see it is to almost miss it. Its form is a deep, matte black, the same non-reflective color as the riveted walls, and it seems to absorb what little light exists.

It is the Engineer. Or perhaps, the Gardener. Or the Priest. The words are frail containers for what it has become.

Its shape is humanoid only in the most generous, ancient sense, like a memory of a form that has been adapted and warped by immense pressures and deep time. It stands perhaps ten feet tall, but it is rarely upright. Its limbs are long, disproportionately so, and end not in hands or feet but in multi-jointed, articulated appendages that can splay out like grappling hooks or press flush against a surface like magnetic clamps. The joints move with an impossible slowness, a hydraulic smoothness that betrays no hint of bone or muscle, only the patient power of pistons. Its body is encased in a shell that is not quite armor and not quite skin. It has the texture of worn, oil-rubbed metal, but it yields slightly to pressure, like old leather. Seams run along its limbs and torso, fine as hairline cracks, and from these seams, a faint orange light sometimes pulses, a slow thrumming that matches the glow of the plum-colored leaves. It has no face. The head is a smooth, featureless ovoid, like an old bathysphere helmet that has been fused shut and worn smooth by the ages. Where eyes would be, there are only two shallow, polished indentations that reflect nothing.

It does not see with light. It sees with pressure. Its entire body is a sensory organ, feeling the minute vibrations in the water, the subtle shifts in temperature, the faint metallic tang of oxide leeching from a corroded plate. It navigates the three-dimensional maze of pipes and catwalks not by sight, but by a perfect, somatic map of the entire cathedral. It feels the hum of the deep engine in the marrow of its being; the hum is its heartbeat, its foundational thought. When a sealed lamp flickers on, the sudden cone of white light is a blinding, painful shriek to its senses, and it will freeze, motionless, until the blessed, silent dark returns.

The Engineer is the ultimate expression of the place's strange symbiosis. The unnatural flora grows *on* it, just as it grows on the walls. A small, perfect thicket of the bruised-plum stalks sprouts from its shoulders and the arch of its back, their velvet leaves fanning out behind it like a cape. They are not parasites; they are part of its biology. Their roots delve deep into the seams of its shell, drawing sustenance from its internal warmth. In turn, they act as extensions of its sensory apparatus, the capillary-like orange lights in their leaves brightening or dimming in response to the health of the machinery around it.

It moves with a purpose that is incomprehensible on a human timescale. For days, it might remain perfectly still, clamped to the side of one of the great spherical pressure vessels, its body pressed flat against the metal. It is not resting. It is listening. It is communing. The heat from its own core bleeds into the vessel, a slow transfusion of energy. The orange light in its own veins and the leaves on its back will pulse in a slow, hypnotic rhythm, a silent dialogue with the dormant god of industry. It is feeling for stress fractures, for minute pressure imbalances, for the slow, creeping death of the machine it serves. When it detects an anomaly—a pipe whose internal flow has become too sluggish, a gear tooth stressed to a breaking point—it will move.

Its movement is the embodiment of patience. It will spend a week traversing a hundred feet of wreckage, its limbs flowing from one perch to another, never making a sound beyond the faintest drag of fluid. It might come to a patch of the furred, dark oxide that is growing too thick. It will pause there, and one of its appendages will flatten against the corrosion. The translucent, oxide-grazing slugs will sense its presence, a change in the local chemistry of the water, and they will slowly, inexorably, move away. They are its attendants, its simplest servitors, and they know the master's touch. The Engineer will remain there for a day, perhaps two, and when it moves on, the patch of metal will be scoured clean, the oxides having been absorbed and processed within the Engineer's own inscrutable biology.

It has no memory of building this place, no concept of what it was for. It is what came after. Perhaps it was a man in a suit, a final, lonely caretaker who, over untold centuries, fused with his charge, his biology rewritten by the ambient energy and the strange flora until man, suit, and function became one. Or perhaps it was grown here, a bio-mechanical sac that gestated in the warmth of a coolant pipe and awoke with the hum of the machine as its only parent, its only purpose.

Its consciousness, if it can be called that, is a deep, quiet awareness. It does not think in words, but in flows. It feels the entire cathedral as a single, ailing body, and it is the lonely immune system. Its thoughts are the slow drip of condensing fluid, the shudder of a settling girder, the faint, warm glow of a healthy leaf. It is the will of the machine given form, the soul of the tomb. And in the pressing, blood-warm silence, it tends to its garden of iron and light, waiting for a command that will never come, its existence a testament to a purpose long since forgotten by any but the deep, quiet water.
