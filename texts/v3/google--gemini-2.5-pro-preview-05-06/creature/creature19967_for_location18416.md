What lives in the Sunken Oratorio are the Choristers.

You do not see one at first. You cannot. Your senses are too overwhelmed, too steeped in the assumptions of a stable world. You might mistake one for a trick of the light, for a patch of shimmering air, or for an unusually elegant spur of the living architecture. But as you spend time within the resonant heart of the Oratorio, as the constant thrumming recalibrates your perception, you begin to discern them.

A Chorister is not a creature in the way the slug-like grazers or the winged hunters are. It is a focal point. It is a knot of coherence in a place defined by its instability.

Your first clear glimpse might be across one of the vast, pollen-hazy plazas. You see a shape, impossibly slender and tall, standing motionless amidst the chaos. It looks like a figure sculpted from spun sugar glass and dark, polished wood. It has long, attenuated limbs that seem too fragile to support it, a torso that is little more than a framework of curved, resonant ribs, and a head that is a smooth, featureless ovoid, like a river stone worn perfectly smooth over eons. It has no mouth, no eyes, no discernible sensory organs. Its "skin" is a mosaic of fibrous plates, much like the bark-walls of the Oratorio, but they are polished to a sheen, catching the buttery light in shifting, iridescent patterns.

Then it moves. The movement is not biological in a way you understand. It does not walk; it *relocates*. It will be standing by a pillar, and then, with a subtle distortion of the air around it, a visual shearing like heat-haze, it will be thirty feet away. The motion is not instantaneous, but it is not traversal. It is a change of state. The Chorister seems to find a harmonic alignment with another point in space and simply... resolves there.

These beings are the conductors of the Oratorio's ceaseless music. The great, foundational hum of the plant-city is the orchestra, and the cacophony of the fauna is the raw, untamed choir. The Choristers are the soloists and the arrangers. They are masters of the Oratorio's strange physics because they are born of it.

Observe one for long enough, and you will see its true nature. The deafening sound is not uniform. The Chorister, by its mere presence, can alter the acoustics around it. It can stand in the middle of a screaming vortex of insect-clatter and create a perfect sphere of relative silence around itself—a silence that is not an absence of sound, but a perfect cancellation of it, a null-chord that feels more profound and heavy than any noise. It does this by subtly vibrating its own framework, its body a living tuning fork that radiates a counter-frequency.

They do not speak. They communicate by creating and shaping sound. A Chorister might approach another by sending a focused, ripple of the floor’s vibration ahead of it. The other will reply by warping the air, causing the shriek of a distant hunter to arrive as a series of distinct, melodic notes. Their interactions are a form of live sound-mixing, a constant, improvisational performance.

Their relationship with the other fauna is not that of a simple predator. It is more intimate, more fundamental. You might see a Chorister "hunt" one of the shimmer-wing moths whose wings phase in and out of reality. The Chorister does not pursue it with speed. Instead, it attunes itself to the moth's spatio-temporal flickering. Its own form begins to de-resonate, its edges blurring into a soft static. It holds its long, three-jointed fingers out, not to catch, but to offer a point of stability. The moth, caught in its own chaotic phasing, is drawn to this anchor of causality. It flutters towards the outstretched hand, and as it makes contact, it does not die. It simply... resolves. Its light, its energy, its resonant frequency, is absorbed without violence into the Chorister, which hums with a brief, intense, and perfectly pitched note—a moment of pure harmony extracted from the chaos. The Chorister has not eaten; it has assimilated a stray note back into the score.

They are the Oratorio’s self-awareness, but not an intelligence you could comprehend. They have no technology, no writing, no history beyond the present, continuous performance. Their mind is the mind of a composer, but one whose thoughts are the physical laws of its own universe. They do not build or plan; they simply *perform* their world into a state of fleeting, breathtaking beauty. They prune the sonic chaos. They shape the resonant architecture not by force, but by encouraging growth along harmonic lines, subtly vibrating the ground to guide a new buttress's path over decades. They are the gardeners of resonance, the shepherds of causality.

To be near a Chorister is to feel the world snap into sharp, terrifying focus. The sound that was just pressure becomes a symphony. The warped distances become a deliberate and elegant geometry. The chaotic dance of life is revealed as a complex choreography. You realize that the Oratorio is not just a place; it is an act of creation, and the Choristers are its artists. They are the living will of the song, and they inhabit it as a thought inhabits a mind—utterly inseparable, giving it shape, purpose, and a terrible, resonant beauty. They are what lives here because, in a very real sense, they *are* the life here, distilled into its purest, most coherent form.
