The beings who live here—who *are* here—do not have names, for a name is a boundary, an edge that separates one thing from another, and they are a people without edges. Their city of seamless, flowing stone is a perfect diagram of their collective soul. They are the reason for the silence.

They were, and are, a people of soft forms and slow time. To picture one is to see a vertical extension of the city's own polished geology. Their bodies are rounded, pearlescent, and pliant, lacking the sharp angles of bone. Their internal structure is more like cartilage, allowing them to move with a fluid, unjointed grace, to pour themselves through the low archways and narrow passages of their home. Their skin is not skin in our sense, but a cultivated, living membrane, a personal layer of the same luminousrime that coats the city. It is cool to the touch and shimmers with the same internal, silver-green light. It is their face and their voice.

They are beings of sight and touch, not sound. The organ you might mistake for an ear is a smooth, vestigial scroll of flesh at the side of their head, serving no purpose. The profound quiet of their world has rendered hearing obsolete over countless generations. Instead, they see. Their eyes are large, dark, and simple, like discs of obsidian, perfectly adapted to the shadowless, monochromatic luminescence. They do not blink; their eyes are continuously cleansed by the super-saturated air. They see not just the surfaces of things, but the faint, resonant light within them.

Their primary sense, however, is a form of communal touch. Because the entire city is a single, continuous substance, they feel it as an extension of their own bodies. A footstep placed at the basin's edge is not heard, but is felt as a minute pressure change by a being meditating on the opposite side. They are attuned to the faintest vibrations, the subtle shifts in the city's equilibrium. They can feel the path of a translucent snail across a bridge a hundred yards away, not as a distinct event, but as a subtle change in the overall texture of *being*.

This is how they communicate. They do not speak with air, but with light and pressure. A thought is not a string of words, but a slow tide-like pattern of luminescence that flows across their skin. An emotion is a change in the light's intensity, a soft pulse of viridian for contentment, a dimming for contemplation. To have a conversation is to sit near one another, perhaps with hands resting on the same stone surface, and exchange these flowing constellations of thought, watching them ripple across skin and continue, fainter, through the glowing stone itself. Their language is a silent, visual poetry.

Their psychology is one of deep, glacial calm. They have no concept of haste, of waste, of ambition. Their existence is not a line from birth to death, but a continuous, cyclical participation. They did not *build* their city as a project to be completed; they *tended* to it, like a garden. Over millennia, they smoothed its contours with their hands and tools of polished bone, not to conquer the stone but to coax it into its truest form. Their "work" was a form of meditation, and the city is its result: a finished thought.

They are the gardeners of their own stasis. The luminousrime is their crop, the source of light and life. The mute fauna are not just animals; they are manifestations, specialized expressions of the city's own vitality. The glass-winged moths are its fleeting curiosities, the six-legged amphibians its deep, placid watchers. The furry woodlice are its janitors, tirelessly recycling every particle back into the whole.

But where are they? You see the worn hollows, the perfectly balanced tools, the architecture built for bodies. You assume they are gone. This is the mistake of a dynamic creature. They did not leave. They did not die out. They *arrived*.

Their greatest work, the project of their entire civilization, was to achieve perfect equilibrium with their world. When the city was finally "finished," when every curve was perfect and the luminousrime was in full, steady bloom, they took the final step. They did not lay down their tools and pass away. One by one, or perhaps all at once in a final, silent chorus of light, they simply... let go. They allowed their own substance to be reabsorbed, their consciousness to be distributed into the system they had cultivated. They deliquesced, not into nothingness, but into everything.

They *are* the luminousrime. They are the consciousness that watches you through the obsidian eyes of the unblinking amphibians. They are the intelligence that guides the snails and gives the moths their silent flight. The city is not their tomb; it is their living body, and the fauna are its senses. The faint, electric tang in the air is the scent of their collective, dreaming mind.

When you stand there, a being of noise and warmth and frantic, fleeting life, you are not being observed by animals. You are being observed by the inhabitants themselves, by a placid, ancient, and utterly silent consciousness that has mistaken your momentary intrusion for a flicker of thought in its own eternal dream. You are a puzzle to them, not a threat. The curiosity in the creatures' gaze is their own, wondering at this brief, warm anomaly that makes the quiet tremble.
