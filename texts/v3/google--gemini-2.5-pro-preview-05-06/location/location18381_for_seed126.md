There is no ground here, not in the sense of soil or rock. There is only the Substrate. It stretches from one uniform horizon to the other, a constructed plane of unimaginable antiquity and scale. To call it "low" is to speak of its largest features; it is a world without mountains, an ocean basin drained of its ocean and paved over by intelligences long since departed.

The terrain is a vast, gently undulating expanse of fused, off-white and pale grey material, something between ceramic and bone. Over continental distances, it swells into low, impossibly broad plains and synclines, like the settled wrinkles in a cloth laid down at the dawn of time. These are the landforms here, buckles in the planet-scale artifact, worn smooth by an erosion that knows nothing of water. This erosion is a process of pure time. It is material fatigue, the slow, molecular-level surrender of a structure that has held its form for billions of years. Everything is covered in a fine, iridescent dust, the shed material of the Substrate itself. It is not grit; it is a powder of micro-fine filaments and shattered crystalline bonds that glitters in the perpetual light. Kicking it up sends a silent, shimmering cloud that hangs in the dense, still air for hours before settling.

The atmosphere is thick and warm, heavy with noble gases and exotic particulates that make the air feel substantial, almost viscous. It carries no moisture. There is no weather, only states of energy. The sky is not a sky but a higher, unseen layer of the artifice—a ceiling so vast its curvature is imperceptible. It glows with a constant, brilliant, shadowless light, a pure white luminescence that is both the source of all energy and the great timepiece of this world. There are no nights, only slow, periodic dimmings, "eclipses" where the light-ceiling modulates its output, plunging the landscape into a soft, silver twilight for a few hours before returning to its full, uncompromising glare. During these shifts, silent tides of energy sweep across the Substrate, visible as faint, aurora-like shimmers that race across the plains at immense speed. These are the storms of this place.

The silence is profound, a pressure on the ears. There is no wind to stir the air, no water to lap or fall. The only sounds are those of the Substrate itself, and they are barely sounds at all. Place a hand on the ceramic-like surface and you can feel a deep, inaudible hum, the thrum of ancient power cores in their final, stable state. Occasionally, a high, thin chime will echo for a moment, the sound of a resonant filament somewhere in the deep structure finally snapping after a million years of tension. Or a faint, crackling hiss, like static, will emanate from a patch of ground as a localized energy field discharges.

In this utterly built, waterless world, life is an extension of the construction. Vegetation is sparse but striking. These are not plants but crystalline Accumulators. They grow directly from the Substrate, seemingly at random, across the vast plains. A typical one might be a latticework spire of cobalt blue crystal, ten feet tall, its structure a perfect fractal. They are immobile, silent, and cool to the touch. Their purpose is to gather and process the ambient light and energy, growing with geological slowness. Over centuries, they form, and after millennia, they might shatter into a cloud of the same iridescent dust that coats everything, their stored energy released in a single, silent flash.

Fauna is rarer still, and just as alien. They are the maintenance systems, still active. Sometimes, from a barely-visible seam in the Substrate, a Glider will emerge. It looks like a pool of mercury given life and purpose, flowing across the surface in a meter-wide amoeboid patch. It is silent, and its passage leaves a trail that is minutely cleaner, smoother, its material infinitesimally renewed. More complex are the Surveyors. Multi-legged, insectoid constructs of black, filament-wound carbon, they move with a disconcerting, silent fluidity. They might patrol a ten-kilometer stretch of a surface feature for a century, measuring stresses, repairing micro-fractures with materials they extrude from their own bodies, before retreating back into the depths of the Substrate. They do not seem to notice other presences; their programming is ancient, their focus absolute.

The world feels dense, not just in its thick air but in its very substance. Under the visible surface lie kilometers, perhaps thousands of kilometers, of the same intricate, technological structure. The slight undulations of the plains are hints of the colossal, non-Euclidean architecture below. This is not a surface; it is a layer, one of countless many. The rules of this world are the rules of a machine in its final, graceful, and impossibly long decay. It is dynamic, but at a pace that mocks biology. The energy tides are its pulse. The growth and shattering of the Accumulators are its metabolism. The skittering of the Surveyors is its immune system.

To be here is to be an unaccounted-for variable in the last equation of a dead universe. You are a ghost haunting a machine that is itself becoming a ghost. The warmth is the machine's cooling breath. The light is its fading memory. The silence is the sound of its purpose, long ago fulfilled and forgotten. And the vastness is the scale of its final, lonely thought, stretched out to infinity.
