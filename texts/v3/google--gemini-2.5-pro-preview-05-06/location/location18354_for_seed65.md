The first thing you notice is not a thing you see, but the thing you hear. It is not so much a sound as it is the condition of the air itself. It is a drone, a single, unwavering, colossal note that fills all of space. It is deafening, but to call it loud is to miss the point. Loudness implies a source, a thing making noise. This is not made; it *is*. The drone has a texture, a grain. It is the sound of a billion tons of granite grinding against a billion tons more, but frozen in a single, eternal moment of contact. It is the chord a mountain would sing if it had a voice. It is a pressure in the ears, a vibration in the teeth, a thrumming that passes through the soles of your feet and into your bones. All other sound is absorbed by it, rendered impossible. The
whistle of wind, the skitter of a stone, your own heartbeat—all are hypothetical, swallowed by the sheer, sonic density of the world.

Your eyes adjust to the light, which is the drone's equal in intensity. It is a harsh, clean, unwavering brightness pouring from a sky of deep, thin-air violet. There is no sun, no single point of origin, but a pervasive, shadowless luminosity that glares off the landscape. The landscape is a plateau, an immense tableland of stone at the roof of the world. It stretches for miles, a gently undulating plain of pale, feldspar-like rock, worn smooth not by wind or water, but by eons of this resonant vibration. The surface is not flat, but a panorama of shallow, miles-wide bowls and low, sweeping ridges, the topography of a world settled into its final, ancient form. There is no dust, no soil, only the immense, solid, singing stone.

And it is cool. A deep, penetrating coolness radiates from the ground, the chill of a high-altitude cellar. The bright light has no heat. In the shallowest depressions and on the leeward side of the great, sweeping ridges, a faint mist clings to the rock. This is the world’s water. Condensation gathers where the vibration is perhaps infinitesimally dampened, forming a breathing dampness on the stone. It never rains. Water does not flow here, it merely materializes and evaporates in a slow, stable cycle, leaving behind ghostly patches of moisture that seem to hover inches above the surface.

In these damp zones, life clings. Vegetation is a misnomer. There are no plants, not really. Only the lichen. It grows in fractal patterns of grey and black, resembling frost, or the delicate electrical branching of a Lichtenberg figure. These are sonoliths, organisms that do not feed on light but on the subtle imperfections in the drone. They find the null points, the quietest micro-havens in the overwhelming sound, and anchor themselves, drawing minerals wicked up by the condensing damp. They are sparse, quiet islands in a sea of ringing stone.

The emptiness of the place is vast. The distance between one patch of lichen and the next can be a hundred yards. The space between ridges can be miles. And yet, the place is teeming with life.

They are everywhere. They are the only fauna, and they are abundant. At first, they look like geology, like features of the rock. They are low, six-legged creatures, their bodies ranging from the size of a hand to the size of a small car. Their carapaces are of the same pale, resonant stone as the plateau itself, polished to a near-perfect mirror by the glaring light and the vibrating air. They are shaped like trilobites or flattened teardrops, armored and sleek, built to endure the pressure of the sound. They have no visible eyes, no ears, no mouths. Their bodies are edged with a fringe of hair-fine cilia that gently touch the ground, tasting the vibrations.

They move with a slowness that is nearly imperceptible. They are the agents of the world’s stasis. They are never still, but they never hurry. A constant, slow, and purposeful migration flows across the face of the plateau. They are not hunting, not grazing, not fighting, not mating. They are working.

Scattered near each creature are its tools. They are simple, but their existence is the strangest thing in this already strange place. They are smaller stones—geodes of quartz, splinters of obsidian, spheres of black iron ore—not native to the smooth, uniform plateau. These tools are carried, cherished, and used. The creatures, the Resonators, slowly, deliberately, use their forelimbs to pick up a tool. They lift it, and with an excruciatingly slow grace, they tap the surface of the plateau.

The tap makes no sound. It is an impact swallowed by the drone a billion times over. But it has an effect. Where the tool strikes the rock, a ripple of pure vibration, visible as a shimmer in the bright air, expands for a moment and is then re-absorbed into the great Hum.

This is what they do. This is all they do. They are the tuners of the world-instrument. They are the gardeners of the drone. They move across the stone, their entire being consumed with listening to the vibration through the ground. They sense a fluctuation, a tiny dissonance, an almost non-existent drift in the fabric of the sound. And they correct it. They choose a specific tool from their collection, a specific striker for a specific frequency, and they tap the world back into its perfect, eternal, deafening equilibrium.

Their sustenance is the drone itself. They metabolize the sonic energy, the constant, immense power vibrating through the very substrate of their existence. Their work is their eating. By maintaining the purity of the sound, they ensure the endlessness of their own feast. They are born into the work, and they will die in the middle of a long, slow swing, to be scoured clean by the vibration until their stony carapaces are just another feature of the land, their tools scattered for another to find and use.

There is no change here. The light is always bright. The air is always cool. The drone is always total. The Resonators are always tuning. It is a system in perfect, unyielding balance. It is not living or dead. It is simply in a state of performance. A single note, held for eternity, by a world that has become a cathedral of pure frequency, tended by a choir of silent stone.
