The air is a solid thing, something to be pushed through. It is water and heat, a palpable pressure on the skin, dense as oil. To breathe it is to drink a hot, metallic-tasting broth. This is the first and most constant sensation: the deep, geothermal warmth radiating not from a sun but from the rock underfoot, and the profound, suffocating humidity that clings to everything.

The space is a vault, a single, immense geode cracked open. Its scale is that of a grand cathedral nave, but it feels smaller, contained by the uniform darkness and the heavy atmosphere. The walls are not worn smooth by time but are a riot of sharp, fractured angles and shattered shear-faces. It is a young cavern, born of some great, recent violence. Shards of obsidian-black basalt and crystalline strata, glinting with embedded metals, jut out like broken teeth. The floor is an uneven field of this same geological debris, plates of stone tilted against each other, with fissures and gaps between them.

There is no ambient light. The darkness is near-total, a feature of the world, not an absence. Yet the space is not unseen. Life here has made its own light. Great, fungal forests rise from the rubble, their structures more architectural than botanical. Thick, pearlescent stalks, some as wide as pillars, ascend into the unseen heights of the vault, their caps fanning out and dripping with a viscous, glowing fluid. The light is a cold, silver-blue, pulsing with a rhythm so slow it is almost imperceptible. This bioluminescence is the only illumination, casting long, mobile shadows that creep across the floor at a glacial pace. The light catches on veils of velvet mosses that hang like tapestries from the sharper ledges, each droplet of condensation on their surfaces a tiny, captured star. Tendrils and vines, thick as pythons and slick with moisture, snake through the debris on the ground, their leaves not green but a deep, bruised purple, and they too glow with a faint, circulatory network of pale light in their veins. The vegetation is lush, a jungle born in perfect darkness and heat, thriving on mineral water and its own internal fire.

And through this impossible garden, there are marks of industry.

Scattered and sparse, a few pieces of immense, incomprehensible machinery interrupt the alien landscape. A massive iron catwalk, its grated floor half-eaten by a rust that looks more like a blooming fungus, stretches from one wall into the blackness, ending abruptly in mid-air. Below it, half-buried in rockfall and overgrown with the purple vines, is what looks like the housing for a colossal gear or flywheel, its bronze teeth green with verdigris.

Near the center of the chamber rests the source of the heat and moisture, the system's patient, dying heart. It is a boiler engine, a vast, riveted sphere of pitted iron, connected to the deep earth by a web of colossal pipes. It does not churn or roar. It breathes. Once, perhaps a century ago, it may have powered a great excavation or enterprise. Now, its cycle has slowed to a near-standstill. Every few minutes, there is a deep, internal *thump*, a sound felt in the chest more than heard, as a single, massive piston completes a stroke. With this beat, a plume of superheated, mineral-heavy steam vents from a filigreed grille at its apex. The steam hits the cool, high ceiling of the cavern and condenses, falling back as a constant, silent rain that sustains the fungal forest. This is the only weather.

There are no animals. No insects drone, no creatures scuttle in the shadows. The quiet is profound, broken only by the slow, resonant beat of the boiler-heart, the soft, near-inaudible hiss of the venting steam, and the rhythmic dripping of condensation from glowing mushroom caps onto stone. The overarching sound is a silence that has weight, a pressure against the eardrums.

The rules of this place are simple and locked in a slow, circular logic. The geological heat is harnessed by the engine. The engine produces steam. The steam creates rain. The rain feeds the light-producing flora. The flora illuminates the engine that sustains it. It is a closed and lonely terrarium, a forgotten industrial project that has accidentally birthed a new kind of wilderness. To be here is to feel like an intruder in a vast, sleeping machine that is having a long and intricate dream of being a forest. The air is thick with the slow passage of time, with the sense that one could stand here for a year and witness only a few pulses of light, a few beats of the iron heart, and the imperceptible growth of a single, glowing leaf.
