You find the place by accident, following a fissure that should not exist. The rock of the upper strata is ancient, worn smooth by eons of deep tectonic grind. But this crack is new. It is a sharp, geometric split, a wound in the stone so clean it might have been cut with a blade. The air that spills from it is not the cool, mineral-dusted breath of the deep earth, but a thick, unwavering warmth, like that of a sealed room. There is no scent of water, no dampness, only the dry, electric smell of ozone and hot stone.

You squeeze through. The passage is brief and angled, the walls black basalt faceted like poorly cut gems. Every edge is sharp enough to draw blood. Then, the passage opens, and you are no longer in a tunnel but within a place. It is a geode, a hollow room in the heart of the world, no larger than a cathedral's nave.

And it is loud.

The sound is the first and most overwhelming sensation. It is not the cacophony of a thousand sources, but a single, monolithic chord of immense power and complexity. It is a bass drone that vibrates in your sternum, overlaid with a billion shimmering, crystalline high-frequency notes. It is the sound of a living thing and a machine and a geological process, all happening at once, and all happening forever. The sound does not ebb or flow; it is a solid wall of noise, unwavering, unchanging. You have to clench your jaw to keep your teeth from chattering with the vibration.

Your eyes adjust to the dim, sourceless light. There is no sun, no fire, no single lamp. The light simply *is*, a diffuse, pewter-colored glow that emanates from the air itself, leaving no true shadows but only deepening the corners and crevices. The walls of the chamber are the same sharp-edged, un-eroded basalt as the passage, glittering with embedded mica.

From the floor, and in great, arcing chandeliers from the ceiling, grows the vegetation. It is lush but entirely alien. There is no soil. The "plants" emerge directly from the bare rock, their roots fine crystalline filaments that have prized open the stone. They are not soft or green. Great, branching forms of spun silica, like enormous, frosted glass sculptures, rise in sparse groves. Their leaves are blades of obsidian, so thin they are translucent, catching the dim light in wine-red and deep-purple glints. Plumes of what looks like white asbestos fiber, soft and deadly, drift in static, unmoving clouds from their tips. There is no decay, no fallen leaves, no cycle of life and death. Every frond, every stalk, every needle-sharp thorn is in a state of perfect, crystalline permanence. This is a jungle of glass and mineral, thriving in absolute dryness. It exists without a single molecule of water.

Among these vitreous groves, life is abundant. But it does not move.

Creatures swarm on the glass-barked trunks and perch upon the obsidian leaves. They are caught, frozen mid-motion, in a state that is not death but a perfect, horrifying stasis. Huge, beetle-like things with carapaces of polished agate are stopped halfway through the act of grinding their diamond-dust mandibles against a silica stalk. Flocks of smaller, winged creatures, like dragonflies made of mica and tourmaline, hang in the air in dense, motionless clouds, their wings a transparent blur that does not beat. On the floor, six-legged grazers with bodies of porous pumice stand with their heads lowered to lick at growths of rock salt, their tongues of solid chalcedony extended and still.

They are all alive. You can feel it. The overwhelming sound of the chamber—that is them. It is the chorus of their locked biologics, the immense energy of their life processes denied the release of motion or time. It is the sound of a billion creatures screaming into eternity without ever moving their mouths. Their existence is a single, sustained moment.

Scattered through this frozen, resonant menagerie are the only signs of deliberate craft. Small, waist-high cairns of stacked, flat stones are placed with unnerving precision in the clear spaces between the glass-plant groves. They are simple, brutalist things, built from the same sharp basalt as the walls. They are not shelters, nor altars in any recognizable sense. As you draw near one, you feel the vibration in the air change, the colossal sound focusing or warping slightly around the stones. They are tuning forks. Or dampeners. Crude, primitive structures built not to live in, but to modulate the crushing, eternal song of the chamber. Whoever built them is long gone, or perhaps they too have become part of the static landscape, indistinguishable from the fauna.

The air remains warm, the light remains dim, the sound remains absolute. Nothing changes. Not the temperature gradient between the floor and ceiling, not the position of a single frozen insect, not the pitch of the unceasing drone. This is a pocket universe where dynamic processes have been severed. It is a photograph being held in the hand of the world, a place that achieved a perfect, impossibly loud equilibrium and was locked there. To be here is to stand outside of time, in a room filled with the shout of life that can never take another step.
