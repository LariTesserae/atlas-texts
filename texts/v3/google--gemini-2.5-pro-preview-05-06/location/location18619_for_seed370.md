This place is a sound.

Before sight, before touch, before any other sense can report, there is the sound. It is not a noise that travels through the air to be heard, but a physical state of being. It is a pressure on the bones, a vibration that works its way into the marrow and rattles the teeth in their sockets. The sound is a chord of uncountable frequencies, a wall of stacked harmonics so dense it becomes a physical presence. It is a deafening, unchanging, eternal hum. It is the only thing that has ever happened here.

Your eyes adjust to the dim, warm light, and you see that the world is a vessel. Or rather, it is made of them. From floor to ceiling, in every direction, the space is a honeycomb of fired earth. Terracotta, ochre, umber, and soot-black, the world is a tightly packed lattice of millions upon millions of ceramic jars, urns, bells, and bowls. There is no ground, no sky, no wall that is not also this dense, overwhelming collection of pottery. You stand on the rounded shoulders of waist-high urns, their mouths stoppered with smaller, inverted bowls. Above you, a catenary arch is formed not of stone, but of the curved bellies of countless vessels mortared together with more fired clay. The scale is that of a grand cathedral nave, but it has the claustrophobia of a crawlspace. There is no room to move, only narrow, precarious pathways between the shoulders of jars, a maze where every surface is the same warm, resonant ceramic.

Everything is constructed. There is no natural substrate. This is a place built entirely by hand, and the evidence is everywhere. The curve of each pot is slightly irregular. Faint, ghostly whorls of ancient fingerprints are visible in the dim light, worn smooth by eons but still present. You can see the drag-marks where a simple tool smoothed a seam, the slight wobble in a rim made by a tired hand. This entire world-chamber was assembled piece by piece, an act of creation on a scale so vast and with technology so primitive it beggars belief. It speaks of a million hands working for a thousand years, or a thousand hands for a million.

And it is ancient. The parameter of erosion here is not one of wind or water, for there is neither. The erosion is a deep, internal weariness. The sound that fills the space is the agent of its own decay. The constant, high-energy vibration abrades every surface, slowly, molecule by molecule. The edges of the vessels, once perhaps crisp from the kiln, are now soft and rounded, like river stones. The intricate patterns that may have once adorned them are blurred into faint suggestions. The air—if it can be called that—is thick, warm, and utterly still. It is choked with a fine ceramic dust, the shed skin of this place, which coats every surface in a soft, velvety layer. To breathe it is to inhale the past.

The light is not cast from a source; it is an intrinsic property of the chamber. The immense sonic energy humming through the structure is converted to heat, and that heat manifests as a dull, internal luminescence. The vessels themselves glow with a faint, ruddy light, like embers in a cooling forge. The temperature is constant, a dry, enveloping warmth that emanates from every surface.

There is no life. No plants push through the cracks. No insects scurry in the dimness. No animals have ever left a track in the ancient dust. This place is sterile, abiotic. The only biology that ever touched it were the hands that shaped the clay, and they are gone, vanished into a past so deep it has no name. The sound is the only occupant. It is the fauna, the flora, the weather, and the god of this space.

This chamber is high, impossibly high. There is a sense, a subtle pressure differential, a feeling in the bones, that you are at a great altitude. Perhaps this entire cathedral of clay is the belfry of a tower so large its base is in another climate, another world. But here, at the peak, there is only the Kiln, the Resonator, the Chorus.

Nothing changes. The light does not wax or wane. The temperature does not fluctuate. The sound does not rise or fall; it is a fundamental constant of the physics of this environment. Time does not pass in events, but only in the slow, imperceptible sanding-down of the world by its own voice. To be here is to be trapped in a single, sustained musical note, held since the beginning of the world. It is a monument, a machine, or a prayer, built by vanished people for a purpose now lost, and it will continue to sing its one, deafening song in the high, dry stillness until it finally vibrates itself into dust.
