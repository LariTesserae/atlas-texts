The sound is the first thing, and the last. It is not a sound you hear with your ears alone; it is a pressure in the bones, a vibration that works its way up through the soles of your feet and settles deep in your chest. It is a drone, but a drone of impossible complexity, a million overlapping frequencies of resonant stone singing in chorus. It is the sound of the world’s own voice, and it is loud enough to make you feel you are trespassing inside a colossal bell just after it has been struck.

You stand at the edge of a basin that stretches to a misty, indeterminate horizon. The air is cool and heavy, saturated with a moisture that is not quite rain and not quite fog. It manifests as a perpetual, slow-motion condensation, beading on every surface and tracing paths like tears down the countless vertical forms that dominate the landscape. This world is a forest, but a forest with no life.

Here, the trees are blades of dark, vitreous mineral. They rise from the ground in dense, near-touching thickets, ranging from the height of a man’s thigh to three times his stature. They are not uniform; some are slender, sharp-edged sheets of what looks like obsidian, while others are thicker, composite structures of crystalline facets, catching the dim, sourceless light in baffling interior patterns. Their edges are fiercely sharp, recently formed, showing no sign of wear or wind-softening. This is a young geology, a world fresh from the kiln, and the air itself feels raw. Walking here is not a matter of following a path but of squeezing and turning sideways through the shoulder-to-shoulder ranks of these silent, singing monoliths.

The ground beneath them is no softer. It is a slick, black mud mixed with a grit of smaller shards and splinters, the detritus of whatever violent process formed this place. The mud isn't soil; it has no organic scent. It smells of ozone and wet rock. Water does not pool here; it is either absorbed into the viscous ground-paste or clings to the mineral blades, forever weeping downwards. Every surface glistens.

The density is oppressive. There is no open ground, only a relentless maze of stone. Yet, this is not a random chaos. Here and there, the pattern shifts. You might enter a clearing—not of empty space, but of arrangement. A perfect circle of taller, thinner blades, all angled slightly inward. Inside such a circle, the drone that fills the world changes key, becoming a purer, more focused tone, a column of sound that you can feel pass through you. These are the only structures: patterns in the field of stone, circles and spirals and long, straight avenues that lead from one resonant chamber to the next. They feel deliberate, anciently so, yet there is no mark of a tool, no sign of any hand but the one that shaped the blades themselves. They were not built; they were arranged, as if by magnetism or music.

Life is not absent, but it is a life that you might not recognize. For a long time, there is nothing but the vibrating stone. Then, a flicker of movement against the grain of the reflections. It is a creature, perhaps, though it seems more a piece of the landscape that has been granted slow volition. It is long and flat, its body segmented into plates of the same dark, glassy material as the blades. It clings to the side of a monolith, its form almost perfectly camouflaged. It moves with an imperceptible slowness, a geological creep, sliding over the wet surface. These are the Resonance Eaters. They do not hunt or graze in any recognizable way. They seem to feed on the sound itself, positioning themselves at points of harmonic convergence, their bodies trembling faintly, absorbing the world’s ceaseless vibration. They are rare, and to see one move is an event that might span an entire day.

There are no days. The light is a constant, dim silver-grey, filtering down from an unbroken ceiling of cloud that seems to rest directly on the tips of the tallest blades. There is no sun, no moon, only this permanent, liminal twilight. Change, here, is measured in the slow growth of the crystals, which might extend a millimeter in a century, or the gradual shift in the planet-wide drone as a new chamber arranges itself somewhere over the horizon. The world is sonically active, geologically young, but dynamically patient. It is a place of immense potential energy, suspended in a state of near-perfect, resonant stasis. To be here is to feel the world as a single, living, breathing, singing thing made of stone and sound.
