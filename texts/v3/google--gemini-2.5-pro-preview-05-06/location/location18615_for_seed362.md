This is the Vinery. The air is still and cool, carrying the clean, sterile scent of ozone and hot metal rapidly cooled. There is no soil underfoot. The floor is a vast and intricate lattice of milky, semi-translucent ceramic and dull grey metal, a circuit board the size of a continent. It is called the watt-soil, and from it, everything grows.

There is no water here. Not a drop. The concept feels alien, a forgotten chemistry. The life that fills this world is a form of electro-mineral vegetation, a kingdom of living glass and conductive tissue that draws its sustenance directly from the grid beneath. From every hexagonal node in the watt-soil, a plant rises. These are the vines. They are not green, but a spectrum of pale, luminous colors: the soft white of a gas tube, the faint blue of a distant electrical arc, the delicate peach-pink of a phosphor screen.

Their trunks are thick, braided cables of fibrous crystal and metallic filaments, coiling upwards to form a low, dense canopy that is also the ceiling. This ceiling is a riot of growth, a near-solid mass of interlocking leaves, flowers, and tendrils. The leaves are not leaves, but thin, flexible panes of bioglass that emit a brilliant, shadowless light, illuminating the entire volume of the world in a perpetual, high-noon glare that gives off no heat. The coolness of the place is a deep, cellar-like chill, the byproduct of the massive energy conversion happening at a molecular level within every cell of this crystalline flora.

The space is layered, unimaginably dense. The ground level, with its colonnades of glowing trunks, is only the first of countless strata. Look up, and through the glowing leaf-panes, you can perceive another forest layer above, and another above that, a palimpsest of worlds receding into a luminous haze. Look down through the translucent watt-soil, and you can see a mirror image: inverted forests hanging in the strata below, their light a deep-sea glow. The Vinery extends in all directions, a solid mass of constructed, living architecture. It is a basin of reality filled to the brim with itself.

This is a factory. The purpose of this endless, silent growth is the fruit. Periodically, at the intersection of several powerful vines, a node will form. It swells not with water, but with complex, charged polymers. It becomes a calyx of spun glass, and inside, a geometric solid of pure, crystalline data condenses. These logic-fruit, as they might be called, pulse with a slow, internal rhythm, their facets shifting according to some incomprehensible computation. They are lenses that warp probability, they are capacitors for storing moments of time, they are the crystallized grammar of an unknown physics.

The process is constant and silent. There is no birdsong, no wind. The only sounds are the subliminal, bass-frequency hum of the watt-soil and the occasional, high-pitched *tink* as a vine adjusts its crystalline structure, a sound like ice settling in a glass. Infrequently, a growth cycle completes. A vine will exhaust the local charge in its node and its light will dim. A wave of darkness will then slowly propagate through a section of the forest, a rolling brownout that is the Vinery's equivalent of weather. As the section goes dark, the fruit it bears hardens, and the harvest begins.

The fauna here are not born, but fabricated. They are the Gleaners. A Gleaner might be mistaken for a knot of fallen vines, a tangle of coaxial cable and dark, smoked glass. They move without sound, their multiple spindly legs finding purchase on the slick surfaces of the trunks. They are part of the system's immune and maintenance response. When a logic-fruit is ripe, a Gleaner will skitter along the branches, its manipulators unfolding with surgical precision. It does not pluck the fruit. It dissolves the stem with a focused beam of energy, catches the geometric prize in a padded receptacle, and scurries off, disappearing into the lattice to deposit its payload into a central conveyance chute, a river of machinery flowing through the heart of the Vinery.

Reality here is a thin veneer. The light from the leaf-panes does not cast simple shadows; it seems to soak into surfaces, creating penumbras of altered color. A path that appears straight may curve back on itself. Distances are unreliable; a column that seems a hundred paces away might be reached in ten, while an adjacent one remains forever distant. The Vinery is so old, so vast, and so completely artificial that it has begun to generate its own physical laws, a side-effect of the countless trillions of logic-fruit it has produced over the eons. The system is dynamic, a settled process that has achieved a state of weathered, perpetual motion. It is an artifact that has become an ecosystem, a machine that has become a world, endlessly building and harvesting itself in the cool, silent, waterless bright.
