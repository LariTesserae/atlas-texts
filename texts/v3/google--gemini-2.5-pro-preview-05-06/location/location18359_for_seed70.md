There is no surface. There may once have been, or there may be one yet to form, but here, in the suspended heart of the world, there is only the Medium. It is not water, not quite. It is a liquid substance far denser and warmer than any ocean, possessed of a gentle, uniform warmth that permeates everything, a pressure that is a constant, firm embrace. To be here is to be held.

You are on the flank of a great mountain, one of an entire range of spires and blades that thrusts up into the liquid infinity. The peaks are high, jagged, and young. Their rock is a dark, almost black basalt, fractured with clean, sharp edges that speak of recent geological violence. There are vast, sloping fields of scree where cliff faces have sloughed away, the fallen stones piled in pristine, un-eroded heaps. Over millennia, the soft, inexorable flow of the Medium would smooth these edges to pebbles, but that time is not now. Now, the world is new, its bones still showing.

Despite the sharpness of the stone, it is almost entirely hidden. Life has claimed this drowned sierra with a startling ferocity. This is a jungle, dense and layered. The canopy is formed not by trees, but by vast, sessile organisms that grow directly from the rock. They are the Glass-Palms, whose immense, fan-like fronds are not cellulose but a kind of flexible, living silica. These fronds, translucent and veined with threads of amber, angle themselves constantly toward the ambient light, catching it and focusing it downwards in soft, rippling spotlights. They sway with an impossible slowness in currents that are too gentle to feel.

Beneath them, the understory is a riot of tangled, fleshy vines. These are the Vessel-Vines, their surfaces smooth and rubbery, the color of bruises—deep purples, blues, and mottled greens. Along their lengths, they swell into rhythmically pulsing bladders, each one a warm, self-contained heart a meter across. They do not beat in unison; each follows its own patient, internal clock. Their slow, asynchronous thrumming is a motion felt more than seen, a deep, pervasive vitality that underlies the silence of the place. They wrap themselves around the basaltic bones of the mountain, anchoring in the cracks, their combined mass creating a dense, near-impenetrable lattice.

On the rock itself, where the vines have not yet reached, there are plush carpets of a velvety, moss-like growth. It bleeds color, from a startling, electric cobalt to a deep, resonant magenta. It is soft to the touch, and releases infinitesimal spores into the Medium, which hang like dust motes in the shafts of light cast by the Glass-Palms.

The light itself is a mystery. There is no sun, no single point of origin. The Medium itself seems to be the source, infused with a diffuse, silver-gold luminescence. It is perpetual day. Because the light comes from everywhere at once, shadows are soft, ill-defined things, existing only as gentle gradients of dimness in the deepest recesses of the vine-thickets. The open scale of the place is breathtaking. From a high ridge, you can see for miles. Other peaks, just as sharp and just as lush, rise from the luminous depths, their details fading into a soft, grey-blue haze. Valleys between them are vast basins of glowing fluid, their floors lost in a brighter concentration of the light.

The silence is profound. With no animals to call, hunt, or scurry, the soundscape is one of pure geology and fluid dynamics. There is a constant, sub-audible hum that seems to emanate from the rock itself—the strain of a young mountain range settling under its own immense weight. Occasionally, a single shard of rock will detach from a high crag and drift downwards with infuriating slowness, turning end over end for an hour before coming to rest on a velvet slope. The currents whisper, a sound so faint it is more a tactile sensation on the skin, a slight variation in the omnipresent pressure.

This is a world waiting for its second act. It has perfected a cycle of growth and stillness. The Glass-Palms draw energy from the glowing Medium. The Vessel-Vines draw minerals from the rock and, perhaps, from the Palms. The velvet mosses decompose what little waste is produced, recycling it back into the stone and the fluid. It is a closed, stable, and deeply lonely system. It is a garden without a gardener, a lung that breathes without a body, vibrant with a life that has not yet learned the concepts of hunger, or fear, or sound. It is a place of immense, patient potential, holding its breath in the warm, bright deep.
