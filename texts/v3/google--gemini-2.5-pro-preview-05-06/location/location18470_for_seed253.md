There is no air, only water. It is not the sloshing, surface-touched water of a terrestrial ocean, but a substance more akin to atmosphere: an omnipresent, uniform medium, dense and cold and absolute. It presses from all sides with a crushing, indifferent weight, but here, in the high strata, that pressure is a known constant, a law of existence. The darkness is just as total. There is no sun, no sky to filter it. The concept of "up" is an abstraction, a direction of slightly lessening pressure, but visually, it is indistinguishable from "down." All is abyss.

And yet, the darkness is not empty. It sings. A sound fills the void, a deep and resonant drone that is felt in the bones as much as it is heard. It is not a single note, but a chord of impossible complexity, a million different pitches layered into a single, overwhelming hum. It is the sound of life, a chorus sung by a billion throats, and it is the only constant besides the cold and the pressure. This is the Great Chord.

You are standing on a platform, a wide, gently curving expanse hundreds of meters across. It is not made of rock or metal. The ground beneath your feet is a dark, almost black material, feeling like petrified wood polished smooth by millennia of slow current. It is a floor grown, not built. You are perched on the capital of a pillar, a single, colossal stalk rising from an unseen foundation in the crushing depths below, reaching for a ceiling that does not exist. This place is The Arbor.

This world is a forest of such pillars. They are the only form of geology, the only substrate. Looking out from the edge of your platform, you can see the others. They are impossibly far away, yet their scale is so immense they remain visible: faint, ghostly columns of deepest violet and indigo, their outlines traced by the pinpricks of bioluminescent life that clings to them. Some are a dozen miles away, others fifty. The space between is a yawning, lightless gulf of water, punctuated only by the slow, drifting constellations of unknown creatures. The pillars are ancient and finished things. They do not grow, not anymore. Their life's work, a geological epoch of slow construction, is complete. They have secreted their own substance, a form of hyper-dense cellulose and silicate, and hardened into their final architectural forms. They are plant and building, botany and masonry as one. They have settled.

The surface of this pillar-capital is not flat. It is a landscape of its own. Root-like buttresses, each the size of a whale, arch over plazas of hardened xylem. Galleries and colonnades, formed by the intersections of ancient, ossified branches, create shadowed cloisters where the Great Chord seems to fraction into individual notes. The very structure is a filigree of organic architecture, a city grown by a single, silent mind over a span of time that beggars the imagination. Everything is made, but nothing was ever manufactured.

Life, the source of the sound and the sparse light, is everywhere upon this living stone. The dominant fauna are the Chordal Cilia, creatures resembling immense, armored sea anemones. Their bodies are rooted deep within the porous structure of the pillar, but their "petals" are long, hardened filaments of chitin that vibrate in the current, each one producing a specific, resonant frequency. A single creature might have a thousand filaments, and a single pillar hosts millions of creatures. They are the instruments, and the entire world is their resonating chamber. Their collective thrum is the Great Chord.

Around them, other life skitters and glides. Translucent, shrimp-like grazers, each the size of a human hand, move in swarms across the pillar's surface, feeding on microscopic energy-fixing films. Their passage is marked by shifting patterns of pale blue light that pulse from their carapaces. Larger things drift in the void between the pillars: immense, slow-moving entities that resemble manta rays made of woven shadow, their bellies aglow with a soft, sourceless green light. They are pilgrims or predators; from this distance, their intent is unknowable.

There is no weather. No seasons. No day or night. The temperature is a constant, just a few degrees above freezing. The slow, planetary currents are the only wind, a force that shifts over millennia, not hours. It is a world in a state of frozen equilibrium. The Chordal Cilia sing their eternal note. The grazers etch their glowing paths. The great pillars stand as silent monuments to a dynamism that has long since passed into stability. To an observer, it would seem that nothing ever happens. But to be here is to understand that everything has *already* happened. The world is a finished piece of music, holding its final, magnificent, and unending chord in the deep, cold dark.
