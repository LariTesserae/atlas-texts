The world is a bubble of silence suspended in the heart of stone. To be here is to be inside, contained. The pressure is a constant, steady embrace, a weight that is not crushing but absolute. The water is heavy, thick with dissolved minerals, cold enough that it feels not wet but viscous, a gel that slows all motion to a deliberate grace. There is no up or down, only a slow drift towards the source of a dim, internal light.

This place is a city, but it was not made with hands. It is a settlement under construction, growing not by plan or blueprint but by biological imperative. From the curved, unseen walls of this pocket-world, crystalline needles grow inwards. They are the bedrock, the raw material of this embryonic geology. They are flawless, blade-sharp, and translucent, catching and refracting the faint light in cold, geometric glints. This growth is the primary sound, a deep, sub-audible groan that is felt in the bones more than it is heard, the stress of a world coming into being.

Woven through and around these crystal spars is the city itself. It is a scaffold of living architecture, a dense, labyrinthine structure extruded by its own inhabitants. The material is a resinous secretion, pearlescent and softly pliant, warm to the touch if one could touch it. It forms a network of tunnels, archways, and sealed chambers, layered one upon the other, so dense that navigating it is like moving through the passages of a lung. The walls are porous, riddled with openings of all sizes, and they pulse with a slow, communal rhythm, the systole and diastole of a single, city-sized organism.

The builders are the dominant form of life, the source of both the light and the architecture. They are polyps of living flesh, sessile and silent, anchored within the resinous walls they create. Each one is a node in the network, a fleshy, gelatinous sac that glows with a faint, individual light—some a cool blue, others a soft violet, still others a deep amber. Their collective light filters through the semi-opaque resin, giving the entire metropolis a dim, dappled luminescence, like a city seen through a cathedral of stained glass at twilight. They are active, constantly filtering the mineral-rich water and extruding new layers of the living cement, extending tunnels, sealing breaches, and climbing the raw crystal spires like a slow-motion mold.

This place is not barren. Though the builders are stationary, the city teems. Small, crustacean-like scavengers, no larger than a thumb, crawl with deliberate slowness along the outside of the resinous tubes. Their multifaceted eyes, black as obsidian, observe the world without expression. They are janitors, picking at stray particulates or nibbling on the sparse vegetation—if it can be called that. These "plants" are not green but appear as thin, silvery filaments that cluster in patches on the older, more established crystal formations. They are chemosynthetic, drawing energy directly from the mineral flux, and they sway stiffly in the near-imperceptible currents.

Through the wider thoroughfares drift the grazers. These are larger creatures, the size of a human arm, with slug-like bodies of a deep, velvety black. They move by gentle, rhythmic undulations of a membrane that runs the length of their form. They are predators here, feeding on the glowing polyps. A grazer will attach its circular, toothless mouth to an opening in the resinous wall, and the light of the polyp within will flicker, dim, and extinguish. The city feels no pain, only a localized signal to rebuild. The grazer will detach, leaving a dark patch in the glowing wall, and continue its slow, silent patrol.

There is no sky, only the suggestion of a vast, curving emptiness beyond the reach of the growing spires. From time to time, a tremor will run through the entire vesicle. A new fissure may have opened in the outer shell, miles away, allowing a fresh influx of super-saturated fluid. In response, the crystal needles will sing their deep, groaning song of growth, and the city of polyps will pulse a little faster, its collective light brightening for a moment as it metabolizes the new nutrients, building itself ever denser, ever more elaborate in the cold, silent, hidden dark. It is a world perpetually dawning.
