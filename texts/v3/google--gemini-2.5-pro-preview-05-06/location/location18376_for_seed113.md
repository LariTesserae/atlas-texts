The sound comes first, a constant, complex roar that fills the skull. It is not a single note, but a chord of immense forces. There is the bass-heavy, bone-deep thrum of machinery operating on a scale that feels geological, a vibration that works its way up through the soles of your feet. Layered over this is the tireless, percussive crash and hiss of moving water, like a thousand waterfalls falling at once. It is the sound of a world's metabolism, loud and unceasing.

The air is warm, thick, and carrying so much moisture it feels less like a gas and more like a fluid you wade through. Every breath is a drink. It clings to your skin, beading on any exposed surface, smelling of wet rock, ozone, and a clean, sharp scent like freshly-snapped green stems. There is no sky. You are underground, in a chamber vast enough to be a cathedral nave, but contained. You can see the far wall, a dim shape across a gulf of mist-choked air, perhaps half a kilometer away.

The architecture is skeletal. Vast, arcing ribs of a matte black material, like spun basalt or unpolished obsidian, crisscross the void. They are impossibly thin for the spans they cross, forming a lattice that serves as floor, walls, and ceiling simultaneously. There are no solid surfaces, only this intricate, multi-layered framework, creating catwalks, terraces, and galleries that spiral up into the dimness. The material is flawless. Its edges are blade-sharp, its faces unmarred by erosion or wear. It seems to have been extruded into place moments ago, still cooling, though it is warm and damp to the touch. This is the city, the urban structure, but it is hollow, a city of scaffolds with no buildings to erect. The space between the structural filaments is vast, giving the overwhelming impression of emptiness.

Water and life cling to this framework. The black material weeps. It does not drip or leak, but perspires, a constant, silent film of clean water that coats every surface. This water is the source of the dim light. It holds a soft, internal luminescence, a violet-white glow that outlines the architecture in faint light. Where this water pools in the open cells of the lattice, it forms shimmering, glowing basins.

From these glowing pools and damp surfaces, the vegetation grows with a desperate, silent vigor. It is not soil-bound, but epiphytic, anchoring itself directly to the slick black struts. Thick, velvety mosses in shades of electric green and deep burgundy carpet entire sections of the framework. Ferns with fronds like jade fractals unfurl from junctions, some as large as a person. Broad-leafed vines, their foliage a waxy, dark emerald, snake along the girders, their stems studded with glowing, fluid-filled nodes that pulse with the same faint light as the water. This is not an ecosystem; it is a hydroponic array. The plants are not wild, they are components, their placement and type too deliberate, too organized. They are filters, or processors, or living conduits, integrated into the life support of the place.

The most profound emptiness is the biological one. For all the lush greenery and a volume of water that could sustain a world, there is no animal life. No insects hum in the humid air. No fish or amphibians stir the glowing pools. No birds call from the high scaffolds. No scurrying things rustle the ferns. The only sounds are the machine-thrum and the water-roar. The only movement is the slow, deliberate unfurling of a new frond, the swirl of mist in the central void, and the rhythmic pulse of light through the water and the plant nodes.

The whole place is active, alive. It is a system in motion. The massive, unseen pumps that drive the water create currents in the air, stirring the mist into slow, ghostly cyclones. The light level shifts on a long, slow cycle, the violet-white brightening and dimming with the tidal pulse of the machinery. Condensation rains down from the upper reaches, not as drops, but as a perpetual, clinging fog that drifts to the floor.

This is a vivarium built for inhabitants who are absent, or perhaps were never intended to be biological at all. It is a lung built to breathe for a body that never arrived. To stand on one of the black, humming catwalks is to feel like a ghost, an anomaly in a perfectly functioning, utterly alien machine that masquerades as a garden. You are the only thing here that doesn't belong, the only variable in a closed, calculated equation of water, light, structure, and engineered life.
