The first thing is the sound.

It's not a sound you hear with your ears alone, but one you feel in the hollows of your bones, a deep and resonant thrumming that works its way into your teeth. It is the bass note of a universe, a constant, foundational hum that suggests a machine the size of a mountain is breathing somewhere nearby. This roar is so pervasive it becomes a form of silence; smaller noises are simply swallowed by it, lost in the overwhelming vibration that saturates the air. The air itself is thick, hot, and wet, clinging to your skin like a film of oil. It carries a metallic, ozonic tang, the exhalation of vast and ancient engines.

You are in a place of profound darkness, a blackness so complete it feels structural. There is no ambient light. But it is not a static darkness. Every eighty-three seconds, the thrumming deepens, the pressure in the air intensifies, and a silent, horizontal wave of pale cyan light sweeps through the chamber from an unseen source. For a single, clinical moment, the landscape is revealed.

And it is a city. Or the skeleton of one.

You stand on a floor of obsidian-like material, worn glass-smooth in great swathes as if by the passage of glaciers, but pocked and etched in others. It is not flat. It slopes gently, carved with immense, shallow channels that guide a slow, constant trickle of warm water. This is the source of the dampness: the city sweats. The immense heat generated by its processes meets the cooler strata of rock miles above, causing a perpetual, clammy condensation that weeps down the titanic structures and gathers in these engineered rivulets.

The structures themselves defy simple description. In the brief flash of cyan light, you see towers that are not towers, spires of void-black ceramic that refuse to hold an afterimage. They are arrayed in a fashion that feels both organized and organic, like the logic of a crystal lattice writ large. They soar up into the oppressive darkness, beyond what the pulse of light can reveal, their upper reaches lost in the gloom. Walkways, impossibly thin and without railings, bridge the chasms between them. Conduits thicker than ancient trees snake along the bases of the spires, disappearing into the floor. There are no doors, no windows, no signs of habitation. It is architecture as pure infrastructure, a settlement built for a purpose, not for people.

The scale is that of a cathedral’s nave, but repeated infinitely in all directions, though you can perceive its edges—a final row of pillars, and then a sheer, weeping cliff face of raw, unworked stone that marks the boundary of this excavated chamber. Yet for all its layered construction, the space is profoundly empty. The gulfs between the black spires are vast, hangar-like voids filled with nothing but the hot, humming air. It’s a city with all its buildings but none of its volume, a metropolis of negative space.

In the near-constant darkness between the pulses of light, other, smaller lights become visible. Clinging to the lower sections of the spires and the edges of the water channels is a sparse, spectral growth. It’s not quite moss, not quite fungus, but a pale, fibrous lichen that feeds on vibration. It absorbs the city's ceaseless hum and transduces it into a faint, sickly green luminescence. It provides just enough light to see the glistening sheen on the floor a few feet ahead, to make out the ghostly outlines of the nearest structures. This is the only vegetation.

Life here is rare, and what exists is alien. If you watch the glowing lichen-beds long enough, you might see movement: a tremor not in time with the city's rhythm. It is a creature like an oversized, flattened woodlouse, perhaps a foot long, with a shell of what looks like smoked glass. Through its translucent carapace, you can see its pale organs pulse faintly. It moves with a deliberate slowness, scraping the resonance-lichen from the ceramic with complex, delicate mandibles. These creatures are the only fauna you have seen, their silent, methodical grazing a tiny, living process lost in the overwhelming, non-living activity of the city.

The world has rules. The cyan pulse is not just light; it carries a charge. Any water it touches flashes momentarily into vapor, a sharp hiss lost in the greater roar. The creatures know to retreat into fissures in the floor just before it comes. The sound itself is a dynamic force; its pitch and timbre can shift subtly, creating zones where the vibration is so intense it can blur your vision or make you nauseous. This is an active place. It is a machine executing its function. The water cycle is a coolant system. The pulses are data-purges or recalibrations. The entire cavern is a single, integrated piece of technology that has been running in the dark for millennia, its surfaces worn into a state of equilibrium by its own endless operation. It is not decaying; it has settled.

It is loud, and it is empty. It is urban, and it is a void. It is a heart without a body, still beating furiously in the dark, circulating heat and energy for a purpose long forgotten, its powerful, lonely rhythm the only thing left alive in the deep.
