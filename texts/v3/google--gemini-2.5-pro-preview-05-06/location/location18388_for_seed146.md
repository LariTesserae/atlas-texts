You are in the deep.

The first thing you notice is not a thing, but the absence of things. The absence of chill. The absence of movement in the air. The absence of moisture. The air itself feels ancient, a dry, warm blanket that has not been disturbed for millennia. It is thick, a palpable, viscous medium you have to push through, and it carries no scent of water, soil, or decay. Instead, it smells of hot stone, ozone, and a faint, sharp tang like shattered flint. It’s quiet here. The muffling density of the atmosphere swallows sound almost as soon as it is made. A footstep is a dull thud with no echo. A dislodged pebble makes a single, sharp *tok* and then is silent.

You stand on a floor that is not a floor. It is a vast, undulating plain of interlocking, crystalline plates. They are black, smoky gray, and shot through with veins of dull, metallic ore. The edges of the plates are not worn down but are fractured and sharp, freshly broken though the break must have happened in a time beyond reckoning. They jut up at odd angles, forming canyons of shadow and jagged, knife-edge ridges that could slice through leather and bone. This is a world formed by fracture, not flow. It cracked into existence and has been locked in this configuration ever since.

Look up. There is no sky. There is no ceiling. There is only a void so profound and absolute that your mind rebels against the scale of it. The space above is not empty, however. Drifting in the immense, silent darkness are the Glimmer-Kites. They are vast, diaphanous beings, their bodies little more than translucent membranes stretched over delicate, chitonous frames. They drift on the slow, colossal thermal updrafts that are the world’s only weather. And they glow. Each one pulses with a cold, internal light—some a soft blue, others a ghostly green or a faint, throbbing amber. They are the constellations of this underworld, a slow-motion celestial ballet in a sky of stone. Their light is the only light, and it is never enough. It paints the razored landscape in ephemeral, shifting patterns of shadow and dim revelation.

This is a world without water, and a world without plants. Yet it is teeming with life. The base of the pyramid is not flora, but fauna. Clinging to the crystalline plates, especially in the warmer zones where the geothermal heat seeps up from the core below, are the Lithovores. They resemble patches of metallic lichen or rust-colored velvet, sessile creatures that live by chemosynthesis, slowly digesting the mineral plates they are anchored to.

Over these fields of living rust swarm the Scrabblers. They are the source of the density here, the feeling that you are never truly alone. They are creatures the size of a human hand, armored in silicate shells that perfectly mimic the smoky crystal of the terrain. They move in tireless, chittering tides, their hundreds of needle-like legs making a sound like sand skittering across glass, a sound that is immediately dampened by the heavy air. They scrape the Lithovores from the plates with mandibles hard enough to score metal, their bodies processing the mineral-rich flesh and excreting pellets of refined, useless dust. Their population is a constant, a perfect equilibrium between the growth of the Lithovores and the appetites of their predators.

Everything is in equilibrium. The heat from below is constant. The number of Glimmer-Kites, born and dying in cycles that span centuries, remains the same. The population of Scrabblers never wavers. Change is not a concept this world understands. There has never been a dawn. There will never be a dusk. It is a system in perfect, frozen stasis.

And yet, there are marks of intrusion. Scattered across the plains, almost lost in the vastness, are signs of other beings. Not the local fauna, but something else. Here, a simple structure of stacked, razor-sharp crystal shards, forming a low wall that offers no real protection. There, a glyph carved into the face of a towering crystal monolith—a symbol that is not geometric, not a picture, but something in between, a complex spiral that seems to pull the dim light into itself. These are not cities or ruins. They are the last, faint whispers of a presence. The makers used simple, hard tools—flint-like strikers to chip away at the impossibly durable crystal. They were a people attempting to map the infinite, to leave a mark on a place that does not record time. Where they came from, what they sought in this warm, dry, eternal darkness, and what became of them are questions the silence refuses to answer.

Deeper in the chasms, away from the faint light of the Kites, live other things. You hear no sound from them, but you can feel the pressure of their passage in the thick air. You see the places where Scrabbler tides have been scattered, the metallic velvet of the Lithovores scraped clean by something other than a Scrabbler's mandible. Shadows move within shadows. The Glimmer-Kites occasionally twitch, their soft light flaring as something unseen passes beneath them through the vast, empty mid-levels.

This place is a paradox: a geologically young landscape that has been static for eons. It is a vast, continental space that feels claustrophobic and intimate. It is barren of all that a life-form from a wet, green world would recognize, yet it is saturated with its own strange biology. It exists in a permanent state of warm, dark, quiet grace, a perfectly balanced machine of mineral and non-aqueous flesh, utterly indifferent to your presence. The only rule is that nothing ever changes. You are the only dynamic thing here, and the silence seems to be waiting for you to stop moving.
