The first thing is the sound. It is not a sound you hear with your ears so much as a sound you feel in your bones, a physical pressure that works its way through your soles and vibrates up your spine. It is a deafening, grinding, all-encompassing roar, a constant cascade of a trillion tiny clicks, hisses, and chitters layered one on top of the other until they merge into a single, monolithic drone. The sound is the sound of a mountain-sized engine made of living parts. It is the baseline of existence here, so constant that silence would be the true shock.

The air is thick, warm, and wet. It smells of damp soil, of mushroomy decomposition, of ozone and musk, the combined exhalations of a billion lungs. It is so saturated with moisture that your own breath fogs in front of your face, instantly joining the soupy atmosphere. Droplets of condensation, heavy with dissolved biomatter, gather on every surface and fall with a slow, thick *plap* that is utterly lost in the cacophony. There is no weather here, no rain from a distant sky. The water is here, born of the air, a product of the endless metabolic heat.

You are standing on a wide, curving rampart high within a cavern of impossible scale. The word "cavern" is not quite right; it implies something dug from rock. This place was not dug. It was grown. Everything is built. The floor beneath you is not stone but a dark, resinous material, smooth and polished in a wide trough down its center from the passage of untold eons of bodies, but rougher and more porous at the edges. It feels faintly yielding, like hard leather.

Looking out, you see… nothing, at first. A vast, warm darkness. The roar of the unseen suggests a space as large as a valley. But then, a flicker. Far below, a ripple of cold, blue-green light pulses through a dense mass, and for a moment you see it: a river. Not of water, but of creatures. A vast, flowing tide of carapaced bodies, each the size of a thumb, packed so densely they move as a single fluid. The light is from them, a wave of bioluminescence triggered by their jostling passage. The river of light flows for a minute, then two, before the pulse subsides and that section of the great chamber is plunged back into darkness.

Then another pulse, this one higher up and to your left. It illuminates one of the great pillars holding up the vaulted ceiling, which is lost in the oppressive blackness far above. The pillar is a hundred meters thick, a gnarled, braided column of the same living secretion that forms the floor. It is not a solid thing, but an intricate latticework, a scaffold of interwoven, petrified sinew. And it is teeming. In the brief, ghostly light, you see that its entire surface is a vertical city. Countless forms, larger and more distinct than the river-creatures below, cling to the scaffolding. Some are bag-like, rhythmically pulsing and filtering the air. Others are spindly, spider-like grazers, methodically scraping away at fungal growths that bloom in orange and violet patches.

The "vegetation" here is not separate from the architecture; it is part of it. The lushness is in the layers. Fungal gardens, fed by the waste of the grazers, cover entire walls in velvety carpets. Thick, rope-like growths, a form of symbiotic vine, weave themselves into the chitinous structure, reinforcing it and acting as conduits for nutrients. Everything is either food or a home, or both. The environment is a closed-loop, an incredibly efficient system of growth, consumption, and excretion that has been in balance for millennia.

The sheer density of life is overwhelming. It is the fauna. They are the architects, the janitors, the farmers, and the structure itself. Clouds of tiny, winged insects, more like animate dust motes, drift in the warm updrafts, their combined wingbeats contributing a high-pitched hiss to the overall drone. Slow, armored beasts, the size of boulders, inch their way along the wider ledges, their immense weight causing the structure to groan. They are not animals living in a place; they *are* the place. Their bodies, shed carapaces, and petrified remains form the ever-growing walls. Their warmth is the climate. Their breath is the atmosphere. Their collective voice is the thunderous, unending sound.

This is a world without tools because the world *is* the tool. It changes, but on a geological timescale. A pillar may grow a few inches in a century. A flow-river of creatures may alter its course over a millennium, wearing a new, smooth channel into the living floor. There is no suddenness here, only the slow, inexorable, and crushingly loud process of life building on top of itself, reaching ever higher into the warm, absolute dark.
