This world is a cavern, but the term feels insufficient, like calling a star a spark. It is a geode hollowed out to the size of a province, a vastness held within a shell of unseen, primordial rock. The air is cool and still, carrying the scent of cut lumber, ozone, and something else—a sweet, sharp, and deeply unfamiliar tang, like fermented honey and static electricity. It is the smell of life without water.

The ground is not stone or soil. It is a floor of newly formed cellulose, a springy, fibrous mat that gives slightly underfoot. In places, it is still raw and pulpy, venting thin wisps of vapor as organic polymers link and set. This is the foundation, the very beginning of the world's substance, laid down by some unseen process. It spreads out into an immense, sparsely populated plain.

From this plain rise the structures of a city. They are not built, but grown. These are towers of lignified, woody tissue, colossal vegetable forms that dwarf the greatest redwoods. Their bark is the color of bone, layered and cross-hatched with the structural intelligence of a thousand generations of growth. They do not branch into canopies but instead rise in fluted, buttressed columns, their peaks lost in the luminous upper atmosphere of the cavern. They are organized, arranged in clusters with vast, open spaces between them, forming boroughs of silent, organic skyscrapers.

This is a city under construction. Living scaffolds of green, pliable vine cling to the sides of half-finished spires. You can see the slow, deliberate pulse of growth, a thickening of a wall here, the extrusion of a new archway there. Sap, thick and viscous as warm glass, weeps from fresh cuts, hardening into amber-colored scabs.

There is no sun. Light, bright and clear as a terrestrial noon, pours down from the cavern's distant ceiling. It isn’t a single point source, but a diffuse, even glow emanating from a vast grid of industrial lamps and reflectors suspended in the high dark. These are powered by the city itself. Running along and through the architectural trunks are great, hardened arteries—pipes of polished, petrified vascular tissue. Within them, a slow, heavy current of refined resin flows. This is the world’s lifeblood, its energy, its currency. Factories, nestled in the hollowed-out bases of the towers, are not places of fire and steam, but of biological alchemy. They are distilleries and refineries, vast, humming chambers where the raw, sugary resin is cracked, energized, and transformed into light, motive force, and the raw material for new growth.

The soundscape is one of deep quiet punctuated by industrial life. The city doesn't roar; it hums. A low, resonant thrumming vibrates up through the cellulose floor, the collective purr of a thousand organic machines. Occasionally, this is broken by the sharp *crack* of a new structural beam locking into place, or the high-pitched whine of a cutting laser trimming an errant growth. The sound is swallowed by the sheer scale of the place and the sound-dampening quality of the fibrous architecture.

And in this dry, resin-fueled world, there is abundant life. It does not drink water. The concept is alien. Life here subsists on the resin. Small, beetle-like creatures with chitinous drill-bits for mouthparts bore into the softer tissues of the city, siphoning the fluid. They glisten, their shells lacquered with sticky residue. Larger grazers, like six-legged, wingless moths the size of sheep, roam the open plains between the tower-clusters. They use long, flexible proboscises to tap into the main resin conduits, their feeding a slow, patient act. Their movements are silent on the soft ground, their flocks casting long, sharp shadows in the perpetual daylight.

Where there are grazers, there are hunters. Pale, mantis-like predators stalk the architectural canyons. They are biomechanical in their precision, all sharp angles and jointed limbs, moving with a speed that disrupts the cavern's calm. They don't drink either; they get their vital fluids from the bodies of their prey, their serrated forelimbs designed to split an armored carapace and access the rich, energy-dense hemolymph within.

The world is active, dynamic. Growth crews, beings in sealed suits piloting spider-like mechs, crawl over the structures, guiding the city's expansion. They are not building so much as gardening on a titanic scale. Far out on the plains, you can see the frontier of creation: a line of mist where the cellulose floor is actively precipitating from nothing, expanding the city's territory into the silent, waiting void of the cavern. This new ground is quickly colonized by the first waves of fauna, following the scent of untapped nutrients. Everything is part of a closed, bizarre loop. The city grows itself, fuels itself with its own sap, and provides the substrate and sustenance for an entire ecosystem that has never known a single drop of rain. It is a single, sprawling organism, dreaming itself into existence in the cool, silent dark deep beneath the surface of a forgotten world.
