The ground is a fine, black grit, the texture of crushed obsidian. It extends in every direction across a vast, windless plateau at the roof of the world. There is no soil here, no moisture, only this sterile, light-absorbing dust. The cold is not the damp chill of a winter’s day, but the absolute, molecular stillness of a place profoundly lacking in energy. The air, thin and without scent, feels like a vacuum against the skin.

Above, there is no sky, only a uniform, sourceless ceiling of dim grey light. It is the color of a monitor that has been turned off, a non-luminous luminescence that casts no shadows. The world is rendered in flat, soft tones, like an old photograph bleached of its contrast.

And in this grey twilight, the city rises.

It's a city without builders, an urban sprawl that has grown according to some alien, non-technological blueprint. There are buildings, or things that hold the shape of buildings—towers that pierce the grey ceiling, broad-shouldered ziggurats, delicate spans like frozen bridges connecting nothing to nothing. But they are not made of stone or metal. They seem to be composed of solidified haze, a fibrous, semi-translucent material the color of ash and old cobwebs. It looks like asbestos, or felted dust, or spun glass. The edges are soft, ill-defined. A skyscraper that scrapes the upper dimness might have the texture of a cloud and the soft, rounded corners of a form worn down by millennia of something other than friction. This is not erosion by wind or water, for there is none. This is an erosion of certainty. The structures look ancient, settled into their final forms, yet they seem to have the permanence of smoke.

This is the Shroud. It is not static. Look away from a slender arch and look back, and its curve may be slightly different—not that it has moved, but that your memory of it and its present reality have failed to perfectly align. The city is an active state, not a collection of objects. It precipitates. In the profound silence, you can almost hear it, a sound at the threshold of perception, a faint, dry static, like the hiss of a dead radio channel. It is the sound of reality congealing. Threads of this grey, fibrous matter will spontaneously knit themselves together in the thin air, thickening from a faint mist into a solid, weight-bearing girder in the space of an hour. Elsewhere, a cathedral-sized dome might be slowly unraveling, its material thinning back into concept, fraying at the edges and becoming indistinguishable from the ambient grey.

The city is vast but hollow. The density of matter is preposterously low. You can walk for miles through plazas paved in the same dark grit, past silent, empty colonnades and towers whose interiors are just more of the same—empty space, structured by the slow, inexorable grammar of the Shroud. The architecture is layered, complex, and utterly without purpose. It is a settlement for no one.

But the world is not entirely dead. There is life, of a sort. It is rare, a brief punctuation in the monochrome emptiness. Sometimes, where a wall is dissolving with particular speed, a flicker of motion will catch the eye. It is one of the Glimmer-fauna, attracted to the energy released as matter loses its form. They are beings that defy easy description. One might appear as a fracture of impossible color, a shard of vibrant crimson light that skitters across a decaying façade before shattering into nothing. Another may look like a patch of oil-slick iridescence breathing on the surface of a pillar, a living stain that flows without a body. They have no discernible anatomy, no metabolism in the biological sense. They are patterns that feed on the un-making of the world. They are silent, ephemeral, and utterly indifferent. They are the only other moving things here.

The fundamental rule of this place is that form is not permanent, and existence is a negotiation. Distance is unreliable; a tower on the horizon may remain the same size for hours as you walk toward it, then suddenly loom over you in a matter of moments. Space itself seems to warp around the denser concentrations of the Shroud. To be here is to feel the foundations of your own perception begin to soften, to feel your own physical certainty start to fray at the edges, just like the slow-dissolving architecture. The quiet is the quiet of a thought experiment, the emptiness is the space between equations, and the city is the ghost of a solution, slowly, constantly being written and erased.
