The ground is not ground. It is a single, continuous living thing, a planetary sheath of biomechanical plant matter. You stand upon the high plateau of the Xylem Mat, an expanse so ancient its growth has slowed to the pace of geology. The air is thin, cool, and carries a static charge that makes the fine hairs on your arms stir. There is no water here. There has never been water. The very concept is a foreign chemistry.

The surface underfoot is not soft, but resilient, a dark, bruised-purple color like the skin of a plum. It has the texture of worn, hard leather, cross-hatched with micro-fissures from which a faint, internal silver light pulses in slow, continent-spanning waves. These are the circulatory or data pathways of the Mat. The entire plateau, a landform the size of a small country, is a single organism.

From this living substrate, at immense and sparse intervals, rise the true titans of this world. They are not trees, but vast, differentiated organs of the Mat itself. To your left, a league away, stands a Grove of Pillars. They are colossal, fluted columns of the same purple-black material, each a kilometer tall, their tops lost in the dim, violet-grey haze that serves as a sky. They show their age in their form; they are not sharp and aspirational but worn smooth, like river stones, their surfaces pocked with hollows the size of houses from eons of micrometeorite rain. They are not dead; the silver light pulses sluggishly up their colossal trunks.

To your right, the Mat has pleated itself into a series of immense, frozen Folds, like a carelessly thrown blanket of continental proportions. These gentle, sweeping ridges are kilometers long and hundreds of meters high, their crests catching the faint, ambient light. Their form is the final, stable state of some primordial upheaval, now utterly settled. This is a landscape at the end of its own history, perfected and unchanging.

The light is a constant, sourceless twilight. There is no sun, no moon. The high, thin atmosphere glows with a weak, diffuse luminescence, a perpetual silver-violet cast that renders shadows soft and indistinct. In this dimness, the world seems both vast and intimate.

And it is loud. The silence you might expect of such a desolate vista is absent, replaced by a profound and complex hum. It is not the wind, which is a mere whisper at this altitude. It is the sound of the world’s metabolism. A deep, resonant bass note underpins everything—the thrum of nutrient-analogues being processed in deep subterranean organelles, a sound felt in the bones more than heard. Layered over this is a chorus of higher-pitched chimes, whistles, and clicks. This is the fauna.

Creatures abound, perfectly integrated into this waterless ecology. Skittering across the Mat are Resonator Crabs, beings the size of small dogs with crystalline carapaces that chime as they move. They do not eat; they press themselves against the silver fissures in the ground, drawing energy directly. Flocks of Glasswing Skimmers, things like dragonflies with membranous, iridescent wings, drift on the thin air, their flight accompanied by a high-frequency shriek that modulates as they communicate. They hunt motes of free-floating energy, catching them in shimmering nets of force that crackle between their mandibles. Nothing here drinks. Nothing bleeds a liquid. Their 'blood' is a stream of cohesive light or a cascade of self-replicating nanocites.

Woven into this ancient, living landscape is a city. It is not built *on* the Mat; it is coaxed *from* it. Xylos. The settlement follows the curve of a valley between two of the great Folds. The architecture is biological, grown. Towers are modified pillars, their interiors hollowed out and lined with phosphorescent moss that casts a gentle, green-white glow. Walls are translucent membranes, revealing the slow, steady pulse of data-sap through vascular bundles thick as your arm. Streets are merely designated pathways on the living Mat, differentiated by a slightly smoother texture.

This is a place of incomprehensible technology that has become indistinguishable from nature. The inhabitants, rarely seen, do not build; they program. They are gardeners of a silicon-based, sentient landscape. Their 'infrastructure' is a series of resonant chambers carved into the base of the Folds, structures that amplify the Mat's hum into coherent sonic broadcasts—news, art, warnings. The city breathes. Its lights flicker in rhythm with the silver pulses in the ground. Its soundscape is a layer of organized intelligence atop the planet’s raw hum.

The world is active. A pulse of energy, visible as a wave of brilliant silver-blue light, races across the surface of the plateau from a distant Pillar. As it passes, the Glasswing Skimmers shriek and scatter, and the Resonator Crabs flatten themselves, their chiming silenced for a moment. This is weather. Not of rain or wind, but of information and energy, the lifeblood of the Mat cycling through its gargantuan form. After the wave passes, the normal, complex thrumming of the world resumes, a noise so constant and all-encompassing it becomes a form of silence. You stand in the heart of a living machine the size of a world, a city grown from its flesh, under a dim sky, breathing cool, sterile air that has never known a cloud.
