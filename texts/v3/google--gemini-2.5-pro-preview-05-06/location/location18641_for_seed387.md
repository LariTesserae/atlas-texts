This place is not a body of water. It is the inside of a sound.

The medium is liquid, thick and warm like blood plasma, but it carries pressure and vibration with an impossible density. To be within it is to be submerged in a continuous, deafening chord. It is a physical force, a deep, resonant, impossibly complex hum that seems to originate from everywhere and nowhere at once. It has a texture. You feel it in your teeth, a grinding shriek that vibrates through your skeleton. You feel it in your chest, a bass note so profound it feels like a slow-motion tectonic event happening inside your own body. This is the baseline, the atmosphere. Sound is the air, the water, the sky.

This entire environment is contained. It is a pocket, a hidden chamber the size of a small town, teardrop-shaped and buried deep beneath a surface that is not knowable from here. The walls of this chamber are not visible. The liquid extends into a dim, murky infinity, but the active space is contained by the currents. Here, the liquid is not still. It is a storm in a bottle, a permanent state of turbulence. Vast, slow, cyclonic currents churn in predictable, eons-old patterns. Ghostly cataracts of fluid pour downwards into nothingness, while screaming vortices spiral upwards, their edges defined by a faint, shimmering distortion. To be caught in one is to be torn apart with agonizing slowness.

Floating in this violent, sonorous void is the city. It is not a city for inhabitants, but an urban-scale machine. Colossal structures drift in formation, anchored by forces that defy the chaos of the currents. They are not built of metal, but of a dull, black, crystalline substance that seems to have precipitated directly from the liquid medium. They resemble inverted mountain ranges, their peaks pointing toward a central, unseen point. Facets the size of city blocks catch what little light exists, glinting with a greasy, non-reflective sheen. These are the Resonators.

This is an industrial place. The entire chamber is a single engine, and its purpose is to generate the sound. The black crystalline spires are riddled with tunnels and conduits, through which the fluid is forced at incredible pressures. The shrieking overtone in the deafening hum is the sound of the liquid screaming as it is pushed through orifices miles long but only inches wide. The deep, bone-shaking bass is the thrum of the entire crystalline structure vibrating in sympathetic resonance. It has been doing this for an incomprehensible length of time. The edges of the structures are worn smooth, weathered not by grit but by millennia of sonic friction and turbulent flow. This is its established, balanced state: a perpetual, high-energy roar.

There is no vegetation. The ecology is mineral and mechanical. The only light comes from the process itself—a dim, orange-red glow that bleeds from deep within the larger crystalline towers. These are the furnaces, the hearts of the machine, where the liquid is super-heated and pressurized before being channeled into the resonating spires. The light is faint, painting the churning fluid in hellish tones and ensuring that you can never see the full scale of the city at once. You only see one spire, or a bridge-like conduit connecting two unseen masses, hanging in the roaring, turbulent dark. The emptiness between these titanic components is vast, a gulf of crushing noise and chaotic motion.

Life is exceptionally rare. It is not biological in any recognized sense. Occasionally, a flicker of motion can be seen clinging to the black crystalline surfaces. These are the maintenance fauna, the "Glass-Eaters." They are creatures of pure, solidified sonar, appearing as shimmering, heat-haze distortions in the fluid, perhaps a foot or two long. They move slowly against the titanic currents, consuming calcified sonic residue and excreting a fine, crystalline dust that patches and reinforces the machinery. They are deaf, blind, and utterly part of the machine they tend. They are the only inhabitants of this city of sound.

The rules here are simple. The currents dictate all movement. The sound is a constant, a presence that erases all other senses. There is no day or night, only the dim, pulsing glow of the furnaces. The world is a system in violent, yet stable, motion. It is incomprehensibly ancient and furiously active. To exist here is not to live, but to endure. To be a mote of dust in a cathedral-sized bell as it is struck, an instant that has been stretched to an eternity. It is a place built for a purpose that has no audience, a deafening song for a god who may have died before the first note was played.
