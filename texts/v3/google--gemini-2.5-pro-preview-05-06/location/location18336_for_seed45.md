This is a world being born in darkness.

The ground underfoot is a knife-field of new rock, still warm to the touch. It is not soil or sand but a brittle, black glass, fractured into a million razor-sharp plates and shards that shift and grind against each other with a sound like whispering static. The air is thin and carries a clean, mineral heat, the kind that rises from a kiln just opened. There is no moisture, no dew, no frost. To breathe is to feel the inside of your lungs become parchment.

This is a place of impossible height. You are standing on a nascent plateau at the top of the world, and all around you, the landscape is still climbing. These are not mountains in the way a weathered, ancient range is mountains. These are geological extrusions, pushed up from a molten heart with terrifying speed. They are the Umbral Spires, blacker than the sky, so sharp and new that they have no dust or softness upon them. Their peaks are not rounded by eons of wind but are fractured, crystalline points that seem to tear at the void above. There is no erosion to gentle them; they exist in a state of pure, upward becoming.

The scale is immense and dizzying. Between the jagged spires that claw their way into the dark, vast and empty basins stretch for miles, floors of the same black glass, impossibly flat. The effect is one of extreme, vertical punctuation in an otherwise featureless void. The spires are sparse, lonely titans, and the space between them is an emptiness so profound it feels like a presence in itself.

The darkness is not absolute. From the cracks in the glassy ground, and from veins running up the flanks of the growing mountains, a faint, internal light breathes. It is a chemiluminescence, a deep crimson and violet glow that pulses slowly, as if in time with the world’s own heartbeat. This is the only illumination. It throws the landscape into a stark relief of glowing lines and absolute shadow. The faces of the spires pointing away from you are simply gone, holes in reality.

There is no life here. No animal scuttles across the glass plains. No bird calls from the impossible peaks. Yet, the world is not sterile. Where the geothermal heat is most intense, a form of mineral life takes hold. It is not vegetation, but something stranger. Filaments of silicon, delicate as spun glass and hued like opal, grow in slow, fractal patterns. They form low, crystalline gardens in the warmest crevices, shimmering with borrowed, internal light. They are utterly brittle; the slightest vibration can shatter a foot-wide colony into a cloud of glittering dust that settles without a sound.

The world is turbulent, but quietly so. The chaos is not of wind or water, but of the earth itself. The ground trembles constantly, a low-frequency hum that you feel in your bones more than you hear with your ears. The great spires are visibly, if slowly, growing. You can fix your gaze on a peak against the starless black and, over the course of an hour, watch a new needle of rock extrude from its tip, glowing a dull cherry-red before cooling to black. Occasionally, a high-pitched *ping* echoes across the basin as a cooling rock face contracts and fractures. More rarely, a deep, groaning tear resounds as a whole spire adjusts its footing on the soft, tectonic mantle beneath.

And there are constructions. Here and there, on the featureless plains between the mountains, stand small, solitary structures. They are cairns, but not cairns made by stacking loose stones. These appear to have grown, extruded from the ground in the shape of a deliberate pile. A dozen or so coffin-sized, perfectly smooth, egg-shaped stones of a milky white quartz, stacked into a neat cone six feet high. They are cool to the touch, and they do not glow. They seem to absorb the faint light around them. There is no sign of a builder, no tool marks, no footprints. They are simply there, scattered across the immense, forming landscape by the dozen, as if they are markers laid by a surveyor. Or perhaps they are just another rule of this place: where the stress of creation reaches a certain symmetrical point, the world itself builds a tombstone for a stillness it will never know.
