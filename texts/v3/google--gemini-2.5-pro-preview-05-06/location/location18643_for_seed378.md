The air is a hot, wet towel over the face. It's heavy to breathe, saturated with a heat that feels mineral, not solar. The light is a white, bleaching glare, uniform and shadowless, as if the sky itself is a single, immense, over-exposed light panel. This intense brightness reflects off every surface, yet the thick humidity swallows detail at a distance, making the horizon a blurry, shimmering line where form dissolves into white steam.

There is no soil. The ground is a vast, uneven expanse of sinter terraces, wide and shallow like the petrified spillways of ancient gods. The material is a pale, creamy travertine, warm to the touch and slick with constant condensation. Water is everywhere and nowhere. It doesn't pool in lakes or run in rivers; it seeps. It weeps from hairline cracks in the sinter, beading up on vertical surfaces before tracing slow, glossy paths downwards. It hisses from unseen vents, rising in thick plumes of steam that drift slowly in the super-heated air. The world is perpetually post-rain, a landscape caught in a permanent state of glistening, dripping aftermath.

Silence is the rule. The oppressive humidity and heat act as a universal damper on sound. There is no wind, only the slow, weighty convection of air. The loudest sound is a soft, pervasive hiss, the collective exhalation of a million geothermal pores. Occasionally, there is the gentle *plash* of a heavy dollop of condensation falling from a high ledge into a shallow, steaming puddle, a sound as momentous as a dropped stone in a cathedral.

This is a city, but not in a way that feels built for comfort. Vast, widely-spaced pylons of a dark, iron-rich ceramic rise from the travertine flats, their bases stained deep orange and green by mineral-rich runoff. These pylons support a sprawling, skeletal network of causeways, pipes, and platforms, forming an elevated metropolis that hangs meters above the treacherous, scalding ground. The construction is layered and ancient, newer pipes clamped onto corroded older ones, walkways patched with differing materials. It is a city that has been maintaining, repairing, and growing itself for a very long time.

This industrial skeleton is the true ecology of the region. There are no plants. Not a single leaf, blade of grass, or strand of moss. The base of the food chain is the city itself. The great pipes, some as wide as buildings, thrum with a silent, hydraulic pressure, their surfaces radiating a steady, intense warmth. And on these warm, damp surfaces, life flourishes. It is a life of mineral accretion. Fuzzy, verdigris-colored growths of chemo-crystalline organisms coat the joints of the pipes. Delicate, brittle lattices of white salt form in the shadows of valves, tasting of ozone and zinc. Smears of sulfurous yellow spread like slow-motion paint spills where steam leaks are most active. This is the pastureland.

And it is heavily grazed. The fauna is abundant, but silent. The most common creatures are the Scrapers, six-legged organisms around the size of a dinner plate. Their bodies are iridescent, oil-slick carapaces, segmented and low-slung. They move with a deliberate, jittery precision, their heads equipped with complex mandible-rasps that they use to methodically scrape the mineral blooms from the pipes. The only sound they make is a faint, rhythmic *skrrt-skrrt-skrrt* of chitin on ceramic, quickly swallowed by the air. They are skittish, freezing at any large movement and relying on their reflective shells to blend in with the glistening, wet surfaces of their world.

Higher up the food chain are the Stilt-Walkers. They are enormous, spindly creatures, their bodies small, teardrop-shaped pods suspended between four or five impossibly long, jointed legs. These legs allow them to stride over pipes and across the open sinter flats, their wide, padded feet distributing their weight so as not to crack the delicate travertine. They move with an unnerving, slow-motion grace. They are ambush predators, standing motionless for hours until a cluster of Scrapers is directly beneath them. Then, with a movement too quick to follow, a long, tubular proboscis whips down from the body, snatches a Scraper, and retracts. There is no snarl, no cry. Just a disappearance.

The entire system feels active yet somnambulant. The city is a great geothermal lung, breathing in heat and water from deep within the planet and exhaling it as steam and mineral nutrients. The creatures move through their cycles of grazing and predation with a dreamlike quietness. Everything is in motion, but it is the slow, inexorable motion of geological time compressed into a biological pace. Below the causeways, in the shimmering heat haze of the geyser flats, darker shapes sometimes move. Low-slung, armored beings, wide as carts, that seem to consume the travertine itself, leaving deep, gouged trails in their wake. They are a reminder that the city is just one layer, a sparse grid of order laid over a foundation that is much older, and much stranger. The bright, silent world continues, driven by heat and pressure, its inhabitants living out their lives in the perpetual, humid glare of the white sky.
