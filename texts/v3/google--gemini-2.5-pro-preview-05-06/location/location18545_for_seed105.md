There is no surface. There is only a direction where the pressure lessens, a slow, fractional easing over miles of vertical water, but it is a theoretical direction, an abstraction. Here, in the high valleys of the drowned range, there is only the deep. The water is impossibly cold, a cold that is not a sensation but a fundamental state of being, leaching energy from anything warmer than its own absolute zero. The weight of it is a constant, crushing embrace.

The floor of this valley is a plain of fine, dark silt settled over eons atop smooth, black basalt. The peaks that define the space are not sharp crags; they are the worn-down teeth of an ancient mountain range, ground smooth by currents older than continents. They are colossal, rounded shoulders of rock, their slopes lost in the crushing darkness above and below. This landscape has been in this state for a very, very long time. The forms are settled, final.

Across this abyssal plain, things grow. They are not forests in any recognizable sense, but groves of immense, hollow stalks that rise hundreds of feet from the silted floor. They are made of a silicate material, a kind of biological glass, translucent and faintly ribbed. This is the Glass-reed. Within each stalk, a pale, chemiluminescent fluid circulates, a sluggish flow of cold, blue-green light that pulses in a slow, asynchronous rhythm. Each grove glows with a life of its own, a city of soft light in the otherwise total darkness. The space between these groves is immense, a valley floor that stretches for miles into the glowing murk, where the light of the next grove is only a faint, hazy nebula on the edge of perception.

And there is sound. It is loud. The first sound is a constant, foundational groan that vibrates through the very medium of the world. It is the sound of planetary-scale currents dragging across the submerged mountain ranges, the tectonic hum of a world under incomprehensible pressure. But this is only the bass note. Layered over it is a chiming, a vast and discordant symphony. The Glass-reeds, swayed by the slow, irresistible currents, knock against one another. Their hollow, glass-like forms resonate, creating tones that are both sharp and mournful, ringing out for miles through the dense water. Each grove is an orchestra of chimes, singing its own chaotic, unending song into the abyss.

The world is active. The light of the Glass-reeds waxes and wanes on a cycle that answers to no sun. Sometimes, a grove will pulse in near-unison, a sudden flare of blue-green that illuminates the valley floor for a mile around, revealing the contours of the ancient, water-swept rock. Then, it subsides. The reeds also reproduce. At the height of a light-pulse, a mature stalk will sometimes vent a plume of shimmering, incandescent spores from its tip. These clouds of light-motes drift on the abyssal currents, a slow-motion blizzard of life, journeying for perhaps a century before finding a place to settle and grow. There are no animals. Nothing swims, nothing crawls, nothing hunts. The only life is this silent, singing, luminous vegetation. The only movement is the slow growth and sway of the reeds, and the inexorable flow of the water itself.

Scattered across the valley floor, often in the deep, dark emptiness between the groves, are other forms. They are simple, stark, and clearly made. Circles of upright stones, each the height of a man, are half-buried in the silt. Lone monoliths, carved with geometric patterns so eroded they are now just soft suggestions, point toward nothing. They are hewn from the same basalt as the mountains, their surfaces pitted and worn smooth by the same ageless currents. They are not dwellings. They are too small, too exposed, too strange. They feel like markers, or focusing instruments for an energy that is no longer present. Perhaps they are the graves of a people for whom the chiming of the Glass-reeds was a hymn. The technology is primitive, a simple shaping of stone, but the enduring will behind them feels immense, a ghost haunting the deep. To encounter one in the brief, pulsing glow of a distant grove is to feel a profound and unsettling stillness in the heart of the world’s loud, cold roar.
