This is a place of sound.

Before sight, before touch, there is the shattering. It is not a noise that arrives at the ear but a force that constitutes the self, a vibration that enters the bones and boils the fluid in the skull. It is a constant, deafening, world-defining roar, but to call it a roar is to simplify it. It is a chord played on an organ the size of a planet, a chord whose notes are the shriek of grinding tectonic plates, the crystalline chime of a million fracturing chandeliers, the bass thrum of a sun’s heart, and the wet, tearing sound of reality being rent and re-woven. The sound is the medium as much as the liquid is.

You are suspended in a warm, dense fluid, a medium that feels thicker than water, almost like a thin, clear oil. The pressure is immense but uniform, a crushing embrace that feels less like a threat and more like a fundamental condition of existence. There is no surface, no air, no up in the conventional sense, only a gradient of lessening pressure so subtle it is almost undetectable. This is a high place, a mountaintop, but it is a peak drowned in an ocean of everything.

The ground, when you drift near it, is a landscape of sharp, black glass. It is a new peak, a volcanic upthrust of obsidian and basalt that has not known the slow sand-papering of time. Every edge is a blade, every surface a mirror of fractured darkness. The geometry is violent—spires that needle into the gloom, sheer cliffs that plummet into deeper noise, and fields of jagged debris where some immense force has recently pulverized the rock. But there is no erosion here, no wearing away. There is only shattering and instantaneous re-formation.

The sound is a sculptor. As you watch, a particularly high, keening note in the omnipresent cacophony holds for a moment longer than it should. In the thick liquid before you, motes of light begin to precipitate. They are not swimming, but being called into existence. They swarm and coalesce, and in the span of a breath, an impossible structure grows, sung into being. It is a filigree of solid light, a lattice of pale, glowing crystal that looks like a cathedral window designed by a snowflake. It stands a dozen meters high, impossibly delicate, humming with a visible overtone of the sound that created it. For a moment, it is the most beautiful thing that could possibly exist.

Then the chord shifts. A bass note of pure grinding force enters the symphony, and the crystalline lattice vibrates against its new truth. Cracks of brighter light race across its surface. With a silent *pop* that you feel as a pressure-shock against your chest, the structure implodes into a cloud of fading dust. This is the only architecture here: temporary palaces of resonance, built and unbuilt in seconds. The ‘ground’ is littered with the faint, ghostly remains of these sonic artifacts, patterns etched into the rock by sound-shadows.

There is life here, but it is sparse and strange. On the black rock, clinging in the few places sheltered from the most violent pressure waves, are thin, metallic filaments. They are not plants, for they do not photosynthesize. The dim, sourceless light that permeates the fluid is not enough for that. Instead, these filaments twitch and vibrate, feeding on the kinetic energy of the sound itself. They grow in fractal patterns, tracing the echoes of shattered crystal cathedrals.

And rarer still, there is fauna. A shape drifts through the turbulent chaos. It is large, the size of a whale, but it has no discernible features—no eyes, no mouth, no fins. It is a form of pure, matte black, a hole in the visual field. Its movement is slow, ponderous, and utterly unaffected by the churning currents or the waves of sonic force. As it passes, something impossible happens. The sound vanishes. In the immediate vicinity of the creature, a perfect sphere of absolute silence extends. The deafening roar of the world is not blocked, but consumed. To be caught in its field is a shock greater than the noise itself—a sudden, terrifying sensory deprivation, a fissure of quiet in a universe made of clamor. The change is so abrupt it feels like a physical blow. You can hear your own blood pounding in your ears, a frantic, tiny drum against the sudden void. Then the creature drifts on, and the reality of the all-consuming sound crashes back in, a physical weight that makes you gasp.

The world is a storm. The fluid medium churns in unpredictable vortices, spun by the shifting pressures of the sound. Newly-formed obsidian needles will shear off a cliff face and tumble into the depths, while elsewhere a new peak is forced upwards by a sustained, deep tone. Light is a byproduct of decay, the dim, ambient glow generated by the constant destruction of the resonant crystals. Nothing is stable. This place is not a landscape, but an event. It is a process of unceasing, violent creation, a reality where physics is a suggestion, and the only law is the turbulent, deafening, and generative song of its own making.
