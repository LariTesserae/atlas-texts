You are in a room made of night and cold. The air, thick as unwoven silk, carries a chill that sinks past the skin and into the quiet of the bones. It is heavy with the scent of wet stone, deep soil, and something else, something like cucumber rind and cold-storage meat. The dark is not absolute. It is a deep, bruised purple, a foundational colour upon which other, colder lights are built.

This place is a cavity, a secret held in the heart of some greater, unbreathing mass. Its scale is intimate. You could cross it in a thousand paces. It is a hollow, a single chamber, a bubble of life no bigger than a cathedral nave. The floor, walls, and ceiling are one continuous substance. Not stone, not soil, but something grown. It feels like dense, interwoven cartilage underfoot, slick with a constant, weeping moisture. This is the substrate, the world-flesh: a colossal, living latticework of root and stem and fungal mycelia, woven so tightly over millennia that it has the density of petrified wood but the cold, damp give of living tissue.

From this living floor, structures rise. They are not built, but grown. Slender, bone-white arches, sharp-edged and new-looking, curve up and across the central void, intersecting in a silent, vaulted geometry. They are towers without rooms, bridges without destinations, supports for a city that exists only as a framework. They are sparse, leaving vast volumes of cold, dark air between them. This is the city, a settlement of pure architecture, where the buildings are the inhabitants’ most profound and only art. The process of their creation is one of patience, not fabrication. The builders use simple tools—scrapers of chert, blades of sharpened bone—to prune, guide, and graft the living material, coaxing it over generations into these stark, elegant forms.

Water is a constant presence, but it does not flow. It manifests. The thick air is so saturated that every surface glistens. Droplets gather on the sweeping undersides of the arches, swell to a critical weight, and fall with a soft, padded *thup* onto the floor below, a sound absorbed almost instantly by the living substrate. There are no rivers, only these slow, patient drips and the dark, mirror-still pools they form in the lowest depressions. The water is black, rich with spores and dissolved nutrients, a cold tea brewed from the world itself.

For all its emptiness, the space is not empty. Life is overwhelming. The air is not just air; it is a blizzard of infinitesimal existences. Look closely at the motes drifting in the faint light, and you will see they are living things: tiny, six-winged insects with thoraxes that pulse a faint chartreuse; translucent, gas-filled jellies no bigger than a thumbprint, floating on imperceptible currents. The walls, the floor, the arches—they are not still. They crawl with a billion lives. Iridescent slugs, paved with tiny shells like mosaics, graze on films of glowing bacteria. Velvet-black isopods, arranged in familial chains, march in slow, silent processions along the sweeping curves of the architecture. Hunters, things of needle-legs and grasping claws, scuttle in the deeper shadows, their compound eyes burning with a brief, predatory orange.

And yet, it is profoundly quiet. The swarming, crawling, overwhelming press of fauna makes almost no sound. Communication is a language of light and chemistry. The air is too thick, the fleshy walls too absorbent, for noise to travel. The soundscape is one of intimacy and texture: the wet, sliding sound of a slug’s passage; the faint *click* of a hunter’s tarsus finding a new hold; the almost sub-audible, resonant hum of the living city’s metabolism, a feeling more than a sound that vibrates up through the soles of your feet.

This is a world in constant, silent motion. A pulse of soft blue light might sweep across one of the arches as a colony of fungi releases its spores, which drift down like the finest snow. In the dark pools, gelatinous spheres hatch into larval predators that wage silent, frantic wars. You might see a shape, larger than the rest, moving with deliberate slowness along a high archway. It is one of the builders, the tenders of this cold garden-city. Its form is unclear in the deep gloom, a column of shadow against the fainter shadows. It pauses, then reaches out with a long limb, using a bone knife to make a careful incision in the living arch, applying a resinous poultice from a pouch at its side. It is not building; it is healing, guiding, whispering to the flesh of its world to grow in a new direction. This is its work. This is its life.

The entire system functions like a single, complex organism: the substrate breathes, the fauna are its microbiome, the builders are its consciousness. All of it is tucked away, a pocket of impossible activity, a city of life inside a tomb of rock, turning on its own slow, cold, and silent cycles, utterly alone in the dark.
