This place is a sound.

Before sight, before touch, you are submerged in it. It is not a note, but a chord of immense complexity, a standing wave of sound so dense it feels like a physical medium. It is a deafening, unwavering, crystalline hum that vibrates in your teeth and sternum. It is the sound of a billion glass bells, all struck at once and held in that single, perfect moment of resonance, forever. The air, utterly still and cool, is thick with this vibration. It is the only thing here that seems to have energy.

Your eyes open to a glare that has no source. The light is absolute, shadowless, and brilliant, emanating from everything at once. There is no sun, no bulb, no single point of origin. The world itself is luminous. This light reveals a chamber, a grotto, no bigger than a grand hall, and it is packed to the point of impossibility.

This is the Chime-Labyrinth. From a floor of fused, opalescent material, countless pillars rise to a ceiling of the same. They are the source of the light and the sound. They are not trees, not stone, not metal, but something akin to spun salt or fibrous quartz. Each pillar, ranging from the width of a wrist to the girth of an ancient tree, is a tightly-packed bundle of these crystalline fibers. They seem to have grown, twisting and braiding together in their slow, ancient ascent, jostling for space in a silent, static battle for position that ended eons ago. The entire chamber—floor, ceiling, pillars—is a single, continuous, constructed entity. There is no natural ground beneath; the structure *is* the place. It was not built with tools; it was grown.

This is the "vegetation" of this world. It is sparse only in the sense that each pillar is a distinct individual. But it is dense in that the gaps between them are mere corridors, tight squeezes, and winding paths. There is no open space. To move through the labyrinth is to constantly be in contact with its cool, vibrating surfaces. The surfaces are not smooth; they are etched with infinitesimal, repeating geometric patterns, like the frost-flowers on a winter window, but with a rigid, mathematical precision. They feel like dry, fine-grained sandpaper.

There is no water. The concept is alien here. The air is so profoundly dry it feels like it could pull the moisture from your lungs, yet it is cool and carries no dust. No drop of liquid has ever touched these surfaces. The life that formed this place is lithic, crystalline, its metabolism based on a resonance with some unseen, underlying energy field of the universe. It does not consume; it transduces. The pillars are converters, turning a silent, omnipresent power into the physical realities of light and sound.

There is no life other than the pillars themselves. No insect crawls on their surfaces, no microbe clouds the air. There are no animals, no predators, no prey. The ecosystem is one of a single phylum, a single organism expressed as a forest of resonant spires. The system is in perfect, frozen equilibrium. It does not change. The light does not flicker, the sound does not waver in pitch or volume. The temperature remains constant. Time does not seem to pass here in a meaningful way; it is a landscape of a single, eternal instant.

To be here is to be overwhelmed. The constant, room-filling brightness erases nuance, bleaching color and flattening perspective. The roaring, static hum is a physical pressure that makes thought difficult, a sensory flood that leaves no room for silence. You navigate the tight warren, the air a cool balm against your skin, your hands brushing against the humming, self-lit pillars. You are inside an instrument, a bell, a crystal, experiencing its one and only state of being. It is a place built without hands, a chorus sung without lungs, a forest that feeds on physics itself, unchanging and absolute.
