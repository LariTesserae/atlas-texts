You are submerged. Not in water as it is commonly known, but in a pressurized, life-saturated fluid, cool and clear and carrying the weight of a vertical ocean above you. This is the Lume, a global ocean-atmosphere tinted a faint, vital green by the endless life it holds. Light is a given, a constant, filtering down from an unseen surface miles above, a diffuse and shadowless silver-green glow that illuminates everything with the clarity of a laboratory.

The world is a single organism. A plant. Its structure fills all of space, yet is mostly empty. Imagine a web, or a sponge, or the trabecular structure of bone, but woven from living cellulose, lignin, and stranger biopolymers. This is the Dendral Matrix. It is a single, planet-spanning entity that provides the only 'terrain' in this liquid universe. Its primary branches are trunks of living wood, kilometers thick and spaced hundreds of kilometers apart, rising from an abyssal floor so deep it is a myth and climbing toward a surface so high it is a hypothesis. Between these colossal pillars, a finer, but still enormous, latticework of secondary and tertiary branches creates a sparse, open scaffolding. You could swim for a day in a straight line through one of the voids within the Matrix and see nothing but the distant, hazy forms of its architecture framing the emptiness. This is a place of sublime, cavernous sparsity, a cathedral made of a single tree, drowned in its own sap.

This place is loud. The sound is not the roar of a storm or the crash of waves, but the deep, thrumming, and percussive thunder of industry. It is the sound of machinery operating on a geological scale. But there are no mines, no foundries, no cities. The Dendral Matrix itself is the factory.

Embedded within its living architecture are vast, specialized organs that perform relentless, mechanized functions. Entire regions of the Matrix are dedicated to this bio-industry. You might drift past a 'piston grove', where colossal cylinders of hardened plant-flesh, hundreds of meters in diameter, slowly and inexorably pump the Lume through titanic filtration membranes, harvesting trace minerals. The sound here is a deep, subsonic *thump-hiss… thump-hiss*, a heartbeat slow enough to be geography. Elsewhere, you may encounter a 'centrifugal vortex', where a whole section of the Matrix, miles wide, spins on a central axis, its outer fronds blurring into a solid wall. This is a biological centrifuge, sorting spores and gametes by mass. The noise is a rising, harmonic drone, a polyphonic chord that has been holding for a million years. There are percussive clatterings from regions where hardened, nut-like nodules are shattered by rhythmic impacts to release nutrients, and the high-frequency hum of immense chemo-electric organs that generate the energy to power the whole system, drawing power from the thermal and chemical gradients in the water.

This is a system in perfect, roaring equilibrium. It is a factory that produces only itself. It refines the Lume to feed itself, it generates power to run itself, it sorts its own seeds to continue itself. The entire industrial process is a closed loop, a roaring ballet of activity that results in no net change. The pistons have always been pumping. The centrifuges have always been spinning. The sound has no crescendo or diminuendo; it is the absolute and eternal sound of the world being itself. Nothing is being built, nothing is being exported. The industrial noise is the sound of stasis.

Life teems in the spaces between. The fauna are not separate from the machine; they are its tenders, its parasites, its living components. Vast, streamlined shoals of silver-skinned fish, thousands strong, move with the engineered currents, their passage a shimmering sigh against the industrial backdrop. They feed on the endless clouds of particulate matter discharged by the filtration organs. Armored, crab-like browsers with carapaces of hardened cellulose crawl slowly along the branches of the Matrix, their grinding mandibles scraping away at algae and biofilm, their slow progress another layer of sound, a soft, widespread rasping. In the great voids, strange predators hunt. They are creatures of vibration and pressure, blind and elegant, sensing the minute deviations in the Lume caused by shoals of fish against the overwhelming hum of the world-engine. They do not need eyes in this shadowless light. They navigate by the unchanging echoes of the ever-present machinery.

The rules are simple. There is no weather, only the engineered currents of the Matrix. There is no day or night, only the constant, filtered light. There is no goal, no evolution toward a different state. The system reached a perfect, established equilibrium eons ago and has never deviated. The life and death of its fauna are simply maintenance cycles. The roar of the cellular engines is not the sound of progress; it is the sound of permanence. To be here is to be an observer in a machine whose only product is its own continued, thunderous existence, a single moment of immense activity, held forever.
