### **A Traveler's Guide to the Worn World (Codename: Terminus)**

**Foreword: A Note on Intent**

Before we discuss practicalities, understand this: you are not embarking on an adventure. You are not a pioneer, an explorer, or a colonist. You are undertaking a pilgrimage to a place that has finished being, a library at the end of time where the last book is being perpetually rebound. Your role is to be a ghost, an utterly silent and respectful observer. The primary directive for this journey is **do not interfere**. The second is **survive**. They are more interconnected than you can possibly imagine. If you feel the call to "help" or "improve" or "make your mark," choose another destination. This world is not for you.

---

### **Part 1: Pre-Departure Preparations**

#### **1.1 Psychological Acclimatization**

The greatest danger in this world is not physical, but existential. Your mind, accustomed to the busy, chaotic symphony of a younger universe, will be profoundly challenged here.

*   **Embrace the Silence:** Spend at least one full week in a sensory deprivation tank or a remote, sound-proofed environment. You must make peace with absolute quiet. In Terminus, the silence is not empty; it is full. Prepare for your own mind to generate phantom sounds—birdsong, voices, traffic—as it struggles to populate the void. Learn to differentiate between your internal noise and the world's true soundscape.
*   **Scale Inoculation:** Your sense of scale and self will be assaulted. Find the largest, flattest, most featureless landscape in your home reality (a salt flat, a desert, an ice sheet) and spend several days there alone. Practice looking at the horizon until it stops feeling like a destination and starts feeling like an abstract concept. You must get used to feeling infinitesimally small, not in a terrifying way, but in a factual, objective one.
*   **Temporal Anchoring:** The City's physics are locally unstable. Before you leave, select a small, dense, physically complex object to be your "anchor." A non-digital pocket watch, a weighty metal puzzle, or a wooden figurine with intricate carvings. During your visit, the act of holding and observing this object, confirming its familiar physical laws, will tether your perceptions to your home reality.

#### **1.2 Physical Conditioning & Medical**

*   **High-Altitude Training:** The air is thin and cold. You must train your body for a low-oxygen environment. Spend at least a month living and exercising at the highest possible altitude available to you. Even with technological assistance, your body's baseline efficiency must be high.
*   **Full Medical & Dental Check-up:** There are no doctors, no dentists, no aid of any kind. Any underlying condition could become a fatal liability. Ensure you are in peak physical health.
*   **Food & Water Discipline:** You will be bringing every calorie you consume. Practice living on nutrient paste or dehydrated rations for an extended period. Your palate must be disciplined, and your digestive system robust.

### **Part 2: Gear & Equipment**

This is a curation of essential gear. Do not skimp. Your life depends on every item.

*   **Atmospheric Suit & Rebreather:** While the air is technically a nitrogen-oxygen mix, its thinness makes it insufficient for unadapted human physiology over the long term. A full-body, insulated environmental suit is mandatory. It should have a closed-loop rebreather system that supplements oxygen and scrubs CO2. **Crucially, the suit's visor must have 100% UV/broad-spectrum radiation filtering.** The unfiltered sunlight is deceptively intense. The visor should also have a polarizing function to manage the blinding clarity and a low-light amplification mode for the deep purple shadows.
*   **Footwear:** Your boots are your primary interface with the world. They must be perfectly waterproofed, heavily insulated, with deep, soft-rubber treads designed for gripping damp, yielding surfaces. The soles should be thick enough to insulate you from the constant, low-frequency thrumming of the planetary moss. After a few days, this vibration can cause fatigue and even nerve damage in the unprepared.
*   **Navigation Suite:**
    *   **Primary:** A reality-anchored quantum positioning system (QPS). Standard GPS will not function reliably, especially within the City where spacetime is fluid. Your QPS must be locked to your home dimension's baseline reality.
    *   **Secondary:** A high-tensile, micro-filament spool. At least five kilometers of it. Brightly colored. This is your physical breadcrumb trail. In the City, it is not optional.
    *   **Tertiary:** A simple, high-quality compass. While it may spin uselessly in areas of physical flux, it will function in the stable zones and on the high plateaus. Knowing when it *stops* working is as valuable as knowing where it's pointing.
*   **Shelter:** A geodesic dome tent rated for extreme cold and high winds. Its anchoring system must be specialized: not stakes (which won't hold in the soft moss-mat), but broad, flat "snow-plates" that distribute the tension and weight over a wide area. Your tent is your sanctuary and your only truly stable piece of reality.
*   **Sustenance:** All food must be brought with you. Nutrient pastes, vacuum-sealed protein bars, and high-energy rations. No open flames are permitted—they could irreparably damage the closed condensation ecosystem. All heating must be done via electrical induction or chemical packs. For water, the suit's condenser will reclaim bodily moisture, but a secondary atmospheric condenser is vital for backup. The planetary frost is pure, but harvesting it should be a last resort to minimize your impact.

### **Part 3: On Arrival - Navigating the World**

*   **Landing Zone:** Choose a spot on one of the high plateaus, far from the City. You need time to acclimate to the world's fundamental nature before encountering its primary anomaly.
*   **The Rhythm of the World:** Your first 24 hours should be spent in silent observation from your landing zone.
    *   **The Condensation Cycle:** Watch the night frost form, a breathtaking lacework of ice. Watch it melt at dawn and vanish into the moss. Understand this is the world's breath.
    *   **The Light:** Note the quality of the light, the razor-sharpness of the shadows. Time their movement. Your sense of time will begin to untether; use the sun's arc as your clock.
    *   **The Thrum and the Glow:** Lie on the ground (on an insulating mat). Feel the deep, slow pulse through the soles of your feet. At dusk, watch for the phosphorescence. The 20-minute cycle of the glow is the most reliable timepiece on the planet. Synchronize your chronometer to it.
*   **Movement:** Walking on the moss-mat is like walking on dense, perpetually damp peat. It is yielding and silent. Place your feet deliberately. Do not run. There is nothing to run from, and a twisted ankle here is a death sentence. The deceptive clarity of the air will make a landmark look an hour away when it is, in fact, a week's journey. Trust your navigation suite, not your eyes.

### **Part 4: Approaching and Entering the City**

*   **The Soundscape of Maintenance:** As you approach, you will begin to hear it: the low, resonant hum of the wind through the lattices, and the distant, arrhythmic *tink… tink, tink…* of a chisel. These are the sounds of reality being reinforced. They are the sounds of safety. If they stop, you should be concerned.
*   **The Threshold:** There is no gate or wall. The City bleeds into the landscape. You will know you have entered when you encounter the first worked stones, the first paths of woven rock fiber. Pause here. This is the moment to seal your mind. You are now entering the workshop. Speak only when absolutely necessary, and then only in a whisper. Move slowly.
*   **Navigating the City's Logic (or lack thereof):**
    *   **Stay Grounded:** As much as possible, stick to the great open plazas on the lower levels. These are the oldest, most stable parts of the City.
    *   **The Bridges are Traps:** Do not cross the high, delicate bridges of woven stone unless absolutely necessary. They are the most visible manifestation of the world's fraying. A bridge may connect two towers one moment and lead to open, empty air the next. If you must cross, secure your micro-filament line to a solid anchor on your side. Have your partner (travel with a partner is strongly advised) belay you. If traveling alone, do not risk it.
    *   **Anomalous Zones:** You will find them. A plaza where your tossed ration-bar hangs in the air. A lattice wall that splits light into a color that makes your eyes water and your head ache. Do not linger. Mark these areas on your map and give them a wide berth. Do not attempt to study them. You are not a physicist, and even if you were, your science does not apply here.
    *   **The Curators:** You will not see them. You might, in a moment of profound quiet, feel a *presence*—the way you feel the presence of a person in the next room. You might see a chisel lying next to a half-carved crystal, still shimmering with a faint warmth. **Under no circumstances do you touch anything.** Treat every tool, every unfinished surface, every pile of stone dust as sacred. You are in a sterile operating theater the size of a continent, and your touch is a contagion. Your presence is tolerated only as long as you are inert.

### **Part 5: Departure & Psychological Aftercare**

*   **Leaving No Trace:** Your guiding principle is to leave the world exactly as you found it. Pack out everything—every food wrapper, every drop of waste. Fill in your footprints where you can. Let the moss-mat reclaim your landing site.
*   **The Chime-Crack:** Throughout your journey, you may hear a sound like a vast, crystal bell being struck and shattering at the same time. This sound will echo across the plateaus. It is the sound of a piece of the world—a distant hill, a stretch of mossy plain—losing its coherence and dissolving into static. Do not flinch. Do not panic. Simply acknowledge it. It is the reason the Curators work. It is the entropy you are privileged to witness.
*   **Return:** Upon returning to your home reality, expect a period of difficult reintegration. The noise, color, and sheer *activity* of a normal world will feel like a violent, painful assault. You will feel a phantom thrumming in your feet. You will crave the settled quiet. This is normal. It is the echo of Terminus. Seek out quiet places. Re-acclimate slowly. But know that a part of you will forever remain in that silent, green, humming place, watching the Curators mend the edges of existence.
