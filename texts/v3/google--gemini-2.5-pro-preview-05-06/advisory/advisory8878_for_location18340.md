### **A Traveler's Advisory and Preparation Guide: The Basin (aka The Weeping City, The Self-Refinery)**

**Foreword: A Note on Purpose**

Before we discuss equipment and protocols, you must first prepare your mind. You are not visiting a ruin in the conventional sense. You are not an archaeologist, a treasure hunter, or a pioneer. You are visiting a functioning, self-contained world. It is not hostile, but it is lethally indifferent to your existence. Your goal is not to conquer, explore, or even fully understand it. Your goal is to observe, to witness, and to leave without a trace. This is not a test of courage, but a test of discipline. If you cannot accept the role of a fleeting ghost, do not go.

---

### **Part 1: Pre-Departure Conditioning (The Threefold Acclimatization)**

Your body and mind, accustomed to varied, sun-driven ecosystems, are fundamentally unsuited for the Basin. Preparation is mandatory and should begin at least three months prior to your planned transit.

**1. Physiological Conditioning: The Hothouse Regimen**

The single greatest physical threat is the atmosphere. It is a combination of extreme heat (est. 40-50°C / 104-122°F) and near 100% humidity. Unprotected, an ordinary human would suffer heatstroke within minutes. Even within a suit, the thermal load will be immense.

*   **Heat & Humidity Training:** Begin a daily regimen in a purpose-built environmental chamber or a high-humidity sauna. Start with short durations and gradually increase your tolerance. The goal is not just to endure, but to be able to perform simple tasks while under extreme thermal stress.
*   **Cardiovascular Endurance:** The thick air will feel like walking through water. The constant vibration and the need for deliberate, careful movement is exhausting. Focus on long-duration, low-intensity cardio. Your heart will be working overtime just to keep you cool.
*   **Hydration Discipline:** Practice pre-hydrating and maintaining hydration with a precisely calibrated electrolyte solution. You will be entirely dependent on your suit's water supply, and you need to know your body's exact requirements under these specific conditions. There is no other source of safe water.

**2. Sensory Conditioning: The Deprivation Chamber**

The Basin's sensory environment is both empty and overwhelming. It will attack your psyche in subtle, persistent ways.

*   **Auditory Immersion:** Find or create a recording of a deep, sub-bass frequency hum (around 20-30 Hz) overlaid with soft, random clicking and dripping sounds. Play this on a loop, especially while you sleep. You must become accustomed to the constant thrumming so it fades into the background, rather than driving you slowly mad. Practice meditating to this sound.
*   **Visual Desensitization:** Spend several hours a day in a dimly lit room with only a single, diffuse, yellowish light source. This will help you adapt to the perpetual twilight and the absence of shadows, which can severely impact depth perception.
*   **Olfactory Simulation:** While difficult to replicate perfectly, accustom yourself to the smell of hot, wet metal, ozone (like after a thunderstorm), and damp, enclosed stone cellars. The goal is to neutralize the shock of the Basin's unique scent profile.

**3. Psychological Fortitude: The Solitude Imperative**

You will be utterly alone, yet surrounded by an oppressive quantity of life. This is a unique psychological challenge.

*   **Entomophobia Test:** You must be at peace with insects and arthropods. If the thought of a carpet of beetles flowing over your boots induces panic, you are not ready. Spend time observing insect colonies. Understand their collective, non-individual nature. See them not as a threat, but as a geological force, like wind or water.
*   **Isolation Training:** Practice extended periods of solitude without external stimuli. The Basin offers no entertainment, no distraction. Your own thoughts will be your only company. You must be comfortable with this.
*   **Claustrophobia & Agoraphobia Screening:** The Basin paradoxically triggers both. The low ceiling of vapor creates a sense of being trapped indoors, while the vast, flat plains can feel unnervingly exposed. Be certain you can handle this duality.

### **Part 2: Essential Equipment & Wargear (The Shell)**

Your survival depends entirely on your gear. There are no corners to be cut. "Good enough" will get you killed.

**The Environmental Suit:**

This is your personal world. It must be a full-body, sealed, positive-pressure environmental suit with an integrated rebreather system.

*   **Cooling System:** This is the most critical component. It must be an active cooling system, likely a network of micro-capillaries circulating a coolant fluid. Passive systems will fail. The suit's power source must be robust and have at least two redundant backups.
*   **Atmospheric Processor:** The rebreather must scrub CO2 and be capable of filtering the ambient air in a catastrophic breach scenario. While the air may not be overtly toxic in short bursts, the chemical and particulate load is unknown. The external intake filters must be rated for industrial chemical vapor.
*   **Exoskeletal Support (Recommended):** A lightweight, unpowered or minimally-powered exoskeleton integrated into the legs can significantly reduce fatigue from walking through the dense air and standing for long periods.
*   **Material:** The suit's outer layer must be non-reactive, abrasion-resistant, and easy to decontaminate. A smooth, non-porous polymer is ideal. It should be a neutral grey or off-white to avoid startling the local fauna.

**Sensory & Navigation Suite:**

Your unaided senses are liabilities here.

*   **Helmet Visor:** Must be wide-view, impact-resistant, and treated with an anti-fog coating both inside and out. Water will constantly condense on the exterior; a micro-pulse vibration feature or a slow-wipe function is essential for maintaining visibility.
*   **Visual Suite:** Your primary view should be augmented.
    *   **Low-Light Enhancement:** Absolutely critical. The ambient light is too dim for the human eye to perceive detail.
    *   **Thermal Overlay:** Allows you to spot the warmer bodies of predators against the cooler background and identify active thermal processes in the machinery.
    *   **Polarization Filter:** This may help you "see" the chemical biofilms on the water's surface by revealing how they refract light differently from the water beneath.
*   **Auditory Suite:** The helmet must have directional microphones with adjustable gain. You need to be able to amplify the faint *click* of a scuttler 50 meters away while filtering out the overwhelming background hum.
*   **Navigation:** GPS is useless. Your suit needs an inertial navigation system (INS) that tracks your every step from a known starting point. As a backup, carry a simple deployment spool of high-visibility, chemically inert fiber-optic cable. In an emergency, you can follow it back. A laser rangefinder is also invaluable for judging distances in the shadowless environment.

**Tools & Utilities:**

*   **The Staff:** A collapsible, non-conductive staff made of carbon fiber or a similar composite. This is your most important tool. Use it to probe the depth of pools, test the stability of surfaces, and maintain three points of contact on slick ground. It is also your primary tool for interacting with the world: use it to gently nudge beetle-tides aside. Never use your hands or feet.
*   **Footwear:** Integrated into the suit. The soles must be thick to insulate from the ground's vibration, with deep, multi-directional treads for gripping wet ferrocrete, tiled surfaces, and ground covered in the ball-bearing-like beetle carapaces.
*   **Sample Collection (For Observation Only):** Any sample containers must be hermetically sealed and contamination-proof. The Prime Directive must be remembered: you are not authorized to take anything, but if protocol demands it, it must be done without cross-contamination.
*   **Sustenance:** All food and water must be carried with you. Nutrient pastes and electrolyte-balanced water are delivered via integrated tubes. Assume a 50% increase in caloric and water consumption due to the thermal load on your body and suit.
*   **Waste Management:** All bodily waste, solid and liquid, must be collected in sealed containers within the suit. *Under no circumstances* is any biological material to be introduced into the Basin's ecosystem. You are a ghost, and ghosts do not leave anything behind.

### **Part 3: On-Site Protocols (The Ghost's Etiquette)**

**Arrival & The First Hour:**

Upon arrival, do not move. Stand, or kneel on one knee, for at least one full hour. Let your suit's systems stabilize. Let the vibration settle into your bones. Let your eyes adjust. Watch the gray twilight, feel the silent condensation beading on your visor, and listen to the hum and the rustle. This is the rhythm of the Basin. You must learn it before you can walk to its beat.

**Movement & Navigation:**

*   **Walk, Never Run:** Move with deliberate, measured steps. The ground is a minefield of slick patches, cracked surfaces, and teeming life.
*   **Follow the Pipes:** The vast networks of pipes and their supporting causeways are your highways. They offer higher ground and a clearer view. They are your safest routes across the landscape.
*   **Respect the Tides:** The matte-black sheets of beetles are not to be feared, but they must be respected. Do not walk through them. Walk around them. If you must cross their path, use your staff to gently "sweep" a narrow channel, giving them time to move. They will flow around you like water. Crushing them is a pointless violation.
*   **The Pools are Taboo:** Never enter the pools, no matter how shallow or clear they appear. Their chemistry is the bedrock of this world and is utterly alien to your biology. The warm, clear water is not an invitation; it is a chemical bath.

**Interacting with the Fauna:**

*   **Scuttlers:** The hand-sized crab-like predators are the most direct physical threat. Their clockwork movements make them predictable. Maintain a distance of at least 20 meters. They hunt at the edges of the beetle-tides. If you are near the tides, you are in their territory. Your thermal vision will allow you to spot them easily. Do not provoke them.
*   **Moths:** The silent, leathery fliers are opportunists. Avoid standing still for long periods directly under pipe racks, gantries, or the mouths of cooling towers. They are a threat from above. Keep your head on a swivel.
*   **The Small Things:** The rust-colored slugs and the pale worms are harmless. Observe them, but do not touch them. Their slime or secretions could have unpredictable effects on your suit's material.

**The Prime Directive: Leave No Trace**

Your visit is an act of absolute observation.

*   Do not leave any equipment, trash, or material of any kind.
*   Do not disturb the machinery. It is ancient and its workings are unknown.
*   Do not kill anything, intentionally or unintentionally if it can be avoided. Every creature, from the simplest worm to the most complex scuttler, is an irreplaceable part of this perfect, desolate machine.
*   When you leave, the only evidence of your passing should be a set of footprints that will soon be washed away by the weeping condensation, and a memory in your mind of a place that builds itself, feeds itself, and hums its own lonely, eternal song in the dark.
