A PRACTICAL GUIDE FOR THE INTER-REALM TRAVELER
**DESTINATION FILE: THE GREAT CONSTRUCT (provisional designation)**
**THREAT LEVEL: AMBIENT (ACUTE IF NEGLIGENT)**
**PREPARED BY: THE NAVIGATOR'S GUILD, SEVENTH PYLON**

So, you've drawn a ticket to the Great Construct. First, put down the brochure. There are no scenic overlooks with guardrails, no charming local markets, and no friendly guides. What you have is a ticket to one of the most sublime and indifferent ecosystems catalogued in the known verse. Survival here is not a matter of fighting anything, but of understanding your own utter insignificance and preparing accordingly.

This is not a holiday. It's a pilgrimage to the heart of a world-machine. Treat it with the respect a microbe pays to the whale it inhabits.

### Section 1: Pre-Departure Mental Conditioning

Before you pack a single item, you must prepare your mind. Most unprepared visitors suffer a psychological break within the first cycle, not from any overt attack, but from sheer sensory and philosophical overload.

*   **Abandon Your Concept of "City":** This is not London or Tokyo scaled up. Thinking of it as architecture will break your brain. Think of it as a mountain range that sweats, breathes, and groans, and you're getting warmer. It was not built *for* anything; it simply *is*.
*   **Embrace the Monotony:** You will see no blue sky, no sun, no moon, no stars. You will see no green. Your world will be shades of grey, brown, rust, verdigris, and the iridescent spectrum of chitin. The light is a constant, sourceless milk. The temperature is a constant, clammy warmth. The sound is a constant, two-part hum and chitter. Do not wait for it to change. It won't. Meditate on this fact until it no longer instills panic.
*   **Learn to Listen:** The primary sound is what locals call the "Duplex Chord"—the low, planetary groan of the core engines and the high, constant scrape of a trillion lives. This is your compass, your clock, and your weathervane. The subtle shifts in pitch and rhythm can tell you about pressure changes, slurry pump cycles, and the movement of large organisms. Learn its baseline rhythm before you even take your first step.

### Section 2: Essential Equipment & Attire

Your comfort and survival are directly proportional to the quality of your gear. Standard-issue multiversal travel kits will fail.

*   **Atmospheric Kit (Non-Negotiable):**
    *   **Full-Face Respirator:** Not a simple mask. The air isn't just "thick," it's a suspension of water, atomized minerals, aerosolized nutrient proteins, and biotic effluvia. You will want filters rated for both industrial particulates and complex organic compounds. The taste of hot metal and wet rock is romantic for a sentence, but nauseating after an hour and corrosive to your sinuses after a day.
    *   **Sealed Goggles:** Even with a respirator, you need to protect your eyes. The constant condensation carries microscopic grit. A single mote of fauna, vibrating at high frequency, can do serious damage if it gets in your eye. Your goggles will constantly fog; get a model with an internal anti-fog coating and an external micro-wiper.

*   **Outerwear:**
    *   **The "Drip Suit":** Forget waterproof. You need something *omni-proof*. The constant weeping from every surface isn't just water; it's a solvent brew of dissolved minerals. It will eat through cheap seals and delicate fabrics. The ideal garment is a one-piece, non-porous suit, similar to what deep-sea welders use, but with better flexibility. Seams must be ultrasonically welded. Cuffs should have layered, watertight gaskets at the wrist, ankle, and neck.
    *   **Footwear:** Your boots are the most critical piece of equipment. They need:
        1.  **Carbide-studded soles:** For gripping wet, rounded stone that may be coated in a thin, slick film of biotic slime.
        2.  **Reinforced toecap and shank:** You will be walking on surfaces that are essentially shifting gravel banks of living creatures. The crunching is unavoidable. You need to protect your feet from the sharp edges of millions of discarded carapaces.
        3.  **Total water integrity:** A wet sock here isn't just uncomfortable; it's a one-way ticket to a fungal infection that terrestrial medicine has no name for.

*   **Tools & Navigation:**
    *   **Light Sources:** Bring three. A powerful, narrow-beam headlamp for task work. A wide-beam lantern for assessing the sheer scale of the space you're in (and to avoid walking off a precipice). And a low-light, red-filtered lamp for observing fauna without disturbing it.
    *   **Acoustic Mapper:** GPS is a joke here. Compasses are useless due to titanic magnetic fields from the core. Your best bet is an acoustic mapping device. It won't give you a map, but by pinging and listening to the echoes, you can get a sense of the dimensions of the cavernous spaces around you and detect the movement of large predators.
    *   **Walking Staff/Ice Axe:** Not just for balance, but for probing. Tap the ground ahead of you. Is it solid stone? Is it a deep crust of beetle-husks? Is it the camouflaged shell of a cart-sized predator? Tap, listen, then step.

### Section 3: Sustenance & First Aid

**DO NOT LIVE OFF THE LAND.**

*   **Food and Water:** The entire world is a food chain, and you are not part of it. The nutrient paste is engineered for a metabolism that digests silicates and metals. For you, it is a violent poison. The water is a saturated solution of everything the world is made of; drinking it is like drinking liquid rock. You must bring a closed-loop system. All food must be in sealed, sterile packaging. All water must be carried in and filtered through a multi-stage purifier capable of removing heavy metals and complex organo-metallics.
*   **Medical Kit:** Your standard kit is a good start, but add the following:
    *   **Industrial-strength anti-fungals (topical and oral):** The air is a perfect breeding ground for things that want to grow on and in you.
    *   **Desiccants:** Pack these everywhere. In your suit, with your electronics, in your med-kit. Battling the damp is a constant war.
    *   **Broad-spectrum Abrasive Scrub:** Scrapes are inevitable. The surfaces are like eternal, wet sandpaper. These scrapes will be instantly contaminated. You need something to scour them clean before sealing them with a bio-occlusive dressing.

### Section 4: Behavioral Protocols - How Not to Die

The fauna will mostly ignore you. You are not food, and you are not a threat, provided you follow the rules.

1.  **Respect the Flow:** The rivers of beetle-like organisms are the city's circulatory system. Do not stand in their way. Find a side-path or wait for the flow to thin. Trying to wade through them is a good way to lose your footing and tumble into a lower level.
2.  **Look Up:** Things drip. Primarily water, but occasionally a dislodged piece of sessile life, a dollop of nutrient paste, or a hapless creature that lost its grip. Keep your hood up.
3.  **Mind the Stillness:** In a world of constant motion and sound, the most dangerous thing is stillness. The large, crab-like predators do not chase; they ambush. If you see a section of the causeway that seems unnaturally devoid of the shimmering beetle-tide, or a space where the chittering is strangely absent, STOP. It's likely a hunting ground. Back away slowly. Do not run. Their response is triggered by rapid, frantic movement.
4.  **Avoid the Pools:** The tea-coloured water that collects in basins and reservoirs is home to things you don't want to meet. They are long, pale, patient, and blind. They hunt by sensing vibrations on the water's surface. Do not touch the water. Do not fall in the water. Give all significant bodies of water a wide berth.
5.  **You Are Invisible:** This is your greatest advantage and the source of the world's existential horror. To the Great Construct, you are less than a dust mote. You register on no sensory organ that matters. Behave as such. Don't try to leave your mark. Don't deface the stone. Don't sample the fauna. Observe, document, and move on. You are a ghost passing through a god's intestines. Act like it.

This world operates on a timescale that renders a human life less than a firefly's spark. Go there to be humbled. Go there to witness a form of life so complete and self-contained it defies our terrestrial definitions. Go there prepared, or don't go at all. The constant, maddening drip you hear in the under-levels is the world's pulse counting down the seconds until your inferior equipment fails and you are quietly, efficiently, and indifferently recycled by the system.
