Excellent. This is a place of profound and dangerous beauty. A traveler must prepare not just their pack, but their mind. Here is your guide.

### A Traveler's Guide to the Felt: Preparations for Survival and Witness

**Foreword: The Nature of Your Destination**

Before we discuss gear and tactics, you must understand what you are visiting. The Felt is not a dead or dying world. It is a world that has reached its final, terrifyingly vibrant stage of life. It is a place of absolute immanence, where geology, biology, meteorology, and industry are a single, churning substance. There are no clear distinctions here, and your survival depends on abandoning the assumptions your own reality is built upon.

You are not visiting a ruin; you are stepping onto the factory floor of a living god-engine that is both building and consuming itself in the same eternal, deafening moment. The primary goal is not exploration in the traditional sense. It is *witness*. Your preparation should be geared toward enduring the environment long enough to behold it without being erased by it.

---

### Part 1: Essential Gear & Attire – The Shell of the Self

Your equipment is your first and last line of defense against an environment that is actively, if impersonally, hostile to foreign bodies. Do not cut corners.

**1.1. Footwear: Your Interface with the World**

This is the single most critical piece of your kit. The Felt is a cold, wet, yielding sponge the size of a continent.

*   **Primary Boots:** Insulated, completely waterproof, and extending well above the ankle. The material should be a durable synthetic polymer resistant to unknown chemical compounds. The water weeping from the Felt is not mere H2O; it is a primordial soup of biological agents and mineral solutes. Your boots must be sealed.
*   **The Overshoe/Platform:** Standard boots will sink, making every step an exhausting struggle. You will need a custom-built, pressure-distributing overshoe. Think of them as a hybrid of snowshoes and bog-waders. They should be wide, lightweight platforms that attach securely to your boots. The surface should be slightly convex to help shed the weeping fluid. This will allow you to walk *on* the Felt, rather than *in* it. Walking without them will leave you knee-deep in cold, organic slurry within a hundred yards.

**1.2. Clothing: The Layered Barrier**

The air is thin, but the humidity is 100%. The temperature is just above freezing, a penetrating cold that leaches warmth from a wet body with terrifying efficiency.

*   **Base Layer:** Synthetic, moisture-wicking material. Absolutely no cotton. This layer’s job is to pull the inevitable sweat and intrusive dampness away from your skin.
*   **Mid-Layer:** Fleece or high-loft synthetic insulation. This is your primary warmth. It must perform even when damp.
*   **Shell Layer:** Your armor. A hard-shell, fully seam-sealed suit (trousers and jacket with a hood) made of a material like Gore-Tex, but rated for industrial chemical exposure. Its purpose is threefold: 100% waterproofing against the fog and weeping ground, wind-proofing against drafts, and providing a minimal but crucial barrier against the shrapnel and boiling fluids ejected by the Stills. Choose a muted color; you do not want to draw unnecessary attention.

**1.3. Sensory Protection: Managing the Overload**

The Felt is an assault on the senses. Protection is not for comfort; it is for sanity and basic function.

*   **Auditory:** Industrial-grade, active noise-canceling headphones are **non-negotiable**. The low-frequency thrumming of the Stills is not just a sound; it is a physical vibration that can cause nausea, disorientation, and lasting psychological distress. Your headphones will reduce it to a manageable hum, allowing you to hear more immediate, high-frequency threats, like the shriek of a venting pipe or the skittering of fauna.
*   **Visual:** High-contrast, anti-fog goggles. Not sunglasses. There is no glare to cut. The world is lit by a dim, sourceless purple glow. You need goggles to protect your eyes from the constant mist, falling ice-needles, and airborne particulates. The high-contrast lens will help you define what little texture exists on the featureless plain.
*   **Respiratory:** A half-face respirator with cartridges rated for volatile organic compounds (VOCs) and acid gases. You will not wear it constantly, but you must have it ready. When a Still vents that oily, shimmering vapor that smells of ozone and hot metal, you have seconds to don it or risk searing your lungs.

**1.4. Navigation & Tools: Finding Your Way in Nowhere**

*   **Non-Magnetic Compass:** Assume the planet’s magnetic field is erratic or non-existent, and that the Stills generate immense electromagnetic interference. You will need a gyroscopic or celestial compass calibrated to the dying star and any other visible cosmic bodies.
*   **Chronometer:** An accurate timepiece is your most important navigational tool. On a flat, featureless plain, distance is time. You will navigate by "dead reckoning": walk on a set bearing for a set number of hours.
*   **Physical Logbook & Pencils:** Waterproof paper and graphite pencils. Your electronics may fail. You must keep a physical record of your movements, the timing of Still cycles, and observations. Map the Stills as you see them; they are your only landmarks.
*   **Light Source:** A headlamp with multiple brightness settings and a red-light mode. While the world is dimly lit, you may need focused light for tasks. The red light will preserve your night vision and is less likely to attract unwanted attention from fauna.
*   **Multi-tool & Pry Bar:** A robust multi-tool for gear maintenance. A small, lightweight pry bar might be useful for examining crystalline fragments or defending yourself in a last-ditch scenario.

### Part 2: Traversing the Plateau – The Psychology of Flatness

**2.1. The Rhythm of Walking**

Your overshoes will keep you on the surface, but walking will still be strange. The ground will yield with a soft *squelch*. Your footprint will immediately fill with dark fluid. Do not look back. The sight of your slowly-erasing trail can be existentially harrowing. Find a rhythm. The thrumming of the Stills can be a metronome if you filter it correctly. Walk, breathe, walk, breathe.

**2.2. Confronting Agoraphobia**

The scale is tailored to crush the human spirit. The perfect horizon, the absence of features, the sheer *emptiness* between the Stills—it will trigger a primal fear of open spaces.

*   **Anchor on a Still:** Never allow yourself to be in a position where you cannot see at least one Still. They are your lighthouses. Choose one on the horizon and make it your goal. This breaks the infinite down into a finite, achievable journey.
*   **Embrace the Fog:** The fog layer, while disorienting, is your psychological shield. It limits your view to a few dozen meters, creating a "room" around you. This can be a comfort, transforming the terrifying infinite into an intimate, personal space. Use it. When the agoraphobia becomes too much, sit, focus on your immediate surroundings within the fog, and recalibrate.

### Part 3: The Stills – Engines of Chaos

The Stills are both the world's most magnificent feature and its greatest danger.

*   **Maintain Distance:** A one-mile exclusion zone is the absolute minimum. Two is better. You can observe their full grandeur from this distance.
*   **Learn the Signs:** You are on a factory floor with no safety regulations. You are your own foreman.
    *   **The Shriek:** A high-pitched screaming sound is the prelude to a steam vent. Note the direction of the wind. The superheated steam will billow and then freeze into a blizzard of ice-needles that can strip exposed skin. Get downwind or take cover.
    *   **The Groan:** A low, grinding groan from the chitinous pipes signals a pressure release of chemical vapor. This is when you don your respirator. The oily cloud is often heavier than air and will hug the Felt.
    *   **The Shudder:** If the baseline thrumming in your feet intensifies into a violent shudder, an overload is imminent. The Still's vascular sacs may begin to pulse erratically, glowing brighter. This is your signal to run or drop flat to the ground. An explosion will send boiling organic fluid and crystalline shrapnel flying for hundreds of meters. The Felt itself will absorb much of the impact, but being caught in the open is a death sentence.

### Part 4: A Field Guide to the Inhabitants

You are not alone. All life here is part of the machinery. Do not interfere.

*   **The Grazers (Armadillo-Filters):** These bus-sized creatures are placid and slow. Their danger lies in their sheer mass. Do not get in their path. Their glowing lichen shells can serve as useful, moving landmarks in the gloom. They are not interested in you. Give them a wide berth.
*   **The Still-Swarms:**
    *   **Crystalline Beetles:** Scavengers. They are drawn to the shrapnel fields after an explosion. They are single-minded and will ignore you unless you appear to be competing for their resource.
    *   **Thermic Serpents:** These translucent, glowing creatures are fascinating but unpredictable. They are mobile batteries. When coiled on a Still, they are absorbing energy. When slithering across the Felt, they are discharging it. Other, smaller organisms may swarm them for warmth. Do not approach one. Their method of energy storage and discharge is unknown; touching one could be equivalent to grabbing a live power line.
*   **The Sky-Kites:** Their calls are part of the soundscape. They ride thermal updrafts from the Stills. While mostly harmless, they may dive out of curiosity. If one approaches, stay still and cover your head. Their membrane-wings are likely tougher than they appear.

### Part 5: Metaphysical and Psychological Preparation

**The Hum:** The noise-canceling headphones are essential, but you can never eliminate the thrum entirely. It will be a constant companion, felt in your bones. You must learn to meditate on it, to accept it as the planet's heartbeat. Fighting it will lead to madness.

**The Thinness of Reality:** Do not attempt to analyze the world with your home reality's physics. The line between living and non-living, energy and matter, is not just blurred; it's a foolish question to ask. The temporary energy lattices near steam vents are a perfect example: they are real, but they are not *stable*. Observe them, marvel at them, but do not interact. Trying to "capture" or "study" them with your own equipment is hubris that this world will punish swiftly.

**Your Purpose:** Accept that you are small. Accept that the world's frantic industry has no purpose you can comprehend, and perhaps no purpose at all. It simply *is*. You are there to see a system that has settled into a final, violent equilibrium. Your role is to be a ghost, a fleeting witness. Leave no trace but the footprints that the Felt itself will heal. Take no souvenirs but the memory of the cold, purple sky and the deep, world-shaking hum of the Stills.

Survive, witness, and be humbled. That is the only victory to be won on the Felt.
