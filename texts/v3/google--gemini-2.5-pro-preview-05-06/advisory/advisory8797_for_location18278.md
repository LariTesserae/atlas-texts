**Multiverse Traveler's Advisory: The Engine-Garden of KV-341c**

**Hazard Classification:** Class 4 (Hazardous Atmosphere, Unstable Terrain, Extreme Isolation). Visit is restricted to experienced travelers with appropriate life-support equipment. Short-duration, non-invasive observation only.

***

### Introduction: The World in a Bottle

Welcome, traveler, to the cavern unofficially designated the "Engine-Garden." Do not mistake its slow, rhythmic tranquility for safety. This is a sealed environment, a biomechanical terrarium operating on a closed loop of logic that has no place for an organism like yourself. It is a place of profound, singular beauty, but it is utterly indifferent to your survival.

To visit the Engine-Garden is to step inside a vast, geological lung that is dreaming of being a forest. The rules here are ancient and slow. Your visit will be a brief footnote in a cycle that has been turning for centuries and will likely turn for centuries more after you leave. The primary goal is non-interference. You are a ghost, a witness, a visitor to a museum where the exhibits are alive, and the curator is a dying machine. Prepare accordingly.

### Part 1: Pre-Travel Preparations & Essential Gear

Your survival here is entirely dependent on what you bring with you. Native resources are either non-existent or lethally incompatible with human biology.

**1. Atmospheric Adaptation: The First and Most Critical Barrier**

You cannot breathe the air. Let this be understood without exception. The atmosphere is a hot, corrosive, low-oxygen mineral soup. Attempting to breathe it will result in rapid heatstroke, chemical burns to your lungs, heavy metal poisoning, and suffocation.

*   **Required:** A full-face, closed-loop rebreather system. Do not skimp. This is not a place for a simple filter mask. Your system must perform three functions:
    *   **Cooling:** It must actively cool the air it draws in before it reaches your lungs. Inhaling air at this ambient temperature (~45-50°C / 113-122°F) would quickly cause lethal internal inflammation.
    *   **Dehumidification & Filtration:** The system needs a high-capacity condenser to strip the excessive moisture and a multi-stage filter to remove the suspended metallic and silicate particulates. These will taste like hot iron on your tongue but will feel like ground glass in your alveoli.
    *   **Oxygen Supply:** Since you are on a closed loop, you will need a sufficient supply of compressed oxygen for double your planned excursion time. Assume no rescue.

*   **Skin Protection:** The pressure and humidity will feel like being submerged in warm oil. A standard spacesuit is overkill and will overheat. Instead, opt for a full-body, non-insulated environmental skin-suit. This should be made of a breathable but waterproof laminate material, designed for high-humidity, high-temperature jungle environments. Its primary purpose is to keep the clinging, mineral-heavy moisture off your skin to prevent severe rashes, chemical irritation, and fungal infections.

**2. Physical & Environmental Gear: Navigating the Wreckage**

The ground is a treacherous field of shattered rock and unseen gaps.

*   **Footwear:** Your boots must have thick, puncture-proof soles and high, rigid ankle support. The floor is a chaos of knife-sharp obsidian shards and tilted basalt plates. A misstep could result in a severe cut or a twisted ankle, both of which would be catastrophic here. Magnetic-soled boots are of no use; the metal in the rock is dispersed and non-ferrous.

*   **Gloves and Hand Protection:** You will need reinforced, cut-resistant gloves at all times. You will inevitably need to steady yourself against rock formations, and touching them bare-handed will leave you with a web of bleeding cuts.

*   **Illumination: A Delicate Balance:** The cavern is self-illuminating, and to overwhelm it with your own light is to ruin the very essence of the place.
    *   **Primary "Light":** Your own eyes. Allow them 15-20 minutes upon arrival to dark-adapt. The silver-blue fungal light is sufficient for careful navigation once you are accustomed to it.
    *   **Secondary Light:** A low-lumen, variable-setting headlamp with a prominent red-light function. Use the red light for close-up tasks like checking your gear or examining a surface. It will preserve your night vision. Only use the white light in an absolute emergency (e.g., equipment malfunction, treating an injury). Blasting the cavern with a 500-lumen beam is an act of vandalism; the slow-pulsing shadows will vanish, and the delicate glow of the flora will be washed out, turning the magical into the mundane.
    *   **Navigational Aids:** Your compass will work. GPS will not. This is a single, vast chamber, but its scale and darkness can be disorienting. Bring biodegradable, phosphorescent trail markers (small sticks or flags) if you plan to explore far from your entry point. Lay them down as you go, and collect them on your return.

**3. Sustenance: Carry-In, Carry-Out**

*   **Water:** The air is water, but you cannot drink it. The constant steam-rain is laden with minerals leached from the rock, likely toxic and certainly unpalatable. Bring a minimum of 4 liters of your own water for a 6-hour excursion. The heat and humidity will dehydrate you faster than you realize, even with minimal exertion. Your suit and rebreather will mitigate this, but not eliminate it.
*   **Food:** Do not, under any circumstances, attempt to consume the local flora. The glowing fungus and purple-veined plants have evolved in a heavy-metal environment. They are almost certainly bio-accumulators of toxic minerals. All food must be brought with you, preferably in the form of nutrient paste or ration bars that can be consumed without removing your faceplate.

### Part 2: On-Site Protocol & Psychological Preparedness

Your behavior in the Engine-Garden is as important as your equipment.

**1. The Rhythm of the Place**

The Engine-Garden has a heartbeat. It is the slow, deep *thump* of the central boiler, occurring every few minutes. Sync yourself to it. Move slowly. Plant your feet deliberately. This is not a place to rush. Haste leads to falls, and falls lead to disaster. The slow, creeping pace of the shadows cast by the glowing fungi should be your guide. Let the environment dictate your speed.

**2. The Sound of Silence**

Prepare for a silence that has physical weight. It is a baseline of absolute quiet, punctuated by three sounds: the boiler's *thump*, the hiss of venting steam, and the gentle drip of condensation. This can be deeply unnerving. You may feel a pressure on your eardrums. Do not wear earplugs; you need to remain aware of your surroundings (the hiss of the steam is your primary warning of a heat-plume event). Embrace the silence. Add to it. Do not speak unless necessary. Your own voice will sound like a shout in a library. This is a meditative experience. Treat it as such.

**3. The Principle of Non-Interference**

*   **Touch Nothing:** Beyond what is necessary for balance, do not touch the flora. The pearlescent stalks of the great fungi are likely coated in their dripping, viscous fluid—its properties are unknown. The velvet mosses are fragile ecosystems. Do not harvest souvenirs.
*   **Stay Grounded:** Do not attempt to climb the industrial remains. The iron catwalk is visibly corroded and ends in a sheer drop into darkness. The metal is untrustworthy and will likely crumble under your weight. Observe these monuments to a forgotten purpose from a safe distance.
*   **The Boiler-Heart:** The central engine is the system's dying god. Approach with caution. The ground around it is likely the hottest, and the web of pipes presents a significant tripping hazard. Do not stand directly under the filigreed grille at its apex. When you feel the deep *thump*, know that the steam vent will follow. The plume is superheated and will cause severe burns, even through your suit. Observe it from at least 30 meters away.

**4. Psychological Anchoring**

The combination of heat, pressure, darkness, and profound isolation can be disorienting and anxiety-inducing. You will feel like an intruder, an alien thing in this self-contained world. This is normal.

*   **Anchor Yourself:** Before you enter, state your purpose and your intended duration. "I am here to observe for four hours." This creates a mental boundary.
*   **Journal:** Using a datapad or waterproof notepad, record your observations. Focus on the details: the slow pulse of the light, the pattern of water droplets on a purple leaf, the specific shade of green on the verdigris of the sunken gear. This analytical focus will keep your mind from drifting into unease.
*   **Acknowledge the Feeling:** Accept the sense of being an outsider. You *are* an outsider. Your presence is temporary. The Engine-Garden was not made for you, but you have the rare privilege of seeing it. Find beauty in its loneliness and your brief intrusion into it.

### Departure

Leave as you came: slowly, quietly, and deliberately. Collect any markers you left. Do a final gear check before exiting. The transition back to a normal atmosphere will be a relief, but the memory of the Engine-Garden—the warm, heavy air, the cool, living light, and the slow, steady heartbeat of a machine dreaming it was a forest—will stay with you for a very long time. It is a place that reminds you that even in the deepest darkness, in the forgotten wreckage of the past, life and beauty can find a way to make their own quiet light.
