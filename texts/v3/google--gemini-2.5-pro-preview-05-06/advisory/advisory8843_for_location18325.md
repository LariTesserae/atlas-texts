### Inter-Continuum Travel Advisory
**Destination:** The Weeping Tableland (Provisional Designation)
**Threat Level:** Amber (Environmental/Psychological; Non-Hostile)
**Prepared by:** Senior Traveler Liaison, Department of Cross-Temporal Excursions

Welcome, traveler. You have selected a destination of uncommon beauty and stillness. It is not hostile, but it is utterly indifferent. Indifference can be more dangerous than malice. Survival here is not a matter of fighting anything, but of enduring a perfect, unchanging state. Do not underestimate the toll that can take. Read this document thoroughly. Your comfort, your sanity, and your safe return may depend on it.

### **Section 1: Physical Preparation & Equipment**

The environment here is singular in its focus: it is wet. Every piece of equipment, every scrap of clothing, every aspect of your preparation must be filtered through this reality.

#### **1.1: The Three-Layer System (Mandatory)**

The temperature is a constant, balmy warmth, so you won't be fighting cold. You will, however, be fighting a constant, penetrating dampness that can lead to chafing, skin ailments, and a deep, morale-sapping sense of clamminess.

*   **Base Layer:** Synthetic or merino wool. Absolutely **no cotton**. You need something that wicks moisture away from your skin and dries quickly. You will be sweating in the humid air, and the air itself will be trying to condense on you. Bring at least three full sets (top and bottom).
*   **Mid-Layer (Optional/Situational):** A light fleece or synthetic puffy jacket. Not for warmth, but for managing comfort during periods of inactivity. It's something dry and comforting to put on inside your shelter.
*   **Outer Shell (The Most Critical Item):** You need the best waterproof, breathable hardshell jacket and pants you can acquire. Look for multi-layer Gore-Tex Pro or an equivalent. Every seam must be sealed. All zippers must be water-resistant. It should have a large, adjustable hood with a stiff brim to keep the constant drips off your face. This isn't for rain; it's for moving through the very air itself.

#### **1.2: Footwear: Your Connection to a Treacherous World**

Your life will be spent on stone made slick by water and a film of emerald algae. One bad fall could be catastrophic.

*   **Boots:** High-ankled, fully waterproof hiking boots with an integrated membrane. The sole is paramount: you need a soft, "sticky" rubber compound with deep, aggressive, multi-directional lugs. Think of the footwear used for canyoning or approaching wet rock climbs. Boots that are too stiff will not provide adequate grip on the slick, uneven stone.
*   **Socks:** Multiple pairs of merino wool or synthetic hiking socks. You will need to change them at least twice a day to prevent trench foot and other moisture-related ailments. Let one pair dry (as much as anything can) while you wear the other.
*   **Gaiters:** Waterproof, high-top gaiters are non-negotiable. They will bridge the gap between your pants and boots, preventing water from seeping in from the top as you move. They will also offer some protection for your shins against the sharp edges of the young rock.

#### **1.3: Navigation & Tools**

*   **Trekking Poles (x2):** Essential. They will provide two additional points of contact on the slippery causeways and help you probe the ground if you are ever forced to step off the stone paths. Fit them with rubber tips to improve grip on the stone and reduce the clattering noise that will feel like sacrilege in the silence.
*   **Navigation:** While the causeways are straight, the unchanging sky and repetitive landscape can be disorienting. A reliable physical compass is insurance. Your multi-universal device's GPS may or may not function, but you should have a downloaded map of the causeway network you intend to explore. Mark your entry point.
*   **First-Aid:** Pack a fully waterproof first-aid kit. Focus on supplies for cuts and abrasions (the rock is sharp) and blister/skin care. Include antiseptic wipes, sterile dressings, and a good skin-drying powder.

#### **1.4: Shelter & Sustenance**

You are a self-contained unit. The world offers nothing but water.

*   **Shelter:** A one-person, high-quality, freestanding tent. It must have an excellent waterproof flysheet and a "bathtub" floor to keep you off the sodden ground. You'll likely pitch on the stone plazas, so a tent that doesn't require stakes is vital.
*   **Water:** The water *seems* pure, but its absolute sterility is unnerving. Is it just H2O, or does it lack the trace minerals necessary for human biology? Bring a high-grade water filter *and* a purification system (UV or chemical) as a backup. Plan to drink more water than you think you need due to the humidity.
*   **Food:** All food must be carried in. High-energy, low-weight, low-odor. Dehydrated meals are ideal. There is nothing to hunt, nothing to forage. Every calorie you consume must be one you brought with you.
*   **Waste:** A system for packing out *everything*. This includes all food wrappers, used supplies, and human waste. The environment has no mechanism for decay. Your apple core will not rot. Your toilet paper will not dissolve. It will remain, perfectly preserved and waterlogged, a permanent stain on the landscape. Behave as if you are visiting a museum.

### **Section 2: Psychological & Sensory Preparation**

This is more important than your gear. The Weeping Tableland is a test of the human mind's ability to handle monotasking.

#### **2.1: Embracing the Silence**

The silence is not empty; it is full. It is filled with the near-subliminal, polyrhythmic sound of dripping and seeping. For the first few hours, it is peaceful. After a day, it can become a form of auditory torture, the "water drip" method on a planetary scale.

*   **Preparation:** Do not try to fight it. Meditate. Practice mindfulness before you go. Learn to sit in silence.
*   **Mitigation:** Bring a small audio player with headphones. Do not use it constantly, or the silence will rush back in with a vengeance when you stop. Use it as a tool. A piece of classical music once a day. A chapter of an audiobook before you sleep. It is a lifeline to a world of change and noise, use it sparingly.

#### **2.2: The Unchanging Light & Time**

There is no sunrise, no sunset, no moon, no stars. Just a perpetual, pearlescent brightness. This will destroy your circadian rhythm.

*   **Preparation:** A reliable, rugged watch is your most important psychological tool. Keep it on your home time, or set it to a 24-hour cycle you create. Your day is divided by the clock, not the sky.
*   **Mitigation:** Live by your watch. Eat at designated times. Walk for a set number of hours. Stop and make camp at a designated time. When it is "night," put on an eye mask to create darkness in your tent. This artificial structure is the only thing that will keep the endless, seamless day from eroding your sense of the passage of time.

#### **2.3: The Absence of Life**

You will not realize how much you rely on the background hum of an ecosystem until it is gone. No birdsong to mark the morning. No insectile buzz to fill the afternoon. No scuttling in the undergrowth at night. This is profoundly unnerving. It will make you feel like the last living thing in the universe.

*   **Preparation:** You must be your own animal life. Hum to yourself. Talk to yourself (out loud). Keep a detailed journal, writing in it, describing your thoughts and feelings. Externalize your internal monologue. The sound of your own voice will be a vital proof of your own existence.
*   **Mitigation:** If possible, do not go alone. A single companion doubles the amount of "life" in the landscape. Conversations become anchors to reality. Just seeing another living thing move is a comfort you cannot measure.

### **Final Briefing: The Philosophy of the Visit**

You are not an explorer discovering a new world. You are a visitor to a monument. The purpose of this city is not for you to divine, but to witness.

Walk the causeways. Feel the immense, patient effort that laid each stone. Touch the slick algae. Look into the perfect silver pools. Listen to the weeping cliffs.

Do not try to "solve" this place. You cannot. It is a completed thought. It is not waiting for a king, a ceremony, or an army. Its purpose *is* its waiting.

Your role is to be a silent, respectful witness for a short time. Your challenge is to allow the silence and stasis of the landscape to enter you without consuming you. Stay for a few days, feel the weight of eternity, take your notes and your pictures, and then leave. Leave it as you found it: perfect, silent, and complete.
