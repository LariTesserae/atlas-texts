**Multiverse Traveler's Advisory & Preparation Guide**
**Designation: Geode-Terrarium Sigma-7 (Informally: "The Weeping Bastion")**

**[[WARNING: THIS IS A CLASS IV 'LIVING ARCHITECTURE' ENVIRONMENT. STANDARD PREPARATION PROTOCOLS ARE INSUFFICIENT. READ THIS GUIDE IN ITS ENTIRETY BEFORE DEPARTURE. FAILURE TO COMPLY MAY RESULT IN PERSONAL HARM, EQUIPMENT LOSS, AND/OR IRREVERSIBLE DAMAGE TO THE DESTINATION.]]**

### **1.0: First Principles: Understanding The Weeping Bastion**

Before we discuss gear and conduct, you must fundamentally recalibrate your understanding of what a "place" is. You are not visiting a structure with life inside it; you are visiting a single, massive, integrated organism that is part structure, part ecosystem. Think of it less like a cathedral and more like the inside of a chrysalis the size of a mountain. Every element is connected and purposeful. Your primary goal is not exploration, but *witnessing*. Your presence is, by definition, a contamination. The goal is to minimize that contamination to near-zero.

**The Three Truths of the Bastion:**

1.  **Everything is Wet:** This is not humidity, it is super-saturation. The air is an active participant, constantly delivering mineral-rich water to every surface. Nothing you bring will remain dry unless it is hermetically sealed.
2.  **Everything is Growing:** The floor you walk on, the walls you might touch, the pillars you stand beside—they are not inert. They are actively precipitating, hardening, and growing. Assume all surfaces are in a state of delicate, slow-motion flux.
3.  **Everything is Purposeful:** The gentle hum, the dripping water, the iridescent beetles, the gelatinous pool-dwellers—they are not ambient phenomena. They are the circulatory, respiratory, and immune systems of the Bastion. Do not interfere with them.

### **2.0: Personal Attire & Gear: The Philosophy of the Second Skin**

Your clothing is your first and most important interface with this environment. Its failure will lead to immediate and cascading problems.

**2.1: Clothing:**
*   **Materials:** Absolutely no cotton, denim, or leather. These materials will absorb the atmospheric moisture, become impossibly heavy, refuse to dry, and cause severe chafing and potential hypothermia (despite the warmth, a constantly wet body loses heat). Your wardrobe must be 100% synthetic (nylon, polyester) or specialized merino wool. Think of the gear used for tropical caving or jungle trekking.
*   **Layering:** A single, breathable base layer designed for high-humidity heat is sufficient. A lightweight, fully waterproof (Gore-Tex Pro or equivalent) but *highly breathable* outer shell is non-negotiable, not for rain, but to provide a barrier between your skin and the weeping walls and condensing surfaces. It will be warm, so ensure it has ample ventilation zips ("pit zips" are essential).
*   **Footwear:** This is your most critical gear choice. The geopolymer surfaces range from smooth and slick as wet tile to rough and crystalline. You need boots with exceptional grip on wet, varied surfaces. Look for canyoneering or spelunking boots with "sticky rubber" soles. They must be waterproof but also able to drain if submerged. High ankles will provide support and prevent sediment from entering. Metal cleats are forbidden, as they will damage the delicate, forming surfaces.
*   **Hands & Head:** A pair of durable, waterproof, high-dexterity gloves are mandatory. You will need your hands for balance, and touching the raw, mineral-weeping walls barehanded is an unknown chemical risk. A lightweight hood or a synthetic beanie will keep the constant dripping from your hair and face, which is a surprisingly significant psychological comfort.

**2.2: Personal Equipment:**
*   **Packs:** Your primary pack should be a caver's pack made from durable PVC or a climber's haul bag. These are essentially waterproof buckets with shoulder straps. Everything inside the pack must be further protected in individual, color-coded dry-sacks.
*   **Electronics:** Assume any unshielded electronic device will be ruined by condensation within minutes. Phones, cameras, and data pads must be kept in hard-shell, IP68-rated waterproof cases (e.g., Pelican cases) at all times unless in active use. Even then, exposure should be minimal. Expect camera lenses to fog instantly when removed from their case; allow time for them to acclimatize. Bring desiccants (silica gel packs) to store inside your electronics cases.

### **3.0: Health & Sustenance: Operating Within a Closed System**

**3.1: Hydration and Nutrition:**
*   **CRITICAL WARNING:** **DO NOT, under any circumstances, drink the water.** The milky liquid weeping from the walls and dripping from the ceiling is not water in the biological sense; it is a geopolymer feedstock, a chemical soup rich in minerals and compounds designed to build the structure. Ingesting it could lead to heavy metal poisoning, rapid internal petrification, or other fatal outcomes.
*   **Your Supply:** All water must be carried in. Calculate your needs for a high-heat, high-exertion environment and then add a 50% surplus. Use durable, sealed canteens or hydration bladders.
*   **Food:** Bring only self-contained, high-energy, no-mess food items (ration bars, nutrient paste pouches). Leave no crumbs, no wrappers, no organic waste. The principle is **Zero Trace.** The Bastion's decomposers (the amoebic creatures, the beetles) are hyper-specialized; they are not equipped to process your trail mix.

**3.2: Physical and Psychological Well-being:**
*   **Breathing:** The warm, thick, heavy air can feel oppressive. Practice slow, deliberate breathing. For travelers with respiratory sensitivities, this location may be untenable. The air is clean but carries the smell of wet clay, ozone, and setting concrete—a sign of its active chemical nature.
*   **Pacing:** Move at a quarter of your normal speed. The heat, the humidity, and the treacherous footing demand a slow, mindful pace to avoid exhaustion and injury. Rest often.
*   **The Soundscape:** The only sounds are the deep, subliminal hum of the central machinery and the constant, arrhythmic patter of a million drips. There are no birds, no wind, no rustling leaves. This can be deeply unsettling. Some travelers find it meditative; others find it maddening. Be prepared for this sensory monotony. Using an audio player with headphones is not recommended, as it will dull your awareness of your immediate surroundings.

### **4.0: Conduct & Navigation: Treading on Hallowed, Unfinished Ground**

You are a ghost here. Your goal is to pass through without being felt.

**4.1: Movement and Navigation:**
*   **Walk Softly:** There are no paths. Each step is a choice. Aim for the older, more established surfaces, which are often darker and covered in thick moss. Avoid the pale, glistening areas where new material is actively hardening. Test every footstep before committing your full weight.
*   **The Three-Point Rule:** When moving up or down any incline, maintain three points of contact at all times (two feet and one hand, or two hands and one foot). The environment is vertical as much as it is horizontal.
*   **Respect the Flora:** The plant life is inextricably woven into the structure. Do not break vines, pull moss, or trample ferns. They are not decorations; they are integral components, likely assisting in water distribution and structural reinforcement.
*   **Respect the Fauna:** Give the large beetles and other creatures a wide berth. They are not animals in the way you know them; they are mobile, biological components of the Bastion's maintenance system. Observe them, but do not touch or disturb them. Their carapaces may carry corrosive or toxic cleansing agents.

**4.2: Interaction with Technology:**
*   **The Heart-Engine:** The central, gill-like machine core is the heart of the Bastion. Approach it with extreme caution. The slow-moving pistons operate with immense hydraulic force. The insulated conduits woven throughout the chamber carry high pressures and unknown chemical agents. Do not touch, lean on, or attempt to interface with any of it. It is an autonomous system; it has no manual override for you to exploit.

### **5.0: Final Advisory: Adjusting Your Timescale**

The greatest challenge of visiting The Weeping Bastion is mental. You come from a world of seconds and minutes. This is a place that operates on the timescale of geology and botany. The dripping water is counting the centuries. The silent unfurling of a fern frond is a momentous event.

To truly appreciate this place, you must abandon your human urgency. Sit. Be still. Let the warm water bead on your waterproof shell. Watch a single drop of milky fluid trace a path down a pillar and see where it begins to harden. Feel the deep thrum of the engine in your chest.

You are not an explorer discovering a ruin. You are a mote of dust floating through a womb, privileged to witness the slow, wet, deliberate birth of a world. Act accordingly. Leave nothing, take nothing, and try, as best you can, to not even leave a ripple in the air.
