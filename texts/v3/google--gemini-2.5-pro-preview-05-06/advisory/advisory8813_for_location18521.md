### **Traveler's Advisory: The Kiln-Garden**
**Threat Level:** Extreme (Environmental), Profound (Psychological)
**Visit Classification:** Non-Interactive Observation & Study
**Primary Directive:** You are a ghost. Act like one.

### **Introduction: A Survivor’s Primer**

Before you even consider the passage, understand this: The Kiln-Garden is not a destination. It is a condition. It is a functioning, self-sustaining geological engine that happens to be shaped like a city. You are not a welcome guest, nor are you an unwelcome one. You are simply irrelevant to its processes. Your survival depends entirely on your ability to insulate yourself from its fundamental nature, while your enrichment depends on your ability to observe it without interference.

Forget any notion of "roughing it" or "living off the land." The land is a furnace and its bounty is poison. There is no air you can breathe, no water you can drink, no food you can eat, and no surface you can safely touch with your bare skin. Your preparation must be absolute. Any failure in your equipment or your discipline will not result in inconvenience; it will result in an immediate and unremarkable death.

### **Part 1: Pre-Departure Preparations - Hardening the Self**

This is the most critical phase. Your journey is won or lost before you ever feel that first wave of heat.

**A. Physiological Conditioning:**

1.  **Heat Acclimatization:** Begin a regimen of extreme sauna and heat-chamber conditioning at least two standard months prior to your trip. You need to train your body to function at the edge of heatstroke. This will not allow you to survive without a suit, but it will reduce the shock and physiological stress your body undergoes even within a climate-controlled system.
2.  **Pressure Training:** Spend time in high-pressure environments. The initial descent is a barometric assault. Train your sinuses and inner ears to equalize under intense, gradual pressure shifts. Practice advanced Valsalva maneuvers.
3.  **Hydration Discipline:** You must become a master of your own fluid levels. For a month beforehand, practice hyper-hydration and electrolyte balancing. Learn to recognize the earliest, most subtle signs of dehydration—a slight stickiness of the tongue, a faint headache behind the eyes. In the Kiln-Garden, these are late-stage warnings.
4.  **Sensory Deprivation & Overload Training:** Spend prolonged periods in isolation with only a constant, low-frequency drone for company. This will prepare you for the psychological weight of the Kiln-Garden's soundscape and its profound loneliness. The drone isn't just noise; it's a presence. You must learn to coexist with it without letting it grind down your sanity.

**B. Essential Gear: The Shell**

Your survival is encapsulated in your Environmental Suit. Do not skimp. Do not rent. Acquire the best you can and learn its every function until it is a second skin.

1.  **The Suit:** A fully enclosed, hard-shell exo-suit is mandatory.
    *   **Cooling System:** This is its most vital function. It must be a closed-loop system, not one that vents to the atmosphere. It will need a high-capacity thermal sink and a robust radiator system. Be aware: the suit's exterior will become dangerously hot to the touch. The system must be powerful enough to maintain a stable 25-30°C internal temperature while the exterior surface is cooking at over 150°C. Redundancy is not a luxury; have a backup cooling loop.
    *   **Atmosphere:** A rebreather and atmospheric processor is essential. It must be a closed-loop system that scrubs CO2 and, crucially, filters incoming particulate matter. The air is thick with silicate dust from the shattering crystal flora. Inhaling this is equivalent to breathing microscopic glass shards. Your filters must be rated for crystalline and mineral particulates down to the micron level.
    *   **Hydration:** An integrated hydration bladder with a direct-to-mouth tube is a given. It should be linked to an internal biomonitor that tracks your perspiration rate and electrolyte levels, advising you when to drink. You will lose moisture at an alarming rate even within the suit. Pack at least three times the water you think you'll need.
    *   **Power:** A miniaturized fission battery or equivalent long-duration power source. Solar is useless here. You need at least 200% of the power required for your planned visit duration.
    *   **Visor:** The visor must be polarized and photo-reactive, capable of filtering the intense, specific wavelengths of the thermal glow. It should also have a HUD overlay displaying suit diagnostics, environmental data (temperature, pressure, air quality), and a real-time mapping feed. An infrared or thermal imaging mode is essential for navigating darker areas and understanding the heat flow.

2.  **Sustenance:**
    *   **Food:** Nutrient-dense, zero-waste paste or gels. Cooking is impossible. You will not be taking off your helmet. Plan your meals to be consumed through a sealed port.
    *   **Water:** As stated, bring an extreme surplus. There is no possibility of replenishment. The concept of liquid water is alien to this place. To spill a drop on the obsidian floor would be to watch it vanish in a hiss of steam, an act of cosmic waste.

3.  **Tools & Navigation:**
    *   **Footwear:** Your suit’s boots must have soles made of a material with an exceptionally high melting point and low thermal conductivity. They should be cleated or textured for grip on the polished obsidian, which can be slick with settled dust.
    *   **Staff/Walking Aid:** A staff made of a non-conductive ceramic composite is highly recommended. Use it to probe the ground, to check the stability of crystalline formations before getting too close, and for support on the great ramps. It is your only safe way to physically interact with the world.
    *   **Navigation:** GPS is non-existent. You will need an inertial navigation unit and a handheld LIDAR scanner to map your surroundings in 3D. The drone and hissing can be used for a primitive form of acoustic triangulation, but this requires a trained ear and specific software. Trust your scanner.
    *   **Sample Collection (Observe Only):** Do not plan on taking physical samples. The flora will likely disintegrate upon cooling, and the rock is part of a singular, monolithic structure. Your samples are your sensor readings, your photographs, your acoustic recordings. Bring high-capacity data storage.

### **Part 2: On-Site Conduct - The Rules of Intrusion**

**A. The Descent and Arrival:**

The drop in pressure and rise in heat is your final warning. Equalize your ears constantly. Keep your suit's cooling system on a low-level standby to manage the external temperature change. When you emerge onto the ledge, stand still for a full five minutes. Do not move. Let your senses, filtered through your suit, adjust. Let the scale of the basin, the drone, the heat, and the dim light wash over you. This is a moment of calibration. Acknowledge your insignificance.

**B. Navigating the Basin:**

*   **The Obsidian Floor:** The vast black plains are generally safe to cross, but treat them with respect. They are ancient and polished. Move deliberately. The reflections can be disorienting; trust your instruments over your eyes. The "islands" of kiln-gardens are to be given a wide berth. The ground around them is the hottest, and the flora itself is a hazard.
*   **The Kiln-Gardens:** Do not enter the groves. Observe from a distance. The spun glass stalks are fragile but their broken shards are sharper than scalpels. The basaltic needles will shred your suit's outer layer with casual ease. The "mineral moss" grows on the most intensely radioactive surfaces. Approaching it is courting a significant dose of thermal and particle radiation that your suit may not be fully rated for.
*   **The City Terraces:** This is where you will spend most of your time. The ramps are wide and stable. The carved dwellings offer refuge not from attack, but from the raw, open intensity of the basin floor. Use them as observation posts. The higher you climb, the "cooler" the air will be, but this is a relative term. Your suit's cooling system will have an easier time on the upper terraces.

**C. Understanding the Thermal Pulse:**

This is the rhythm of the Kiln-Garden. It is your clock and your only form of "weather."
*   **The Swell (Flood Tide):** The drone deepens, the hissing intensifies, and the ambient light brightens to a fiercer crimson. This is when the flora "grows." It is the moment of greatest beauty and greatest energy. Your suit will work its hardest during this phase. Find a sheltered spot on a mid-level terrace and simply watch.
*   **The Ebb (Low Tide):** The energy recedes. The light dims. The drone softens. This is when the silica flowers shatter, creating the delicate, constant chiming and a fresh cloud of silicate dust. The air will be at its thickest with particulates. Ensure your filters are working at peak capacity. This is a period of rest, a good time to move between locations.

### **Part 3: Psychological Preparedness - The Weight of Stone and Time**

The Kiln-Garden’s greatest threat may be to your mind.
1.  **The Drone:** You must learn to hear the music in it. Hear the grinding of geology, the hiss of planetary respiration, the chime of creation and destruction. If you fight it, it will feel like a drill boring into your skull. If you accept it, it becomes the heartbeat of the world.
2.  **The Emptiness:** The city was made for millions. You are alone. The scale of the architecture and the total absence of its creators can induce a profound sense of agoraphobia and existential dread, a condition some travelers call "Lithic Melancholy." You are a transient thought in an eternal stone mind. Do not try to fill the silence. Let it be. Document the structures, appreciate their passive genius, and honor the silence of their builders.
3.  **The Light:** The constant, sunless, thermal glow can disrupt your circadian rhythm. Rely on a chronometer within your suit. Allot designated "rest" periods where you dim your visor and sit in a sheltered alcove, even if you do not sleep.

### **Departure Protocol**

Leave nothing. Not a single piece of discarded equipment, not a food wrapper, not a mark on the stone. Your LIDAR scans, your recordings, and your memories are your souvenirs. As you ascend back through the narrow passage, the pressure lifting and the heat receding, the silence and coolness of the outside world will feel profoundly alien. You may find yourself missing the drone. This is normal.

You went to the Kiln-Garden to bear witness to a world without water, a life born of heat. You have seen the bones of a planet and the ghost of a civilization built in its heart. Carry that weight and that wonder with you. It is the only thing you are permitted to take.
