### **Traveler's Advisory: The Autotrophic Stacks (Cell-Cathedral Designation 734)**

**Classification:** Non-Hostile, Bio-Mechanical, Sterile Environment.
**Primary Challenge:** Sensory Adaptation & Psychological Endurance.

#### **I. Introduction: Understanding Where You Are Going**

You are not visiting a "place" in the conventional sense. You are preparing to enter the circulatory system of a single, vast, living machine. Think of it less like exploring a cave or a jungle and more like performing a micro-endoscopy on a sleeping god. The environment is not hostile, but it is **intolerant**. It operates on principles of absolute sterility and glacial, perfect efficiency. Your presence is, by definition, a contamination risk. The entire experience is predicated on you being a ghost—a perfect observer who leaves no trace, not even a ripple. The rewards are immense: a vision of biological and mechanical synthesis on a scale that redefines life itself. But the price of admission is total self-control.

#### **II. Pre-Travel Preparations: Conditioning Your Body and Mind**

This is the most critical phase. Do not rush it. Arriving unprepared is not an option.

**1. Physical Conditioning: The Drowning Practice**

*   **Fluid Breathing Acclimation:** You cannot simply arrive and breathe the ambient medium. Your mammalian panic reflex—the one that screams *DROWNING*—must be systematically un-learned. You will need to undergo weeks of gradual Fluid Breathing Acclimation Therapy (FBAT). This involves sessions in a training tank filled with a beta-version of the viscous, oxygen-rich fluid. Medics will monitor as you learn to consciously suppress your diaphragm's spasms and allow your lungs to fill.
*   **The Slow Breath:** The exchange is not like air. It's a slow, deliberate inflation and a heavy, syrupy deflation. Your heart rate will drop significantly. You must train your body to function in this low-energy, low-frequency state. Practice meditative breathing exercises aimed at profound slowness. The goal is to be comfortable with a respiratory cycle that might take a full minute to complete.
*   **Muscular Adaptation:** Movement will be like swimming through warm honey. Conventional locomotion is useless. You will be buoyant. All your training should focus on core strength and small, precise movements for orientation and gentle propulsion. Think more like a sea turtle than a swimmer.

**2. Psychological Conditioning: The Silence and the Sameness**

*   **Sensory Deprivation Training:** The utter silence, broken only by the thrum of your own biology, can be maddening. Spend significant time in anechoic chambers and sensory deprivation tanks. Learn to find comfort in the sound of your own blood pressure, the faint creak of your joints. Your internal world will become your entire soundscape. You must learn to be a good neighbor to yourself.
*   **Shadowless Acclimation:** The lack of shadows will severely impact your depth perception. You'll need to spend time in simulated environments with perfectly diffuse, uniform lighting. This trains your brain to rely on parallax (subtly moving your head to see how objects shift position) and object occlusion to judge distance, rather than relying on light and shadow.
*   **Pattern Overload Desensitization:** The intricate, pulsing geometric patterns on the flora can be hypnotic and, for some, trigger nausea or epileptic-like responses. Visual training software that exposes you to complex, slowly shifting fractal and geometric patterns can help your brain adapt and learn to process the visual information without being overwhelmed.
*   **Patience Cultivation:** Nothing here happens quickly. You must shed your human-scale sense of time. Practice long-form meditation. Learn to observe a single, unmoving object for hours. The central machine's movements are geological. If you are not equipped with profound patience, the environment will feel like a prison of infinite boredom rather than a cathedral of slow creation.

**3. Medical Preparation: Achieving Sterility**

*   **Full Biome Scrub:** Before entry, you will undergo a complete medical sterilization. This is non-negotiable. Your skin, your gut, your respiratory tract—every part of you will be cleansed of its native microbiome. This is a strange and unsettling feeling, but it is the fundamental price of entry. You will be, for the duration of your visit, a sterile object.
*   **Suit Integration:** Your travel suit (see below) is not just clothing; it's a second skin. You must be fitted and trained in its use for weeks in advance. It must be perfectly sealed. A single loose hair or fleck of dead skin could compromise the environment you're about to enter.

#### **III. Arrival and Acclimatization: The First Breath**

The transition is the moment of truth. You will enter through a sterile airlock that will slowly fill with the warm, clear medium.

*   **Do Not Fight It:** When the fluid enters your mouth and nose, your training must take over. Relax. Exhale all remaining air. Allow the viscous liquid to fill your lungs. It will feel heavy, invasive, and utterly wrong. The pressure will be immense. Trust the medium. It is saturated with more oxygen than you could possibly need.
*   **Equalize:** The pressure will build in your ears and sinuses. Equalize gently, as you would when diving. The feeling of the fluid filling every cavity is profoundly alien.
*   **Buoyancy and Orientation:** You will be weightless. Take a moment—perhaps your first hour—to simply float. Get used to the gentle, constant pressure on your entire body. Do not attempt to move. Just be. Let the low hum of your own body be your anchor.

#### **IV. On-Site Conduct: The Rules of a Ghost**

Your prime directive is to **observe without interacting.**

*   **Movement is a whisper:** Propel yourself with slow, deliberate finning motions of your hands and feet. Any sudden movement will create pressure waves that, while not damaging, are a form of noise in a silent place. Aim for grace and efficiency. Use the large ceramic beams to gently push off and change direction.
*   **The Sharp Edges are Law:** The prompt notes "impossibly sharp edges." This is not an exaggeration. The ceramic structure is a crystal lattice printed at a molecular scale. Brushing against an edge will not scrape you; it will cleave your suit and your skin with the perfection of an obsidian scalpel. A breach is the ultimate catastrophe—not for you, but for the environment. Your blood and tissues are the deadliest of pollutants here. **Maintain spatial awareness at all times.**
*   **Do Not Touch the Flora:** The waxy leaves and crystalline calyxes are not plants; they are organs. They are the lungs and kidneys of the chamber. Touching them is akin to poking a stranger's liver. The oils on your suit, the heat from your body—these are contaminants. Observe their slow, pulsing light from a respectful distance.
*   **Respect the Machine:** Do not approach the central armature. Its movements are slow, but they are inexorable and powered by forces you cannot comprehend. Getting caught in one of its cycles is not a dramatic death; it is a slow, silent, and inevitable compaction.
*   **Contain Yourself:** Under no circumstances should you expel any waste. Your suit is a zero-discharge system. Everything is recycled. A sneeze, should your sterile state somehow fail, would be an ecological disaster on par with an oil spill.
