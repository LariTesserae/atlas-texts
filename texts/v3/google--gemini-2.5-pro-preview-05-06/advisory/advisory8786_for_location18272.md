Excellent. You've received a translocation ticket to what local multiverse catalogers have designated **Orbis Internus**, or more colloquially, "The Cauldron." Visiting this location is not a holiday; it is a pilgrimage to the heart of industrial creation. It is profoundly beautiful and will kill you in a hundred different ways if you are unprepared. Read this guide. Memorize it. Your life will depend not on your courage or your wits, but on your preparation and your humility.

### **A Traveler's Guide to Orbis Internus: The World in the Shell**

#### **Foreword: A Matter of Perspective**

First, disabuse yourself of all terrestrial notions. There is no 'up' or 'down' in the way you understand them. There is only **Axis-ward** (towards the central light) and **Hull-ward** (towards the outer shell). There is no weather, only engineered currents. There is no geology, only ongoing construction. You are not visiting a place; you are visiting a process. You are a dust mote in a clockwork mechanism the size of a small moon. Act accordingly.

---

### **I. Pre-Departure Checklist: The Non-Negotiables**

Your survival is entirely dependent on your equipment. Do not cut corners. Do not assume your standard multiverse travel suit is sufficient.

1.  **The Environmental Suit (Deep-Dive Rig):**
    *   **Pressure Resistance:** You need a Class-9 or higher abyssal-rated hardsuit. The pressure is described as a "firm embrace," which is a poetic way of saying it will neatly and instantly turn your body into a protein slurry if your suit integrity is breached. The suit should be rated for at least 10,000 meters of water pressure, not as a risk assessment, but as a baseline for structural integrity.
    *   **Thermal Regulation:** The water is a constant, vampiric cool. A passive suit will not do. You require an active heating system with redundant power sources. Without it, the chill will seep through the suit's contact points, slowly, then rapidly, leading to hypothermia within a single cycle. Your suit should report both internal and external temperatures constantly.
    *   **Life Support:** A closed-loop rebreather system is mandatory. The water is oxygenated by lichen, but not for you. Your air supply should be rated for at least 40 hours to comfortably cover one full 28-hour cycle plus a generous safety margin. Bring spare scrubbers.

2.  **Sensory Integration & Dampening:**
    *   **Auditory:** The single greatest shock to unprepared visitors is the sound. It is not merely loud; it is a physical assault. Your helmet must be equipped with full-spectrum psycho-acoustic dampeners. You don't just want to muffle the sound; you need to cancel the bone-conducting vibrations. Configure it to filter out the constant grinding roar, allowing you to focus on specific, proximal sounds: the click of a Scuttler, the whine of a welding drone, your own breathing. Without this, you will be driven mad or, at the very least, be incapable of situational awareness.
    *   **Visual:** The light from the Axis is clean, white, and unrelenting. Your visor needs auto-tinting polarization. The sharp shadows can be disorienting and hide obstacles. You’ll also want a low-light/thermographic overlay for the eight-hour "night" cycle when the Axis dims to violet and for exploring the deeper, Hull-ward regions.

3.  **Propulsion & Navigation:**
    *   **Personal Propulsion Unit (PPU):** You are a swimmer in a world-ocean. You cannot rely on finning. A compact, variable-thrust PPU is essential. It should be whisper-quiet to avoid attracting unwanted attention. Practice maneuvering in three dimensions; the feeling of open space is immense and can induce vertigo.
    *   **Navigation Suite:** There are no landmarks, only repeating patterns. Your HUD must have a robust navigation system. It should use the Axis as a central Z-axis, the curvature of the hull as a ground plane, and the glowing grid lines on the hull as X and Y coordinates. You should be able to set waypoints, track your own path, and, most importantly, pinpoint the location of your entry/exit point. Getting lost here is a death sentence with a very long fuse.

4.  **Power:**
    *   Your suit, your PPU, your sensory dampeners—all of it drinks power. Your suit's primary power cell should be supplemented by at least two emergency backup cells. There are no outlets here. Some have theorized that one could siphon power from the hull grids via induction, but this is an untested and monumentally stupid idea. Bring everything you need.

### **II. Arrival and Acclimatization: The First Plunge**

The moment of your arrival will be a sensory blitzkrieg.

*   **The Embrace:** You will feel the pressure instantly. It is an unyielding, total-body sensation. Your suit will groan. Trust it.
*   **The Roar:** The sound will hit you like a physical wave, a deep, chest-cavitating hum. Engage your dampeners before you arrive if you can.
*   **The Light:** The brilliance of the Axis will wash out your vision. Give your visor a moment to adjust.
*   **The Scale:** Your human brain will struggle to process the distances. A pylon that looks a stone's throw away may be fifty kilometers distant. Trust your instruments, not your eyes.

Your first action: **Stop.** Do not move. Float. Let your systems stabilize. Get your bearings. Identify Axis-ward and Hull-ward. Check your suit's integrity diagnostics. Take a full five minutes to simply exist and allow the awe and terror to coexist without overwhelming you.

### **III. The Inhabitants: A Field Guide to Not Getting Processed**

Everything here has a function. Nothing is hostile, but almost everything is dangerous by virtue of its single-minded purpose. You are not part of their system. To them, you are contamination, debris, or simply not there.

*   **The Silver Schools (Indicators):** These arm-sized fish are your only truly safe companions. They are beautiful to watch. Their presence indicates stable water quality. Their sudden absence should be considered a warning sign of a localized environmental shift (e.g., a chemical flush from a construction site). Do not attempt to catch them; you need to conserve your energy.

*   **The Scuttlers (Janitors):** **THIS IS YOUR PRIMARY HAZARD.** These whale-sized biomechs are relentless. Their purpose is to clean. To them, a stray girder, a dead fish, or a non-responsive human in a metal suit are all the same category: debris to be recycled.
    *   **Identification:** A slow, graceful, multi-limbed arthropodal movement. A single, placid blue optic sensor. A low-frequency clicking sound—this is their echolocation. If you can hear the clicking without your suit's aid, one is very close.
    *   **Behavior:** They are not aggressive. They will not hunt you. But if you are immobile in their path, or stray into a designated cleaning zone, they will methodically grapple you, cut you into manageable pieces with their saws, and feed you into their internal furnaces.
    *   **Avoidance:** Maintain a respectful distance of at least one kilometer. If one is moving toward you, *you move*. Do not assume it will go around you. Its path is programmed and efficient. Its blue light may look peaceful; it is the color of absolute, unthinking purpose.

*   **The Goliath Grouters (Founders):** You are unlikely to encounter these unless you venture deep Hull-ward. They are colossal, slow, and utterly oblivious.
    *   **Identification:** A shape like a mountain-sized water bear. The sound they produce is not just a sound; it's a low-level seismic event, a moan you will feel in your teeth. You will feel a Grouter long before you see it. They trail ribbons of freshly excreted, glowing composite material.
    *   **Avoidance:** The primary danger is their sheer mass and the industrial process they embody. Do not go near the sediment pipes that feed them or the new foundations they lay. The currents around them are unpredictable, and the composite they extrude is dangerously hot before it cures.

*   **The Construction Units (The Swarm):** These are the frantic, chaotic agents of creation. Treat any active construction site as you would an active volcano inside an earthquake. Plasma cutters fire beams of energy for hundreds of meters. Magnetic hammers generate stunning shockwaves. Plates the size of neighborhoods are swung into place on tethers. Stay away. The spectacle is best viewed from a distance of *at least ten kilometers*.

### **IV. Sightseeing and Etiquette in a Machine-World**

*   **The Ballet of the Void:** Find a safe, quiet spot in the mid-water between pylons. Turn off your PPU. Just float and watch. See the schools of fish move like a single silver organism. Watch the distant, silent ballet of construction drones. It is the most sublime and lonely experience imaginable.
*   **The Night Gardens:** During the 8-hour "night" cycle, find an established, older section of a pylon or the hull floor. The bio-filtration lichen mats will come alive with a soft, silvery-green phosphorescence. It is the closest this world comes to a forest, a quiet, working garden of breathtaking scale. The silence is also more profound at night, as much of the heavy construction pauses.
*   **The Axis Gaze:** You can travel Axis-ward for a long time. The light does not get hotter, only more absolute. The water remains cool. How close you can get is a matter of debate. Getting too close may interfere with your suit's electronics. Find your own limit, but be prepared to retreat at the first sign of system malfunction.

#### **The Unspoken Rules:**

1.  **You Are Irrelevant:** This cannot be stressed enough. This world was not built for you. You are a guest in a factory. Do not interfere. Do not touch the lichen. Do not impede the Scuttlers. Do not fly through the construction swarms.
2.  **Follow the Flow:** The entire world-ocean circulates on massive, engineered currents. Learn their patterns. Use them to travel efficiently and conserve power. Fighting them is futile.
3.  **Leave Nothing Behind:** Not a tool, not a hull-scratch, nothing. You are a ghost. Maintain that status. The Scuttlers will enforce this rule if you fail.

### **Final Thought**

Orbis Internus is not a destination for thrills or relaxation. It is a place for contemplation on an impossible scale. It will recalibrate your understanding of life, engineering, and purpose. It is the sound of a world being born, not in fire and geology, but in logic and rivets. Go prepared, be humble, and you will carry the memory of its magnificent, grinding, lonely beauty with you for the rest of your travels.
