Excellent. This is a place of profound existential weight. Preparing for a visit requires more than just packing a bag; it requires preparing the self. Here is a guide for the ordinary human multiverse traveler contemplating a visit to what we will call the Heartstone Locus.

### **Traveler's Pre-Departure Briefing: The Heartstone Locus**

**Designation:** Pocket Dimension K-817, "The Heartstone Locus"
**Classification:** Spatiotemporal Eddy, Kenopsic Hazard, Non-Euclidean Isolate
**Threat Level:** Amber (Psychological/Existential). Physical threat is negligible; spiritual and temporal risks are profound.

**1. Introduction: Understanding Your Destination**

Before you even seek the entry key—the smooth black stone—you must understand what you are preparing for. The Locus is not a "place" in the conventional sense of coordinates and geography. It is a sealed thought in the mind of geology. It operates on a timescale that renders a human lifespan equivalent to the flash of a firefly. It is not hostile. It is not welcoming. It simply *is*, and by entering, you become a momentary anomaly within its perfect, patient system. Your goal is not conquest, exploration, or even survival in the typical sense. Your goal is to *witness* and to return with your consciousness intact. This is a pilgrimage, not an expedition.

**2. The Entry Protocol: Surrender, Don't Force**

*   **Locating the Key:** The heart-sized black stone cannot be found by active searching. It presents itself. You will come across it in a place where it does not belong: a sun-bleached desert, a bustling market stall, the bottom of a cereal box. When you find it, you will know. It will feel cold, impossibly so, and holding it will feel like holding a small, sleeping gravity well.
*   **The Transition:** Do not fight the pull. The transition is not a physical act, but an attentional one. Find a quiet, safe place in your origin world. Sit. Hold the stone in both hands. Let the world around you fall away. The sounds will thin, colors will desaturate. The pull is on your focus. Do not try to "go into" the stone. Let the stone become your entire reality. The feeling is akin to falling asleep, but with perfect clarity. There is no door, no flash of light. One moment you are outside, the next, you are inside. Be prepared for this seamless, disorienting shift.

**3. Environmental Acclimatization: The Five Truths of the Locus**

Upon arrival, your human instincts will scream in protest. You must override them with prepared knowledge. Meditate on these five truths *before* your journey, until they are articles of faith.

1.  **You Do Not Need to Breathe.** The gelatinous medium (henceforth "the Medium") is not breathable, but it will sustain you. It is a psychoreactive substance that interfaces directly with your biology at a cellular level. Attempting to breathe will induce panic. Relax your lungs. Let the pressure hold you. Your body's need for oxygen will simply... cease.
2.  **You Cannot Fall or Float.** The Medium negates weight and buoyancy. You are held in perfect, neutral suspension. There is no up or down, only the concave floor below and the infinite Medium around you.
3.  **You Are Not Crushed; You Are Held.** The immense pressure is uniform and absolute. It will feel like a solid, comforting embrace from all directions. It is alarming at first, but it is not harmful. It is the fundamental state of being in the Locus. Do not struggle against it; yield to it. It is what keeps you whole.
4.  **Movement is an Act of Will.** Pushing through the Medium is like moving through ultra-dense, setting gelatin. Every gesture must be deliberate and forceful, yet the Medium absorbs sound and impact. You will feel the strain in your muscles, but your movement will be silent and slow. Plan every action. Do not waste energy on frantic motion.
5.  **The Silence is a Canvas.** The ambient quiet is absolute, broken only by the deep, geological groan of the Locus itself. This silence will act as an amplifier for your inner monologue. Your thoughts will seem deafeningly loud. This is the primary psychological challenge of the visit.

**4. Recommended Attire & Equipment: The Philosophy of Less**

Your standard multiverse kit is mostly useless here. Pack for the soul, not the body.

*   **Attire:** Simple, smooth, form-fitting clothing. A single-piece garment without pockets, zippers, or loose fabric is ideal. You want nothing that can snag on the crystalline flora or create drag in the Medium. The temperature is constant and cool, but you will not feel "cold" in a conventional way; the Medium regulates your thermal state.
*   **Mandatory Equipment:**
    *   **A Personal Totem:** Bring a small, simple, smooth object from your home reality. Not the entry stone, but something else—a worn coin, a simple metal ring, a polished worry stone. When you feel your sense of self beginning to fray, hold this object. It is your only anchor to your own temporal and spatial origin. Do not lose it.
    *   **A Discipline of Mind:** Meditation, a memorized poem, a complex mathematical equation, a song you can repeat endlessly in your head. You need an internal mental routine to structure your thoughts and prevent them from dissolving into the silence.
*   **Prohibited Items:**
    *   **External Light Sources:** Do NOT bring a flashlight or glow-stick. The light here is cold, biological, and ancient. A harsh, artificial light would be an act of profound violation, and it may have unpredictable effects on the light-eating fauna. It would be like screaming in a library of sleeping gods.
    *   **Weapons or Tools:** Useless. You cannot swing a blade or fire a projectile with any effective force. Trying to hack at the crystalline vines or obsidian floor is pointless; you are softer and more temporary than anything here.
    *   **Recording Devices:** While a simple imager might function, be warned: what it records may not be what you see. It may capture temporal echoes, spatial distortions, or its own internal state reflected back at it. Reviewing such footage can be deeply disorienting.
    *   **Food, Water, Oxygen:** The Medium provides. These are unnecessary burdens and contaminants.

**5. Psychological & Perceptual Preparedness**

This is the core of your preparation.

*   **Embrace Slowness:** The Locus operates on geological time. The vines grow over centuries. The snail-creature is a blur of motion compared to the rock. You are a frantic, ephemeral spark. You must consciously slow yourself down. Move slowly. Think slowly. Observe one single glowing leaf for what feels like an hour. Try to match your perception to the rhythm of the place.
*   **The Spatial Fold:** You will see the side of your own head. You will reach for a vine and see your hand appear on the other side of the chamber. This spatial non-Euclidean geometry can induce vertigo and nausea. **Do not try to map the space.** Trust only your immediate, local perception. The Locus is the size of a room, but its internal topology is infinite. Look at the object in front of you. Act on the object in front of you. That is all.
*   **The Temporal Echoes:** You will see flickers of a past. A hand arranging pebbles. An animal taking its last breath. These are not ghosts. They are recordings held in the Medium. Do not try to interact with them. They cannot see you. They are as much a part of the landscape as the moss. Acknowledging them is fine; obsessing over them is a path to losing your own place in the present moment. Remember, soon enough, your own movements will be just another flicker for a future visitor to witness.
*   **Confronting the Predecessor:** The artifacts—the pebble spiral, the rib bone, the scratches—are the most important warning in the Locus. See them for what they are: the remnants of a consciousness that failed to adapt. The pebbles are a desperate attempt to impose order. The bone is a monument to a lost world. The scratches are the record of a mind counting down seconds in a place that knows only millennia. Learn from this unknown visitor. Do not try to count. Do not try to build. Do not try to wait for something. Simply *be*.

**6. Exit Protocol: The Act of Remembering Outward**

The Locus does not have an exit door. Just as you entered by letting the outside world fade, you must leave by actively remembering it. The place is seductive in its peace and eternal stillness. It will not want to let you go, not out of malice, but out of simple inertia.

1.  Find a stable position. Hold your personal totem.
2.  Close your eyes (if you can; if not, simply de-focus).
3.  Ignore the Locus. Forcefully and vividly, reconstruct your home reality in your mind. Remember the feeling of wind on your skin. The taste of hot coffee. The chaotic sound of traffic. The specific face of someone you know.
4.  Build that world in your mind until it feels more real than the crushing silence around you. Cling to the *noise*, the *imperfection*, the *speed* of your world.
5.  You must pull your attention *out* of the stone. It will feel like pulling your foot out of thick mud. With a final, wrenching effort of will, you will find yourself back where you started, holding the cold black stone, the sounds of your world rushing back in a deafening flood.

**7. Post-Visit Decompression**

Do not expect to return unchanged. You will carry an echo of the Locus's silence with you. For days, the world will seem unbearably loud, fast, and flimsy. You may feel a phantom pressure on your skin. You will have a new, profound appreciation for erosion, patience, and the deep time that underpins all realities.

You went to the Locus and became a flicker in its memory. It is now a permanent, silent chamber in yours.
