### **Traveler's Advisory: Geode-Class World 734 (Callsign: "Anchorage")**

**Foreword: Read This First**

Congratulations on your approved transit. Anchorage is a destination unlike any other in the monitored multiverse, and your selection indicates you have a high tolerance for environmental and metaphysical stress. That tolerance will be tested.

This is not a vacation. This is an expedition into a location where the fundamental laws of reality are being written, revised, and occasionally, erased. The purpose of this guide is not to enhance your enjoyment, but to ensure your return. Survival in Anchorage is an active, moment-to-moment process. Do not get complacent. Do not get comfortable. Do not assume anything is permanent.

---

### **Phase 1: Pre-Departure Preparation**

Your preparation begins weeks before you even approach the transit gate.

#### **I. Physical and Psychological Conditioning**

*   **Auditory Acclimatization:** The Hum is your primary environmental challenge. It is not merely a sound; it is a pressure on the skull, a vibration in the bones. Begin acclimatizing now. For at least four hours a day, expose yourself to a custom-made audio track of low-frequency industrial noise, brown noise, and infrasound (at safe volumes). Learn to sleep with it. Learn to think through it. You are not trying to ignore it; you are trying to integrate it, to make it the new silence. Arriving unprepared will subject you to debilitating migraines, vertigo, and nausea within the first hour.
*   **Vestibular Training:** The verticality and precariousness of Anchorage requires an ironclad sense of balance. Spend time on climbing walls, practicing yoga or other balance-focused disciplines, and using virtual reality simulators that expose you to extreme heights. You must be able to cross a swaying, open-mesh walkway a mile above a chasm without freezing up.
*   **Mental Fortitude:** You will experience phenomena that contradict your understanding of cause and effect. This is called "Drift." It can be profoundly disorienting. Practice mindfulness and meditative techniques focused on grounding yourself in your own physical sensations. You must become a rock of self-awareness in a sea of uncertainty. Keep a physical journal and write in it daily; the act of marking pen to paper is a small but powerful personal anchor.

#### **II. Essential Gear and Packing List**

Assume standard multiverse travel gear (base layers, rations, medkit) is a given. The following are Anchorage-specific necessities.

*   **Outerwear:** Your primary shell jacket and trousers must be **hydrophobic and oleophobic**. The constant "damp" is a greasy, mineral-rich condensate that will soak through standard waterproof gear and ruin it. Look for materials designed for industrial chemical work. Your clothing will be slick and heavy within minutes of arrival; ensure it allows for a full range of motion.
*   **Footwear:** This is paramount. You need boots with two key features:
    1.  **Exceptional Grip:** Soles should be of a soft, high-friction compound, capable of holding firm on wet, slick metal and sharp, crystalline Protolith. Magnetic soles are a popular but debated option; they are excellent on most city surfaces but useless on the rock and can be lethally misguided during a high-energy Drift event.
    2.  **Reinforced Toes and Shanks:** Forged steel or carbide is recommended. You will be kicking things into place, bracing yourself against shifting surfaces, and potentially having your foot wedged in a suddenly-closing seam of rock. A standard hiking boot will be shredded. Bring a small carbide file to re-sharpen the edges of your boot soles, which will be abraded by the Protolith.
*   **Gloves:** You will never take them off outside your designated habitat. They should be reinforced, thermally insulated, and provide excellent dexterity. The surfaces of Anchorage are either freezing cold metal, razor-sharp rock, or covered in the aforementioned greasy film.
*   **Sensory Protection & Augmentation:**
    *   **Hearing:** Bring a multi-stage system. At a minimum: high-fidelity musician's earplugs (to cut decibels without total occlusion) and industrial-grade noise-canceling headphones (for rest periods). Locals often learn to "wear the Hum," but you will not have this luxury.
    *   **Vision:** Your primary eyewear should be a set of sealed, ballistic-grade goggles with photochromic lenses. They will protect you from the damp, from sudden jets of steam, and from shards of rock. The lenses must adapt to the low-light conditions, which fluctuate from the dim purple glow to the sudden, blinding flash of a discharging reality-anchor.
*   **Navigation and Safety:**
    *   **Compass/GPS:** Useless. Discard them. They will only lead you astray as spatial laws bend.
    *   **Safety Harness and Tethers:** A high-quality climbing harness is not optional. You will be tethered to anchor points on many of the outer walkways. Have at least two tethers with locking carabiners. Practice using them until it is second nature. Falling is not a possibility; it is a statistical probability you are actively working against.
    *   **Personal Locator Beacon (Local Tech):** Upon arrival, your first acquisition must be a "Sub-Etheric Stability Pinger." This device does not track your location in space, but your anchor-point in consensus reality. If you are caught in a severe Drift, it is the only way a rescue team can find you. It is your soul's lighthouse.
*   **Sustenance:** Bring high-calorie nutrient paste and vacuum-sealed rations. While life is abundant, *do not eat the local flora or fauna.* The Gasket-Moss and Condenser Ferns are hyper-accumulators of heavy metals and exotic minerals from the industrial runoff. The Geode Beetles are living batteries full of things your biology cannot process. Water is everywhere, but it MUST be filtered through a specialized catalyst-filter capable of removing heavy metals and complex hydrocarbons. A standard charcoal filter will not suffice.

### **Phase 2: Arrival and Acclimatization**

**I. First Impressions & Immediate Actions**

The moment you arrive, all your preparation will be put to the test.

1.  **The Hum:** It will hit you first. A physical force. Do not fight it. Let your jaw hang slack, breathe deeply from your abdomen. Equalize the pressure. Let it vibrate through you. It is the heartbeat of this world; trying to block it is like trying to block your own blood flow.
2.  **The Air & The Light:** The cold will seep in, and the greasy film will begin to coat you. Your goggles will be essential. The light from below is disorienting. Resist the urge to look down into the chasms for too long. Focus on your immediate surroundings.
3.  **Find Your Anchor:** Your first priority is to get from the arrival platform to the designated "Stable Zone" of the district you'll be staying in. Do not explore. Do not deviate from the marked path. Follow the illuminated arrows or, better yet, your designated local contact. Walk slowly, deliberately. Every footstep is a commitment. Always maintain three points of contact when on ladders or steep inclines.

**II. Navigating the Environment**

*   **The Ground Beneath Your Feet:** Trust no surface completely. Protolith is a living thing. A flat ledge can develop a crystalline spine overnight. Before stepping onto a new rock surface, look for signs of stress: fresh cracks, shimmering internal light, a subtle change in the pitch of the Hum underfoot. On city structures, check the bolts. Locals are constantly tightening them. A loose, vibrating bolt is a sign the platform is under new strain.
*   **Dealing with Drift:** Drift is the locals' term for the low-level, constant reality failure. It is usually subtle. A corridor that feels longer on the way back. A door that opens into a different room than it did an hour ago. A reflection showing you with your shirt on backwards.
    *   **If you witness a mild Drift event:** Stop. Take a breath. Anchor yourself to a physical sensation (the weight of your pack, the cold of a handrail). Wait for it to pass. Do not panic. Panicking can "deepen" the Drift around you.
    *   **If you witness a major Drift event (a wall becoming transparent, a view into a nebula):** Do not move towards it. Do not attempt to interact with it. Brace yourself, close your eyes if you have to, and wait. These are leaks from the raw chaos the city is built to contain. The stabilization engines will eventually correct it.
    *   **"Drift Charts":** The locals navigate by charts that map "paths of high stability." These are not geographical maps but more like tidal charts of probability. Always consult the latest chart before heading to a new district.

### **Phase 3: Social & Cultural Integration**

**I. The Locals**

The people of Anchorage are defined by their environment. They are pragmatic, patient, and possess a gallows humor you may not immediately appreciate.

*   **Communication:** They are slow to speak and direct. They value action over words. An offer to help tighten a loose panel or clear a condenser drain will earn you more respect than a lengthy conversation. Do not fill silence with small talk; silence is a rare commodity here, a brief respite from the Hum.
*   **Etiquette:**
    *   Never complain about the noise, the damp, or the danger. They have chosen to live here. You are a guest.
    *   Respect their work. The clang of hammers and hiss of steam mean they are actively keeping you alive. Show gratitude.
    *   Watch their movements. Notice how they test a surface before putting their full weight on it. Notice the deliberate, unhurried pace. Mimic it. Rushing is seen as suicidal and foolish.
    *   Their currency is often a mix of standard credits, favors, spare parts, and stable-state data. Be prepared to trade skills or goods.

**II. The Living World**

*   **Flora:** The Gasket-Moss and Condenser Ferns are integral to the city's ad-hoc water recycling system. They are beautiful but toxic. Admire them from a distance.
*   **Fauna:**
    *   **Pylon Shrikes:** Ignore them unless you are near a high-voltage pylon, in which case, move away briskly. Their "shrieks" are often warnings of an impending arc discharge.
    *   **Geode Beetles:** These are fascinating and important. They are slow-moving and predictable. Do not step on them. Their crystalline carapaces are sometimes harvested for use in delicate scientific instruments. A glittering tide of them moving across your path is not a threat, but a process. Wait for them to pass.
    *   **Strider-Flocks:** You will likely only see these silent, graceful creatures as silhouettes on a distant wall. They are incredibly shy and pose no threat. Their ability to move effortlessly across vertical Protolith is something to be marveled at. It is a reminder that in this place, life has found a way not just to survive, but to be elegant.

### **Final Warning: The Existential Toll**

Anchorage is a place that erodes more than just boot soles. The constant Hum, the perpetual danger, and the metaphysical instability of the Drift will wear on your psyche. This is the phenomenon known as "Reality Sickness." Symptoms include memory fragmentation, emotional detachment, and a loosening sense of self.

This is why your visit is temporary. Your mission has a deadline for a reason. Do your work, document your findings, and when your time is up, leave. Do not be tempted by the raw, terrible beauty of the Protolith's birth. Do not become fascinated by the Drift. Anchorage is a workshop for reality, and you are not one of the engineers. You are only a visitor, privileged to witness the slow, grinding, and glorious act of a world coming into being.

Stay safe. Stay anchored. Return.
