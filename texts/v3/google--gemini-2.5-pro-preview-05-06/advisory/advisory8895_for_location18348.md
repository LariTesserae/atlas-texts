### **Traveler's Advisory: The Sheath of Basalt ("The Boiler")**

**Foreword: A Matter of Indifference**

Before we discuss gear and procedure, you must adjust your mindset. The Boiler is not a ruin to be explored for treasure, nor is it a monster to be slain. It is a machine. A vast, impossibly old, and utterly mindless machine that has been performing its one and only function since its builders vanished. It is not hostile. It does not know you are there. It will not react to you. If you are boiled alive by proximity to a caloric conduit, it is no more malicious than a stovetop burning your hand. The Sheath operates on a set of physical laws as rigid and unforgiving as gravity. Your survival depends on understanding and respecting those laws absolutely. You are a ghost in this machine; the moment you try to interact with it, you risk becoming a permanent, silent cog.

---

### **Phase 1: Pre-Departure Outfitting & Conditioning**

This is the most critical phase. If you arrive unprepared, you are already dead. The environment is so specialized that standard-issue multiverse gear is insufficient and, in some cases, dangerous.

#### **I. Atmospheric Adaptation Gear (The "Breathable" System)**

The air in The Boiler is the primary threat. It's a triple-threat cocktail of extreme heat, 100% humidity, and exotic particulates.

*   **Clothing: The Second Skin.** Do NOT wear conventional clothing. Cotton will become a sodden, heavy blanket, chafing your skin raw in minutes and potentially leading to systemic heat rash and fungal infections. Synthetics will melt if you get too close to a heat source. You need a specialized two-part system:
    *   **Inner Layer: Capillary Cooling Suit.** This is a full-body, skin-tight garment made of a micro-fibrous weave. Its purpose is not insulation but moisture management. It will actively wick the torrential sweat from your skin and spread it over a large surface area. It won't evaporate (nothing does here), but it will prevent pooling and keep your skin temperature *fractionally* lower. Think of it as managing your body's personal swamp.
    *   **Outer Layer: Non-Absorbent Shell.** A thin, tough, and highly porous jumpsuit, likely made of a ceramic-polymer composite. It should be entirely hydrophobic. The constant drizzle will bead and run off it. It must be tough enough to resist tearing on sharp basalt or rusted metal. It offers zero insulation. Its only jobs are to shed water and provide abrasion resistance. It should have reinforced knees, elbows, and seat.

*   **Respirator: The Dehumidifier.** You cannot breathe the air directly for more than a few minutes. The heat will sear your lungs, and the humidity will effectively drown you from the inside. You require a closed-circuit rebreather, but with a critical modification: a **thermic exchange and condensation unit**. As you exhale, the unit captures your breath, scrubs the CO2, and diverts it through a cooling array that forces the extreme humidity of the ambient air to condense into water. This collected water is then drained via a tube. The now-dehumidified, oxygen-rich air is cooled to a tolerable temperature before being fed back to your mask. It's heavy and loud in your ears, but it's the only way. Your life support is not about providing oxygen, but about *processing the atmosphere into something survivable.*

*   **Footwear: Gecko-Grip Boots.** Your boots need soles made of a soft, high-friction compound designed for wet, uneven surfaces. The Boiler is a world of slick, grated metal and damp, sharp rock. The tread should be deep and channelized to displace water. They must be completely waterproof but breathable enough to work with your capillary suit. High ankles are mandatory for support.

*   **Gloves.** Textured, non-slip gloves are not optional. You will be using your hands constantly for balance. Every surface is either slick with mineral water, sharp as a razor, or dangerously hot. Your gloves should be thin enough for dexterity but durable. Many travelers embed small, low-power thermal sensors in the fingertips to give a haptic warning buzz when approaching a surface hot enough to cause damage.

#### **II. Navigation & Utility**

*   **Light: Dual-Source Illumination.** The ambient light from the Fuse-Lichen and Caloric Cores is deceptive. It creates deep, disorienting shadows and flattens your depth perception.
    *   **Headlamp:** A wide-beam LED headlamp is your primary tool for immediate navigation, illuminating your path foot by foot.
    *   **High-Lumen Flood Lamp:** A handheld or shoulder-mounted flood lamp is for orientation. Periodically, you must stop, brace yourself, and light up the chasm to understand your position relative to the macro-structure. Seeing the vast, terrifying scale is better than being lost in the oppressive dark.

*   **Nav-Kit:** GPS is useless. Compasses will spin wildly due to the immense electrical fields.
    *   **Laser Rangefinder & Altimeter:** Your primary coordinates are vertical. Knowing your altitude is key. The rangefinder helps you judge distances across the chasm or to a specific platform.
    *   **Spool of High-Vis Line:** The oldest trick is the best. If you plan to explore a section, anchor a brightly colored, reflective line. It’s your only guaranteed way back.
    *   **Thermal Imager:** This is not a luxury; it is a critical survival tool. The most immediate danger is the invisible radiant heat from the Caloric Cores and their primary conduits. A thermal imager will let you "see" the heat walls and identify which pipes are safe to approach and which will cook you from ten feet away.

*   **Harness & Rigging:** You are a climber in this world, not a pedestrian. A lightweight climbing harness, several locking carabiners, and at least 50 meters of static rope are essential. You secure yourself before you do anything else: before you rest, before you consult your gear, before you drink.

#### **III. Sustenance**

*   **Water:** Your rebreather will collect a surprising amount of water, but it will be laden with minerals from the chasm walls. You must run it through a high-grade purification filter that specifically targets heavy metals. Carry a primary bladder of at least 4 liters of your own purified water, and only use locally sourced water to replenish it after rigorous filtration. Dehydration is rapid and catastrophic here.
*   **Electrolytes:** Your sweat will pour out of you in rivers. You must be constantly replacing salts and minerals. Electrolyte tablets, powders, or concentrates are more important than food for short-term trips.
*   **Food:** No-cook, high-calorie, low-volume nutrient paste or bars. The humidity will spoil anything else, and the thought of firing up a stove in this heat is absurd.

### **Phase 2: Arrival & Acclimation**

Your entry point will likely be at the "rim" of the chasm, where the normal world's atmosphere meets The Boiler's. The transition is brutal.

1.  **Gearing Up:** Do not enter without your full gear active. Seal your suit, activate your rebreather, and check your harness.
2.  **The Plunge:** The descent into the chasm is a physical blow. You will move from cool, dry air into a wall of heat and pressure. The temperature will jump 40-50 degrees in the space of a few feet. Your gear will immediately be coated in condensation. This is the moment of commitment.
3.  **The Hum:** The first thing you'll notice after the heat is the hum. It's not a sound; it is a vibration in your skeleton. It comes from everywhere at once. It will shake your teeth and resonate in your chest cavity. You must learn to ignore it. Meditate, focus on your breathing, do whatever it takes, but do not let The Hum unnerve you. It is the city's heartbeat.

### **Phase 3: On-Site Conduct & Hazards**

**Rules of Movement:**

*   **Three Points of Contact:** At all times, have three of your four limbs on a stable surface.
*   **Test Everything:** Tap walkways with your foot before putting your full weight on them. Pull on rungs and handholds before you trust them. Rust is everywhere.
*   **Look Up:** The constant drizzle is not the only thing that falls. Rust flakes, loose bolts, or even whole sections of catwalk can break free. Your shell is for abrasion, not impact. Be aware of what is above you.
*   **Respect the Pipes:** The large, silver-sheathed conduits are the city’s arteries. Assume they are all hot enough to kill you. Use your thermal imager. The radiant heat alone can cause severe burns without physical contact. Give them a wide berth.

**Environmental Hazards:**

*   **The Heat:** It is omnipresent and relentless. Don't push yourself. Take frequent, short rests in cooler areas (near the main chasm walls, away from machinery). Monitor your body's core temperature if your suit has that function.
*   **The Water:** The constant lukewarm drizzle causes two problems: it makes everything dangerously slick, and it leaches minerals that can corrode your gear over time. Wipe down critical equipment regularly.
*   **The Flora: Observe, Do Not Touch.**
    *   **Coppery Moss:** This stuff lives on the rock and is harmless, but it's part of the ecosystem. Leave it alone.
    *   **Fuse-Lichen:** This glowing lichen is the city's living light. It's also a key part of the thermic cycle. Damaging it is not just vandalism; it's interfering with the machine. It's considered extremely poor form.

**Psychological Hazards:**

*   **Sensory Monotony:** The constant hum, the unceasing drip of water, the uniform heat, the dim light—this environment can lull you into a stupor. Stay mentally active. Set small, achievable goals (e.g., "reach the next main junction," "check my water levels").
*   **Agoraphobia & Claustrophobia:** You will experience both, sometimes simultaneously. The sheer vertical scale of the chasm is terrifying (agoraphobia), while the dense, cramped, and oppressive architecture will make you feel boxed in (claustrophobia). Focus on your immediate surroundings, your "bubble" of safety.
*   **The Silence of Life:** The most unsettling aspect for many is the total lack of animal life. No birds, no insects, no rats. The quiet is absolute. This profound biological deadness can weigh heavily on the psyche. Your rebreather's hiss and the hum in your bones will be your only companions.

**A Final Word**

The Sheath of Basalt is a monument to a purpose we no longer understand, built by hands long turned to dust. It is beautiful in its terrifying, mechanical perfection. Your goal is to be a witness, not a participant. Move slowly, touch little, and leave nothing behind. This place has existed in a perfect, humming equilibrium for eons. You are a fleeting, fragile anomaly. Act like it. Listen to the hum, feel the heat, and know your place... which is to not have one. Travel safe.
