# The Grey Hush

Follow the clouds.

They are the only inhabitants that ever leave, and they always leave in the same direction. Watch one go: exhaled at rooftop height, grazed along the canopy line, milked of its brief warm shower, and then released over the eastern rim of the valley into the wide air — and the wide air takes it east, because the wind above the valley runs east, has always run east, as if the world had assigned the valley a recipient.

The crossing is long. Three hundred miles of brown country lies between — a steppe of stiff grass and scattered stones that has never heard the roar, has no opinion about it, grows an inch a year and considers that sufficient. The clouds cross it in loose herds, cooling as they go. They left the valley as breath — warm, vegetable, faintly sweet, dense with dissolved pollen and the fizz of the cisterns — and mile by mile the warmth is taken from them, the green taken from them, until what drifts east is grey, and mineral, and cold, and carries the valley the way a letter carries a voice.

Then the land stands up.

An escarpment rises out of the steppe like a decision: a wall of pale stone, high and cold, running north to south beyond seeing in either direction. Here the land finished deciding what it was a very long time ago — it decided *stone* — and it has kept the decision with a fidelity the valley will never know and could not survive. The clouds cannot climb it. They stack against its western face, sag, and die there, and their dying is rain: thin, cold, patient rain, the valley's breath given back to the world at last, a thousand miles of green shouting reduced to water ticking on rock.

Call what the rain falls on a city, because it has the shape of one. But hold the word carefully this time, because everything about the shape is reversed. The valley's city is added. This one is taken away.

## The Quiet

That is the first thing, before the shapes, before the grey: the silence. Not an absence — a pressure. The Hush is quiet the way the Roar is loud: totally, structurally, as a property of the place rather than a condition of the moment. Sound does not fill this country. Sound *visits* it, singly, and is received, and ends.

And sound here behaves. That must be said, because of where the rain comes from. In the valley, sequence has come loose; echoes arrive before their events; the acoustics do not entirely obey. In the Hush, obedience is absolute — is, if anything, excessive. Echoes arrive after their events, always after, and often *long* after: the crack of a stone that split at dawn will reach you at dusk, having taken the whole day to walk the canyons, stopping, it seems, at every corner. Nothing here precedes itself. Everything here comes late, and arrives worn, and is forgiven.

Which is the Hush's work, and the heart of its kinship with the valley, though neither place has ever been told. Because the orphans come here. The pre-echoes — the stranded anticipations that scouted too far ahead of events that then declined to happen, the small sharp sounds kept like fish in the leaf-cupped cisterns — some of them dissolve. They go into the water, and the water goes into the breath, and the breath goes east with the herds, and rains out against the stone. The stone drinks them. And the stone, having all the time in the world, *arranges their events.* A cliff face will hold a flaw for nine hundred years — nursing it, aligning it, wetting it and freezing it and wetting it again — and then, on some anonymous grey morning, split with a single gunshot report that is not new. It is the exact crack that fizzed, faint and futureless, in a green-lit cistern a continent and an age away. The event has finally been supplied. The debt is paid. The ledger, somewhere, closes.

The Hush is where the valley's loose causality is taken in and settled. Every sound the Roar issues without backing is honored here eventually, at stone's convenience, one report a century if that is what solvency requires. The valley does not know it has a guarantor. The escarpment does not know it is one. The rain knows, and says nothing, being rain.

One voice is constant: the wind. A single wind, always from the west — from the valley's quarter — steady, dry once the clouds have died, and old. It may genuinely be one wind; it has the habits of an individual, the routes, the returns, the tempers. It is the mason here, and also the flautist: passing through the carved openings of the city it raises a low continuous tone in the empty streets, a note below melody, the Hush's entire civic noise.

And under the wind, at certain dusks, when the air lies right across three hundred miles of steppe: the Roar itself. Arrived. Worn by the crossing to a green murmur, the full deafening volume of the valley delivered as almost nothing — a shell held to the world's ear. The Hush hears its neighbor every evening the weather permits. It is the only thing the Hush could be said to listen to.

## The Unbuilt City

No hand cut a stone here. No tool has ever touched this place — the two cities agree on that, it is nearly the only thing they materially share — and yet: boulevards. Arcades. Plazas, and things that read unmistakably as towers, as bridges, as amphitheaters, hollowed and freed from the escarpment's western face in courses and quarters that run back into the stone for miles. The wind did it, and the valley's rain did it, grain by grain, over spans of time that make the word *over* seem hasty. The valley grows the grammar of architecture. The Hush is eroded into it. Subtraction arrives at the same language addition does, which suggests — though neither city can be asked — that the language belongs to neither of them. The valley never learned it from anyone. Neither did the stone. Both are being spoken through. The grammar is the world's mother tongue, and these are the two mouths it currently uses: one at the speed of a slow walk, one at the speed of mountains.

Everything here is old, and shows it, and this is the valley precisely inverted. Where the Roar's edges are crisp and almost cruel — leaf-blades unweathered, thorns translucent as new fingernails — the Hush is rounded everywhere, thresholds worn into shallow smiles, corners relaxed, stairs dished by the passage of no feet, the entire city soft with time the way a coin is soft with handling. Nothing here is being built. Everything is being *finished*, infinitely slowly. Watch a wall for a lifetime and you will not see it change; come back in ten thousand years and it will have become more exactly itself. The wind removes stone and what remains is always architecture — as if architecture were what stone is underneath, and erosion merely honest.

The light is the valley's opposite too: direct, hard, unmediated, a high pale sun throwing long clean shadows down the boulevards — and the shadows *behave*, pointing away from the sun with total obedience, converging on nothing, keeping sequence, keeping faith. In the Hush even the light is solvent.

But here is the thing that matters, the correspondence that makes the two cities one estate. The shapes match. Measure the windows the wind has opened in these facades — their proportions, their orientations — and they are the windows the foliage refuses to fill, the same held voids, tenant for tenant. The doorways stand at the same height of no head. The stairways pitch for the same no climber's stride; the balconies overlook the insides of the same walls. The Implied keep two addresses. Every one of the valley's carefully leased absences has a second residence here, cut in stone, maintained by wind, identical to the inch — and the deepest of them, the emptiness the size of a cathedral around which the whole Roar builds like a sentence around its unsaid word, has its counterpart in the deepest quarter of the Hush: a vast hall hollowed in silence, kept in silence, the same room's worth of nothing, held at both ends of the world at once. Whoever the grammar is for, the world is keeping their rooms ready in two materials and two tempos, in case they prefer green, in case they prefer grey, in case they are slow in coming, in case they are fast.

And the rain, its work done, goes home. It sinks through the stone, finds the deep aquifer, and begins to walk west — centuries underground, beneath the incurious steppe, until it rises at last under the valley's warm breathing floor and is drawn up into the sap. That hydraulic thunder, lower than hearing, the ocean pumped uphill through a forest's worth of throats: that ocean fell *here*, as the quiet death of exiled clouds, a thousand years before it roars. The two places are one respiration. The valley exhales; the Hush receives; the water takes the long way home to be shouted through again. Some measurable part of the Green Roar is the Grey Hush's silence, returned with interest.

## The Lichen

There is life in the Hush. One life, in one form, and it came in the rain.

Lichen: grey-green, flat, undramatic, the only green in the whole stone country — and it is the valley's green, faded exactly as much as three hundred miles and a change of state will fade a color. It grows at erosion speed, the one tempo the escarpment honors, and so it is the one life the Hush has ever permitted to stay. It does not build. It *stains*. It records.

And it carries the citizens. Remember what a citizen is, in the valley: not an organism but a recurrence, a habit, a tune the city keeps humming through whatever instruments are available — and a habit dissolved fine enough travels in water. On a north-facing canyon wall, hours' walk into the carved quarters, there is a bloom of lichen with a left-hand twist to its growth and a flare at what would be twelve feet, holding open at its crown a bare patch of the same proportions as a certain window. It is the braided column of the northwest corner — the valley's oldest burgher — transposed into pigment and stone, completing one recurrence every three thousand years where its cousin manages forty in a season. It has fallen, in its way, perhaps twice. It is unmistakably the same column. The families of the Roar have country relations in the Hush: the same tunes, played on rock, slowed past the reach of any ear, faithful past the reach of any doubt.

The memory-districts come through too. A quarter of the valley collapses and is quoted in green within weeks, down to the broken stem on the corner; and then, over millennia, the quotation is made again here, in lichen, across an acre of cliff — the district's plan legible from the rim as a variation in stain, the broken stem included, the indoor weather suggested by a band of mineral bloom at what would have been rooftop height. The root-mat is the valley's short memory. The escarpment is its long one. Between them, breathing at each other across the steppe, the two places forget nothing at all.

## The Room That Fits

Which leaves the deepest chamber but one, and what is in it.

At the end of a boulevard the wind has been finishing since before the valley first roared, there is a small room. In the room there is a hollow, worn into the living stone at sitting height, and the hollow is polished — polished as if by ten thousand years of daily occupation, though nothing has ever sat in it. No hand made it. The wind made it, grain by grain, around a patience.

Sit in it. Anyone — sit. It fits you. Your exact spine, the set of your shoulders, the weight of your particular tiredness: received, all of it, as if the stone had been expecting you by name. It has fit everyone who has ever tried it, each of them exactly, which is impossible, and which is also simply what stone can do when it is given enough time. The valley, remember, would need a week to learn your sitting, and would learn only yours, and would keep regrowing it forever after in leaves. The Hush learned your sitting ten thousand years before you were born, along with everyone else's, by refusing to commit — by being worn so patiently toward the shape of *a* sitter that it arrived at the shape of *any* sitter. Speed commits; that is the valley's glory and its limit. Stone waits, and waiting is the Hush's entire genius, and the hollow is its masterpiece: hospitality with no guest yet, perfected in advance, kept dusted by the wind.

This is the Hush's side of the conversation the two cities have been having across the steppe since before either could be said to begin. The theaters of the carved city all face west — toward the valley — their stages empty, their acoustics tuned by ages of subtraction to gather sound out of the evening air. And at dusk, when the wind drops into the doorways and the whole city goes flute, the gathering succeeds: the green murmur arrives, three hundred miles worn, the Roar at its only volume, still asking its one question — *no one yet, no one yet, no one yet* — and the Hush answers as it has always answered, low and continuous, in the flute-tone of its kept and ready voids, in stone's tense, which has no *yet* in it and no hurry either:

*Whenever. Whenever. Whenever.*
