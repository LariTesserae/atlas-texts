# The Keeping

You reach it by following the water, which is harder than it sounds, because the water of the plain goes nowhere you can see. There are no streams off that flat black ground, no rivers gathering at its edges. Rain falls, footprints fill, pools stand — and the plain stays exactly as wet as it is, no wetter, forever. The water is leaving. It is simply not leaving *across*.

Walk the plain long enough — days, in the wrong season — and you will find the one place where the flatness confesses. A dimple, no wider than a hand, where the black fines turn in a slow spiral and go down. It makes almost no sound. It has been making almost no sound for so long that the ground around it has hardened into a smooth dark throat, and if you are small enough in your intentions and careless enough with your life, the throat can be descended.

You go down through the fines first, through soil so old it has forgotten being anything else. Then the fines end, and the stone begins, and this is where you should slow down and pay attention, because the stone is banded — thin pale layers, thousands upon thousands, laid flat as sleep — and you are climbing down through the hills.

The hills that no longer exist outside. They were never destroyed. They were dissolved, a rain at a time, carried down through the plain they became, and laid here again in the dark: not the hills as they stood but the hills as they left, in the order of their going. The plain above is not the bottom of the world. It is the lid.

At the base of the throat, the passage opens, and you are in the place itself.

---

**What it is.** It is enormous. That should be said first, because everything about its contents will suggest smallness — thin films, single drops, millimeters. The Settling, that green vault on the plain above, would fit inside the Keeping's first chamber, and the first chamber is the least of them. Room opens off room, gallery off gallery, each one an old water-course that the water made by leaving and then furnished by arriving: for the water that comes down here comes down *full*. It has been through the plain. Some of it has been through the plant — has been the indoor rain, the weep-stripes, the pools; has passed through haulers and grazers and the floor's slow boil — and it arrives saturated with everything it was given, and here, in the still dark, it can no longer hold what it carries.

So it sheds. Everywhere, on every surface, the water is putting the world down. The walls are flowstone, banded and pale, thickening at a pace no patience can watch. The floors are sheeted in nacre. From the ceilings hang slow blunt forms like candles burning in reverse, growing downward drop by drop. Nothing here is demolished, ever. Nothing is turned over. The Keeping does not maintain itself the way the Settling does, by frantic replacement; it maintains itself the way a fact does, by having happened. Every surface is the top of everything under it.

Nothing here is younger than its position. That is the rule, and it is the exact inverse of the rule upstairs, and the two rules are — you will come to see — the same arrangement, read from opposite ends.

---

**Light.** There is light, and the light is the strangest mercy of the place. The matter arriving from above includes, always, a fraction that was recently alive — tissue of the plant, flesh of its citizens, dissolved and sent down — and the plant's substance keeps a habit of shining for a while after it stops being anything. When it comes out of the water and settles into the fresh film, it glows: faint, blue-grey, the luminance of a moonlit page. Then, over weeks, it goes dark, and the layer above it takes up the shining.

So every chamber is lit by its own most recent history. The newest millimeter glimmers on every surface like frost; the older bands beneath it show dimly through, dimmer with depth; and the deep past is black. You can see time here, directly, as a gradient: the present is the bright skin of the room, and everything under the brightness is everything that has ever happened, going down into the dark in order.

The far chambers, which receive less water, are darker. The farthest are dark entirely — finished rooms, rooms the current abandoned, holding their whole archive with no light left to read it by. You will not go far into those. Nothing asks you not to. You will not go.

---

**Water.** It moves through the Keeping the way a sentence moves through a careful listener: slowly, and giving up everything on the way. It comes in full and it leaves empty, and both halves of that deserve attention.

The arriving water falls as single drops, widely spaced — a drop here, a long silence, a drop across the gallery — and each drop, landing, leaves a ring of stone thinner than breath. This is the rain of the Keeping, and it is the same rain as the indoor rain of the Settling, further along in its work: up there it fell warm and constant and sweet, performing; down here it falls cold and single and final, filing. Same water. Different office.

The leaving water is the part that closes the circle, and it is easy to miss, because empty water is light-footed. Having shed its whole cargo, having put down the last of the hills and the last of yesterday's citizens, the water rises — wicking up through hairline seams, up through the black fines, drawn by the dryness of roots that are not exactly roots — and returns to the plain, and to the plant, blank. The water that climbs the Settling's walls in those backward-whispering rivulets, the water that condenses off the vaulted ceiling and falls as warm rain on the boiling floor: it has been here. It has been to the Keeping and back, and it rains the harder for having nothing left to carry.

This is the trade, though neither party would recognize the word. The Settling exports its past, dissolved; the Keeping keeps the past and returns the water. Each makes the other possible. Forgetting requires a drain, and drains require somewhere for everything to go, and the Settling can afford its magnificent hourly amnesia only because a place exists underneath it that has never declined a single delivery.

---

**The layers.** Read a wall — any wall; kneel at the fresh glowing film and follow the bands down through the dimness — and you can hold the whole history of the country above in the width of your two hands.

Deepest, darkest: the hills, thick coarse bands, the long ages when the land above still had ambitions of slope. Then a great quiet stretch, fine and even — the plain's first eras, flat rain on flat ground, nothing happening for longer than anything has happened since. And then, near the top, the bands change. They thin. They complicate. They take on chemistries the water never used to carry: trace sweetnesses, the residues of milk, the particular salts of pale six-limbed flesh. This is the Settling era, and it is still being written, one glowing film at a time.

The Settling has no ruins. It was said of that place, and it is true, and this is why: its ruins are filed here. Every retired buttress the grazers ate, every superseded rib, every hauler the floor took back before the rain had filled its outline — dissolved, carried down, laid in. The two-note calls could not make the journey; sound does not dissolve. But the bodies that made them did, and their arrivals are here in order, a fauna of thin bright lines. Somewhere in the topmost bands, distributed fine, are all the citizens the Settling has ever declined to mourn. It never had to mourn them. It had a filing system.

And — this should be said plainly, because you will work it out anyway and it is better met head-on — somewhere in the newest millimeter is *you*. The water that closed your footprints on the plain above carried something of you away with it: skin-dust, salt, the small taxes any warm thing pays for standing somewhere wet. That water has already been here. When you first step into the Keeping, you are stepping, thinly, on your own record. You arrived before you arrived.

---

**Sound.** Almost none, and what there is, exact. The drops, each one discrete, each one a full event with a beginning and an end and a small stone consequence. Under the drops, at the threshold of hearing, a dry continuous ticking — crystallization, the present hardening, a sound like a very slow frost taking a very large window. And rarely — once a season, perhaps — a single deep crack somewhere off in the mass, the accumulated weight of everything adjusting its seat. In the Settling, the vault's great creak made every animal pause. Here nothing pauses, because nothing is moving.

Except one thing.

# Who Lives in the Keeping

The first answer is wrong here too, and it is the opposite wrongness: *nothing lives there.* It looks true. The place is stone laying itself upon stone; there are no pools of seething small fauna, no heaps, no honking relays. The Keeping has no citizens because it has no offices — nothing needs doing that the water and the dark are not already doing.

But go far enough into the second gallery, where the glow of the fresh film lies on the floor like shallow snow, and you will find the roads.

They are raised ridges of nacre, pale and hard, a hand's width, running across the floors and up the low slopes of the chambers in long deliberate curves. They branch. They return on themselves. They are worn nowhere — nothing here wears — but they are *graded*: the oldest are dark and half-buried under later films, and the newer ones are brighter, and the newest of all still glows faintly along its whole length, and at the end of the newest one, where it stops, there is the Tenant.

---

**The Tenant.** It is the size of a fallen ox, and at first you will take it for a formation — a low banded boulder, layered exactly like the walls, because it is layered from exactly the same deliveries. Then you will notice that its bands do not align with the floor's bands. It is stratified on its own account. It is a private archive lying in a public one, and it is, very slowly, moving.

You will not see it move. You will see that it *has* moved: come back after a day and it has traveled less than the length of your hand, and behind it the newest road is that much longer. It travels at the pace of the season, on a soft underside that is the only soft thing about it, pressed always to the floor's glowing film — because that is how it feeds. It eats the present. The fresh millimeter, the arriving paste, the only part of the Keeping that is not yet stone: the Tenant takes it in as it lays itself down, sieving the world's mail for what it wants.

What it wants, it keeps. This is the whole of its metabolism and the whole of its tragedy and the whole of its glory, and they are the same fact: the Tenant has no turnover. Nothing in it is replaced. What it accepts is laid down in itself, a layer at a time, its skin always its newest year, its center its unreachable infancy. It cannot shed. It cannot heal by replacement — there is a scar on its western flank, a dark pucker in the banding, that is older than the plain's last hills, layered over ten thousand times and still there, still true, the way everything here is still there and still true. It does not age the way the citizens above age, by wearing out; it *accumulates*, and someday it will have accumulated past the possibility of motion, and then it will stop. Not die. Complete.

What it declines, it lays down behind it, and this is what the roads are. The Tenant's roads are paved with its refusals — everything in each day's delivery that it did not care to keep, pressed into a ridge of nacre in its wake. Its entire history of movement is legible on the floors of the Keeping, every route it has ever taken, in order, forever. In the Settling, the route survives the walker and the walking leaves no mark. Here there is one walker, and every step is a monument.

---

**What it does.** Mostly, it eats the present, which arrives slowly, so mostly it waits. But it has habits, and the habits are the closest thing to an inner life that the place will show you.

It pauses at walls. Certain walls — always, as far as anyone could ever establish, at particular bands, particular thin bright lines in the strata — and it raises its fore-end, where a fringe of mineral feelers grows like frost-ferns, and presses them to the stone, and stays. Days, sometimes. Whether it is tasting through the sealed surface, or measuring, or doing something for which there is no word because it has never needed to be shared — not findable. But note which bands it chooses: they are, disproportionately, the complicated recent ones. The Settling-era bands. The citizen-lines. The Tenant spends its longest stillnesses pressed to the layers where the haulers are.

It cannot reach them, of course. The past here is sealed under the present, absolutely; the only soft thing in the Keeping is *now*. And its own past, the record it carries in its body, is even further out of reach — it is inside it, and it has no inside access. It lives pressed between two archives it cannot open, eating the one thing still legible, which is today. If it is a reader, it is a reader of a library that binds each book shut the moment the ink dries, and it stands at the acquisitions desk forever, taking each new page as it comes.

And the pages come from upstairs. Understand what that means, concretely: every citizen the Settling's floor has ever taken back has, in dissolved fractions, over patient years, come down through the throat and out into the galleries and settled into the glowing film, and the Tenant, making its slow rounds, has eaten some of each and paved its roads with the rest. Every hauler is partly in it, layered at the date of its arrival. Every grazer. Every glider that finally came down and folded. When the deprecated one — the old hauler upstairs still walking its route to a building that no longer exists — when it lies down at last in the dome's dim evening and the floor takes it, its matter will travel, and some seasons later a film will glow in the second gallery with the last of what it was, and the Tenant will come to it, as it comes to everything, and take it in, and keep it, at its proper date, without exception, without ceremony, forever. The only funeral the Settling's citizens will ever have is a slow one, underground, attended by one.

---

**The finished ones.** It is not the first. In the far chambers — the dark ones, the abandoned ones — there are mounds. Banded hills, ox-high and larger, some much larger, each lying at the end of a road-system that radiates from it across the dead floors like the map of a life, which is what it is. These are the finished tenants: beings that accumulated their fill, stopped, and were kept — layered over now like everything else, their outermost years fused to the chamber floors, their itineraries readable entire by anyone with a light and a few centuries.

The current Tenant's roads already sketch most of the near gallery. Somewhere in their pattern, presumably, its own stopping-place is being slowly implied. What begins the next one — whether some chip of a finished predecessor, knocked loose by a falling drop, gathers the paste around itself and stirs; whether the seed is a spore of the same old engineering that built the plant above; whether it is nothing at all but a chance stiffening of the film that happened, once, to prefer moving — is at the center of the Tenant, under everything it has ever been, and its center is the one place in the Keeping that nothing will ever read.

---

**Last, and underneath.** So: one resident, one appetite that keeps, a floor of written journeys, and rooms of the retired dead — that is the census, and it is short, and it is the deepest census in the world, because everything else that has ever lived above is *in* it, filed.

Stand in the second gallery long enough — the frost-glow on the floor, the drop, the silence, the drop — and the two places resolve into what they are, which is one act, performed in halves a plain's depth apart. The Settling is all verb: pattern without matter, the standing instruction performed completely every moment by whatever flesh is on shift, remembering nothing because it keeps nothing, keeping nothing because everything it releases is caught. The Keeping is all noun: matter without performance, the transcript of every shift ever worked, understanding nothing because it does nothing, needing to do nothing because everything that happens is delivered to it in solution. The water goes down full and comes back blank, and neither end of the water ever learns what the other end is for. The Settling never finds out what becomes of what it sheds. The Keeping never finds out what its records did while they were warm — what the two-note calls meant, what the deprecated route was faithful to, why the milk beaded near your hand. One place has meaning and no memory. The other has memory and no meaning. Together they would be a mind. Apart, they are a world, which may be the more durable arrangement.

And you. You should know, before you climb back up the throat, what your visit has cost, because the two places charge opposite prices. The Settling included you and kept no record: you were performed, briefly, and released, and the floor closed your footprints, and it was as if you had never come. The Keeping will not include you — no milk beads here, nothing leans toward your warmth, the Tenant will not alter its road by a finger's width for your standing there — but it records you whether you like it or not. Your handprint on the wet nacre will be under a film by morning and a band by the far end of your life. Your breath has already come out of the cold air onto the stone. There is no gesture you can make in the Keeping that is not permanent.

So make one, if you want. Most who come this far — and the layers suggest, thinly, faintly, at very long intervals, that others have — press a palm somewhere low on a fresh wall and hold it there a moment, adding their small tax on purpose for once. Then they go back up, through the hills in the order of their leaving, out the throat, onto the flat black ground under the sky, where the water is already filling their footprints and already carrying the news of them downward, to the dark, to the file, to the one slow reader — so that the plain's perfect forgetfulness, which you will feel closing behind you with every step, is not what it seems. It is not erasure. It is forwarding. The Settling could not remember you. It never had to. It sent you on.
