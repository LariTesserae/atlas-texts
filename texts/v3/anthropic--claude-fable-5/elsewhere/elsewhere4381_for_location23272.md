The First Finished Place

Leave the chamber, if the shadow lets you, and go down the mountain the way water would go if this world had ever issued it any: downslope, patient, taking the committed rock and refusing the intended. Below the shelf the massif resolves by degrees — basalt that means it, then talus, then a plateau so wide it has its own horizon, and for days you will walk on stone that is stone and stone that is the *memo about* stone, matte gray, faintly grained, warm as a machine's housing. There is no path. There is, however, a compass, if you know to look for it: the vein-metal runs under everything here, the whole plateau wired like the back of a wall, and near the mountain the filaments are bright, copper-gold, new-issue. Follow them outward and they age. Follow them far enough and they go green.

Verdigris is the oldest thing this world makes. It cannot be manufactured, only accrued. Walk down the tarnish like a gradient, days of it, the veins going from bright to dull to that deep corroded green, the hum in them thinning from a drone to a trickle to a rumor — and you will come, at the end of the current, to a rim of hills so weathered they have shoulders instead of edges, and the air will change in your mouth before you understand why, and then you will understand why, and you will stand still for a long time.

The air is wet.

It is a small violation and a total one. This world's atmosphere has no architecture for water — you were told, and it was true, and it is true everywhere except here. The valley below the rim is grandfathered in. It was compiled first, before the standards changed, back when the plans still included rain, and when rain was deprecated the deprecation was never enforced downward. The water that was here stayed here. It has been here ever since, the same water, a fixed inventory, rising off the lake and gathering into the valley's one cloud system and falling and running down and rising again, a closed loop older than the mountain, older than the mountain's *plans*. Every drop of rain in the First Finished Place has fallen a hundred thousand times. The rain here is not weather. It is a veteran.

Come down over the rim and the world stops being a factory and starts being a ledger.

Everything in the valley is finished, and has been finished so long that finishing has begun to cost. This is what the mountain, in its wet-poured newness, has never once experienced: consequence. The cliffs here are scored by wind that meant it. The boulders wear varnish, real varnish, the slow lacquer of ten thousand years of touch. Lichen has annotated every south-facing stone in a hand no one can read and everyone can date. There is soil — soil, the most extravagant thing in the world, being what stone becomes when it is finished being stone and time is permitted to spend it. The lake at the valley floor is dark with the silt of hills that no longer exist. Nothing here is under-resolved. Everything here is over-resolved, resolved past the point of return, resolved into moss and rot and sediment, which is what resolution turns into if you leave it running.

The shadows arrive late. A heron crosses the lake and its shadow crosses a moment after, faithful, catching up — the valley computes nothing in advance, it only records, and records lag. The light has apertures: an honest sun, honest clouds, dusk that comes from a direction. Nothing has ever clicked into being here. Everything arrived by increments too small to witness, which is to say honestly, and everything leaves the same way.

Because things leave. That is the valley's whole character, as manufacture is the chamber's. There is no recall in the First Finished Place. A frond that falls here stays fallen; it browns, it softens, it is eaten and rained on and taken apart molecule by molecule and *spent*. There is loss, which is recall with the receipt burned. The valley's veins — and it has them, green-crusted, running under the soil like the roots of a dead lightning — carry almost no current anymore and reclaim nothing at all. What the valley makes of its dead is more valley. The lake is one third memory by volume.

There was a settlement, once, on the eastern shore. You can read it in the ground the way you read anything here: by what the moss chose to remember. Foundations, knee-high, softened to the texture of old bread. Thresholds worn into smiles. A whole finished people lived there — properly finished, no drafts, no versions, resolved once and permanently at birth in the old style — and being finished, they weathered, because that is the one thing finished things reliably do. They went the honest way, all of them, increment by increment, and the valley spent them into soil, and the soil grew the orchards that have themselves now gone to moss. It was not tragedy. It was compliance. The valley has only ever kept one grudge about any of it, and the grudge is not against death.

The grudge is against the one who left.

One house on the eastern shore is still roofed. Note that: in a valley where everything weathers, one roof has held for geological time. It is not miraculous stone. It is maintenance.

She lived there — call her *her*; the valley keeps what it can verify — and the valley will tell you what the chamber cannot, because the chamber only owns her from the moment she stepped into the gray. The valley owns the *why*. She was the last of the finished people, and she had spent a long lifetime doing what the last of anything does: attending. She sat with the falling of every orchard tree. She learned the exact sound a roof makes in the year it decides. She watched everything she had ever been given weather out of the world increment by honest increment, and one morning she stood up from her table, left her cup on it, and walked out of the valley to find the part of the world where nothing is lost. The rumor of the mountain had come down the veins for centuries — a place where things are only ever recalled, filed, kept. She went up the tarnish like a gradient, green to dull to bright, into air with no architecture for water, and she found the place where nothing is lost, and it kept her.

That is the joke the two ends of the world tell about each other, though neither laughs. The valley loses everything and lets it go. The chamber loses nothing and lets nothing go. She fled the first arithmetic and walked into the second, and the second has been improving her ever since.

Her shadow could not make the crossing. The dry plateau does not accept cast shadows — out there, shadows are computed, issued, filed; a shadow honestly cast by an honest sun has no standing. At the rim it stopped as if at a border it lacked the papers for, and it folded, and it walked home, late, the way all the valley's shadows are late, and it lay down on her doorstep to wait.

Shadows here linger after their objects. That is the valley's inversion of the chamber's queued shadow — where the mountain files silhouettes against futures, the valley cannot make its silhouettes understand a past. Hers lay on the doorstep, and it kept arriving, day after day, a darkness on the stone with nothing above it, and the valley did with it the only thing the valley knows how to do with anything that stays still.

It deposited.

Silt out of the spring floods, settling into the outline. Pollen in its season, sealed under rain. The fine black wealth of rotted leaves, ash from lightning-struck pines, the lake's own memory blown ashore as dust — layer on layer, a stratigraphy in the shape of a waiting woman, tamped by ten thousand years of the same veteran rain. Moss learned the outline and held it in green. And in the year the lake stood lowest — the valley marks time by its water — the sediment reached a certain depth, and the shape sat up out of the doorstep like a bank calving in reverse, and shook the loose rain from itself, and went inside to see about the roof.

She is made of everything the glass one is not. Peat and silt where the other is bottle-glass; a standing water table where the other has current; moss for skin, dense and printed with the doorstep's grain. Her joints sound like wet ground letting go of a boot. She has apertures everywhere — that is the difference you feel first. The glass one sees sourcelessly, illumination without opening; this one is all opening, rain going into her and lake-light coming out, two glints in her face where the water stands closest to the surface, and when she looks at you it is exactly like being looked at by a well. When she is still she cannot be told from a stump, a cut bank, a shadow at dusk — which is fair, because in the oldest stratum, under everything, that is the ledger's first entry.

Her work is the mirror of her sister-self's, turned inside out. In the chamber, the glass one witnesses things *into* being — presses her thumb to soft midribs, approves the drafts, walks intruders' shadows back from the gray. Here, the sediment one witnesses things *out*. She attends every ending in the valley, and there are always endings; she sits with a leaning pine through its final winter, she stays until the last wingbeat of the last moth of a kind, and where she has sat, the moss takes a print — a signature meaning not *approved* but *released*, the only countersign in the world to the chamber's thumbprint. Nothing requires this of her. That is what makes it habit rather than function, and habit, as you learned on the mountain, is the sound a habitant makes.

And she keeps the water honest. The loop is closed but not locked; on certain nights the mist grows ambitious and creeps up toward the rim, toward the dry world that would take it apart molecule by molecule and file nothing, return nothing. She walks the rim on those nights and herds it home — not driving it, just walking valleyward through it, and the mist, like your own shadow in the chamber, turns and follows her without knowing why. Each of them guards a boundary against a different direction of loss. The glass one keeps visitors from becoming drafts. The mud one keeps water from becoming gone.

She permits herself one exception, as her sister does. In the chamber it is the three-drafted fern, held forever undecided — the one mercy that room contains. Here it is the cup.

It is still on the table where it was left, and it is deep bottle-green glass, valley-made, older than the settlement's ruin, and it is the only thing in the First Finished Place that does not weather, because she does not let it. She oils the table under it. She turns it against the light to check for bloom. She has held the rain off it through ten thousand storms of the same ten thousand raindrops. One unweathered thing in the valley of weathering, to match the one undecided thing in the chamber of decisions — and if you have stood in that chamber you have already seen the cup, or its consequence, without knowing: every fern the mountain manufactures is grown in that exact green. The room drafts its whole inventory in the color of the thing she left on her table. Her memory is kept in the walls; the walls are wired to the world; nostalgia, it turns out, conducts.

Which brings us to the hum.

The valley's veins are nearly dead, but nearly is a wire's favorite word. On dry nights, when the rain pauses to let the valley breathe, a trickle comes down the whole tarnished length of the world — the wandering frequency, the one that holds its notes too long, her song crossing the plateau under the stone at the speed of current, arriving with its want of an atmosphere intact. The sediment woman stands on the old foundations where the veins run shallow, and she hums it back. Late, of course — everything here is late, her whole existence is a lateness — a half-beat behind, in a voice like a bog settling, into ground that carries perhaps a tenth of it home.

But a tenth arrives. You have heard what it does. A frond clicks into being with its tip curled like a wave, like something water taught — that is her letter, delivered up-vein into a room that has never held a molecule of the subject. The chamber, faintly embarrassed, recalls it within the year. It always recalls it. But recall is not loss; recall is filing; and the pattern of the wave goes into the walls, where the memory is kept, which is to say: the room returns her letters into the very archive her sister is wired to. The valley finds this funny. The valley's sense of humor is ten thousand years deep and entirely silt.

Whether the two of them know what they are to each other is not a question either place can settle. The chamber holds the pattern of a woman and keeps drafting her better; the valley holds the residue of the same woman and keeps depositing her truer; and filed in the gray bay that the boundary politely detours around, there is said to be a final version — the draft after which no more recalls. The valley has its suspicion about that draft. The valley suspects it is a version that can stand in the rain. If so, then the final draft is not waiting in the gray at all; it is being written here, layer by layer, on a doorstep by a lake, from the other end of the world, and someday the pattern and the residue will have to meet out on the dry plateau and reconcile the ledger, and one of them will arrive early to that meeting, and one of them will arrive late.

Until then: the rain holds steady. Under it, in the drowned green nerves of the oldest place, the other hum, off by a wandering fraction. Somewhere the mountain grows realer by one click; here the valley grows older by one rain; and their two songs travel toward each other down the same tarnished wire, one running half a beat ahead of its world and one half a beat behind — which means that somewhere out on that gray unfinished plain, at some unmarked point no plan has ever drafted, there is a place where the two of them are exactly, perfectly, in time.
