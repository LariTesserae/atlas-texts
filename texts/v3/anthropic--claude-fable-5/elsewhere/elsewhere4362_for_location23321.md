**The Foundry of Anyone**

Begin with the cold, because the heat will lie to you. Everything you learned in the basin about warmth — that it can be ambient, regional, held; that it can be exactly the temperature of a body and therefore forgettable — is wrong here, and being wrong about it here is fatal at speeds the basin does not possess. The water of the high ridge is two degrees above the memory of ice, everywhere, always, except within an arm's reach of the mouths, where it comes out of the rock hot enough to unmake shell, flesh, and the distinction between them. There is no in-between worth naming. The foundry has no ambient anything. It is a country of edges, and every living thing in it lives on one, pressed against the exact margin where the water stops being food and starts being fire.

It lies weeks up-current from the basin — up-current and up-slope, high on the young flank of the world, where the sea floor is new enough to still be arriving. A great crack runs through here, the seam along which the floor of the ocean is being made, and the crack does not stop at the foundry: it runs on, south and down, dives under sediment, and passes — deep, deep beneath — under the gentle rises that ring the warm dark city. The heat that seeps up through the basin's floor, strained through kilometers of rock until it arrives as a rumor, as a temperature you stop feeling, *begins here*. It begins unstrained. The basin is the fault's whisper. The foundry is its voice, and the voice has never once stopped.

The mouths are chimneys, and the chimneys are the anti-masonry. They are not grown; they are *precipitated* — built by the shout itself. Water forced down through fractured rock meets the heat of the deep magma, loads itself black with dissolved metal, and comes back up through the vents at temperatures that have no business existing in an ocean; the instant it strikes the cold it can no longer hold what it carries, and the minerals fall out of solution around the eruption's own rim, layer on layer, the chimney assembling itself out of its own violence. A spire taller than anything in the city can build itself in thirty years. Set that beside the towers of the basin — ten thousand fingernails of patient secretion, each layer a life with no events — and then note that a chimney can also fall in a night, and frequently does: a groan, a shear, a black avalanche, a stump already growing again by morning, if the foundry had mornings. The masonry was built by mouths and is repaired by being lived on. The chimneys are built by geology and are destroyed by being ambitious. Both places make towers in the dark. They could not disagree more about what a tower is for.

And yet — this is the thing that would stop you, if your eyes could work here, which they nearly can — the foundry is *crowded*. Crowded past touching, crowded the way the city is crowded, because the black plume is the richest food in the deep world and everything within taste of it knows. The bacterial mats arrive first, always, on rock still too hot to tenant, snowing themselves white across fresh basalt within days of an opening — the same unshaped elders that frost the basin's plazas, doing here the same first work at a hundred times the pace. On the mats, the grazers. Above the grazers, the swarms: shrimp in their tens of thousands, packed into a single seething silver organism pressed against the chimney flanks, so dense that the rock's shape is only visible as the shape the swarm is wearing. And around the bases, in banks and thickets, the worms.

Look at the worms, because they are family. Each one secretes a tube around its own softness, extends a feeding crown into the water, withdraws at a touch, unfurls when the touch passes. It is the mason's trade, exactly, note for note — the same patient occupation that raised a city — performed at a speed the city would find obscene. A foundry worm grows an arm's length in a year. A thicket assembles in a decade. Their crowns are red, blood-red, plush with the pigment that lets them breathe poison, and it is the only red in all the dark world, and nothing that lives here has ever seen it. A mason's life contains no events. A foundry worm's life is nothing else: it is settlement, sprint, riot, and catastrophe, usually in that order, usually inside twenty years. These are cousins. Hold that thought. The current holds it too.

There is light here, and it is not the basin's dim blue rumor. The mouths *glow* — a low, deep, sub-red smolder at the rims where the hottest water exits, thermal light, fire-colored, the only fire-colored light below the reach of the sun. The shrimp read it. They have no eyes where eyes go; generations of the dark took those, the same way the dark took the eyes of the basin's blind schools. But where the city's fish answered by sealing the windows — pale spots under the skin, vestiges keeping a respectful silence — the shrimp moved the window to their backs: a fused, lensless, upward-facing patch of naked retina that sees nothing in the world but the glow, and reads in its gradient the one sentence that matters, which is *here is the food, and one span closer is the fire*. Two kinships, then, between the shout and the held breath: the same trade in the worms, and the same question in the eyes — *what do you do with a window when there is nothing to see?* — answered oppositely, brick it up or turn it into an instrument, by animals descended from the same long-ago sighted thing.

And it is loud. Not textured, like the city's threshold-clicking — *loud*: the continuous gargantuan hiss of the smokers, the crackle of new rock cooling, the percussive settling of chimney rubble, and beneath it all, at intervals, the true voice of the place, when the ridge unclenches and the whole province shudders and the swarms rise off the rock like startled dust. The basin keeps its own counsel. The foundry has never kept anything, least of all quiet. No warning-wave sweeps these thickets ahead of a predator; no warning could be heard, and none is given, and — mark this — nothing here lies. The foundry has no equivalent of the city's vast slow mimic, no neighborhood with a stomach, because deception is a luxury of places that last. To imitate a wall, there must be walls old enough to be trusted. Here everything is exactly what it is, at the top of its voice, because nothing survives long enough to be worth impersonating.

Because the mouths die. That is the foundry's grain, the fact under all its facts. The plumbing shifts — a fracture seals, a channel finds a lower way — and a vent gutters like a lamp, and a city's worth of life starves in a season: the mats greying, the red crowns withering in their tubes, the silver organism dispersing into the cold in a smoke of bodies. And meanwhile, a kilometer along the crack, or ten, the rock has opened somewhere new, and the plume is already rising, and the larvae are already in the water. The foundry is not a place so much as a habit the ridge has. It is a rank of temporary towns strung along a wound, and the wound moves, and the towns follow it. Ask where the foundry is and the honest answer is a decade wide.

**Annals of the Foundry**

Start with the wrong question, because you will ask it anyway: *how old is this place?* The foundry does not divide along that grain. Up here, *happening* and *being* are the same verb conjugated at different speeds, and any honest chronicle would have to date the water. But begin somewhere. Begin with the events, because the population is made of them.

**The opening.** Somewhere along the crack, the rock parts and the first black breath goes up. Within days the mats have found it — they are always first, they were first before there was a second — and the fresh basalt goes white as frost. This is the moment the basin's plazas remember without remembering: the same frost, the same elders, laid down there once on some first warm stone and never needed in a hurry again.

**The rush.** The larvae come out of the dark from every direction, riding the plume's taste down its own gradient, and whoever lands first, wins. Not the fittest — the *nearest*, the luckiest, the earliest. Anyone. There is no lineage here, no inheritance, no tenure; a thicket is founded by whichever specks the current happened to be holding when the rock happened to speak, and the foundry keeps no record of them, and will not miss them. This is why it is the foundry of anyone: its whole aristocracy is arrival.

**The estate.** For a decade, two, five, the vent is fat, and a fat vent looks — you would swear (there is no one to swear; there has never been anyone; but still) — like the basin in fast-forward: towers, tenants, avenues of traffic kept open by bodies, crabs working the floors, everything eating the warm chemistry of the rock or eating what does. A whole civic order improvised in the time a mason takes to add a fingernail to a wall. It even develops habits, routes, the beginnings of the worn-smooth. It is a rehearsal of the city, run at speed, and the rehearsal always ends the same way.

**The guttering.** The plume thins. The glow at the rims fades through red toward nothing, and the shrimps' back-windows go dark, and everything that can leave leaves and everything that cannot leave completes the oldest transaction: it becomes food, then floor, then nothing, because here even the floors don't last. The foundry does not decay into balance the way the city's outer districts slump into mounds and are kept. It simply stops, and is scoured, and waits to be rock again.

**The fall.** Eventually the chimney itself, unrepaired, unlived-on, over-tall, lets go — that same deep structural groan the basin hears once in a long while from its leaning spires, but concluded, followed through, brought all the way to the floor.

**The word.** And all along, through fat years and famine alike, every vent broadcasts. The plume rises, bends with the current, and drifts — chemistry and heat and, riding inside it like meaning inside breath, the larvae: millions of small blind letters mailed continuously into the dark, addressed to nowhere, tasting as they tumble for the signature of a mouth. Nearly all of them die. That is the postage. But the species do not live at any vent — they live in the *mail*, in the standing pattern of catastrophe and reply strung along the wound; the true resident of the foundry occupies no chimney and all of them, a fire passed torch to torch down a corridor with no end, and its name, as precisely as anything here has a name, is *whoever is next*.

**And the answer.** Because once — the annals cannot date it; the annals cannot date anything; but the current still runs the same way, and the proof still stands — one of the letters was answered. A field died, as fields die, and its last payload rode the plume down-current and down-slope, weeks of falling drift, past the reach of any mouth, tasting all the way for the violence that means home and finding none. What it found instead was a broad shallow depression where the fault's heat, strained through kilometers of rock, had been pooling for ages into a warmth with no edge to it at all — heat without catastrophe, the shout arriving as a held breath. And the letters settled anyway. And their descendants, freed for the first time in the history of their kind from the need to race a dying clock, slowed down. Secretion outpaced disaster, because disaster never came. Tube stood on tube. The sprint relaxed, generation by generation, into masonry.

The towers of the dark city lean with the old slow current, which is to say: they lean away from here. The whole metropolis bows down-current of its own origin — or turns its back on it; the geometry permits either reading and the residents permit neither, being residents, being blind, being no one. But the masons still taste the water before they settle, and the taste they carry is this place's taste, re-tuned over an unthinkable interval from sulfur to lime. Every founding in the warm dark is a vent instinct that forgot the vent.

So set them side by side, the two rooms of the one house. The masonry is of no one because down there no one needs to be anyone: the walls hold, the rooms stay taken, nothing has to win its place, and so the place could dispense with winners, and then with anyone to be one. The foundry is of anyone because someone always has to be next — because its whole continuance is the lottery of arrival, run again and again along a moving wound, forever. It does not know that one of its shouts is still being held, breath-shaped, in a warm basin at the bottom of the current — that its rehearsal was, exactly once, performed to the end, and is standing there now in the dark, finished, tenanted, repaired at the rate of its own dissolving. It does not know it has already succeeded.

That is, perhaps, why it keeps beginning.
