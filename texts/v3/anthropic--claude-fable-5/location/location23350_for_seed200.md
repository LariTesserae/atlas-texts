The basin has no name because nothing here has ever needed to distinguish it from anywhere else. It is simply where everything is.

**The rock first**, because the rock is youngest. Underneath all the green lies stone so new it still has edges. Basalt that cooled into ropes and pillows and was never granted a single winter, never a freeze, never a flood violent enough to round it. Where the vegetation parts — and it parts rarely, grudgingly — you can see surfaces with a glassy sheen, conchoidal fractures sharp enough to cut, the chemistry of the deep earth sitting exposed and unweathered at the surface like something undressed. There is no soil in the proper sense. There is no sediment. There has not been time, or rather, there has been time but the place has refused to spend it. What passes for ground is a mat: root over root over moss over the half-dissolved bodies of leaves, a living carpet laid directly over raw stone, sometimes a meter thick, sometimes thin as skin, and you learn the difference by whether the ground gives like flesh or knocks like bone.

The rock weeps. This is the basin's only hydrology. There are no rivers — rivers require erosion, require a landscape that has agreed to be carved, and this one has not agreed. Instead, water comes out of the stone itself in slow continuous seepage, beading along fracture lines, gathering in the cupped tops of lava pillows, sliding down faces of black glass in films too thin to be called streams. The air is so close to saturation that the boundary between weather and landscape has dissolved: water condenses on every cool surface, on the underside of leaves, on the backs of the larger animals when they hold still. It never rains, exactly. It also never stops raining. Moisture simply circulates — up as vapor off ten thousand warm pools, down as drip and bead and sweat — in a loop so closed it might as well be a single held breath.

**The heat** is total and without gradient. No mornings cooler than afternoons, no shade meaningfully different from sun, because there is no sun to speak of — only a luminous gray-green dimness that filters down through the canopy and the permanent vapor haze above it, the light of a world inside a greenhouse inside a cloud. The temperature is that of blood, everywhere, always. Things that fall do not cool. Eggs need no brooding. The heat is not an event, not a season; it is a property of existence here, like mass.

**The green** is layered to the point of architecture, though nothing built it. Tree ferns and horsetail-like columns rise from the root-mat, their trunks furred with epiphytes which are themselves furred with smaller epiphytes, growth upon growth upon growth until you cannot say where one organism ends. The canopy is not high — this is a low place, a basin near sea level, and the plants seem to feel no ambition toward height when every cubic meter of air is already wet and warm and full of light's dim sufficiency. Instead they grow *thick*. Leaves overlap like scales. The space between any two plants is occupied by a third. In some hollows the vegetation forms enclosed green rooms, vaulted with fronds, floored with moss, lit by what little glow makes it through — and these rooms extend, one opening into the next, for kilometers. The basin is large. You could walk for days through it, valley-broad, ringed at its far edges by low domes of newer rock still too hot or too raw to be colonized, black hills the green is patiently surrounding but has not yet climbed.

**And the animals.** This is the thing that overwhelms, the thing a visitor would remember after forgetting everything else: the basin is *full*. Not populated — full, the way water is full of itself. Every surface carries something. The moss is in constant low motion with invertebrates, a glittering traffic of segmented bodies. Amphibious things the size of hands and the size of dogs lie stacked in the warm seep-pools, layered two and three deep, gill-frills pulsing, utterly unbothered by contact — crowding is not a condition here, it is the medium. Climbing animals move through the canopy in such numbers that the fronds shiver continuously, a vegetation that never stops trembling. Gliders cross the green rooms on membranes of skin. Things with too many eyes watch from drip-lines. There are no predators in the way other places have predators — or rather, predation here has the character of grazing, slow, ambient, unhurried, because nothing is scarce and nothing needs to chase. The food web is less a web than a fabric, woven so tight it doesn't flex.

**The sound** of all this is a wall. A standing chord. Insect drone in three or four registers at once, the bell-notes of amphibians calling in overlapping rounds, the percussion of condensation falling from a hundred million leaf-tips onto a hundred million leaves below, the wet respiration of the pools. It is loud — genuinely loud, a sound you feel in the chest — and it never modulates. There is no dawn chorus because there is no dawn. There is no evening hush because nothing here has ever experienced an evening. The chord was playing before you arrived and the unsettling certainty, standing in it, is that it has been playing without variation for a very, very long time.

**Because here is the strangeness of the basin**, the thing that is wrong in a quiet way: it does not change. Not slowly — *at all*. The seep-water flows but deposits nothing and carves nothing. The animals are born and die in such perfect demographic balance that the population of any pool is effectively constant. The plants grow only as fast as they decay. A frond that falls is consumed at exactly the rate new fronds emerge. Walk the basin, mark a place in memory — a particular green room, a particular pillow of black glass with seven amphibians stacked in its cup — and return in a year, in ten, and the room will be the same room, the pillow will hold seven sleepers, perhaps not the same individuals but indistinguishable, as if the place maintains not its inhabitants but its *configuration*. The basin is not young, despite its unweathered rock. It is something rarer: a place that found its equilibrium early, perhaps within a generation of the lava cooling, and then locked. Erosion never got purchase because the living mat sealed the stone before the first storm could touch it. Succession never happened because the first community to arrive was, by accident or by some deep fitness, final.

Nothing here has ever made anything. No nest beyond a depression in moss, no dam, no mound, no mark. The animals are numerous past counting and innocent of tools, of construction, of any impulse to alter what is already, from their standpoint, complete. There is nothing for them to want.

The dim light holds steady. The chord holds its note. The rock, sharp and black and new, waits under the green for an erosion that is never coming, and the water beads, and slides, and beads again, and the basin goes on being exactly, eternally, deafeningly itself.
