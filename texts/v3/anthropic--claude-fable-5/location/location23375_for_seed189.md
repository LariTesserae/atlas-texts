# The Hollow in the Green

You do not arrive here so much as you are *swallowed* into it — down through soil that is not soil, through a throat of woven root, until the passage exhales you into a space the size of a small chapel. And then you stand in it, blinking, because there should be no light this far under the earth, and the light is everywhere.

The chamber is a void inside a single living mass. There is no rock here, no soil, no mineral anything. The floor is root. The walls are root — or stem, or rhizome; the categories blur, because the plant has not decided yet what it is. The ceiling is a dense vault of pale interlocking growth, braided like knuckles, and through every gap in the braid comes a green-gold light with no sun behind it. The light comes from *depth*. It comes from the plant itself, or from somewhere the plant remembers, filtered through a hundred meters of living tissue overhead until it falls into this pocket warm and ordinary, the way afternoon light falls through leaves. It is daylight. It is wrong. You stop questioning it within minutes, which may itself be one of the chamber's properties.

## The Walls Are Recent

Everything here is new. The hollow is days old, perhaps hours — it is hard to say, because time behaves strangely (we will get to that). The surfaces have the rawness of a wound or a sprout: pale green-white, glossy, sharp-edged where shoots have split through each other and not yet softened. Nothing is weathered. Nothing has had time to scar. Where the walls flex — and they do flex, slow as breathing — fine cracks open in the cuticle and bead immediately with sap, clear and faintly warm, smelling of cut grass and something underneath the cut grass, something like ozone.

The dampness is everywhere but nowhere pooled. Condensation silvers the underside of every arching root. Droplets gather, swell, fall — and the fall is one of the few honest measures of time in here, a slow irregular percussion onto the spongy floor, *tok... tok-tok... tok*. Sometimes a droplet falls upward. You will tell yourself you imagined it.

## The Emptiness

For all that the chamber is made entirely of living matter, its interior is almost vacant. That is the strangeness of it: total vegetation, near-total void. The plant is the architecture, the geology, the world — and the world has hollowed out this one room and left it nearly bare. No undergrowth. No moss carpet. No tangle. Just the smooth flexing floor, the vaulted braid above, the warm wet air between, and three things:

**First**, a single tendril descending from the ceiling's center, thin as a finger, ending in a closed bud the size of a fist. It does not move. It is the stillest thing in the chamber, and the longer you watch it, the more the rest of the room seems to be arranged around it, the way a room arranges itself around a lit candle.

**Second**, a small animal — you will see it perhaps twice in a long stay. Something like a salamander, translucent, the green light passing through its body so that it appears as a moving distortion more than a creature. It travels the walls. It eats the sap from the fresh cracks. It is the only fauna, and it watches you with an attention that feels less like an animal's and more like the chamber checking on you through a borrowed eye.

**Third**, the marks.

## The Marks

Someone was here before you, when the chamber was even younger — or older; again, time. On one wall, at shoulder height, a row of notches has been cut into the living surface with a stone edge: simple tally-marks, seven of them, the cuts already half-healed, lips of new growth folding over the wounds like the plant slowly closing a book. Below the notches, pressed into what was once soft tissue and is now firm, a single handprint. Human-sized. Six fingers — or five, depending on the angle you view it from, and both counts are correct, and you will check several times.

Near the floor, almost absorbed, the haft of a flint hand-blade protrudes from the wall. The plant has grown around it. Whoever carried it either left it deliberately or could not pull it free, and the chamber is in the process of making it part of itself, the way bark swallows a fence wire. There is no other artifact, no structure, no path worn into the floor. Whoever they were, they made these marks and went away, or went *in*, or had always been the marks and nothing else.

## The Sound

The chamber is loud. This is the thing that no description prepares you for. A space this small, this empty, should be quiet, and instead it roars.

The roar is the plant. Behind every wall, sap is moving — not seeping but *coursing*, in channels wide as your torso, and the sound is exactly the sound of a river heard through a hillside: a deep, fluctuating rush that rises and falls on a rhythm too slow to be a pulse and too irregular to be anything else. Layered over it: the creak and pop of growth. The walls are still growing, fast enough to hear. Fibers tick and snap as they lengthen. Every few minutes something deeper gives way with a *crack* like a green branch breaking, and the whole chamber shudders, and the light flickers as the braided vault overhead resettles.

And there are echoes that arrive before their sounds. You will hear the *tok* of a falling droplet, and a half-second later watch the droplet detach and fall. You will hear your own footstep and then take it. The chamber's acoustics run slightly ahead of its events, as if sound here is a draft and the world a fair copy made just after. If you speak, do not be alarmed to hear your sentence finish in the air a beat before your mouth does. The chamber is not mocking you. It is, as near as anyone can tell, simply *eager*.

## The Breakdown

The weirdness here is not decorative. It is structural, and it deepens the longer you attend to it.

The geometry does not close. Walk the chamber's perimeter and you will count the wall's major roots — say, eleven of them. Walk it again: thirteen. The room is not larger or smaller; it has simply been *revised*. The notch-marks on the wall sometimes number seven and sometimes eight, and the eighth is always freshest, as if the tally is being kept by something that continues counting in the carver's absence.

Growth runs at the wrong speed in patches. There are zones — you learn to feel them as a thickening of the warm air — where a new shoot will emerge from the wall, elongate, leaf out, yellow, wither, and be reabsorbed in the span of a held breath: a full life cycle compressed into seconds, *loud*, a sound like wet paper tearing in fast-forward. Elsewhere, a droplet of sap hangs mid-fall, fixed in the green light, and stays fixed for as long as you watch, and is gone the moment you look away.

The light has tides. Twice in what feels like a day, the green-gold brightness dims toward dusk and then recovers — but the dimming moves *across* the chamber as a visible front, a wall of evening sliding from one side to the other, so that you can stand with one hand in afternoon and one in dusk and feel the temperature differ between them.

And the bud — the closed fist of the central tendril — is the axis of all of it. The revision of the walls, the eager echoes, the tides of light: they all orient on it the way iron filings orient on a magnet. It has never been seen to open. The half-healed tally on the wall, you eventually suspect, is somebody counting toward something. The chamber, growing and roaring and rehearsing its sounds before it makes them, has the unmistakable atmosphere of a place that is *almost ready*.

## To Stand There

The air is blood-warm and wet on the skin. The floor gives slightly, alive under your weight, and your footprints close behind you like water. The river in the walls never stops. Somewhere the translucent salamander licks a fresh wound in the green. The light flickers when the vault resettles. A droplet hangs. Your voice answers you before you speak.

It is a small room at the bottom of an enormous living thing, empty of nearly everything, full of nearly everything's *anticipation* — a pocket of held breath, freshly grown, gloriously loud, where the plant that is the whole world has cleared a space and lit it and is waiting, with what may as well be patience, for the bud to open and find out what it has made.
