**The Pocket**

There is a place high in a range whose name has worn off — somewhere above the line where even lichen gives up, where the rock has been scoured to its honest mineral self — and the place is not the mountain. The place is a hollow *in* the mountain, no larger than a modest room, and you find it the way you find anything at that altitude: by accident, half-blind, looking for shelter from wind that no longer reaches you the moment you step inside.

The entrance is a seam between two slabs of frost-split gneiss. It should not admit a body. It does, and afterward you will not be able to agree with yourself about how.

---

**The floor** is dry stone polished to a dull pewter sheen — not by water, which has not been here in geological memory, but by something subtler. The polish is the kind that time itself leaves: surfaces that have been *occupied by duration* for so long that they have settled into their final shapes. Nothing in the pocket is sharp. Every edge has been rounded by patience. Dust exists here, a sparse grey film of it, but it does not drift. It lies where it lies, and has, you sense, for a very long while.

There is frost in the corners, but it is the wrong frost. It does not glitter. It is matte, almost soft-looking, a crystallization not of water but of *cold itself* — as if temperature here had been wrung out so long and so thoroughly that it precipitated, and now sits in pale grey deposits, like the ash of stillness. If you breathe near it, your breath does not fog. The air takes the moisture from you politely and you never see where it goes.

**The light** has no source. This is the first thing you check and the last thing you stop checking. It is a dim, even illumination, the colour of dusk recalled rather than seen — somewhere between slate and the inside of a closed eye. It does not cast shadows correctly. Your shadow exists, but it lags. Move your hand and the dark shape on the floor follows a half-second behind, smoothly, unhurried, like a companion who knows where you are going and sees no need to rush. Eventually you stop moving quickly, out of a kind of courtesy.

---

**The objects.** They are scattered — five, or seven; the count varies with the direction you turn while counting, and after a while you accept this the way you accept weather.

Near the entrance: a low form like a stool or a plinth, made of a material with the heft of basalt and the warmth of nothing at all. It is neither cold nor warm to the touch. It has no temperature. Your hand reports *contact* and then falls silent on the subject. Its surface is covered in fine, regular striations too small to be tooling marks and too organized to be crystal — the residue, perhaps, of manufacture at a scale where matter and instruction are the same substance.

Against the far wall, half-buried in the pale frost: a ring, waist-high, standing on edge without leaning on anything. It is broken — about a third of its circumference is simply absent — but the absence is *finished*. The break-faces are as polished as the rest. Whatever removed that arc did so completely, taking even the possibility of jagged edges with it. Through the ring you can see the wall behind it, and the wall behind it is fractionally wrong: the stone seen through the ring is the same stone seen around it, but younger. Less weathered. As though the ring frames the wall as it was, and the frame is slowly going out of date.

In the middle distance — and the pocket has a middle distance, which a room four metres across should not — a few smaller artifacts lie spaced apart with the careful sparseness of stones in a raked garden. A hexagonal tile that is always at the periphery of your vision and never quite at the centre. A coil of something like cable, except that following it with your eye from one end to the other takes longer than the coil's length can account for; somewhere in its loops there is more path than there is cable, and the surplus has to live somewhere, and it lives here.

These things are not ruins, exactly. Ruins imply collapse. These have not collapsed. They have *settled*, the way a glacier settles, the way a word settles into a language — eroded into balance with their surroundings by an erosion that works on more than matter. The pocket has weathered its contents and its contents have weathered the pocket, and the result is an equilibrium so old it reads as natural.

---

**The geometry** is where the place stops pretending.

Walk the perimeter clockwise and it takes perhaps forty paces. Counterclockwise, closer to sixty, and the extra twenty are not hidden anywhere you can point to — the room is simply more generous in one direction than the other. The corners, when you sight along them, do not sum. There are four of them, plainly, and yet standing in any one corner you can see only two others, and the missing one is always the one you just left. The ceiling is low enough to touch and high enough to disappear into the dim, and both of these are true at once, without strain, the way a thing can be both far and small.

None of this feels violent. That is the strange mercy of the pocket: the breakdown is *mature*. Wherever reality tore here — and it tore, long ago, perhaps when the broken ring was whole and doing whatever it was built to do — the tear has had eons to heal at its own pace, scarring over into a stable wrongness. The laws of the place are not absent. They are simply local, and old, and worn smooth like everything else.

There is one active seam left. Along the eastern wall — east by the compass of your own arrival, since no other east applies — runs a hairline of *unlight*, a black thread that is not shadow because shadow is the absence of light and this is the absence of the absence going on around it. It is migrating. Patient observation across hours shows no movement; patient observation across the scuff-marks and frost-stains it has left in its wake suggests millimetres per year, maybe less. It is the last slow stitch of whatever wound this is, closing, on a schedule indifferent to anything that breathes.

---

**Sound.** Almost none. The pocket does not echo — your footsteps land and are absorbed, your voice goes out and does not come back, and after the first attempt you do not speak again, less from fear than from the sense that speech here is litter.

But it is not perfectly silent. At long, irregular intervals — twenty minutes, an hour — there is a single sound: a soft *tick*, mineral and small, like one pebble considering another. It comes from no fixed direction. It might be the frost growing by one crystal. It might be the unlight seam advancing by one increment too fine to measure. It might be the broken ring's framed wall losing one more year. You will hear it three or four times during a visit, and each time the silence afterward is deeper, as though the tick is the pocket spending its sound budget for the day.

**Nothing lives here.** Not as a tragedy — as a completeness. There is no niche. There is nothing for water to do, nothing for roots to hold, nothing for a body to eat or breathe or want. The pocket is post-biological the way the inside of a crystal is: not hostile to life, simply a chapter in which life is not a character. Whatever made the artifacts may not have been alive either, in any sense you'd recognize — the striations on the plinth suggest authorship without hands, intention without appetite.

---

**Leaving** is easier than arriving. The seam in the gneiss is wider on the way out, or you are narrower; the dim light hands you back to the grey altitude daylight without ceremony. Outside, the wind resumes mid-sentence. The cold outside is honest cold, the kind that comes from somewhere and goes somewhere.

You will check your watch and find the time more or less accountable — perhaps short an hour, perhaps over by a few minutes, errors small enough to blame on yourself. The only durable evidence is this: for some days afterward, your shadow will seem, in low light, very slightly slow. Not enough to swear to. Just enough to make you turn, now and then, and look at it — and find it already still, already correct, already pretending, like the pocket itself, that it has been this way forever.
