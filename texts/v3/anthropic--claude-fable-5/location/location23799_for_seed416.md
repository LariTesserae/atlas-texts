**The Warm Room**

There is no way to say where it is, exactly, because "where" implies a landscape around it, and the room does not acknowledge anything outside itself. It sits embedded in some larger made thing — a hull, a foundation, a machine the size of a weather system — the way a geode sits in rock. You would have to already be inside the greater structure, and lost, and small, to find it. The entrance is a seam that was never a door. It has simply been open longer than anyone has been alive to close it.

The room is perhaps the size of a chapel, though it feels smaller because the ceiling comes down in slow ribbed vaults, like the underside of a tongue. Everything is constructed. There is no stone here, no soil, no substrate that was ever wild. The walls are a material that current physics would describe badly — something like ceramic, something like settled smoke — laid down in layers so long ago that the layers have begun to disagree with each other, delaminating in places into thin curling leaves, like the pages of a book left in steam.

Because it is steam, more or less, that fills the room. Not thick — the air is breathable, if you don't mind breathing something at the temperature of blood-heat and then well past it. The heat has no visible source. It emanates from a spine of dark machinery running along the floor's centerline: a low ridged structure, waist-high, that hums at a frequency below hearing and radiates warmth the way a sleeping animal does. Whatever it does — whatever it was built to do — it has been doing for so long that the doing has become indistinguishable from existing. Heat comes off it in slow shudders. The room is its exhalation.

The darkness is nearly total. What light there is comes from the machinery's seams: a dim ember-line, less illumination than the memory of illumination, the color of iron three hours after the forge. It shows edges only. It shows movement. It shows the wet.

Because the room sweats. That is the water here — no rain, no pool, only condensation. The hot air rises off the machine-spine, touches the cooler vaulted ceiling, and gives up its moisture in beads. The beads fatten. They travel the ribs of the ceiling in long deliberate migrations and drop, one at a time, irregularly, into the dark. *Tik.* A long silence. *Tik-tik.* Silence. This is most of the sound in the room, and after enough hours it stops being sound and becomes something closer to a pulse. Beneath it, always, the sub-audible hum. Nothing else, unless the fauna are moving, and even then not much — the residents of the warm room have evolved, or been designed, or drifted, toward quiet.

There are a great many of them. This is the surprise of the place: for a chamber with no light, no plants, no visible food, it is crowded with life, layered with it, near-touching. The walls host a mineral bloom — not vegetation, nothing that photosynthesizes, but a slow crystalline exudate the machinery weeps through microfractures in its housing, a salt of unfamiliar chemistry that forms pale crusts and frills. This is the base of everything. Small grazers work it constantly: flattened, palm-sized creatures, soft-bodied, that rasp the crust with a sound like a fingertip on paper. Their undersides glow very faintly where they digest — a phosphor smear, gone when they lift away. In the ember-light you can see dozens of them at any moment, and the walls are worn into shallow scalloped hollows by generations of them, a hundred thousand years of grazing recorded as erosion. The room is polished where bodies have passed. Every edge that could be sharp has been rounded by traffic.

Above the grazers, in the vaults, hang the drinkers — longer-limbed things, jointed like ideas of insects but jointless where you expect joints, which position themselves under the ceiling's condensation routes and simply wait, mouths open to the traveling beads. They have staked out territories along the ribs. Occasionally two of them contest a drip-line, and the contest is nearly silent: a slow pressing match, a clattering of limbs like dropped chopsticks, then one retreating along the vault. The water rights of the warm room are old law.

And there are hunters — of course there are hunters, the room has been in balance too long not to have them. Something pale and low-slung moves along the floor's edge where the ember-light doesn't reach. It takes grazers. Not often. The room's whole metabolism is slow, geared to the drip and the hum, predation on the schedule of tides rather than pounces. When a kill happens the only evidence is a brief scuffle, a paper-tearing sound, and then the *tik* of the ceiling resuming its count.

The strangeness of the place is quiet and structural rather than lurid. The temperature never varies, and yet the room has weather — pressure fronts that pass through with no source, making the steam lean all in one direction for an hour, making the fauna go still. The seams of the central machine sometimes brighten in patterns, and the patterns repeat on a cycle of days, and the grazers arrange themselves along the walls in configurations that mirror the patterns, and no one is present to say whether this is communication or coincidence or worship. The delaminating wall-leaves, when they finally detach and fall, fall slower than they should.

Whoever built the greater structure built this room for a purpose, and the purpose has been lost, and in its place an ecology has moved in the way water moves into a footprint. The machine keeps the heat. The heat keeps the water moving. The water keeps the salt-bloom fed, and the bloom keeps everything else. It is a complete world, humid and dark and blood-warm, no bigger than a chapel, running in the dark inside something enormous — a heart that no longer knows what body it beats for, and beats anyway.

*Tik.*

*Tik-tik.*
