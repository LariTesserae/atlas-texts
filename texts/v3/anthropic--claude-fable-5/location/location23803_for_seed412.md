The mountain is younger than the people who worship it. You can tell by the edges — every ridge up here still carries its factory sharpness, unblunted, cut clean against the sky like something removed from packaging an hour ago. Ash hasn't had time to become soil. Rain hasn't had time to memorize a path down. The whole massif has the raw, waiting quality of a thing that has been made but not yet lived in.

And near the summit, tucked into the lee of a blade-edged spur where the wind can't reach, there is a room.

Not a room in the architectural sense. A pocket. A hollow perhaps forty paces across, walled on three sides by fresh stone the color of iron filings, open at one end to a drop of four thousand meters of cloud. You would miss it from any distance. You almost miss it standing at its lip. It announces itself first as heat — a wall of it, wet and mineral, rolling out of the hollow like breath from an open mouth — and second as sound.

The sound is the thing you will remember. The hollow is *loud*, continuously, from below: a deep arrhythmic drumming, like an enormous heart that has never needed to keep time for anyone, overlaid with a shriller register — a hissing, ticking chorus, thousands of small percussions, stone cooling, stone forming, stone being *decided upon*. The noise fills the pocket completely. Inside it, you cannot hear your own footsteps. You cannot hear yourself speak. The hollow permits no other voice.

The floor is where the mountain is still being made.

It doesn't look like lava, though it glows a little — a dim, deep-orange smolder visible only in the low light of the pocket, which is perpetually dusk-dark, shadowed by its own walls and by the steam that never fully clears. The floor looks more like the surface of something *thinking*. Seams of hot material run through the dark rock in patterns too regular for geology and too fluid for design: they branch, converge, reroute. Watch one seam for ten minutes and you will see it withdraw from a course it had committed to, go dark, and reappear two meters away, brighter, as if the mountain reconsidered. New rock rises out of these seams in slow extrusions — fins, ribs, small blunt towers a hand-span tall — and the ticking chorus is the sound of each one annealing into permanence. The floor of the hollow is measurably higher than it was last season. The mountain is 2.3 kilometers tall and not finished.

No physics you were taught accounts for the heat budget here. The seams glow but do not radiate correctly; you can hold your palm a finger's width above one and feel only warmth, then step back a full pace and be nearly scalded. Heat in the hollow pools in invisible volumes, like furniture. Regular visitors — there are none anymore, but there were — learned the rooms within the room, the standing-places, the corridors of survivable air.

The dampness lives in the ceiling of the world. Steam rises off the working floor, hits the cold thin air above the walls, and falls back as a fine intermittent rain — warm on the way down, mineral-sweet, beading on every fresh edge. The rain never reaches the hot seams; it vanishes a hand-width above them with a sound lost in the general roar. But it collects in the dead zones, the finished places, in sharp new basins that hold water the color of weak tea. These pools tremble constantly with the drumming from below, their surfaces never still, ringed with pale crusts of whatever the mountain is made of that isn't stone.

Around the pools: the vegetation, such as it is. A dozen presences, no more, scattered with enormous space between them. Cushion plants, black-green, dense as felt, each one growing exactly at the boundary of a survivable heat-volume — living thermometers, mapping the invisible rooms more precisely than any instrument. One of them is old, older than the mountain by any reckoning, a meter across, and no one has ever explained how it arrived here or what it germinated in, since the rock beneath it is younger than it is. Filaments of something red — plant or plant-adjacent — hang from the wettest overhangs, fed on falling steam, swaying to the drumbeat. Nothing else grows. Nothing at all moves under its own will. No insect has ever been recorded in the hollow. Birds crossing the summit ridge detour around the airspace above it, all of them, always, in a smooth arc, as though around a marked hazard only they can read.

The built things are few and hard to date. A row of anchor-rings driven into the north wall, metal, unrusted, spaced for climbers or for something tethered. A flat platform of worked stone near the entrance lip, its surface incised with a spiral of grooves that channel the warm rain into a single runnel and off the edge into cloud — a drain, an offering-track, an instrument, no one knows. Half-buried in the finished rock at the hollow's center, and this is the artifact that troubles people, is the corner of something rectilinear: a smooth grey edge, matte, seamless, that the forming floor has been building *around* — carefully, deferentially — for as long as anyone has watched. The new rock never touches it. The seams route wide of it. Whatever it is, the mountain-making process treats it the way a mason treats a wall that isn't his to move.

The prevailing theory, held loosely because all theories about this place are held loosely, is that the hollow is not a vent or a wound but a *workshop* — the one exposed cell of a process running through the whole young massif, and that the drumming, if you could slow it down enough, resolves into repetition with variation. Into revision. Someone once recorded forty hours of it and claimed the pattern never repeated exactly but never wandered either, the way a craftsman's hammer never repeats and never wanders.

Come at dusk, which is the only light the hollow really has — its own dim orange from below, the grey seep of clouded sky from above, red filaments swaying between them. Stand at the lip where the heat first reaches you. Feel the drumming come up through the fresh sharp stone into the bones of your feet, feel the warm rain begin on the back of your neck, and understand that you have found the one place on this enormous mountain where the mountain is honest about itself: not a landform, not yet.

A draft. Still loud with drafting.
