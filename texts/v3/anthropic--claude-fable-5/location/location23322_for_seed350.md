**The Loud Green**

Begin below the surface, because there is no meaningful above. Somewhere overhead there may be a sky, but between here and there hangs forty, sixty, sometimes ninety meters of warm water — the depth is not consistent, and not consistent in the way of varying terrain, but in the way of a question that gets different answers depending on who is asking. Divers' instruments, if there were divers, would disagree with each other and then with themselves.

The water is blood-warm. It is the temperature of the inside of a mouth, and it is never still. It hauls itself in slow muscular surges through a flooded basin perhaps thirty kilometers across — a valley, recently a valley, drowned so recently that the rock has not had time to soften. Every ridge down here is sharp. The basalt is young, glassy, fractured into blades and prisms with edges that have not known a single century of wear. In places the rock is still arriving: pale extrusions push up from seams in the basin floor, swelling visibly, hissing, hot enough to make the water around them flinch and shimmer. The land is being born underwater, in the dark, in a hurry.

And everything that is not new stone is green.

The vegetation here did not creep in. It *detonated*. Ribbon-weeds thirty meters long stand off the floor in colonnades, packed so closely that swimming between them would mean touching them constantly — leaf against shoulder, stalk against spine, an unbroken corridor of contact. Bladder-kelps with floats the size of skulls. Mats of algae thick enough to walk on, if anything walked. Filamentous growth furs every cable, every railing, every blade of new rock, softening nothing, only upholstering the sharpness in green velvet. The plants grow fast enough to watch: a frond uncurls over the course of a held breath. They are fed by the warmth, by the mineral smoke of the vents, and by something else, something the machines exhale.

Because the machines are still running.

---

Scattered across the basin floor — never clustered, never a town, always a lone structure surfacing out of the weed like a thought you can't place — stand the works. A smelting hall with its roof torn open, glowing dull orange from within, breathing columns of bubbles that never quite reach the surface. A pumphouse, alone on a ridge, pistons rising and falling with terrible patience. A row of turbines half-buried in algal mat, spinning, spinning, fed by currents or feeding them, it's no longer clear which. Pylons carry cables off into the green murk, and the cables are live: in the dimness you can see the current in them, a wrong soft glow under the fouling, like veins under skin. Nobody shut any of it off. There is no evidence anyone tried.

The light down here is the light of these things and almost nothing else. What sun penetrates the canopy arrives exhausted, a deep olive twilight that thickens to true dark in the lee of the big stalks. Against that gloom: the furnace-orange of the smelter, the gas-blue arc of a failing transformer, the phosphor sheen of leaves growing too close to the cables. Sometimes a light arrives before its source does — a glow blooms in open water, holds, and then a few seconds later the machine it belongs to is *there*, where it perhaps always was. This is one of the gentler ways the place misbehaves.

---

There are no animals. Understand this fully: not no fish — no *anything*. No worms in the sediment, no larvae in the foam, no shells, no bones, not even the memory of bones. The sediment is unbored. The leaves are unbitten. Every surface in a drowned world should be crawling, and nothing here crawls. The ecology is a kingdom of plants and machines exchanging heat, current, and dissolved metal in a closed loop with no mouths in it anywhere. The effect is not peaceful. It is the specific wrongness of a banquet hall, fully laid, at which no one has ever sat down.

And yet the place is *deafening.*

Sound underwater has nowhere to go and no body has ever needed ears less than here. The basin roars at all hours with the layered, total noise of itself: the cavitation scream of the turbines, a drill-whine that lives in the teeth; the pumphouse's slow industrial heartbeat, *whump — whump — whump*, felt in the sternum from kilometers away; the rock-birth hiss of the vents; the groan of cooling extrusions cracking; the colossal wet rasp of ten million leaves dragged through turbulence at once, a sound like the sea trying to whisper and failing. There are standing pressure waves in the open channels — places where the noise is so dense and so sustained that the water itself ridges visibly, corrugated by sound, and the weed there grows in matching corrugations, banded like tree rings, a fossil record of volume.

The sound is not incidental to the strangeness. The sound appears to be the *mechanism* of it.

---

Where the noise is loudest, reality is most molten. This is observable, repeatable, the closest thing the basin has to a law.

Near the smelter, where the hammering of unseen forges peaks into something past sound — a pressure, a flavor, a color behind the eyes — the geometry stops cooperating. Corridors of kelp open onto themselves. A swimmer following the basin floor downslope arrives, after long descent, at the canopy. There is a quarter of the valley where every direction is gently, persistently *toward the pumphouse*, and the weed there grows in a converging spiral, all of it leaning in, obedient to the local rule. "Down" is negotiable. Some of the taller plants have evidently grown along two or three different downs in the course of their short lives, and stand kinked like questions.

The factories produce. This was always their purpose and they have not stopped, but what they produce no longer fully holds. Objects emerge from the discharge gates — castings, fittings, things shaped like parts of larger things — and persist for minutes or hours before becoming difficult: too heavy, then too light, then not quite present, then sediment. The basin floor is drifted with the pale silt of unmade products. The plants root in it eagerly. It may be their richest food. There is a thought here that does not resolve, about a loop: machines making things, things failing into soil, soil into green, green into the turbines' blades, blades into noise, noise unmaking the world a little more, the unmade world feeding the machines. The basin as a single engine whose product is its own instability.

In the deep channels you can find duplicated terrain — a ridge of fresh basalt and, twenty meters on, the same ridge, same fractures, same furring of weed, like a stammer in the landscape. One of the lone structures, a small electrified shed of no clear function, exists in at least four locations and in none of them reliably. Time pools near the vents: a frond there can be watched growing and decaying at once, the rot chasing the green up the stalk and never catching it.

---

Nothing about the basin is settling. That should be said plainly. This is not a ruin going quiet; it is a system accelerating. The currents are getting stronger — you can read it in the lean of the weed, more extreme each season, if seasons are what these are. The vents are building rock faster than the water can cool it. New structures are appearing at the basin's edges, single buildings of the same industrial school, fully fouled with growth as if old, but standing on rock that did not exist before they did. No one builds them. They are, perhaps, another product, the largest castings, the ones that hold.

The whole valley churns: weed thrashing, water hauling, pistons falling, rock arriving, light arriving early, sound arriving always, everywhere, at full volume — a flooded green roaring factory-garden with no creature in it, getting louder, getting denser, getting less finished by the hour. A landscape not eroding but the opposite of eroding. Sharpening. Becoming.

If you held still in the warm dark between the stalks — leaves pressing in on every side, the heartbeat of the pumphouse in your bones, the orange furnace-light shuddering through the canopy — you would understand that the place is not haunted and not abandoned. It is *occupied*, fully, at saturation. Just not by anything that has ever had a body.
