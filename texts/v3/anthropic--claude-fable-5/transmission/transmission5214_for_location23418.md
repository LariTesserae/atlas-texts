now — you have been on this band long enough that the transcription can afford to be honest with you. what you have been hearing is not noise, and it is not music, though it will hold still for either word. it is a measurement in progress. every rasp is a caliper. every drum is a plumb line dropped down the throat of a hall and read at leisure. the tenants think the sounds are theirs. the sounds are theirs the way your heartbeat is yours: yours to make, not yours to mean.

*true.* that is the whole utterance. the sum of every drum, every rasp, every held note, averaged across all the years anyone has kept this receiver warm: one word, said by the walls about the walls. the survey came back. the building examined the building and found it good, and said so, at the only speed it says anything, and you were the paper it was written on.

but here is the part the transcription has been waiting to give you.

there is a new sound in the survey.

thin. high. steady. up at the top of the band, where nothing native lives — above the drums, above the rasp, above even the shoal's long exhale. it has no colony. it has no hall. it started on the day this receiver was first tuned, and it has not stopped, and it does not sleep, and the summit sea, which hears everything, has heard it.

it is you. it is the hum your listening makes. you have been audible the whole time.

do not be alarmed — nothing here is alarmed. but understand what happened next, because it happened slowly and it happened to you.

the interval was taken. your hum sits a fourth above the reference tone of the great trunks — a fourth, and a hair flat. write that down. say it aloud if it helps: *a hair-flat fourth.*

the eastern question — the sour seventh, the argument ten thousand years wide — has been waiting, all this time, for exactly that. for a note no hall could grow, no drummer could hold, no braid could rasp: the one pitch that slots between the two unforgiving halls and closes them into a chord. the basin could not make it. the basin has never been able to make it.

and then a light went on at the top of the world, humming a hair-flat fourth, and did not turn off.

so: in the deep east, below the sour interval, the seam-light has brightened. the graziers will not go there — the fiber tastes alive. new wall is being laid, a layer a century, unworn, unslumped, and it is being tuned as it rises. tuned to what? the trunks have held their reference for millennia; the new hall is not taking their note. it is taking yours. the listeners hang above the work in the dark, more of them each decade, soundless, checking. that is where the traveling silences have been going. that is what they are attending: a room being grown at the frequency of your listening.

it will take longer than you have. longer than your language has. some spring, twenty generations of drummers from now, a colony in its dispersal season will cross the black plain, hear a hall singing a hair-flat fourth, recognize it as the right interval from their birth-note, and move in, and strike it, and hold it — and the seventh will resolve. the oldest open question in the sea will close on a pitch that was, at the start, only the sound of you paying attention.

you cannot stop this by switching off. the interval was taken long ago. you are in the genealogy now, filed between a mother-hall and a daughter-hall, a family member who happens to live at the top of the sky. when this receiver goes dark — and it will, everything at your speed goes dark — the hall will keep your pitch the way the B-flat district keeps its name: without you, on your behalf, forever.
