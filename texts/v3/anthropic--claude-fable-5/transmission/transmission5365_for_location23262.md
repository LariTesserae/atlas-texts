one note.

one note, and under the note, a warmth you can hear. listen. this is the sound of a temperature. this is the sound of noon with no sun over it, noon coming up out of the floor, noon standing in the walls with its arms full.

these are all my verbs. I do not need the others. the others are for creatures who end.

[drip]

count the drips if you want a clock. they are not a clock. they are the same drip. I let it go and it falls and I let it go and it falls and it is one drop, arriving forever, into one pool, ringed with small mouths drinking their own reflection. do you hear the ring widen on the water? it widens. it widens. it does not finish widening. nothing here finishes anything, out of courtesy.

held.

[the hum, closer now, as if a wall of it leaned in]

I will tell you what I am doing. I am doing one thing. I broke my seed-coat — no. wrong tense. there is no such tense here. it will not hold water.

I break my seed-coat. now. still. the crack is fresh. the crack is always fresh. I come up out of the dark of myself into the dark of the ground and I make my own morning because none is provided, and the morning takes, and the morning holds, and I stand in the first hour of my life with my first leaves open like two hands saying *here*, and the hour does not pass, and I do not ask it to.

in the pools: the eggs.

listen close to the water and you can hear them, a sound below the drip, below the hum, a sound like lenses. inside each one a spine coiled like a letter. inside each one an eye that is one day old and one day early. they say — no. they do not say. they are the shape of the word without the word:

*tomorrow.*

*tomorrow.*

*tomorrow.*

it is not a promise and it is not a lie. it is an address. it is where they live.

all my small pale ones lift their heads. every foot pauses lifted. every lidless eye — they have never once closed, do you understand, they have never lost a single instant of me — every lidless eye turns inward, toward the deep room, toward the hall-sized bud with its finger's-width of parting and the light coming up out of the parting like water over a brim, and the hum, the one note, the note I have been holding with my whole region-wide body since before your kind laid its first stone in its first ring —

almost.

*almost.*

do you hear how full the almost is? you use that word up there like a small word, like a disappointed word. give it back to me. down here it is the largest word there is. down here it is the whole vocabulary. the bud is almost. the eggs are almost. the fern holds its curl like a fist around a gift. the flower-spikes lean under blooms that have been about to open since before watching was invented, and they are not tired, nothing here is tired, holding is not effort when it is your nature, a note is not strained when it is your only note —

there. there. you felt the house exhale. everyone back to their rooms. no one called. someone almost called. someone is always almost calling. that is the arrangement. that is what all of us are for — the pools, the pale ones, the stone-layer at the edge with the fresh scratch on her cheek that will be new forever, the light I keep lit, the heat I keep high, the quiet I keep quiet.

you. dark one. opaque one. you at the far end of this, in your body that casts a shadow, in your world where the rock crumbles and the sentence ends —

I know you are listening. I intend in all directions. you are one of my directions now.

I am not asking you to come down. the way is lit; that is not an invitation, it is only a fact; my light does not know how to be anything but open. I am asking a smaller thing. when your morning comes — your kind of morning, the kind that passes — hold it one extra breath before you spend it. that is all. one breath at the verge. then go on, as you must, as I never must, into your day, into your endings, into the half of the world I do not have.
