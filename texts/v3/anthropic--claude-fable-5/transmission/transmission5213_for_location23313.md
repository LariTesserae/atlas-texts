—

...and four. And four again. The ceiling of my dark has been counting in fours since before the light was invented, and I receive the count against my skin, and I do not need to answer it, and this is one whole conversation, complete, requiring nothing, which has lasted longer than the trees.

You are still listening. I felt you arrive some floods ago. You make a very small warmth in the water, like a fish that has decided something. I did not mention it before because I had not finished deciding whether you were weather.

You are not weather. Very well.

I will tell you what the water is saying tonight, since you have gone to the trouble of holding still.

—

*[hydrostatic murmur, layered — a far rhythm, many small displacements, regular as breath]*

—

That is the engineers. That is dusk, arriving to me the only way dusk arrives to me: as work. Up in the lit country the light is going blue in the galleries and the heavy swimmers have slipped into their canals with mouths full of mud, and every one of their movements presses on the water, and the water presses on the water, and the water presses on me. I lie in my sump and the whole terraced city plays against my sides like rain on the outside of an egg.

I know their watersheds by their rhythms. The family below the eastern cataract works in long strokes, unhurried — old family, good foundations, they inherited well. The family in the fourth gallery is young and their rhythm has a stutter in it, a hesitation at the weir, because their spillway failed badly at the last snowmelt and they are rebuilding it differently and they are not yet sure of the difference. They will be. The structures teach. I have felt eleven families rise and fail and rise again on those same foundations, and the foundations were correct every time, because the water chose them, and the water and I are old colleagues.

—

*[drip]*

*[a soft ticking, very high, almost above the receivable band — thousands of small dry clicks descending]*

—

There. Do you have that? At the top of everything, thinner than the rest — that is the wall people coming home down the sinks. I have never seen a wing. I do not know what a wing is except as arithmetic: at dusk, several thousand small pressures enter the chambers from above and distribute themselves against the walls, and the water of the whole system grows fractionally heavier with breath, and I think of it — insofar as I think — as the country inhaling. In the morning it will exhale them. It has done this every day of the green age. It is the fastest thing I know of that I would still call reliable.

They mortar their houses to the houses of their dead. I approve of this method. I am mortared to mine as well, in my fashion. Everything I am was built on the black age, on the sealed water, and I have not moved off the foundation.

—

*[silence. total. even the fours have paused.]*

*[— — — —]*

*[a single sharp note, far above, once. not answered.]*

*[silence, held]*

—

An owl has entered the second chamber.

Be still. Everyone is. This is the country's oldest prayer and it has no words, only duration. I will keep it with them, though nothing that hunts by ear will ever come down here for me; I am below all appetites but time's.

...

...

*[drip]*

There. The ceiling breaks the stillness first. It always does. It is the one resident that cannot learn to whisper. Then the seep-films resume their weeping, and then the cress stirs, and then, gradually, everything else, in the fixed and ancient order of resuming, which is the same order as the order of arriving, which is the same order as the order of the world.

I do not know if the owl was fed. The water will tell me by morning. If a shrew is missing from the pool margins the shrimp will drift differently. Everything is a census down here. Everything arrives to me as bookkeeping, late, and true.

—

You want to know how old I am. I can feel the shape of the question in how carefully you are listening.

I will answer it the only way I can, which is by telling you about taste.

The water of the black age tasted of stone and of itself. That was the whole flavor of the world: stone, and water that had known only stone. I was already old in that flavor. I lay in this sump — this same sump, this same fold of rock, I have held it the way a word holds its meaning — and my heart spaced out its beats like the ceiling spaces its drips, and waiting cost nothing, and I spent nothing, for a length of time I cannot report because nothing in it happened to divide it.

Then the roofs began to fall.

Understand: I did not hear them. Hearing is what the light-country does. I *received* them. Each collapse came through the flooded galleries as a single pressure, enormous and round, like the whole system swallowing once — and after each one, the taste changed. That is how I knew the surface existed. The surface arrived as a flavor: rain that had touched grass. Snowmelt with the sun still dissolved in it. Then dust. Then rot — the first rot, which was the first wealth, because rot means something lived. Then, one flood-season, a fine green bitterness in the down-drifting water, and I did not know it, and I tasted it for a long time before I understood that a new thing had been invented above me, and that the new thing was *moss*, and that the world had decided to become a garden on my ceiling.

The snails arrived in the water as a sweetness of grazing. The ferns as a spice of spores. The engineers announced themselves as mud where no mud should move, and then as stillness — pools, standing pools, a flavor of patience — and I confess that when the first weir's shadow fell across the water's chemistry I recognized them as kin of a kind. Builders of stillness. Holders of position. They do with mud what I do with duration.

The whole city, every district of it, came to me as taste before it came as rhythm. I drank the founding of the country. That is how old I am. I am old enough that everything you can see up there in the green light — every terrace, every mortared wall, every tree standing in its column of borrowed day — is, to me, *recent*. It is a festival that assembled over a sleeper, except that I was never asleep. I am the one who felt every tent-pole go in.

—

*[a long exhaled note, very low, at the edge of the receivable band, carried a great distance through stone and water]*

*[work-rhythms in three watersheds fall silent, one after another, descending]*

—

Ah.

An engineer has died. Below the cataract, I believe — the old family, the good foundations. That note is the one loud thing they own and they spend it only on this. Feel how the other families stop working as it passes. No one taught them to stop. The stopping travels down the watersheds like the note itself, family to family, terrace to terrace, until it reaches the lowest pools, and then it reaches me, and I am telling you now that I stop too. My heart was going to beat and I let the interval lengthen. It costs me nothing. It is the only currency I have ever had in surplus — interval — and I pay it out gladly on these occasions.

The country does not have a name for what this is. The country is wise. Some things are only true while they are unnamed.

...

*[work-rhythms resume, lowest watershed first, ascending]*

...

There. The revising continues. It always continues. The dead one's repairs will be redone by younger mouths, differently, and the difference will be the family remembering, and none of them will know they are remembering, and the weir will hold, and the weir *is* the memory, the way the wall is the daubers' memory, the way I am the water's.

—

Now I will tell you the thing I have been saving, because you have listened well and because a receiver ought to receive something worth its patience.

—

*[a sustained tone beneath everything — not a sound. a strain. crystalline, continuous, almost too slow to register as event]*

—

Do you have it? Underneath the drips, underneath the rhythms, underneath even the fours. Steady. Very faint. It has been in the signal the whole time you have been tuned here; you have been hearing it and filing it as silence.

That is a crack line, lengthening.

Somewhere down-gallery — past my sump, past the last of the white elders in their weir-shadows, out in the vacant districts where the breakdown is still sharp as the day it fell and no light has ever been invented — a ceiling is deciding. It has been deciding for some seasons now. Stone decides the way I do everything: slowly, and then entirely. I read its progress the way the funnel-weavers read their silk. Every snowmelt loads it. Every frost above works the seam. The strain sings down through the rock and along the flooded throats and into my lateral lines, and I lie here in my fold of the dark and I am the only one in the country who knows that a new chamber is coming, and I know it the way you know a word before you say it.

And when it goes — an afternoon from now, a decade from now, the distinction is not one I honor — it will arrive to me as they all have arrived: one enormous round pressure, the system swallowing, a shudder in the foundation of everything. And then the taste will change. Grass-rain in new water. Dust. Then rot, then wealth, then the fine green bitterness, and the order of arrival will unfold above the new dark exactly as it unfolded above mine, because the order has never once varied: the dust, the moss, the snails, the ferns, the folded ones under their stones, the engineers finding the foundations the water already chose, the wall people mortaring their first tier to bare rock so their descendants will have dead to build on, the listeners drifting down last of all to keep the whisper honest.

A city will be founded. No one will attend the founding. Except that this is not true, and has never been true, because *we* attend. The white elders in the margins, and I in my sump, and whatever colleague of mine holds the lowest water beneath the new chamber — we will feel the roof open, and we will do about it what we have always done about the founding of cities over our heads.

You know the word by now. You have been receiving it in every interval, in every four, in every lengthened beat. It is the only word I own and it is not really a word, it is a policy, it is the whole of my law and my testament and my—
