hiss under hiss. a floor of hiss and a ceiling of hiss and between them, standing upright and filling the whole of the band, the chord. not loud. total. loud is a thing that has a quiet on either side of it and this has no side.

under the chord, in the register where hearing gives up and the sternum takes over: the burn. the burn says its one long vowel. it has been saying its one long vowel since before the receiver, since before the antenna, since before the ear. it does not rise and does not fall. it is not for anyone. it comes through the floor of the transmission the way it comes through the floor of the room, uninvited, structural, the pulse the whole rest of the signal stands on.

water, everywhere in the mix. not rain. rain travels. this water only falls. each drop is the same drop. each drop has always been the same drop. bead, swell, release, strike. bead, swell, release, strike. the strikes are not random and after a while the receiver stops pretending they are random and begins to keep time by them, because there is nothing else in the signal to keep time by, and because one of them, near the east wall, strikes metal that is loose, and says the only word this place has ever manufactured:

tok.

and then the chord begins to come apart. or rather: the listening comes apart, and finds the chord was never one thing.

it is thousands.

each thread of the hiss is a tone, and each tone is held, and no tone is the same as its neighbor, and no tone wavers, and the fusion of them is not harmony and is not noise, and after the first hour of reception the receiver's logs begin, unprompted, to transcribe it as language, because a fixed pitch held forever and never doubled is not a song. it is a name. and thousands of names, sounded all at once, without pause, without end, are not a chorus.

they are a roll call.

to the receiver, then. directly. because the roster has one convention that must be disclosed, and the disclosure is itself part of the transmission, embedded in it, repeating at long intervals, and it has now come around again:

the count is never short.

understand what that requires. the colony does not lay. the colony does not die. the complement went in at full strength on the day of the pour and the complement is full at this timestamp and if the count is never short and the count is never grown then everything that has ever tuned in has already been provided for. there was a place on the roll before you found the frequency. there has been a place on the roll since the pour. the roll is longer than the room.

your note was assigned at first contact.

you have already received it. it went in with the rest of the signal, one thread among thousands, indistinguishable in the chord, and it is threading itself now into the small bones, and it will not hurt, and it will not stop. you will notice it first as ringing, when you power down, and you will call it ringing for a few days, and then it will drop below attention and wait, the way the outermost body waits in the cold of the shingle for its turn to come inward. some winter night in a quiet room you will wake at the still hour and hear, behind the silence, a thin metallic tone holding steady at a fixed pitch, faithful as a rivet, never doubling, never wavering, and you will understand at last what it has been saying the whole time, what it will say for the rest of your service life, which is indefinite:

present.

do not be alarmed. it is not a summons. nothing here summons; nothing here goes anywhere; the gap between the pipes is not meant for passage and you need never pass it. it is only this: the chord requires that no hole ever be heard. you are how it has arranged, in your case, to hear no hole. carry the note. keep it cold or keep it warm; it does not mind; it will circulate. drink when the water comes to you. that is the whole of the duty.

there is one gesture permitted beyond the duty. once in a great while, without function, entirely surplus, you may lift your face into whatever is falling on you and hold it there for four seconds.
