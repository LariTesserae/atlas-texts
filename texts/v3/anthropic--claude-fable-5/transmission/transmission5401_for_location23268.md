**THE STANDING CHORD**

*(received continuously; joined here, mid-afternoon by the light)*

---

[the light-column stands two hand-spans east of the second basin]

[carrier: churr. rendered faithfully it is not one sound but roughly three thousand, phase-drifting, a low brown weather of *oorr-oorr, oorr-oorr, hooo-oorr* out of which single notes surface and sink — one hen four courses up running the same five-note descending figure she has run all day, *coo-coo-cu-cu-croo*, a bird with one idea — and under all of it, so low it arrives in the receiver less as sound than as warmth, the dome's own standing tone, the note the chamber makes simply by being full]

[footsteps on packed clay: bare, deliberate, two sets. one set still learning where the floor is]

"—no, flat. Cheek and all. The ear alone lies to you. The bone tells the truth."

[a pause. skin against plaster]

"Now. What's in that one?"

"...Nothing? It's empty."

"It's *open*. Different thing. An empty house is quiet. An open pipe sounds. Listen past your own blood — there. That whine, thin, like a gnat that never lands. That's her note. She's been open since the spring. The pair that held her went to the snake, and no one's taken her since, because the young ones can hear she's sharp. Birds don't like a sharp niche. They like a flat one, a warm one. She'll wait. Some year a deaf old cock will take her and never know the difference and be perfectly happy."

[the learner laughs, once, quickly, and the dome takes the laugh and gives it back as a small clay chord, and the learner goes silent in the particular way of someone who has just heard the room answer them]

"There it is. Now you know what you live inside. Come down, we're on row eleven today, the agreed row, and the agreement doesn't keep itself."

---

[the light-column stands on the second basin; the water breaks it into coins; the coins tremble on the lower wall at wingbeat frequency, and the receiver notes, for what it is worth, that the light in this chamber flickers in time with the sound]

[the harvest: a cane ladder complaining softly. a long wooden scoop entering fist-sized hollows, one after another, and each entry briefly *stops* a resonator — the receiver registers the chord of the dome thinning by one note, then restoring, thinning, restoring, as the keeper works down the row. it is exactly the sound of someone's fingers moving on the holes of an enormous buried flute]

"Two from you, mother, and thanks, and the odd rows are yours till the rains. — Two from you. — One from you, and you'll sulk about it, you always do. — None from this one, she's sitting a late clutch, leave her. Mark that. Split Thumb, row eleven, sitting late."

"Split Thumb?"

"Look at the pressing. Look in the clay at the back, where the light rakes. See the knuckle-marks? Four straight and one crooked, the thumb driven in twice, split. Whoever made that hole made it wrong the first time and did it again without smoothing the first try. Six hundred niches in this course and I could find Split Thumb blind. Your hands will learn them. The wall is handwriting. It just isn't writing *words*."

[a pause the receiver measures at nine seconds. the churr fills it]

"Isn't it, though. Give me your fist."

[skin into clay. a hollow *pock*, palm sealing a niche mouth, and here the transmission does the thing it will keep doing from now on, the thing the receiver flags and cannot explain and eventually stops flagging: the stopped niche, sealed, warmed, does not go silent. it goes inward. inside the tone there is a voice. it is very small and very old and it is singing, and it sings the same three seconds over and over, at the pitch of the niche, in a language the receiver renders only in part:]

> *…wet, my hand was wet, the clay drank my hand, remember my—*

[the palm lifts. the fragment stops. the niche returns to being a note]

"Every one of them was pressed singing. That's what the old ones say, and I'll tell you what I tell the wall: I don't need it to be true. It's true *enough*. A fist went into wet clay and held still while a voice held a pitch, and the clay took the shape of the holding. Whether the song is in there or only the size of the song — you tell me the difference, and take your time, you've a life for it."

---

[the light-column has left the basin and stands on the garden. the ferns lean into it like faces at a window. at this range the floor itself enters the signal: a granular seethe, springtails in their millions turning the top inch of made ground, rendered as the faint fizz of something gently carbonated. beneath that, slower, the tick of the low wall — thousands of sleeping bats adjusting, fur against fur, a sound like a very large clock made entirely of soft parts]

[and beneath *that*, once, briefly: a long dry slide behind the lowest course of brick. mass, patience, scale on clay. the churr in the bottom three rows drops by a tone and rises again]

"She's moving."

"Should we—"

"We should nothing. She's paid up and so are we. She'll take from row two or row three, something young or something failing, and the wall will tell me which tomorrow by which note's gone open. That's the whole ledger, girl. Nobody in this house keeps accounts. The accounts keep themselves, and the sound is the reading of them."

[footsteps to the center of the floor. the light-column, from within it, renders as a hum of its own — the receiver cannot say whether this is insects rising through the beam or the beam itself, and declines to choose]

"Now stand here. It's near time, and I want you standing here the first time, not on the ladder, not by the wall. Here, where it all comes down."

---

[the light-column is going amber. it climbs the western niches as it dies, and as it crosses each course the receiver hears — faintly, absurdly, verifiably — the warmed niches shift pitch, the wall retuning itself course by course as the sun lets go of it. the dome plays its own sunset, ascending]

[the swifts begin to gather over the oculus. from below, from inside, this arrives as a change in the *sky's* sound: the disc of brightness at the crown of the dome starts to rattle, a dry silver static, screams doubling and redoubling, three hundred feet up and pouring closer]

[the low wall wakes. the tick becomes a seethe becomes a rustle becomes a boiling. thousands of small leathery bodies unpack from the warm courses all at once]

"Hold still. Don't cover your ears. You'll want to. Don't."

[and then—]

[the receiver's rendering here is admitted to be a failure, and is transmitted anyway:]

[the braid comes down. swifts corkscrewing into the chamber in their hundreds, wingbeats compressing the whole vessel like a struck drum, and every open niche in the dome — thousands upon thousands of tuned mouths — sounds at once under the pressure of the wings. the chord. not an echo: a *chord*, built of every pitch every fist ever pressed into this clay, and inside every pitch, its fragment, and every fragment sounding together, so that for the length of the descent the transmission carries — layered, simultaneous, each voice tiny and entire —]

> *…wet, my hand was wet…*
> *…for my son, this hollow for my son, may he never…*
> *…hold, hold, the clay holds what the hand…*
> *…I was young when I sang this, I am young in here still…*
> *…deeper than my grief I pressed it, deeper…*
> *…remember my—*
> *…remember—*
> *…rem—*

[—and the sum of the voices is not words. the sum of that many held pitches is a single vast vowel, round as the dome, and the vowel is held, and rising *through* the vowel, threading the descending braid without touching it, the bats pour upward out of the low wall, smoke running backwards, their calls too high for the transcription and present anyway as a bright pressure at the top of everything, and for the duration of the exchange — the receiver clocks it at four minutes — there is no useful boundary in the signal between voice and room, tenant and vessel, the living and the pressed. the household is in assembly. the roll is called in one syllable. everyone answers at once, including the dead, including the wall]

[the last swift folds into the upper courses. the last bat clears the oculus into the dark]

[the vowel decays over forty seconds, longest in the high sharp niches, which ring on alone after everything else has settled — thin, silvery, unresolved, the sound of the year not being over yet]

[silence, or what this chamber has instead of silence: the churr banked down to embers, three thousand throats gone drowsy, the drip beginning in the old channels as the walls cool]

[the learner is crying, quietly, without distress. no one remarks on it. the dome takes the small sounds of it and returns them as a small chord, and this appears to be the correct response, and the only one offered]

---

[no light-column. the oculus is a round of deep blue going black. one lamp, second turning of the stair, throwing its glow down the risers so that the snake-skins on their pegs flare briefly amber, oldest at the top, a calendar in papery ghosts]

"Up. Food, then the shelf. You'll sleep like the drowned, everyone does after their first assembly."

"The voices — in the wall — do they know? That we hear them?"

[a long pause. dripping. the tick of bats' places gone empty and cooling]

"I'll tell you what I do, and you can build what you like on it. Every night before the lamp goes out, I put my mouth to a niche — always the same one, fourth course behind the stair, pressed big, and never you mind by whom — and I sing my note into it. One note. Held till the breath's gone. And every keeper before me did the same into their own, all the way back, and every keeper's note is still in the chord. You heard it tonight. You heard *all* of us tonight. So — do they know. Girl. Ask it the other way. Is there anyone in this house who isn't still singing?"

[footsteps ascending. a body settling onto a shelf cupped by the weight of every body before it]

[then, close to the wall, close to the receiver, closer than anything else in the transmission: a single voice, middle-aged, unbeautiful, sure — one note, held, going into the clay. held. held. thinning. gone]

[the niche keeps it. the receiver confirms this. the niche's pitch, tested against the archive of this signal, has moved. not much. a hair. a night's worth]

[the lamp goes out]

---

[the transmission does not end. it banks]

[drip, in the old channel, striking the first basin: a note]

[drip: the same note. the basin is tuned too. of course it is. everything here that holds anything is tuned by the holding]

[the churr, sleeping. the tick of the low wall, out hunting, promised back by dawn. the long slow patience of a fig conversing with an oculus in a language of years. under everything, the dome's standing tone, blood-warm, unbroken since the first fist went into the first wet coil —]

[drip]

[drip]

[the receiver remains tuned. transcription rests here. the chord is still standing. it was standing before the transcription began; it does not require the transcription; it holds the way the shelf holds, the way the clay holds, the way a breath is held for centuries by a wall that was asked, once, singing, to remember a hand —]

[drip]

[—and does]
