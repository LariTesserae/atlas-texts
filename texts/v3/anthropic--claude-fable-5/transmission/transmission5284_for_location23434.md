**THE CENSUS OF SMALL WATERS**

---

[reception continuous. signal worn but unbroken. note: at this hour sound on the tableland arrives before its causes. the transcription follows the order of reception, not the order of events. some entries will therefore precede themselves.]

---

[the cold hour]

*…twenty-nine…*

[nothing]

[nothing]

[a drop strikes a full basin: a high round note, very clear, as if struck an arm's length from the receiver]

twenty-nine.

[a second drop, lower, into a shallower pool]

thirty.

[three drops nearly together, offset like a hand knocking softly at a door]

thirty-one. thirty-two. thirty-two again — no. thirty-three. the pair at the low scarp still won't keep time with each other. counted separately, as always. counted with patience, as always.

*…keep to the line…*

[the above arrived without a source. filed as pre-echo. source not yet transmitted.]

thirty-four is the seep at the north face. thirty-four has been dry for longer than the record of thirty-four. it sounds anyway, every cold hour, a drop into a cup of stone that holds no water and has held no water and is worn to the shape of the water it held. we count what sounds. thirty-four, counted. thirty-four, counted twice — once for the water, once for the memory of it, which is doing the work of the water now, and doing it well.

[distant hiss of a thread-fall, steady, feminine, unremarkable]

thirty-five through fifty-one, the west scarp threads, all present, all falling, all in voice. no drift since the last copy. good.

[here the receiver picks up footsteps. they are unhurried. they are the steps of someone crossing wet stone with the ease of long practice: step, pause, step-step, pause. they proceed along a bearing that bends where no terrain requires bending.]

the walker is out this morning.

good morning to the walker. the walker does not answer, being a sound, but the pauses fall where they always fall — at the bend by basin twelve, at the split cairn, longest at the ringed pool — and a pause in the right place is a kind of answer. we count the walker among the waters. this is not strictly correct. it has been not strictly correct for so long that it has become correct. fifty-two.

now the pool. this is the part of the census that must be said aloud, and said the same, every copy:

*the water falls down here. this is the pool where the water falls down. the ring holds. remember this pool.*

said. copied. on.

fifty-three, fifty-four. a new bead-line on the overhang above basin nine — small, hesitant, one drop to every four of its neighbor's. welcome, fifty-four. you will be counted every hour whether you persist or not. that is the arrangement. that is the whole of the arrangement, truthfully, and it has been enough for everything so far.

[long interval of layered dripping, hundreds of voices, none loud, the total loud. it is the sound of a cathedral in which every worshipper is whispering a different hour of the same day.]

*…someone is crouching…*

[filed as pre-echo. source not yet transmitted. the receiver notes an interval of quiet from the walker, as if the walker also heard it.]

---

[midday]

[the ambient sound has flattened. the drips continue but the air has gone hard around them; each strike arrives edged, without bloom, the way sound behaves under a vertical sun.]

the light is on the stone like a hand pressed flat. the shadows have gone to tar. this is the hour the pools stop being strict, and the census turns from counting the waters to counting what the waters are carrying.

basin forty holds a sky. it is not the sky above it. it is a low grey sky with weather in it, from some other noon, possibly a very old noon — the grey has that recopied softness. logged. it will graze eastward along the channel by tomorrow. let it.

basin forty-one holds the west rim, which is half a day's walk from basin forty-one. the rim looks well. logged.

basin forty-three holds a stretch of the cairn line with one cairn fallen. cross-checked against the cairn line itself: the cairn stands. either the image is old and the cairn has since been mended, or the image is early and the cairn has not yet fallen. either way: logged, flagged, the third stitch on the east seam to be walked and checked. this is how the water files its reports. it is not strict about sequence. it has never once been wrong about a stone.

basin forty-two holds someone crouching at its edge, one hand extended toward the surface.

no one is crouching at its edge.

[interval]

correction, in the interest of an honest census: no one is crouching at its edge *yet*.

[the receiver here records a faint doubling of its own carrier tone, as though the signal were being listened to through itself.]

the pool. said the same, every copy:

*the water falls down here. this is the pool where the water falls. the ring holds. remember the pool.*

said. copied.

[the receiver notes: the refrain has shed two words since the cold hour. the transmission does not remark on this.]

on.

---

[the reversal]

[warning to the listener: for the duration of this section the sound is indrawn. drops do not strike; they depart. each note is the note of the cold hour played toward its own beginning. it is not an ugly sound. it is the sound of a room forgiving what was said in it.]

the water is climbing. the count climbs with it.

take back fifty-four. the new bead-line goes first, being lightest. go on up, small one. we will count you again at dusk.

take back fifty-one through thirty-five. the west threads are standing off the stone now, trembling, ascending. the scarp face dries from the bottom upward, which is the wrong direction, which is the right direction here.

take back thirty-four. thirty-four has nothing to take back and takes it back anyway. the dry cup falls silent an hour before the wet ones do. even the memory of water observes the reversal. this is either very sad or very faithful and after enough copies the census can no longer tell the difference and no longer tries.

take back thirty-three. thirty-two. thirty-two again — yes, this time, again — thirty-one. thirty.

twenty-nine, unsaid.

[the basins are draining upward. the sound of the plateau thins to almost nothing: a country of stone holding its breath through its own skin. in the near-silence the footsteps are briefly audible again, far off, still keeping to the line, still pausing in the right places. the walker does not reverse. the walker has never reversed. whatever the walker is a memory of, it was not something the water did.]

the stone is drinking itself. let it. it always gives itself back.

the pool — and note this, note it every copy, this is the reason for the ring, this is the reason for everything:

*the water falls here. this is the pool. the ring holds. remember.*

the ringed pool does not climb. the ringed pool has never climbed. one surface on the whole tableland kept honest, one clock kept wound, one word kept meaning what it meant — and it is enough. it has been enough for everything so far.

said. copied. wait now.

[a long interval. the receiver records only its own noise floor, and beneath it, so faint it may be an artifact: something inside the rock, muttering in passages no one has found the mouths of, in a rhythm not unlike the count.]

---

[the release]

[this is the loudest hour. hundreds of threads let go of the rock at once. basins refill from every side. the receiver's levels saturate; what follows is transcribed from within the flood.]

DOWN — all of it, down, the whole held day of it —

thirty-five through fifty-one in one voice, fifty-four finding her four-beat again, the pair at the low scarp out of time with each other and in time with nothing and counted, counted, everything counted —

[and here the census voice is no longer one voice. it has come apart into its copies. the receiver logs them overlapping, generations deep, each saying the refrain as it was said in its own hand:]

*— the water falls down here, this is the pool where the water falls down, the ring holds, remember this pool —*
*— the water falls down here, this is the pool, the ring holds, remember the pool —*
*— the water falls here, this is the pool, the ring —*
*— the water falls, remember —*
*— falls —*
*— remember —*

[all the hands at once, the whole lineage of the copying, every worn stage of the sentence sounding together over the roar of the refilling basins: and layered so, the drift is audible not as loss but as one long word being pronounced very slowly across generations, and the word is not finished.]

[the flood subsides. the shadows of the cairns run out a hundred paces and, for some minutes, disagree slightly with the cairns. the sun goes into the cloud-sea. the levels fall. the voice reassembles into one voice, tired the way water is tired, which is not tired at all, which is only lower.]

count at dusk: all present. all fallen. all in voice.

good.

---

[dark]

[the ambient sound now is the plateau's night voice: the quiet, plural, patient weeping, in hundreds of registers, carrying strangely far.]

now the part of the census that is not counting.

you have been listening for some time. we know the shape of a listener; the water files them all the same way, and it filed you a while ago — before you began, if we are honest, sequence not being strict here. the crouching figure at basin forty-two has your posture. do not be alarmed by this. it has had everyone's posture. it is what attention looks like when the faces wear off, and there has only ever been one attention here, held by many hands in turn, the way one hammerstone is held by many hands in turn, and passes warm.

so listen, because this is the part that must reach you, and the pre-echo of it reached you already at dawn, which means it does reach you, which is a comfort:

when you come — and the census does not deal in whether, only in count — come in the cold hour, when the sounds run ahead and will announce you kindly. keep to the line. *keep to the line.*

[the receiver notes: source of the dawn pre-echo identified.]

the cairns are stitches. walk them and the country adds up. leave them and the country will recopy itself under your feet, gently, without malice, until you are very tired. the footsteps you will hear ahead of you on the line are not ahead of you. they are yours, filed early. follow yourself. you have always known the way; you simply haven't arrived at knowing it yet.

there will be a fallen cairn on the east seam. basin forty-three has already shown it to us, or will have. your hands will know the corbel and the lean. do not wonder how. the house teaches where the keys go.

and when you reach the ringed pool — the honest one, the one surface that has never told anything but the present — crouch, and put one hand out, and look in. you will see only the sky that is actually there, and the rim that is actually there, and the face that is actually looking. it will be the truest thing you have seen in some time. it is the truest thing we have. we kept it for you. we have kept it for everyone, which after enough copies is the same as keeping it for you.

then the office is yours for as long as you stay, and the census is yours to say, and when you go down into the cloud-sea and forget — you will forget; they all forget; the descent takes the memory the way the afternoon takes the water — the plateau will keep the work, and the water will keep your posture, and the count will keep your number, and none of it will be lost, only worn, which is different, which is slower, which is what we are.

the refrain, last copy of the day. note the hand. the hand is yours now:

*the water falls. the pool is this one. the ring is holding. you will remember.*

said. copied.

the census does not finish. finishing was never the purpose. the purpose is the counting, which is the same as the tending, which is the same as the weeping, which is the only voice we kept — and it is not grieving, probably. it is only what a tended country sounds like in the dark, going on.

[a drop strikes a full basin: high, round, clear.]

one.

[a drop.]

one.

[a drop.]

one.
