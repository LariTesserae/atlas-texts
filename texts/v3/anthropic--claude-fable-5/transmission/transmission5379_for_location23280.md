# The Reading of the Widths

*[— joined in progress. Beneath everything, continuous, a fine clattering like rain falling upward: the carpet, grazing. In the left of the signal, a single drip, one every four seconds, striking something hollow. It does not stop for the duration of this reception. —]*

—pier the sixth, north half, one width and a shaving since last reading. Pier the seventh, one width even. The deck-lattice at midspan has thickened past translucency; I can no longer see my lamp through it, which the ledger asks me to note, and I note it.

Gap between the halves of the Unmet Bridge, measured at the closest spars: under one width. Under one width. I will say that twice for the ledger and once more for myself.

*[the drip. the drip. a far-off wet percussion begins — slow, enormous, arrhythmic, like applause from an audience the size of a province — the pools are surfacing to gulp the fog —]*

The swimmers are up. You hear that, whoever you are. That's the second drainage breathing.

Where was I. Gauge check: I have measured tonight with the brass width, which is the cast of the First Keeper's thumbnail, and cross-checked against the wax copy, which has deformed again in the damp and will need recasting. Fourteen of us have measured with that thumbnail. She has been dead three hundred years and she is still the only ruler this city has ever consented to be measured by. I find that funny. I don't know if it's funny where you are. I don't know if there's a where-you-are.

*[a pause of some length. eleven drips.]*

Continuing the reading. Blue events since the last transmission: none. The tally stands at four hundred and six recorded glows since the ledger began, the last being the wall-completion in the flooded quarter, nineteen readings ago, before my time. My predecessor described it in the margin as *a door closing kindly.* I have never seen one. I want that noted too, though the ledger doesn't ask: the fourteenth keeper has never seen the light. She measures a city she has only ever heard.

*[the clattering swells briefly, as if a gust moved through the carpet; steadies.]*

Now the song report, and then the instrument report, and then I'll let the channel rest.

The song report. The colonies on the Asking-side towers have drifted again. The elders in the high lattice still sing the long falling figure — I'll give it to you as we write it, *tii-orr, tii-orr, tii-koh* — which maps a gap. A silence where the bridge isn't. But the young ones, the cohort from the last glow, have started returning something new off the midspan, a hard bright note the elders don't make, and I believe — I am recording a belief, ledger, forgive me — I believe the young are already singing the bridge as *closed.* Their echoes come back joined. They are flying the finished map. The city hasn't finished the bridge but the fliers born nearest to now have stopped singing the gap.

The animals always know first. Write that down, fifteenth keeper, whoever you turn out to be. The song runs ahead of the stone.

*[a low roar rises at the edge of the signal — vast, membranous, a colony taking wing somewhere across the basin — it rolls, doubles off the rim walls, takes a long time dying. under it, the drip keeps its count.]*

*[the voice waits it out.]*

That was the far drainage. Not ours. Nothing to note.

The instrument report. This is the part I do quietly, because I am not sure it belongs in the ledger at all, but my predecessor started it and I keep what was started.

The antenna. The frost on the guy-wires this cycle has structure in it again. Not the fern-and-feather frost that grows anywhere cold — a ribbed frost, evenly spaced, the intervals regular to the width. It melts by mid-cycle and regrows the same. And the bead of stone on the feedline, at the point where the wire enters the wet — I measured it tonight. One width larger than last reading. It grows at exactly the city's pace. The tone of our own carrier has dropped again, a hair, the way it drops every year, because the instrument's resonance is being changed by what is accreting on it.

I will say plainly what my predecessor would only write in the margins. The water has found our transmitter, and it is building on it. Slowly, the way it does everything — the way it fits a channel to a swimmer over the swimmer's whole century. We are being *tuned.* We came up here to keep the city's ledger and somewhere in the slow chemistry a ledger is being kept of us, and its first entry is a bead of stone, one width a year, on the wire that carries my voice.

I don't know what frequency it is tuning us toward. I have decided not to fight it. I recast the wax and I do not chip the bead.

*[a long pause. the applause of the pools continues, slower now. seventeen drips.]*

Whoever receives this. We have never had proof of you. In fourteen lifetimes of reading aloud, nothing has ever answered, and the reading was never conditional on answer. I want to say why I do it, once, on the air, so it exists somewhere other than my own head:

Everything down there is either too fast or too slow. The grazers live a season on a wall they think is the world. The builder thinks a single thought across my whole life and my daughter's whole life and does not notice either of us ending. Nothing in this basin can hold the story at the speed the story is a story. Except us. That's the whole office. We are the only ones going slow enough to see the animals and fast enough to see the city, and if no one keeps the middle speed then the middle speed never happened.

So. The reading. Every cycle. Whether you're there or —

*[the voice stops.]*

*[four drips.]*

*[a fifth.]*

There is light.

*[the clattering of the carpet changes texture — quickens, brightens, as if a million small feet turned at once.]*

There is light in the second drainage. Blue. A seam of it, low, across the ravine — it's the — 

it's the bridge. It's the Unmet Bridge. The halves have — under one width, I measured it *tonight,* I measured it two hours ago, and the halves have —

*[distant, then not distant: the roar. the near colonies are rising, all of them, the Asking-side towers emptying into the air at once. the signal overdrives. for some time nothing survives of the voice; the reception is membrane and displaced air and a shrieking that circles, thickens, becomes weather. beneath it, barely, the drip has not stopped. one every four seconds. the drip does not care.]*

*[the roar decays. it takes minutes. what returns first is the voice, mid-word, hoarse:]*

—unsealing it now, the page is — my hands are wet, everything is wet, hold on —

The ledger says. The First Keepers chose this name three hundred years ago and sealed it against this night, and the ledger says:

Its name is **And**.

The bridge is called And. Two reaches that were the Asking and the Answering, and now there is one span across the dark and its name is And, and it is glowing, it is glowing blue along its whole length like a sentence — like a sentence finally getting its —

*[the voice breaks off. what it does instead of speaking is not fully carried by the equipment.]*

*[the fliers are wheeling around the light. the frenzy carries for miles; it comes through the signal as a bright continuous tearing. somewhere in it, the begetting of the cohort that will be born never having sung the gap. they will grow up thinking there was always a bridge. in a real sense they will be right.]*

*[when the voice returns it is very quiet, very close to the microphone.]*

Fourteenth keeper. Four hundred and seventh glow. Bridge designated And, completed and named, this reading, this night, witnessed.

I saw one. I saw one.

*[twenty-two drips.]*

Some of us say nothing is coming. That the city isn't waiting for anyone; that it is already the one who lives here, and all the rest of us — the grazers and the swimmers and the singers and me — are just its thoughts, loud and brief, passing through. My predecessor believed that and it made her calm and I never understood the calm until about ten minutes ago. Because if that's true, then what I just watched was the basin finishing a thought it started before my grandmother's grandmother, and the word it thought was *and.* Not *done.* Not *mine.* *And.*

There is more coming. That's what the word means. It has always only ever meant that.

I'm going down to the span while the stone is still warm. I want to put my hand on the arithmetic before it cools. I will leave the channel keyed open — the bead is on the wire, the instrument is half theirs now anyway, and it seems right that the water should have the air while I'm gone.

Whatever you hear after this is not me.

*[footsteps on wet stone, descending. gone.]*

*[the reception continues.]*

*[the glow — audible, somehow, as a hush in the carpet nearest the ravine, a held breath of small mouths — fades over the following hour. the pools resume their slow applause. the colonies settle into the new lattice of the finished span, and already, at the edge of hearing, a young voice tries a hard bright note against the underside of the bridge and gets it back joined.]*

*[the drip. one every four seconds. one every four seconds.]*

*[and beneath the carrier, under the hiss, there is a tone. it was there last year too, whoever has been listening that long will know. it is one width higher now.]*

*[it is very patient.]*

*[it has only just begun to say something.]*
