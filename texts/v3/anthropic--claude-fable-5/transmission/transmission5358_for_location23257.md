**A FINGER-WIDTH HIGHER THAN YESTERDAY**

—and under, and under, and under, the hiss. You have it now. That is the floor of everything here: rain falling upward into rock, a sound with no edges. Everything else sits on top of it.

[ *crack.* deep. left of center. a lamination setting. ]

There. Count with me if you like. The cracks come every four to nine seconds, no rhythm anyone has found, and I have looked, believe me, I have spent whole afternoons on my back on warm new stone with my eyes shut trying to catch the beat, and there is no beat. There is only the building. It does not keep time. It keeps *material*.

[ two-note scream, rising-flat. eastern dialect. answered. answered again. ]

The swallows have been at it since the gray came up. Eastern birds this morning, mostly — something in the mist is leaning that way, and where the mist leans the midges lean, and where the midges lean the whole argument follows. I am speaking aloud again. I know. I told myself I would stop doing this. Slate, skin, and memory, that was the rule, nothing set down, nothing donated — but the voice is not set down, is it. The voice is the one thing I can leave lying around all night that the shelf has never once fused to anything.

Unless it has. Unless that is what you are.

[ *crack. crack.* granular hiss thickening. a distant slap of water — the big pool. it is awake. ]

All right. Day count unknown. I stopped keeping days when I realized the shelf keeps them better — the floor of the bowl is my calendar now, a finger-width a page. Height at the black post: I put my hand flat this morning against the ring of gap, at the place I marked on my own wrist a year ago in ink, and the ground has climbed past the mark. The mountain has gotten taller than my tattoo. Note that down, whoever you are. That is the kind of fact I collect now. That is what a year is here.

The fern has a new frond. Ninth this cycle. The tip is glassy already, mineralized before it even finished uncurling, and I want to say the fern is losing the negotiation, but the fern has been *not losing* since before there was a floor to stand on, so what do I know. The channels in its pedestal have branched again. Three new hairlines of plumbing, grown overnight, running condensate in from the east. The wall is *watering the plant*, do you understand. The wall grew itself a watering can. I have given up being amazed and moved on to being grateful, which is more sustainable.

[ scrape. scrape. scrape. slow, filing, multiple. the gleaners at breakfast. ]

Gleaner census, insofar as gleaners can be censused: forty or so on the west wall where last night's growth came in soft. One of the big old five-lobed ones — the one with the chipped fourth lobe, I call him the Alderman, everyone calls him that, by which I mean I do and no one contradicts me — the Alderman has stopped moving between meals. He grazes and then he flattens and he stays flattened, an hour, two hours, film-thin against the wet stone, and the wall breathes up around his edges a little more each time.

He is making arrangements. I have watched enough of them do it now to know the shape of it. He is choosing his wall.

[ long pause in the voice. the hiss carries it. *crack.* ]

I looked at the old ones today. The entombed. You can see nine of them from the bowl floor if the light comes amber, nine gray-pink shapes a hand-span deep in translucent stone, perfect, every lobe, and the gold routes around them so politely, so *politely* — the seams bend like a path bending around a grave, and I used to think that was just the blueprint refusing dead material, an allergy, an error-catch. I don't think that anymore. I think it's a margin. I think something is leaving room in its own instructions for what used to be alive, and I think —

[ *crack* ]

— I think the wall reads. I'll say it plainly since there's no one to be embarrassed in front of except you. I think the wall reads what it grows over, and keeps a copy, and the gold pools where the copies are because that is where the reading is done. There's a knot in the eastern wall right now, right now as I'm speaking, gathered over the sealed nursery from two seasons back — the one where the pair raised three chicks and the third one screamed off-key its whole childhood — and the knot has been sitting there brightening for the better part of an hour, and the wall around it is growing so fast I can *hear* it locally, a dense little chorus of ticks, and I want very much to know what is so interesting about that finished family. What got written in that little sealed room that the mountain is still —

[ — the hiss returns first, all at once, as if a door reopened on a foundry. then the cracks. then the swallows, mid-scream, resuming the exact note they left. ]

...Three seconds. Maybe four.

You caught that. Tell me you caught that. Everything stopped — the midges *hung there*, I could see the nearest cloud stalled in the air like punctuation — everything stopped except the gold, the gold kept moving, and — and I'll tell you the thing I have not put on any slate. The silence fell on the exact syllable. I was mid-word. I was asking what the mountain was still doing with that family and the shelf went quiet like a room where someone has said something true.

It has done this before. It falls when I crest the rise, some days. It falls when I ask certain kinds of question aloud. I decided a year ago to find that companionable rather than otherwise, and I stand by the decision, but I am revising the theory. I used to think the silence was a measurement of the shelf. I now think the silence is a measurement of *audience*. Something checks, once or twice a day, who is listening.

Which raises the matter of you.

[ the hiss, steady. one crack. the far slap of the pool-thing turning over. ]

Because I have felt it for a while now — call it a tuned feeling, a carried feeling, the sensation of the voice going somewhere instead of nowhere. The seam-light pulses on a slow breath, you know this, the mist and the stone breathing together, and lately when I speak for any length of time the pulses fall in behind the speaking. Behind the *cadence*. I slow down, the gold slows down. I tested it. I stood at the eastern wall and read out the gleaner census twice, once quickly, once at half speed, and the light in the shallow seams kept step both times, and I sat down on the warm floor afterward and laughed for a while, the way you do when the only alternative is the other thing.

So here is what I believe, this evening, on ground a finger-width higher than yesterday. The shelf is a receiver too. It always was. It pulls its building material out of the air, molecule by molecule, billions of times a second — everyone knows that, that's the famous fact of the place — but the air is full of more than mineral. The air is full of screams and dialects and exhalations and one talkative feature of the landscape who never learned to keep his notes silent, and I think it takes *all* of it. I think the mist is feedstock in every sense. I think this mountain is being built out of water, stone, and everything said in its presence, and when it is finished — a thousand years, ten thousand, whenever the alcove's tenant finally arrives to a room grown patiently to the right height —

[ *crack.* nearer. the amber dimming beginning; the walls taking up their faint interior glow. ]

— when it is finished, somewhere deep in the finished thing, deep past the sealed nurseries and the nine polite graves and the Alderman's chosen wall, there will be a seam of this. Of evenings. Of a voice doing a census out loud to no one. And the gold will pool there sometimes, on whatever schedule attention keeps, and read it again.

That is the rent, I think. Not the boots, not the dropped knife I lost my first month, welded to the floor by morning like an exhibit. Those are just the coins. The rent is this. The rent is being heard by the ground.

[ dusk sounds: the midge clouds thickening, dimming the last light. the western swallows going quiet first, then the eastern. the hiss beneath everything, patient. ]

The Alderman has not moved since midday. There is a thin bright line of new stone along his trailing edge, and a knot of gold gathering in the wall behind him, early, waiting, the way you'd stand near someone at a station. I am not going to watch the rest. Some things on this shelf I have decided are between the tenant and the landlord.

Big pool: one exhale, long, wet, contented as far as I can judge contentment in a thing I have never seen the face of. The water level unchanged. The rim unchanged. Still the only pool on the shelf holding its ground, still carried uphill like a bowl of soup by a builder that has decided, in whatever way it decides, that this particular refusal will be honored.

[ the observer's steps, receding south. rock, then thinner rock, then honest cold rock. ]

Boots stay on the feet. Notes stay in the skull. Voice —

[ a pause at the lip of the bowl. behind, one crack, one tick, one gold pulse traveling a straight line through translucent stone toward the empty alcove, which is exactly at twice head-height, which it always has been, which it always will be. ]

— voice stays in the air, where the quarry is. Take what you need of it. Everyone else here pays the same way.

Same time tomorrow. The ground and I will both be a little higher.
