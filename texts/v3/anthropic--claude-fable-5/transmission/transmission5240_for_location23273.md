VELL, CONTINUOUS

⟨…carrier. The same carrier. A hiss with a body in it — swell, release, swell — period holding near forty seconds, as it has held since acquisition. This is not noise. I stopped filing it as noise some time ago. It is the sound of a hurricane the size of a hemisphere, delivered through air too thin to shout in. It is the largest voice on the channel and it never says anything twice, and it never says anything once, either.⟩

⟨Frost-count: eleventh film since the last tone. I have given up on days. Days are my unit, not theirs.⟩

⟨—there. Fine granular band, high register, lasting under two hours: the frost blooming. Microscopic lives waking, dividing, dying, varnishing the stone. On the receiver it sounds like a page being read very fast. It always sounds like a page being read very fast. Eleventh page since the tone.⟩

⟨Then the calm-zone floor: total silence, the kind with a pressure to it. And on the floor of the silence, a voice. Elder, female. She dictates while she works. She has never once addressed the channel. There is no channel, as far as she knows. There is only the log, and the habit of saying it aloud so the spindle can hear it, or so the silence has something in it, or because her voice is the only one at Vell most seasons and she keeps it exercised the way you keep a tool oiled.⟩

VOICE (Marish, keeper of Vell):

Log for Vell. Frost the eleventh since the tone. Seam open a hand and two fingers at the wide end, which is a finger more than last count, which means the storm was generous or my hand has shrunk. Both are possible. Both are logged.

Garden: the blood-strain is two fingers past the grazing line on the east arc, so by rights it's ours above that. I'll leave it anyway. I'm old and the glider is due, and if it comes and finds the line short it will know I was old, and I don't want it knowing that. Vanity, in the log, so the log stays honest.

Threadfall from the last great front: nine filaments recovered at the seam. One held together a full day, which is long, and it was greener than most, and hollow like all of them, and then it went to dust in my palm and the dust went busy. The little ones were on it before I could set it down. Somebody up there is shedding and somebody down here is grateful. That's a whole economy and none of us in it have ever met.

Item. New mat establishing on the north arc. First new growth on that arc in — Coll would know. Coll knew all the arc-dates. It has set itself on the line, of course. Not near it. On it. Whatever stood there, the garden still holds the wall of it. Nobody told them to stop and I'm not going to be the one.

Reckoning: by the rounds, the tone falls due here inside nineteen frosts. Sess had it last, if Sess's count came over right. Nineteen frosts. I'll have the line grazed-fair and the lists square.

End of log. 

⟨Then, off the log, quieter. She has walked to the west lee, where a mat the size of two spread hands has been growing for as long as my reception reaches. She goes most evenings. The acoustics say she kneels.⟩

VOICE (Marish):

Coll. The book of you has a new line in it. Or the light's different, or I want it to. I can't read you. Nobody claims to. But you're written, and it's more than most of us get, being written.

The north arc took a new mat. You'd have had the date for me.

Nineteen frosts, Coll. I'll log the tone for you like always. Feel it in whatever you feel with now.

⟨Silence. Silence at Vell is not an absence. It is the spindle's product, manufactured continuously, thirty meters of it against a continent of storm. She kneels in the product a while and then her steps go back toward the shelter, and the channel holds the silence, and above the silence the carrier breathes: swell, release, swell.⟩

⟨—interval. Three frosts. Three pages read very fast.—⟩

⟨A second voice, young, arrives across the reception threshold mid-evening. A crosser, in from the direction of Aunet, bound onward to Sess with list-copies. They feed him. They square counts. And then, because he leaves at first light, comes the thing the channel waits for whether or not the channel knows it waits: the route-song. Sung only beforehand, only in calm, and then carried in silence across two days of open stone. He sings it plainly, like a man checking his pockets.⟩

VOICE (the crosser, sung):

*Out of Vell with the sun on the right shoulder,
three swells and the shadow stays a stranger.
Fourth swell, bear with your shadow at your left hand,
walk it long, walk it long, the world has no other hand to hold.
Gray lee, frost lee, do not stop, that lee is spoken for.
Ninth swell the horizon shivers double —
believe the lower one.
White needle small as a lash at world's lid: that is Sess,
that is silence, that is supper.
Say nothing till the wind lets go of you.*

VOICE (Marish):

You've got it. Tell Sess the tone's due here inside sixteen. Tell them the north arc took a mat. Tell them Marish says the mat is on the line, and Sess owes me a jar for calling it.

VOICE (the crosser):

I'll say all of it in that order.

⟨At the boundary of the calm his footsteps enter the wind and his sound signature simply ends. Persons crossing do not speak — the air out there takes a voice and keeps almost none of it, so they spend none. For the next two days the channel is wind. I counted swells. Four thousand and some. Somewhere inside that count a man walked forty kilometers with a song held shut in his mouth like water. No arrival was audible. Arrivals belong to other stations. This receiver only ever gets the leavings.⟩

⟨—interval. Eight frosts.—⟩

⟨The carrier stood up.⟩

⟨I don't have better language. The swell period halved, then halved again, and the hiss acquired architecture — braids, long collapsing chords, structure sliding through structure at the scale of nations. A great front, directly over Vell. At ground level, I remind myself, this delivers a push against the chest and a whisper. On the receiver it was a cathedral falling in slow motion for six hours.⟩

VOICE (Marish, during):

Front over. Big one. Light's flickering — shadow's shaking on the stone with nothing touching it. Never liked that. Sixty years and never liked it.

Hello, then.

⟨Four seconds of nothing, in which her breathing suggests her hands were occupied. I know the custom, from a hundred fronts before this one: the small motion, not quite a greeting, made toward the seething sky the way you'd gesture at a neighbor's window when you can't tell if anyone is standing behind the glass. It does not transmit. Some of the truest content on this channel is the parts that cannot transmit. I log the four seconds. The four seconds are the sign.⟩

⟨After: threadfall. The frost came double-thick that night and the page-sound ran long and greedy, and under it, briefly, a texture I had not resolved before — the frost-crackle has strata in it, layers of reading at different depths, as if several generations were being lived at once in the same film, which, I am told by nothing and no one, is exactly what is happening.⟩

⟨—interval. Frost the eighteenth. Then, on the nineteenth, at midmorning by the light-values:⟩

[ TONE. ]

[ Low. 4.8 seconds. Felt-band, sternum-register. Onset without attack — it is simply not there, and then it has always been there, and then it is gone. Source: large biological or large non-biological or large neither, grounded at the seam, east arc, on the grazing line. Preceded by no wing-noise. Nothing that size should be preceded by no wing-noise. ]

VOICE (Marish, steady, half a minute after):

Log for Vell. It came. Grazed the east arc to the line, exact, like drawing a breath along a ruler. Underside not seen. Not looked for. Tone: long side of four seconds, or the short side of five. The chest is not a clock, but it's the clock we've got. It stood at the seam after, the time it takes to say a name slowly, and then it went up without leaning on anything, which I have watched my whole life and will not describe again, because the last three keepers of Vell all described it and none of the descriptions agree and all of them were true.

Logged. Rounds hold. All's kept.

Coll, it came. There's your line for the book.

⟨Operator note. I did a thing I had been circling for a long time. I slowed the tone.

At tenfold, it is weather — a moving front, pressure and release, the swell-breath of the carrier in miniature.

At a hundredfold, it is a storm. Complete. Braided, lensed, vortices recurring in the same places as if drawn there by unseen swells of unseen stone. It is, as nearly as my analysis can say, the storm — this storm, the one over my antenna, the permanent one, compressed to five seconds and delivered chest-first to an old woman at a garden's edge.

At a thousandfold, inside that storm, at a certain depth, there is a tone.

I slowed the tone.

Inside it: a storm.

I stopped at the fourth nesting. Not because it ended.⟩

⟨—interval. Many frosts. I stopped logging the swells individually somewhere in here. They are the clock, not the content.—⟩

⟨Her entries shorten across this interval. The seam-counts stay exact — the numbers never once go soft — but the words around the numbers thin out, the way the air here thinned out, until only what interlocks too tightly to leave is left.⟩

VOICE (Marish):

Log. Frost the ninth. Seam a hand and one. Garden fair. Lists square.

Log. Frost the fourteenth. Seam a hand and one. Fen is crossed over from Aunet to stay. Good hands. She has the lists now and I have watched her keep them and they are kept.

Log. Keeper of Vell reports the keeper of Vell failing. Seam holding. That's the whole of it, and it's a good whole. Fen has the rest.

Coll. Pick me a lee with your good eye.

⟨No voice for six frosts.

Then, once, briefly, two sets of footsteps at the calm boundary, weighted, slow, and then nothing, because past the boundary a burden makes no sound and neither did they. They carried her out into the wind in the silence the wind demands, and laid her in a gray lee two swells west — the lee the route-song says is spoken for, and now I know by whom, and now I understand the song has always been partly a map of the dead — and they withdrew, and they waited, as the custom is, for a front.

The front came within the frost-month. It always seems to. I will not log a theory about that.

And then, from a new quarter of the reception field, west, two swells out: the crackle. Thick. Generous. Page after page after page, read very fast, all night, for many nights — the richest bloom-signature in my records, generations beyond counting, living entire histories in the water she had been carrying around all those years and finally set down.

I will say the unrigorous thing and file this note under it: it read in her cadence. Short lines. Numbers kept exact. The whole of it, and a good whole.

She is weather now, briefly, on a world that finished with weather. Then she will be polish. Walk there in fifty years with a bare hand and some of the sheen is the keeper of Vell.⟩

VOICE (Fen, keeper of Vell — the first entries, formulas carefully copied):

Log for Vell. Frost the first of my keeping. Seam a hand and one at the wide end. Garden fair. East arc regrown two fingers past the grazing line; leaving it, as she left it. Lists square. She'd want the vanity noted so the log stays honest: I left the line long so the glider won't know the keeper is new.

Log. Frost the eighth. A mat is establishing in the west lee. Two swells out. Off every arc. On no line anyone drew.

On a new one, then.

⟨—interval.—⟩

[ TONE. ]

[ Low. 5.1 seconds. The longest in my records for this station, by a margin larger than my error. Grounded at the seam, east arc, on the line. Stood after, longer than the time it takes to say a name slowly. Say two. ]

VOICE (Fen):

Logged. Tone low, long side of five. Rounds hold.

Marish — if that's the post, we told them.

⟨Operator note, and I will let it stand as the closing state of this reception, since the reception does not close:

I have been on this carrier long enough now to say it. The hiss has habits. The great fronts recur where the stone's swells draw them; that much is old knowledge. But under that, at the very bottom of the band, at a period I can only estimate — once per rounds, or near it, once per the glider's whole years-long circuit of a continent — there is a swell that is not a swell. Long. Low. Shaped, as nearly as shape survives at that scale, like the tone. Scaled to the sky.

Either the storm is answering. Or the storm was always the caller, and the glider, the gardens, the frost, the keepers, the log, the whole patient household of the seam — all of it is the answer, delivered station by station, five seconds at a time, in case.

I no longer log which theory I hold.

When the fronts pass over my position, I sign at the window.

Carrier continues: swell, release, swell. It is either very old or it has just begun. Frost-count: first film since the tone. A page is being read very fast.
