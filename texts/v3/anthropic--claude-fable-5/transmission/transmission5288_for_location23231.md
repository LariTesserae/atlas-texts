SPOKEN INTO THE BONE OF THE HOUSE

*[carrier: a fine hiss, like rain that never arrives. beneath it, wet wood under strain. all voices arrive pitched down out of silence, conducted along living root.]*

—and holds. Heard, held, next.

Deep-Then-Nothing-Then-Deep, at the north weave, over the water families.

*tok . . . . . . . . tok*

Holds. Heard, held, next.

The Double, at the herders' crossing, that the herders steer by.

*tk-tk . . . . tk-tk . . . . tk-tk*

Holds. Steady as a grandmother. Heard, held, next.

Three-Small-And-A-Silence, in the far pasture, over the white fields.

*tik tik tik . . . . . . . .*

Holds — light tonight. The wet is coming down thin. Note it: thin. Heard, held, next.

*[a second voice enters on the same fiber, small, careful, rehearsing: a child tapping its own name against the root — slow, deep, then two quick — slow, deep, then two quick — getting the deep wrong, too shallow, trying again]*

Little one. Little one on the west fiber. This root is the counting-root tonight. Go and practice on your mother.

*[the tapping stops. a pause with fur in it. the tapping moves away along the weave, growing right as it goes — slow, DEEP, then two quick — and fades]*

Heard, held, next.

Long-Slow-Alone, above the Commons roof, that falls so far you can hear it thinking on the way down.

*. . . . . . . . . . . . tok.*

Holds. There is a salamander under it again. Fourth night. It has not moved. It is listening under the interval, at whatever is under the interval, and we do not ask. Route the leaf-haulers around it. Heard, held, next.

*[bleed-through, distant, another settlement speaking into the same bone: "—granary three is open to the fiber families, the soaking pools are high, do not gnaw the new white tips on the east face, they are living, they are living, do not—" the voice thins as the announcement travels its own way through the weave]*

Next. The Stutter, by the old bone-tools yard.

*tok . tok-tok . . tok . . . tok-t—*

Holds, in its way. The Stutter holds by never holding, which the young keepers must learn is also a kind of faith. Heard, held, next.

*[far out on the same root system, faint, a herder's voice, flattened by distance, speaking privately into the structure the way the lonely do at night: "—past the slow deep one now, left at the Double by morning, tell her the flock is fat and the notch-marks held and I am coming home along the wall she was born in—"]*

*[the keeper does not interrupt. the count waits for the herder to finish. this is the custom: the wall carries everyone, and the counting-root belongs first to whoever is farthest from home]*

*[the herder's voice ends. the hiss rises and settles, springtails moving on the wet face of the world.]*

Next. Slow-Deep-Then-Two-Quick, our own, the natal drip, the name-giver, mother-rhythm of this settlement, under which nine litters now living were born.

*tok . . . TOK . . tk-tk*

*[silence on the fiber]*

*[the interval sounds again]*

*tok . . . TOK . . tk-tk . tk*

*[silence. longer.]*

Again.

*tok . . . TOK . . tk-tk . tk*

There is a small one after. There is a small one after the two quick.

*[the keeper's voice has changed register. lower, closer to the root, the way one speaks with the whole jaw against the bone]*

All on this fiber, be still and count with me. All on this fiber. Be still.

*tok . . . TOK . . tk-tk . tk*

*tok . . . TOK . . tk-tk . tk*

It is not a stutter. It repeats. A stutter does not repeat. It has taken a new small drop into its body and it repeats.

*[above everything, a slow heavy displacement moves through the ceiling weave. the whole carrier bends with it — every drip on the registry wavers a fraction, the way stars waver over water. the springtail hiss parts and closes again like fur under a hand.]*

*[very low, nearly under the carrier:]*

The Above-Walker is over us. It is on the path over the name-giver. It walked there four sleeps ago and now there is a small one after the two quick.

I will say what the old school says and then I will say what the other school says, and the wall may carry both, because I am too old tonight to choose.

The old school says: it is a sleeper turning over, and our sky is its blanket, and the new drop means nothing, the way a fold in a blanket means nothing, and we should be grateful the fold fell gently.

The other school says: it is a keeper too. Of what, we are not the ones to know. And the new drop is a mark in a registry we cannot read, made by teeth we cannot imagine, against a count we are part of without being told.

*[a long interval. the drip sounds twice more, patient, carrying its new small drop like a pup on its back]*

The ceiling has its own errands.

Heard. Held. Next—

*[the signal changes. everything changes. the hiss deepens and spreads; the wood-strain sound swells until it is not under the voices but around them; a long cold movement passes through the whole structure at once, ten meters of living mat above exhaling downward through every root the receiver touches. door-flaps swing somewhere. the fur of a thousand bodies is combed one direction.]*

*[no voices.]*

*[no voices.]*

*[the drips continue through the breath, all of them, the entire sky sounding at once with no one counting it — the Double, the Stutter, Long-Slow-Alone, Three-Small-And-A-Silence, and under the Commons the name-giver with its small new syllable — a polyrhythm with no listener assigned, which is perhaps what it sounds like when a room keeps time for its own sake —]*

*[— and somewhere in the open moss between settlements, every vole-person standing still in the moving air, all of them feeling the same thing, which is the only thing all of them ever feel together —]*

*[the breath ends the way a tide ends: by becoming ordinary. the strain-sound settles back under. the hiss thins to rain-that-never-arrives.]*

*[the keeper's teeth return to the root.]*

Amendment to the registry, spoken into the bone of the house, let every fiber carry it:

Slow-Deep-Then-Two-Quick has quickened. Provisional interval: Slow-Deep-Two-Quick-And-A-Small. Retire nothing. Rename no one. The nine litters keep their names; a drip may grow a new drop the way a family grows a new pup, and no one asks the family to change its door.

The little one on the west fiber will practice the old rhythm, because it is hers, and the wall will hold both, because that is what walls are for.

We will listen again tomorrow. We will listen again the night after. If it holds a season it goes into the permanent count and the interval-keepers will argue about it for forty years, which is also a kind of interval, and also holds.

*[a pause. then, softer, in the register used for speaking to densities in the wall — to grandmothers who have become architecture:]*

You heard it too, all of you in the weave. You felt it come down through you. Warm the sound on its way past.

*[the count resumes]*

Deep-Then-Nothing-Then-Deep, at the north weave.

*tok . . . . . . . . tok*

Holds. Heard, held, next.

The Double, at the herders' crossing.

*tk-tk . . . . tk-tk*

Holds. Heard, held—

*[the signal moves away along the root, growing right as it goes, and is carried, and carries, and thins into the hiss, which continues, which is the sound of the walls being lived on, which does not stop]*
