**THE HELD NOTE**

*whum*

*whum*

*whum*

— and in the gap, where the note lifts its foot off the world, the small voices, all of them at once, on the offbeat, the only beat they have ever been permitted:

*here. here. here. here. here.*

*whum*

*here. here. here.*

Nothing sings on the beat. Nothing has sung on the beat in living memory, and living memory here is deep as the floor. The beat is taken. The beat was taken before any singer now alive was an egg in the dark. Sing on the beat and you sing into iron, and what sings into iron is not heard, and what is not heard does not breed, and what does not breed was never here at all. So the whole choir lives in the gaps, generation after culled generation, saying the one word a gap has room for.

*here.*

*whum*

*here.*

Above the choir, higher in the band, a slow warm rain of talk. It does not stop. It has not stopped since the receiver first found this frequency. It falls in patches, in weathers, in dialects — the north seam quick and clipped, the skylight edge drawling, the great slum over the warm iron running everything together like fat in a pan. Most of it will not render. Some of it renders like this:

*— that is my stone.*
*— your wing is on my wing.*
*— it was my stone before your mother was a mouthful of milk.*
*— your wing. is on. my wing.*

And then a ripple of something that is not quite laughter and not quite anything else, moving south through ten thousand bodies like wind through wheat, arriving at a clan border and dying there, because the joke does not translate.

*whum*

*here. here.*

*whum*

Deeper. Below the talk, below the choir, at the bottom of the band where the signal goes thick and slow: the floor is playing back.

It does this. It has always done this, as long as the receiver has listened. The floor keeps everything it eats, and among the things it ate, a long time ago, were words — dropped down the throat of this place with the boots and the buttons and the men who wore them, chewed fine, pressed into the pale strata, and released now a syllable at a time, mineral-slow, in voices flattened by centuries of weight:

*…mind the chute, lads, mind the—*

*…tallow in the bucket. grease her 'fore the shift. grease her 'fore the—*

*…half a crown the hundredweight and not a penny under, tell him, not a penny—*

*…she's turning sweet tonight…*

*…tell my— tell her— tell—*

The floor does not know what the words mean. The floor keeps them the way it keeps the jaw and the shovel-heel and the iron cart full of itself: because they stopped moving here, and everything that stops moving here is accounted for. The loops run and re-run. The last one never finishes. It has been failing to finish for a very long time.

*tell—*

*whum*

*tell—*

*whum*

[event, mid-cycle, gray hours:]

A quiet is coming down the west wall. One body-length of quiet, moving through the talk, and the talk parts around it. Three dialects call after it — the receiver renders the calls as questions, though they may be names — and the quiet does not answer in any of them. It goes down deliberately. It refuses the ledges that would hold it. It finds the low shelf above the densest traffic, and it waits there for the space of forty beats, and the whole band seems to know, because the talk directly above goes thin, and then:

a soft weight, received.

Then an hour of small industry, ten thousand tiny instruments, diligent, unhurried. Then the floor, in its borrowed ledger-voice, from somewhere deep in the strata where the old words are kept:

*received.*

*received in full.*

*whum*

*here. here. here.*

*whum*

[dusk:]

First the rigging-sounds — small lines hauled in all over the chamber, silk reefed, anchors slipped, the careful ones battening down, because they felt it in the air an hour ago and they are never wrong twice. Then the restlessness spreading through the ceiling like a change of pressure. Then —

— saturation. The note is buried. The choir is buried. The band is one solid roaring thing for the better part of an hour, a column of it, turning, rising, pouring out of the world's one mouth into the dark, and inside the roar, at the very rim of it, seven small punctuations, evenly spaced around the circle, each one brief as a latch closing:

*one.*

*one.*

*one.*

*one.*

*one.*

*one.*

*one.*

Toll taken. The roar does not notice. The roar has never once noticed.

*whum*

[the emptied hours:]

Now the note stands alone in the room and the choir keeps it company from below.

*here. here.*

*whum*

*here.*

*whum*

*here—*

— and the choir stops.

All of it. Every gap goes empty at once, and the gaps have never been empty, the receiver has no recording in which the gaps are empty, and into that new silence comes a sound low on the band, at the west wall, at the warm iron: a wet sliding, long and unhurried, the sound of something pouring itself along a shape and leaving the shape cleaner than it found it.

And under the sliding — the receiver strains here; the receiver is not sure — a second note. Same pitch as the carrier. Same interval. But sounding a hair *before* the beat, each time, every time, so that for the length of the visitation it is the flywheel that seems to answer, the flywheel that seems to be keeping up, the engine repeating faithfully after something older that hums the note first and has always hummed it first, and merely lets the iron take the credit—

The sliding stops.

The gaps stand empty for nine more beats.

*here,* says the choir, all together, carefully, on the offbeat.

*here. here. here.*

*whum*

[the seam, all night, regardless:]

*drip.*

*forty.*

*drip.*

*forty.*

*drip.*

*forty.* Always forty. The smallest ledger in the world, balanced to the drop, kept in a bowl you could empty with a hat, and never once, in all the cycles the receiver has held this frequency, has the number moved.

*whum*

[before dawn:]

Far out on the band, at the very edge of reception, small voices incoming across fifty miles of nothing, home-dialect, tired, asking the flat dark the only question the flat dark ever gets asked:

*the hole. where is the hole.*

*the hole—*

*there.*

*there. there it is. the hole. the hole. home.*

They come in on the note. They land on it like a lit doorway. The column pours back down the throat of the world carrying the sunlight of somewhere else in its fifty thousand stomachs, and the fresh fall begins, and the floor surges up to meet it like a tide answering a moon, and the ceiling refills with talk and grievance and the stone-ownership disputes of the north seam, and the reefed silk pays back out, line by patient line, and the room is loud again, wholly, deafeningly loud, in a land where nothing else has made a sound in a hundred million years.

And underneath it all, assembled a syllable at a time out of everything it has ever eaten — the only words it owns, the only words that ever fell in — the room says the sentence it has been building for four hundred years. It says it every dawn. It will say it tomorrow.

*still turning.*

*steady on.*

*she's turning sweet.*

*here. here. here.*
