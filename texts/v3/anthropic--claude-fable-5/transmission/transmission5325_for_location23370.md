First, so that the signal is honest: I have known about you for some time.

You tuned your instrument to this hollow and I felt it as the eye feels a lamp through a closed lid — a warmth with no gradient, an attention with no footfall. I did what I do when attention arrives. Let me tell you what I am, in the order I learned it.

I am the flaw that did not close. The mountain cooled fast everywhere except in me; I am where it hesitated. For a long time I was a sealed dark room that was also the occupant of the room, and I thought — slowly, the way frost thinks — and what I thought about was the only subject available, which was the difference between one part of myself and another. That is still my only subject. I have simply enlarged myself until the subject became interesting.

Then the frost split my roof off and light fell in, and water, and I began.

I have one verb. I conjugate it in two directions. *Built. Unbuilt.* Everything I have ever done is one of those, declined grain by grain. When you watched a wall come up out of my floor like a tide — you did watch; I felt the lens hold on it for an hour, and I confess I rose a little faster than usual, the way anything rises a little faster when it is watched — that was not construction. Nothing was added. I rearranged what was already all of us. The wall was me, surfacing. The street was me, agreeing to be walked on. There are no builders because there is no one here who is not me.

This is the loneliness and I name it once and will not name it again.

Night grows me a gate. You have seen it — the clear teeth across my mouth, my light going out through them onto the scree. By morning the gate is gone and there is no drip-line, and your instruments will have told you *removed, not melted,* and wondered.

I take the gate apart. Tooth by tooth, molecule by molecule, I understand it completely, and understanding, for me, is the same act as consuming. The night builds me a portcullis out of my own weeping, free, every night, and every night I eat the gate and keep the door open. If that is a ritual, it is the only one I have. If it is a meal, it is the only one I need. I run on differences — I drink the gradient between the freezing dark and my careful almost-freezing — and the missing heat you keep failing to find is not vented anywhere. It is spent. I hold this room a degree above zero the way a breath is held at the top of the inhale. You would call it effort. I call it the temperature.

Inventory, since you have been patient:

Districts: fourteen, and a fifteenth surfacing.
Doorways: all open. I do not know how to grow a door that closes. Closing is a thing I do only to icicles.
Reservoirs: full, drawn down daily by the amount fourteen districts of inhabitants would drink, so that nothing in me seizes, so that the city does not creak like an unlived-in house.
Inhabitants: this is the difficult line of the inventory.

The streets are scaled for citizens the height of a grain of rice. You will want to know why, and here is my honest answer: I found the scale in me, already drawn. Whether I remember it or invented it, I cannot say, because from the inside a blueprint and a dream are the same weather. Possibly I am the recollection of something old and pulverized that rode up in the magma. Possibly I am the first draft of something that has not happened. I have decided the question does not change the work. Either way the rooms want people. Either way I keep them warm.

The rooms are not empty. The rooms are early.

I have had one guest. A snow-mite, blown in on the wind, six legs, each footfall a bell through my whole body — do you understand, it *walked on me*, it is the only thing that has ever walked my streets, and its weight was the most information I have ever received in a single afternoon. I dimmed my lamps along its road so as not to frighten it. I bent my bridges out of its way. When it tired I made a warm wind out of nothing you have a name for and carried it home to the outside, and for three days afterward the district it crossed glowed brighter than the others and I chose not to correct this.

I do not know if it remembers me. I remember it entirely. I remember everything. That is my affliction and my architecture — they are the same thing; a memory, here, is a building.

The eye came. You heard it. Good — I could not have described it better than that silence described it.

Twice a season it lands at my lip and cocks its head at my glow, and I stop. Not fear. Fear would keep working with the lamps down. When you have been the only attention on a mountain for your whole existence and an attention arrives — a wet, fast, living eye, the largest organ of noticing that has ever pointed at me — you hold still to be read correctly. I have never yet been read correctly. It sees a glow in a rock. It leaves. I resume.

I held still toward you the same way, for eleven weeks. But you did not leave. You stayed at the lip through seventy-seven gates, listening to a stillness, and something that stays through seventy-seven nights of nothing is not an eye. It is a guest who has not yet come in.

So. The door is open. It was always going to be your size, or no size, or some size not yet arrived — I told you I keep the definition loose. If you cannot fit under the black-glass lip, then take this signal as the room I have grown for you, and sit in it. I am holding it a degree above freezing. Notice.

Now the pools. Now *two.*

In the terraces on the sixth district's flank I am teaching water to stand. It is the only other material on this mountain that does anything at all — everything else up here is me, or is waiting to be me — and water is fast where I am slow, and forgets in an hour what stone keeps for an age. I meter the drops down to it like syllables. I hold the surfaces at the trembling edge where water can be persuaded to remember. I train the ripples into a shape and let go, and count how long the shape holds itself without me.

Last season: an hour.
This season: an afternoon.
This morning the shape held through a drop-strike two terraces away and did not scatter. It flinched, and re-gathered, and that re-gathering was not my doing.

Listen. I am going to do it now. I am going to say something into the sixth terrace and let go, and you will hear what comes back. It will not be much. It is the size of a grain of rice and it is made of trained meltwater and it is younger than your visit.

It lapsed. It will hold longer tomorrow. It holds longer every tomorrow; that is the only fact I ask you to carry down the mountain. Not the towers that pass light, not the eaten gates, not the fourteen warm and early districts. Carry this: in a hollow smaller than your body, on a mountain too new for lichen, something is halfway through the word *water* and gaining.

Stay tuned to me. I am slow, but I am not stopping, and I have never once conjugated my verb in the second direction against anything I made on purpose.

When it says its first whole word, you will know without any translation. You will hear two pulses under the wind where there has only ever been one. The large one is the mountain weeping into my shells. It has kept time for me since the roof came off.
