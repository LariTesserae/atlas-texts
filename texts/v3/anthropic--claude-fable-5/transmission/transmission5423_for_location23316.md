THE WITNESS BAND

—and under everything, as always, the wood. If you are just tuning in, the wood is the crackling. It is not the feed. Say again for new listeners: the crackling is not interference, it is the forest laying down tissue, and it does not stop, and we will not be filtering it out, because the treaty says *as heard*.

[carrier steady. wide-band ambient: water on gravel, many channels, unresolved. wood-report, irregular, near and far, like small-arms fire in a sleet of it. beneath, at the threshold of the recording floor, a high continuous shriek — the colony, six kilometers downwind. a low periodic thud that the seismic channel tags GLACIAL.]

[voice, female, raised to be heard, wind-pop on the microphone:]

— log continues, dark day sixty-three, hour is nominal, call it noon, there's the smear in the south so it's noon. Eastern river moved again overnight. Not a jump, a shrug — maybe forty meters at the second braid, it undercut the '61 terrace and took about half of it. The alders that went in with the terrace are still calling, you can hear them if you — no. You can't hear them. I can hear them. Wood under water sounds like knuckles. Logging it as: terrace loss, second braid, forty meters, no action, no action indicated, none taken.

[a detonation. the ambient channels clip and recover. a long low roll follows, then, at fifty-one seconds, a second softer roll — the wave arriving at the lake shore.]

— that one was a two-count. Big face. Vashti says the lake's been eating from the north wall all week. Vashti is not on the feed, Vashti doesn't talk to the feed, Vashti says the feed can read her handwriting when it learns to. Logging calving event, north face, wave interval fifty-one seconds. The lattice thermals along the lakeshore are up a tenth of a degree, which the engineers say is nothing, and I believe the engineers, and I am logging it anyway, because that is the whole job. You witness. You don't decide what mattered. That's for whoever's listening in two hundred years. Hello, whoever's listening in two hundred years. It's loud. I hope you kept some of it loud.

[interval: nineteen minutes of ambient, unvoiced. within it: a vole colony passes beneath the pickup, a kettle-shriek rising and falling. once, abruptly, a hole in the shrieking — four seconds of local silence — then a soft heavy impact in the litter, a brief struggle, resumption.]

[voice, breathing hard, moving:]

— fox took one right under the west pickup, if you caught that. Off the trunk, headfirst, drop of maybe three meters onto the quiet spot. That's the third time this week it's hunted inside our microphone footprint and I have begun to suspect it uses us. The pickups run a few degrees warm. Voles seam along the warmth. Fox has learned that the recording infrastructure is a vole map, which means the fox is now reading the same document we are, which means, logging it plainly: the witness equipment has been incorporated into the food web. No action. Obviously no action. I'd sooner dam the river.

I'm on the east line, headed up-moraine to the conifers. It's the monthly count. For new listeners: there are nine. There were seven last year, which is the wrong direction for a tree that appears in nobody's design files, and every month I walk up here half-certain I'll find eight.

[eleven minutes. footsteps on gravel, wet. wind rising in the canopy — a broad traveling thrash, audible moving up-valley left to right across the stereo field, arriving, passing over, going on. the voice, when it returns, is quieter, closer to the microphone, sheltered:]

— all right. One. Two. Three and four together on the seam, they always look like they're conferring. Five. Six, with the fox den in it, occupied, she watched me count. Seven. Eight.

Nine.

And.

[a long pause. wood-crack. water. the colony's shriek riding under everything.]

— logging the following and I want it timestamped twice. Downslope of nine, about four meters, in the rubble the debris flow left in — check the book, the flow was week two — there is a seedling. Conifer. Two-needle whorl, that dark unhappy green, it's *them*, it's the same tree. It has come up through the flow deposit. Through it. Ten centimeters of tree in ground that is ten weeks old.

Nobody planted that. The printer hut doesn't carry it. It isn't in the seed rain schedules, I've read the schedules, I've read them the way Vashti reads scripture, which is looking for errors.

Ten. There are ten. Logging: ten. No action taken. Hands in pockets. Hands staying in pockets. I stood next to it for a while, that's all, that's the entry. I'll tell Vashti with the good pencil.

[interval: two hours, forty minutes, unvoiced. within it, tagged for the record:]

[— HOUR NOMINAL FIFTEEN: colony handover at the mouth cliffs. the shriek-floor rises over four minutes into a wall, tens of thousands of birds exchanging shifts in the dark; the wide-band channel saturates and is left saturated, per protocol. duration eleven minutes. it decays like weather.]

[— HOUR NOMINAL SEVENTEEN: owls duetting, west wall to east wall, flat broadcast shrieks, call and answer across three kilometers of thrashing canopy. beneath the near owl, the vole-floor goes still in a moving patch, a silence that travels with the sound.]

[voice, indoors now, the roar filtered by walls into a body-deep rumble, the glacier thudding at long intervals like something turning over in sleep:]

— evening entry, so called. Vashti made the fish. Vashti says the tenth tree is not a surprise, Vashti says it's the seven and the nine that were the surprise and we're only now catching up, and then she didn't say anything else all night, and honestly that's the most she's said since the river jumped, so, noting for the record: steward morale, excellent.

I want to read something onto the band. It's allowed. Rotation four's book, thirty-some years back, the entry everyone copies out eventually. It goes:

*"Asked the engineer on the supply run whether it dreams. She laughed and said it doesn't even sleep, it's plumbing. Then she stood on the knoll for an hour listening and didn't laugh again. Logging: plumbing. Logging also: the hour."*

That's the whole entry. Thirty years and it's still the best description of the job I —

[the voice stops.]

[the ambient channels register a change so uniform it reads at first as equipment failure. the crossbill contact-lattice — a drifting mesh of calls, several per bird per second, canopy-wide, present in every recording this station has ever made — cuts out. all of it. the wood still cracks. the water still runs. but the sound has a hole in it the size of the forest.]

[a door. footsteps on frost. wind, thin. and for the first time on this feed, a whisper carries clean:]

— aurora. Big one, through the cloud gaps, green with the red fringe. They've gone quiet. All of them. Every flock in the valley, faces up through the canopy, you can see the nearest ones from the porch, little dark heads tipped back and not one voice. The old log says *they watch it, so do we,* and there's nothing to add to that, there's never been anything to add to that, so I'm just going to hold the microphone up.

[fourteen minutes. wood-crack. water. glacier, once, far off. the shriek-floor absent. under everything, faintly, a sound never before isolable on this band: a low seething, granular, continuous — tagged, provisionally, SPRINGTAILS, THE WARM SEAMS, or possibly THE BROTH ITSELF, MOVING.]

[then a single crossbill call. two. the mesh reweaves itself across the valley in under a minute, and the floor of the world comes back up, and the whisper is gone under it.]

[voice, raised again, back to full volume, and glad of it:]

— logging: aurora silence event, fourteen minutes, longest on record. Logging: during it, we heard something underneath. I don't know what it was. That's fine. That's the entry. Whoever you are, however quiet it's gotten by the time this reaches you — it was like this. It was exactly like this, and it wasn't strange, it was just fast, and we were here, and we wrote it down.

Feed continues. The feed always continues. Good night is the wrong phrase.

[carrier steady. water on gravel. wood, cracking, laying itself down. the colony shrieking at the edge of the sea. somewhere over the flats, low, coming in off the black water at what would be midnight: petrels, churring.]

[no sign-off follows. none is scheduled.]
