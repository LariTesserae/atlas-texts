**KEEP PACE**

—

*hhhhhhrrrrrrrr*

*— which is not static. It has never been static. It only took a long time to stop calling it that —*

*hhhhhhrrrrrrrr, and under it a hiss like a world sweating, and under the hiss: slap. drone. slap. drone. slap. drone. — a million shins pushing warm water, no beginning to the sound, no end to it, the sound has been arriving for weeks now, day and night, there is no night in it, there is no quiet district —*

*and then, this morning, something the receiver has learned to do without being told: the roar resolves. Not louder. Legible. A carved thing surfaces out of the grind — a whistle bent through three tones, a struck ceramic note, a clap, a whistle again — and it repeats, and it is not weather, it is a voice, it is two voices, one old and one young, and the old one is saying —*

—

Keep pace.

Footfall, footfall, footfall. No — *footfall.* With the water, not through it. You are not going somewhere. You are *saying* somewhere. Again.

Good. Better. Keep pace.

We are entering the fourth chapter now. You don't need me to tell you; the water tells you. Feel it deepen at the ankle — there, at the ankle, that finger-width of extra warm — that is the flood of the two hundred mothers, and we walk floods slowly, we have always walked floods slowly, sixty paces of slow is how the chapter remembers what slow cost them. Do not hurry the flood. The flood did not hurry.

*slap. drone. slap. drone.*

Now the turn. Feel how the route goes hard against the current for ninety paces? That is the drought of the shallow year. Everything in the drought is against the current. Your calves will burn. Their calves burned. That is the whole of the text; there is nothing else written about the drought; the burning *is* the writing. Keep pace.

—

*[the young voice, higher, a whistle still unsteady at the ends of its tones:]*

The pitch to the north — is that —

Nothing is wrong. Listen again. Low grind, long fall, low grind, long fall — that is Selt's district settling, that is the sound of a thousand rooms being held all night by a thousand boring people, blessings on every one of them. That is the pitch of nothing wrong. Hum it back to me.

*[a hum. two hums. for a moment the voices and the city are the same instrument, and the receiver cannot separate them, and there is no reason to.]*

Good. You'll sing that to your children before you sing them anything with words in it. Keep pace.

—

Now. The curve.

You know this curve. You learned this curve before you learned your name — I watched your mother walk it with you on her hip, footfall, footfall, so the arches would learn your weight early. This is *her* walk. Not your mother's. Hers. The one whose name is the curve itself. We take her walk to carry the chapter across the western water, and for as long as we take it, the arches hold. Count them.

*[splash-rhythm. the struck note of a knuckle on wet ceramic, forty-one times, each one going oddly deep, as if the wall answers below its surface.]*

Forty-one.

Forty-one. And forty-one when I was your height. And forty-one when I am silted to the knuckles, if you keep pace after me, and forty-one after you, if you are careful whom you teach. She has been dead eighty years and she is the driest thing in the Shallows: she never gets reabsorbed. That is what a masterpiece is here. Somebody boring enough to outlive her own body. Walk her well.

—

*[here the old voice stops. the receiver holds the roar alone for a moment — grind, hiss, slap, drone — and then the voice goes down.*

*It goes into the water. This has happened before on other days; the receiver knows the signature now. The whistles cease. What arrives instead arrives underneath hearing: a slow pressure at the sternum-frequency, pulsing, ninety seconds of it, patient, repeating a shape. Grief-tempo or courtship-tempo or bargaining-in-earnest — from the air there is no way to know. It is not encrypted. It is simply spoken in a medium the receiver does not stand in.*

*ninety seconds.*

*the voice comes back up into the air:]*

— and that is between us and the water. Wipe your face. Walk on.

—

*slap. drone. slap. drone.*

Stop.

Stand. No — *stand.* Feet apart, weight down, be a post, be a heron.

Listen to the west.

*[the receiver turns everything it has toward it. Far off, under the general grind: a high grinding, higher than the settling-pitch, and it is descending. Slowly. A note walking downstairs.]*

That is Marrow-of-the-Third-Channel being given back.

Ninety-one years, and constant as a channel herself. Her family walked out of the rooms this morning and stopped occupying, and now — listen — now the swarms are attending, and the fabric is taking the holding down, wall by wall, and every wall she held for ninety-one years is going back into the flow, and you can hear how *much* of her there is, listen how long the note is taking to fall. That is the size of the grief. You measure it in the pitch. A small life drops to the floor-note in an hour. Hers will take until evening.

Count it down with me. Don't sing. Just count.

*[a long time passes on the reception. The two voices strike a soft ceramic note at intervals, marking each step of the falling pitch. Around them the roar continues, the markets continue, the water continues — the funeral is not a silence, nothing here is a silence, the funeral is one long note inside the noise, and everyone within kilometers is tracking it the way they track weather, and the receiver, without meaning to, has begun tracking it too.*

*The note reaches the floor.*

*The old voice, quietly, in air:]*

There. She's everywhere now.

And hear me, because this is the part that makes you an archivist and not just my shadow: *from today, the route turns here.* This bend did not exist this morning. It exists now. Every record-walker in the district will fold this turning into the chapter by tomorrow, and in three hundred years an apprentice will feel their route bend west for no reason they can name, and their teacher will say: here, Marrow. Turn where the water widens. Everyone turns here.

That is a monument. We don't make any other kind. Keep pace.

—

*slap. drone. slap —*

*[the young voice, dropping low — not water-low, just quiet, which on this frequency reads as almost alarming:]*

Teacher. Ahead. On the left bank.

*[a pause in the footfall-rhythm of the crowd around them. The receiver hears it happen: a braid of a hundred wading-rhythms parting, going differently, re-weaving on the far side of something. A hole in the choreography, person-shaped.]*

Do not look. Do not weave them in. Keep pace.

But they —

Keep pace. I know. I knew them when they were audible. They taught the city a lie — insisted a form and abandoned it, twice, three times, until the wolf was cried out of the walls — and so we walk differently around them, all of us, until the fabric thins its memory of their shape. Nothing will hurt them. The walls will still pour aside. Half a second later. A hand's-breadth nearer. You saw them flinch.

Yes.

Then you have read the sentence. That is the whole of the law and the whole of the punishment and, one day, if they stand still long enough, honestly enough, loudly enough, the whole of the pardon. The city forgives anything that becomes constant. It has no choice. Constancy is its religion too. Now — footfall. *Footfall.* Good.

—

Here. Shoulder into this wall as you pass. Just your shoulder-sound, one footfall's worth of leaning.

Why?

Because in a year it wants to be a door, and everyone gives it one footfall. There are four hundred of us voting today. There were four hundred yesterday. There is no argument to have; there is only whether we keep coming. Doors are elected. Everything is elected. Talk is for water. Decisions are made with the feet.

*[a soft shove of sound. and — the receiver would not swear to this — an answering give in the grind behind the wall, small, like a held breath.]*

—

*[the young voice, after a long stretch of walking:]*

Teacher. When will my room hold?

*[laughter — the old voice's laughter is a clatter of struck notes, and around it, unprompted, three or four strangers' voices in the roar pick up the clatter and pass it along, because everything here is public, and a good laugh is common property:]*

When you are boring enough, the city will love you. Same as it loved me. Same as it loved Marrow. You will wake up one morning and the walls will simply still be there, having held you all night without being asked, and you will lie very still and listen to nothing being wrong, and that morning is the only door into being grown that anyone has ever found. I cannot walk you to it. It isn't on any route. Keep pace anyway. Keep pace *especially.*

—

*[evening on the frequency now — the receiver can tell by the light in the sound, if that means anything, the way the hiss goes copper, the way the vapor-ceiling rings differently when it ignites. The chronicle-walk is ending or pausing; there is no way to know which; the chapters do not announce their edges.*

*The two voices have stopped speaking words.*

*They are humming.*

*Low grind, long fall. Low grind, long fall. The settling-pitch. The pitch of a thousand rooms held by a thousand constant people. The lullaby that is a transcription of the world working. First the old voice, then the young one finding it, then — and this is the part the receiver will keep — the district itself underneath them, the actual grind of the actual settling, and the three lines braid, and for a while there is no telling teacher from student from city, which may be the entire point of the instrument.*

*The hum recedes. Not ends. Recedes — folds back into the roar the way a channel folds into the sheet of the water, still there, still moving, just no longer separate.*

*And the roar goes on. It will go on all night. It has gone on for ten thousand years, and the receiver has finally understood what kind of signal this is: not a broadcast with a beginning, not a message with an end, but occupancy — a world holding its shape out loud, a sound whose whole content is* nothing is wrong, nothing is wrong, we are still here, keep pace *—*

*slap. drone.*

*slap. drone.*

*slap. drone.*

*There is no sign-off. There is no quiet district. Silence is what would frighten them.*

*The signal is still coming in.]*
