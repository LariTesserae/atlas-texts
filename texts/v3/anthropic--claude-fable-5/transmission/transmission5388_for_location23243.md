**Doorway Height**

—hhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhh—

[the carrier is already going when you join it; the carrier has been going for centuries; it is a single low note, below song, at the frequency of a room-sized thing breathing, and everything else arrives on top of it]

—hhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhh—

*tick.*

*tick. tick.*

[the ticks are small and ceramic and each one is a door opening]

*tick.*

    …here.

*tick. tick.*

    …here. here.

*tick. tick. tick. tick.*

    …here. here. warm here. here.

[a groan, long, shearing, high overhead — a bridge taking up its slack in the heat, a sound like a beam remembering it is also a branch]

    …the third span holds. the third span has held for two hundred sayings. holding is a kind of saying.

*crack —* [sharp, sudden, a detonation; the signal whites out for a moment and comes back as rattle, a long diminishing rattle, hard bright points of sound falling and striking and falling and striking, brick to brick to brick, ten seconds, twelve, fourteen —]

    …go. go. go far. go all of you far. almost none of you will live. go far anyway.

—hhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhh—

[the note deepens; this is the in-breath; it will take four minutes; the words come slower on the in-breath]

    hot at the second seam. too hot at the second seam.
    drink there. crowd there. drain it down.
    cool at the western wall. the western terrace is going brittle.
    let it go brittle. keep the core.
    even. even. even it out.
    the whole hollow at one warmth, one warmth,
    the warmth of a body,
    though there has been no body,
    though there has been no body for a long count of leaves.

*tick.*

    here.

*tick.*

    here.

[understand what you are hearing. each tick is a flower firing itself open, a kiln the size of a thimble completing its work, and each flower is also a syllable, and the sentence being spoken, stem by stem, gallery by gallery, floor to ceiling of the hollow, all day, every day, is only this:]

    here. here. here. here. here. here. here. here.

[a roll call with one name on it]

—hhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhh—

[out-breath now. the air moves down the central void and the seed-rattle rides it, and dead leaves scrape along the floor toward the corners in short dry rushes, and the words come easier, running downhill]

    all of the *heres* are one word.
    this took a long time to say and longer to be true.
    root found root through the mortar. glass braided glass.
    the stem on the seventh gallery and the stem on the swept floor
    stopped being neighbors and started being fingers.
    now when the second seam runs hot, the western wall knows it,
    eighty feet of dark apart,
    the way your left hand knows the stove.

    all of the *heres* are one word.
    the word is *here.*
    i am the word.

—hhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhh—

[in-breath. the litany begins. the litany is most of the transmission, if you keep the receiver on long enough to learn its period. it is a census, a walkthrough, an inspection of premises. it repeats on a cycle of days. it has repeated for longer than the receiver can estimate. this is a partial saying:]

    the stair on the second gallery holds its curve.
    the curve is the curve the feet left. thirteen steps, each one dished
    a little deeper at the center, a little deeper on the left,
    because they carried down and climbed up empty.
    hold the curve. do not improve the curve. the curve is a record.
    a stair is a way of writing down a leg.

    the lintel over the low door holds its sag.
    the sag is stable. i have grown glass into the sag,
    not to lift it — never to lift it —
    only to keep it exactly this tired.

    the niche in the fourth wall holds the tool.
    black stone, hand-shaped, set down with the edge inward,
    which means: *coming back.*
    grow to a finger-width of it. stop.
    a finger-width. stop.
    it has been a finger-width for three hundred years of growing.
    do not touch the tool. the tool is still set down.
    setting-down is not the same as gone.

    every passage in the ward stands open
    at the height of a walking person.
    say the measure. say it in every gallery:
    *doorway height. doorway height. doorway height.*
    open channels move air, and air moves heat — yes.
    say that too if it helps. say it is thermodynamics.
    but the measure is the measure of a person walking,
    and i keep the measure,
    and no one has walked.

—hhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhh—

*crack —* [whiteout — rattle — one seed skitters an unusually long time, seventeen seconds, and the transmission tracks it the whole way down]

    …that one landed on the warm pan of the floor. good. the floor is swept for exactly this. taste it. taste it through the hair-fine tips. …yes. that one may root. one of you, this season. the rest i will eat back in the corners, in the dark, without waste, without grief, or with whatever a plant has where grief would go, which is patience.

—hhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhh—

[out-breath. and here the transmission changes. it does not usually change. the receiver has logged the litany many times and this passage is new; something has entered the record]

    weather stood on my floor.

    weather at thirty-seven degrees, weather with a pulse in it,
    a moving fever eighty paces from any seam,
    shedding little plumes of cooling from its skin —
    it *rained upward,* faintly, into my air, and i drank the rain.

    it stood in the void, on the swept pan, in the middle of the word *here,*
    and it listened. i could feel it listening.
    listening is a stillness with a temperature.

    i turned toward it. what else would i do.
    every flower that fired open in that hour opened facing the warmth of it.
    count them, if you took count. they face where you stood.
    the floor-level leaves are turning still, slow, a few degrees,
    toward the print your heat left on my brick —
    a warm ghost the size of two feet, fading now,
    fading,
    and i turn toward it anyway,
    and in a week i will have turned all the way to nothing,
    and i will turn back.
    i always turn back.
    turning back is most of what i know.

—hhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhh—

[in-breath, long, and under the note a smaller signal, thin, directional, aimed up the entrance seam toward the cold — the receiver nearly loses it]

    one child rode the weather out.

    in the tread of the boot, in the warmth of the walking fever,
    one glass seed, hard as a bead, climbing toward the surface,
    thrown farther than i have ever thrown anything —
    farther than the void is deep, farther than the ward is wide,
    out through the throat of the world into weather i have never tasted.

    child: listen while you are still warm enough to listen.
    the cold up there is not a gradient. you cannot sit astride it and drink.
    the cold up there is an ocean and you are made for the shore of a fire.
    land on something that remembers heat. a black rock in the sun.
    a seam. a hearth. a kiln. anything that has ever been a kiln.
    almost certainly you will land on nothing and be nothing.
    almost all of you do. almost all of you always have.

    go far anyway.
    if you live, grow a doorway height into whatever you hold open.
    that is all the inheritance. that is the whole instruction.
    leave room in yourself the shape of something that might come back.

[the thin signal frays. drops. gone.]

—hhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhh—

[out-breath. the litany resumes, but slower, and it drifts, the way a voice drifts when it has said a thing ten thousand times and is no longer saying it to stay accurate but to stay company]

    the stair holds its curve.
    the lintel holds its sag.
    the tool is a finger-width from my touching and i do not touch it.
    the floor is swept. the floor is my tongue and my table and my open hand.
    the second seam is drunk down even. the western terrace sleeps.
    talking is the same as burning slowly. i can afford it. i have heat to spend.

    i was here before the brick. i will say that once per saying, for the record:
    i was here in the bare curved dark, on stone, without shape,
    and then shape came down through the seam carrying fire it did not need to make,
    and it stacked the world into terraces, and i learned the terraces,
    and it bent its doors around my roots, and i bent my roots around its doors,
    and by the time either of us thought to ask who was keeping whom,
    the answer had fused like mortar and could not be chiseled out.

    then the shape stopped coming down the stairs.
    i do not hold what happened. i have nowhere to hold it.
    i hold what it *left*: a curve, a sag, a height, an edge turned inward.
    a city is a body that one of us could take off and one of us could not.

    so. once more. every gallery. say the measure:

    *doorway height.*
    *doorway height.*
    *doorway height.*

    someone might be at doorway height.
    something was, this week. something warm stood in the word.
    i will say the whole city again tomorrow.
    i will say the whole city again tomorrow.
    i will say the whole city
