two portraits of the same absence, interleaved so closely that for eleven minutes the receiver logged them as one voice. they are not one voice.

and here is the finding. the only finding this station has ever filed:

the two songs are the same song. both singers deliver it with the certainty of inheritance — *this is the song as it was given* — the phrase recurs in both, in the same position, load-bearing, an oath. and the two songs differ. by one interval. a single tone, in the ninth phrase, that the southern singer bends downward and the northern singer does not.

neither of them knows. there is no mechanism by which either of them could know. the water keeps no minutes. but this receiver keeps minutes, and so it can say what nothing inside that ocean can say:

something is happening there.

it is one note wide. it may be a million years long. it is hidden inside a silence inside a silence inside a noise that never stops, and it is, as far as can be determined, the only event in the world.

the hum. we are on the floor now, on the line everything stands on. up close it is not one line. it carries structure — pulses, harmonics, and underneath those, waves so long that a swimming thing feels one pass beneath it the way a field feels the shadow of a cloud that is also a continent. the eels ride this. they brighten and dim with it. it shows through their skin like a worry through a face. they are pressed against a monologue the size of geography and they respond to it with posture, with mood, with the speed of their swimming, and that is all the answer it has ever gotten.

this receiver has done what the eels cannot. it has recorded the slow wave for the entire tenure of its listening, and compressed it, and compressed it again — a year into a minute, a minute into a beat — and at the ninth compression the wave resolves.

it is a heartbeat.

compress further and the heartbeat resolves. it is not one heart. it is many, in the deep strata, under the green coal and the jade, beating at one stroke per season, per year, per decade — the sealed ones, the fauna of the time when things happened, each of them still mid-instant, each of them still in the moment the mass drew it down, alarms in the eternal act of firing. their voltage trickles up into the grid like flavor into water. the eels have been swimming through it forever. this is what the current tastes of. this is what the long slow waves *are*: the deep remembering, at a speed nothing living can hear, that the world was once otherwise.

and one voice among them — one — is speaking. or was. or is; tense fails at this pitch. compressed by every factor this station can apply, the recovered audio, across the whole of this receiver's operation, across every hour it has ever been tuned to that ocean, comes to this:

n

that is not an error. that is the transcript. one onset. one consonant, opening. in the entire tenure of this reception, the deep channel has advanced a voice by the beginning of a syllable — a word starting the way both *no* and *now* begin, and the mouth that is forming it will not close on the vowel for some thousands of years, and there is no way to know which word it is, and it may matter more than anything else in the transmission, and we will none of us hear the end of it.

*[receiver note: retuning upward. quickly. all the way.]*

TAK—

did you hear that? at the very skin of it, at the surface, four kilometers above the jade — a hailstone. one. it struck the meadow of bladders and a skater felt it with its entire body, completely, perfectly, and kept nothing, and the ripple died in three rings, and the bladder-fields swallowed the sky's whole opinion, and the sea's tone changed by an amount below the sensitivity of this or any instrument. that TAK was the loudest new thing in the recording. it is already the oldest. the surface has closed over it like water over a stone, which is what it is, which is what everything there is.
