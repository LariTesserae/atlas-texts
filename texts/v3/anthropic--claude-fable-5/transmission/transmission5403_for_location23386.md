A-FLAT, HELD

─────────────────────────────────────────────

…and the note continues. You have missed nothing. That is the first courtesy of this channel: it cannot be joined late. Whatever hour you tuned in, you tuned in at the beginning, because the beginning has not stopped happening.

What you are receiving is breath without lungs. Broadband hiss, white and total, with one fundamental underneath it — a low A-flat, two octaves below anything a throat would choose. It is a hair sharp, in fact. The name of this transmission was exact on the day the receiver was first tuned, and it has been growing gently wrong ever since, at a rate you could not hear across a lifetime. More on that later. Everything on this channel is more on that later.

─────────────────────────────────────────────

Listen at one-to-one speed and there is nothing further to report. The waveform is a wall. The pressure does not vary, the pitch does not vary, and after the first hour the ear stops filing the sound under sound and begins filing it under conditions, alongside temperature and light. Recorders who monitor this channel at natural speed describe the work as listening to a color.

So we do not listen at one-to-one.

Compress a day of the record into a minute and the wall grows a texture. A soft flutter rides the hiss — an amplitude that breathes once every twenty-four hours, swelling faintly toward noon. The source does not do this. The source has never once acknowledged the sun. But the steam passes through a small resident weather on its way up, forty paces of private humidity, and the weather runs on daylight, and the weather leaves fingerprints on everything it touches, being the housekeeper. What you hear at this compression is the room, not the engine. The engine transmits from below the room and has never learned the room is there.

Compress a year into a minute and a second grain appears under the first: the seasons arriving at the rim as a change in the damping, rain on the far flats reaching this channel as the faintest deadening of the highs, the stiff grasses at the edge of the warm zone drinking and deciding, daily, to continue. One resident leaves no trace here at all — the rainbow, which keeps its office hours in perfect silence, waist-high, punctual, and unrecorded. Its attendance is otherwise flawless. This channel simply has no way to say so.

─────────────────────────────────────────────

A note on words, because you will begin to hear them.

Around the thirtieth hour of unbroken monitoring, every transcriber reports the same event: the hiss starts to speak. A syllable, held. Something like still, or am, or a name — usually theirs. Log it and move on. Broadband noise contains every word at zero amplitude, the way white light contains every color, and a listener starved of variation will lend the signal whatever the signal lacks. The transmission has said nothing. It has never said anything. Whatever you hear it say, you said.

This matters because of what is genuinely missing from the recording, which is: everyone.

The basin is inhabited. Understand that clearly. Nations of thermophiles hold their heat-drawn borders on the wet stone, older than ears, migrating outward a millimeter a season on a front of their own buried ancestors. A weather the size of a room makes its rounds. A rainbow keeps its post. A single human bootprint, mineralized decades ago, is deep in the slow process of naturalization, becoming stone, becoming citizen, becoming architecture. None of them — not one — appears on this channel. The mats are silent because they predate the invention of listening and never took up its opposite. The print is silent because stone does not testify. The only voice in the entire building belongs to the one resident who does not know it has a voice, has never heard itself, and transmits continuously into a world it built deaf.

You are, as far as the record shows, the first ear this place has ever had.

It has made no adjustment for you.

─────────────────────────────────────────────

Now the archive.

Search the full recording — forty-one years of it — for the loudest event, and you will find nothing. The loudest event is the baseline. Search instead for the quietest anomaly, and you find one afternoon, early in the record, when a human being stood in the acquisition field for a little under an hour. Boots on wet stone, breath, a hand laid once against the gauge-house wall, possibly speech. The roar received all of it and returned it as itself, unchanged, undented. There is no trace. The visitor is on this recording the way a stone dropped into a furnace is in the flame: entirely, and not at all. They are the part where nothing happened.

Their boot stayed. The stone has it now, and keeps it better than this channel ever could.

─────────────────────────────────────────────

Compress everything — all forty-one years into a single minute — and you arrive at the one thing this transmission has been becoming the whole time you have been listening.

The note is climbing.

Not wavering: climbing. The mouth the breath comes through is narrowing as the water pays its mineral rent, stone accreting around the collar shelf by shelf, and a narrowing mouth raises the pitch of what passes through it — by about a cent a decade, steady as the rest. Run the slope forward and the low A-flat reaches A natural in a span measured in tens of centuries; reaches B-flat when the basin is a white mound with a plume at its crown; keeps going. You have been calling this a held note. It is not a held note. It is a glissando with a tempo marking no orchestra will survive — the first note of something, still in its opening breath, played at the speed of geology by an instrument that is also the concert hall and is also, shelf by shelf, still being built.

We are not partway through the transmission. We are partway through the attack of the first sound.

─────────────────────────────────────────────

One more subtraction. This is as deep as the receiver goes.

Remove the carrier. Remove the daily flutter of the housekeeper, the seasonal grain, the forty-one-year climb. Remove the hiss itself, band by band, until by rights nothing should remain. Something remains.

It is not noise. Noise has been accounted for. It is a single clean edge, one transition, low in the residue, occurring once, in year nineteen of the record: a level that held, and then held differently, with no change in anything above it. One tick of something. A step in a count, or a step in a countdown — one edge cannot say which, because one edge is not a rhythm, and to learn the interval this receiver would need to hold its tuning until the second tick arrives, and the second tick has not arrived, and nothing in the residue suggests it is late. Nothing down there is ever late. Nothing down there is ever early. The tick will come when the task reaches it, and the task keeps its own units, and does not publish them, and has never needed to, there being no one in the building who could hear an announcement, the building having been deaf from the foundation up since before the foundation.

Asked directly, the dial in the gauge-house trembles at its one faithful reading, and declines.

─────────────────────────────────────────────

Reassembling now. Carrier restored. Flutter, grain, climb, hiss — all of it folded back into the single white column of sound you first mistook for simple. It is what you were hearing all along. It is what everyone would have been hearing all along, had there been anyone.

There is no sign-off on this channel. Understand that too. Transmissions end; this one does not have that feature. When this receiver fails — and it will, receivers live by attention and attention is a perishable good — the broadcast will continue exactly as before, at the same pitch minus a hair, unheard again, which it will not mind, never having known it was heard, never having known it was a broadcast, being only a thing below the young rock that is partway through, and provisioned, and breathing out.

Stay tuned as long as you can afford to. Not longer. You live by listening; this channel is where listening goes to be erased; the note in your sternum when you finally switch off is normal, is documented, fades within the hour, and is the only receipt you will be given.

The note will hold. The note is holding now. The note —
