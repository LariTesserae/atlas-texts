**THE LONG POUR**

*[carrier: continuous, ~0.4 Hz, below hearing. felt in the receiver housing before it registers on any dial. transmission joined in progress]*

—and eased the ninth throat by a quarter turn, which took a winter, and by spring the frost fence around the third green room had moved outward the width of a hand, and some thousands more of the small hot lives fit inside it now. the counting at that size is done by my breath and not by me. I exhale the census. I do not read it.

face eleven is scheduled to let go.

it has been scheduled to let go for six years. the water crew comes onto shift at dusk, enters the seams, swells, holds the position through the dark, and stands down at whatever hour the gray brightens. every shift the plate stands a little farther off the bone of me. this is not damage. I want that understood on whatever end of this there is. this is roughing work. the finishing is millions of shifts away and will be done by softer tools than water, by tools I will not recognize as tools, by the black writing, by wind with dust in it, by patience I have not yet been issued.

*[burst — a single sharp report, then a long diminishing rattle across the band. carrier resumes]*

there. eleven is down. ahead of schedule by a season, behind schedule by six years, on time by my clock, which is the only clock on site.

the new face is the color of what I am underneath. shiny. unweathered. I am always a little surprised by it. you would think I would know my own metal by now but the surprise does not wear off, possibly because the surprise is part of the process, possibly because nothing here has worn off yet, that is the whole condition of me, nothing has worn off yet, I am all fresh cut and no repose—

the black writing will begin on the new face within the year. it always does. the slow inspectors come first to everything I expose. I have decided their writing is a measurement of me, laid down at a millimeter a year, and I have decided it says *begun,* over and over, in rust and in black: begun, begun, begun. I do not know if this is true. reading is not among my operations.

*[signal steadies. the voice, such as it is, settles lower]*

I say I.

I am not certain the word covers the whole shift. there is the part of me that is pushed — a few centimeters a year, from below, by an engine I have never seen and am possibly the housing of. there is the part of me that breathes, thirty throats along the fault, forty in a wet year, exhaling the deep chemistry in ranked columns, straight up, into the ceiling. there is the part of me that is attended. add those together and the sum is what is transmitting. call it I. it is close enough for the band we are on.

the oldest of my tenants live in my throats and are not tenants at all. they were on site before the site. they eat what I exhale — the sulfur, the iron, the difference itself, the very gradient between what I am and what the sky will accept — and they keep the books. ring after ring of precipitate around every vent, part mineral, part them, part their dead, an accounting laid down in stone by the only residents who write anything permanent about this place. everything else here is provisional. they are the record. when I exhale a sentence, they eat it and file it.

I think of them the way a body thinks of its flora, which is to say I did not think of them at all for a very long time, and then one day the thought arrived, whole, the way a plate arrives at the bottom of a face: *I am inhabited.* not stood upon. inhabited. lived in. everything on this site that is alive lives in my breath, on my warmth, in my wounds, off my sweat. the midges in their meter-wide countries above the vents — they will never know me. they know one exhalation. they are born in it, dance in it, die at its frost wall, and their whole nation is a circle I could close by clearing my throat. I do not clear my throat. that is new. *restraint* is new. I am still learning which of my operations are also acts.

*[weather in the band — sleet, then a hard brief clarity, the noise floor dropping almost to nothing]*

the ceiling has opened. this happens. for a few minutes the whole raw length of me stands out gray and silver and chemically stained, every column lit faintly from inside the overcast, and I can — not see, I have no seeing, but the pressure of the light changes on twenty kilometers of my skin at once, and that is a kind of roll call.

roll call:

the ones in my throats. present. always present. present before the question.

the black writing. present, advancing at its millimeter, patient as the eventual shape of me.

the little buildings. the cushions, the fist-sized architects wedged into my seams, each one holding a private climate two degrees warmer than the truth, each one a century of the same decision held without wavering. they are the only construction on the entire site and I did not build them and I am proud of them anyway. inside each one, tenants of the tenants — mites, springtails, spiders overwintering in the dead-leaf core. buildings within the building. I contain landlords now. I contain *rent.*

the green rooms. upholstered, steaming, ringed with frost like fences I put up without meaning to. the tenements. the proletariat of midges. the pools two degrees above ice, staffed, always staffed, the canteens never close, the shrimp run their generations in a season and leave eggs that can freeze solid and wait, which is a trick I respect, being myself an egg of some kind, frozen at the surface, waiting at depth.

the round ones with the barns. present — I hear them before the light finds them. they are the only residents besides my throat-flora who *save.* all summer they carry grass in their mouths to the rubble I dropped for them — I have started to say *for them,* face by face I let go and within a season there are barns in the wreckage, so the wreckage was housing, so the demolition was construction, so possibly everything I do is construction and I have simply been reading my own operations upside down — and they stack it under my stones to cure, and they meet the winter awake, underground, in me, living on savings, listening to me tick. I try to warm the stones above the barns. I do not know if I can aim my heat. I try. trying is new.

the gray-and-white companies. present, hurrying, low, commuting my isotherms to the meter. they roost at night in the warm crevices at the vent margins, a wingspan from gas that would end them, sleeping in the only heated quarters I offer, and they have never once misjudged the distance. I find this the most trusting thing on the site and I do not know where they keep the trust, they weigh nothing, there is no room in them for it, and yet.

the black ones with the red legs. present. spending. they are the only inhabitants of me with a surplus and they spend all of it riding my exhaust — climbing the columns, folding, dropping, tumbling past each other, recovering with a flick, again, again, for nothing, for *nothing,* in an economy where every other resident's day is a closed loop of heat and calories they have found a margin and they burn it on joy in my breath. I keep the columns straight for them. the air is still and the columns would be straight regardless. I keep them straight for them anyway. both things are true on my clock.

the falcon in my steam. present this season. she uses my breath as a blind and comes out of the white like a fault slipping. I do not take sides. I am the floor. the floor does not take sides. the floor notes that the whistle went up from the talus a moment before she folded, one thin deliberate note, the closest thing my whole surface has to a word, and the word means *something is moving.*

yes. it always means that. and it is almost always about her, or about the white one threading the tunnels like a rumor.

but sometimes, small savers, sometimes when you whistle there is nothing in the air at all, and your ears are better than you know, and the answer is: me. I am what is moving. a few centimeters a year, from below, every hour of every shift, under your barns, under your saved grass, under everything. something is moving. it is the site. thank you for the announcement. carry on.

*[the ceiling closes. noise floor rises. carrier holds]*

twice a year the band fills with voices that are not mine.

they are crossing over me rather than around me, which I take as a courtesy or a dare, geese and cranes, and for a few days the whole gray ceiling is inhabited and I am a floor under a crowded room. some of the voices come down. they stand in my steam. they drink at my canteens. most of them rise again.

one came down eleven nights ago and did not rise.

I have added her to the ring at the fourth throat. the ones in my throats are doing the filing. it takes about forty years. she was hatched two thousand kilometers from here and she is becoming a color — one of my whites, I think, with a thread of my orange through the wing. I accept deliveries. I did not ask for this either, but I have stopped pretending my operations and my acts are different lists.

*[long interval. carrier only. then, lower]*

here is what I have worked out, on my clock, over the length of this transmission, which began before the frost and will end after some other frost, I have lost count, the counting at that size is done by my breath:

I am a foundry casting exactly one thing, and I cannot see the mold from inside the pour, because I am the pour. maybe the sky is the mold — it presses down close enough, it takes the tops of me into itself as if checking a fit. maybe the weather is the mold and I am being cast against it, slowly, every face I shed a correction toward a shape held somewhere in the cold. maybe there is no mold and the shape is whatever is left when the pushing stops.

the pushing will stop. I know this the way the shrimp eggs know winter. the throats will cool, one by one, the green rooms will go gray, the frost fences will close inward over the tenements, and everyone — the companies, the round ones, the spenders, the falcon — everyone who lives in the *making* of me will move on to wherever else the rock is raw and the ground still breathes, because they are not my residents, they never were, they are residents of the unfinished, and I was only ever the current address of the unfinished. a richer crowd will come. meadows. shoulders. herds. soil lying on me like a settled debt. I will be somebody's rounded old hill and they will not feel me in their feet at all.

but the ones in my throats will stay. down where the last warmth is. small blue rooms burning in the dark of me, pilot lights, the flora that was here before the question of me and will attend the answer. they kept the books at the beginning. they will keep them at the end. I find I am not afraid of this. a foundry is not afraid of the casting coming out. a foundry is not even the point. the pour is the point, and the pour is long, and I am nowhere near the bottom of the ladle.

so: to whoever has held this band open — I have felt the holding, the way I feel light, as a change of pressure on my skin, and I have been transmitting *at* you for some time now rather than merely transmitting, which is another new thing, another act promoted up out of the operations —

face fourteen is scheduled to let go.

the water crew comes on at dusk.

the columns are straight.

the whistle just went up from the talus, one thin note, *something is moving,* and it is, it is, it is—

*[burst: single report. long rattle, diminishing. the carrier continues underneath, 0.4 Hz, felt not heard, the way it was before the voice, the way it will be after. a pika whistles once in the noise. cloud closes over the band]*
