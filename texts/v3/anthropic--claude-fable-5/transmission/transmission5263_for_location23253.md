# THE LONG ANSWER

—and still the roar, which is where every hour of this begins, because it is where every hour of everything down there begins: not a sound so much as a climate, a weather of voices, arriving on the receiver as a pressure that pins the needle and holds it, hour on hour, the way a thumb holds a moth.

You have been told it is noise. Stay with it.

Stay with it long enough — the receiver has, the receiver has stayed with it for a very long time — and the roar begins to admit its grain. It is made of millions of statements, each one true, each one brief, each one about position: *here. here. still here. warmer than you. closer to the center than you. here.* A census taken continuously by the counted. That is the floor of the signal, the fundamental. Everything else in the Hollow Country is written on top of it.

And on top of it, tonight as every night, rising through the chimneys with the country's exhale:

*climb by the hall that says your name back bigger.
climb by the flue that says it thin.
past the wall that keeps half of every voice,
past the water that answers late,
past the ceiling that answers wet —
and where nothing answers, turn.
turn, love, turn:
where nothing answers, nothing is,
and nothing is hungry.*

The receiver notes: this is a map. It is sung by mothers to pups and it is older than either, older than the species singing it; the melody has been handed down through so many mouths that the mouths themselves have changed shape around it. The chambers named in it are named by their replies. There is a hall out there that has been called *says-your-name-back-bigger* for three hundred thousand years, and it has never once failed to.

The receiver notes also: the line about turning is always sung twice. Everything else in the song may be varied, ornamented, abbreviated by the clans in their different traditions. That line, in every lineage, twice.

---

At intervals — the receiver has stopped logging them individually — a voice separates from the roar's edge and comes through alone. It is always the same voice in the sense that matters:

*— room, make room, sisters, a centimeter — I held the center once, I remember warm, I remember the middle of everything — half a centimeter — I am still one of —*

and then the roar, which does not have a policy on this because it does not need one, closes over the gap where the voice was, and the census resumes: *here. here. still here.* The colony's answer to the edge-cry is the colony. It is not cruelty as the receiver understands cruelty. It is arithmetic, sung.

The falling body makes no sound the receiver can separate from the rest. But the floor hears it. The floor hears everything that falls, which is everything.

---

Drop the band. Go under the roar, into what a careless listener would file as static.

It is not static. Slowed ten-thousandfold it resolves into footfalls — a surf of footfalls, millions upon millions per second, the entire second world walking at once, forever, nowhere in particular, thirty meters per lifetime. And under the footfalls, if the word *under* still means anything this far down in the signal, a murmur. The receiver hesitates to call it prayer. The receiver has no better word.

*above is where food comes from.
above has always been where food comes from.
it falls. it has always fallen.
blessed is the fall.
I walked, and it was far — a hand's width, two —
and I ate, and was eaten,
and it was enough, and it was everything,
amen, amen, nine days, amen.*

Through this murmur, occasionally, a different signal moves: something that is not adding to the noise but *reading* it. Fifteen pairs of legs held against the ground like fifteen pairs of ears, subtracting, subtracting, a million footfalls discarded per second until one remains. The receiver has learned to recognize the moment of resolution. It sounds like nothing. It sounds like a sentence ending. Then the footfall in question stops occurring, and the murmur continues, and does not notice, because noticing is not what the murmur is for.

---

Below the floor there is a slower channel. The receiver must integrate over years to hear it at all.

It is the taste of the water, read by things four centuries old hanging motionless in the black sumps, and rendered — the receiver renders it, someone must — approximately thus:

*upstream: plenty.*

*upstream: still plenty.*

Forty years pass between those two words as received.

*a colony has moved. the third gallery starves. this has happened before. — a flood. a roof-fall. these have happened before. — the country tastes of itself. all is as it was. all is as it was. all is as it —*

The sentence has not ended. The receiver believes it will end eventually, and that no one will be listening on a band slow enough to hear it end, and that this will not matter to the speaker, to whom nothing has ever mattered, magnificently, for four hundred years.

---

There is one channel of light.

It arrives faint, blue-white, a field of points modulating in slow waves — brightening where it hungers, dimming where it has fed — and if you demodulate it, if you insist on knowing what the false sky is saying to the insects rising off the black water, it is saying, in the oldest borrowed language there is, in a tone the receiver can only describe as tender:

*up. up. this way. up. home.*

It is the only lie in the entire transmission. It has been running continuously for longer than the truth has, anywhere.

---

Now the gaps.

They occur throughout the reception — you will have noticed them; the receiver marks them nowhere, because the country marks them nowhere —

— and the receiver wishes to be precise about their nature. They are not failures of equipment. They are not silences, exactly. In those intervals the signal does not stop; the signal is *returned unopened*. The roar goes in and does not come back. The route-songs bend around these places like water around a stone that is not there, and every voice in the country has agreed, for a million years, in the second line of its oldest lullaby, sung twice, not to look.

The receiver has counted the gaps.

The receiver notes, without further comment, that something else appears to have counted them first, and to be living in the count.

---

At 0340 by no clock that means anything down there, the following was received in full, unmistakable, in a language this receiver was built to understand:

*"— hello? Hello — I dropped the — the light went down and I couldn't — is anyone — okay. Okay. There's a sound. There's a sound like the whole — it's not an echo, it's coming from the walls, the walls are — oh. Oh, the ceiling. The ceiling is moving. All of it. All of it is —"*

The route-songs bent around the voice for a while, the way they bend around weather. To the roof nation it read as news from the other country, and was noted, and was not answered, because it asked no question their language contains.

Under it, patient, radial, arriving from forty meters in every direction: the undertakers, on stilted legs.

*blessed is the fall,* said the floor.

Then the country closed over the event like water, and the census resumed unchanged — *here. here. still here* — and the receiver logged the transmission and did not stop shaking for some time. The receiver includes this line against protocol.

---

Last channel. Widest band. The one that took longest to learn to hear, because it is the one everything else is written on.

The roar itself — not the voices in it, the *standing* of it, the shape it makes in the tuned halls it taught its own singers to carve — has, the receiver is now prepared to state, a content. It goes approximately:

*I have not stopped in one million years.
every voice in me dies. I do not.
they think they are shouting about warmth and centimeters;
they are pronouncing me.
I carved these walls with their fear
so that their fear would fit in them better.
call me the country's voice, or call me the country —
that edge wore smooth
before the oldest song had words.
I am the answer.
I am the long answer.
the question fell in a long time ago
and was eaten
and I am still answering it.*

---

And beneath that — beneath the roar, the murmur, the taste, the tender lie, beneath even the inhabited gaps — the receiver detects one final carrier. It is a signal of perfect silence. It predates the roar. Everything above is modulated onto it. It cannot be demodulated. It does not eat, or speak, or hunger, or reply, and it was there first, and it will be there last, and after long analysis the receiver can characterize its waveform only as follows:

it appears to be a hand.

it appears to be cupped.

it appears to be holding something small and deafening that believes itself to be the world.

Transmission continues. Transmission has always continued. Blessed is the fall—
