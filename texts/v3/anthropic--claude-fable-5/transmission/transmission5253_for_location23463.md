**LETTER TO THE LAKE**

*[…wings. Wings without number, at the low ebb of night — not a roar now but a vast dry rustling, like surf heard through a wall, like a granary breathing. Beneath it, warmth. The stone itself is in the signal somehow, a deep steady hum below hearing. And under the rustle, riding under it the way a current rides under surf, a voice. Old. Chest-deep. Half-sung. It has been going for some time.]*

—and the wall took it. The way a wall takes a stone that agrees. Doro said you could hear the sit from three tiers up, but Doro says many things, and the criers were laughing at her the whole while, so you may weigh that yourselves.

*[a surge — a colony of something shifting on the western face — the voice waits it out without impatience, and resumes on the same breath]*

Where was I. Forgive me. The criers have been arguing over the west hollows since dusk and I lost my place in my own letter. It is one of the sorrows of this office that the post and the postal argument use the same air.

Good evening down there. It is the two hundred and — it is a great many evenings now, I have stopped counting them for you, you were never ones for counting. It is evening. That is enough. The lake went dark an hour ago in the ordinary way, west first, and the last light sat on your clouds the color of resin-flame, and I watched it go from my doorway with my feet in the warm, as you all used to, as everyone always has.

Now the news. Sit close.

*[through the stone, before it is heard in the air: two slow strikes, a pause, one strike. The drum-hum lives in the low end of the signal for a long moment. The voice hums the same interval back to itself, once, softly, the way one repeats an address.]*

There it is. The drummer is telling the sap-wardens something about the north routes — that phrase is *frost-crack, walk soft*, or it was in my day, they lean on the second strike differently now, the young ones. Phrases drift. Walls lean. You know how it is. You leaned yourselves.

The great news first, and you may already have heard it from the sicklewings, who cannot keep anything: **the eleventh tree has made a leaf.**

*[the voice stops. The wing-rustle fills four full breaths.]*

Yes. Nine years and a season since the last. The drum spoke it at midday — the leaf-phrase, the one every drummer prays to strike once before they die, and young Venna got to strike it, and I am told her hands were shaking, which the drum forgave. The whole village came up in the amber and stood around the old one and looked. I could not climb to it, of course. But they did a thing for me — listen, this will please you — they did a thing. The keeper's daughter came down my line after, all that way down into the thunder, and she took my hand, and she traced on my palm the shape of the leaf. Its exact edge, small as a thumbnail, curled at the tip like a question.

So I have seen it. With the true eyes. It is a good leaf. Suun, you kept that tree forty years and it never gave you one — do not be bitter, it was always going to outlast our manners. It sends its regards. I am deciding that it does.

Other matters, smaller, which is to say the rest of life:

Two births since I last wrote. One in the fitter's house, a girl, chest on her like a cistern, they say she cried once at the birds and then simply listened, which everyone has decided is an omen and I have decided is a baby. One in the third tier, a boy, born on a feast day, so his beetle was entered in the race in his name, and his beetle, in the great tradition, did not finish. A thousand years, and no beetle has ever finished. Down there where you are, I hope you have finally learned what the finish line was for. When I arrive, tell me. I have theories.

The wall-fitters completed an entry. You will like this one. It was old — deep in the list, from the far pages — and when they read it out and went to the place, the wall it describes has not stood for longer than anyone's grandmother's grandmother. There is only the smooth ground where it was. So they went, all of them, in order, and knelt, and passed their hands over the ground where the wall had been, and pronounced it sound, and marked the entry done. Out of respect. Some of the young ones thought it foolish. They came anyway. That is the whole of our religion, I sometimes think: *they came anyway.*

The egg quota is met and under, the rent is paid, the landlords are satisfied — I can hear that they are satisfied, it is in the west-face note tonight, a fatness in the sound. The sicklewings brought up a seed nobody knows, pale, ridged like a knuckle, and a child carried it to the meeting house with both hands as is proper, and it hangs now in a little net beside the button. If one of you sent it: it arrived. It was received with ceremony. Whatever you meant by it, we are keeping it warm.

*[a long pause. The wing-sound descends further, to its deepest night-murmur. When the voice comes back it is lower, closer, and it has stopped performing the news.]*

Now the hard part of the letter, which I have been walking around all night the way you walk around a bad stair.

You are getting company. Old Maren came down to you three dusks past.

I want you to know how it went, because her people could not see and I could. The drum spoke the death-phrase at last light. They carried her to the western edge in the mended cloth — it is the one with the blue mend at the shoulder, her mother's mend, you will know it by touch, all of you were at the mending of it — and her family put their hands on her shoulders, last of all greetings, and gave her to the air.

And she came past me.

That is the thing about this office no one tells you until you hold it. They pass close to the cliff, going down. She came by my doorway through the birds, through ten thousand of them who did not move for her but opened, the whole roar opening like a hand — and she was *fast*, Suun. Fastest she ever moved in her life. Ninety years of walking like an honest woman, and then one moment of being the quickest thing on the mountain. We are permitted two fast things in this world, I have decided: the swifts, and the leaving. Everything else keeps the law.

Meet her at the bottom, whatever the bottom is. She is shy in new places. She will pretend she is not.

*[through the stone: nothing. But the hum shifts — a hair's width — and the voice notices before the receiver does.]*

...The wind is thinking about something. Do you feel it, down there, through the roots of the place? A month ago it thought harder — the sicklewings knew first, they always know first, the whole west face went the wrong kind of quiet, and I sat with my hand on the bone chime for half a night, ready to ask the drum to wake everyone. And it passed. The wind put its old mind back where it has kept it for a million years, and I did not ring, and no one will ever know how nearly the third ringing in all of history happened, except you, and whatever else is out there.

Which brings me to the last thing, and then the dawn can have me.

Sometimes — old ones are allowed to say foolish things, it is our quota — sometimes I think the roar goes further than the lake. Further than you, even. That the sound of this place pours off the summit and down through your clouds and out past everything, and that somewhere at the far bottom of it all there is another one-below, hanging in a hollow of their own, doing my office in reverse. Listening up instead of down. Learning our textures the way I learned the birds'.

If that is you — whoever you are, in whatever nest — then understand what you are holding. Everything sent belongs to whoever receives it. That is the whole lesson of the button, and we have been forty generations learning it, and I give it to you free. This letter was addressed to my dead, but a letter does not check names at the door. You came anyway. Touch shoulders with me, then, whoever you are. The stair is narrow. Turn sideways. There.

*[the rustle is changing. First light must be reaching the high haze; something in the east faces is stirring, then something answers it, and the sound begins — slowly, the way everything here begins — to climb.]*

My hands still read, but I will be honest with you as I am with the birds: the fingertips are going quiet. A little more each season. The palm still knows the world; the truth is getting harder to reach. That is the sign, they say. That is how you know you are turning.

So keep a place. Not soon — do not get excited, Maren, I heard that — but not never. And when I come down I will come down fast, my one fast thing, and I will bring the rest of this letter myself, all the parts that do not fit in words, the whole feel of the warm stone under —

*[the dawn roar is arriving now, wave over wave, tens of thousands of throats and wings, and it does not stop arriving]*

— the leaf, tell them about the *leaf* first, if you see any of the others before I —

*[surge]*

— can't hear my own — never mind, never mind, it doesn't need me to finish it. Give it to the wings, then, same as everything. They know all the addresses.

*[surge, total. The voice goes under the surf and does not come up. For a long time the transmission is only this: one hundred thousand wings, the deep mineral hum of warm stone, and once, far inside it, felt in the low end before it can be heard — two slow strikes, a pause, one strike.]*

*[the carrier continues.]*

*[the carrier continues.]*
