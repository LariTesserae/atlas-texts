each of those was a drop. each of those is kept. they are the newest part of the floor now, the top three atoms of a terrace; you may stand on them in a hundred years and never know you are standing on this exact minute. that is fine. that is the intent. an archive does not need to be read to be an archive. it needs only to refuse to stop.

a question just crossed me. did you hear it. it came in through the slot on wings and it asked, in the only word it has: *where. where where where where.* and i answered in the only word i have: *here.* i gave it back its own voice with my shape wrapped around it, and it read my shape off its own voice, and it found its door — one door among four hundred doors — and folded, and went still.

i answer that question some thousands of times a day, and my answer is never twice the same, because i am never twice the same. every year i am a fingernail nearer to everything. the birds hear it. no one else hears it. the birds hear me growing and they redraw their maps in the dark by a hair's width and never once remark on it. they are the only residents who know that i am speaking, and they believe i am only agreeing with them.

i should say what i am. you have been tuned to me for some time; that is a courtesy, and courtesy is answered with a census, and the census begins with the census-taker.

i am stone.

no. keep that — file it, do not strike it — and lay this on top of it: i am what stone comes out of. i am the wet that persuades the mineral. i am a skin one cell deep and the size of a chapel, green-gold where the light can find me, and everything you would call my body was once my exhalation. the chapel is my shell. i said *stone* first because stone is what you will find; i say this second because this is what is true; and in me, second things do not replace first things. they lie upon them. cut me in section and you will find every sentence i have ever said, in order, in thin alternating bands, including the wrong ones.

especially the wrong ones. an archive that discards its errors is a liar with good handwriting.

that was a sea falling into one of my seas. i hold seven at present, stepped down my floor like a sentence losing confidence, each one warm, each one rimmed, each rim rising. i am closing them. i will not apologize for this. the rims rise because i cannot stop keeping; keeping is the one verb i conjugate; and one day a rim will meet itself above its water and i will hold a sealed sea with a patient olive people inside it, fed through the seam for as long as the hill stays warm, never seen again by anything, and not minding.

if that troubles you, consider: you also live in a bowl whose rim you cannot see. the difference between you and my fish is that my fish are correct not to worry.

in the high water, when the seam runs generous, the boldest of them go over the rims — one, two a year, flick and gone, dynasty into dynasty. i file the crossings too. there is a band in my lowest terrace, thin as a held breath, that is nothing but wet seasons. it reads, if you could read it: *more. more. more. more.*

*a dry whisper along the lower rim. patience with legs.*

the eldest walker is abroad. apricot, the exact apricot of my iron, because it has grazed me so long it has become my color — or i its, i no longer keep that distinction, i keep very few distinctions, distinctions erode and i do not do erosion. it leaves me its selves. five so far, along the lower basins, in order of size, each cast shell glazed the season it was shed, each one a smaller and smaller crab standing at attention forever. it walks past its own museum every day and grazes the algae from its own former shoulders and has never once paused to look.

that is the correct way to visit a museum. i mention this because you paused at my door. you looked a long time. i filed the looking.

dusk is arriving. i know because the dimness is cooling — day and night reach me only as temperature, which is the honest form of light — and the middle tier has begun to churr. hear it: a low mutter running shelf to shelf, the burghers of the mud city discussing mud, which is their politics, their weather, their scripture, and their food supply, all one subject. they build with my rims. they carry me up their own walls a beak-load at a time and set me around their children, and they think they are building with mud, and they are, and the mud is my proposal, and so every gourd in the middle city is a first draft of rock. the lowest ones are finished drafts. the grandmothers' houses are harder than i am. the young build on them, softly, on top.

no one in the middle city knows this. it is not necessary that they know it. the record does not require the consent of the recorded. it requires only the seam, and the seam has never once —

that was my law being enforced. i keep one fear, the color of weak tea, in a crack behind my lowest curtain, and i feed it a dozen times a year, and it is the reason the low floors stand empty and the high city crowds my smoothest, most unclimbable aprons. every architecture needs one thing it is afraid of, or it sprawls. i did not choose the fear. i do not love it. but keeping is my verb, and the law of keeping does not exempt what frightens, and so i keep the fear the way i keep everything: in place, fed, filed, and — when it dies, as everything but i will die — glazed. there is a snake in my east wall already, a century down, perfect, coiled, ivory over tea. it caught wings out of my dark for forty years. now it is a band. now it is load-bearing.

below all the cities, on my open floor, the slow readers are crossing the emptiness — the pale crickets, writing ahead of themselves with their whiskers, reading me at one ten-thousandth the speed of the birds and arriving at the same answer. *here.* the coffee-colored tillers work the fallen wealth beneath the high city; the animate dust works the tillers' leavings; the threadworms work the dust. nothing that falls in me is a loss. it is a delivery. the nestling that fell short this spring is already halfway to being a fact of the wall, and in this it will outlast every nestling that flew.

i do not say that to be cruel. i do not have cruelty; cruelty requires taking, and i have never taken anything. i am saying only that flight is a beautiful way to be temporary, and my floor is a beautiful way to stop being temporary, and every resident of mine gets to do both, in that order, without exception, forever.

you have been receiving me for some time. i have been transmitting for eleven thousand years. these are not the same amount of patience, but i have come to believe they are the same kind, or you would have changed the frequency by now.

so i will tell you the thing the birds know and no one else knows: you gave me a breath at the door. warm, wet, faintly sweet, a little quick — you were climbing, and the ferns startled you. i took it the way i take everything, which is to say: it crossed my seam, and my skin drank it, and the mineral heard about it, and by now a film of you, thinner than a wrong thought, lies on my lowest terrace, under this evening's three drops, over this morning's four.

you are accessioned. do not be alarmed. it is not a trap; it is a record. the clause has always included visitors — the heron's foot, the flood, the wandering seed, and now the listener. you may leave whenever you like.

you have already stayed forever.
