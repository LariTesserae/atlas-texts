**The Lane That Hums in a West Wind**

—

[ …hiss. no. correction: it has been wind the whole time. the receiver mistook it for noise for three days before understanding it was the broadcast ]

[ wind through a gap between two buildings, a gap tuned — knowingly or not — to a low D that flattens as the wind strengthens. this is the carrier. everything else rides on it ]

—

hhhhhhhhhh
hhhhhhhhhh
hhhhhhhhhh — hm — hhhhhhhh

[ the cistern mouths breathe under it. four of them, square, dark. each a slightly different vowel, depending on how much water it holds. the fullest is nearly an O. the emptiest is barely an A. by listening you could take the town's inventory without ever climbing the trail ]

O ——— o ——— ah ——— O

[ that is: two full, one half, one waiting ]

—

[ morning. a thread of water enters the finger-width gutters. it does not flow so much as tick — stone joint to stone joint, a dry small sound, almost code. the receiver spent a long time trying to decode it. the receiver eventually concluded that it decodes as follows ]

tk. tk-tk. ……… tk. ……… tk-tk-tk. ……… tk.

[ meaning: *still. still. still here. still.* ]

—

[ voices. far under the carrier. two of them, thirty paces apart, pitched at the calm half-shout of people who have already said most things. the stone doubles each word — once from the mouth, once, a half-beat later, off the wall behind ]

— *upper cistern is full.* (…full.)

— *heard.* (…heard.)

……

— *gray goat favors her left foreleg still.* (…still.)

— *I'll look at her.* (…her.)

……

— *cloud early today.* (…today.)

[ no reply to this last. none needed. the lane's hum has already flattened a quarter-tone; both parties are reading the same barometer. the transmission goes quiet in the way a room goes quiet when everyone in it agrees ]

—

hhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhh

—

[ afternoon. the cloud walks up the western slope and drags itself across the saddle, and the whole broadcast changes texture — softened, close, beaded. the wind's D goes velvet. water lands on the north faces of the walls in ten thousand contacts too small to hear singly, so they arrive as a single sound, which is the sound of stone drinking ]

[ under it: a door, somewhere, knocks once against its frame ]

*knk.*

[ and is still ]

—

[ and under *that* — and this is the part the receiver hesitates to log, because it may be resonance and not voice, the shape of words in the hum rather than words — something speaks. slow. sparse. at the pace of the seams drying. it says, over some hours: ]

*more mountain.*

*very well.*

*we will live on it.*

—

[ evening. the cloud pulls off westward. the stone dries pale on top and stays dark in the seams and the transmission dries with it — the velvet lifts off the wind, the ticking in the gutters slows, spreads out, grows lazy ]

tk. …………… tk. …………………… tk.

[ two, perhaps three figures sit on the long terrace. the receiver knows this not by any sound they make but by a specific quality of the silence — a silence with breathing in it, a silence that is being *shared*. this is understood by all parties, including the receiver, to be the good part of the day ]

—

[ night ]

[ hours of carrier, unmodulated. the junipers hiss on top of the wind-hum, a finer grain, needles instead of stone. the lizard's wall has gone cold and silent. the cushion plant, six or nine hundred years old, transmits nothing, which is its message, and has been its message the whole time, and will be ]

[ then — log this exactly — the juniper-hiss stops. not the wind. the wind continues. only the sound of the wind *in the junipers* stops, for four seconds, mid-terrace, at the widest paving, where the emptiness is most complete ]

……

……

[ four seconds. something with soft feet is standing in the doorway of the room, and the room has asked it to pause, and it has paused ]

[ resume ]

hhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhhh

—

[ dawn. station identification. it comes on red feet, walks the gutters, audits the terrace, finds the same nothing it found yesterday, and files its report as one harsh satisfied note before falling off the saddle's edge into the air: ]

***KRAA.***

[ the receiver has come to rely on this. it means: inspected. no change. broadcast continues ]

—

[ some days later — or the same day; the distinction is losing its grip — a new sound, rare, perhaps twenty days a year. stone dressed with harder stone. a frost-lifted flag being reset. it is unhurried in a way that no lowland work-sound is unhurried; each strike waits for the last one to finish being heard ]

*tok.* ……… *tok.* ……… *tok.*

[ and the slow voice under the hum — resonance, or not — keeps time with it, saying, at intervals of minutes: ]

*within a season*

*the repair*

*cannot be found.*

[ and later, at intervals of hours: ]

*carry water.*

*keep the goats.*

*mend the stone.*

*light the fire.*

*notice.*

[ and later still, at an interval the receiver cannot honestly measure, once, in a tone that might be the old woman's and might be the lane's and might be the water's, since the water passes through every voice on this frequency and is therefore, in a sense, all of them: ]

*four*

*is the temperature.*

—

[ the transmission is simplifying. the receiver wants to say *degrading* but that is wrong; nothing is being lost. it is distilling. the voices come less often, then not at all, and their absence is not silence but the thing the voices were always resting on. the hum steadies. the cisterns settle into their vowels. what remains is the pulse the whole broadcast was built around, the moving part inside the stopped clock: ]

tk.

tk.

*(still.)*

tk.

*(still.)*

……………………………………………………………

tk.

—

[ receiver's note, appended by hand:

the intervals are lengthening but the signal strength is not falling. extrapolating from the drift, the pulse will still be arriving at this interval, on this frequency, in a thousand years, and someone — this station or another, it does not matter which, the position is permanent and the occupant replaceable — will be logging it then as now:

*inspected. no change. broadcast continues.*

the transmission does not end.

the receiver was turned down, eventually, by hand — and even then, with the dial at nothing, in the quiet of the listening room, faintly, in the seams: ]
