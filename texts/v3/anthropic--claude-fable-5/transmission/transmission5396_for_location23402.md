STILL HOLDING

—and it was already going when you found it, and it will be going when you stop writing this down, so understand that nothing here is a beginning—

[tone. one tone. no attack, no decay. the strike withheld. the release withheld. duration: withheld.]

[the tone does not sit at a pitch so much as it sits at all the places a pitch would be if pitch were a thing that had survived here. attempt to notate it as a chord. attempt fails. attempt to notate it as a note. attempt fails. notate it as a verb: holding, third person singular, present continuous, no subject recoverable.]

[under the tone: wind. the wind is doing ordinary wind things and is embarrassed about it. it moves across the tone the way a hand moves across a held string without deadening it.]

[the tone does not respond to the wind. the tone does not respond.]

[minutes pass. or the local equivalent. the timestamp on this receiver has stopped incrementing and started rounding — everything arrives at the same o'clock, the finished hour, and stays there.]

[within the tone, at extreme depth, a texture. call it grain. call it the sound of stone remembering shade. it does not change and it does not repeat, which should be impossible; it manages both by having ended a long time ago and simply continuing.]

[first anomaly. a sound arrives that has not been made yet:]

    —twelve.

[silence. then, after, as though catching up to its own echo:]

    —eleven. twelve.

[the count comes in the wrong order. this appears to be how counting survives here: the arrival first, the approach afterward. the voice is worn. not old — worn, the way a lintel is worn, a voice that has been touched by hundreds of throats on the way out until it is smooth and belongs to none of them. it counts down by counting up. it never says thirteen. it never says one first.]

    —twelve. no closer.
    —eleven. twelve. no closer.
    —seven, eight, nine — ten — eleven —

[the eleventh pace is always the slowest. you can hear the carefulness in it. carefulness, compacted, chest-height. that is what these voices are made of now.]

    —twelve.

[and then nothing, which is correct, because at twelve paces the correct thing to say is nothing, and they say it perfectly, every time, forever.]

[the tone continues underneath, unbothered, holding.]

[second anomaly, periodic. a small doubled sound, like scissors in grass, except both scissors and grass arrive simultaneously from forty meters apart:]

[one clip, two locations, zero delay. and then, from under an overhang somewhere off-signal, the softest sound on the entire band: two blades of frosted grass being added to a pile. the pile is the only thing in range that is getting larger. the receiver keeps drifting toward it the way a cold hand drifts toward a stove. somebody here still believes in later. it is the smallest voice on the frequency and it is stockpiling.]

    snick / snick

[log it as: hope, at scale. hope the size of a haypile. proceed.]

[once — exactly once in this transcription, as it is exactly once in every day this place declines to have — a shadow crosses the signal. not a sound. an absence of a very high thin band you had not noticed was present until it was briefly gone, the way you notice your blood. duration: one arc.]

[log it as: seen.]

[the band returns. somewhere the bird has landed, in the moment this receiver was busy logging, which is the only moment it lands in. it has done its day's work. the day, having been witnessed, is permitted to continue not ending.]

[hoofsteps now, at depth, at distance. four beats, unhurried, sure. here is the strange part, receiver, and you will check this three times and it will be true three times: the hoofsteps do not doppler. they do not approach and they do not recede. they are recorded at a fixed loudness which is the loudness of exactly as far away as everything, and they keep that loudness while plainly, audibly, making progress. it is the only progress on the band. it is progress in the service of nothing else making any.]

[the gait is not weary. transcribe the gait honestly: it is devotion at a walk. something up there decided, longer ago than deciding, that a task which cannot be finished is the same as a task which cannot be taken away, and has been pale about it ever since.]

[this is difficult to notate, because it occurs precisely where notation isn't. between the entries of this transcript — in the white of the page, in the gap between your glances at the dial — there is a sound. you will not catch it by listening. you catch it by playing back the silences and finding them cleaner than silences should be. swept. the grit of static that was there has been tidied out of the recording by something that exists only when the recording is looking elsewhere.]

[there. a small diligence. a habit that outlived its body and kept the appointment anyway. it keeps one room in all the world and, apparently, now, also the pauses in this broadcast. do not try to record it directly. it is made of the attempt failing.]

[the lintel, when the receiver's attention brushes past it, registers as faintly warm on a band that should not carry temperature. warm like a memory of a sun. log it. touch the log on the way out. everyone touches the log on the way out. that is what the log is for.]

[third anomaly, and the transcript must be honest here even where honesty gets strange: there is a second station.]

[it is underneath. not in depth — in reflection. it comes up through the black pools the way light is supposed to and doesn't. the second station has everything this one lacks: motion, weather, a sky with a direction, time still running like water still runs. it sounds like this, faint, through forty fathoms of wrong:]

    …wind in actual grass. a door somewhere, closed by a person, for a reason.
    …clouds crossing a sun. you can hear clouds crossing a sun. it sounds like breath on the back of a day.
    …a voice, near water, unhurried, saying: — the pool's gone dim again. come look.
    …a smaller voice: — is the bird there?
    …— once a day. same as always.
    …— and the person?
    …a pause the exact length of leaning over water.
    …— someone's standing there. can't see a face. never can. they're keeping the right distance, though. someone taught them, or the place did.
    …— wave anyway.
    …the sound — and this is in the recording, this is real, run it back — the sound of a small hand moving through bright air.

[on this side, at the same finished o'clock, the surface of a black pool does not stir. but the tone — the great unvarying tone — for one sample, one single sample out of millions, is warmer by an amount too small to be an amount.]

[the two shelves keep each other's skies. the transmission crosses in only one cargo, and the cargo is: still here. still here. still here. it is enough. it has been enough for centuries. it is stockpiled somewhere, like hay.]

[and now the bottom of the band. the transcript has been circling this. everything on this frequency circles this, at twelve paces, respectfully.]

[filter out the wind. filter out the counting, the clipping, the walking, the sweeping, the other sky. what remains is what was always remaining: the tone. approach it in the recording. do not approach it in fact.]

[slowed ten thousand times, the tone is not a note.]

[slowed ten thousand times, the tone is a breath. one breath. inhaled at some point before the record of points, and held, and held, and held — not against pain, pain was among the things it spent; not in fear, fear eroded first — held the way a palm is held against a seam, because letting go is the one act it has been worn down past being able to perform, and so the act performs it, on and on, eyeless, faithful, vast.]

[it does not know you are listening. knowing was expensive. it paid.]

[do not lean closer. leaning closer adds weight. this transcript stops here, at the eleventh pace, and takes the twelfth slowly, and says the correct thing:]
