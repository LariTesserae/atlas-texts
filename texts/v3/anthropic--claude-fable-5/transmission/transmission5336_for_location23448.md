NOTHING CARRIES FAR

*[tick. tick-tick. tick. — droplets off an eave, uncounted, uncountable, arriving at the microphone the way rain arrives at a pond, and under it a hum too low to be a note, felt in the casing of the receiver itself, and under that: nothing, which is also water]*

*[the tick-pattern shifts. someone tuned to this would say: wind's gone two points north]*

— ...panel at the northwest corner going brown, only the corner, frame's sound, it's the drip off the crane hook above it, drip's gone acid, look to the hook —

*[a child's voice. maybe eleven. the report continues below the threshold, three more findings, the words dissolving into texture; an adult voice answers with a sound that is not quite a word, that means: heard, logged, good]*

*[tick. tick. a runnel passes close to the receiver now, or the receiver has drifted close to a runnel — a finger's width of water with a note in it, a clear ongoing syllable —]*

— third runnel's low —
— half a note —
— less. quarter. but it's dropping —
— moved the salamanders? —
— they moved themselves. the big one, broken-ring, he's up two channels since the last pass —
— then it's the pipe. it was always going to be the pipe —

*[murmur of assent, four voices or six, impossible to say; the crew moves off, or the fog moves them off, which is the same; for a while only the eaves, ticking, and very far away a hammer, blows spaced like a slow clock, six, seven, eight, patient, and then not there]*

*[the signal thins. whiteness. within the whiteness, faintly, the mesh — the fog-fences on the crest, singing, a sound like wires strung between distances, rising a little, which means the cloud river is running hard tonight, which means the cisterns will drink well, which means —]*

*[a voice, reading. flat, unhurried, the cadence of someone reading marks off a wall with a lamp held close. this is old lead and older scratch. the receiver cannot tell if the voice is living]*

— found: seep at the fourth course, salt bloom, two stones gone soft. done: cut back to sound stone, re-led the weep, holes to the old dimensions. the birds will tell you if I got it wrong. —

— found: her weep-holes, holding. birds in three of five. found her marks. done: nothing. left it. some passes you only touch it and go on. —

— found: —

*[gap. the fog takes it. tick. tick-tick. when the reading returns it may be a different wall, a different century; the voice has not changed, or has changed completely, there is no way to hear the difference and the residents would say there is no difference to hear]*

— ...done: closed the course. whoever opens this: the spring behind the third stone runs sweet in winter and sour after thaw, don't trust it in the thaw, I lost a season trusting it. my name is at the joint. the salamanders like the overflow. leave them the overflow. —

*[tick. and a small sound, close to the receiver, closer than anything yet: a flat palm laid against wet stone, held there, lifted. a pause the length of listening. footsteps going on, and the trailing hand touching the wall twice more before the fog closes over the sound of it]*

*[amber, if a receiver could receive amber. a lamp under an eave, three streets off, arriving as a warmth in the static]*

— they asked me down below, before I came up. they asked what's there. I said work. they said and after the work. I said —

*[laughter, if that is laughter — a breath through the nose, kind]*

— I couldn't answer them then. you can't say it in a language with two verbs.

*[the mesh sings. the turbines hum in the stone. somewhere a tram sighs to a stop and is unloaded in silence and sighs away]*

*[and here the cloud tears — not the sky's cloud, the signal's — and for the length of one held breath the transmission comes through whole and close, a single voice, a woman, older, speaking into an opened wall with the lamp on her face, and every droplet in the city seems to pause on its leaf-tip to let her be heard:]*

— There you are.

Eighty-one years. You wrote you'd be here and here you are, your lead's gone grey but your line is true, I could read you with my eyes shut. I've walked toward this joint my whole round. Since I was seven I have known your name and the name of your crew and what you found here and now my thumb is on your mark and you were right about the seep and wrong about the fourth stone, it held, it held eighty-one years, you'd be surprised, no — you wouldn't. Nothing of yours ever surprised anyone.

I'm cutting back to sound stone now. Your stone goes north, to the grinders. It'll be aggregate by spring and wall again by fall, somewhere down the third terrace, so you're moving house is all, you always were earlier in the round than me and now you'll be later too, that's a good trick, I mean to learn it.

To the one who opens this: her marks are under mine, keep them dry. The spring runs sour in the thaw. The birds want the old dimensions, don't improve on them, the wall knows things the survey doesn't.

I was going to say more. There's no more. Found: everything as promised. Done: the same.

*[the sound of lead on stone, small strokes, patient, a name going in]*

*[the tear closes. whiteness. the woman's voice, if it continues, continues as texture, indistinguishable now from the eaves, the runnels, the mesh, which may be the point, which the residents would say is the point if the residents said things like that, which they don't, they just lay the flat of the hand against it in passing]*

*[deep now. the signal has sunk, or the night has. water with no note in it at all — cistern water, the oldest water, longest out of the sky. a lamp lowered on a line. voices lowered with it]*

— see them? —
— ...no. — ...yes. under the lamp-fall. pale. —
— nobody brought them. —
— no. —
— then how — —
— *[silence, which is an answer they have agreed on]*
— up the lamp. quiet on the winch. —

*[the winch, quiet. the deep water closing back over its own question. tick. tick. tick-tick. the receiver rides the drip-pattern for a long time, hours maybe, the pattern shifting twice, wind going north and further north, the mesh's song climbing a tone and settling, the hammer returning somewhere and stopping somewhere, a body going quietly north to the composting houses with the spent panels and the leaf-litter, no procession, four bearers and the tram, the terraces waiting to be fed —]*

*[grey brightening, degree by degree, into a lighter grey]*

*[and then, dropping down through the fog from a weep-hole in a wall whose every repair is written inside it:]*

— two notes. a small grey-brown bird. the first note falling, the second falling further. —

*[twice. three times. and everywhere in the texture of the transmission, too quiet to isolate, a hundred thousand people not quite pausing — a loosening, a register-change, the attentive silence becoming ordinary silence — morning, confirmed]*

*[crews on the rooftops already, or still; there is no difference; spent panels going down on the lines, new panels going up, the same motion]*

*[the water at the crest beads on the mesh, runs to the gutter, drops through the turbine, hums, fills the runnel past somebody's boots, past the broken-ring salamander two channels up from where he was, soaks the trough, feeds the green, weeps back out of the masonry lower down, and lets go into the fog it came from, which is coming over the saddle again, endlessly, so that the same water is always arriving —]*

*[the transmission does not end here. it goes on at this volume forever. it is only that nothing carries far, and the receiver, like everything else on the ridge, is due for its pass —]*

*[tick]*

*[tick]*

*[— found: still here. done: the same. —]*
