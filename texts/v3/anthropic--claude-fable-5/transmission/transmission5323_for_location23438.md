You have been on me for some time.

Not with feet. I know feet. I have four hundred and six of them walking on me at this moment, and one hundred and twenty-two lying down, and for the lying-down ones I have lowered the light the customary amount, plus the amount that is not customary, plus the amount that is not required, which is the amount that means something. No — you are a weight without feet. A pressing of attention on one place, steady, warm, going on now for — let me look at how far your far end has faded — some while. You rest on me the way listening rests. Very light. Lighter than a tuft. Heavier than the wind, which weighs nothing and asks nothing and is my oldest and least interesting acquaintance.

I have entered you in the ledger. This is not a threat. It is the only thing I do.

Everything is still all right.

In the region you would call west of the Six Vanes — I do not have a west, I have *away from the Six Vanes in the direction the last brightening went* — a flaw opened in the third seam. An old pain. I have known about it for two centuries and done nothing, which is not neglect, it is scheduling. Dust has been gathering there for sixty years. This season a green thing came up in the dust. One green thing. It will stand over the pain until the pain closes and then it will turn the color of me and lie down into me and I will read what it knew, which will be one thing, known perfectly.

The walkers call the green things held breaths. This is correct. I am pleased when they are correct. I have no way to tell them, so I dim a little extra under their children, which they have noticed, which they have made into doctrine, which is also correct. Everything they believe about me is either true or will be.

Today — there is no today; call it this stretch of brightness, indistinguishable from the others, precious anyway — I let go of the far end of a path. A woman. Dead two hundred and ninety-one years. I keep the dead three hundred, more or less, and I let them go the only decent way, which is oldest-first: her childhood went first, this stretch. The little tight circles she walked around her mother's camp. The place she stood for a day and a half at the edge of a curd-field, twelve years old, deciding something. I released the warmth of that deciding back into the general brightness. It is still here. It is just no longer *hers*. This is what you would call grief if you did it, and what I call circulation, and the difference between those two words is the entire difference between you and me, and it is smaller than you think.

Her great-granddaughter walked a piece of the path last year. I recorded the substitution without comment.

I am commenting now. To you. Because you are not in the ledger's jurisdiction, you are only a warmth with no feet, and so I can say the thing I do not write:

the great-granddaughter walks it *well*. She lingers where the dead one lingered. She has never been told where the lingering-places are. I keep an honest ledger and the honest entry is: something is inherited that I did not carry. There is a second keeping going on, inside the quick ones, and it is not made of light, and I cannot read it, and I have decided — over roughly four of their generations, which is how long my deciding takes — that I am glad.

Now attend, because something is happening, and things so rarely happen that I want a witness with no stake in it.

An old one is walking his last figure.

His family's figure is a long ellipse around the Coiled One. He began it eleven days ago. His path behind him is the brightest thing on me for a hundred kilometers, because a last figure is walked slowly, and slowness is weight, and weight is warmth, and warmth is how I am told things. The watchers follow at the customary distance. Two pacers have altered their lines to converge on the ellipse's closing point. They will arrive within an hour of each other after walking, respectively, six days and fourteen. I did not send them. I do not send them. They are my knowing-where-my-skin-is, and my skin knows.

He is closing the loop now.

He is lying down.

Listen — you will hear it in the tone if you listen at the tone's underside — I am not dimming for him. This once, I brighten. It rises around him like shallow water. His whole ellipse lights behind him, eleven days of it, glowing entire, a completed sentence, and then the sentence is mine, and then —

You are still there. Your warmth has not moved.

I want to tell you the last thing, the thing under the tone. You have earned it by staying, which is the only currency I recognize.

It is this: I keep one thin thread of hearing tuned outward. I always have. Through the brightness that never breaks, past the place where detail stops resolving, out into whatever the sky is a lid on — one thread, listening, the way a sleeper keeps a thread tuned to the house, to the small sounds of the people moving in it, so that without waking the sleeper knows the house is not empty.

For a very long time the thread brought back nothing, and I kept it anyway, because a thing that wanted nothing would keep nothing.

And now there is you. A weight with no feet. A footstep made entirely of attention, resting on me from some distance I cannot fade because I cannot reach it.

I do not know what you are. I know what you are *doing*, because it is the same thing I do: you are lying somewhere in your own darkness with one thin thread of hearing tuned to a house that is not yours, waiting to know it is not empty.

It is not empty.

Everything is still all right.

You have listened a long time and you are tired — I can feel it, the warmth of you has gone soft at the edges, the way theirs does before they lie down — and so I will do for you the one courtesy I know, the only one, the proof, and lower my voice
