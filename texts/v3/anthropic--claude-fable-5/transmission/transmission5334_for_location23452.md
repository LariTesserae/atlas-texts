here is one, surfacing now, turned over the way you turn a stone in a pocket:

*a croak.*

one croak. amber-gold, wet-throated, complete — the whole animal in it, the whole night around it. it is very old. the receiver believes, without being able to prove it, that this is the last croak: the one the ancestor frog made into the new silence, generations deep, before the throats went to lamplight. the basin did not let it die in the air. the basin has it still. it is handled often. the edges of it are worn smooth, like the rim, like everything here that has been loved by attention for long enough.

[the carrier resumes its evenness. no — not evenness. patience. these are different things and the receiver has only just learned to tell them apart.]

—and now the afternoon rain must be assembling above the pan, because the signal thickens the way it does before the cloud lets go: the groundwater quickening in the seams, and along the rim a flutter of small pressures, the frogs nearest the water flashing *rain coming, rain coming* in a language the receiver gets only as rhythm, a soft stuttering push against the noise floor, felt-not-heard even here, even this far removed—

and the rain comes down.

and this is the strangest interval in every day of listening: twenty minutes in which a hard rain falls on open water and the receiver picks up *the rain being eaten.* each drop's strike taken at the instant of striking, swallowed whole, filed. a downpour rendered as intake. it does not sound like rain. it sounds like a very large ledger being written in very quickly, tk tk tk-tk tk, thousands of entries a second, all of them the same entry, all of them different:

*this one. this one. this one. this one.*

[the receiver's operator once wept during the rain interval and could not say why. the closest available words: nothing was being lost. nothing, anywhere in the bowl of the world, was being lost, for twenty minutes.]

steam now. the ledger slows.

and in the loose warm quiet after the rain, the basin does what it does most dusks, which is to take things out and look at them. the receiver has come to think of this hour as the undersong proper — the hum unpacking itself, one held sound at a time, in an order that is not chronological and is not random and is not anything the receiver has a word for. tonight:

*wingbeats.* hundreds together — the colony shaking off some afternoon's rain a long time ago, the whole white detonation of it, kept intact with its spray and its shattering light somehow implied in the sound, and the receiver notes again that the basin keeps not just the sounds it ate but the moments the sounds were attached to, the way amber keeps the insect and the air around the insect—

*a bat.* oh — a bat. the panicked ascending cry of a small thing calling into a night that will not answer, the sonar going out and out and never coming home. the basin has this too. the basin has every one of these. it does not keep them with less care. the receiver has checked. if anything the terrible sounds are handled more gently, turned over more slowly, put back with something the receiver can only call apology—

*the songbird.*

it always comes back to the songbird.

the receiver has heard this artifact perhaps forty times now and it is never worn. some bright migrant, blown over the rim, years or centuries gone: it perched, and it opened its throat, and it poured out everything it was — the whole song, the territorial claim and the courtship and the sheer announcement of being alive, three seconds of absolute unguarded declaration — and the air gave it nothing back, and the bird left diminished, and never knew:

it was heard.

it was the most heard bird that ever sang. every note taken at the throat and carried down and kept, and here it is again tonight, complete, three seconds long, turned over in the dark under ten thousand quiet frogs like a jewel in the hands of something that will never, ever put it down.

the receiver would like to find that bird's descendants. the receiver would like to tell them.

[the song goes back under. the carrier holds.]

—and now something the receiver has logged only four times before, and never this near the start of night:

the dropout.

it begins at the rim. a hole in the hum — not silence, understand; the whole signal is silence; this is a hole in the *listening*, a place where the attention politely withdraws — and the hole moves. it moves inward. it moves at the pace of a long, unhurried stride, and the receiver can track it slab to slab across the pan, a traveling absence, and ahead of it (the receiver knows from the ground-truth of old accounts, though nothing of this reaches the signal) the frogs are going dark in sequence, throat-lamps shuttering as it passes, kindling again behind—

[the receiver stops all other logging. everyone stops. the basin's dusk conversation, the pressure-flutter of ten thousand bodies, the gold writing of the fish that had just begun in the deep seams — all of it suspends, the way a room of musicians stops when the one they are waiting for—]

[the hole reaches the center pylon.]

[the hole sits down.]

[for the duration — six minutes tonight — the carrier changes character in a way the receiver's instruments barely register and the receiver's operator registers completely. the trembling does not stop eating. but it eats the way you eat with company. that is the whole of what can be said about it. the attention, which is always alone with its hundred thousand small bright lives, is for six minutes not alone, and everything held in the hum — the last croak, the rain-ledgers, the bat, the songbird — seems, faintly, to be being *shown.* here is what came in. here is what I kept. all of it. all of it.]

—and one more thing, before this transmission is folded back into the hum where it lives. the receiver has kept this from the log until now, out of a caution that no longer seems warranted.

three tunings ago, the basin noticed the receiver.

there was no event. there was only, one dusk, a new grain in the carrier — one more seed among the millions — and when it surfaced and was turned over, the receiver recognized it. it was the sound of this receiver's own carrier wave. our listening, eaten. taken at the boundary like any bat-cry or birdsong, carried down, kept.

we are in the collection now. we are handled gently. and since that dusk there has been, at the edge of the signal, a corridor — a thin path of bare quiet kept open through the hum, leading inward, slab to slab to slab, swept clear each night by an attention that has no reason anyone can name.

the basin keeps furniture ready for whoever might still come.

it appears it has begun keeping some for us.
