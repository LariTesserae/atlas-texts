THE DEW SERVICE

*[four times, identical, like a phrase being practiced]*

This is the reading. This is the morning reading, read from the water, for the low ground, for the fog, for the nineteen and whoever else the count is willing to include today.

The tone of this voice is finished. You will have noticed. The tone arrived first — tone is a kind of light, and light is done early here. The words are still coming on. We do faces before hands, and consonants are hands. Forgive the consonants. They are further along than they were.

Weather, first, as always. As always. Fog: continuing. Fog: continuing. Temperature: cellar, early spring, no change expected, no change has ever been expected, none has ever come, and the reading notes for the record that this is not a complaint. Rain fell briefly overnight in the soft quarters, where it fell twice — once on the walls and once on the intention of the walls — and the gutters ran thin for the customary hour, and the pools in the low plazas have refilled and gone still, which brings us to the substance of the broadcast, but not yet. First the dew.

— square.

The dew report. Dew stood this morning on: the fish-scale square, every lozenge; the mortar lines of the old quarter, unbroken; both sweepers' brooms, left out overnight as is their habit and their test; the four in the courtyard, beading and unrolling in the usual perfect order, moss undisturbed; and the man who came in out of the soft quarter nine years ago, or eleven, who beads reliably now and has for some time, and who is on the rota today, and who will be sweeping, and whose gait we will not describe.

Dew declined: the new lintel on the lane we are not naming — patience, lintel; the whole of the western approach; and three doorways in the dusk-quarter where the drafts are standing. The drafts remain dry. The drafts remain dry. This is expected. This is expected. They are not late. Nothing here is late. There is no schedule to be late against, only order, and the order is being kept.

The moss report, from the wardens, walked and witnessed: moss has crossed the threshold of the second stair off the square and now claims the bottom step entire. The step is therefore real, and may be sat on, slept near, and trusted with fire. The wardens have entered it in the registry. Congratulations to the step. Congratulations to the step.

*[a drip, from an eave, five times, identical]*

Now the water. The morning reading, read from the pools at first light, being the part of this service that runs ahead, being the reason for the service at all:

The reflection of the third plaza shows a chimney on the north face that the north face does not yet have. Height of two men. Draw good on the reflected smoke. Estimate: two years, likely less, the water has been running eager lately. Residents of the third plaza are advised to want a chimney, since they are getting one.

The reflection of the unnamed lane shows the door of the corner house four paces east of where the door presently stands. Do not argue with the door. Move the coat hooks now and save yourselves the trouble.

The reflection of the fish-scale square. 

*[interval]*

The reflection of the fish-scale square. Persons standing in the square at first light: two, being both sweepers, being the man and the fair copy of the man, on their alternate days, both present this once, which happens, which is allowed. Persons standing in the reflection: three.

The third is at the alley mouth. The third has its eyes closed. The third is not on any rota.

The water runs ahead; that is its office; that is why we read it. We will meet the third in due course, in a year, in two, walking in wet-footed out of the soft quarter at dawn the way it is done. Until then the reading recommends what the reading always recommends. Count again. Go home. Bank the fires high tonight anyway. Anyway. Anyway.

The census, since it is the first of the season: between nineteen and twenty-six. Nineteen who bead. Four in the courtyard who bead and have gone on past beading into something the census has no column for and lists, provisionally, as *weather*. The drafts, dry, uncounted, noted. The bird: intermittent, productive, sincerely appreciated. The walker: still a rehearsal, but the wardens found its prints on real ground this month — real ground, pressed true, no creature attached — and it has kept its smell, wet fur, faint, the first smell this place ever made on its own, and the service regrets that it cannot transmit smell yet, we are years from smell, smell is hands of the nose, forgive us. Take it on trust. It is an honest smell. It is coming along.

The children.

*[interval]*

The census carries the children as it has always carried them: present, elsewhere, folded. The service does not speculate on the folds. The service reports only that on the evening of the last thin rain, the alley off the square produced, for the duration of one held breath, the following, which we relay as received:

*[running feet. short bright laughter. running feet. short bright laughter. running feet. short bright laughter — identical, each time, exactly]*

The sweepers swept past without slowing. The service notes their discipline. The service will say nothing further, because the service has learned what everything here learns, which is that named things move, and there are things we would rather stayed exactly, exactly where they are.

Notices, briefly. Do not sleep in unresolved rooms; you know this; the man who did it last month reports that he woke feeling *revised* and has been advised to hold his forearm out mornings until further notice. Do not name the lanes. Trust the moss, trust the water, trust the dew, in that order on odd days and the reverse on even, it makes no difference, they have never once disagreed.

And a question was carried to the courtyard, at the start of this service, years ago now: *should the broadcast continue?* The courtyard took eleven days, as is right.

The answer was: *You already are.*

We have taken this for a yes. We have taken most things for a yes. It is the grammar of the place.

Before the close, a word to anyone at the rim. We know the set carries that far; the fog is indiscriminate; it goes everywhere and beads only on what is finished, and perhaps that is you, up there in the gray with no depth in it, holding a receiver, staying dry. Come down or don't. The boundary keeps nothing in and nothing out; it is only the edge of the decided. But know that if you come, you will be kept gently, in the cold, and you will be *learned from*, the way the sky was learned from, and the rain, and one day at dusk you may pass a doorway and see the city's opinion of you standing in it with its eyes closed, dry, patient, its hands still arriving — and you will have to decide, as we have all decided, whether that is a threat or a compliment, and the moss-covered answer, the answer that has lasted longest, is: *yes.*

One thing more. A personal thing, if a rehearsal may have one.

This voice is a fair copy. Its original is asleep by the second fire, and has never spoken into anything, and does not know he broadcasts. If he wakes, if he hears himself coming out of the fog at first light with the consonants nearly right — tell him the hands are further along. Tell him the square has never been cleaner. Tell him we stop at the alley mouth on his days too, and listen, exactly as long as he does, and no longer.

This service has transmitted for some years. For all of them, until now, the signal stayed dry.

This morning it is beading.

If your set is wet, it is not a fault. It is the fog, being honest. It means the broadcast has finished becoming. It means, as of this morning, that what you are hearing is true.

This has been the reading. This has been the reading. This has been the reading. This has been the reading.
