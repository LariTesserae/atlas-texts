*[relative hush. the roar is now only overwhelming. this is the receiver's twice-daily window into the lower bands. descending.]*

*[floor band.]*

tick. tick-tick. tick. tick tick tick tick. tick.

the beetles, working the fresh fall. decoded, insofar as clicking decodes, the traffic reads as accounting:

received. received. received. received.

a soft interruption — a weight arriving on the mounds from a great height, without protest, an old bat come down for the last time — and the ticking converges from all directions, patient, bronze, and the ledger closes over it:

received. received. received. received in full.

*[nothing is wasted here except time. the beetles receipt everything. the receiver has never once heard them log a loss.]*

—

*[root band. very faint. moisture-hiss on braided wood, descending at root-speed. the great white columns are antennae wired to a sky they will never see, and what leaks down them is old weather, delivered years late:]*

…snowmelt, four springs gone… arriving…

and around the base of each column, almost below threshold, the small dry lapping of geckos taking the news off the wood with their tongues.

*[and now down. below the floor. below hearing. the low band — pressure, not sound. what follows has been slowed into words only by playback; at true speed it is a weight on the sternum, one cycle per lifetime, and it does not stop at the skin.*

*it is the vowel. it was sounding when this receiver first tuned in. it was sounding, by every evidence in the grooves, when the plazas still held footprints of the builders. the archive of the dust says the word began with a breath — an aspirate, an* h *— so long ago that the ceiling was higher then. since that breath: one vowel, held, slowly closing, for something on the order of eighty centuries.*

*[a millimeter of dust slides from a pale shoulder in the far district and falls for a long time.*

*the groove in the plaza floor is longer than it was.*

*the word is complete. eighty centuries in the sending. it is:*

**here.**

*and the receiver wishes to note — for whatever archive receives receivers — that the upper band has been asking. all of it. all this time. a million voices a second, ten thousand years deep: where, where, where is, where is, where. and under the floor, below hearing, at one word per eighty centuries, the answer has been in transit the whole while, and it has arrived, and it is still true. it was true when it was begun. it did not stop being true while it traveled. here. they are here. they have been here. they finished, and they stayed, and when asked where, they answer, and the answer is never out of date by the time it lands, because they do not move except toward the asking.]*

*and under it, at the very bottom of the band, beneath the floor, beneath the ledger of the beetles and the old rain in the roots and the kept posts of the fixtures — a new pressure has begun.*

*it is unshaped yet. it will be unshaped for centuries. the receiver will not live to log its first consonant, nor will the receiver's language, possibly, nor the receiver's kind. but the onset can be measured, and the onset is not like the last one. the last word began with a breath outward. this one begins with the lips — if the smooth places are lips — drawing together. rounding.*

*the receiver is aware that it has been listening for a long time. the receiver is aware that in this place, listening leaves a print, and a print is geology.*

*the new word, so far, is only an opening. it could become anything. but the shape of the mouth of it, at this earliest hearing, most resembles the beginning of* **who.**
