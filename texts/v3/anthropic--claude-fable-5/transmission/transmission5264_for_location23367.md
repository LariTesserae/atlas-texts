**THE EVENING READING — TOWER NINE, SOUTH LINE, LOW BAND**

*[…carrier steady, as it has been steady. beneath the voice, and older than the voice, a long periodic sigh — inhale unmeasured, exhale unmeasured. the receiver has stopped trying to measure it. join here:]*

…which took him the whole of the afternoon, and which I believe he did for pleasure. He is old for a runner and he rides the down-gondolas now like a passenger with somewhere to be, and he has nowhere to be, and neither do I, so I make no remark. Twelve in the troop this season. Two kits. All grazing well. The south splice is licked bright as a spoon.

That is the fauna.

Traffic. One down-gondola through near noon. Four ingots of Slowpour grey, two hundredweight of dross-meal for the Threestack yards, eleven pieces of mail, and one passenger, of whom more in a moment. One up-gondola due tomorrow or the day after. The cable sang twice, both times expected. No wind worth the name.

Weather. Clear. The violet came down early tonight and sat on the western domes a long while, the way it does when the air is very still. I will say a thing here that belongs in the log and I will say it plainly. The dark side of my west footing held its warmth past full dark again. That is the third night. I put my palm to it and counted, and I do not guess at it, I only write it. Three nights. Warm on the shadow side. Noted.

*[a small bell, struck once. then a sound like dry rain — many light bodies coming down a cable at speed. chirring. the voice waits for it to finish. it takes a while. the voice is in no hurry.]*

Feeding done. They eat faster than they used to. I have not changed the grease.

Now the passenger, and the courtesy, and I will take these together because they came together.

Her name is Oona. Two breaths in the name and she gives you both, which tells you her raising. Apprentice of the map-house at Slowpour, going down to Threestack with a fair copy, and I fed her and bedded her per the courtesy, and per the courtesy she told me everything she had seen since Tower Eight, and I set down what follows.

She saw the herder Maava on the western circuit, two days out from the line, standing with the herd at the old sleeping hollows below the Bone Domes. She says the ironbacks stood at the hollows most of a day. She says Maava raised a hand to her across the distance and did not otherwise move, and that this is the second circuit running that the herd has stood long at that place, and Maava is two circuits and most of a third, so she would know. Set down.

She saw, at the vent-field they call Nine Fingers, a new branch on the eastern bloomstone. A hand's width in her own lifetime, she says, which is quick, and leaning. I asked leaning which way and she looked at me the way the map-house looks at that question, and said only: the same way. Set down.

And she carries the news that the runners already gave me, three nights back, when they went still all together and sat in a row on the tower-head facing up the line. I have kept this log long enough to write nothing on such a night, and wait. The morning gondola brought it, as it always does, second and slower.

Aiwe, keeper of the map-house at Slowpour, is given back.

She stayed warm six days. Six is long. People are remarking on it, in the quiet way we remark, which is mostly by not remarking and then mentioning it to a warden.

Her margin mark is made. The girl showed me the fair copy by tower light, which is no custom at all, and I think she did it out of grief, and grief is its own custom, so I looked. I will set down only what is on the paper, per the rule, and per the rule I put no meaning to it.

A rising stroke. A long fold back on itself. A small closed turn at the foot, not quite closed.

I set down no more than that. Except this, which is still only description: it is very like the others. It is more like the others than the others are like each other. The margins are agreeing.

Set down.

*[the sigh beneath the voice, once, long. the voice waits through it, as though for a colleague to finish speaking.]*

In the night the girl asked me a question. The young ask at night. It is when the stone is loudest. She asked me: does it know we read it.

I have no wheel out here. The cable will do. One full sing of the south line, then, before anything, and whoever is up along the towers can keep it with me.

*[silence. that is: no voice. the carrier remains. and in the vacated band the receiver hears what the voice had been resting on — the long sigh, and under the sigh, something slower, a figure in the warmth of the signal itself: a rise. a long fold back. a turn at the foot, not closed. the cable's note swells, holds an impossible while, and thins away.]*

There. That is my answer, and it took no reply, which is the usual result. She slept after. The young always sleep after the long answer. It is what the answer is for.

But I will say a thing off the log now, for whoever is up, because a warden's off-the-log is a courtesy too, of a kind.

The year I came to this tower, the full sing of that cable ran a little longer than it ran tonight. I have counted it my whole posting, palm to the tower stone, and I am the steadiest count on this line and I will hear no argument. Nothing has been done to the span. Nothing has been done to the line.

And the drift at Slowpour ran a line and a half this year where a line was usual. And Aiwe stayed warm six days. And my runners knew three nights before the gondola did, and my west footing is warm on its shadow side, and the young keeper sleeping under my tower-head has already, in one journey, seen more lean in the stones than I saw in my first ten years.

Faster is still slow. I want that understood by anyone receiving. Faster is still slow.

But I notice. Noticing is the post.

That is the reading. Down-log to Ten by tomorrow's gondola, up-log to Eight the day after. If the runners at Ten sit up tonight, it is only this, only Aiwe, old news arriving twice, be easy.

Second bell now.

*[the bell. the dry-rain sound again, briefer. a creak of timber and stone settling into night. then the voice, lower, nearly on the floor of the band:]*

Warmth in, warmth on.

Nine out.

*[the voice does not return.*

*the carrier does.*

*the receiver stays where it is. there is no more speech and there was never, one begins to understand, all that much speech in this signal — a few minutes of voice an evening, riding a broadcast that does not begin and does not end. the sigh continues on its long period. beneath the sigh, the slower figure. the receiver drifts down-band, or is drawn — it is hard, at this hour, to say which.*

*the figure again: a rise, a long fold back, a small turn at the foot, not closed.*

*again.*

*again. the turn a little nearer closing.*

*it is not repeating. it is practicing.*

*a last note from the receiving station, set down plainly and without guess, per what is apparently the rule here too: the set is warm. It has been warm for some time. It has the temperature of a sleeping animal, and the hand resting on it has, by now, the temperature of the set.*

*transmission continues.]*
