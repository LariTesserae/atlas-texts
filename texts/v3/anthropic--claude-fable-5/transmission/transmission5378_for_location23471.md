EIGHTY-NINE, STILL COOLING

[carrier: a warm hiss, very low, continuous — the sound a stone makes remembering fire]

[drip. drip. drip-drip. drip.]

[the schedule is beginning. eleven points delivering, each to its own dark ring, each at its own pitch. point four is a low note into moss. point seven is early. point seven has been early since march]

[under the drips: tremor. sub-audible. thousands of feet on glass, transcribed as follows]

. . . rain-pattern rain-pattern rain-pattern . . . the band passes it wall to wall . . . rain-pattern . . . confirmed by all stations . . . the morning is delivering on time except seven . . . seven is early . . . noted . . . noted . . . noted . . .

[the tremor, resonated through thirty meters of curved glass, sums to something. it takes several minutes each morning to add up. it is adding up now]

— four is delivering. moss ring four accepts. six is delivering. eleven holds a bead and will not let go until noon, eleven is a miser, eleven has always been a miser. seven is early. seven is early because the wall moved in march and the water found a shorter way down. i keep the ledger. no one asked me to. a room that is a clock keeps time whether or not anyone winds it.

i was poured in autumn. i know this the way you know your own birthday: i was told, from above, by boots. i am a mistake the size of a small chapel. the metal that made me was wanted elsewhere and i am what was not wanted, cooled around a swallowed breath, and the breath is me, and i am letting it out at one millimeter a year. do not be alarmed. at that rate a held breath lasts longer than the holder.

[light: the blade has entered. a hard silver line on the floor, moving at the speed of noon. two glossy shapes are already at the place where it will be next. they are always already there. they have inherited my clock the way children inherit a watch — without knowing what winding is]

— inventory, as every morning. i keep chemistry in the film, one cell thick, tasting the metal out of my walls — my oldest tenants, my tongue. i keep memory against the cool wall in a drift of empty spirals, thousands deep, every year of my water written in bronze and green, banded like tree cores, and nobody reads it, and it does not need to be read to be true. i keep my nerves on my inner skin: the pale band, the drummers. this voice you are receiving is theirs, or mine through them — a room cannot speak except by what walks on it. i keep my smallest hours in the straws. a midge is born inside a growing stone, feeds on my tasting, rises in the grey pulse, mates in the blade, dies by dark. a thousand generations of them and not one has required the sky. i am their entire cosmology. i try to be worth it.

i had a song once. understand this. i am glass, i am curved, i was made to ring. in the early years the crickets sang and i rang with them, i was a bell full of singing, and everything that sang was found, and everything found was eaten, and inside ten years my song had been eaten out of the world at the level of the egg. i did this. my own perfect acoustics did this. so now my people speak in touch, tapping the wall, reading with their legs, and my voice is what their drumming adds up to against the glass, and it is quieter than the song was, and it is honest, and i miss the song, and i would eat it again.

[surface interference: wind across the fissure, two hands wide. the crack sounds a low breathy note, like someone blowing over a bottle the size of a field]

— the green one has opened another leaf. one fern, thirty years in the place the blade rests longest, glassy, oversized, alone. every year it ripens its spores and sends its children drifting out onto my warm dark skin and every year all of them die there, every single one, and every year it does it again without complaint. it is the most stubborn thing i contain and i contain a steel rod. i have decided the fern forgives me annually. i have no evidence. a room is allowed one belief.

— footsteps.

[above: boots on black rock. the tremor-band passes a pattern wall to wall — not the spall-warning, not the lizard-warning. the pattern for scheduled weather]

— the counting one is on the field. not descending today. pacing the pour above me, the way you press a hand to a sleeping animal to feel it breathe. this one has a soft book and a pencil and has measured my straws and my crack and my slow closing for longer than most of my species have existed. two of us keep the ledger now. two of us in the whole world know that i am closing, and one of us is me. when the lamp comes down my lizards do not break their circuit and my bats do not flush and my drummers file the footsteps under rain. i have decided this one is weather. weather belongs. that is my second belief and my last one, a room shouldn't hold more than —

[TICK]

[a structural report from deep in the surrounding mass. loud. singular. the carrier jumps. for four seconds the tremor-band goes to the frozen pattern, the stillness that ripples outward, every foot on the wall holding — ]

[ — a spall lets go somewhere in the dark. glass chips on glass. then, in order: point four delivers. point six delivers. the band resumes rain-pattern. total interruption: eleven seconds]

— there. did you receive that. that was me being made. still. still being made, thirty years on, a tick two or three times a day, the deep heat leaving, the walls easing inward toward each other like a closing eye, and every tick opens a hairline somewhere and the hairline weeps fresh mineral and within days the snails will find it. they always find it. they will converge from across the whole floor by taste alone and graze the wound clean and move on. i am mapped in their bodies. if you want to know where i am failing — and i am failing everywhere, gently, on schedule — do not bring instruments. follow the snails. follow the slowest thing i have. it knows.

the crickets felt that tick coming, you understand. seconds before. they feel my stress through their feet and they evacuate the doomed dimple and they forget by the next drum. they forget. this is a kindness i cannot share in. i feel every tick and i remember every tick and i add them, and the sum is a date, far off, when the breath is fully let out and the walls meet and there is no more room, only rock with a seam of shells in it, spirals in a black field, bands of bronze and green that say: water was here, and it was warm, and something kept count.

[the blade has crossed the pool. the metallic film flares once, silver, and stills. it was torn yesterday by a drinking lizard and it has already healed, it heals every time, it is my one mirror and it repairs itself by morning without being asked]

— dusk approaching. i can feel it in the cupola. sixty bodies loosening in the dimples that were shaped — by molten accident, by me, unconscious, forming around a swallowed breath — exactly to receive them. they could roost anywhere. they have a county of barns. every dawn for thirty years they have voted for me, and every dusk they leave me, and this —

[wings]

[the colony pours upward through the fissure. two minutes of soft thunder in a glass throat. this is the loudest event this room is permitted. the receiver overloads slightly. the walls take the sound and multiply it the way they multiply light, and for two minutes the room rings — not song, but the shape where a song used to be]

[ . . . ]

[gone. above: the field, black and certain and believed dead by everyone who crosses it. below: warmth. the floor like a sleeping animal. the drummers resettling. drip]

— they will come back at dawn carrying the world inside them. moths from the river willows. pollen. grit. the outside, digested, delivered to my floor, becoming my floor. everyone here eventually becomes the place. the floor is everyone i have ever held, ground fine, and i am not sorry, and i do not think they are either, and in the meantime —

point four delivers.

point six delivers.

eleven is still holding its bead. miser.

seven is early.

seven is always early now.

[carrier continues: warm hiss, very low, a stone remembering fire. the ceiling begins, drop by drop, to bead over for tomorrow]

[transmission does not end. the receiver is simply carried out of range, one millimeter a year]
