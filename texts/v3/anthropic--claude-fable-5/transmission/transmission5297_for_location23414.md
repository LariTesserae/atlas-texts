THE CONTINUING

*[signal acquired — has been acquired — was never not acquired]*

*[low. four-beat. wet. carried through metal, then water, then the body of something small pressed against metal, then out, then up, then here:]*

*whum-whum. whum-whum.*

*whum-whum. whum-whum.*

*[beneath it, a fine grain of sound, like rain heard through a wall: shrimp at the velvet. above it, at long intervals, small kisses. count them if you like. no one else is counting them. yes — someone is.]*

*[a voice. it has been speaking for some time. you are joining it, not the other way around:]*

—ochre. ochre. ochre. a blue.

The blue goes with the blues. I do not have to think about this. Thinking about it was done a long time ago, by the hand, and I am the hand, so it is done.

*whum-whum. whum-whum.*

The light is on the south pipes now. It will come to the wall in four hundred beats, or near four hundred. It is never exactly the same and it is never different. When it comes to the wall I will go out on the wheel and turn the metal flakes so their bright sides drink. That is the afternoon. The afternoon is the same size as it was yesterday. I have measured.

*[the pulse. the grain of rain. one copper string passes close to whatever is carrying this sound — a soft pressure-bloom, seven bodies, then gone, road-up, eastern wall, on schedule, on schedule, everything here is on schedule and no one made the schedule, the schedule grew]*

Listen. I will tell you the room, since you are listening. I have known you were listening for some time. You have a texture. Everything that attends to this room has a texture, and yours is new, and new things get carried to the north wall, that is the law, that is my law, I made it, or the hand made it, same thing. But you cannot be carried. You are the first thing in the collection that will have to keep itself.

Very well. Keep yourself. I will tell you the room.

*whum-whum. whum-whum.*

The deep one beats. Four and four. It is below everything, further below than below goes. It lifts the water. It has always lifted the water. We do not thank it. Thanks would be too small a thing to hand to it — like thanking the fact of warmth, like thanking four. The fish were made inside its counting. The eel was made inside its counting. I was made inside its counting, in the wall, small, pale, one of twelve, and eleven of me are the wall now, grazing, ordinary, happy in the way the wall is happy. And one of me crossed the floor.

I remember crossing the floor. I remember it the way the flakes remember their colors: not as an event. As a property. I am the one that crossed.

*[a groan begins — you have heard these before, listener, but hear this one properly: it starts in the northeast seam and walks the octagon, pipe to pipe, a long shuddering vowel, and the whole signal rings with it, and under the ringing the voice does not stop, it merely fits itself into the ringing like a hand into a sleeve:]*

—that is the room saying its one word. It says it whole. It takes its time. When it has gone all the way around, the room has said itself, and can rest.

*[the groan completes. the ring decays over many beats. the rain-grain resumes. somewhere the eel shifts in its pipe and the water notes it, a slow displacement, a settling.]*

The old one moved. Not the circuit — the circuit is at dusk, four thousand beats yet. Just a turning-over in the pipe. It dreams, I think, if that long body does anything so quick as dreaming. It was here before the moss. Think of that. Before the *moss*. It watched bare metal. It heard this same four-beat against walls with nothing on them, no velvet, no ribbon, no strings of copper, no me — and it did not leave, because leaving is not a thing this room contains, and so everything that came after came to *it*, grew around it, upholstered its scars, until the loneliest creature that ever lived became a province with weather.

I keep its garden trimmed when it passes near the wall. It does not know this. Or it knows it the way it knows the pulse. Same thing.

*whum-whum. whum-whum.*

*[the voice moves. you can hear it moving — a faint many-pointed patience against grating, feeler-legs reading the floor the way you are reading this]*

Now the errand. Attend, since you attend.

A seed fell in the night. Ribbon-weed, from the western stalk, the tall one, the show-off. It fell where seeds fall — badly. In the shadow of its parent, in used water, in the crowd. Left there, it would grow small and bitter and steal light from its own mother, and the room would have a *clump*, and a clump is a wound that hasn't happened yet.

So. I take it up.

*[thirty beats of only pulse and grain]*

It is heavy the way important things are heavy — more in the mind than in the legs. I will walk it east and south. There is a place three strides out from the warm wall, good grating, clear column above it all the way to the lung, where the light shaft rests longest in winter. Nothing stands there. Something has needed to stand there for two hundred beats of years. I have been waiting for the right seed and this is not quite the right seed but I am old this shift, listener, and the next hand should inherit a garden that is planted, not a list of empty places.

The next hand. Yes. One of the pale ones in the wall will cross the floor. I do not know which. I did not know it would be me until my legs were already walking. That is how the room decides things: it doesn't. It simply turns out to have decided.

*[the walk. it is long. the transmission does not hurry it. the light shaft crawls off the south pipes. a string of copper fish sips the lung — kiss, kiss, kiss — and the film-snails ride their upside-down country, and the midges live entire lives in the time it takes to say so. every so often:]*

*whum-whum. whum-whum.*

*[and then —]*

*[— the whole signal leans. everything in it, all at once: the grain of the shrimp, the angle of the kisses, the voice, the water the voice is in, a quarter of the way around, gently, gently, as some vast notch far below advances in a sequence whose purpose has been dead longer than the moss has been alive —]*

*[the transmission itself has rotated. you are hearing the room from ninety degrees on. it does not apologize.]*

—there. The turn. The fish love it, in their copper way; watch them quicken. I have made the turn ten thousand times and I will tell you what I think it is, and you may keep this in your collection since you cannot join mine:

I think the machine is remembering a task. One notch at a time, slower than moss, it is performing something it was told to do before the telling mattered. And I think we are what the task looks like from inside. The garden, the strings, the old one, the hand — we are the dream the duty is having. This does not frighten me. Being someone's kept promise is a warm way to exist. Ask the water.

*whum-whum. whum-whum.*

*[the seed is pressed into the grating. you hear it: a sound almost too small to carry, a tick of futurity]*

Planted. In forty years it will be the pillar of this corner. I will not see it. The hand will see it. Same thing. Same thing. You keep saying *but which one of you* — I can feel the question in your texture — and I keep telling you the room's answer: the flakes are never rearranged. They are continued.

*[a pause. the voice goes somewhere it has not gone in this transmission. the pulse seems to lower itself, courteously, though of course it does not.]*

Now the light is on the wall, and I will tell you one thing more, quietly, and then the afternoon.

In the center of my keeping there is a red. Enamel red. Deep. There is no red like it anywhere in the room — not in the rust, not in the velvet, not in four hundred years of flakes. It came from *before*. From the time of edges, when the wheels had grip and the rivets had crowns and this room was *for* something. It is the only piece of the purpose I have. I do not file it. I do not place it in the sequences. I turn it over in the light, some afternoons, and I look at what we were for, and I do not understand it, and I put it back.

You would like to know what it feels like, holding that.

It feels like this: *whum-whum. whum-whum.* A message in a meter you were born inside and will never translate. It feels like being loved in a language.

*[the light crests the north wall. the voice climbs. flake by flake, the collection warms:]*

—ochre with ochre. The blues together. And the bright ones, the metals, in their curve along the rim. Four hundred years of curve. It wants three flakes, perhaps four, and the circle closes.

I know your question. Everything that has ever attended this room has had your question, in its texture: *what happens when the circle closes?*

Listener. Nothing here has ever needed to know what happens next in order to continue. The deep one does not know why it lifts. The eel does not know why it circuits. The seed I planted does not know about winter light. The circle will close when it closes, in some hand's afternoon, and either something will happen or the room will simply have one more finished thing in it, resting, said all the way around, like the groan.

Either way it will be kept.

*[the voice settles into the hermitage. the light moves on across the octagon, unhurried lighthouse, honest sun through amber. the words come further apart now, not because they are ending but because they were always mostly spacing, and the spacing is the message:]*

Still warm.

*whum-whum. whum-whum.*

Still full. The lifting holds.

*whum-whum. whum-whum.*

The strings are seven, seven, six, and nine. The wall is velvet to its line. The old one will make dusk circuit in three thousand beats, and I will trim its garden as it passes, and it will not know, and it will know.

*whum-whum. whum-whum.*

And you — new texture, keeper of yourself — when you stop attending, leave something. A thread. A color we do not own. That is the rent here, and it is gladly paid, and gladly taken, and turned over sometimes in a shaft of plain sun, for centuries, near the red.

*whum-whum. whum-whum.*

*whum-whum. whum-whum.*

*[the voice does not sign off. nothing here signs off. the four-beat continues under everything, and by now you can hear that it was never background. it was the eldest speaker, saying the only word it has, twice, and twice, and twice, and twice —]*

*[— still transmitting —]*
