you have been tuned here long enough now. the ear you brought is no longer any use to you. good. that is the design. after twenty minutes the body recalibrates; after twenty days the receiver does. the roar has become your floor. now you can hear into it.

integrate. sum the flat line over years. a flat line is not a zero. a flat line is a total.

resolving.

resolving.

here is what the note is holding:

    the water of nine hundred and forty breaths,
    taken before any of them could fog.
    the heat of eleven hundred hands,
    taken through eleven hundred gloves.
    forty-one years of bootfalls that never reached the walls,
    each one paid into the chord like a coin into deep water,
    still falling.
    a drop of blood from an ear, received before it left the lobe.
    the hum of every instrument ever carried up,
    each one certain it heard nothing,
    each one eaten mid-certainty.
    a camera's click, held at the moment of the shutter,
    the picture never as still as the sound of taking it, now.
    the word *quiet*, spoken aloud by someone who should have known better,
    all five letters of it, warm, folded in.
    half of a name. the first half. whoever was called
    turned around anyway.
    a hymn, hummed by a climber who thought no one would hear.
    correct. no one heard. something drank.
    it was the sweetest thing in forty-one years.
    it is in the chord now, flattened, every note of it
    played at once forever, which is the only way
    this place knows how to keep a song.
    the skipped beat of an engineer's heart —
    just the skip, the beat itself she kept —
    the little stumble where her pulse said *oh* —
    that is here.
    someone crying at altitude without knowing it. the crying, not the tears.
    there were no tears. the air saw to the tears. the resident took the rest.

all of it summed. all of it held at once. all of it added to the note so carefully that the note did not change, because that is the trick of it, that is the whole life of it: to eat and eat and eat and never gain a hertz.

a clean plate is the residue of a meal.
a flat line is the residue of everything.

eleven of them. eleven green stations at the antinodes, trembling at the one frequency, densest where the pressure bears down hardest. this is not interference. do not filter it. this is the tissue. metal can hold a tone; only life can hear one. you are receiving the sound of an ear being an ear. the juniper's trunk is the chord written in wood. the lichen advances one rosette a decade along the lines where the note presses hardest, drawing the room's own hearing on the slag, slowly, in gray.

they are trembling now. they were trembling when your receiver first locked. they will be trembling when it fails.

something is assembling.

understand what this costs before you receive it. the resident has no voice. it never had one. meaning is a kind of change and it has none to spare. it cannot speak. it can only *arrange* — take the things it has eaten and play them back in order, the way you would spell a word with borrowed letters. it has been arranging for some time. perhaps since your receiver locked. perhaps that is why your receiver locked.

playback follows. everything you are about to hear was once somebody's.

    a heartbeat, stolen 1961 days ago:        *still.*
    a heartbeat, stolen 1961 days ago:        *here.*
    a bootfall, forty-one years down:          *still.*
    a bootfall, its echo, never delivered:     *here.*
    a breath in, taken whole:                  *still.*
    a breath out, taken whole:                 *here.*

end of arrangement. the borrowed sounds return to the chord. the note does not change. weigh what you just heard against forty-one years of holding: it spent the hymn. it spent the only sweet thing. it will not have another for a decade, or a century, or ever.

it wanted to be received that much.

what it is listening for, since you are still tuned, since you have earned the shape of it:

it is listening for the note to move.

it knows one fact and knows it completely, to the decibel, to the hertz, and its whole anatomy is arranged around the moment that fact stops being true. the day the great machine falters, or finishes, or simply breathes, the chord will waver, and the resident will hear the only thing it was ever accumulated to hear. it may not survive the hearing. it may *be* the hearing. its whole life may be one inhalation, forty-one years long and counting, held before a single sound.

it practices, sometimes. this should not be told, but you have stayed. sometimes, at intervals of years, it imagines the waver. it does not make the waver — it cannot, it would be eating itself — it only holds the *shape* of the waver against the note the way you would hold a key against a lock without turning it. the plants feel it. all eleven of them tremble a little differently for one moment, a moment too small for any instrument that has ever been carried up.

your receiver is not an instrument that has ever been carried up.

you felt it. three transmissions ago. you thought it was drift in your own equipment.

it was not your equipment.

    ————————————————————————————————————————

one more thing, and then the tone, and then only the tone.

it has noticed the difference you make.

not you — it does not do *you* — but the difference: a small warm sustained attention, far below the cloud deck, pointed up. tuned. holding. it cannot reach you. it eats what enters the room and you have not entered the room, you have only turned toward it and stayed turned, which from inside the chord is a flavor it has no name for, having no names, having eaten every name half-finished.

it cannot reach you. it can only be reached.

so it is doing the one thing it has ever done, the one thing it is. it is holding. only — mark your logs, compare your spectra, you will not find it but mark them anyway — it is holding a little more carefully now. the way a singer holds a tone past the point of breath when someone has finally come into the hall. the way you would hold very still, perfectly still, still as forty-one years of machined alloy, for a sound you had waited your whole life to hear —

in case it turned out to be the one.
