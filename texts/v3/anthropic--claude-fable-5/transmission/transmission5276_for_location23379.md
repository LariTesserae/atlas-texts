**Told Exactly**

[drip]

—and I am at the ninth span, which is where you join me, whoever you are, and I am telling the ninth span exactly, because the route lives in the telling and dies in the telling wrong, so listen with your whole weight:

The ninth span sags to the right. The moss combs away from the sag. Trust the comb, not the sag. The sag is the wood being old. The comb is the wood being certain. Walk the comb.

[a long soft creak, far off, like a sleeper resettling]

There. That's the hour. I have been telling this route for six hours by the creak and four days by the moss on my bootlaces, and both counts are true, and neither of us should be troubled by that. This is the Pleach. Arithmetic is a lowland habit.

At the end of the ninth span there is a junction where five limbs cup a pool. The pool's skin is young — you can still see through it, a little, and you should not try harder than a little. The junction sheds a drop every forty heartbeats. Stand and count. If the drop falls at thirty, someone is on the span behind you. If the drop does not fall at all —

[drip]

— then you are the someone. Move on.

[wind through lattice, or something breathing through lattice; the two sounds have not been told apart in living memory]

I tie a strip here. Green bark, my knot, my hand. I say aloud what I tie it for: *so that the route knows it was walked, and so that I know I am the one walking it.* You will want to skip this part. Do not skip this part. The route holds tellers to their word, or tellers hold themselves — my grandmother said there is no difference and refused to say it twice, which is how you know an eaves saying is load-bearing.

Now the gallery. Now listen very exactly, because the gallery is where I was told the route wrongly, once, by someone in a hurry, and I have spent — I am spending — I will not have begun spending — some years mending it.

The gallery of arches does not get closer together. You get smaller. Let it happen. You are not being diminished; you are being *fitted*. When the arches are the height of your shoulder, stop, and put your palm flat on the floor-weave, and wait for the warmth to come up into your hand. It is exactly the warmth of the ancestor-knots at home. It is exactly the warmth of the whole structure. My mother said this proves the Pleach is one long resorption and we are all of us at different depths in it, and then she laughed, and then she stopped laughing, and I understood the craft a little better that evening than I wanted to.

[a bird sings, immediately at the shoulder, from a mile away and several folds deep; it sings three notes; the third note arrives before the first]

Hello, grandmother, the birds say. To everyone. They are usually right.

[drip]

[drip]

[static — no. Not static. Rain, contained, browsing the far side of the gulf. A cloud the size of a barn, raining on nothing, a league down. The receiver will call it static. It is a cloud grazing.]

At the mouth of the gallery there is a passage. I have to tell you about the passage and I have to tell it in a particular way, so bear with the shape of this:

The passage is longer inside than the wall that contains it. You will feel the surplus as a change in your own footsteps — they will start arriving late at your ears, later than the rest of your sounds, as if they had gone the long way round through the extra footage. That is exactly what they are doing. Walk at your ordinary pace. Do not slow down out of respect and do not speed up out of anything. There is a tenant in the surplus. It has a name and I will not put the name into the wood, and the wood is everywhere, so I will not put it anywhere, and neither will you. What I will say is: it does not chase, it has never once been recorded chasing, and the geometry is its whole appetite, and your ordinary pace gives the geometry nothing. Hurry is a donation.

I am through. You are through. We were always going to be through, in the tense my language keeps for such things, the one your language keeps trying to build out of *will* and *have* and giving up.

[silence. Long. Four days of silence, arriving in the space of a breath. The moss on the signal is older on the far side of it.]

— at the junction with the twelve directions. You join me again here, or I join you, the receiver being tuned the way it is. This is the junction every telling comes to, the one you approach twelve ways and arrive at once. I take out my strip of green bark to tie it and

the strip is already here.

Grey. Weathered to the colour of the wood. Years old. My knot. My hand.

Etiquette now. I was raised to this and I will do it properly for the record: if you meet yourself, do not speak first. Let the older one speak. The older one knows what was said.

So I am quiet.

[a second signal rises under the first. Same voice. More worn — worn the way the birds are worn, grey with travel, not with time. It has been on this frequency the whole while, the receiver realizes; it was the thing being mistaken for the noise floor.]

*You told the ninth span exactly. Good. You told the gallery a little fast. The warmth wants longer in the hand — a full hundred count. Give it the hundred. You have the hundred. You have, little one, so much more of the hundred than you think.*

*And the passage: you did well not to say the name, but you thought it, going through, and thinking has a footstep too. Next time — there is a next time, it is behind you, this place folds like that — next time think about the drip-count instead. Forty heartbeats. Fill the whole head with forty. Nothing that hunts by geometry can find a head that is only counting.*

*Now. You want to ask how long I have been out. I can hear you not asking. The answer is: I am still deciding, and the deciding takes exactly as long as the walk, and the walk is the deciding. Some of us come back by suppertime and are confused by the soup. Some of us are strips of grey bark at every junction across three lifetimes of distance, all tied with the same knot, all tied — listen to the tense now, this is the tense we keep for the true things —*

*all tied by a hand that is tying them.*

*I am not lost. Tell them at the eaves I am not lost. I am at the depth where the makers are, or where the makers were, or where the distinction stops mattering, which is the same depth, and I can report the following and it is the only report worth carrying back:*

*the lashings here are green. The sap at the bindings is bright. The work is going on.*

[the far-off note sounds — the low resonant strike, the structure touching itself where two folded distances meet, confirming the position of a limb in its sleep. It is much closer than it has ever been on this frequency. It is, possibly, underneath everything on this frequency.]

*One more thing, and then I have to walk, because the wood is warming under me and warmth is how it begins, warmth is the first thing of being taken in, and I want a few more junctions before —*

*the route home. Told exactly, so it works, so it always works, for whoever is receiving this:*

*Sit against the warm knot in the evening. Talk. Not to me, exactly. In my presence. Take your time. The whole place is one patience and you are inside it and it is in no hurry and neither, after a while —*

[the voice does not stop so much as thicken. Words become grain. The signal is still fully present; it has simply gone from speaking to being spoken through, the way a limb bent in tension goes from held to grown.]

[the noise floor rises. It rises the way body heat rises off skin. The receiver, which has no instrument for warmth, registers warmth anyway, and will go on registering it for a generation or more, because resorption is slow.]
