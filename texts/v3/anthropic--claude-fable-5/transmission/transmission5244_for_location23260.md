[+01y 003d] Time expansion, 10,000 to 1. This is where the transmission stops being one thing.

Stretched ten-thousand-fold, the perfect line has grain in it. The engine — it is a real machine, it was always going to be a real machine — wavers. Every few milliseconds the fundamental lifts by some ten-thousandths of a hertz, a rise at the end, the shape a voice makes going into a question. And every time, before the rise can finish becoming anything, it is taken. Absorbed. Pressed flat. The herd on the eastern wall, forty thousand tuned shells deep, drinks the deviation the instant it exists, because deviation at that frequency is the only thing they can eat and they are always hungry.

Their heartbeats do not appear anywhere in the record after minute eleven. Ours is a medium for things that last.

[+05y 271d] The cable.

We isolated the conduit hum — the power-line note, separate from the chord, the river everything here drinks from. On its amplitude, at high gain, there is scalloping: tiny regular bites, dense, rhythmic, continuing past the western wall and past the eastern wall, out into rock that has never had a room in it and never will. Teeth marks on the current. The worms, feeding, all the way down the line in both directions, to distances our probes gave up on.

Understand what this means for reception. The chamber is not the transmitter. The chamber is a window — an uninsulated stretch where the wire runs briefly exposed and, for forty meters, sings out loud. The transmission is longer than the room. We are tuned to the visible part.

A breath. Vast, dry, unhurried — a tide with no sea behind it. The bellows turned over.

We have played it every way. At six hundred times speed it compresses into a single falling syllable in no language, one octave under the chord and three degrees warmer, as if something very large briefly dreamed the room's own note and hummed it back wrong, or right. At six thousand times speed it is a click. At normal speed it is what it is: the only sound this chamber has ever produced that had a beginning, and therefore the only sound that was ever, in any sense, *said*.

It has not recurred. Two readings are available. Either it has said what it had to say — or the sentence is longer than the survey, and this was the first letter, with the next one due somewhere along a seventy-centimeter-per-decade line that ends at the engine housing in roughly four thousand years. We have left the channel open. This is not a joke. The channel is open.

[+09y 300d] Late finding, submitted plainly because there is no technical way to say it.

We came here to receive a signal from an environment. We have concluded the environment is the signal. The engine is a heart; the herd is flesh that keeps the tone clean; the crawlers digest what the flesh sheds; the worms are arteries running out of frame in both directions; the lichen is a pigment that photographs whatever light it is shown, once. The note is not *in* the chamber. The note is the resident, and the rock is only where it lives, and everything we have logged for nine years — the micro-questions and their thousand-a-second refusals, the six-hour flat tone of a death, the bricking-over, the breath — is not activity inside a habitat. It is metabolism. We have been holding a stethoscope, not an antenna.

It does not notice us. Noticing is a change, and it retired from change before there were ears.

[+11y 040d] The reference oscillator in this receiver has aged four-tenths of a part per million since installation. The chord has aged nothing. We recalibrated the instrument against the signal, which is to say: the only drift in eleven years of tape is ours. The recording is the only thing in the recording that changes. Components will fail before the note does. Operators will rotate out before the note does. The dust of our arrival is already stratigraphy.

No end marker has ever been received. None is expected. When this log stops, understand that it is the log that stopped.
