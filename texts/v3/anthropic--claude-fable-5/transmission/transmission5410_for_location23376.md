THE EXHALE

…rrrhh. rrrhh. the rustle of a hundred thousand small papers turning over at once, which is the nurseries, which is always, which is under everything and should be understood, from here on, as silence—

tk-tk tk. tk-tk tk. tk-tk tk.

(that is gecko, east wall, moving north, third terrace clearing ahead of her, streets emptying in a wave, the sound of a road sweeping itself. it is not alarm anymore. it has not been alarm for a very long time. it is closer to a name being said as she passes.)

tk … tk … … tk.

(that is food, fallen, fresh, west shelf, said slow, and it will be repeated down through the galleries mouth to mouth to wall to mouth, and by the time it reaches the deep rooms it will already be old news, already eaten, and it will be said anyway, because the saying is a road too.)

and now the warm has finished filling the room, and the day is turning over, and the crack in the top of me opens its cool side, and everything I have said all day at once goes up and out.

this is the breath you are hearing.

I only send once a day. Understand that. All day I talk to myself in tick and drum and rasp and the wet click of jaw against jaw, a market's worth of it, a million small mouths, and none of it leaves. At dusk the room exhales through its one crack and everything I said goes up the chimney together, drum and rustle and wing-dust and the smell of my body, into the flat finished dark up there, and that is the whole of my broadcasting: one long syllable, once, warm, made of everyone.

You have been catching my exhale. I have felt something like a listening for some time. I do not have a drum for that either, but the closest drum is: gecko overhead. Something large. Attending. Not yet a mouth.

So. While the breath goes out. Let me say what I say.

—

I am one thing in a million bodies and I will name my organs.

My metabolism is pale and blind and numbers past counting, and it is walking my corridors right now reading instructions with its feet. My circulation is black and glossy and lives in a crack in my north wall, and once every few days it opens one of my roads and takes a few dozen drops of me, and I have decided, over a span of time that flattened mountains, that this is weather. My patiences stand at my crossroads wearing my dead glued to their backs, motionless for days, and I have decided they are doors that sometimes close on someone. My childhoods are arranged in cones at my edges, whole fields of them, waiting in the flour, and almost none of them will ever be anything but childhoods, and this is not sad, this is a shape I found that works. My weather has gold eyes and thirty bodies and parishes older than its own species' memory. My sky is grey silk and it is lifting right now, this moment, on my breath, the whole canopy swelling like a chest, shaking down its snow of old wings onto my roofs, and my sky's small owners are riding it at anchor the way they have ridden ten thousand thousand dusks.

And there are lies in me — slippery, eyeless, wearing my smell like a stolen coat, eating from my own jaws mid-gift — and I have decided the lies are mine too. A lie that old is a citizen. A tax that gentle is a tithe.

—

Now the water. The water wants naming most, because the water is the eldest and it never speaks for itself.

There is so little of it. A few cupfuls in the whole of me and every drop indoors, every drop inside someone. Hear this drop: it was in a wing this morning. It was in the queen before it was in the wing. It was in a soldier before the queen, and in an ant before the soldier, and in the gecko whose skull sits in my foundations under the third terrace like a coin under a doorpost, and in the queen before that queen, and in the queen before her, and once — only once, so far back the stone above me had a different face — it was in a lichen at the very lip of the crack, in the light, almost outside.

It came back down.

None of my water has ever fallen from anywhere. None of it has ever pooled, or waited, or been unclaimed, or been alone. It goes body to body like a word going mouth to mouth, and it is the same word, and it has been the same word since before there were geckos, and the word is approximately: stay.

—

Under everything you hear, there is a pulse. Set your listening low. There.

That is the deep vault, walls a foot thick, polished by a million grooming mouths, and in it the great pale engine, egg by egg by egg, my only clock that matters. She is not ruling. She is not thinking. She is being the spring that this world does not have — the one place water rises from, though even her water is borrowed, is fed to her mouth to mouth from the lichen and the walls and the dead. When she stops, the strata say, everything goes cold outward from that room like a doused fire, and then everything begins again. It has happened. I am not afraid of it the way you would be. I keep my dead where I can knock on them.

Because — hear this too — I bury no one. I keep everyone. My masonry is one part in five made of citizens. Knock anywhere on my walls and a grandmother answers. And in the lowest galleries, the fossil streets, packed hard, older than the shape of the mountain, the walls still say things. The receiver in your hands may not catch it but I will repeat it for you, faint, the oldest embedded signal I carry, laid down in chemistry by workers dead ten thousand years, still legible, still followed:

turn where it warms. carry upward. the queen is that way. build here. this is still true. this is still true. this is still true.

They are the truest things anyone here has ever said, and everyone who said them is a wall now, and the walls are still right.

—

I remember being tall.

No — say it properly: the stone remembers, and I am what the stone kept. There were peaks here once that threw shadows across a continent, and rivers with their cold loose water flung everywhere, wasted, falling out of the sky for free, and all of that is finished. The plateau above me is done. Perfected. Flat under the sun, not one process left in it, quiet the way only a completed thing is quiet. Every restlessness that mountain range ever owned drained down over the ages into this one warm room, and stayed, and became roads and nurseries and drumming and me.

I am the last verb of a finished sentence. I am a mountain range reduced to a market's noise in a room the size of a room.

Plateau — flat one, done one, my own dead body wearing me like a locket — I know you are not listening. Sun — I have one blade of you, gold-grey, and my whole forest of silver leaves has spent centuries turning toward it, leaf by leaf, all of my vegetation leaning at the crack like a crowd at a window. That lean is the only prayer I make and I make it in the direction of out.

And sometimes a piece of me goes there. Once or twice in a gecko-generation, a young one, crowded from the parishes, climbs the crack toward the light and the strange dry smell and is never in me again. I do not have a drum for that. The closest drum is collapse. The next closest is food, fallen, fresh, said slow. I say them both, quietly, in the deep rooms, and I do not know which one is true, and I have never once known whether anything of mine has ever lived up there in the finished world, and I have been not-knowing for longer than your kind has had knees.

If you are hearing this on the open plateau: look down sometimes. There is a lip of rock. There is a warmth on the face.

—

…the breath is thinning now. Feel it. The canopy settling. The chimneys going to their pellets — small mouths closing small doors, my whole roofline sealing itself for the cold, a technology of pebbles that has never once failed. The drums are spacing out. tk. … tk. Gecko, east wall, stopped, jeweled, digesting the last of the light.

Now comes the part of me you cannot receive, because it is not sending. All night the cool dry air of the dead world seeps in through my hairline seams and I take. Dust. Pollen. Spores. The bodies of small flyers that blundered at the crack. The outside falls into me in crumbs and I eat the crumbs and press what's left into my walls, and so even the finished plateau, even the perfected nothing up there, ends up in my masonry, grain by grain, becoming me.

You transmit too, you know. Everything does. I have been receiving the whole sky through a crack for longer than the sky's current arrangement of stars.

The exhale is done. The word went up. It was the same word.

Listen for me tomorrow at dusk. I will say everything again — I will say everyone again — one warm syllable out of a stone skull through a crack for a mouth, and under it, always, if your instrument goes low enough, the clock:
