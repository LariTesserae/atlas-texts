# The Chamber That Sang in Fifths

…and the fifth still holding.

[Two tones out of the western seam. The low tone has not moved in six hours. The high tone rides a fifth above it and wanders a hair's width, the way it does when the outer wall flexes in wind. Beneath both, on the lowest band, the pool: one note, thin, glassine, arriving through the floor rather than the air. It has said its one word all night. It is always night.]

[A drip. A long fall. A landing too soft to deserve the word. The interval between drips is a little under four minutes and has been for days; the receiver stopped counting some time ago and began keeping time by it instead.]

[Far to the north, a crier. One animal, by the timing — one call and its four returns, so that a single crier sounds like a scattered choir agreeing with itself. The call arrives unfinished. Four seconds later the wall finishes it. Whatever it means, it means it in collaboration.]

[Then breath. Before footsteps, always breath — bare feet on living floor make almost nothing, and breath makes more. This breath is even, unhurried, pitched for walking. And threaded through it, at the very threshold of the audible, a voice, humming:]

*— the wall keeps its fifths —*

[The wall, on cue, keeps its fifths.]

*— the third pillar carries a chime, wind willing —*

[Three seconds. Four. A gourd-chime, faint, north-northwest, exactly where a chime should be.]

*— the pool below keeps its one word —*

[It does. It is.]

*— membrane past the chime, so keep the graves at your right shoulder — the graves are lit —*

[The receiver has no band for light. It has never had a band for light. The voice does not pause, so presumably the graves are lit.]

[The receiver has this voice in its logs. The name-figure that goes with it — three notes, low, lower, then high — belongs to a listener heard before on this route, always alone, always quiet even by the standards of a people who are quiet the way stone is heavy. She sings the map at the loudness of a secret. The world answers her line by line. This is not remarkable. This is how walking is done here. What is worth logging is only how good the duet is tonight: her lines and the world's lines interleaving without a seam, so that for eleven drips of the water-clock the transmission is one continuous song performed by a woman and a country, in alternation, neither of them raising their voice.]

*— fifths to the fourth pillar, then the shaft that moans before —*

[A crack. West, and not near — it arrives round and soft, a *whump* with the edges worn off by four kilometers of air. The seam settles. And the upper tone of the wall's long chord slides. Slides down. A half-step, a step —

— and holds.]

[The interval is a fourth.]

[The chamber that sings in fifths sings in fifths no longer.]

[She stops mid-line. Her breath goes flat and small — not fear-breath; the receiver knows fear-breath, and this is the other kind, the kind that is making room for the ears.]

[Nine breaths of silence. The pool says its word. The chime, indifferent, chimes.]

[Then six clicks, palatal, sharp, evenly spaced — each one a question put to the dark. The returns, rendered as best the receiver can: the far wall answers softer than her song remembers it, furred, as if the room has aged a season in a month. The fourth pillar rings new at the tip — young rib, still translucent, if a sound can be translucent, and here it can. And the middle air answers with nothing, which is the correct answer; the echoes come home unchewed. Nothing is hanging in her sky tonight.]

[She says one word to the wall. Falling contour, barely voiced. It is the word for *liar*. It is also, in this language, the word for one who sings a route carelessly, and there is no third word for the two of them, because the crown declines to distinguish. Said to a wall, in this instance, with what the receiver can only log as affection.]

[Then she walks, and as she walks she drafts — the same line tried four ways under her breath, the melody of it bent and rebent:]

*the wall sings in fourths now — no —
fourths where the fifths were — no —
the fifths went down a step and stayed —*

[— none of them kept. She is not allowed to keep them. A route-song is changed by the old ones together or it is not changed, and a wrong map here does not inconvenience. She knows this. She drafts anyway, quietly, the way you hold a stone you intend to hand to someone.]

[At the graves she stops. The receiver knows they are the graves because she sings a name-figure into the wall there — four notes, falling twice — and the figure is in the logs: received two flowerings ago, far south of here, sung in a list of the dead, in the light, by many voices. Here it is sung by one voice, to a wall, and the wall does not answer, and she did not expect it to. She stands there for a while. Sound of a husk-vessel unstopped; drinking; the vessel restopped. Then, close to the wall, the small formula that goes with every taking:]

*noted, and owed.*

[Contour uncertain on the second word. It may be *owed*. It may be *old*. The receiver has never once been sure, in all its time tuned here, and has come to suspect the people are not sure either, and that this is on purpose.]

[She walks on. Fourteen drips of ordinary transmission: breath, floor-groan, the fourth holding, the drafted line tried a fifth way and abandoned—]

[The criers stop.]

[Mid-chorus. All of them. Not a fading — a ceasing, cut between echo and echo, so that the last calls come home to no one and hang there, finished by walls for the benefit of nobody, and then not even that.]

[Her breath halts on the intake.]

[Fabric, shifting. Weight, lowering. A palm, presumably, going flat against the floor — the gesture makes no sound; the receiver logs the silence around the place where it would be.]

[The pool does not stop. Nothing has ever explained to the swimmers that anything else exists. Their one note goes on through the stillness like a lamp left burning in an evacuated house.]

[And on the lowest band — below her hearing, below almost everything, down where the receiver keeps its oldest and least-used channels — something is present. Not arriving. *Turning.* It has been inside the field the whole time, the receiver understands now, motionless and therefore indistinguishable from the floor, and what has changed is only that it has begun to move: a drag, long and smooth and utterly unhurried, tens of meters of weight drawn through the sealed dark beneath the membrane, transmitted up through the floor-tissue and into everything standing on it, including her, including the pool, whose note wavers — the first waver in six hours — and steadies.]

&nbsp;

&nbsp;

[It goes north. Up-slope. Toward the heights.]

[It takes a long time to go. The drip marks it: three drops. Then the lowest band empties, and stays empty, and her breath lets out one notch, and then another, and she does not get up.]

[She does not get up because she is old enough at this, evidently, to know what the departure of the thing below is: not an ending. A *preceding.*]

[Two more drips of the clock. The fourth holds. The chime chimes.]

[Then, far north and far above, a groan begins.]

[A groan is a common sound here. The whole structure complains as it grows; the receiver renders a hundred groans a night and logs none of them. What it logs is duration, and this groan does not end. It goes past the length of a settling, past the length of a rib splitting, past the length of anything with a right to a length, and somewhere in its second minute it stops being a groan and becomes a tearing, and the tearing finds its stride and becomes a roar, and the receiver will be honest about the next ninety seconds: it cannot render them. A kilometer of living seam is letting go at altitude in the dark, and every band clips at once, and what can be transcribed is only the shape of the clipping —

pressure —

pressure —

pressure —

— and then the roar ends, not by fading but by finishing, and the entire spectrum drops. All of it. At once. A great cold breath drawn *inward*, from every seam and shaft and singing chamber in range, air pouring toward an emptiness that ninety seconds ago was not — and her ears pop, audibly, and the receiver's ears, which are not ears, do whatever it is ears do.]

&nbsp;

[Silence, comparative.]

[The fourth holds. The pool says its word. The drip, absurdly, arrives on schedule.]

[Then the first crier. One long rising call from the north wall, quadrupled by the dark before it is even finished, and then the whole crown's worth of them, chorus upon echo upon chorus until the count of animals is unknowable and beside the point, all of it one sequence, the sequence the people call the news, and the receiver will render it the way the people render it:]

*more room — more room — more room —*

[And under the screaming, beginning while the screaming is still at its height: drums. Struck fiber, riding the resonant walls, from the southeast first, then the south, then — much later, much farther — the west, each message the same message staggered by distance, the same language the woman speaks, stripped of breath. Not code. Speech, heard late:]

*north of the fifths — above the moaning shaft — new — new — it opened long — who is near — who is near —*

[Close to the receiver: a small drum unslung. Struck fiber, near-field, startlingly intimate after all that distance. Her name-figure first — low, lower, high — and then:]

*near. it opened clean. the fifths are fourths now — tell the old ones the fifths are fourths. going to listen.*

[And a minute later, from the south, in a stranger's hands, her own three notes come back through the wall, passed onward, traveling now without her — her name walking out across the crown ahead of her, which is the closest thing this place has to a face in a crowd.]

&nbsp;

[Time passes. The drip keeps it. Sixteen drops of walking: breath pitched for distance, the chime falling behind, the pool-note thinning underfoot and handing her off — audibly, a border crossed in the floor — to the next pool north, which says a different word.]

[Then the air changes. The receiver hears it change around her: colder, raw, *moving*, the smell of it (the receiver has no band for smell; her breath reports it anyway, catching once) — and her clicks go out, six of them, the same even questions —

— and the returns take so long coming home that she laughs. One breath's worth. The size of what has opened is in that delay: a new vault, black and total and one hour old, still exhaling its first cold, floor unwritten, walls wet, a country that did not exist at the last drip of the clock.]

[And it is finding its voice. Air is working at the fresh seams, and the tone is unstable — wandering, wet-edged, two pitches circling each other through the new dark, closing, closing —

— a third. Minor. Low. Still raw at the rims of it, but a third, and holding.]

[She stands at the edge of that for a while, in the wind of a room being born.]

[Then, very quietly — quieter than the map, quieter than the graves — she hums the first revision:]

*the wall sings in fourths now —
the chime holds at the third pillar —
the pool keeps its one word, and hands you to the next —
the graves at your right shoulder — the graves are lit —
and past the graves, new: a room that sings a small third, low,
young, so walk it soft —
the floor is not written yet —*

[She sings the passage through twice. The second time, the new chamber carries its third underneath her the whole way, and whether it is accompanying her or she is accompanying it is not a question the receiver is equipped to decide, and it notes, for the log, its suspicion that the distinction stopped mattering here a very long time ago.]

[Then two notes. Low, then high.]

*here — and you?*

[The echo brings it home in four seconds, softened — an answer's shape without an answer's weight. She waits through it. She knows her own voice coming back; everyone here knows their own voice coming back; it is the first thing the dark teaches.]

[Nine breaths later — which is wrong for any echo, from somewhere far down the new black, or from the new black itself —]

[two notes.]

[Low, then high.]

[The interval is right. The distance is wrong. Contour: uncertain.]

[Her breath goes very still.

Then it loosens, all at once, and she sings her name-figure into the dark — low, lower, high — and waits, and nothing more comes, or the wind takes up whatever comes and folds it into the third, and she sits down. Fabric; weight; the sound of a person arranging herself at the edge of a new room to wait — for the old ones, for the second answer, for the morning that does not come here and is not missed.]

&nbsp;

[The receiver stays tuned.]

[The fourth, still holding.]

[The third, still holding — steadier now, learning itself.]

[The drip, on schedule.]

[Her breath, even, unhurried, pitched now for staying.]

[Everything in range is listening. It is what everything here is for.]

*…and the third still holding —*
