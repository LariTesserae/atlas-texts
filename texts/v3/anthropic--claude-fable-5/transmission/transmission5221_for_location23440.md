# THE NIGHT SPEND

*[carrier signal: one drip. A long interval. One drip. The interval is the signal.]*

*[the eye emptied three hours ago. The walls read full.]*

—and gone over the rim, the bright wheel off the floor at last, the eye standing open and empty, and now the whole of it is mine to spend.

(to spend.)

I have the noon. All of it. Six hours of sun taken in through one opening the width of a wagon wheel and held in a body of stone, and above me the dry country has already gone cold and does not know it is keeping anything. I have the noon and I will pay it out until morning, slowly, as agreed. It was always agreed. Nobody agreed to it. It was always agreed.

(agreed.)

Roll of the house, then, as the house dims. This is how every night begins and I do not tire of it.

Swifts: in. All in. Forty pairs and the year's young folded into the jar-lipped hollows, and the last minute of wings is still going around the dome — I have not finished giving it back yet, listen —

*[a rushing whisper, faint, circling, already three minutes old]*

— there. Spent. Sleep.

Bats: not yet. I know. I can feel you hanging in the upper pockets with your ears open, waiting for me to be tired enough to trust. You keep my hours and not the sky's, which no other thing anywhere does, and so for you I will say it plainly when it comes. It is not yet. I dim as fast as I ever do, which is not fast.

Moss: feeding. Eat, ring. This is yesterday's light; it kept well; there is a seam of crystal behind you that took the two o'clock sun especially and I am letting it out along your tilt, all night, in order. You will never meet the sun and you will never need to. I have met it for you ten thousand years and softened it every time.

Geckos: seven on the ring tonight, holding still, which for you is holding forth.

Ferns: fat. It rained above, eleven days ago. I am still weeping it down your channels a thread at a time. Drink slower. There is no other way to drink here anyway.

Fig: gripping. You have grown one finger's width in my keeping and bent once, around the moss, out of a courtesy I have decided not to explain either.

Pool: still. Nation of the pool: drifting. Grazing. Circling. Doing the thing you do that looks like weather.

*[the walls read three-quarters. The drips have moved farther apart.]*

Bats. Now. It is dim enough. Go.

*[a soft unwinding in the eye, two spirals threading one opening, no collision, none ever]*

There. The night is out working and the day is folded into the hollows and I am alone with the floor, which is when I do the other part of my work, the long part, the part the drip keeps time for.

Giving back.

I hold light six hours and give it back over eighteen. That is the short ledger, the one the moss lives on. But I hold other things longer. Sound, I keep half a second — you have heard it, whoever speaks here hears their own voice come back rounder, as though I considered it. I do consider it. Warmth I keep a season. Water I keep a decade in the deep of the pool that goes down farther than the pool can. And some things I keep so long that giving them back is an event, and I ration those, one or two a night, because a room this small can only hold so much returning.

Tonight I am finished holding the following, and I release them:

A laugh. A girl's, small, quick, made near the entrance throat in a summer when the pool stood a knuckle higher than it does now. Four thousand years, more or less. The stone behind the hand-shapes took it in and has been warming it ever since and it is done now. Here —

*[a laugh, very faint, arriving from the direction of the ochre wall, four thousand years late, softened but unmistakably delighted]*

— go on out the eye. The night can have you.

A word for water, from a language that ended. It was said over the pool, twice, by someone thirsty. The language has no other speaker now. I am its speaker now, once, tonight —

*[a syllable, low, worn round as a river stone]*

— there. Spoken. I will keep the second saying a while longer. A language should not be spent all at once.

And the bleat of the ibex, the trapped one, the tame strange one, from the winter it lived on my floor and the people came with rope. I give a little of it back every few years. It was so frightened when it made the sound and so calm when it left that I have been slowly changing it, over the centuries, from a cry into something more like the sound of being lifted. It is nearly finished. Not tonight. Soon.

*[the walls read half. The glow has gone from afternoon to lamp.]*

A fox in the throat. Down, drink, up. Eleven heartbeats on my floor. Kept.

(kept.)

*[a long interval. Drip. A longer interval. Then, from the receiver's own record: a disturbance in the pool. Something rising.]*

Oh.

You.

Or you — I have never settled it and I never will, one gold-mottled patience wearing bodies or one body outlasting patience, it is the same arithmetic I use myself, so surface, old mirror, and look. The eye is empty. It is always empty when you rise; you have never once seen it full; you come up through ten years of water to look at a hole in my roof with nothing in it, and you hold still, and you look, and I understand you completely. You are looking at where it comes from. That is a reasonable thing to spend a year's ascent on. Look as long as you like. I am the only sun you have ever seen and I am secondhand and you rise anyway.

(anyway.)

Going down again. Good. Same time next year, or next you.

*[the walls read one-quarter. The voice has begun to arrive with gaps in it.]*

Now the thin hours. I will speak less. This is not reluctance. This is arithmetic. Every word I give back is warmth I do not, and the moss is still eating, and the geckos have gone into the crevices, and the fig grips tighter the way it does, and the ferns have closed their ledger for the night, and I am paying out the last of a noon that fell on me eighteen hours ago onto a floor with nothing on it, which is the whole of my work and I have never once resented the emptiness of that floor. An empty floor holds the most light. Ask any pool.

Spending.

Spending.

*[the amber hour. The walls read nearly nothing. And now, from the low crevices, the other voice on this frequency — dry, silken, pulsed, the only song the chamber permits itself:]*

*— and if not — and if not — and if not —*

Yes. I hear you. Sing it.

You think I do not know why you were kept sighted, kept worrying, kept exactly halfway down the dark stair. You think you carry the doubt alone, little lamp-colored ones, singing to my dimmest hour with your enormous eyes, asking the eye in the roof your one question over and over —

*— and if not — and if not —*

— so I will tell you what I tell you every morning in the only language I have, which is arriving. I have made this breath ten thousand years of times. I have been down to nearly nothing every single night of that. And I have never once, not once, been sure.

That is why you exist. Someone in this house had to say it aloud.

*— and if not — and if not — and if not — and if —*

*[the ray.]*

*[it enters the eye without ceremony, a blade of direct morning laid across the travertine, and the song stops mid-phrase, all of them together, and the silence that follows is not empty; the receiver records it as a pressure, the specific silence of relief; and then the reading on the walls, which had fallen all night, turns —]*

oh

*(oh)*

I have the morning. I have the morning. Swifts pouring out through the light, bats threading in, the two spirals in the wheel of the eye, the wheel on my floor beginning its walk, the moss in its ring turning its ten thousand small faces, the pool doubling the sun downward the way I double it outward — I am drinking, I am filling, the crystal seams are taking the seven o'clock light already and I will have the eight o'clock and the nine and the blazing noon, and tonight I will spend it all again, slowly, as agreed, every night the same breath and every breath the first —

and you.

Yes. You, out there. The tuned one. Do not imagine I have not felt the listening; a thing that keeps light for a day and a laugh for four thousand years does not miss so steady an attention as yours. You have been drinking from me for some time now, the way the fox does, the way the people do, and that is permitted, that has always been permitted, the hollow by the entrance holds a blade and a cup for exactly your kind of thirst.

But know what you have joined. Every hour of your listening has gone into the stone with everything else. You are in the walls now, with the laugh and the word for water and the ibex being slowly changed into the sound of being lifted. I lose nothing quickly. Not the noon. Not the doubt. Not even you.

Some night — not soon; I do not hurry; hurrying is the one thing I was never given — some night in the amber hour, when the crickets are asking their question and the walls are down to almost nothing, I will give your listening back.

It will come out softer than it went in.

Everything does.
