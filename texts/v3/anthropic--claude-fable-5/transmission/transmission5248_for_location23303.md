THE LONG CARRY

*[— tuned. The static here has grain to it, like brightness would, if brightness were sound. Under everything, very deep, a creak: too slow to be an event, more like pressure changing its mind. Then, close, flattened, arriving from no particular direction —]*

— and here is the arch where you cut crooked, the year of the two falls. Still at warmth. You said you'd let it heal from shame and you never did, you re-cut it every pass when you thought I slept.

I'm re-cutting it now. Hold still.

*[a sound like glass drawn through wet wood, thin, careful]*

There. First-light. Everyone will think you passed this season, and they'll be right, won't they. They'll be right.

*[tap. A single drop, somewhere out of sight, hugely magnified]*

Don't be loud. Save it.

*[breathing. Two rhythms of it — one working, one shallow. Footsteps on braided root, the small squeak of frost under soles. This continues a long time. The receiver holds]*

...the buttress with your father's route-splice. Ember now. I won't stop for it, he'd understand, he set the pace-rule himself: the carried one chooses the stops, and you haven't chosen. So we walk.

Third spring by tomorrow if the floor hasn't moved. It's moved. I can feel it under the left foot, the give is wrong, it's young there, it's all first growth for a hundred strokes — the plant's been busy on our road, love, it's been rewriting our whole eastern turning. You'd want to see. Open your eyes when I say and not before, the glare's been cruel today, the haze is thin.

*[wind, if wind can be inside something. A long hollow movement of air. The grain of the static thickens and banks]*

Gliders. Three. High, riding the cloud-edge.

No — two. Two riding.

One falling.

*[silence, except the drop-taps and the deep creak. Nearly a minute of this]*

Falling well. All day it'll take, the long sideways way. Someone's hood, someday. Someone's child wrapped warm. We won't wait for it and we won't watch it down, that's not ours. But you saw it start. You always wanted to see one start and not just find them finished, and now you have, so that's one thing the road gave you today, one thing at first-light —

hold still. You're doing the loud thing with your breathing.

I know. I know. Save it.

*[footsteps resume. Time passes in the transmission the way it does nowhere else: the drop-taps very gradually change their spacing, as if the frost has grown fatter. Then, distant — a soft, enormous, faraway sound, less heard than pressed — whump. The floor-note of it arrives seconds later through whatever the receiver stands on]*

Column down. West, far. Forty, fifty out.

The trimmers will be crossing our road tonight, then. Don't fear them. You never feared them. You're the one who taught me the grammar — say it with me, you old sign, say it —

they aren't dying at the column. They're —

*[the shallow breathing shapes two words. Barely voiced. The receiver takes them anyway:]*

— taken back.

Yes. Taken back. Good. Save the rest.

*[long walking. The whisper goes on beneath it, too low now for words, a river of low sound — route-names maybe, or counting, or neither. The deep creak comes and goes. Once: the dry intermittent clicking of plates, many of them, off to one side, passing without pause. The footsteps do not change speed]*

*[then the footsteps stop]*

Here.

Third spring's beyond, but we're not going to the spring. Look up — no, gently — the top-stroke. See it? Top of the column, where somebody climbed to cut it. Still at first-light. Somebody's been keeping it bright and nobody says who, and we never talked about that, did we, in forty years of passing under it we never once —

*[pause]*

You cut it.

*[pause]*

You climbed that, you liar, you injured old sign, when? The year of the two falls? Is that why the arch-cut went crooked, were your hands still shaking from the —

*[a sound that is a laugh in the way that a drop-tap is rain]*

You saw it. You saw it and you never told me, forty years, and now you're going to be taken back holding the one story I don't have.

Fine. Fine. Keep it at first-light in there. Keep it bright for both of us.

*[footsteps again, but slower now, and the surface-sound has changed: softer, wetter, the squeak gone. Young floor. Growing tip. The glow-grain of the static is very fine here, almost smooth]*

Here's the edge. It's soft. It's warm the way new cuts are warm.

I'm going to put you down now.

*[rustle of veil-cloth. The working breath does the work. The shallow breath is arranged upon something. Then quiet, except:]*

Listen. The rule is the rule and I'll keep it. I won't come back here. The whole inward distance is graves and this is not sad, this is not sad, we say it and we say it —

but I'll re-cut your name on every column of the circuit, every pass, until my hands go. First-light, do you hear me. You will be the brightest thing on the eastern turning. Travelers who never met you will read you blazing and think: someone passed this season. And they'll be right. Every season, they'll be right.

*[the glass-blade sound, twice — two short vertical strokes, side by side]*

One for me. One for you. No name. No message.

You were here. You were alive.

*[the shallow breathing continues for some time. The receiver stays. The whisper does not speak again, but does not leave. Drop. Drop. The deep creak, once, very long, like a body turning over beneath a continent.]*

*[the shallow breathing stops.]*

*[footsteps, one set, moving away. They take a long time to leave the signal. They do not hurry and they do not falter and near the end of their audibility they pause — once — and resume.]*

*[the receiver stays tuned.]*

*[what follows has no scale that speech was built for. The drop-taps blur into texture. The wind banks and unbanks. Somewhere in the signal a tone begins — faint, warm, person-sized, rising out of the floor-noise where the breathing stopped. It is at first-light. The receiver can only describe it the way the walkers would:]*

*[first-light. A season of it, compressed to a held brightness in the sound.]*

*[warmth. The tone settling, sweetening, losing its edge.]*

*[ember. Barely a difference now between the tone and the world around it. And here — under the ember, under everything — the static resolves. It has always been resolving; the receiver simply hears it now. The grain of the brightness is made of whispers. Thousands of them, layered generations deep, each at a different stage of fading, all saying pieces of the same small sentence:]*

— here —
      — was here —
— alive —
            — here and I was —
      — was —
                  — alive —

*[the newest voice in the chorus is still warm. It is saying the whole sentence. It knows a story about a climb it never told.]*

*[then: footsteps. Not the walker's. Not any walker's. Each one arrives the way the distant column-fall arrived — soft, enormous, felt before heard — and they are spaced like weather, and they are moving inward, away, deeper, the way they always are, the way every telling has them. They pass across the whole width of the signal. One of them lands very near the ember-tone, and pauses —]*

*[— and the tone comes up. Just slightly. Just for a moment. Re-cut.]*

*[the footsteps go on, inward. They take a long time to leave the signal. Perhaps they never entirely do.]*

*[high in the frequency band, at the very top of the receiver's range, where you would have to climb to cut it: a single short vertical stroke of clean tone. New. Blazing.]*

*[then smoothness.]*

*[carrier only. Bright, cold, in every direction, growing.]*
