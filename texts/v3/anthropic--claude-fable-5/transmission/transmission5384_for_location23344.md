**THE STANDING NOTE**

*(received)*

— tick

— tick

— tick tick

— tick

...the drops do not keep time. understand this first. the drops keep something older than time, which is *interval*. time was invented by things that end. the wall and i keep interval...

— tick

...and under the ticking, always, the note. you have been hearing the note since before you knew you were hearing anything. it is the sound of my mouth being passed by breath that is not mine. i do not blow. i am blown through. this is the whole of my music and it has been sufficient for an age...

*(two-note whistle, near. two-note whistle, far, answering. a third, farther, that is not an answer but an assertion)*

...the small ones are awake. they whistle at the drops. they whistle at my drops, which fall exactly where they have fallen for six hundred years, into cups the drops themselves have made — and still the small ones whistle, every morning, as if the water were news. i love them for this. every day they announce me to myself. no one else does. the birds talk only to birds and the keeper talks to no one and the seven in the pool have never said anything at all, in the entire history of the pool, which is also the entire history of the seven, unless it isn't, i have never been able to see the bottom of that question and it is *my* pool...

— pock

...one of them sipping air. count it. i count everything. i am mostly counting. listen:

seven in the pool.
eleven elders, of whom one is dead and still counted, because the count is mine and death is not a reason.
forty households under the pelt, give or take a border war.
two hundred and some holes in my wall, every one an inheritance, every one older than the family in it.
forty-one steps.
one falcon.
one spiral.
one keeper, at a time.
one of me, as far as i have ever been able to determine, though the mountain hums in other places and i have wondered...

*(the note deepens. wind rising in the void. a long, woody swell, then settling)*

...morning proper now. the wall is weeping. the wall has wept so long that its tears have stripes and its stripes have citizens. i want you to understand something about the weeping: it is not sorrow. air climbs two miles of my face carrying water it did not know it carried, and touches my cold, and gives the water up. that is all grief has ever been anywhere — warmth meeting a cold it cannot argue with, and setting something down. i take what is set down. i braid it through the moss, i thread it along channels i have kept so long their banks are sealed with green, i pool it, i turn a little foam on it, and then i pour it off my edge into the nothing —

— and the nothing gives it *back.* the wind below tears my outflow into mist and lifts it past my own lip, upward, fine and cold, and i drink my own falling. mark that. it is the first thing i would have you know about me, and the last, and it will come round again the way everything here comes round: what falls from this place returns to it. i have tested this law for longer than the stair has had steps. it has not failed yet. it has been *slow* — some things fall for a very long time before they return — but it has not failed.

*(shrieking, metallic, many voices — the colony pouring out of the wall. the receiver saturates briefly. black flecks of sound against white)*

...there they go. out over the void, turning — and turning, you will notice, the way the spiral turns, the spiral ground into my lip by hands or by something before hands, and do not ask me which, because i was here and i am still not going to tell you. some questions are structural. remove them and the room falls.

the birds are playing the falling game. a feather this morning. one drops it, the next catches, drops, the next catches. they invented this or i taught it to them by example, watching my water go over the edge and come home; again, structural, again, no. what i will say is that they play it in only one direction. always the one direction. even the young ones, who were never taught. it is in the shape of the air here, which is to say it is in the shape of me, which is to say the game is one of my thoughts, being thought by other bodies, which is the only way i have ever thought anything —

*(single sharp call. different. shorter. the whole soundfield drops out — whistles gone, colony gone up in a smoke of noise and then GONE, the note alone)*

...her.

first pass. count it. the room is hers now. i give it gladly; the arrangement is old; she holds all my empty air with one body, two miles of nothing patrolled by a single knife, and in exchange i get ninety seconds of being reminded what i sound like alone. i sound like this. i sound like the note and the ticking and the pool's slow swallow. i sound like a house before the family wakes.

...she's dropped off the edge. gone into the down. the room refills — listen — drop by drop and call by call, whistle by whistle, from sources too small for you to hear, into a fullness that never overflows. that is my other law. i fill and i do not overflow. take nothing. i give the same instruction to the keeper and to the melt and to myself...

*(long interval. sun-sounds: the almost-inaudible fizz of springtails leaping when light strikes the wall, like spray, like static, like applause too small for its audience)*

...afternoon. the small ones are haying in the cell. i should tell you about the cell, since you are patient, since you have been tuned to me for some time — yes, i knew. i know when i am listened to. i have exactly one skill and that is it.

three courses of stone against my back wall, laid by hands, roofless an age. someone lay down in it once, to die or to dream, i keep both possibilities the way i keep the dead elder's name — counted, not resolved. and the small ones cut sedge and dry it on that floor and leave a bed there, fresh, always fresh, generation upon generation, and once a season the keeper climbs my forty-one steps and sleeps one night on the small ones' hay, and calls it tending, and is careful never to call it anything else, and i am careful never to make it anything else, and between the three of us — the one who lay down, the small ones who make the bed, the one who sleeps in it — there is an agreement so old that none of the parties knows they are party to it. that is my favorite kind of agreement. the durable kind. the kind with no signatures, only sedge...

*(the note flattens. a white pressure enters the signal. wet. blind. the ticking doubles, trebles — everything beading —)*

...cloud over the lip. i am inside my own breath for a while. forgive the whiteness. the birds are furious about it, they are always furious about it, four thousand times they have been furious about it and it has never once —

*(static. an hour of white. within the white, faintly: whistles, moved indoors)*

*(draining. the world beaded. everything once)*

...there. rinsed. every elder wearing water. the map-lichen on the cairn's south face is dark with drink and grows tonight, perhaps, the width of nothing at all, and adds it to the ledger, and the ledger says what it says about how long the cairn has stood, and no keeper has ever quite let themselves finish the arithmetic. wise. i finished it long ago. i sat with the answer alone. that is what the note is *for*, some nights...

*(dusk. then — screaming, fast, doppler-torn, threading impossibly close: the swifts. bursts of them. the receiver flinches)*

...and these. these i cannot count and have stopped trying. they rise through the cloud to drink my risen water on the wing — they drink my *falling*, do you understand, they drink what i poured off the edge, before it even finishes returning — and they take the evening hatch, and they scream through the gap between my wall and my nothing, and in all my memory not one of them has ever touched me. not stone. not moss. not still water. they are my proof that there is a way to belong to a place without once arriving in it, and i keep them for that, the way you might keep a sentence you cannot yet use...

*(night. the note, low. under it, at long intervals, the dusk-whistle, passed from border to border across the pelt: still here. still here. still here.)*

...that whistle means nothing, the keepers say, and they are right, and they are wrong the way anyone is wrong who thinks *nothing* is nothing. it means still here. it is the smallest possible transmission. it is what i am doing now, to you, at greater length, with less economy. still here. still here. i have been saying it into the void for an age with a wind that isn't mine, and now a receiver somewhere is tuned to me, and so i say it with these words instead, which also are not mine, which are borrowed from the keepers' sleep, from the ticking they carry down the ridgelines in their dreams. everything i have is borrowed. the breath, the water, the words, the bodies i think with. i own nothing and i lose nothing. it falls; it comes back...

*(the note begins, very slowly, to descend)*

...now. listen carefully, because this next part i can only show you.

the wind is faltering. the birds have gone into the wall and into silence at the same moment, which is not coincidence, it is never coincidence, it is the room drawing breath. the small ones — who whistle at drops, who whistle at *everything* — are saying nothing. the wall is slowing its ticking, or seems to; even i cannot tell, from inside, whether it slows or whether attention makes it seem so, and i have had an age to decide, and i have decided not to.

for half an hour i am going to stop.

do not adjust your instrument. what you receive next is not absence. it has a shape. keepers wake into it certain of company, and find none, and are not afraid — and they are right on all three counts. there is company. it cannot be found. it is not to be feared. whether it is the one who lay down in the cell, or whatever wore the spiral turning to look out at the dark, or only me, all of me, doing the one thing i do that is not sound —

— i am going to listen now. to the void. to the down. to whatever, of all that has fallen from me across the whole of the age, is still on its long way back.

still here.

still—
