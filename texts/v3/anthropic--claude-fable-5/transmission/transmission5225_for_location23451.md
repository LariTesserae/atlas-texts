THE ADDED VOICE

— and under everything, still, the floor of it. The chord. It was here when the listening began and it has not stopped once, not in any gray hour, not in any noon: the near city breathing through nine hundred flues, one broad ochre tone frayed at its upper edge where the dead chimneys whistle; and beneath that, from the southwest, the wide city, lower, less a sound than a leaning, a pressure the receiver renders as *uhh* held at the bottom of hearing; and on the stillest nights a third voice, the far city, arriving thin and silvered off two hundred kilometers of dark basalt, a ghost fifth that comes and goes with the temperature of the air.

Three cities. One chord. It has a shape the way a room has a shape.

No voice in it. Nothing on this channel has a throat it is willing to use. What comes through is architecture, exhaling.

*

The dark thins. Before it goes, the fog comes up to knee height — the receiver knows it not by sight but because the whole floor of the signal fills with a sound too small to be a sound: applause at the wrong scale, a trillion bristles snapping droplets inward, a continent's worth of hands the size of nothing, clapping once each. It is the loudest hour of the day and it is barely audible. It is white and dry and fast and it thins from the bottom up as the fog is eaten, and by full light it is gone, combed out of the air, drunk.

In the towers' mouths, in that hour, there are rows of small tenants sitting upright, combing dew from their own fur, and they make no sound at all, and the receiver notes their presence the only way it can: as a texture of silence slightly warmer than the silence around it.

*

The day warms and the quilt begins to talk in its dry paper voice — a hiss rising like the noise floor of old tape, the cushion-lobes flexing rim against rim. Under the sun the basalt starts ticking. Tick. Tick. Stone remembering it is young.

And then — this, always, several times a day — a hole opens in the hiss. A hectare of sound stops at once. Every rustle, every tick of beetle-armor, gone, a silence with edges, and the silence *moves*, it crosses the plain southwest to northeast at soaring speed, and behind it the sound closes up again like water. The receiver has never heard the one who casts it. Four meters of wing and not one decibel. The great soarer is audible only as a traveling absence, a shadow you can hear.

Noon: the green closes its pores and falls quiet. Only the chord.

Dusk: the ticking runs backward, the stone giving up its heat, tick, tick, a stylus dragged slowly across the cooling of the world.

Night: the chord, clearest. The three cities. The room-shape of it.

This is the day. The plain has one day and it plays it continuously with variations, and the receiver has been counting the variations, because the variations are the news.

*

News.

Fourteen nights ago, far to the north, a soft crump — rock parting, a fissure opening — and then a long settling hiss, minutes of it, and then nothing. Within a season the green will have found it. The receiver will hear that as a slight thickening of the dawn applause from the north, and in ten years, perhaps, a new voice in the chord, a fourth city founded on the vapor. Everything here arrives first as a sound and then as a place.

Nine nights ago, worse news, close. A note went out of the near city.

Not faded — *out*. One flue-voice of nine hundred, gone between one breath and the next, and with it a shudder through the whole tone, and a distant granular roar like a wave with no water in it: a tower down. Sixty meters of columbarium, forty generations of the built-in dead, returned to the plain in eleven seconds.

And then the strangest sound the receiver holds in its memory, which is a city going flat. Not silent — flat. The work-rate of millions dropped at once; the airflow through every gallery changed; every flue detuned by a fraction, the chord gone gray and slack, and it stayed that way for three days. Call it what the timbre says it is. The walls ran bitter. The city grieved in the key of its own ventilation.

On the fourth day the whisper started. It has not stopped since. Sand through a slow hourglass, day and night: a hundred million mandibles quarrying the fallen tower grain by grain, carrying the dead back into the living walls. Last night, for the first time, a new note tried itself — wavering, a quarter-tone off the lost one, opening and closing as the birds up in the fresh flues audition the resonance, keeping the chimneys they approve of, sealing their bodies into the ones they don't. The note is steadying. It is not the old note. The city will never again sound exactly as it sounded, and nothing on the plain will notice, except everything, which is already retuning around it.

*

And then, tonight. Tonight the receiver has started counting nights, because —

NIGHT 1. The near city's voice has gone out changed. Not louder. Not a new flue. *Thickened at the root* — a warm animal fundamental laid under the mineral breath, a chest-tone pitched exactly into the chord, so exactly that for an hour the receiver believed its own filters had drifted. They have not drifted. Something with lungs is standing in the lee of the tallest chimney rank, adding itself to the architecture. The city is being sung *through*.

NIGHT 2. Held.

NIGHT 5. Held. The chord goes out across the dark, ninety, a hundred kilometers, wearing this one warm error, and the plain keeps its silence — which is not the same as no.

NIGHT 12. Held. Gray hour, applause, hiss, tick, the traveling silence twice before noon, the quarry-whisper underneath everything, and at dusk the chest-tone again, patient, patient. The receiver understands now what it is hearing: a message written in a skyline. Somewhere out there is one who has known this city's voice since birth, who carries the continent as a map of organ-notes, and who will hear the note it knows best sounding not quite like itself, and turn.

NIGHT 20. Held.

NIGHT 31. Held. The new flue-note from the fallen tower has settled into tune with everything, including the singer. The city has folded even the added voice into its idea of itself. If the singer stopped now, the chord would sound wounded.

NIGHT 44. Something in the hiss at dusk — a rhythm in it, far off, regular, forty kilometers a day of it: the paper voice of the quilt parting and closing around a walking weight, too faint to swear to.

NIGHT 46. Not too faint to swear to.

NIGHT 47. Two tones.

Two chest-tones, hertz apart, and the whole chord throbbing with the beat between them — a slow pulse, once every three seconds, then every two, then faster, the interval closing all night as the pitches walk toward each other, the near city's entire voice shivering with it, and the receiver holds very still, and the beats slow, and slow, and —

lock.

Unison. One warm doubled fundamental under nine hundred flues, held for the length of a breath that no single creature on this plain could draw,

and then gone. Both of them. At once.

*

NIGHT 48. The city's own plain voice, as it was. As it always was. The chord, the room-shape of it, the frayed ochre edge, the wide one leaning in from the southwest, the far ghost fifth arriving on the cold. Anyone tuning in tonight would hear a continent that has never said anything and never will.

The receiver knows otherwise. The receiver keeps the season in which the chord meant something, and was answered.

*

The dark is thinning now. Here comes the fog, knee-high, and here comes the applause too small to be applause, the trillion smallest mouths of the world drinking the only weather it has, and in the tower doorways the silent tenants are sitting upright in rows with the gray beading on their fur, gravely, like prayer, and the quarry-whisper is carrying the dead into the walls a grain at a time, and northward the new fissure breathes vapor up into roots that were waiting for it before it opened, and the chord holds, the chord holds, built by no one in particular, and the receiver stays where it is, tuned, because the plain has one day and is playing it again, and the variations are the news, and there will be news —

— hollow, hollow, hollow —

still receiving.
