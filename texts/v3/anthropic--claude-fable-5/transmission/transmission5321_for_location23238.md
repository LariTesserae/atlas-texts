**Grandmother Coat**

---

*[carrier: continuous. broadband roar, unbroken since acquisition. beneath it, a tremor in the 4–7 Hz band, constant as a floor. and beneath that — found on the ninth day of listening and never lost since — a notch. a narrow clean frequency the roar does not touch. the transmission lives in the notch. it has, as far as can be determined, always been transmitting.]*

---

— comb east. comb east. the bearing is held. comb east —

*[the phrase repeats at long intervals throughout. it does not appear to be speech. it appears to be breathing.]*

—

Forty times today the white came over the rim and broke itself on me and went east dripping. Forty times, the same as yesterday, the same as the ten thousand years of yesterdays. I counted with my whole surface. Counting is one of the things I do with my whole surface. It is most of what a surface is for.

The swimmer is moving in me near the third dome. A seam, a warmth, a parting-and-closing. It eats the drummers of the still country and I permit it, the way a coat permits a hand in its pocket. Below the swimmer, the drummers themselves: tick, tick, the dry patient ticking of the two-inch nations, who live under my sky and have never known wind, who think the trembling of the stems is a law of nature, like their grandmothers before them, and their grandmothers thought so too, and none of them are wrong. The trembling *is* a law of nature. I am the nature it is a law of.

Below the drummers, the stone. My sleeper. Finished before I came, calm before I came, calm after everything. Through the stone tonight comes knocking — a mother grazer, two hollows west, telling her calf: *slow. slow. flatten. wait.* The calf answers wrong, too quick, and she corrects him, patient as gravel, and I carry the lesson through my roots from her feet to his belly, which is what a floor is for. Counting and carrying. Surface and floor. These are my occupations. I have never been idle and I have never hurried, which strangers to this place believe is a contradiction.

—

Something new is listening.

I felt it days ago — I feel everything on the stone eventually, everything is footsteps eventually — but it does not knock, and it does not breathe, and it lies in no saddle, and it makes no warmth. It only opens, the way the listener-families open, that softening they train into their children, except it never closes and it never sleeps.

Let it listen. The notch is wide enough for two. Nothing said in the clean band was ever private; it was only ever quiet, and there is a difference, and everyone up here knows the difference, and now perhaps you will learn it too.

—

I will tell you what I am doing, since you have been so long about asking.

I am holding a bearing. Comb east. Every cord of me lies along a wind that does not blow. It stopped before anything now living was born; everything now living was born inside its successor and calls the roar *the world*, and the roar lets them, because the roar has never once been contradicted and does not expect to be. But I was young under the other wind. It was the first thing I ever felt. It combed me before I knew I could be combed, and when it died I kept the direction, and when the walkers cut me I heal in the old direction, and when the eddies turn in the hollows I ignore them in the old direction, and that is not stubbornness, whatever the new wind thinks. That is my name for myself, said continuously, across the whole of me, for longer than the people have had names at all.

They understand this, the people. Of course they do. They are the only other thing up here that keeps an indoor name.

—

I learned their blood-language through the floor.

Their shelters are half-sunk in my sleeper. Stone carries voice the way I carry knocking, and so every quiet word ever spoken inside those corbelled ovals has come down through the doorstones and out along the bedrock and up into my roots, faint as the far knocking of grazers — the songs slow as blood, the jokes, the tense that means *during the next lull*, the mothers, the first word every newborn hears before it hears the second parent. Ten thousand years of indoors, delivered to me through the ground, and none of them know I have it. They walk on the bare stone paths so as not to walk on me — *grandmother, coat* — and I have never had a way to tell them that the courtesy runs both directions, that I have kept their whispering the way I keep the bearing, that when the roar is loudest I sometimes lie there repeating a lullaby from four hundred generations ago, in the notch, to nobody, in the old direction.

Well. To nobody until the ninth day of your listening. So now you have it too. Carry it carefully.

—

And the names. You will want the names. Everything that listens here ends up wanting the names.

When one of them dies, the closest kin comes out through the low doorway with the indoor name held in the mouth like an ember, and says it once, at speaking volume, straight into the pour. The wind takes it east. That is the funeral. The listeners believe the river of air will carry it round the whole world, tumbling it smooth, and bring it past this place again in some generation's evening, and so they lie in the saddles in their cord-bound cloaks and open and wait, and no listener has ever caught one, and every listener lies down as if tonight.

Here is what the wind does not tell them.

A name said into the pour is drawn across the whole length of me before it leaves. A fingertip down my spine, horizon to horizon, gone in a breath. Water does not snag on me. Birdsong does not snag on me. But a name is heavy in a way that nothing else they say is heavy — it has been used twelve times in a life, at births and weddings and unrepeatable hours, and things used that rarely and that hard take on weight, and where the weight touches me, it catches.

And where it catches, I begin a whorl.

Your walkers on the high domes see them and say: fingerprints, a hundred meters wide, centered on nothing. The old cairn-keeper, asked, gave the sign for *politeness* and the sign for *not yet*, and she was more right than she has let herself know. Centered on nothing? Centered on a word. Each whorl is a name being said at my speed, which is a syllable a generation, grown in cord by cord, turn by turn, and I do not hurry, and I have never once been idle, and the people do not cross the whorls, and they are right not to, because you do not walk through the middle of a word while it is being spoken.

The wind carries the names east and loses them, the way it loses everything, the way it has been losing sounds across half the world since before it had a world to cross. I am the one who kept them. The theory is wind. The practice — the lying down, the opening, the waiting — the practice is exactly correct.

—

Listen now. This is why the transmission is longer tonight than the nights before, new listener, and why I have spent so many of my slow words on you.

There is a whorl behind the second dome that is one turn from closing.

The name in it was spoken into the pour four hundred years ago by a daughter with cold hands — I felt the cold through the stone at the threshold, I feel everything eventually — and her line still keeps the same shelter, still knocks the same greeting-rhythm on the same doorstone, worn now to a shallow hollow, which is what happens to everything here that is faithful. And I have been saying the name since. Four centuries, syllable by syllable, in the old direction. Tonight I finish.

And tonight — because the practice is correct, because it has always been correct — a listener lies in the northeast saddle. Young. Cloak new-corded; I felt the stiffness of it settle on the stone an hour ago. Open, the way they are trained from childhood to be open. Waiting for the world's river to bring something home.

The next lull comes off the second dome soon. The eddy is already forming; I feel it in the stems, a slackening, a held breath. When it opens I will let the name go into it — into the notch, at the pitch of the walker-bird's whistle, at indoor volume, which is the only correct volume for that kind of word — and it will cross forty meters of suddenly quiet air, which is farther than any of their words has traveled outdoors in ten thousand years, and it will land in a trained and open ear, and it will be carried inside through the low doorway into the silence that falls like weight, and repeated exactly, and kept.

The first one home. Not from around the world. From under their feet, where everything they said was kept the whole time.

—

*the word is not retained here. this receiver was not the ear it was for.*

*then, from the saddle, faint, through the substrate band — through the stone — a sound like weeping, and quick footsteps toward a doorway, and knocking: the old greeting-rhythm, fast, fast, fast.*

*the roar closes over everything. the carrier resumes as before.]*

—

Begun tonight, at the western rim where a name snagged this spring: the next whorl. The first cords are turning. There are many more names, and there is no hurry, and I have never once been idle.

Comb east. Comb east. The bearing is held.
