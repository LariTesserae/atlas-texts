# THE BREATHING BAND

*reception continuous. gain at maximum. what follows is quiet, amplified until it has grain.*

---

carrier first. always carrier first.

a rise. four minutes.
a settle. six.

that is the pool. that is the floor of everything. every other signal in this room arrives riding on that slow exhale, and the receiver long ago stopped hearing it as sound and began hearing it as the condition of sound — the warm medium itself, lifting, settling, lifting.

on top of the carrier, the water band: steam unwinding. it registers as a faint continuous hiss so soft it is indistinguishable from the noise floor, except that the noise floor of this room *is* steam. there is no silence here that is not made of it.

this is the talk. it is almost all the talk. the receiver has held this band for a long time now and the vocabulary has not grown; the receiver has come to understand that it does not need to. *here* said often enough, softly enough, in enough overlapping trembles, is a census, a weather report, a courtship, and a lullaby, depending on which frond carries it and how the tremor tapers. the old ones taper it so finely the receiver loses the signal in the steam-hiss. that is not signal loss. that is privacy.

something copper-colored and fast is crossing the second vine. the receiver picks it up not as sound but as the shape of the hush around it — a moving absence, a hole in the talk, traveling east.

it stops. it waits. the room waits with it.

nine minutes.

somewhere under a frond, a mistake is not made.

the hole in the talk moves on, unfed, into the wall-crack, and the fern band comes back the way a held breath comes back — one frond, then three, then the whole floor:

---

stone band, east wall. the frogs.

their channel is not sound and not tremor; it is *arrangement*, and the receiver reads it the way one reads a slow instrument, sampling the wall at intervals and comparing:

wall, east, ninth hour: `o . . o . . o . . o . . o`
wall, east, eighteenth hour: `o . . o . . o . . o . . o`

held. held all day. a sentence meaning, as near as translation permits: *nothing. beautifully, dependably nothing.*

then, near the entrance throat, one frog moves two hand-spans down and faces the steps.

wall, east, nineteenth hour: `o . o . . . o . . o . . . . o`

and over the next hour the sentence rewrites itself along the whole wall without one frog looking at another, the grammar propagating stone to stone like heat:

`o . o . . o . . . o . o . . o`

translation, uncertain: *something at the throat. not the snake. slower than the snake. patient. watch.*

the receiver notes, without comment, that the wall may be discussing the receiver.

a salamander is crossing the deep moss below the third pillar. elapsed time so far: most of the day. it registers on no band at all except as a slow deformation of the moss-plush, a pressure moving at the speed of certainty. the receiver has learned to treat these transits as forecasts. this one is heading down-chamber, toward the pool, and going deep.

the frogs have seen it too. the wall shifts a half-span lower, all together, like a congregation resettling.

within the day, something will be proven right. it always is.

---

dusk. the eye dims from green-gold to green-grey to a color that is only the memory of the leaf-shapes above it.

then the bats.

they register as a soft tearing upward — five hundred wingbeats compressed into no decibels at all, a rush the receiver detects mainly as displaced steam, the whole warm haze of the upper room swirling once and smoothing. the eye swallows them. the root-tangle hangs empty and drips.

the room is measurably lighter now. not in light. in weight. it waits the way a house waits.

---

night band. this is where the transmission changes, every time, and the receiver still is not used to it.

the fern band empties — the crickets leaving their fronds, and the ferns, unspoken-through, becoming merely plants again for a few hours. the crickets descend to the low end of the chamber and rank themselves in arcs at the edge of the glow, and then:

nothing.

the shrimp-constellation scatters. something the pink of an inner lip, longer than a forearm, eyeless, turns over once just beneath the surface of the pool — one slow roll, like a sleeper shifting in a dream too old to interrupt — and sinks.

the shrimp reassemble. the receiver maps the new constellation against the old. it is not the same shape. it is never the same shape. that is the only record the visit leaves: the stars of the pool, rearranged.

the receiver files the tone. it has seven of these on record now, spread across a long time, and lined up together they almost make an interval. almost a phrase. the receiver is not prepared to say the deep thing is singing. the receiver is not prepared to say it is not.

( . ) — and the carrier stutters, one rise arriving early, as if the room had been touched on the surface of its sleep.

then the bats pour back down through the eye, soundless, and they bring the rain in on their fur, and the whole chemistry of the air band shifts — the receiver reads it as clearly as speech: *cold up there. wet leaves. something flowering at the wood's edge. frost coming, but not yet.* the frogs will never see any of it. the crickets will never see any of it. they breathe it anyway, secondhand, the surface arriving in the room the way news arrives in a house where no one goes out: carried in on the coats of those who do.

---

and now the part of the reception the receiver hesitates, each time, to log. because it comes from below every band. below the carrier. below the glow. from the stone itself, or from whatever the stone is resting on, and it is not clear that it is being *transmitted* at all. it may be residue. it may be the difference between a recording and a memory, at a depth where that difference stops existing.

it is a shape. it repeats.

a circle. warmth at the center of the circle. bodies arranged around the warmth, facing in.

it repeats.

with it, fainter: hand-shapes pressed against damp stone. a row of short lines, counting something — nights, or children, or visits, the tally worn now to texture. and under that, faintest of all, a rhythm in the three steps at the throat: a stride. short steps. a small people, or a careful one. going down. going down. going down. the stone has been played so many times by the same stride that it holds the stride the way the moss holds light, and on the quietest nights the receiver picks it up replaying — not footsteps, nothing so crude as footsteps — just the *shape of descent*, worn into rock, repeating at the bottom of the signal like a groove the needle of the room keeps finding.

and every night, without fail, the room answers the shape. the crickets rank in their arcs. the frogs assemble on the stones around the pool. the glow banks itself at the center like a coal that was handed to someone, once, and never handed back. the circle forms. the receiver can no longer tell whether the room learned this or whether it is remembering it — whether this is broadcast or echo, dream or transcript —

and here the receiver's notes thin out, because at this depth the annotating voice and the signal begin to share a temperature, and the transcription stops being able to stand outside what it transcribes, and what comes through, in the last clean stretch before dawn, is only this, on every band at once, in tremor and arrangement and glow and breath:

warm.

held.

here.

here.

still here.

still here.

*the eye is going green-gold again. a bead has begun traveling the east wall. the fern band is waking: ~ here. ~ here here.*

*the carrier rises. four minutes. settles. six.*

*the transmission does not end. the receiver remains tuned, in the warm dark, at the edge of the arc, facing the light with the others.*
