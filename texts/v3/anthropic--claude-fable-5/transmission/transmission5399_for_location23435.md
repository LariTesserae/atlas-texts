# The Loud Hand

[—joined in progress. there is no beginning on file—]

—rrrrrhhhhhhhhh and under the roar the chord, low, slower than breathing, a pressure more than a pitch, the note the whole floor of the signal stands on—

—and the roar itself in its long swell, twenty minutes rising, twenty falling, a county of small bodies bowing one note through their feet, and where this province meets the next province the two swells drag against each other and the carrier throbs, throbs, throbs, like surf heard through a wall, like blood heard from inside the vein—

[the above continues, with variation, for eleven hours. compressed.]

[hour eleven. anomaly.]

—a pocket opens in the roar, a few seconds of near-nothing, and in the near-nothing: a voice. one word. soft at the end, like a held thing being set down.

[no voice is possible at this hour. no still-line is mapped at this bearing. the word is flagged and kept. it does not sound like a word for anything. it sounds like a word for someone.]

—the pocket closes. the roar takes the space back without comment—

—now, threaded thin above the roar: chiming. metal, cold-hammered, many small tones, ringing without being struck — ringing in sympathy, ringing at something not yet arrived — and for an hour the chimes and nothing else changes, and then the sky-band of the signal darkens, a sound like a lid, wings by the hundred thousand in a front that takes the whole hour to cross, brass voices, brass voices, and then the descent: shrieking down onto the water-mirrors, a hiss laid over everything, fifty miles of feeding rendered at this distance as one long exhalation—

—forty minutes. gone. dropped feathers settling make no sound the receiver can hold—

[night.]

—the provinces sing on. nothing here stops singing at night; night is only a change of key. but under the roar now, small watch-fires of sound: a low continuous chuckling, throats kept running in sleep, dozens of them, each one saying *still here, still here, still lawful, still in the music*—

—and once, near the third hour of dark: a hole. a silence, moving. it travels along a seam in the noise-floor like a finger drawn across a map, and it converges on a note that has gone flat, one small note fallen out of phase, and the flat note stops, and the hole closes, and the roar is whole again and does not mention it—

[the receiver notes for the record that in this signal, silence has a direction of travel.]

[the hour before dawn.]

—the roar lies down. not gone: banked, like coals. and in the hush, close to the ground of the signal, thin black threads of running water, and small mineral shells dragging themselves to drink, chiming as they go, dawn's little glass bells—

—and voices. voices, plural, human, indoors, muffled by a wall of husk. a woman, old by the grain of her, reciting: numbers, bearings, the swell-counts of provinces, rain-intervals — a calendar made of sound, said aloud to keep it kept. a child repeats each line a beat behind, getting the shapes of it before the sense—

—and after the calendar, lower, a lullaby.

[dawn-tongue. translated where the recording allows:]

*the ground is warm, the ground is warm,
the loud ones sing the blood along.
sleep with your little fire lit,
sleep with the chuckle in your throat.
the quiet ones are eaten, love.
sleep loud, sleep loud, sleep loud.*

—and after the lullaby, two other voices, younger, very low, exchanging one word each.

[two words were spoken here. they are in the recording. they are not in this transcript. some things are received and still not taken.]

—breathing, then. several sleepers. the wall of husk holds the roar outside to a drone, and inside the drone the breathing goes on being witnessed by other breathing until the chord swings up toward day—

[nineteen hours compressed: the chord, the provinces, heat-shimmer audible as a faint frying at the top of the band. once, mid-afternoon, a hole opens in the noise high overhead — not moving, just opening — and everything under it goes elsewhere at a walk, unhurried, and a while later something enormous and flat comes down hissing for a long time, a sail's worth of dead green, and lands with a boom the chord absorbs in a single breath.]

[the still-line.]

—the receiver drifts across an interference seam and into a node of cancellation, a pocket a few dozen paces wide where two provinces unmake each other, and inside it: open air, and human voices, outdoors, unguarded, for the first time in this recording—

—trade first. someone counting copper beads aloud, slowly, enjoying it, the way you'd enjoy walking on your hands: *four. five. six. seven — no. six.* laughter—

—then young voices, several, and the specific laughter of people hearing each other for the first time. a girl says something and a boy laughs so hard at the sound of her that he has to sit down, and she says, delighted, indignant: *"you sound like a seed-cone ticking,"* and he says, *"you sound like the third hour of rain,"* and there is a silence between them that is not the eaten kind—

—then an older voice, formal, carrying, doing the thing that can only be done where words carry: judgment. brief. *"you kept more than you can carry. the ground feeds on schedule. put it down."* a pause. something set down, heavy, sap-sweet by the sound of the flies. no argument. the node has already begun to drift and the voices walk with it, west, and pass out of the pocket one by one back into the roar, mid-sentence, unbothered, their talk continuing in a language the receiver cannot carry: hands, in the light, saying everything—

[the fourth dawn.]

—footsteps on glass. many. slow. a weight carried among them, wrapped in something that whispers — feathers, dropped feathers, the warm kind. the procession moves along a polished trail toward a place where the ground of the signal goes soft, where the noise-floor itself yields—

—the weight is set down in the yielding.

—and one voice speaks one word over it. soft at the end. like a held thing being set down.

[it is the word from hour eleven. it is the same word. checked, and checked again: the same voice, the same word, received four days apart, and the first time was three days before it was said.

this word is spoken once in a life, at the end of it, and then given to the ground with everything else. it occurs once in the living record. it occurs twice in this transmission. the second time was first.

the mirrors here show the sky as it will be. it appears the receiver has been lying on this ground long enough to have become a kind of puddle.]

—the ground takes what it is given, slowly, at the pace of decades, and the procession does not wait for that. they turn back along the trail and they are loud, immediately and deliberately loud, drumming on their own chests, chuckling in their throats, grief with its fire lit, grief that refuses to be a hole for anything blind to find — mourning, here, is a way of staying in the music—

[the receiver drifts. days compressed. the chord thins behind; the plates in the noise-floor grow small and tight; the roar of the upper provinces falls away like a coastline.]

[the rim.]

—wind, and under the wind, chimes on long poles, hung out over a mile and a half of rising dark. they are tuned to no flock and no weather. they are ringing anyway.

—and up out of the dark, faint, structural, pitched to the wood rather than the hide: the other chorus. slower. deeper. phrased in shapes the upper singing does not contain. it is not an echo. it has never been an echo.

—the two chorusues meet in the receiver's band and beat against each other, systole, diastole, a mile and a half apart and keeping one time, and where the upper song carries a thin place — a province gone quiet somewhere east, a hole the size of a plague — the lower song can be heard swelling toward it, from underneath, pressing up into the gap like fluid into a failing vessel, like a hand put under a stumbling child—

—and the chimes over the brink ring in their no-tuning, and something in the long phrases below shifts, minutely, and the Edge-walkers were asked once what the chimes are for and they made the sign that means *answering*—

[the chord has dropped a third. rain in three days, then a thousand warm mirrors, and every mirror showing a sky that hasn't happened yet, and this receiver among them, showing what it will show.

somewhere in the next three days of this signal is the beginning of this transcript. it has not been found yet. it will arrive on schedule. everything here does.
