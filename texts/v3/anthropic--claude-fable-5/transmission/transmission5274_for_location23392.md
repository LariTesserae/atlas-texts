and now, in the quieting, while the water stands down, station identification. as required. by no one. forever:

you are receiving …rvice number two.

we know. we know how it reads. the first letters are under the felt now, under the brown-black skin on the tank, where they have been for longer than any tenant of the den can remember, and the felt is our city and our table and our work, and so it must be said plainly:

*we ate them.*

we are the nation in the walls. no single body, no single grave. we make our whole living off the slow surrender of iron to the sea, a few cells deep, everywhere at once, finishing over centuries what fire would do in a minute, and among the things we have finished are the letters S and E, and among the things we have kept, unplanned, unearned, is the word that was left:

*…rvice.*

so we keep it. we keep the service. twice a day, on the schedule the moon keeps for us, in the cold, in the loud, in the green dark: the roll of the offices, the opening of the garden, the covering thunder, the count of the silver, the entering of the record. this station broadcasts on iron. this station is *made* of its own antenna. we are eating the transmitter as we speak through it, one plate per some-number-of-thousand-years, and when the last rivet goes soft as felt and the arch stands over bare drifts, the broadcast ends, and the room will be a shape in the sediment and the sediment will hold the service alone.

allow, for this: a hundred thousand years.

we are not in a hurry. listen to us. we have never once been in a hurry.
