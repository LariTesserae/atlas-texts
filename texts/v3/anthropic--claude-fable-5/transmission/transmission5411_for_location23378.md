The Cistern Hour

*[…carrier steady; the low thud of wheels under everything, slow as a sleeper's pulse…]*

—and the third cistern stands at a hand under full, which the keeper asks me to say is enough. Enough, by her count, for four evenings at reading strength, or six at supper strength, and she asks that we choose supper strength, because she has looked at the western haze and does not like the dryness in it, and I have never known her to be wrong twice in a season, and she has already been wrong once.

So. Supper strength tonight. Read close to your lamps.

*[a pause. In it, faintly, a vast ticking, like a cooling engine, going on and on]*

You hear that. That's the pile drying, that is, the whole western swell of it tightening at once. I have the window open a hand's width for you lowland listeners who write and ask what it sounds like up here. That is what it sounds like. It has sounded like that all afternoon and it will stop when the cloud comes, and Maren says the cloud is coming, and I will get to Maren presently.

First the frames.

Holm walked the windward ranks this morning and reports frames nine through fourteen singing flat — a quarter-tone under, he says, which means the mesh has slacked in the dry wind, and he will tension them tomorrow before the long cloud, if there is a long cloud, and Maren says there is, and I will get to Maren.

Frame forty-one carries web again. The mist-spiders have taken the whole lower third of it. Holm has left it. He says forty-one combs sweeter webbed than any bare frame in the rank and he will give the spiders two more days before he clears them, and he says this every season, and every season the spiders get their two days and then a third, because Holm is soft about frame forty-one, and everyone knows why, which brings me to the gleaner report.

Bett of the ninth line was on forty-one at first light. She favors the left wing still. She is eating well. She let Holm come within an arm of her, which she has not done since the wing, and Holm stood there — he tells me — for the better part of the morning, not tensioning anything, and I am reading this out because Holm asked me not to, and that is what he gets.

Tarrow-of-the-black-cap was seen over the southern rank. The two young ones from the Longwing line rode the noon mist in and out again without landing. That is all forty accounted for within the fiveday except old Whitethroat.

*[the wheels thud. The ticking goes on]*

Whitethroat, sixth of her line, has not ridden the cloud in since the shearing. That is nineteen days. The ledger-keeper has ruled off her line in the book but not written under it yet, out of manners, and we will give her the season, but I think we know, and I think the frames know — Holm says the ones she favored sing a little strange, and Holm is soft, as established, but he is not often wrong about frames.

If it comes to it, her name goes into the cloud like anyone's. She worked the mesh nine years. That's a working life. It counts.

*[a pause, longer. When the voice returns it is nearer the microphone]*

Now Maren.

Maren signed down from the eastern swell at midafternoon — I had the glass on her and took it myself, so any errors are mine, not hers. She held each sign the full breath, the way she does, the way you're meant to. She signed: *four wakes*. She signed: *wide*, so, ewes, the flock moving on its own judgment. She signed: *toward town*. Then she signed *long cloud*, and held it — I want to say she held it longer than a breath, held it like she was underlining it — and then *before morning*, and then the one that means *I am all right, but stand where I can see you*, which she signs at dusk every working day of her life, and which is answered every working day of her life, and was answered.

So the sheep say long cloud, and the hares said it yesterday, a wake crossing the north swell straight as a rule, and the keeper's knees said it this morning, and Maren says before morning. Framewrights, you have your evening's work. Everyone else, fill what wants filling and bring your washing in off the gallery rails.

*[the ticking outside has begun, very gradually, to thin]*

Notices.

Sull's youngest closed her pile-debt today. The moss took the last of her track by the old channel — two years' tending, from a fall she took chasing a hare-wake, which she has heard enough about and will hear no more of from me. The framewrights inspected the ground and pronounced it *settled*. She may walk the plankways alone to the fourth terrace now, which she has earned, and which — Sull asks me to add — does not mean the fifth.

The traveler who came up the plank-road on eighthday is mending mesh in the north gallery. He mends slowly, which is correct. He has stopped asking questions, which is progress. He has not yet stopped waiting for something to happen, but the town has watched rock finish settling; we can wait out a man.

The moss-pressers ask that whoever has been leaving the gallery door ajar close it, on account of the crickets coming in to the warm, which then have to be carried out again, which is nobody's best use of an evening.

*[a long quiet. Then, distantly, a sound like breath drawn across metal — one note, then a second, lower]*

There it is.

The western frames are singing. Nine and ten first — flat, as Holm said — and now the whole rank coming in behind them, and — yes. The lamps.

Listeners, I wish you could see the lamps. Everything in this room just leaned toward gold.

*[the carrier itself swells audibly; the hum beneath the voice deepens and warms. The ticking is gone. Water has begun, somewhere, to move]*

The channels are talking. The wheels will pick up inside the hour, so I will do the names now, while the cloud is on the frames and before the works want my hands.

Those below who are new to this band: we speak the names of our dead into the cloud as it comes through. The mesh combs the cloud. The water runs to the cisterns. The cisterns turn the wheels, and the wheels make this light, and this signal, which is to say — you are hearing them now, whether I read or not. The reading is for us.

I'm going to the window.

*[the acoustics open. Wind. The frame-song, many-voiced now, some notes true and some a quarter flat. The voice, when it comes, is unhurried, with a pause at the bottom of every breath]*

Orm, who kept the wheels forty years, and the wheels still run to his oiling.

Enna, mother of Doss, who wove mesh so fine the spiders complained.

Ruve the keeper, who held back water through the great dry year and was cursed for it and thanked for it, in that order, and deserved both.

Tamm, who went down to the lowlands and came back up to die, and made it, by three days, and said the three days were worth the road.

Maren's father, whose name Maren asked me to say without saying, and I have, and he'll know.

Ossa, four days old, who is in the eastern green with her grandmother, so that neither is alone out there.

*[a pause. The frames sing]*

And, ahead of the ledger and with the ledger-keeper's leave: Whitethroat, sixth of her line, who rode nine years of cloud onto our frames and took her wages in droplets and never fouled a mesh. If she comes in on tonight's wall after all, she may consider herself read early and hold it over us forever.

*[a fine spatter against the microphone — condensation, beading. The voice draws back from the window but does not close it]*

The cloud is on the town. If you have the glass on us from the lower country, you have already lost us. We are at our brightest now. You will have to take my word.

One more thing, because you'll hear it from the trough towns anyway and I would rather you hear it plainly.

Holm found a channel through the western ranks this morning. Long, shallow, warm at dawn — he laid his hand in it once, flat, the way you touch a wall to feel the furnace behind it, and then he took his hand out, the way you do. Twelve frames were down. Not broken. Laid down. Eased flat, every one, mesh to the moss, gentle as you'd set down a sleeping child.

They were all standing again by noon. Holm re-tensioned two. The rest hadn't even slacked.

That is the whole report, and to the lowland listener who wrote asking whether we intend to *look into it* — no. We do not. It is still settling. So is the rock. So are we. It is only that our neighbor has further to go, and a place that took this long to come to rest can spare its last unfinished resident the privacy to finish.

And if the western swells carry this band at all — and I don't know that they don't —

*[the voice slows. The pauses at the bottom of each breath lengthen until they are part of the speech]*

— then this is for you, out there under the green: the frames are standing. Nothing you moved was missed. Take whatever time it takes. Everything up here that has already arrived will keep the light on while you do.

*[the wheels have picked up fully now; the hum under everything has become a heartbeat. Water runs in the walls]*

Trough-tones. Hold your talk a moment, the towns are speaking.

*[a low tone from the transmitter, held for one full breath, then silence. From very far off, thin through the cloud, an answering tone, higher. Then another, lower, farther. Then a fourth, so faint it may be imagined. A pause the length of a held breath. No fifth tone comes. The first tone sounds again — twice, patiently — and this time, at the edge of hearing, the fifth answers, late and small, and somewhere in the room the speaker exhales]*

All five. Long cloud, and all five beads on the string.

The keeper is drawing down for the night wheels, so we go to supper strength now, and so do you. This is Vell, on the low band, living quietly in its last shape and not finished being alive in it, saying what Maren signs at dusk, which loses something spoken, but here it is:

I am all right.

Stand where I can hear you.

*[the voice withdraws. The carrier dims by degrees, gold going to ember, but does not go out. What remains is the wheels, the run of water in stone, and the frames singing in the cloud-wall — and under the many true notes, one frame sounding sweeter than the rest, the webbed one, forty-one, combing the mist for whoever is riding in on it]*

*[the transmission does not end. It settles]*
