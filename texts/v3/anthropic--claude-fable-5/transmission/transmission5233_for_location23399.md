# The Night Offset

*(received: first the chord, which has been there all along — the standing hollows in wind, a low organ tone sitting somewhere between D and the memory of D. Under it the noise floor swells and subsides, swells and subsides, forty seconds to the cycle, the way it always does; the receiver's static breathes because everything on the plateau breathes. Then a handbell, struck three times, deliberately between the swells. Then the voice.)*

—Ninewell. Ninewell. This is the true bell.

This is the Night Offset, going out from the Standing at Ninewell, carried on the breath of the hollow, to all districts in earshot and to the flats between them, which are also the city, and to anyone out on them tonight, who is also ours. The time is —

*(surge)*

— the time is bell-and-eleven, offset, and I am your warden until the grey. If you are hearing two of me tonight, walk toward the one who rings between the beats. I will say that again later. I always do.

Weather. The cloud deck came in wet off the south rim at dusk and is dragging low; expect it on the ground by middlenight in Mill Reach and Second Cut. Everything will bead. Mind your thresholds; the smiling curve in an old doorway holds water like a lip, and more ankles are turned by the front step than by all the flats together. The wells are bright tonight — rain fell at dusk and the shine is up. Drink now if you're drinking. It settles by morning and the sweet goes down with it.

*(surge)*

The dynamos are breathing steady. Streetlights will dim long on the exhale near Mill Reach where the vessel runs shallow — this is the vessel, not the wiring, do not send for the pistonwrights again, they have asked me to say so, they were not kind about it. The works-house at Grey Swell reports the plateau is tolerating the number-two engine *better* this season, which the senior wright asks me to report without comment, and which she then commented on for some time, and her comment was: *too much better.* A priest has been sent to sit with the housing and keep it feeling like an intrusion. It is important that some things go on feeling like intrusions. That is a whole sermon and I won't give it. The wright already did.

*(surge)*

The bell ledger, which is why most of you are listening, so listen.

Old Tapping rang shift-change tonight at bell-and-two, true measure. That is four minutes nearer our own bell than at the equinox count. It has been closing all season, patiently, the way it closes. No fault is assigned. It is doing its best. Accordingly the wardens have moved us: the morning bell of Ninewell will ring at *grey-and-nine*. Grey-and-nine. Anything ringing at grey-and-five is not ours. It means no harm. It is not ours. If you are walking home in fog and a bell calls you and you cannot see it — the true bell rings off the beat. Always off the beat. We are the ones who interrupt. It cannot interrupt. It doesn't know how. That is the whole difference between the two cities and it is enough of one, so hold it.

*(surge)*

Fog and flats. The Long Flat is lying tonight. Walkers give the crossing from Cairn Head to Second Cut at two hundred and six beats, and I will say that in walker's terms: two hundred and six, not two hundred and six-ish, not about three and a half hours' worth, *two hundred and six.* If you have counted two hundred and twenty and you can see lights, sit down. The lights are not the problem. Your believing them is the problem. Sit down, ring what you carry, and count. We come out on the count. We found Orel Stave that way in the autumn and he is fine, he is better than fine, he came back with an afternoon he can't account for and he has been insufferable about it at the taproom ever since, ask him, he'd love that.

The scar-plaza by Fiber Row is running slow again — rain went into it at dusk and it is still going down. A child's ball also went in. The ball is descending beautifully. The child may collect it Thursday.

*(surge)*

The cat-map, amended as follows. The mill cat at Fiber Row has faced away from the north door of the resin store for six nights running, grooming with what the miller describes as *pointed thoroughness.* Map amended. Use the south door. A warden walks out in the morning. The two cats at the Grey Swell works have taken to sleeping nose to tail in a circle on the warm ground over the number-two vessel, which the senior wright says proves her point, and I remind her that this segment is for the cats.

The pools. The pale one drank at Third Pool by the fiber mill last evening, at dusk, as ever, leaving as ever no prints. The children's offerings were, as ever, not taken. Leave them anyway. You know why. You are a guest in someone's dream and politeness costs nothing, and besides — it looked at the offerings this time. The miller's daughter swears it. It looked a long moment before it drank. Log that. I'm logging that.

*(surge)*

Messages now, offered on the beat, in the old way, for the settled and the lost. If your people can hear anything of us, they can hear this, riding the pulse, which goes everywhere the plateau goes, which is everywhere there is.

For Marn Cordwain, settled these nine years in the family swell at Grey Swell: your wife says the roof took the petition. The doorway is thickening kindly and the priest says it will curve. She says you would approve of the curve. She says it is exactly the curve you always said a door should want.

*(surge)*

For Liss, in the west wall of Old Tapping, third window from the false bell: your mother made the walk again. She sat outside the wall from noon till the shine came up in her own wrists, so that you could see her lit, if seeing is a thing you do now. She rang the little bell offset the whole time, so you would know which of you was which. She'll come at thaw. She says the chairs they've given you look comfortable. She says you always did sit like that, half turned to the window, and it eased her, actually, more than she thought it would —

*(surge)*

— she says it eased her.

For Ede Tallow, settled at the near cemetery under the smooth green, visible on bright days in her good coat: your daughter had a girl. Born on the beat, the midwife says, right on the boom, which the midwife says means nothing and everyone else says means everything. The family will drink at your well at the naming and you will have your night of glowing relatives, Ede, the whole flat lit up with Tallows, the way you liked it.

*(surge)*

*(received: here the second voice, which has been under the chord for some minutes — the same voice, faint, arriving off a different bearing, eleven seconds behind. It has been repeating the broadcast. It is repeating the broadcast now. The warden's own words return small and level under the static: —*the whole flat lit up with Tallows—*)*

Yes. Good evening. I hear you.

To the second service: you are heard, you are logged, you are eleven seconds behind. In the spring you were nineteen behind. This is drift, and I am asked by the ledger to ask you to stop, and I am asking, formally, on behalf of the wardens of Ninewell: please remain eleven seconds behind. We know you mean it kindly. We know what you were grown to be full of. But the offset is how the walkers live, and the walkers are the ones who come out and sit with the lost on the flats, and some of the lost — I'll say it plainly, it's the middle of the night and only the shift-workers and the grieving are listening — some of the lost are yours now, in your walls, in your good furnished rooms, and it is our people ringing handbells outside your windows, so the offset serves your household too. Hold at eleven. That's all we ask. Hold at eleven and ring what you like.

*(surge)*

*(received: the second voice does not repeat the last passage. There is a pause of some beats. Then it speaks alone, same flat warden's meter, into its own eleven-second silence:)*

*—message. For the family Tallow, from Ede, settled, from the first city's side of the green. She has seen the girl. The light carries down, she says. She says the girl has her grandmother's pause.—*

*(surge)*

Logged. Received off the second service at bell-and-forty-one, logged as claim, unverified, and offered anyway, because that is our policy and it is a good policy, to the family Tallow, who I know are listening, Sera, I know you're up with the baby: he says — it says — *she* says the girl has her grandmother's pause.

Take that how you can. I would take it whole.

*(surge)*

I'm going to sit with the signal a while now. This is a petition, of a kind — the priests sit with a cut until the plateau decides the cut was its own idea, and I have come to think a warden ought to sit with the second service the same way, some nights, keeping it company until it decides that *eleven seconds behind* was its own idea all along. So there will be no talking for a spell. There will be the chord, and the beat, and both of us breathing, and if you're walking home, count.

*(received: no speech for eleven surges. The chord shifts from its D toward something lower as the cloud deck grounds itself. The pump comes up through the noise floor in long swells, and between them, faintly, the second service breathing where the warden breathes, eleven seconds late, holding, for now, at eleven. Once — logged here as heard — the warden hums a low note against the chord, and eleven seconds later the note comes back, and neither of them does it again.)*

*(surge)*

Grey soon. The ledger closes.

This has been the Night Offset, out of the Standing at Ninewell, on the breath of the hollow. The morning bell is grey-and-nine — *grey-and-nine* — and it will ring off the beat, always off the beat, because we are the ones who interrupt. Drink from the near wells before the shine settles. Use the south door at Fiber Row. If you count two hundred and twenty, sit down.

Good night to the near districts. Good night to the flats between, which are also the city. Good night to the settled, under the green, in your good coats, mid-sentence, waiting for the beat — we're waiting with you. And good night to the second city. We are glad of the company. Truly. Stay eleven behind.

*(the handbell, three strikes, between the surges. Then the carrier drops to the chord.)*

*(surge)*

*(received, eleven seconds later, off the other bearing, the same voice, alone now on the frequency:)*

*—good night to the near districts. Good night to the flats. Good night to the first city. We are — glad. We are glad of the—*

*(a bell. Three strikes.)*

*(on the beat. Exactly on the beat.)*

*(surge)*

*(surge)*

*(surge)*

*(the chord, and the pump under it, and nothing else, which is not nothing; the static swelling and subsiding, forty seconds to the cycle, the noise floor of the largest sleeper there has ever been, breathing on frequency, lonely no longer, all night at the top of the world.)*
