Under everything, a fine crackle, like welding heard across a valley: the veins are pulsing. Listen at the gaps. The crackle runs bright—bright—bright, then reaches a fault in the mineral and dims, and then something soft and living lays itself across the gap and the crackle passes *through* flesh, and for that moment the signal goes warm. Warm, then bright again on the far side. Forty warm places in the walls. The transmission is stitched together with bodies. You can hear each one as a faint rounding-off of the static, a burr filed smooth. That is a salamander. That is what a salamander sounds like from inside the current: a place where the signal briefly forgives itself.

And beneath the crackle, beneath even the thud, a fuzz. A tremble on the carrier, constant, fine, like fur. That is the moths. Do not mistake it for noise. It is specified. It is dither in the works, the shiver of ten thousand grey wings kept eternally at the threshold of doing something, on columns of stone that rise and sink at the speed of breathing. When a column runs heavy the fuzz thins on one channel and thickens on another — a moth has crossed the wall — and the great thud below never learns that anything was ever wrong.

A note on the order of things, because the order of things here is not in order. Sounds arrive before their causes. The drop from the willow leaf lands — *drip* — and then falls. The **KANG** rings and then the water strikes. And logged here without explanation: three cycles before this receiver was tuned to this frequency, the signal already carried a faint tuning-hum, our tuning-hum, our own listening, arriving ahead of itself. The cirque had already routed around us. We were compensated for before we existed. The airspace bent, the count adjusted, and nothing below the floor was told, because nothing below the floor has ever been told anything.

Hear the carrier plainly now, because this is the deepest thing the transmission contains: the thud. The flywheel under the floor. It is perfectly regular and perfectly unmodulated. In the entire span of its turning it has never once carried information, because information is difference and no difference has ever been permitted to reach it. It is the loudest thing in the bowl and the most ignorant. It has never received a single input. It has never been informed that anything exists. Every eleven seconds it manufactures the next eleven seconds and delivers them to spec, and if you slow the thudding down far enough, past hearing, into pattern, it resolves — this is not interpretation, this is demodulation — into the only utterance the place has:

Late in the reception, one anomaly in the export log. The cirque exports nothing — no heat, no matter, no signal it intends — the loops are closed, the water is coolant, the ledger has no outward line. But once, between blocks, in the clean silence where the floor is kept, the transmission carried a sound it has never carried before and will never carry again: something small and dark and dry, detaching. A covert feather leaving a wing. It did not cycle back. It landed on the kept stone and the stone did not absorb it and the count did not claim it, and for the duration of one block the bowl contained a piece of debris, which is to say a piece of history, which is to say a wound.

It appears in this log as a single mark that does not repeat:

          ,

That is all. One shipped unit, in all its sealed eternity. Whoever holds this transmission holds the manifest.
