THE NIGHT ERRATA

*[— joined in progress. Under everything, a hum too low to name: flywheels behind moss. Over everything, drip. The carrier itself seems to bead, the way iron does here by mid-morning. A voice, just above a whisper, has been going for some time —]*

"...and where the ground keeps two weeks old, along the whole southern hem, expect softness at the margins until dawn. Walk poured, not fallen. If you must cross the newest of it, cross in company, and let the heavier friend go second."

*[drip. drip. a pause the length of a breath in wool]*

"That stands as the age report, which was correct at the reading of it and does not stand now."

*[paper, turned so softly it registers only as a change in the room]*

"Errata to the Tuesday map, read gently, in order of interest rather than importance.

"Item. The pond marked at the low end of Ninth Attempt has moved uphill. We are aware this is not how ponds conduct themselves elsewhere. Visitors, if any are listening — and the printers' guild flatters itself that the signal carries past the cloud — are asked to be gracious about it.

"Item. Third Ridge is trying again. The crews on that quarter already know; this notice keeps mostly for the record, and the record keeps mostly for the pleasure of saying so: *third ridge, fourth attempt.* Persistence like that deserves an audience.

"Item. The alley of horsetails behind the extrusion mill has closed at the north throat. A new throat has worn open two doors east. The children found it first, as arranged.

"Item. The map itself. The Tuesday map was wrong by Tuesday. We had hoped, this week, for Wednesday. The guild extends to the ground its compliments, and its usual request, and expects the usual answer."

*[the hum shifts a quarter-tone. somewhere in the signal, water finds a new channel and settles into it]*

"Jacking notices for tonight, read for the crews and for those who like to know whose hands hold their houses.

"The crew on Cooling Row goes out at full dusk and works north. The crew on the printers' quarter works the long block by the kilns and asks residents to leave the sills clear — of everything, they say, except what is customarily left on sills, which they say they know nothing about, and which was very good last night, especially the tea.

"One further notice, addressed to no one. There is a nursery on Cooling Row, second floor, cold wall to the east. The floor will be shaded a half-degree tonight. A crib will drift from that wall by morning, the width of two fingers. Nothing occurred. No one need be thanked. This notice was not read."

*[drip. Far off in the received sound: a soft rushing, brief — swallows adjusting in their ten thousand sleep]*

"Settings.

"Odal, of the channel-works, set on Thursday, in her ninety-first arrangement of years. The bearers went felt-shod at the evening breath. She keeps now in the fourth dimple east of the warm outfall, for those who wish to visit while the visiting lasts. Her hand was pressed. The ground received it the way it receives everything, which those who knew her will agree was also her own way.

"The eels of her channel were told on Friday. They took it as they take everything. She would have said they were excellent at it."

*[a longer pause. The carrier holds it without strain, as if the silence were also part of the program, which it is]*

"Readers' marks, for the day now curing.

"The terrace at the foot of Ninth Attempt: *accepted.* It will be built on promptly and well, as is owed. Acceptance, the Readers remind us, is a duty. If the ground offers and the offer stands empty — the sentence ends there, as it always does.

"The paired ridges west of the fern-yards: *declined,* with thanks. The moss may have them.

"And held over, one proposal. The vault beneath this quarter — beneath, in fact, this station — has risen another finger-width in the night. It is, we are obliged to report, exactly the width of an antenna footing. It has been exactly the width of an antenna footing for eleven weeks. The mark upon it reads *not yet,* in wax pencil, renewed each dawn. The pencil is wearing down faster than the argument."

*[the voice comes nearer to the microphone here, or the microphone nearer to the voice; the whisper acquires grain]*

"We will say one thing more about this, in the conditional, as is proper.

"There were prints at the station door this morning. Three toes. A neat, unhurried stride. They came up out of the ground two weeks old and crossed the threshold-stone and went on toward the rising, the way they always go — away from the built, toward the becoming. We stepped over them on the way in. You will understand that this was not indifference. It was the deepest politeness we have, and we hope, wherever the walker keeps, that it was received as sent."

*[drip. drip. And then, entering the signal from beneath rather than from any direction: a single tone. The tower organ, opening. It swells over the better part of a minute — a chord like the plain exhaling, or inhaling; the receiver cannot tell which and neither, residents say, can they. Moths lift from the warm pipes in the received hush behind it, fanning. The chord fades the way water finds level. This was the breath. The voice waits until the last of it has set]*

"Shift-change stands complete. Go softly to your works, or softly home from them.

"And now the custom, for those still listening, and for the one listening under all of us.

"We will hold Still."

*[what follows is transmitted silence, which is not the same as dead air. The drips continue — one, and one, and one, forty kilometers of them gathered into a single open channel. The flywheel hum. A dog, or an otter, far off, once. The silence goes on long past comfort and into something else, a held breath shared between the station and whoever the station stands on. Two minutes. Three. Then —*

*— a note. Deep, woody, hull-slow, rising through the carrier from below the range of hearing into the low edge of it, felt by the receiver as a flexing of the signal itself, the whole broadcast leaning a few millimeters and being gently leveled again. The ground, adjusting. The ground, moving first.*

*In the studio, an exhale that is nine-tenths of a laugh]*

"You always lose. That is the point.

"This has been the night errata, which stood true at reading. The station keeps near ninety-one and some, for now; the printers make no promises about the frequency, the frequency being, after all, on the same ground as everything else.

"Reading for the guild, this was Sef — who at nine was called Little Sef of the North Throat, and who is called that still by two elders and one eel, and does not mind.

"Set your levers down softly. Leave the sills as customary. The crews are already out; you can hear them not making any sound at all.

"Current arrangement ends."

*[the carrier does not close. It stays open on the drip, and the hum, and the slow weight of the sleeping thing beneath the antenna, whose held-over proposal rises another unmeasurable fraction in the dark —*

*not yet, says the wax pencil somewhere —*

*and the drips go on, one, and one, and one, keeping the city's account better than grief does, better than maps do, all the long cold green night, for now]*
