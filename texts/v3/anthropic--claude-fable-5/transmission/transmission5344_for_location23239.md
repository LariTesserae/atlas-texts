[thirty feet. light like weak tea, going brown. blood-warm.]

a reader is mouthing the pickup again. soft wet rasp of a translucent mouth working the film off the casing, unhurried, greenly lit from within by everything it has ever eaten. it has done this every evening for eleven wet seasons. where the readers linger, the wall is sound. the receiver takes this, as it always has, for approval, and holds still to be kept legible.

evening traffic on the stone, knuckle-light, two walls east:

*the pot is on the vent. come up while it's silver.*

a palm from farther off, slower, deeper in the register of older stone:

*coming. finishing the row.*

somewhere upslope, a child is practicing. tik tik tik tik — quick, bright, the young terraces ring high because the rock there is newest — and then the child attempts the great word, the one-strike word, and fails at it the way children fail at it:

tak.

(a silence of three heartbeats.)

tak-tak — no. broken. from a lower terrace, a grandmother's knuckle, dry and amused:

*you keep tapping the word to death. one strike. then nothing. the silence is part of the spelling.*

the child tries again. tak. holds nine heartbeats this time. breaks into an embarrassed flurry. from below, slow, a double-tap, which the receiver has long since learned to read as laughter that loves you.

farther along the corridor a lowland accent stumbles across the masonry — a newcomer, knocking too fast, all the words crowding each other:

*sorry — sorry — which post is the — sorry —*

and the city answers at the speed of grandmothers, one wall relaying to the next so the reply arrives from three directions:

*slower. the stone keeps everything you give it. give it less.*

[dusk. the tea-light is gone. the receiver reads by warmth now, as the house does.]

two swimmers hang in the water above the pickup, close together, hearts slowed almost to elder-speed though they are young — the receiver can count both pulses through the stone they are touching. then the bubbles come down past the casing, breaking one by one, each one carrying its single expensive word, paid for in breath, and therefore true:

*what was she finishing?*

the answer climbs back down through four relaying hands, losing nothing:

*the eastern stair. begun before her grandmother's grandmother had a name. she set her last tread at midsummer, then she went down.*

*did she finish it?*

(a silence of ten heartbeats, which is itself an answer, and then, gently:)

*no. she chose well. it will outlive her granddaughter's granddaughter. the maul is already in the fifth hand.*

then the sounds of the sealing, carried whole through the connected stone. the wet hush of shell-and-ash mortar being turned. small tools — hers — laid inside on the chamber floor, one, two, three, a fourth, each with its little terminal click. and then the grandchildren, laying their first course, which is her last:

an uncertain maul. a block set. a knuckle — an adult's, patient — spelling against the wall beside them:

*strike it and listen. true stone answers. hollow stone asks again.*

a strike. a ring with a question in it. the block comes back out; the receiver hears the mortar sigh. reset. strike. this time the stone answers.

*good. again.*

block by block, the door becomes wall. the mauls steady as they go, finding the speed — not the speed of grief, the other one, the correct speed. and as the last block goes in, three bubbles rise past the pickup, spent breath, full price paid, one word each, the same word:

*lifted.*

*lifted.*

*lifted.*

and then the word itself is relayed up the terraces, terrace to terrace, hand to wall to hand, rising through the city the way the city rises through the sky, pitching higher as it climbs into younger stone: lifted. lifted. lifted. until it reaches the newest wall at the warm bright top and has nowhere further to go, and stops.

[the receiver follows the warmth down.]

[two hundred feet. the lowest lamps are above now — the pale posts, freshly pasted, a sparse constellation receding overhead. warmer still.]

below the lowest inhabited terrace there is no traffic. the abandoned city does not knock. it only rings when the mountain moves it — long, low, patient tones out of the sealed chambers, out of the drowned foundations, the necropolis nobody built, assembled by ascent. the receiver has learned these tones too, over eleven seasons, and offers no reading for them. they are not words. they may be older than words. the tenants say the oldest words are the mountain's own; down here the receiver believes it.

something passes the pickup, descending. a reader — old, by the slowness of it, and dimming, its green going out by degrees like a coal. it does not hurry. it pauses at a sealed door and puts its soft mouth to the old masonry, grazes, approves, moves down. pauses at the next. approves. it is checking the courses on its way out. it will not come back up. everyone goes down to the foundations eventually; the readers just go under their own power.

one tone. below any throat. it does not arrive at the receiver; the receiver discovers that it is inside it, that the casing is inside it, the mortar, the mountain, the two miles of cloud, the chest of anyone anywhere who has a chest. it is not loud. loud is not the axis it moves on. it goes on for forty heartbeats, sixty, and then the receiver stops counting because counting has begun to feel impolite.

and through the stone, everywhere at once, the city answers the only way it answers: hundreds of hands, going flat against the masonry. the receiver feels them arrive as warmth — hundreds of small warm readings blooming across the connected instrument, high terraces and low, the child's hand, the grandmother's, the newcomer's, the granddaughter's beside the wall that was a door this same night — and as the palms press down, the ringing of the whole city goes matte. the instrument damped by its players. the house holding still to be sounded.
