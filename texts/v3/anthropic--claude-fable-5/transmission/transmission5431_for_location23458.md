**THE NIGHT LEVEL**

*(as received)*

—and the channelers at Second Try confirm it, so I will say it again for anyone who came in late: the Long Seep took its corner. Took it on its own, sometime in the night between the fourth and the fifth, no hand on it, no cut. Dami walked the length of it yesterday and says you cannot tell where his mother's work ends and the water's begins, and he sat down on the rock and had a moment, and I think we can allow him that. Forty years his family has been arguing that corner's case. The water has ruled in their favor.

That concludes the water report, except to say that the pools are skinning early this year, and the geese at Marek's are walking on it and complaining, and the ice is a finger thick at the margins and thin as a word in the middle, so, children, no.

You're listening to the Night Level out of the Anchorage, at two thousand and forty-one meters and rising. The rise is even. The sky is clear all across the shelf tonight and the cold is the deep dry kind, so bank your channels and bring the cultures in off the porch, Ostra, I am talking to you.

[carrier steady — a goose, far off, once]

Messages now. Slowly, as always, so they can be written down.

To Ilse at Third Rise, from the loam-sheds at the Anchor: *the culture took. It is lively. Come and smell it.* End of message.

To the Vane family at the western scarp: *your father's tools have been found where he left them, at the head of his last cut, laid out in order. Nobody has touched them. Come when the weather allows and not before.* End of message.

To all towns, from the surveyors' hall: *the interim levels requested at Whitepool and at Second Try have been taken. Both true. Both even. You may stop asking.* End of message. And the surveyors have added, in their own hand, *the bread is proving,* which is not standard language, and which I am reading anyway.

To the girl at Root Six holding, from her cousin at the Anchor: *yes.* End of message. I don't know either. Good luck to you both.

[static — brief — carrier resumes]

—births. One birth. To the family Sorel at Whitepool, a daughter, sound and loud, born on the third at two thousand and forty-one meters and twelve centimeters. Twelve centimeters, Whitepool. She is already higher than any of us were. Write it on the lintel and put your hand flat on it, the way we do.

And one giving. Old Sef Andrede, channeler, of Second Try, at ninety-one years and thirty-eight meters, has been given to the beds. His family asks me to say that he chose his pocket years ago, the willow stand east of his corner — the corner, yes, that corner, the one the water took this week, four days after him, and if anyone would like to believe those two things are connected, his family says, they will not stop you. He goes out with the spring carrying. He will be soil by the time the geese come back. *Everything growing here is somebody.* Tonight it's Sef.

[pause — small water audible under the carrier, seep and tick]

The warden logs now. In order, as always.

Root One: no change.
Root Two: no change.
Root Three: no change; the warden notes three hundred and forty geese in the dooryard and rising, and requests patience from anyone visiting, and stepping-room from the geese.
Root Four: no change.
Root Five: no change.
Root Six: no change; the warden adds, *warm rock, clear night, all quiet, all well,* which is also not standard language, and which I am also reading anyway, because it is a good sentence and it is true.
Root Seven: no change.
Root Eight: no change.

Root Nine.

[pause]

I am going to read this one exactly as it was sent, because that is what the baseline is for, and because the warden at Nine has kept her log for forty-one years so that on this night she could send eleven words and be believed.

Root Nine reports: *change. Base temperature up three-tenths. Steady since the fourth. Logging hourly.*

There is a second line. *The warden's daughter, aged nine, pressed her ear to the root, as they all do at nine, and reports a sound. The warden declines to characterize the sound. The warden has pressed her own ear to the root. The warden also declines. The warden requests that everyone remain calm and keep the room warm.*

That is the whole of it. I will tell you what I know, which is what you know. Forty-one years of no change means that three-tenths of a degree is not nothing, and it also means we will know exactly what it is when it becomes anything, because she wrote it all down, every empty day of it, the most boring literature on earth, and tonight it is the most valuable document on the continent, just as they always said it would be. The recipe has instructions for this. They are being followed. The surveyors go out at first light — an extraordinary leveling, the first ever called between summers, and I expect the figure will be even, and I expect I will read it to you tomorrow night in this chair, and I expect the exhale in the squares will be the same exhale as every year, only earlier.

And to the warden family at Root Nine, from all of us, from every town whose lights you can almost see: *the whole Shelf is keeping the room warm with you.* End of message.

[static — the carrier warbles — a long dropout — resumes lower, softer]

—don't need to know what the yeast is thinking. We never have. We were given a recipe and a country the age of our grandmothers and we were asked to be patient on a scale that has nothing to do with our own lives, and we said yes, mostly without discussing it, and look. The corner took. The culture is lively. The child at Whitepool is twelve centimeters ahead of all of us. And somewhere under Root Nine, something that was seeded before any lintel on this shelf was carved is doing whatever it has always been doing, only three-tenths of a degree more so, and a nine-year-old put her ear against it in the cold and heard — something. As they all do. As you did.

I put my ear to Root One when I was nine. I have never said on this frequency what I heard, and I am not going to start at two thousand and forty-one meters, but I will say this: I was not afraid, and I have not been afraid since, and I do this program because of it.

[pause]

That's the level for tonight. Remaining notices tomorrow. Weather: clear, hard cold, the cloud-tide expected up the western scarp by midmorning as always, so if this signal goes soft on you around then, it is only the land wearing the sky. It is not the world ending. It is the world arriving. It does that here daily.

And now, as always, before I go: the water.

[the microphone is moved — a scrape — set down near the window slot — the room noise ends]
