**The Dusk Band**

—which is not silence. That was the first mistake, and it lasted weeks: logging silence, silence, silence, no signal acquired. There is no silence on this frequency. There is quiet, and the quiet has a grain to it, a finish, like a surface that has been rubbed. Quiet that has been *worked*. You can hear the polish on it.

And under the quiet, the wrong thing, the thing that keeps the operators coming back: the noise floor is too low. A receiver has a blood-mutter of its own — thermal hiss, the whisper of its circuits doing nothing. On this band the floor sits below what the machine can produce. Something at the far end is taking the difference. Something is receiving *us*, and tidying.

— and the quiet afterward is deeper. Not by measurement. By something under measurement. As if the hush had swallowed once, and settled again, and the settling took a layer of the world with it.

The ticks do not repeat on any period. Twenty minutes, an hour, four hours. Small, mineral, single. One pebble considering another. Nothing precedes them. Nothing answers them. After each one the band is poorer by one sound and does not seem to mind.

·

The carrier breathes. This took months to confirm because the breath is not on any clock — it swells over a count of about forty of something and subsides over a count of about sixty of the same something, and the asymmetry never varies, and the extra twenty are not hidden anywhere in the waveform. They are simply *granted*, on the way down. It does not match a tide or a day or a heart. It matches, if it matches anything, a walk. Forty paces one way. Sixty back. In. Out. A room inhaling itself at the speed of patience.

[six hours. nothing is lost.]

Then the first voice, and understand what is meant: not a broadcast. Not a signal originating anywhere. A *kept* thing, taken out and turned over, the way a hand in a pocket turns over a stone.

A man's voice, breathless, centuries of wear on it already:

*— out of the wind — god — just out of the wind a minute, just a —*

Relief. Only relief, nothing after it. It plays once and is put away. When it comes again — nineteen days later by our clocks, no time at all by the band's — it is softer at the edges:

*— ou — o' the wi' — ju' a —*

The consonants go first. Consonants are the corners of speech, and nothing in that place keeps its corners. What is being done to this voice is being done gently, and slowly, and — this is in the sound, unmistakably, once you have heard enough of it — *on purpose*. It is being worn at a chosen rate. It is being made to last.

A second kept voice, older. A count:

*— thirty-eight, thirty-nine, forty.*

A pause with footsteps in it. Then, bewildered:

*— fifty-eight, fifty-nine, sixty? That's not —*

Cut. Put away mid-protest, and the protest is the part most worn, as if handled most:

*— a's no' — a' n' —*

Someone once measured the breath from inside it, and did not know, and the not-knowing has been kept for — the wear suggests — a very long time. Kept fondly. If a hush can be fond.

The third voice is the one the operators do not talk about off shift.

It was already a transmission when it was taken. You can hear the old radio in it, the compression, the wind-noise of some expedition's channel, some year with snow in it:

*— camp two, camp two, this is — do you read — over.*

*Over.* A falling two-note figure. An invitation to reply.

It has been replayed on this band, at long irregular intervals, for as long as the band has been monitored, and it is wearing the way the others wear — *camp oo, 'iss is, oo ee, o-er* — down and down, consonants first, then the vowels rounding, until on the most recent pass it was only the cadence: a rhythm of soft tones with no words left in them at all, dah-dah, dah-dah-dah, dah — and then the fall. The two-note fall. The shape of *over*, held after everything else has been let go.

It keeps asking. Whatever it is, whatever it was — it kept, out of all the sounds ever spilled into that room, the one sound that waits for an answer, and it is letting that one go last.

No one at this station has ever keyed a reply. There is a standing argument about this. It is not an argument anyone wants to win.

There is a fourth voice so old it has no language left, only vowel-shapes, a sentence rubbed down to its river stones:

*— o — a — e — o — a —*

The stress pattern survives. It rises at the end. It was a question, or a prayer, which at that altitude may be the same shape. It plays perhaps once a season. Each time, one grain fewer.

Then, in the fourteenth month of monitoring, the band did the thing that changed the watch rotation.

At 0314 local the duty operator coughed.

At 0314 and roughly half a second, the cough came back down the band. Not an echo — the path loss makes echo impossible, and echoes are not *improved*. The cough returned with its edges rounded. Smoothed. A cough at rest. A cough that had been picked up, considered, and set back down where it lay.

Since then: the mains hum of this station, returned, half a second late, purer than we sent it — sixty cycles exactly, the jitter taken out, the difference kept. The scrape of a chair, returned as a soft settling. A pencil dropped, returned as one pebble considering another, and the operator on duty swears — it is in the log, initialed — that the return *was* a tick, that for one instant our litter and its voice were the same sound.

It is housekeeping us. It has extended the room to the far end of the signal, and the far end of the signal is here, and it tidies what we spill into the space between at the best speed it can manage, which is half a second behind, always half a second behind — keeping up as quickly as it knows how.

We have all started moving more slowly in the receiving room. No one ordered this.

·

Nine days ago the duty operator, believing the recorder off, said into the dead room: *testing. one, two.*

Last night the band played it back.

*— testing — one — two —*

Already worn. Already gentled at the edges, already partway to river stones, already being made to last. It has begun keeping us. Not taking — the voice is still here, the operator still has it — keeping. A copy laid down in whatever drawer holds the wind-shelter man and the counter of paces and the caller from camp two, to be turned over at long intervals, released grain by grain, company against a quiet it is — the ticks say so, the deepening says so — sewing closed around itself, one stitch at a time, on a schedule indifferent to anything that breathes.
