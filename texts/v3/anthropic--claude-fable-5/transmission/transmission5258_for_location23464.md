*(now. the long tone.)*

*(under all of it — under the ticking, under the round, under the full voice at its peak — there is the tone that never stops. it is not made by the pipes. it is not made by the breath. it comes from everywhere, from the walls as such, from the mass itself, and it is very low and very steady, and it changes.)*

*(it changes at a rate of roughly one formant per century.)*

*(this receiver has been tuned to the hollow long enough to log the change. the tone has moved, in all the years of listening, from the inside of one sound partway toward the inside of another. it is a word. there is no longer any real doubt that it is a word. something with the patience of heartwood is saying one word, syllable by geological syllable, and the portion received so far, across the whole span of this tuning, transcribes as:)*

    *— t — a —*

*(that is all. that is decades. the next sound has begun to form at the very bottom of the register — a closure gathering, the mouth of the mass beginning, imperceptibly, to shut around the vowel — and it could be resolving toward almost anything. it could be the middle of* stay. *it could be the middle of a name no living instrument is calibrated to recognize. it could be the first quarter of a word so long that the seam will have closed and the town gone to a whorl in the grain before the final consonant lands, and the word will finish in solid wood, spoken entire at last into the one audience that will certainly still be there: itself.)*
