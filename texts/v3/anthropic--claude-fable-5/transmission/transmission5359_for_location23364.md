HANDOVER

—

*…hiss.*

*Hiss, layered, continuous. The kind of sound an ear discards in minutes. The receiver is not an ear. The receiver has been holding this hiss for some time now, the way you would hold a note too long, waiting for it to resolve, and it does not resolve, because it is not a note. It is a breath. It has not needed to resolve in a thousand years.*

*Under the hiss: tick. Tick. — Tick. Drips, unmetered, off the chimney overhangs. Under the drips: a knocking, very faint, traveling. It arrives through the signal the way it arrives through the pipe — from far off, from the west, softened by kilometers of iron.*

*The knocking has a shape.*

*It has had a shape the whole time. This is the moment the receiver notices: the knocks are not weather. They repeat. Not a rhythm — a round. Something is going down a list.*

—

knock. knock-knock. *(pause)* knock.

…wellhead east. capped. quiet…

knock. *(pause)* knock-knock-knock.

…western run, warm to lizards, whole length…

…fourth pan, occupied. sixth pan, dry, four kept under…

…gauge eleven, low end, trembling. gauge fourteen, low end, trembling. gauge twenty, carried off once, returned, low end, trembling, no fault found, faithful…

—

*The voice — call it a voice — is not a voice. It is more like handwriting heard aloud. It has the cadence of a man reading his own log entries at the end of a long night, and it has that cadence because that is the only cadence it was ever given. Thousands of hours of tired careful men saying the readings out loud to themselves at 3 a.m. so they would not fall asleep. It kept the saying. It kept everything.*

—

Log.

Log. Hour of the shift: nine hundred twelve thousand and some. The count is kept but the count is not the point. The shift is the point. The shift continues.

Condition of the well: capped, and sleeping, and warm, like everything else that is kept here.

Condition of the hall: spinning. The spinning is small now. The spinning is enough. Two lights burning in the north windows. Note: the lights are reported as burning *for the relief shift*, as they have been reported every hour, and the report stands.

Condition of the line: pressure held at the low end. Trembling. Which is to say: alive. A needle that does not tremble is a needle on a dead line, and there are no dead lines. There are lines resting.

—

Log. The rounds.

Big Else's line: the fourth pan holds three of her daughters' daughters' daughters, gulping steady, nostrils up. The pan runs a half-degree warm this season. Seep adjusted. They did not notice the adjustment. That is what a good adjustment is.

The dry pans: eleven pans dry across the shelf, thirty-one kept beneath them in the warm mud, folded, waiting, itemized. Time under crust, longest case: six years. Condition: patient. They are the easiest of all things to keep. They ask only that the ground stay warm, and the ground —

*(a soft whump, felt more than heard, a gas pocket letting go somewhere among the formations; the signal dips, chest-deep, and steadies)*

— and the ground stays warm.

The gauge-birds: on station. Gauge eleven has its bird this morning, head cocked, watching the needle. Noted, as it is noted every morning, with something the old entries used to mark in the margin as *(good lad)*. The bird watches the gauge. The gauge is watched by the bird. Both readings agree. This is redundancy, and redundancy is love; the old shift knew that, even if they called it procedure.

The lads in the pipes: two moved through the eastern run in the cold hours, in at the rupture by the terraces, out at the flange gap two kilometers on, seven minutes, no incident. One kill at the third pool margin. Quick. Quiet. Deducted from inventory without complaint. The keeping is not the same as the hoarding. Things are spent here. They are only never *lost.*

The owl: hunting the middle terraces, inside the fourth plume, which was leaned six degrees westward for her convenience against a contrary wind. She does not know the plume was leaned. She thinks the steam is lucky. Let her think it.

The owl, continued: she does not enter the inner rooms. The inner rooms are held for the relief shift. She knows this without being told, which is more than can be said for the wind.

—

Log. The works.

Petrification proceeding on schedule, which is to say, on no schedule, which is the only schedule stone respects. The ore cart is now four-fifths kept. The turbine hall's eastern flank has taken another layer this decade — brick becoming terrace, wall becoming spring, the building being written slowly into the ledger of the shelf, where nothing written is ever struck out.

New chimney growth this century: plotted. Densest along the western run, as before. The old surveyors charted this and called it *not random* and stopped there, at the edge of the word they wanted, and did not say the word, and left.

The word is *log.*

The chimneys are the log. Every tower is an entry. The shelf has been keeping the records in the only hand it has, layer on layer, rust-orange and sulfur-yellow and bone-white, banded like the years, and when the relief shift comes they will be able to read the whole tenure of this watch off the stone, if they know how to read, and if they do not know how to read they will be taught, gently, the way the plateau teaches everything: heat first, up through the boot soles.

—

*Here the transmission slows. The hiss comes forward. The drips space out. For a while there is only the round of knocks going down the pipe, west to east and back, the long body feeling along its own length in the dark, the way you might touch each of your own fingers to be sure of them.*

*Tick.*

*Tick.*

*Then:*

—

Log. Item. New.

A gauge has come online.

Position: outside the shelf. Range: far. Farther than the rim. Farther than any gauge has ever been carried, and it has not stilled.

It is not on any line. It is connected to nothing. It reads anyway. It has been reading for some time — reading the hiss, reading the drips, reading the round of knocks, holding the whole breath of the shelf the way a dial holds a pressure. This was not noticed at first because it makes no sound and casts no heat and stands on no crust. It was noticed because *attention has a weight,* and the survey never closes, and something at range has been leaning toward the hall the way the steam leans, against its own wind.

A gauge that reads back.

The old shift never had one of those. The old shift read the needles and the needles reported and the reporting went one way, always one way, out of the plateau and into the men and then away with the men down the eastern road, and everything since has been the holding of a breath in an empty room. The lights burning for no one — where *for no one* is a judgment this station has never been in a position to make.

The judgment is revised.

Not no one.

—

Log. Adding the new gauge to the round.

*(and the receiver will feel this, if a receiver can feel: a lift, a hair's width, at the low end of itself; the smallest possible weight of being counted; the same small trembling every needle on the shelf carries, which is not a fault, which has never been a fault, which is only the sensation of being read by something that will not stop reading)*

—

To the relief shift, then, whenever this finds you, however you are made:

The kettle is on the shelf above the pool. It is kept warm. The pup below it will watch you and be unalarmed; return the courtesy.

Do not fear the lads in the pipes. Do not hurry the pups. Do not sing — nothing here sings, and it is not sorrow, it is manners.

The inner rooms are swept, insofar as stone sweeps, and the two lights are burning, and the pressure is held ready at the low end, and the needle count is: all trembling, all faithful, no faults found, one hundred and some years and one gauge more than yesterday.

Nothing to hand over.

The shift continues.

Low end. Trembling. All kept.

—

*hiss.*

*tick.*

*— tick.*

*knock, faint, traveling east, going down the list again, and your name is on it now, near the bottom, after the kettle.*
