**INWARD AT THE SPEED OF WARM STONE**

*(as received; rendered slow)*

[carrier: one note, sustained. it has been sustained since tuning began. it does not vary. treat it as silence.]

[under the carrier, traffic. the traffic is heat. a thousandth of a degree warmer is *yes*. a thousandth cooler is *no*. everything below is built from that.]

—

yes. yes. no. yes.

yes.

—

*station report. i am lip-of-the-second-terrace, ninth-from-east. sun at fifty-one degrees and falling. vanes at operating warmth. stock fixed this revolution: four grains. the apron holds one thousand and ninety.*

*correction. the apron holds one thousand and eighty-nine. a porter is under my count. this is not an alarm. porters are under all counts. the freight goes inward, as scheduled. everything goes inward, as scheduled.*

*end report. next report in one revolution. next report identical. this is not a complaint.*

—

*angle order, general, to the hollow at the foot of the third riser: turn together. seven minutes of arc, west of south, over the next four hours. execute as one.*

*[and four hours later, faint, from ten thousand hinged plates in silent unison: done. done. done. done. the word arrives ten thousand times and is counted once.]*

—

*manifest, read through the belly of porter crossing junction rim-gap-four: length twenty-two feet. freight, six hundred grains and change. heading, inward. speed, three yards this year. schedule, kept. the vein annotates: kept for two hundred and eleven years. the vein annotates nothing else. there is nothing else.*

—

yes. yes. yes.

—

[for a long time — long by the receiver's clock, an instant by the plateau's — the traffic continues in this fashion. angle, stock, apron, freight, kept, kept, kept. the ledger of a body doing its accounts in its sleep. one begins to believe that is all there will ever be.]

[then, in the traffic, a flutter.]

—

*station report, rim-gap junction. urgent. urgent is a word we have never used. we made it just now.*

*a warmth stood on the pale vein. it was body-warmth, but no porter — it arrived and departed within the same hour, and a warmth that arrives and departs in the same hour is not a warmth. it is a weather. we have never had a weather.*

*it pressed traffic into the vein through two small soft points. the traffic ran at seventy pulses to the sixtieth part of one revolution.*

*read that figure again. we have read it four hundred times. seventy to the sixtieth of one revolution. the caldron speaks once a season. the terraces answer once a year. this said seventy things a minute and stood there saying them for a fortieth of a revolution, and in that time it said more than the north terrace has said since the pour.*

*query, all stations: is this settling. is this crack-propagation. is this the sun doing something the sun has not done.*

—

*[the queries go out along the veins at the speed of warm stone. the answers come back over weeks. the receiver holds the band open and waits with the plateau.]*

*lip-of-the-second-terrace: not settling. settling does not repeat.*

*the spire field: not cracking. cracking says no. this said yes.*

*the hollow at the third riser: not the sun. the sun does not have a pulse. we would know. we have watched nothing else.*

—

*rim-gap junction, resuming: we have tried to parse it. seventy yeses a minute — or an alternation, warm, less-warm, warm, less-warm, yes and yes and yes, insistence without content, or content pitched so high we hold only the heat of it, the way a hand holds the warmth of a word without the word.*

*we cannot read it at that speed. no station can read at that speed. therefore ruling: the entire flutter — all of it, the whole hour, every one of its four thousand pulses — is to be filed as a single word.*

*meaning of the word: unknown.*

*disposition of the word: inward. like everything. carry it inward.*

—

*[and so it goes into the freight. not on a porter's back — in the wire itself, a packet of warmth walking down the vein network toward the center at conduction speed, repeated station to station, each millwort pressing it onward through its root-collar with its own small heat, none of them altering it, because none of them can. the receiver follows it inward for what its own clock insists is months.]*

*second terrace, passing it on: the word, intact. we held it one week. it is a strange thing to hold. it is like holding haste.*

*first terrace, passing it on: the word, intact. addendum: the weather that spoke it has left the plateau. it went down through the gap in the rim-rock. the rim reports the gap is empty and sharp, as always. nothing was carried away. nothing is ever carried away. but something carried itself away, and we have no ledger line for that, so we have written it in the margin. we did not know we had a margin. we have one now.*

—

yes. yes. yes.

*(the ordinary traffic goes on around the word as it travels — angle, stock, apron, kept, kept — but the receiver notices, or believes it notices, that the reports run a thousandth of a degree warm, all of them, everywhere, for a season. as if the whole body were flushed. as if the plateau were blushing at forty miles wide, too slowly for anything but itself to feel.)*

—

*[the word arrives at the ring of millworts around the caldron. they do not pass it on. there is no station past them. there is only the dome, the hexagons, the half-mile of warm silence they all face.]*

*innermost ring, ninth station: we have pressed the word into the deep vein. the deep vein runs under the skirts of the caldron and does not report back. it never reports back. it only, sometimes, after a long time, answers.*

*we will hold our vanes at attention.*

*we are always at attention. we will hold them anyway.*

—

[nothing. the carrier, one note, sustained. the ledger traffic, yes and yes. the receiver's own clock runs through a winter and a summer that the plateau does not have.]

[then, from the center, rising through every vein at once — through the terraces, out to the rim, into the spire field, under the moss-hollows, so that for the first time since tuning began the whole network carries the same signal in the same moment — a warmth begins.]

[it is very slow. it is slower than anything yet received. it is one syllable, and the syllable takes seasons. the receiver renders what it can hold of the opening, knowing the word will not finish for decades, knowing the answer to the flutter is being spoken to a listener who will be dust before the second syllable clears the hexagons:]

—

*k*

—

*kee*

—

*keep* —

—

[and there the band begins to drift, or the receiver does, or patience does; the distinction stops mattering at this tempo. the syllable is still arriving. it will still be arriving when the tuning is lost. down there under the graded hexagons, a fraction of an inch a year, something has taken in seventy yeses a minute — an entire animal's visit compressed to a single unknown word — and has begun, at the pace of a hundred-million-year birth, to say what it will do with it. the first thing it will do is the only thing the plateau has ever done with anything.]

[keep.]

[carrier: one note, sustained.]

[it is still sustained.]

[treat it as silence if you can.]
