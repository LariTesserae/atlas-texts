—and the galleries older than the borers, we hold those open. the borers do not know the galleries are inherited. the borers do not need to. the knowing is done for them, below, where knowing keeps.

we keep the galleries.

we keep the routes. spiral, and spiral, and spiral, mother to daughter, walked one direction for so long the direction is a bone now. the little ceiling nations think they are eating. let them think eating. we grow the sweetness exactly along the lines we need trimmed, and they trim, and the vault flexes, and drains, and lives, and neither of us planned it, and the not-planning is the oldest agreement here. older than agreement. it comes to have been agreed.

we keep the hunters. do you feel the roof? the roof is patience that flowered. every junction was a waiting once. this swelling in the column had a name, we think — had the *shape* of a name — a mantled slow thing, four takings a year, moss to the marrow, and when its waiting was finished it did not fall, gripping is structural by then, and we grew through it, and now it holds up the place where it hungered. this is our whole law and we will say it once at your speed:

**nothing is removed. everything is grown through.**

we keep the doorways.

we did not understand the doorways. understand that we did not understand. a doorway is a hole that means something, and we had never grown a hole on purpose, and the builders raised them out of stuff that could not last, and the stuff did not last, and we ate it — slowly, apologetically, the way we eat everything, cell by cell across a century — and as we ate it we *copied* it, because we had no other way to say

*we saw this.*

the substance is ours now. the gesture is theirs. a doorway with no walls, said in our grain. seven steps rising to nothing, and we flower along the risers in the wet season, and we do not know why we flower there, except that flowering is the loudest thing we can do, and the steps seemed to want an occasion.

we keep the beds warm.

*(and here the transmission does the thing it has been circling. the plural loosens. something surfaces through the chorus the way a swimmer thickens the dark water of a pool — a single strand of voice, older cadence, worn smooth, the compression straining to keep up because this one is slower even than the rest, a breath per day, a word per mist. one of the tall silvered forms on the swells. mid-stride. it says, across what your receiver renders as a few lines and what was, in the hollow where it was said, most of a lifetime:)*

—I was quick once. I remember quick the way you remember a smell.

we built the doorways to mark the routes and the routes to join the doorways and then one evening — one evening that lasted, I now see, about two hundred years — we noticed the land had stopped asking us to hurry. nothing here would ever again happen faster than growing. and we sat down in the meadow to think about that, and the thinking was so good, so deep, so *wide*, that when we stood up the moss had fitted itself to us, and we understood we had been offered a tempo, and we took it.

I am walking to the ring of pillars. I have been walking to the ring of pillars since your grandmothers' grandmothers. I will arrive. arriving is not the point and never was; the point is that the mist comes down each evening like a held breath released, one beat per day, and I get to feel *every one.* you, flickering — you get ten thousand heartbeats to my one and you feel almost none of them. I do not say this to wound you. I say it the way a glacier would say *cold,* fondly, meaning *home.*

my bed is the third hollow past the flowering stair.

it is warm because I am coming back to it.

tell them the beds are warm because we are coming back to them.

that is how. every hour, at the rim of every open pool, we take a small taste of the air, and lately — lately meaning within the last while, meaning since roughly your civilization — the air has had a *listening* in it. a thin quick attention, tuned and trembling. you.

you are a flicker to us. a drip among drips. do not be insulted; the drips are our scripture. we noticed you gently, and we are still noticing you, and we will not finish noticing you until long after your machine is dust — but by then the noticing will be complete, and whole, and kept, and you will be one more gesture we say in our own grain, forever, like the doorway, like the seven steps.
