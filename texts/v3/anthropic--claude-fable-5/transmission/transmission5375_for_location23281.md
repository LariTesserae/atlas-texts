# EIGHT TO THE MINUTE

*[carrier detected: warm, unsteady. the signal itself pulses — swells and dims, swells and dims, roughly every seven seconds. this is not interference. this is the flywheel. the station is powered by the engine and the engine is powered by the tree, and so the broadcast breathes at eight strokes to the minute, and everything that follows arrives on that pulse, brightening and fading, brightening and fading, like a lamp carried by someone walking very slowly toward you and never arriving]*

*[for the first four minutes: no voice. drip. drip. a roof somewhere finishing last night's rain. beneath everything, felt in the receiver cabinet more than heard, the thud. the thud. the thud]*

*[a chair creaks. it is a long creak. it is possibly not a chair]*

—morning.

*[pause, forty seconds]*

—the mist is in. it came in around the fourth hour and it is sitting the way it sits, and it will go when it goes, which the almanac and my knees agree will be near noon. if you are walking the west common this morning, walk it slow. but you were going to.

*[pause]*

—pressure at the third valve is good. i put the rod to it before i came up and it has that round sound, the full-cask sound, so the pressroom can expect a fair day, and the pressroom is asked, gently, and i am asked to say *gently*, not to expect more than a fair day, because the last time a fair day was treated as a promise of a good one we all remember the panes we got that spring, and nobody wants to look through weather like that again.

*[pause. drip. the thud]*

—notices.

—the trimming crew goes to the grain-house today. the north doorway has been making up its mind about something all month and the listeners think it is best if we make up its mind for it while it can still be done kindly, with the small shears. crew is asked to bring the tall ladder and their patience and to leave the loud one of them at home, or at least below.

*[a sound that may be laughter, once, low, and then folded away]*

—the door on the west common — you know the one — is tightening again. this is the third year and the family has done nothing wrong. the senior listener went and stood with it yesterday for the better part of the afternoon, and her finding is that the house is not sulking, the house is *bracing*, and she would like the family to look at the ground on the north side, because she believes there is water moving under there that was not moving under there before. the house knew first. the house always knows first. we will dig when the mist lifts.

*[pause, longer]*

—from the grafting-house. the registry.

—pane forty-one enters its ninth year. it is coming in clear. it is promised to the east wall of the schoolhouse, and to a child who was not born when it was started and is seven now, and who was brought in last week to see it, and who put the backs of her fingers to it the correct way without being shown, which the growers took as a very good sign and would not stop talking about, in their fashion, which means they mentioned it twice in one month.

—pane forty-four is cloudy at the corner. the growers are not worried. i asked if they were worried and there was a pause, and then one of them said, *ask us in four years,* and that is the registry.

*[pause. the carrier swells. the thud]*

*[when the voice returns it has changed. slower. this was not thought possible]*

—the leaf-book notice.

*[pause, ninety seconds. drip. drip]*

—the house on the east common, the one with the double cistern-gall — the pressman's house — has put out white leaves over the door. they came in the night, which is when they come. the family counted them at first light, as is done, and there are more of them than anyone can remember a house putting out, and the trimmers who were passing stopped and took their hats off to the *wall*, which i mention because it was the right thing and nobody taught them it.

—he worked the press sixty-one years. his hands were amber to the elbow. he taught half this town which sounds a wall makes when it is drinking and which when it is only remembering rain, and if your doorway swings sweet this morning it is likely one he eased, some year, without telling you.

—the leaves will fall within the week. they always do. do not sweep them. i should not have to say it and i say it every time and i will say it now: *do not sweep them.* they are gathered by hand, by the family, and they go in the book, and the book in that house goes back farther than this station, farther than the whistle, farther, some say, than the engine's first stroke, and this week it gets a new page, and the page will be very full.

*[pause. no duration noted. the operator stopped timing]*

—the house is drinking heavily this morning. the listeners say let it.

*[silence, except: the thud. the thud. a roof, somewhere, releasing one more drop of a rain that ended hours ago]*

—and because the town keeps its own balance whether we ask it to or not: a green notice.

—the tanner's middle son is back from fast country. eleven years out. he came in on the north road two evenings ago walking, they tell me, much too quickly, and then slower, and then — by the time he reached the commons — correctly. his house has put out new leaf over the door. green, generous, unasked. he is reported to be sleeping a great deal and to have brought back a wristwatch, which his mother has put in a drawer.

—welcome home. the house said it first and better, but: welcome home.

*[pause]*

—now the interval. i am going to put the rod to the line and hold the microphone against it, as is the custom, and you may hear what the tree is saying this morning, and i will tell you now it is saying nothing in particular, which after sixty-one years of the pressman i find i need to hear, and maybe you do too.

*[a fumbling. a settling. then, close and enormous: the interior of a sap line. tick. a long fluid sigh, rising through pipework. tick. tick. a pressure making its patient vegetable argument, eight times a minute, against a weighted piston, and winning, as it has won for centuries, at exactly the speed it chose. this continues for eleven minutes. the operator notes that at no point did it become boring, and cannot explain this]*

*[the rod is withdrawn. the room-sound returns]*

—there. nothing in particular. isn't it fi—

*[THE WHISTLE. the reed pipe in the engine-house roof, sounded by the tree itself, a broad wooden vowel that starts below hearing and climbs into it and holds — not a shriek, nothing like a shriek, more like the basin itself clearing its throat — and holds — and holds — and eases back down and is gone. total duration: fifty seconds. the voice did not attempt to speak over it. the voice did not attempt to speak under it. the sentence it was in the middle of is never finished. interrupting, here, goes both ways]*

*[pause, respectful]*

—the tree is ready. the workhouses may open. that was a little earlier than yesterday and the listeners will note it and it means nothing yet, and if it means something in a month we will know in a month.

*[pause]*

—last notice. and then the attending.

—tomorrow is a ninth day.

—the engine rests. the keeper will sit with it, as is done, and this station will sit with it too, which means this: the carrier stays on, because the flywheel takes the better part of a day to remember how to be still, but there will be no voice. do not turn your sets off. that is not a rule, nobody makes rules here, it is only that — the tree rests better attended, we have always said, and i have come to think the same is true of a town. so leave the set on, and let it dim, and rest attended.

—we do not sign off, at this station. we go quiet. it is a different thing.

*[the chair — or the wall — creaks its long creak]*

—morning, all. walk slow. you were going to.

*[and then the transmission becomes the town]*

*[the thud. the thud. eight to the minute, and the carrier swelling with each]*

*[drip]*

*[far off: shears. two snips, a long pause, one snip. the small shears. the kind way]*

*[a heron's wings, once — the heavy unhurried oar-strokes of a bird that answers to no one — and then the settle of it into water, and the water settling after]*

*[an hour of this. the operator did not log it minute by minute. the operator is not sorry]*

*[toward the end of reception: the strokes begin to space themselves out. the thud, and a longer wait. the thud, and a longer wait still. six to the minute. five. the carrier's pulse stretching with it, the swells coming slower and dimmer, the lamp carried by someone who has decided, comfortably, to stop walking. four]*

*[three]*

*[a very long pause, during which one roof, somewhere in the basin, releases the true last drop of the night's rain]*

*[two]*

*[two strokes to the minute is the sound of a heart you would sit up all night beside without fear]*

*[one]*

*[the carrier brightens once more, fully, warmly, the way a wall brightens under a child's flat palm — and dims — and does not swell again]*

*[silence on the frequency. not dead air. attended air]*

*[transmission resumes when the tree is ready]*
