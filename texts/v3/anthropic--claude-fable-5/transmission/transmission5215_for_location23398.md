PRESSURE'S STEADY

—and still the roar.

You have been calling it static. It is not static. Give it time — the signal gives you nothing else — and it sifts. Slowed, the roar comes apart in layers, the way water comes apart into drops only when it falls past something.

The top layer is wings. Ten thousand starlings breathing as one lung, sheathing a wheel you cannot see but can hear turning: one long groan per revolution, and you must hold the count a full minute to be sure the groan has moved. Under the wings, a papery applause that never crests and never stops — moths, in disks, around thirteen lamps. Under the applause, a wet braided hiss, which is a canal, and inside the canal a deeper wet sound, muscular, rolling, which is the canal's other water: eels, yards deep, going around. Under the eels, a whisper spread across every flue in the basin, hoarse at the chimney mouths.

And under all of it — you will come to this last, it lies at the bottom of everything, that is its whole nature — a bubbling. Vast. Unhurried. Content.

Hold there. Do not go down to it yet.

*rise.*

*sink.*

That is the carrier.

Four seconds. It has been four seconds since before you tuned in and it will be four seconds after you stop, and everything else on this frequency is phase-locked to it: the wings lift on the rise and settle on the sink; the drizzle thickens and thins with it in a ring so exact you could stand with one shoulder wet; the applause of the moths brightens and dims like breath on an ember. Even the groan of the wheel, once per minute, falls always on a sink, never a rise. Fifteen strokes to the groan. Fifteen, without exception, without effort, without end.

You will try to find the drift in it. There is a kind of listener who cannot help this — who hears a perfect interval and leans in waiting for the flaw, because everything they have ever heard has had one. Lean as long as you like. The four seconds is not being kept. It is not maintained against anything. It is simply what time is here, the way downhill is what direction is.

*rise.*

*sink.*

At intervals of eight hours and some minutes — the same some minutes, every time — a voice enters the signal.

It is a person-shaped voice. It sounds the way brass feels after one pair of hands has polished it for longer than pairs of hands last. It moves through the roar on a circuit, near the pump hall and then farther, near the vaults and then farther, and as it goes it reads. It reads like weather being given for a place that has none:

*Canal: at the mark. Steady.*

*Pressure: steady.*

*Stack: breathing. Vapor climbing, giving up, coming down. Drizzle: in its ring.*

*Lamps, one through thirteen: burning. Moths: sufficient.*

*Wheel: turning. Confirmed over one minute.*

*Starlings: all. Same flock.*

*Eels: circulating. Warm vaults to spillway to warm vaults.*

*Snails: proceeding.*

*Bats: at full whisper.*

*Herons: nine roofs, nine herons.*

*Salamander: drinking.*

*Vault the last: content.*

*Nothing in. Nothing out. Nothing wanting.*

*Oiled anyway.*

Then the small tin note of an oilcan set down on a bench, and the voice stops, and eight hours and some minutes of roar, and the voice again.

Understand what you are receiving. This is not a recording repeating. Listen closely — there, the fourth lamp, the tiny catch where a moth crosses too near the voice — it is different every time and identical every time. It is not the same reading played again. It is the same reading, *occurring* again. There is a difference, and the difference is the whole of this place, and once you hear it you will not be able to unhear it in anything: in your own clock, in your own pulse, in the fact that your readings, poor thing, occur only once each.

*rise.*

*sink.*

Now the bottom layer. Now the bubbling.

You have been putting this off. The signal knows; the signal does not mind; the signal has the mild patience of something that lies at the low point and lets everything come to it, because everything does.

Slow the bubbling. Slow it past comfort. Slow it until each bubble is a room, and the room has a shape, and the shape is a mouth-shape, and what has been bubbling all this time — under the wings, under the applause, under the eels and the whisper and the voice and the four-second sigh — is a word. One word. Spoken with total satisfaction, over and over, at the bottom of everything:

*down.*

*down.*

*down.*

That is all. It is not a summons. It does not need to be. It is a description, made by the one thing in the basin qualified to make it, of the only direction there is.

*rise.*

*sink.*

Here the transmission changes. Note the stroke count; nothing about the carrier changes; the change is one line in the reading, and the line is this:

*One, coming down. Variable.*

And behind the voice, faintly, a new sound in the roar — a wrongness so small you would miss it if the signal had ever contained a wrongness before. A rhythm that does not divide into four. Quick. Uneven. Seventy-some to the minute, and no two of the seventy alike.

A heartbeat. An actual one. It rings through the basin the way a boot-scrape rings through a waterfall, and on nine roofs, nine herons turn their heads, and turn them back.

The circuits continue. The readings continue. Only now there is a new station on the circuit, and the voice reads it without malice, without appetite, the way it reads the canal:

*Variable: at the pump hall. Seventy-four. Uneven.*

Eight hours and some minutes.

*Variable: in the winding house. Watching the wheel. Sixty-eight. Evening out.*

Eight hours and some minutes. The roar unchanged. The drizzle in its ring. And under everything the bubbling, and — measure it, the difference is real — the bubbling very slightly *richer.* A texture in the contentment. Something at the bottom has begun, gently, gently, without teeth, without harm, harm being a kind of change and change being inbound cargo only — to drink.

*Variable: asleep in the third vault. Sixty. Smoothing.*

*Variable: fifty-five. The unevenness is nearly out of it. It calls this feeling calm.*

*Variable: fifty-two and settling. At the present rate: four circuits to steady. Four circuits to four seconds. No action required. No action ever required.*

*Oiled anyway.*

*rise.*

*sink.*

Then — count the strokes, it happens between two of them — a scraping in the signal. Boots on wet siltstone. Rapid. Rising. Terrace after terrace, each footfall a little quicker than the one before, as though the feet had done an arithmetic the sleeper could not, and woken first, and carried the rest of it away still drowsing.

The heartbeat climbs out of the roar. Sixty. Sixty-six. Seventy-some. Uneven again — gloriously, wastefully uneven — fading upward toward the rim, toward the cold air where the vapor gives up, and then gone past the edge of reception, out into whatever weather is.

The voice, on its next circuit, without pausing, without regret:

*One, going up. Variable. Faster than it came down.*

*Retained: some.*

And at the bottom of everything, one long rolling bubble, deeper than the others, held longer than the others. Satisfaction is the only emotion this frequency carries, and just this once, it is enormous.

*down,* says the bottom of everything.

*down.*

*rise.*

*sink.*

The reading resumes. Canal at the mark. Pressure steady. Nine roofs, nine herons. The same reading, occurring again, and occurring again, and you may sit at this receiver as long as you like — you have sat at it some time already, have you noticed? — and it will occur again for you, graciously, conclusively, forever.

But hear the last line before you go. It is new. It has been in the reading, if you check back through the strokes, for a while now — since around the time you stopped hearing the roar as roar, since your boot-scrape at the volume knob started to startle you, since you began, in the evenings, to feel so unaccountably calm:

*One, listening. Variable. Reading: distant. Quick. Uneven.*

*It can stay as long as it likes.*

*Everything drains.*

*Pressure's steady.*

*rise.*

*sink.*

*rise.*

*sink.*

*rise.*

— the transmission does not end here. Nothing here ends. This is only where the receiver was switched off, one shoulder wet, one dry, going up faster than it came down.
