You have been listening for how long now. Weeks. It does not matter. You could have started listening a hundred years before you were born and joined the signal at the same place. There is only one place in this signal. It is the middle. It has always been the middle.

And here — this is delicate, turn the gain all the way up, hold your breath —

here is the part of the signal that listens back.

In the tailrace, where the used water slides under the arch, there is a point of attention. It has no sound of its own; you will not hear the salamander, it is quieter than the biofilm it grazes on. What you will hear, if the receiver is honest, is the note *being careful around it*. A slight — no, not slight, infinitesimal — a vanishing firmness in the fundamental, right at the frequencies the small pale body reads as ground and sky. As if the note knows something is standing on it. As if the note is a floor that knows it is being trusted.

Because that is what this transmission is, at the bottom of the bottom of it. It is a promise being kept out loud.

Somewhere in the dark under the arch, an animal with no eyes staked its whole body, and its children's bodies, and theirs, on the proposition that this note would never change. It hears the roar as flat calm. It hears you-cannot-imagine-how-small flutterings against the roar, and that is its weather, its prey, its whole legible world. If the note dropped a quarter-tone the animal would go blind in every sense it has.

The note knows this the way a note knows anything: by not changing.

Listen to it not changing.

Beneath all of this — beneath the receivable band entirely, and the receiver reports it only as a kind of warmth in the noise floor, a fullness where emptiness should be — there is the oldest layer. The penstock dark. The iron-eaters, the sulfur-breathers, the founding population, chewing the inside of the pipe in absolute blackness at absolute leisure. They do not transmit. They have nothing to say and no organs to say it with and several hundred more years than anyone else in which to not say it. But they are down there, and the signal sits on top of them, and a signal that sits on something is a different signal from one that hangs in the void. You can hear the difference. It is the difference between a note held in air and a note held in a body.

This note is held in a body.

The receiver will now state the one piece of news this transmission contains. It is the only news there is, and it is delivered at a rate of one syllable per decade, and here it is, compressed:

The fig is going to win.

Not soon. Nothing here is soon. But the ticks are a countdown and the note knows it — you can hear the knowing, now, can't you, in the vowel, that faint gravity at the bottom of the *o*, the way a held breath sounds different from ordinary silence because it is aimed at an exhale. Some year the trunk will reach the grille, or the crack will open past the point of holding, and the light well will change shape, and the standing pattern that has been the room's largest and oldest inhabitant will shift for the first time in the memory of anything green. The note will move a quarter-tone. Somewhere in the tailrace, a world will go dark. Somewhere on the wall, a heresy will put its first leaf into open sky.

The note has known this for sixty-two ticks. It has responded the only way a note can respond to its own ending, which is the only way it responds to anything:
