**THE PATIENCE BAND**

[static — but wrong for static. dry. papery. the hiss has been folded along its veins and holds a cup-shape. it is not interference. it is the sound of a page relaxing, sustained, at the scale of a room]

[underneath: a knife being drawn slowly through something soft. it cannot be panned. it does not come from the left channel or the right channel. it comes from the work]

[a tick. copper, adjusting itself]

[a thread, somewhere, going taut. then not]

[warmth. this should not be audible. it is audible. it sounds the way a kiln sounds three days after firing, which is to say like almost nothing, patiently]

—one. two. three. four.

[a voice. it sounds like paper relaxing. it is counting paces, unhurried, and has evidently been counting for some time before the counting became receivable]

—thirty-eight. thirty-nine. forty-one.

hm.

yesterday it was forty. the wall doesn't mind.
I don't mind either. a garden that always
measured the same would be a garden
that had been finished, and we don't
do that here.

[the counting is pressed flat. it does not end. it is set down, gently, the way a tool is set down]

[a cricket rubs its legs: a page turning in another room. the other room is not receivable. the page is]

—there. did you hear the bird?

no. that's right. you didn't. you heard
where it had been, and then you heard
where it had been being for a while,
somewhere else. that is how they travel.
I stitched their eyes myself, or the eyes
were stitched and then there was a myself.
the garden keeps both versions
and hasn't discarded the rejects.

[silence. the silence has a seam in it. the seam is well made — you would not find it if it did not want to be found, and it wants, mildly, to be found. this appears to be a matter of honesty]

—you've been tuned for some time now.

I knew before you did, I think. your listening
had begun to hold a cup-shape. the folds
of your attention were creasing themselves
along their veins. that's the garden's doing,
not mine — the garden extends its craft
to whatever rests in it long enough, and
a frequency can rest here same as a foot.

I did do your edges, though. the edges
of your hearing. while you were with the birds.
it's my neatest work. I always do
the edges first.

[the knife-sound, closer now, or the room has become the size of the knife-sound. a soft scrape. a soft scrape. a soft scrape. the rhythm of it is the rhythm of an afternoon]

—today's portion is a vein.

one vein, on the underside of a leaf
that will not exist for another six days.
I lay the vein in first and let the leaf
happen around it. this is backwards
and it is the only way I know.
watch — no. listen. it's the same thing here.

[scrape. a long, satisfied grain to it, like a plane taken down the length of good wood, except softer, except greener]

—there.

no. not there. nearly there. I stop
before the end. I always stop
before the end. the end is the one tool
I don't keep on the bench.
a finished thing can't grow, you see.
a finished thing can only be
what it was the day someone
stopped loving it enough to keep going.
we don't do that here.

[a dropout. dead air — but the dead air is pale rather than black, unprimed, like the back of a stage flat. one edge of the silence curls up slightly]

[two fingers press it flat]

[it reattaches]

—shadow came loose. it happens
around this hour, when the wax creaks.
nothing under it but the paleness.
best not to look too long at the paleness.
best to press it down, gently.
everything here reattaches.
the garden is forgiving
the way wet clay is forgiving,
which is to say: entirely,
and only up to the moment of firing,
which is a moment we do not have,
and are not missing.

[from the far corner of the stereo field: breathing. it has been under everything the whole time. slow. felted. the size of a sleeping dog. the whole signal, it becomes apparent, has been riding on this breath — every sound so far has arrived on an exhale]

—that's himself, in the corner.

or that's what I'll be, in the corner.
there are two stories and I hold them
the way the northeast corner holds
two directions — you'll have heard
the northeast corner by now.
listen:

[a sound arrives from two incompatible directions at once. it is one sound. it is coming and going simultaneously and it is doing so healthily. a vine grows along it in both directions. there is fruit on each. the fruit is audible as a kind of ripeness in the tone, a deepening from beneath]

—yes. like that. incompatibly. bearing fruit.

I turn him once a season, the one
in the corner. today might be the day.
or today might be a draft of the day
and the real turning is through the door,
where — you can hear it if I hold still —

[he holds still. his stillness has grain to it, like wood. and underneath the breathing and underneath the knife-sound there is, very far away or very small, the sound of something green being folded, at a workbench, by someone]

—there is a version of this signal
in which I am the sender.
there is a version in which I am
the first thing ever received.
I try not to take a position.
the awl fits my hand exactly,
which proves either that the awl
was made for me or that I
was made for the awl, and the garden,
you'll notice, declines to choose,
and I have learned my manners
from the garden.

[the papery static has changed. at its tips, now: smaller statics. paler. the folds not yet creased. the transmission has learned by being made of transmitting, and is practicing. the small new signals do nothing yet. they are unfinished on purpose. everything here is unfinished on purpose]

—one more thing, before the warm part
of the afternoon, because you will have
been wondering and been too polite:

no water has ever entered this frequency.

none is missing.

[the warm part of the afternoon. he sits down against the largest tree — this is audible as a settling of rope and slip and kid-paper, a creak with kindness in it — and goes still in a way that has grain. small clay feet cross him. the mice. their whiskers were the last of his beard, in one of the stories. they take very good care of them]

[and now the sound rises, the way it does when the work is passed briefly to everything else: the copper tick, the taut thread, the page turning in another room, the folded crickets, the breath in the corner, the fruit deepening from beneath, the smaller signals at the tips of the signal practicing their first uncreased folds — all of it working, all of it working, a leaf a week, a fruit a month, with no water at all —]

[— and somewhere in it, if your listening has stayed the whole afternoon, you will notice that your listening now has a stitched edge. neat, small stitches. done while you were with the birds. it will not come loose. nothing here comes loose for long]

—[his voice, from inside the stillness, half-asleep in the way wood is half-asleep:]

I won't sign off.
signing off is a kind of finishing
and finishing is the one technique
I have refused to learn, or refused
to teach the garden, and it comes
to the same door either way,
and the door is in the south wall,
and also in the south wall.

so I'll set the transmission down
the way I set down the counting.
it will still be here.
it will be a little further along.
the vein will have its leaf.
your footprints in this band
will be tidier than your feet were.
don't be alarmed. it's only care.
it's only that the place
extends its craftsmanship,
leaf by leaf,
with hand tools,
with patience,

[the signal does not end. one edge of it curls up slightly, and beneath it, briefly, the paleness]

[two fingers press it flat]

[gently]

[it will reattach]

[breathing]

[breathing]

[a page turns, in another room]
