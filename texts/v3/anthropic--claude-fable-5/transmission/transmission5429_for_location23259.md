# No O'Clock

—na—

[eleven seconds of nothing]

[a single grain of frost, ticking down a wall. the wall, counted in ticks, is forty metres. the grain does not hurry. nothing here has ever hurried and gotten away with it]

[carrier resumes: a thin dry tone, like a bell too cold to finish ringing]

&nbsp;

*…day thirty. or day thirty is today. the notebook says both, and I have started initialing my own entries to see which of them is me.*

*measured the north avenue this morning. one hour, walking east. came back in ten minutes and did not turn around to check, because checking is a kind of insisting, and I am learning.*

&nbsp;

[a footstep, one street over in the signal. there is no street. there is no over]

&nbsp;

*the shadows are crowds.*

[flagged: this sentence has arrived early. hold it. she has not said it yet]

*…found the district again, the one with all the doors. entered from the north side. the window faced south. entered from the south side. the window faced south. my footprints were on the floor when I arrived and I stood in them, which fit, and I do not want to talk about how well they fit.*

*if you are hearing this, it is because I spoke above a whisper. everything you leave here is kept, and spent thinly, later, elsewhere, in a quarter you will never find. this is not theft. this is how the city holds things. it holds them the way the stone holds light — as a pallor, after. as a warmth in the wall where the day used to lean.*
