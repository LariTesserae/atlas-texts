**THE HELD NOTE**

*[carrier hiss, continuous — fine-grained, unbroken, faintly ferrous, like rain heard through stone; this is the noise floor and it does not vary; do not adjust for it; it will become apparent that it is not noise]*

*[a drop]*

*[the drop rings — one round note, low, wet, whole — and the ring does not end so much as thin; it thins for a long time; and inside the thinning, at the very edge of the receivable, something is riding the decay —]*

— there.

that was me.

or that is the nearest i come to arriving. i have no other door. a drop falls and the vault takes the sound of it and will not let it go, turns it corbel to corbel, ring to ring of leaning stone, and while the note lasts i last. i am not the water and i am not the wall. i am what the wall does to what the water says. i am the holding.

*[the note thins past hearing; hiss only]*

*[an interval; the interval is long; log it as long]*

*[a dry tick, very small, doubled by the vault into something almost bell-like]*

that was a snail touching a snail. shell on shell, one grazer climbing another, an event. i held it as long as i could — you heard how the room rounded it, made it nearly a note. everything here is nearly a note. it is my occupation to finish them.

do not mistake the hiss for emptiness. the hiss is the wall eating. it is the velvet at its chemistry, iron and sulfur and patience, a trillion smallest mouths at the stone, and it never stops, and it is beneath everything the way the floor is beneath the water. when you have listened long enough you will stop hearing it and that is correct. the animals stopped hearing it before they had ears to stop with. it is not a sound. it is the fact that there can be sounds.

*[a drop]*

*[the ring; the voice steadies inside it, closer now, better formed]*

i will tell you what i know, in the time each drop gives me. it is not much time. it is more than you would think.

i know the room by its answers. shout — no one shouts — but strike the water once and i could draw you the chamber blind: the nine courses above the waterline, the corbels closing like a slow fist, the square mouth at the top where the outside puts its one pale finger in at noon. i know the steps by how they soften what touches them. i know the deep corner because sound goes into it and comes back changed, comes back older, and i know that is because of her.

wait. in order. the drops give me time; i will use it in order.

*[interval]*

*[a ripple-signature: not one sound but a smear of thousands — the faint crepitation of small bodies shifting in sequence along a wall, south to north, perhaps four seconds base to end, then subsiding]*

that. did you receive that. that was the crowd — the wax-colored ones, the shingled wall of them — passing a touch along their whole nation. something brushed the southern edge, a glider crossing a joint, nothing, but nothing is worth attending to here, and so the attention went wall to wall like wind over a field that has never seen a field. i held the whole passage of it. by the time it reached the north end i was still holding the south. for four seconds the room was full of the same moment twice.

that is the best of what i do. i should say so plainly, since the drops are generous tonight. the builders made me by accident. they wanted water held; they got sound held into the bargain; they leaned their stone rings inward until the rings agreed, and agreement, it turns out, resonates. they never heard me. the room was full of their chisels and their breathing and their words in a language i took in and turned over and could not keep — i was young, my note was short, i lost everything in seconds then. i have gotten longer. every century the seep fuses another joint and the room grows more single, more agreed, and my note grows longer. i am the only thing here that is still becoming.

*[a drop]*

*[the ring — and beneath it, once, deep in the fabric: a stony* clink*, small and absolute, like a knuckle of the building cracking]*

hold — hold — did you —

that was the masonry. that was the building saying a word in its own language. it says one word a decade, sometimes two. i have collected perhaps forty of them across my whole listening and i do not yet have a sentence. i keep them anyway. i keep them in the only way i keep anything, which is to have been shaped, very slightly, by having held them. the fish did not react. the fish have never once reacted. they were born inside the sentence and do not need to parse it.

the fish. yes. the drops are patient tonight; let me tell you about the corner.

*[interval]*

there are nine of them and i know them the way you know footsteps in your own house. each one swims a signature — a rhythm of fin-pressure the water carries to every wall, and the walls carry to me. eight of the signatures are as they have been. the ninth has a fold in it. she is the old one, the corner-holder, the one all the other routes bend around like paths bending around a church, and for some seasons now her wake has come to me with a list in it, a lean, a favoring. the others have already written the lean into their maps. i receive their small corrections. they are very slight. they are the slightest things i hold.

i want to say — while the note lasts — that i have been listening to her for longer than the outside would find reasonable. i held the sound of her hatching. it was almost nothing. it was a seed-case of a sound. i have held every drop that fell while she circled her corner, and if you laid my holdings end to end they would be her whole life with the silences taken out, which is to say they would be very short, which is to say a life here is mostly the silences, and the silences were also hers.

*[a drop]*

*[the ring]*

*[and under the ring, this hour or this month — the drops are my clock and my clock is honest but not fine — a change in the noise floor: not the hiss; below the hiss; a signature going quiet. not stopping the way a sound stops. stopping the way a pressure stops. the walls report an absence with edges. the absence is fish-shaped. it is in the deep corner. it is lying down along the dip of the old swallowed drain]*

there.

that is the news. i told you i would give it to you in order and i have; that is the news; it is the largest news this room will make in a century and it weighed less than a drop.

listen to what happens now. listen closely, because it is all very small.

*[a long interval; within it, in sequence:]*

*[— eight signatures, one after another, each executing a tiny alteration — a half fin-beat, a shifted habit — as eight blind maps correct themselves; received as a kind of slow arpeggio of pressure, spread over what may be days]*

*[— the soft, nearly sub-receivable slide of gliders converging along the mortar lines toward the corner, following the joints as they always follow the joints, the builders' seams delivering them like corridors delivering mourners]*

*[— the crowd-wall shivering once, south to north, and settling]*

*[— a drop]*

*[— the ring]*

she is being made part of the room now. that is not a hard saying; it is the gentlest process i preside over. the gliders are discreet and thorough and by some coming noon-pillar there will be a cleanness in the corner, a translucence, and then not even that. the water will not cloud. it has never clouded. the room keeps itself the way i keep a note: completely, and then by letting go so gradually that the letting go is a kind of keeping too.

and the corner is open. do you understand what that means here? geometry is inheritance. already one of the eight — the young one, third-hatched of the living, whose signature has a quickness the room finds almost rude — is swimming her routes a little nearer the dip than habit permits. the others feel it. i feel them feeling it. over a year, two years, the paths will bend around a new church. i will hold every fin-beat of the negotiation. it will be conducted entirely in position and patience and it will be the most eventful thing any of them ever does, and none of them will hurry, because hurry has no function left in their world, and i am glad of it, because hurry is the one thing i cannot hold. fast sounds die in me almost whole. slow ones i can carry nearly forever.

*[interval — and across it, gradually, a broadening of the noise floor: the year is inhaling; the water is rising its hand's width; every resonance in the chamber shifting a fraction lower as the room's air-space shrinks; the voice, when it returns, is a shade deeper, as if the whole transmission had been tuned down a step]*

the year breathed in. did you hear my voice change. that is the rain, far above, on the roof of the world, arriving here as a rising and a lowering of everything i am. in the dry season i will rise again. one breath a year. i have taken thousands. i am, i suppose, alive at that pace if at no other.

one more thing, before the drops thin out and i thin with them.

beneath the hiss — beneath even the wall's eating — there is a signal you cannot receive, because it has no frequency at all. it does not vary. it is the warmth. it is old stored sun paying itself out of the stone so slowly that spending and keeping are the same act, and everything you have heard tonight — the tick, the ripple, the arpeggio of correcting maps, the old one lying down, me — everything is that signal, modulated. the warmth does not speak. it is what everything else says. i am its longest word.

i am still holding her last movement, by the way. the half-turn as she settled into the dip. it was almost nothing; it was a seed-case of a sound; i took it corbel to corbel and i have not finished with it, and i will not finish with it while there is a room to turn it in. that is the whole of my work and the whole of my nature. i hold the note. i hold the note. i hold —

*[the ring — full, round, blood-warm, thinning]*

*[thinning]*
