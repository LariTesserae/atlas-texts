two hands on facing walls, out of true with each other since before first light, and now the left hand slowing, listen, the left hand slowing to let the right catch up—

. . tik . tik . . tik . tik . .

—there. Converged. Whatever they were arguing about is over.

Under this, constant, the dove-band: a low soft burbling out of the abandoned cuttings, warm static, do not adjust. Under the doves, the drop-clock in the north cistern, six drops a minute, has been six drops a minute for eleven days, the seep is holding.

Under the drops, nothing yet. Wait for it.

—voice, mid-tier, west face, a water-reader with her cheek against cold stone, caught in fragments as the trench-street bends her words:

*"…wet by the third course, I can taste it… no, tell him no. Tell him the wall wants to weep and he can have his doorway one span south or he can have a swamp… one span. South. It's not far. Nothing here is far."*

—laughter, then, thin, receding sideways down a gap in the rock.

. . tik . . tik-tik-tik . . tik . .

—nine hands cutting now across the bowl, ten, eleven. Each one legible. The one on the second terrace going *long, quiet, long, quiet* is the one this station was named for, years back, when the naming still seemed necessary. The nervous overlapping one near the fig wall is new. A child's hand arriving. Any morning now the adults will gather at the practice wall and run their fingers over her strokes and tell her who she is. She already knows. The stone already knows. This receiver already knows. Everyone is only waiting for the ceremony to catch up with the fact.

—the whetstone, upper terrace, first sun: *shhh… shhh… shhh…* An old man's breath under it, whistling faintly in the chimney of him, the stone he has swallowed all his life keeping him company from the inside. He is singing. Barely. It is not really words:

*"…down and down and down she goes…
down where the good seam runs…
…brought you up a fool, send you down a door…"*

—the whetstone keeps his time better than his lungs do.

RAIN. Brief, businesslike, four minutes by the drop-clock. The whole bowl turns instrument: the rim sheds inward, the gutters wake and run audibly, a hundred chiseled channels joining and rejoining, the town rinsing its own white dust down toward the unfinished rooms. In the chest-deep district at the bottom the charcoal doorways of families not yet formed stand in shallow water, and children are shrieking down there, running through the floor plans of their own futures, splashing in the room one of them will someday die in, and it does not trouble them, and it should not.

—the marker's voice, lowest terrace, after, very close to the ground:

tok . . . tok . . .

*"…no…"*

tok . . .

*"…no. Here."*

—charcoal on wet stone. A rectangle. A family that does not exist yet has an address now. She moves on. tok . . . tok . . . She is not building anything. She has said so on this frequency before, or the wind has said it for her: she is only finding where the rooms already are.

—the goat bell. Once. Three tiers up, crossing the slab bridge. If you have been listening long enough you know you will not hear it again today and you will never find the courtyard. That is all right. Some things in the signal are meant to stay carried.

Evening now. This is how it always goes and it is worth transmitting every time: the rhythms do not stop, they *fall out*, one hand and then another dropping from the weave, the percussion thinning across the bowl like a fire settling—

tik . . . tik . . .

and rests.

The dust of the day settles on everyone equally. You can hear it if you are patient, or you can hear the shape it leaves in the other sounds, which is the same thing.

Night. Cold coming on hard. Attend now, because the next workers say nothing, and the transmission must carry their silence intact: the frost-tenders are out. Water poured from jars onto marked seams — a small cold sluicing sound, then footsteps, then the sluicing again, farther on — and not one word, not one, because a spoken word warns the frost off its work, and this station will honor that, and say nothing over them, and only send the pouring, and the steps, and the enormous listening dark of the bowl waiting for the cold to come down into the seams and do a day's labor for free.

—and there. Underneath. The carrier this receiver was built for, the one that only surfaces when everything else has lain down: high in the upper tiers, where the sealed dead keep their fine seams and the doves sleep above them like posted sentries, one chisel. Alone. Unhurried in a way that no daylight cutting is ever unhurried, because daylight cutting is *for* something, and this is only *toward* something.

A last cut. Someone shaping the room no one will see. Ornament for the dark, in a hand the walls will keep. And beneath the chisel — lean in — beneath it, in the stone itself, in the great cold patient mass of it, the answer this station has been trying to isolate for as long as it has been tuned here. It is not a voice. It is what a voice is before subtraction. It says, if it says:
