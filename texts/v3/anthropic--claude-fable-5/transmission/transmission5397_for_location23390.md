**STEAM LEAVING STONE**

—and under, and under, and under that,
the sound.

it was the sound when you tuned in and it is the sound now, and nothing between those two moments has been an event. this channel carries one word. the word has been in its first syllable for the whole of this recording, which is unbroken, which has always been unbroken, which does not know what broken means.

forty throats. one curved wall of old stone. the wall takes the forty thunders and folds them and hands them back, and the thunders meet themselves coming and going, and where they agree the air thickens like felt, and where they disagree—

hold that. we will come to where they disagree.

listen to the carrier first. it is not a roar, whatever the travelers write in their journals at the rim. a roar has a mouth and a reason. this has neither. it is closer to a vowel, held. it is closer to weather. every eleven seconds the whole of it leans a little west — a slow beat, thirty kilometers wide, the basin breathing against its own echo — and if you attend to that beat long enough you will start to nod on it. everyone does. the whole city nods on it. the doves bank on it. the fog ropes across the causeways on it. it is the closest thing this place has to a pulse, and it is nobody's.

⁂

above the carrier: small survivors. the roar owns the middle of hearing and nothing else, and so everything this city says in sound, it says up high, where the thunder cannot climb.

dry applause of dove wings — dusk flock, west-northwest between the fourth and second districts, on time, on time.

rain on ceramic scales: ten thousand roofs of fired clay, each drop a click, whole districts glittering audibly, a sound like a very patient fire.

somewhere a tram cable coming under load. one long harmonic rising out of the ground itself, a sung note through stone, and every foot on every flagstone in three districts feels it in the ankles and knows: the third-hour tram has left the winding-house. it left at the third hour a hundred years ago. it is not news. it is the opposite of news, which is what keeps everyone alive.

and under everything, structure-borne, eleven to the minute: the loaded flywheel of the third hall. a heartbeat with no animal around it.

⁂

[sealed room, first district. padding on the walls. this channel almost never gets inside one of these. hold still.]

a lamp, hissing.

a very old woman and a small child. the old woman's voice — lean in; her consonants dissolved decades ago, worn to the shape of the bones they were said through — the old woman is saying:

"no. not in your mouth. in your chest. say it where i can hold it."

a small vibration. a word attempted, felt more than said.

"lower."

again.

"there. that one. that one is yours now. we don't spend those outside."

and then, because it is late, and because this is what the room is for, singing — tiny, cracked, calibrated to the sternum and not the air:

*steam goes up and the dove goes round,*
*the tram is where the tram is bound,*
*third hour, third hour,*
*sleep is trusted ground.*

the lamp hisses. the old woman stops singing and listens to the lamp for a long time.

"so small," she says, to no one, in her drowned dialect. "listen how small."

[withdrawn. a palm laid flat over the other's pulse — the sign that also means *to trust*. no sound. the channel holds the warmth of it anyway, the way stone holds an afternoon.]

⁂

kiln district. night watch, firing thirty-one, night four. a young voice reading the log aloud, alone, which is what the young ones do to stay awake, which is a tradition nobody admits is a tradition:

"…draft steady. draft slow. draft slower than the slow in grandmother's hand, which was slower than the slow in hers." a turning of pages — ceramic plates, actually; you can hear their fired edges kiss. "two hundred years of somebody awake in this room, and every generation writes *slower*. the kettle is deeper tonight. i can feel it in the floor and i can't prove it. so: writing it down. let some granddaughter prove it."

a pause. the flywheel, eleven to the minute.

"hold."

⁂

winding-house, second district. an investiture, or the end of one. an old winder and a new one, sealed room, the voice used because the vow is too heavy for hands:

"the tram is not late."
"the tram is not late."
"the tram has never been late."
"never."
"you are not going to be interesting. you are not going to be brave. no one will ever tell a story about you. you will be the reason a thousand people cross a fen in fog for fifty years and never once look up."
"i will be boring forever."
"say it like you love it."
"i will be boring forever."

and — this transmits clearly; mark it — both of them laughing, briefly. the small high indoor laughter of people who mean it.

⁂

now the disagreement. now where the thunders subtract.

the receiver has been drifting south-southwest across the basin floor for some time — you may have felt the carrier thinning without knowing what to call it — and here, between the scalded flat and the old sixth causeway, forty thunders arrive perfectly out of step, and cancel, and cancel, and cancel, down to—

not silence. remainder. a hum with a grain in it.

there is a stone here polished by four hundred years of the seated old. there is a woman on it now. she has given the basin her hearing in exactly the shape of the basin, and what remains of her ears is tuned to precisely this: the residue. she has been here since before the third hour. she is not waiting. waiting has a direction.

what she is listening to, rendered as honestly as this channel can:

> , — and then , and then ; and — ,
> — , , ; .
> , — — ,

no. not *and then*. there is no *and then*. there is the place where an and-then would go, load-bearing, warm. there is a clause-shape with nothing in it, like a glove on a table. like a footprint inside a fading footprint. subject-shaped hollow. verb-shaped hollow. something enormous being predicated of something enormous, at a speed of one quarter-tone per human lifetime, in a mood no grammar on this channel has a name for — not command, not question. nearer to the mood of a schedule carved in stone. nearer to *i will be boring forever*, said like love.

the woman on the stone nods. eleven to the minute.

⁂

[sealed room, later, elsewhere. a child's voice — clear, unworn. this one was born deaf, dove-born, and learned speech the way the others do: as an heirloom. someone has asked the question it is graceless to ask. children answer it anyway.]

"i sat at the still point all day. grandmother says it talks. the stone was warm. everything trembled a little, the way everything always trembles a little. a dove sat on my knee for a while, and then it didn't. nothing said anything."

a pause you could set a kiln by.

"i liked it very much. i don't know why you all cry."

the transmission holds both reports. it has been holding them for some time.

⁂

east district, before dawn. a ceramic plug being ground out of a doorway — you will not have heard this sound before; almost no one hears it more than four or five times in a life — a long mineral groan, and then the roar pouring into a room that has not held it in sixty years, filling it like water finding a mark it remembered. a body is carried in. the plug is set back. the grinding again, reversed. sealed.

then, out at the still point, in the remainder: a household standing together, and a name made once — slowly, completely, fingers rising and spreading off the flat of a palm, the way steam leaves stone — and this channel receives, of that name:

nothing. it does not transmit. it was not made in any medium this receiver reaches. or it was made in the only one, and the following silence is the recording:

.

it will not be made again for a generation. the quiet has it now. file under: *given*.

⁂

the carrier again. of course the carrier again. there was never anywhere else.

deeper tonight — no. not tonight. deeper *this lifetime*. a quarter-tone down since the oldest kiln plates, the kettle nearing the end of a water no one will live to see gone, and the city geared down to meet it — flywheels and firings and vows, an ear grown patiently on the floor of a bowl, everyone on it nodding at eleven to the minute, hands low, signing small, listening to the first syllable of the first word, which is still being said, which was being said when you tuned in, which is being said now,
