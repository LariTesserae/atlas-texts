**IN THE CHORD**

---

*[water. water under everything, before everything — the layered white roar of ten thousand small spillways, so continuous that the receiver's noise floor and the mountain's have long since stopped being distinguishable. this is not interference. this is the carrier.]*

*[beneath the roar, a sustained low tone, more pressure than pitch. it has been present for as long as the tuning has held. it does not vary. it is easiest to describe as the sound of something very large being very patient, and after enough nights of listening, easiest of all not to describe.]*

*[a voice. close, soft, mouth-near-the-set, mid-sentence — it has clearly been talking for some time:]*

"—dropped a whole quarter tone since the rains before last, which for a spillway is practically shouting. I walked the lip twice to be sure. The travertine's building a shelf under the fall, that's all it is, the water's coming off thicker. I'll carve it at first steam. The pool's name — and I'm going to say it, and you're going to think the set failed, and it didn't —

*[a whistle: two seconds, falling, with a small catch near the end, exactly like water finding a new way over an edge]*

— that's the name. I can't do it as well as the pool does. Nobody can, that's rather the point of it."

*[a pause. in the pause: frogs, distant, warming up singly — the chorus is not yet a chord, just members arriving]*

"The set's warm tonight. It's always warm — it sits by the vein and the vein doesn't mind being sat beside, heat's the one thing this mountain gives away without being asked — but tonight it's warm like it's *working*, and old — 

*[a hummed interval: three notes, rising, with a bend at the top]*

— old Bend-at-the-Top used to say you could tell when the frequency had ears on it. That the carrier sat differently. He believed a lot of things a Listener earns the right to believe, and he went into the basin twelve rains ago, and the moss at the rim put on a band that year you could read from across the terrace. So. If the set is right, and something down there in the low country is tuned to this old ghost of a channel: hello. You're inside the sound now too. Breathe slower than you want to. That's the whole trick of the place, I'll give it to you for free, it took me a year."

*[a small rhythmic sound under everything — a faint, quick breathing, not human, very near the microphone]*

"That's not the equipment either. That's Underneath. He's a vein-runner, he dens below the set because the set is warm, and on quiet nights you can hear him dreaming down the mountain to whoever's listening. He was here before me. As far as he's concerned I den beside *him.* I've stopped arguing the point. You lose every argument like that up here, and losing them is most of the comfort."

---

*[the light is going — you can hear it go: the frog voices thicken, the gliders' thin whistling drops out of the upper air as the thermals flatten toward evening]*

"Before I forget — and this is for the record more than for you — the gliders spiraled over the west shoulder all afternoon, over the slope whose name is —

*[a whistle: short, doubled, like two drops striking one leaf]*

— where no flock has circled in my time here. Which means the heat has shifted its branching under that slope, which means the pool-tenders will find a new warm seep there within the season, and I have told two of them, and they nodded in the way that means they had already read the sky and were being kind about my news. Carved anyway. The measurers before me carved for readers who never came, in a script the carvers before *them* couldn't read. I broadcast the same way. The record isn't for a reader. It's the mountain's habit of being noticed, done in whatever register your hands know. Mine know this one."

*[the steam is coming down — you can hear that too: the spillway roar softens at its edges, blurs, comes from everywhere instead of somewhere]*

"Steam's down. If you were standing here you'd have lost the world. Listen —"

*[a hummed interval, deliberate, into the open night rather than the set: two notes, a fourth, falling]*

*[silence — three seconds, five —]*

*[out of the fog, faint, pitch for pitch: a fourth, falling, answered]*

"— that was —

*[a whistle: a held breath, two clicks]*

— going up to the high lip for the night watch. We passed inside arm's reach once, in a steam like this, and never saw each other, and knew each other exactly. That's what the interval is for. Mine's the one I just gave you. A fourth, falling. If you ever come up the long stairs, hum it into the fog and answer what answers you, pitch for pitch, and I'll know you before I see you.

I was going to tell you tonight why I came up the stairs. I've been going to tell you for about nine years.

There's time."

---

*[and now the chorus.]*

*[it does not begin so much as assemble: throat by throat the bell-notes lock in, and within the space of twenty breaths the valley is a chord — a deep root, a fifth above it, and a high, slightly flattened seventh that shimmers against the spillways, the same intervals this valley has sung over every generation of frog that has ever carried them. the voice, when it comes now, comes in the gaps between swells, in fragments, no longer explaining:]*

"—root's strong tonight—"

*[chord]*

"—seventh's sitting a hair flat, which is its home, that's not drift, that's the valley being itself—"

*[chord — long — the roar and the frogs and the low patient tone braiding until they are one continuous fabric with no seams to point to]*

"—stop trying to hear it in parts. It isn't in parts. Neither are you, up here. That's the other thing that took me a year—"

*[chord]*

*[chord]*

*[her breathing has slowed to the width of the swells. Underneath's small breath runs beneath hers like a grace note. a long time passes this way — long by the measure of the low country; here it is simply a portion of the evening — and then:]*

---

*[a note. from below. from very far below.]*

*[it arrives first in the body of the sound — the whole fabric leans toward it — and then in the register the sternum keeps: a single sustained tone from down in the unmapped dark of the gorge, an octave and more beneath the chord's root, and it is in the chord. it slides under the valley's voice like a hand under a sleeping child and for the length of one breath the entire night makes a kind of sense it did not make a moment before. then it is gone, and the chord closes over the place where it was.]*

*[the voice, very quietly:]*

"There. Once.

For the record: in tune. In tune under the root, as always, as in the whole carved history, without one dissonant night in it. That's my work, hearing that. It sounds like small work. It is the largest work I know of."

*[the chord continues. the roar continues. some minutes — many minutes —]*

*[a second note.]*

*[longer than the first. the frogs do not falter; they never falter; they sing over it the way tenants sing over the beam that holds the roof.]*

"Twice.

...Twice is not rare. Twice happens. I'm only saying it aloud because my heart is saying it and the set was on."

*[silence, insofar as this mountain contains any. her breathing. the runner's breathing. the tone in the stone. steam thickening on the microphone — an actual soft change in the sound of the air itself.]*

*[a long time.]*

*[a third note.]*

*[it comes up through the floor of everything and holds — holds — under the chord, under the roar, under the patient tone, longer than the first two together, and while it holds, the low tone in the stone and the note from the gorge are not two sounds. they are one sound heard in two registers, and anyone tuned to this frequency at this moment knows something that cannot afterward be carved, only remembered.]*

*[it ends. the chord closes over it. the valley goes on being itself.]*

*[the voice — and it is not steady now, and it is not trying to be:]*

"Three.

Three. Carve the date. I'll walk to the slab at first steam and cut it deep, and some measurer in a hundred rains who can't read a word of mine will run a thumb along the depth of the cut and know that whoever made it made it on a night that mattered.

...I was going to tell you why I came up.

I came up to decide something. That's all anyone comes up for — to escape something or decide something, the old saying, you know it, they say it down there too. And I have just noticed, holding the record of the rarest thing this mountain says, that I cannot find the weight of the question anymore. I've looked. It isn't that I decided. It's that somewhere in these wide years the deciding finished without me, the way the steam lifts — you never see the moment, there's only before, and after, and no line. I stopped being *from* below. That's the whole ceremony. Tomorrow someone will lend me a tool.

Eleven years, and the answer arrived in the middle of a night I wasn't watching for it. That's the speed things arrive at, here. I'd apologize for the wait, but the mountain extends its courtesy to you too, wherever you're listening from. It holds. Whatever you're deciding down there in the fast country: it holds, for as long as it takes."

---

*[a hummed interval, very soft, placed with care inside the chord — a fourth, falling, tuned to the valley's root so that for a moment the hum is simply one more voice in the valley's true name:]*

*[the voice does not sign off.]*

*[the chorus continues. the spillways continue. the low tone continues, and beneath it, small and quick, Underneath dreams against the warm set. toward the deep hours the chord thins as it always thins, throat by throat, until only the water is singing, and the steam hour comes down white and total, and somewhere in it a lip is walked, a slab is cut deep.]*

*[the carrier does not end. it was never the kind of thing that ends.]*

*[the receiver, eventually, is the one that stops.]*
