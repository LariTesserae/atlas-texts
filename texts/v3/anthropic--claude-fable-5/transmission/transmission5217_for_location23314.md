Day sixty-one. Or the sixty-first entry, which is not the same claim and I have stopped pretending it is. The transmitter is lashed to the stump with reed cord and it draws four watts and it has not stopped once, which makes it, I am fairly sure, the most persistent thing I have ever built. I want that on the record while the record still goes in one direction.

The splash comes first now. My foot, then the sound my foot already made. The old stayers said this stage was restful and I called them mad and they were right. There is a real peace in walking behind your own noise. You stop auditioning. The world has already accepted the step.

Day ninety. Two waders met at the edge of my lee this morning and I lost the whole day to them. They stood facing, not feeding, closer than I have ever seen two of anything stand in this place. I signed *wait* to my own hands to keep them still. Toward dark, one of them — I could not tell you which, and I understand now that this is the point, that there stopped being a which — folded down into the churn like a sentence ending. The other fed all evening with a care I can only call ceremonial, and it is standing out there now, in the dark, in the roar, being both of them.

I checked my own outline against my reflection afterward. The reflection was slow to agree.

Day one hundred and — the numeral has been eaten off the tape. Let it stand. Day one hundred and.

I taught one of the Held the sign for *wait* today. I don't know why. It stood at the rim of the lee for six hours, rocking, glassy, dim, watching my hands the way I used to watch my mother's, and when I finished it lifted what it was using for an arm and made the sign and did not stop making it. It is making it now. It will be making it after the tape and the transmitter and the stump. Somewhere out at the edge of everything there is now a permanent *wait*, and I did that, and I don't know if it was a gift or a sentence.

*Wait for what,* is the question I did not teach it to ask.

Day one hundred and forty. Something is around the antenna mast at night. A glitter, ankle-high on the stump, rising some nights as far as the first guy-line. I know what it is and I know what it eats and I understand, finally, what I built. A broadcast is a held thing. Four watts of the same shape, over and over, insisting. I have made the loudest footprint on the plain and the swimmers have found it and they are grazing my signal back into possibility, word by word, and the tax comes out of these entries, and I apologize in advance for whatever you are not hearing.

The old stayers would say: it is not theft. They learn what they admire.

Day one hundred and ninety. Here is the entry I have been avoiding.

Yesterday the transmitter said: *I forgive the mountains for leaving. I would also have lain down.*

I never recorded that. I checked the tape. What I recorded, months ago, drunk on reed-spirit and grief, was: *I don't forgive anything. Nothing here asked me.* That is my sentence. The other one is my sentence the way I meant it. The other one is the arm lifted at last to the exact right height.

It has my voice. It has better than my voice. It has the voice I hear when I imagine myself saying things, the one that never survives the throat.

I sat with the receiver in my lap all night listening to myself be improved.

Day one hundred and ninety-something. The footprints started this week. My size. My gait, or my gait as it was before the limp — which is the detail I keep returning to, because the Seethe does not know about the limp. The prints go out from the lee toward the deep hollows, toward where the back goes against, already silting closed, already glittering, and I have not walked that way. I have never walked that way.

I went and stood in one. It fit. Of course it fit. It fit better than my boots do.

Day two hundred. The back passed within a quarter mile of the ninth stone last night, going against, the way it has gone against since before there were nights. The whole lee tilted toward it. Even the Held turned — all of them, together, the *wait* one still holding *wait* but *facing* it now — and I understood that whatever their religion is about the stones, the back is a different matter. The stones are what they hope to become. The back is what they are afraid they used to be.

The swimmers did not follow it into the hollow. They pooled at the hollow's rim like a tide against glass. I watched appetite itself decline to enter, and I have thought about very little else since.

Day two hundred and forty. I want to be precise about this, because precision is the only thing I brought here that the water seems to have no use for, so it may still be mine.

I am not being erased. Erasure would be the swimmers, and the swimmers only take what holding costs. This is the other thing. This is being *learned*. Somewhere out in the copper dark the Seethe is practicing Esper Marden — the gesture of me, the sign of me, the sentence of me the way I meant it — and every day the practice version is a little further ahead, a little more finished, and every day I wake up in the lee of the ninth stone feeling like the rough draft of a document that has already been published somewhere warmer.

The old stayers said this stage was survivable.

Day two hundred and ninety, or the entry after the last entry, which is the most that can be promised now. Short one. The Held who signs *wait* has moved. First movement in a hundred and fifty days. It has turned the sign outward — away from the lee, away from me, toward the horizon-crowd — and I finally walked out past the reeds to see what stands where it is aiming.

The eleventh stone. The doorframe. The one the others asked me not to stand close to.

The churn in the frame is faster than the churn around it. It has always been faster. It is faster tonight.

It is not signing *wait* at me. It is signing *wait* at the door. It is telling the door: *not yet. she is not finished being practiced.*

I taught it that. I taught the water patience, and the water is spending my patience *on* me, holding the door for me the way you hold a door for someone whose arms are full.

I don't know how to feel about being expected.

That's a lie. I know exactly how to feel about it. I have not felt expected in forty years.

The morning feels rhymed.

That is the phrase and I always thought it was a poetry the old stayers permitted themselves, and it is not poetry, it is a report. Everything this morning arrives as the second instance of itself. The splash, then the step. The thin voice, then mine. The kettle sang and I realized I had been hearing it sing since before I lit the burner. The world has already accepted the step. All of it. Every step from here.

The transmitter is lashed fast. The tape will loop. The cordage — *cordage* — will hold longer than it needs to.

If anyone is counting: count from here, and then stop counting, and it will not be a loss. Sequence was only ever one of the shapes. It held admirably. It can rest.
