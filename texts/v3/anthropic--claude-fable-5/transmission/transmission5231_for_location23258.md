[and now the soft place]

[this is the finding we delayed reporting, because none of us wanted to be wrong about it]

[in the compressed archive there is a recurring dimple in the voice. a damping — a small acoustic dimness, a region where the bass loses a sliver and the glassy overtones dull, exactly as if a wet, warm, sound-swallowing body were standing in the central gallery, at the crossing of the four leaning pillars. it lasts, in real time, about an hour. it recurs. we have it eleven times in three years, at intervals that are not regular but are not random either — they are *seasonal*, they cluster where a certain slant of light must fall through the turf-thin roof, the way anniversaries cluster]

[the first assumption was an animal. but nothing lives in the hollow, and the dimple does not move like a body. it does not enter by the seam or leave by it. it simply *arrives*, fully formed, at the crossing of the pillars — stands — breathes, we can hear it breathe, there is a flutter in the held note at exactly the tempo of human breathing at rest — presses, once, something warm against something humming, we can hear the column's tone go soft under a palm that is not there — and then, after an hour, it does not depart. it is *set down*. released, the way you release a note by lifting a finger from a string]

[it is not a body. it is the *memory* of a body, performed. the hollow is re-sounding, in its own voice, the shape of an hour when someone stood inside it and listened. it kept the whole damping — the size read from the blotted wavelengths, the breath read from the flutter, the palm read from the softened column — and now, eleven times in three years, at intervals only it understands, it hollows itself into that exact shape again and holds the shape for exactly one hour and puts it away]
