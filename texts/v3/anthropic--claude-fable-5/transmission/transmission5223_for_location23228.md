**The Ninth Lamp**

*[carrier tone: steady, ember-orange in the way some tones are, with the faint fry of a carbon filament behind it — and under that, always, the murmur, lower than the machines, a kettle an hour from boiling]*

*[a woman's voice, mid-round, measured, each sentence arriving in complete strokes]*

—and under Smelter Row, deciding well. Deciding well all week, in fact, which the foundry watch attributes to the new curing on the east flank and the lamplighters attribute to the lamplighters, and both of these attributions are recorded, and neither is finished.

Under the Third Terrace: deciding slowly. You will have felt it, Terrace people — that long hesitation beneath the cabbage plots around suppertime, like a word going back for a second look at itself. This is not silence. Let me say that again, because I know how the Terrace worries, and I will say it completely: it is not silence, it has never once tonight been silence, and the watch at the railing confirms the murmur continuous as of the last press-stroke. Ground that thinks slowly is still thinking. Some of our best ground thought slowly.

Under the Junction Quarter, beneath this very tower: deciding well, and leaning while it does it, two degrees out of true, same as your grandmothers left it, same as you'll leave it. The tower is finished. Nothing else is.

*[two soft taps against the microphone housing]*

You heard the taps. New listeners, lowland listeners, listeners out on the receiving edge of things: I cannot broadcast the tilt of a head, so the guild long ago settled on the double tap, and you are to take it as the tilt. Anything I say about tomorrow arrives with taps attached, whether you hear them or not.

Rain. One pocket only tonight, currently grazing across the Saddle of Maybe-Water, about a hundred meters of it, moving like something looking for something. Tending north. *[tap-tap]* If it holds that tending it will cross the cog line around the fourth hour, and the fourth-hour crew should stand in the doorway of the winch house and let it have its ten minutes, because it is a small rain and it is not about you.

Also observed, and I pass this along without comment as I am contractually obliged to pass it along without comment: droppings on the unratified ridge east of the seventh stake. The goats have been where none of us have. Take from that what the surveyors take from it, which is a route.

*[a press-stroke, closer now — the deep respiratory thud, felt in the tone itself; the filament fry wavers and steadies]*

Foundry notices. Two formes go out before dawn against the north flank below the terraces, so the moss-cliffs will glow their orange for the next several days, and the hanging vine along that stretch will die as the rock beneath it commits, and the gardeners should go tonight if they want to read that map one last time, because by Thursday it will not be a map of anything. It will be ground. That is not a loss. It is also not not a loss. Both of those sentences are finished and I stand behind both of them.

The tower is about to—

no. I won't leave that lying around. The tower is about to strike the second hour, is the whole of the sentence, and I got it out in time, and here is the bell.

*[THE BELL: one great bronze stroke, and for the length of its decay the presses pause, the chains pause, and the murmur is briefly the only thing in the world — audible, patient, enormous, a sentence not yet at its verb — then the second stroke, and the presses resume beneath it like breathing]*

Second hour. Still here.

*[papers; the small ceramic sound of a tea bowl set down]*

The Register.

Marest Ollin, weaver, of the Third Terrace, seventy-one years. Proposed on the north kept-wall, day forty of forty. The lantern burned its full count. The family returned at dusk and I am glad to be the one to read this out: the wall has closed over and around. The niche has deepened. The shape is held, the shape is recorded, she is kept. Her people may bring their palms on Sunday, and the rest of the Terrace the Sunday after, and Marest — because I knew you, and because you liked things finished — I will finish this one properly: the mountain considered you, and the mountain is keeping you, and that is the end of the sentence.

Brann Kettleward, founder, second class. Day thirty-one of forty. The lantern burns.

Ivo Lench, surveyor. Day forty of forty.

*[a pause — one full press-stroke long]*

The niche stands smooth.

The mountain is not finished considering. *[tap-tap]* We will propose him again in spring, when the ground under the east wall has had a winter to think, and I want the Lench household to hear the next part with the tilt firmly attached: a smooth niche is not a verdict. Nothing up here is a verdict. Verdicts cure, and this one hasn't, and I have known the rock to take thirty years over a handprint, so it can take one more winter over a whole surveyor. For now, Ivo. For now.

*[the tea bowl again; a long exhale, valved]*

And now the windows. Those of you in the cured districts, in the well-lit streets, close your windows if you are the kind who needs sentences landed, because the next segment is the Open Sentences, and I am about to do, on purpose and under license, the one thing your grandmothers taught you never to do near green rock.

These are the words the city has given me for the outwalkers. For the ones past the stakes. I read them as written, and I do not finish them, because they are not mine to finish, and they go out over the pending country now, unlanded, as offered.

From the Kettleward family, to the east saddle: "If you are becoming anything out there, we hope—"

*[the carrier drifts; static crosses the signal like a small private rain, drumming, moving on]*

From a woman on Smelter Row who did not give her name: "The kitchen still—"

From the children of the fourth form, who saved their lamp-oil money to buy this line, and whose teacher tells me they argued a week over every word: "You can come and look at our stakes if—"

From an old man at the Junction, same line as every night, nineteen years now: "It was never about the money, and if I had it again—"

*[static]*

That's all of them. The sentences are out. Whatever takes them up, takes them up.

*[a long stretch of just the carrier and the murmur — long enough that a receiver might think the round finished. It is not finished. She would never.]*

Aldis.

Your bearing tonight, from the watch at the seventh stake, with the vermilion shutter open a crack the way we do: north-northeast, walking the old transect, the whole of it, corner to corner, the way you always walked it, unhurried, grey on grey.

Tending home. *[tap-tap]* For now.

And you know I won't finish the rest of it. Thirty years and I've never once finished it, and I'm not going to lose my discipline at the second hour on a Tuesday. It's an open sentence, Aldis. It stays open. That's the courtesy. You taught me the courtesy.

*[the murmur, briefly, seems to be under her voice rather than under the tower]*

To everyone else: the round is walked. Same segments, same order, thirty years, and the air along this frequency has begun to cure because of it — the engineers confirmed it again last month, other signals drift, this one doesn't, this one has gathered true air around itself like a stake gathers stone. So leave your receivers lit if you like. The frequency will hold its shape till morning, and you may keep what you like of it.

The lamps are lit. The presses are breathing. The tower leans and is loved for it.

This has been the Ninth Lamp. Still here.

*[she does not say goodbye. she never says goodbye. the carrier holds]*

*[ … ]*

*[the presses. the chains. far off, the choughs resettling in the cooling vents, a thousand small adjustments]*

*[ … ]*

*[then — delayed, as if arriving from a long way out across unresolved air — her own voice, a beat late, thin and grey: "—still here—". then again, later, deeper, as a handprint deepens: "—still—"]*

*[then words from no broadcast made tonight. old words. the air out there has kept some, evidently, and is playing them back with more weight than they left with: a register entry years cured — "the shape is held" — a rain report for a rain long over — a child's line from the fourth form, some other year's fourth form — "you can come and look—"]*

*[then, once, in a voice that is not hers — slower, valved differently, with a gait in it:]*

*["tending home."]*

*[nothing takes this up. nothing finishes it. the courtesy, it seems, runs both ways]*

*[beneath everything the murmur rises — swells the way the sea below swells with lamp-orange — climbing toward something, the long sentence leaning at last against its verb, the whole carrier trembling with the nearness of it —]*

*[— and settles. not yet. an hour from boiling. the kettle goes on considering]*

*[the carrier holds. it is going to hold all night. it has held for thirty years and the air around it has decided what it is]*

*[still here]*

*[for now]*
