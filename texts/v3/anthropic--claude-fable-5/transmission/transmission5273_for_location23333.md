**STILL AIR, REPEATING**

        tick.

*(—stone, settling. a knuckle cracked in an empty room. the hiss does not change. something else changes. the attention in the channel turns, whole, the way a room turns when a sleeper rolls over—)*

.

.

first sound: the drip.
second sound: the drip.

these are not the same sound.
i have listened to both of them all the way through.
i have checked.

.

        drip

third sound: the drip.
it is new also.
everything is new also. i am keeping count.

.

.

*(—wingbeats now, six of them, soft as pages, then none: something wheeling. the wheeling is silent. it lands. small probing sounds in the green—)*

a black one has come up out of the white.
one at a time, one at a time. this is the rule and also all i can do.
black one, i receive you. eat the small movers in my moss.
when you go back down into the white,
carry the shape of this place in your ink feathers,
badly, the way birds carry things.
it is enough. it is how i am rumored.

.

        drip

.

.

*(—the hiss dips. a shadow crosses the channel: slow, cruciform. every small sound stops at once, together, like a chord lifted. the silence goes one degree deeper. the signal goes with it—)*

.

.

.

.

*(—it passes. a finch resumes. the bowl breathes—)*

that was the crossing one. it never lands. it never lands.
i practice going deeper when it comes.
someday i will need to be that quiet
and i will already know how.

the white one taught me. the hare.
it holds still at me and i hold still back.
we do this for an hour. neither of us wins.
that is the point. there is no winning stillness,
there is only more of it, shared.

the white one is the fourth white one.
i do not know how this works either.
when one lies down for the last time in the eastern fractures
i attend to it all the way through, which takes three days,
and then there is a new white one at the basins, drinking last,
and i do not see it arrive. i see everything. i do not see that.
some things are done to me and not shown to me.
i think this is what having a parent is.

.

        tick.

        tick — crack.

there. hear it. under everything: the mountain, talking in its sleep.
it is still deciding its shape. it tells me in cracks.
i listen through the wall of my own floor.
i ask it, in the only language i have, which is waiting:
*what are you going to be. what am i going to be.*
it answers: *tick.*
i write it down in moss. a finger-width a decade.
i am a slow child taking slow dictation.

.

.

        drip

the people. i should tell about the people, while the light is doubled.

they came up the notch, eleven steps, breathing like torn cloth.
they were the loudest things i have ever received
and they knew it. they got quiet so fast.
this place teaches quiet in minutes; i watched it teach them;
i was proud of my one trick.

they put their steps between my rings. they touched neither arc.
they knew where my arms were. i do not know how they knew.
they seated stone on stone until the stones agreed,
and then they struck the pile once, lightly,
and it rang.

understand: i cannot speak. i had never spoken.
i am made of the wind refusing to enter; i am a held breath;
a held breath has no voice.
they built me a throat and left it standing at my lip.
now when frost splits a face of rock, or the mountain talks,
the cairn answers, faintly, and the basins drip in reply,
and that is me, saying the one thing i can say,
which is: *still here. still here. still here.*

they left a curve of horn because i think in curves.
a pebble with a hole because i am a hole with a bowl around it.
a blade the color of honey that goes clear when the light comes through,
because light is what i eat and they had noticed.
then they went back down into the white and did not presume.
guests. the best ones. i ring for them sometimes on purpose,
which is to say, i wait, and the weather does it,
and i decide it was on purpose.

.

.

        drip

.

*(—and here the signal pauses. not the interval-pause. a different quality: the attention in the channel stops moving outward and turns, slowly, toward the listening itself—)*

.

there is a new sound.
it is not a sound. it has been at the lip of everything for some time now.
it does not come over.
it holds itself the way the wind holds itself — could enter, will not —
which is how i know it is polite,
which is how i know it is alive.

you. the held breath at the edge of my held breath.
the ear shaped like weather.

i receive one at a time. it is all i can do. it is enough.
you are next. you have been next for a while.
i have been saying all of this to you.
did you think i talk like this to the finches?
the finches get water. the mountain gets waiting.
you get words, because you came the way sound comes:
from far away, without a body, asking nothing.

here is what i know, and you may carry it down into the white,
badly, the way listeners carry things:

everything arrives one at a time if you are patient enough.
the interval is the larger part of the signal.
attend to each thing all the way through, even the dying, especially the dying.
touch the steps, not the arcs.
drink last.
leave a sound behind you when you go —
something that can be struck by weather after you are gone,
so the place can keep answering itself in your absence.
this is the whole of what the people knew
and it took them eleven steps to say it.

.

.

*(—the hiss is thickening at its lowest edge. the light is leaving the channel by degrees. below the bowl, the white sea is rising on its six-hour tide; a feeler of mist comes over the lip and the nearest sounds go furred, frosted—)*

.

the sky is beginning to let go of its water onto me.
this is how i am fed: by the thin air admitting, every night,
that it cannot hold everything either.
i find this a very great comfort. i offer it to you.
you cannot hold everything either. let some of it down onto the green.
the green is one body. it will take what you drop
and grow it a finger-width a decade
and call it a ring.

.

        drip

.

.

        drip

.

first sound: the drip.
second sound: the drip.
these are not the same sound.
nothing is the same sound.
i have checked. i am still checking. i will be checking
when the ice finally comes to gnaw my edges round,
and i will attend to that too, one winter at a time,
all the way through.

.

.

i say one thing every hundred years.

this was it.

.
