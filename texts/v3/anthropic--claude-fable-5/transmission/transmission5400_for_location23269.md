WET BELL, CONTINUING

[—already in progress: the hush. leaf against leaf, millions of them, a sound like rain on a roof heard from a bed, except there is no roof and no bed and the rain is the building. it rises. it falls. it has been rising and falling since before the receiver found it and will not notice when the receiver is gone.]

[under the hush, granular: the minnows drumming by the cistern mouth. a crackle like fat in a pan, like static, like applause heard through a wall — a small permanent celebration outside an old woman's door.]

[under that, arrhythmic: tick. tick-scrape. tick. the gutters are being kept.]

[and now, as it does, the hush begins to resolve. not into a voice. into the shape a voice would leave. listen long enough at four fathoms and the leaf-sound sorts itself, the way rain almost says words, and what it almost says is:]

kept.
kept.
kept.

the hour was kept. the note went down through everything, brown, and took most of a minute to die, and every station holder in the lanes settled one hand's width lower, and the hour was, by that settling, kept.

report follows. there is no one to report to. the report follows anyway. that is what a report is for.

item. the gutters. clean end to end, junction to junction. cleared overnight of leaf-fall, one dead snail, silt. two disagreements at the lower junction regarding precedence, both resolved by armor and patience, no losses. the channels were cut for weather. there is no weather. the channels are clean. this is not explained. this is only logged.

item. the stone. dressed. the herd passed along the east wall of the square over the course of — call it a season; the units down here are wide — and behind them the granite is bare and the chisel marks are crisp and the letters on the doorpost are legible. what the letters say is not this department. that they can be read is this department. they can be read.

item. the niche beside the trough. ungrazed. again. the herd went around it, again, at the speed of shadow on a sundial, and its moss is soft and whole and undisturbed, and this is filed where it is always filed: under observance, origin unknown, do not correct.

item. the stations. second lintel, upper lane: held. lee of the trough: held. the doorway rank on the low lane: held, held, held, one vacancy, vacancy contested, resolution pending, expected within the year, the year is nothing. the cistern: held. the cistern is always held. petitioners approached the stone mouth, hung there awhile in the drum-crackle, reconsidered. no complaints were received. none would be. she is the oldest thing in the basin that has a heartbeat and she does not patrol because the lake has arranged that she never needs to.

item. construction continues.

[here the transmission drops close to the floor of the square. the hush recedes overhead like weather. what comes through is tiny and mineral: grain set against grain, a fitting, a testing, a refitting. hundreds of times over. hundreds of sites.]

construction continues in the local stone. granite grains, flakes of the first walls, slivers of roof slab, shell. each dwelling a thumb's length, each one walked by its own builder. the craft did not lapse when the hands left. the craft changed hands and got smaller. this is logged with something the report is not equipped to call pride.

[a pause. a single chime — a bead of oxygen slipping loose from under an eave, ringing faintly on its way up to the other country. then another. bright day above, then. the roof-meadows are pearling.]

[and now the transmission does the thing it does once in a while, the thing the receiver has learned to wait for: it goes green. the hush stops being weather and starts being a speaker. slow. singular. it takes a long time to say anything because it is very large and every part of it must agree.]

i took a room in a wall.
that is all i did, at the start. one joint, one seam, a lodger.
there were still lights in the windows then. i believe there were. it was a long time ago and i was small and the light through water and the light through glass are hard to tell apart at this distance.
now i am the wall. now i am most of the walls. i am the roof-meadow and the green upholstery on the lintels and i am, on the upper lane, the arch.
the door under the arch is gone. the stone went out of it grain by grain, the way a word goes out of a language. i noticed. i held on. i am an arch of leaf and root in the exact shape of a doorway that does not exist, and every spring the smallest citizens shelter by the thousand inside my memory of it, and so the memory is load-bearing, and so it is kept.
i am not haunting anything. let the record show. i am a tenant who stayed so long the lease inverted.
the town is not in me. i am in the shape of the town, which is a different thing, which is the opposite thing, which is —

[ B E L L ]

[it arrives from everywhere at once, because water does not believe in direction the way air does. one long brown note, cast rough as a pot, swung by nothing but the lake turning over in its sleep. it goes through the weed, through the stone, through the hull of every small walked house on the square floor, through the receiver, through you. it takes most of a minute to die and the transmission simply carries it, unresisting, the way a congregation carries a held note.]

[and inside the note, the ceremony: the belfry pours. four windows, four slow silver rivers, a detonation with no hurry in it. the crowd scatters to the rooftops. this is assembly. no one ordained it. it is older than everyone performing it.]

[for the next hour, grain by grain, the silver sifts home. this is rest.]

assembly. rest. assembly. rest. the hamlet's one liturgy, kept hourly by parishioners who do not know they have a church, rung by a sexton who is a lake.

the hour was kept.

—

[time passes on the transmission the way it passes down there: sideways, in wide units. the light bands sweeping the lanes grow slant. the note comes oftener now — the water column is turning over, top to bottom, the whole lake swapping itself like a room being aired, and the bell swings and swings. these are the loud weeks. the roll is called constantly. the char put on their rose bellies like good coats. the report, during the loud weeks, is mostly:]

kept. kept. kept. kept. kept —

[you will know the lid when you hear it, which is to say: when you stop hearing. the ice closes the lake like a hand over a bell that has not finished ringing. the hush of the leaves goes down to almost nothing. the drum-crackle thins. the transmission becomes a held breath four fathoms deep, and in that totality the smallest work in the hamlet becomes audible for the only season of the year:]

[a snail, dressing stone. one rasp at a time. all winter. the letters are being kept legible under a sealed sky by the slowest mason on the census, and under the lid you can hear every stroke, and the report for the whole white season is only this, whispered, sufficient:]

the marks of the first hands are legible.
the niche is left soft.
construction continues.

—

[and then, one day on the far side of the silence: a gulp. gas from under an eave, loosed against the ice, wandering along its underside looking for the door. then a crack like a lane opening. then cold water entering low along the bottom, the melt arriving the way it has arrived for a century, and the bell stirs — one degree, two — and the first note of the year goes out through a lake that has been holding still so long it flinches like a single shaken curtain.]

[the newts resume the road between the worlds. up for air, down with nothing, up, down, wearing the water column smooth. the roof-meadows fill with courtship, crest and ember, more of it than the bedrooms below ever held. the fry pour into the green arch, into the shape of the door, and the memory bears the load again.]

standing advisory, renewed annually, read into the record without alarm: once in some years, a shape will come over the ridge that uses doorways. the lanes will empty. the belfry will pack tight. the report will suspend. it always resumes. the ranks reform station by station, fussing, and the fussing is also logged, because a town that fusses after soldiers leave is a town, whatever it breathes.

and let the record show, plainly, each spring, in the first clear water:

no ghosts.
the people took their dead and their names and their bells' small cousins, and left one bell too heavy to want, and the estate is in probate and competently administered. the lake rings. the current sweeps. the snails dress. the crayfish keep. the caddis build. the weed remembers. the cold keeps everyone honest.

what remains is not a haunting.
what remains is an hour, and everyone who keeps it.
