**THE NIGHT COUNT**

*[carrier hum, warm and slightly furred, as though the signal itself has been breathed on — a voice already in progress, pitched to carry about three feet, held close to a microphone somewhere in the steam]*

— and the egg pool holds at egg. The flea pool on the second rim east runs a shade slow tonight; if your baskets are down, give them another breath and a half before you draw them. The bread shelf is true. The bread shelf is always true. Otthe says her tins came up dense and good, and she has left one on the fourth-rim railing for whoever cut her door-seam this morning without being asked. She did not see who it was. She says the tin is under the second lamp and the lamp is lit, but of course the lamp is always lit, so: second lamp, going east, the one with the crooked hood. Take the bread. She won't hear you thank her and she doesn't need it.

*[the carrier dips. A long soft exhalation moves through the signal, near and far at once — one sound made in a dozen places. The voice waits inside it. Somewhere behind the voice, faintly, glass fogs and clears, or something like that. The breath passes.]*

There.

That was a good one. Even out at the mast I felt it in the planks.

The hot pools next, for the newcomers, and I know there are two of you still with us, so listen: the pool below the foundry outflow is at won't-forgive and will stay there. It is beautiful and it is turquoise and it is not for you. The stone at its rim is new stone, a season old, and new stone lies. Walk where the causeway walks. The causeway is not a suggestion.

For the house on the third rim west: your stilts have anklets again, all four, and the south one is thick. The cutters saw it from the high path today. Vess raised his saw to you at noon — that was *come*, if you didn't read it — and you didn't come, so now I am saying it out loud, which costs you nothing and cost him a half-mile walk to tell me, so mind it. They'll be there after the second breath tomorrow. Have your door open. If your door will open.

*[static, briefly — a fine mineral crackle, like white dust settling on water]*

Personal traffic.

Miren on the sixth rim asks the foundry to hold two hooks' worth of rimfleas from the morning netting; her daughter is up from the low country and has not eaten proper food in a year, by which Miren means our food, and she said this in a tone I will not attempt over the air.

To the Herrek children: I walked past your rim today. The button is half under. The coin from the winter is a shine in the stone now, a little sun with a lid on it. And the milk tooth — the milk tooth from last spring is gone in.

It is kept.

You may go and see, but you will only see the rim, which is the point, and which I believe you understand better than the grown people do.

*[the carrier dips again. The exhalation. Longer, this time, or the receiver has begun to expect it, which comes to the same thing. When the voice returns it is fractionally slower, as if it went out with the breath and came back a step behind itself.]*

The settling notice, now, and then the small things.

Ana Serit was carried up on the second morning of the cutting season, as most of you know, because most of you were on the causeways and stood still as she passed, and she would have liked the look of that, all of you upright and unmoving in the steam like a fence of people. The frame took her well. The water has already begun; her family went up yesterday evening and say the sheath is starting at the hands, which is right, she was a cutter forty years, let the stone start where she started on it.

She is in the Settling Pool two spans east of her mother. The family knows the place. In time the rim will know it for them.

She used to say — I will tell this once and then it is traffic like any other — she used to say that cutting the stone back was not a fight, it was a conversation, and that the stone always got the last word, and that this was fine, because it was also going to get *every* word, eventually, all of them, hers and mine and this one going out over the air right now, and a person could either resent the keeping or say something worth keeping.

So. That is what she said. Now it is said on the night count, and the mast heard it, and the mast is half stone already, so make of that what you like.

*[a long pause. Water, if it is water — a continuous thin filming sound underneath everything, so quiet it may only be the shape of the silence]*

Small things.

The kettle-dog behind the foundry: nostrils clear. Fedder checked at dusk with a mirror, as he does, and the mirror fogged. Proof. Twelve years I have read that item and twelve years it has been proof, and the two newcomers may laugh, one of them did laugh, I heard about it — but you will be here in the fog with a mirror yourself one winter, checking, and you will feel what we feel when it clouds. Which is not relief. It is more like being answered.

The glasswings are rising heavy off the fourth rim films. Good year for the birds. If you have not stood under a column of them at the right hour, when the high sun gets into the steam and the whole rising of them glitters — do that, this month, while it lasts. It costs a walk. Everything here costs a walk. That is how you know it isn't small.

One newcomer left on the supply run. This is not a criticism; he was decent and he worked and he could not sleep, and he told me why, and I will tell you, because it is the same why as always: he lay awake waiting for the basin to breathe, and when it breathed, his chest went with it, and he minded.

The other one — the other one, I am told, was sitting on the Vess porch three nights ago when the breath came through, and she had already breathed out.

She didn't know we noticed. We noticed.

Welcome to the steps.

*[the exhalation again. This time the voice does not wait for it to pass; it lets a sentence lie flat under the breath like a hand on a railing, and picks it up after]*

— which is nearly the count, then.

Last item, and it concerns this voice, so bear with an old woman a moment. The mast is being kept. Where the guy-wires enter the stone, the anklets are past cutting now — the cutters and I have talked it over and we have decided, in the way things get decided here, by nobody deciding, to let them be. The signal will thin a little each year. You have heard it thinning. Some of you younger ones have never heard the count come through clean and don't know the difference, and that is all right; you have also never heard the engines with their first valves, and the town breathed then too.

One year this voice will go into the rim like the causeways went, like the schoolteacher went, like Ana went, a thin bright line in cross-section, and some child on a broken edge will point and say *that was the talking one*, and be told yes, and go on climbing.

Until then: the egg pool holds at egg. The bread shelf is true. The door on the third rim west gets cut tomorrow after the second breath. The tooth is kept. The mirror fogged.

The plateau is fair. Sleep in it.

*[the voice does not sign off. It simply stops adding words, and what remains on the carrier is the basin itself: the furred hum, the filming water that may be silence, and then, at long intervals, the single soft exhalation of the whole scattered town moving through the signal like a welt of heat through a pool — once — and a long time later, once — and the receiver, wherever it is, dry country, high air, keeps holding the frequency, and somewhere in the third or fourth interval the listener's own chest goes with it, uninvited, and the count is over, and the keeping goes on]*

*[carrier continues]*
