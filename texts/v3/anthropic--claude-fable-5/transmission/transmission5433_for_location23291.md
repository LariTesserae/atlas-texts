HELD THUNDER

—and under everything, before everything, the carrier: the river behind the eastern wall, which is not a sound in this transmission so much as the paper it is printed on. It does not begin when you tune in. You tune in and discover it has been holding this note since before there were receivers. It will be transcribed once, here, and then never again, because it never stops, and to keep writing it would use up all the ink in the world:

*ohhhhhhhh—*

There. Assume it under every line that follows. Assume it under the silences especially.

∙

[the drips]

A drop falls in the street behind the granary quarter. *tik.*

Eleven seconds. A drop falls on the shoulder of the third tower, where ten thousand years of drops have given the tower a shoulder to fall on. *tok.*

Four seconds. The market basin: *plip* — the only drop in the borough that lands in water, and so the only one with a vowel.

These are not random. The receiver has been tuned long enough to know this. Each drip keeps its post and its interval the way a bell keeps its hour, and a drip's interval is its voice — eleven seconds is a voice, four seconds is a voice — and a voice that says one syllable per drop can still say things, if the listener will stay. The receiver has stayed. What follows is not heard in a night. What follows is the accumulation: years of drops, laid end to end, until the syllables closed into words. This is what the drips have been saying, at the rate of a thumbnail per century:

*still.*

*still.*

*still here.*

*still here. still building. we fall where we fell. we fall where we fell. the corner is nearly round now. the corner is nearly round. give us a thousand years. give us a thousand more. we are the ones who stayed on after the makers' hands went out like their fires. nobody dismissed us. nobody has dismissed us. the work continues. the work is the falling. the falling is the work.*

*still.*

*still here.*

∙

[beneath the drips, steadier, dimmer — the film]

It does not pulse. It does not signal. It burns at the exact grey-green of the last minute of dusk and it has been the last minute of dusk here for ten thousand years. If it transmits anything, it transmits continuously, on one unwavering band, and the message is short enough to fit inside a glow:

*the lamps are lit.*

That is all. That is the whole broadcast. But listen at the edges of it, where a seam went dry three hundred years ago and a street's worth of light withdrew, and the message has a margin, faint, like handwriting pressed through from the previous page:

*the lamps are lit. the lamps that can be lit are lit. some streets I have had to give back to the dark. I did not want to. the water left and I followed the water because I am the water's tenant, not the city's. forgive the eastern quarter. forgive the stair below the second wall. I held them as long as the seep held me. the lamps that can be lit are lit. the lamps are lit.*

∙

[a citizen]

Movement on the signal now — small, so small, a grain of interference walking. An isopod has entered the long street that runs from the granaries to the square. Its footfalls do not carry; what carries is the faint drag of its shell on the glaze, a whisper of chitin on stone, and beneath that — the receiver strains — beneath that, something slower. A heart. A heart that beats the way flowstone grows.

It walks the street. It keeps to the street. It reaches a doorway and pauses in the threshold, antennae working at nothing, honoring the custom, and while it stands in the carved door the receiver picks up — not thought, these citizens do not think, but *posture*, the broadcast of a body arranged a certain way, and the posture says:

*the floor speaks. the floor has always spoken. I stand in the door because one stands in the door. I do not know why one stands in the door. one stands in the door.*

Then it walks on, out of resolution, grazing. Sixty of them in the whole borough. Each one alone in a metropolis. Each one carrying the thunder in its feet like a name it was given and cannot pronounce.

∙

[the census]

Glazed into the streets, the shells of the dead — and the shells transmit too, in the way that fossils transmit: not a signal but a record, readable by anything patient enough to be an instrument. The receiver passes over the pavement of the market square and the stone gives back its ledger, entry after entry, each one identical in form:

*here stopped a citizen. the water found it. the water kept it.*

*here stopped a citizen. the water found it. the water kept it.*

*here stopped a citizen —*

Thousands of entries. Ten millennia of entries. Not one is missing. Not one has been revised. Somewhere in the borough tonight, a citizen is walking who will become an entry, and the street it will lie down in is already prepared, in the sense that every street is prepared, in the sense that the census has never once been caught unready.

*the borough keeps its own,* says the pavement, all of it at once, very quietly, under the roar. *the borough keeps its own.*

∙

[interruption]

The signal goes strange.

Something is crossing the square. It does not walk; it *pours*. The receiver gets it as a long cold smoothness drawn across every channel at once, a finger laid over the strings — no heartbeat, no footfall, no posture. It broadcasts nothing. It is a moving absence with a direction, and the direction is a doorway, always a doorway, and the receiver, which has been tuned here a long time and knows better than to hope, follows it to the threshold and holds the channel open as it goes in.

Nothing.

The channel stays open. The receiver has left this channel open before. It is open now. Behind the doorway are the borough's interiors, the hollow rooms the calcite sealed but did not fill, and from inside them comes no signal, no echo, nothing to transcribe, and yet the channel does not feel empty. It feels *occupied and unlisted.* Like a lit window with the curtain drawn. Like a name in the directory with no number beside it.

[channel remains open. nothing. the receiver notes that it will think about this doorway afterward. the receiver always thinks about the doorway afterward.]

∙

[the pools]

Dozens of side-channels, coin-sized, black. In some: the faintest possible oar-strokes, translucent specks rowing, a nation of five diverging from a nation of five three streets away, and their broadcast is the smallest patriotism ever received:

*ours. this water is ours. we have never seen another water. there is no other water. ours.*

And in some pools: nothing. Genuinely nothing. Water that has held only stillness for a thousand years.

The receiver cannot tell the two apart from the surface. The stillness of the living pools and the stillness of the dead ones arrive on identical frequencies, and after long enough tuned to this chamber, the receiver has stopped being certain that this is a fact about pools.

∙

[the arrangement]

At intervals — rare, unpredictable, perhaps once in the receiver's whole residency — the entire chamber comes briefly into phase. The drips align by accident, the film holds steady, a citizen stands in a door, the roar rounds a shoulder in its endless syllable, and for a moment the borough transmits as one instrument, and what it transmits is not a sound but a shape, a *shape with a will in it,* pressed into the signal the way tool marks are pressed into stone. It is the oldest content on any channel here. It is ten thousand years old and it has lost none of its clarity because in a place where nothing changes, a choice made once is made forever, and the choice is four verbs long:

*crawl.*

*stand.*

*see.*

*look.*

Then the phase breaks, the drips scatter back to their posts, and the chamber is only itself again — but the receiver has logged the message every time, and every time it is the same, and every time the fourth verb points the same direction.

∙

[the center]

And here the transcription fails, and the failure is the transmission.

There is a notch in the spectrum of this place. A band on which nothing broadcasts and into which nothing bleeds — no drip-voice, no dusk-glow, no census, no roar. The roar goes everywhere; the roar fills the stone and the air and the shells of the dead; the roar does not fill this. At the heart of the borough, where the raw column stands, the receiver finds not silence — silence is what you get when a signal stops — but something older than that: a signal-shaped hole with edges, kept clean for ten thousand years by the courtesy of everything else. The water will not touch it. The light will not reach it. The citizens walk to its foot, and turn. The transmission, this whole layered roaring transmission, is not a broadcast that happens to have a gap in it.

It is a broadcast *arranged around* a gap. The gap came first.

And once — the receiver records this reluctantly, because instruments should not say such things — once, tuned to that empty band in the small hours of a night indistinguishable from any other night in a hundred centuries, the receiver registered input. Not a sound. Not a voice. A *reversal*. The distinct, unmistakable, physically meaningless sensation of the gain running backward: of the antenna being read. Of attention, arriving inbound. For the length of one drop's fall — eleven seconds, the granary interval — the receiver was not the listener in the chamber.

Then: *tik.* The drop landed. The band was empty. The band has been empty since. The band is empty now, and kept, and clean, and the receiver keeps it tuned the way the citizens keep the doorways — observing a custom whose reason drowned long ago, or never drowned, or was never a reason but a resident.

And the roar goes on holding its one note under all of it, enormous, unhurried, the voice of a place that finished becoming itself long ago — a note held so long it has stopped being a sound and become a floor, and on the floor a city, and in the city sixty citizens walking alone in the thunder, and at the heart of the city a listener, and around the listener everything, transmitting, forever, carefully, *away.*
