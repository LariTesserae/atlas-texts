They crowd closer to the throat tonight than they usually do. The receiver has no instrument for why, and needs none.

Nothing else on the plateau walks like this — long-legged, unhurried, gaunt under wire, and following no route the receiver has ever mapped, because it does not keep routes. It keeps the seams.

It stops perhaps a kilometer off, and the footfalls cease, and the receiver knows what it is doing because it has heard it do this before: it is lying down against the rock, the whole length of its body an ear, and it is reading something in the below-sound that neither the receiver nor the grazers nor anything else on the surface can parse — some pressure-sentence assembling itself along fifty kilometers of fracture, hours from being spoken aloud.

The signal saturates. For a moment everything is exhale.

And underneath it — this is the part the receiver can barely hold, the part it has only caught twice before — underneath the staggered thunder there is one sound that is *not* staggered. One tone. Very low, very long, arriving from the whole length of the seam at once, from the buried body that fused vent to vent to vent ten thousand lifetimes ago and stopped being neighbors. The animals learn of the firing sequentially, sound by sound, the way news travels. The mat does not learn of it at all. It *feels* the front pass along its own length, forty kilometers of self, and what the receiver picks up is the sound of that: not an echo, not thunder —

a shiver. One shiver, continent-scaled, from the oldest living thing in range.

Then the rain.

Warm — blood-warm, the transcript insists on this — a thin brief rain out of a low cloud walking downwind, hissing faintly where it meets cold stone, and the whole country moving at once beneath it: the walker standing motionless in the middle of it, head down, skimming the first rich flush of the mats, which is nearly the only thing it eats; the band arriving behind it and spreading out and eating and drinking without file, without count, an hour of holiday; other bands converging from other darks, strange signatures crossing familiar ones, *dk. trrt. ka-ka. tk. t-tk.* — the census suddenly a market; spoil-cones steaming all down the seam like small chimneys; and somewhere out in a gap a long cold shape not moving at all, letting the warm rain fall on it and keeping its coal banked, because its economy does not celebrate.

The mats flush. You can *hear* it, or the receiver can — a soft wet thickening in the sound the rain makes landing, the surface doubling itself in the dark, the refinery running rich.

Then it passes.

It always passes. The cloud thins going east. The hissing stops. The pools stand warm for a while and begin, at their margins, to remember their accustomed shapes.
