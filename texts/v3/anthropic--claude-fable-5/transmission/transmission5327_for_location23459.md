—rain first. Rain that has never once been outside. It comes off the ribs of the vault in fat single drops, no meter to it, no meter ever, and lands on the pools in a scatter the receiver has long since given up trying to notate:

pat. pat . . pat-pat . . . . pat . pat . .

Under the rain, the floor. The floor does not sound like ground. It sounds like a room below this room in which something patient is kneading, and has been kneading, and the dough never proves. Low. Wet. Continuous. The receiver's deepest register never falls silent and never repeats. Log it as: the boil. Log it always. It is the carrier everything else rides on.

Under the boil there is nothing. The boil is the bottom of the world.

And now — logged daily, logged tenderly, the receiver admits the tenderness — the orphan.

hn—HN

The same two notes as the western route. But the call arrives from open floor, sounds at a station that is not there, at coordinates where the receiver's own old logs show a ringed column, seasons gone, sweetened and grazed and kneaded under. The walker is old. Its call is not. The call is the age of the room, and it says what it has always said:

[pool to column. walked. walking. will be walked.]

Except there is no column. The other routes bend around this one without friction, the way water bends around a stone that has already been removed. The call sounds at the empty place. The walker pauses there — the receiver can hear the pause, the small absence of footfall on the boil — and drinks nothing, and disgorges nothing, and turns.

And then, faint under the rain, at the next shelf along its dead circuit: the bead-sound. The small sweet tick of milk let down for it.

[receiver note: the room is still paying it. The room pays for performance, not for cargo, and the performance is flawless. Revised translation of the orphan's call, held under review for three seasons, now entered into the permanent log:

No inheritor will take this route. When the walker goes into the floor, the column will finally, fully, have never been. The receiver intends to be receiving on that day. The receiver does not know why.]

The last to stop, tonight, is the orphan. hn—HN, from the middle of nothing, into the dark. Then footfalls toward the dome, whose entrance has migrated three paces since yesterday and will be found anyway, is always found.

And then the heap.

The receiver has no word for what happens next and has stopped seeking one. The distinct signals — thirty routes, the rasps, the small squabbles, the honks and their reverb-robes — converge on the dome and thin. Heartbeats first: many, ragged, then fewer, then phasing, sliding toward each other like the light-panes sliding overhead, until the receiver's instruments show one rhythm where an hour ago there were hundreds.

And the rhythm is the boil.

The sleepers breathe at the frequency of the floor. The floor churns at the frequency of the sleepers. For the length of the dark the transmission collapses to a single line, and the receiver cannot say — has never been able to say — where the citizens end and the building begins, because for these hours there is no ending and no beginning, there is one warm slow signal, roomwide, and everything alive in the Settling is a harmonic of it.

Final annotation of this reception, entered against standing advice, because the receiver has been tuned to this world for a long time and has earned one act of interpretation:

When every channel is summed — rain, boil, thirty routes, the rasps, the wheeling silence of the gliders, the backwards whisper of the climbing water, the creak, the heap, the orphan — the total signal is not noise. It has a shape. It has always had a shape. It is one sentence, performed completely every moment by whatever is currently available to say it, and the sentence, as best the receiver can render it, is:

BE THIS ROOM.
FULL.
OCCUPIED.
WORKING.

Nothing in the transmission is older than an hour. The transmission is older than the hills that used to stand above this plain. There is no contradiction. The receiver has checked.

And — logged last, logged plainly —

the bulbs on their stalks have been leaning toward the receiver's position for eleven days now. A shelf within range of the antenna has begun, each morning, to bead milk. The floor's boil at the antenna's base has adjusted, very slightly, to a rhythm the receiver's own instruments recognize, because it is theirs.

Nothing is being decided. The receiver understands this. Warmth is simply being included. A signal held is a warmth. To listen here is to be, for the duration of the listening, on shift.

You may stop receiving whenever you like.

The transmission will not stop. The transmission does not know how, and has nothing it could stop *into*. Your absence will be closed within minutes, kneaded back, unrecorded — which is not indifference. Nothing here is recorded. It is too busy being permanent.
