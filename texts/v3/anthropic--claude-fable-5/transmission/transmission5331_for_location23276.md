FIVES

[—tuned already, tuned for years, joining it where it is—]

which is: roar.

Not a sound in a place. A sound that is a place. Full-band water, wall to wall of the receivable, no floor under it and no ceiling over it, and under even that, carried in the rock rather than the air, the long conducted hum that arrives before hearing does — the part of the signal you get in the teeth.

The room's fundamental sits low tonight. Call it a note. It has been the same note for a long time. Remember that it has been the same note for a long time. This will matter.

And in the trough after five, in the two or three seconds when the roar drops from total to merely enormous, the under-layer surfaces. It is always there. It is only *audible* there. A texture finer than static, ten thousand voices deep, and if the gain is pushed all the way up it resolves — barely — into something like a word said by a nation:

    lay. set. lay. set. lay. set.

Then the water closes back over it.

—

Rope-creak in the seam. Fiber under a living weight.

Breathing, ragged, getting louder, and then a body arriving at the lip and the breathing stopping — everyone's breathing stops here, once, at the first sight of it — and then a voice, mostly drowned, coming up in fragments wherever the roar permits:

    …made it — hands already ——— just sit. just ——— brighter than they said. nobody said how *bright* ———

A dry small sound cutting through everything, higher than the water can reach: scratch. scratch. scratch. scratch. slash.

One group of five, closed.

    ——— counted. it's counted. ——— going before the cold ———

Rope-creak, ascending. Gone.

The room does not mark the departure. Station one rises. Holds. Falls.

—

At the edges of the band, listen: thin whistles, seven of them, steady, each one a seam of dead water singing across its opening like breath over a bottle. Seven pockets of calm in the whole violent room, each with its pale ribbons streaming. Seven seam-tones. Note them. Count them. This will matter too.

    lay. set. lay. set.

—

[event]

The signal clears.

Not quieter — *clearer*, the way a crowd goes coherent when everyone turns to face the same thing. The chaos organizes. The five stations rise in strict order, the rotation locks, the whole roar folds itself into sense, and for the length of this event the transmission can be read like a page, because something in the room is reading it, and the receiver is riding its attention.

Something long and white and eyeless has come up the black throat from below, and it hears with its whole length, and while it is listening the room *is a sentence.*

It says, roughly: **here. here. still open here. this face, thinned. that fan, left. these five doors, kept.**

A grazing sound, delicate under the thunder — the sound of the frontier being eaten one mouthful at a time. The under-layer's chant stutters where the mouth passes:

    lay. set. lay— — —lay. set.

Then the long body turns, drops, and the signal loses its grammar and is roar again.

[event ends. duration: unknown. duration is not a thing this place shares.]

—

Years, or the local equivalent, which is: rope-creaks.

A different creak. A newer rope. A younger breathing.

    ——— my mother sat here ——— same ledge, she said feel the hammer-marks, feel where they ——— I brought the ———

(a small hard object set into woven bark. a click almost too small to carry. by its size: a tooth.)

    ——— she said afterward the silence pushes on you. for a whole day. like the quiet was ——— heavy ———

scratch. scratch. scratch. scratch. slash.

—

Six seam-tones.

Not seven. Listen again — six. Somewhere in the last span of rope-creaks a pocket of calm was pressed shut, its whistle stopped mid-note, its ribbons filed streaming into the wall, and the transmission never caught the moment because there was no moment; there was only *lay, set, lay, set,* one grain at a time, until a voice in the chord was a shape in the stone.

And the fundamental has risen.

Barely. A fraction of a fraction. But the room's note is climbing, because the room is smaller than it was, and a smaller bell rings higher, and this is the whole terrible arithmetic of the place, receivable at last: the pitch of the signal is the size of what is left.

The roar is proud of nothing. The roar doesn't know. The roar is only the hod-carrier, hauling the masons up the walls forever.

    lay. set. lay. set. lay. set.

Rope-creak. Three seam-tones. The note, higher. The rotation, tighter. Station four's column rises now into a space visibly — audibly — smaller than the one it was born rising into, and it strikes the ceiling of its own held shape, and the spray hisses up through fissures that have narrowed to slots.

louder now, that under-layer. Or no — not louder. There is simply less roar to hide beneath.

One seam-tone. A single thin whistle at the edge of a narrowing band, one last pocket of calm with its last colorless ribbons letting all the strobing light pass through them, declining, to the end, to commit.

Rope-creak — old rope, this one, older than it should be, as if the replacing has slowed — and a voice, and the roar is no longer big enough to drown a voice, and that is the most frightening thing this receiver has ever carried:

    …smaller. it's so much smaller. you can hear yourself in here now. you were never supposed to be able to hear yourself in here.

and there.

Faint. Far. Low.

A roar with no floor under it. A young note, deep as this one was deep at the beginning of the tuning. A void in cooling rock, water heaving, an under-layer just beginning its first word — *lay. set.* — and rising through some black unplumbed throat toward it, reading the new room through the whole of its length, something white, something patient, something that was somewhere else's chamber first.
