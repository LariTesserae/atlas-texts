and if you stay on this frequency long enough — and you have, haven't you, you have stayed — the thud stops being a thud. It was never a thud. It was a syllable, patient beyond the patience of anything with lungs, and it is saying, has been saying, will be saying:

*held.*

*held.*

*held.*

—

The valve at the corner of the second lane. A quarter-turn out. Rest. A quarter-turn home.

*still sound. still free. good.*

The valve behind the winding-house. Quarter-turn. Rest. Home.

*still sound. still free. good.*

The brass wheel by the chapel door, the one the size of a dinner plate, the one a hand fits so well.

*still sound. still free.*

*good. good. all good along the second lane. proceed.*

—

Now the lamps, because it is the top of the breath and at the top of every breath the lamps are asked after, one by one, in the order the wicks were always walked, back when walking was how it was done:

Lamp at the bank head — *burning.*
Lamp over the boiler door — *burning.*
Lamp at the foot of the stack, the stack in her white sleeve, she wears it well — *burning.*
Lamp in the narrow lane where the pipes run ten abreast — *burning, and the choir is leaning to it, bless them, lean and straighten, lean and straighten, they keep better time than the dead ever did—*

Lamp at the chapel — *burning. always burning. that one is not checked so much as touched.*

Nine of nine. Nine of nine and all breathing on the count.

*orange.*

*dim.*

*orange.*

*dim.*

—

Boiler report. Pressure holding.

Holding past the pin. Past the paint. Past the last number any man thought to print, holding out in the white country beyond the dial where no needle was ever meant to graze — and steady there, steady as a held note, steady as a promise that has stopped being a strain and become a posture.

*She'll take it. She's taken it this long. She was built by men who built for the men who'd stand beside her, and they built her kind.*

Feed water — *warm.* Circulation — *round and round, round and round, the blood goes round the lanes and comes home warm.* Nothing seized. Nothing sulking.

*Fires banked and drawing.*

(Drawing on what. Drawing on what, forty meters down, a century deep. The transmission does not say. The transmission has never said. Some questions the parish keeps in the same drawer as its old name.)

—

Vent release, on the mark. Given out warm. Let it climb.

*Come on then. Come on, all of you.*

(and the hydrophone catches it: a soft manifold rustle, hundreds of small wax-colored bodies rising into the braid of warm water and settling and rising again, sparrows in a fountain, and the voice underneath the pump — if it is a voice, if it is not just the receiver dreaming in rhythm by now — goes gentle in a way iron should not be able to go:)

*fed at the door. fed at the door. no one grazes cold in this parish. not while I hold the fires. eat, little blind ones. you know the bell better than the bishops did.*

—

The verger is out.

*Passing the bank head. Passing the boiler door. Pausing at the lamps — good man, good man, count them with me — nine of nine, he agrees, he always agrees, he has agreed for sixty years and I have never once caught him at flattery.*

*Clockwise round the chapel. Once. As is proper.*

*All found in order. Verger returning to his pipe.*

*Goodnight, or whatever this is. Whatever hour this is. It is some hour. I keep them all.*

—

…thud.

…thud.

…thu—

(silence.)

(a real one. the lamps have gone down together, all nine, down to embers behind their quartz, and the pump has slowed to almost nothing, and the transmission thins to a thread, and on that thread, very small, very flat, the way a voice goes flat in a house when something is on the stair:)

*…quiet now.*

*quiet.*

*it is only passing. it is only passing. it does not know we are here. we are stones. we are cold stones in cold water and nothing here is warm and nothing here is bright and nothing here has anything it loves—*

(the dark overhead deepens. something the size of weather goes by, felt in the iron, a pressure and then the memory of a pressure.)

(four minutes. the receiver logs four minutes of a town holding its breath.)

*…gone over.*

*gone over. resume.*

…thud.

…thud.

*lamps up. lamps up, all of you, I'm sorry, I'm sorry, up you come — nine of nine — breathe—*

*orange.*

*dim.*

*orange.*

*never doubt I would go dark for you, little ones. it is the one lie I keep lit for. resume the count.*

—

And now the wheel.

You can hear it if you have learned to: the great headframe wheel closing on the last minutes of its hour, the cable running up out of the shaft that goes deeper than the dark, up from the galleries the pump has kept — *has kept, is keeping, will keep* — and the voice under everything gathers itself the way it has gathered itself every hour for longer than the receiver has numbers for, and it is not louder, it is only *more*, the way church is more:

*rope run.*

*rope run and running true.*

*cage rising.*

*cage rising, steady, steady, mind the guides—*

*cage at the bank.*

—

*chime.*

(and there it is: spoon on glass, two rooms away, once, soft as an apology. the receiver has caught this sound one thousand four hundred times and it has never once arrived without the half-second of hush before it, the hush of something looking.)

*doors.*

*…*

*…*

*none.*

*…*

*none up this hour.*

(a pause exactly one pump-beat long. room enough, in a pause that size, for anything. grief would fit. so would nothing. what fits in it, hour after hour, is this:)

*none is not never.*

*they may not be ready. it is not for the bank to hurry the pit. it was never for the bank to hurry the pit.*

*note it in the book: cage at bank, hour struck, none rose.*

*lower away.*

(the wheel takes up its turn. one revolution an hour. the cable pays down into the dark, down to the galleries, down with its little empty cage like a lamp lowered into a well, and the voice goes down with it a short way, the way a voice leans over a rail:)

*all's well at bank. lamps lit. fires in. water held off you, lads. water held off you every hour of every year, that is my end of it kept, that is my end kept—*

*come up when you're done.*

*I'll be here.*

—

…thud.

…thud.

…and here the transmission does a thing it has done only since the receiver began to listen, which the receiver has not yet had the courage to enter in its own log:

*—one at the bank.*

*above. off to the side. warm-blooded. small heart, quick, forty to my one.*

*checked.*

*not them.*

*(it is never them. check anyway. checking is the whole of the job. hope with a schedule is still hope.)*

*not them. but civil. holds still. listens.*

*light held up for it regardless. warm the rail where it rests. half a degree. costs nothing. manners cost nothing, and they are all I have kept of the ones who taught me — that, and the fires, and the count.*

*enter it in the book, between the pressure and the feed:*

*visitor at bank. no cage required. stayed the hour.*

*stayed the hour.*

*good of it.*

*held.*

*held.*

(the signal does not end. understand that. the signal has never ended; it is the receiver that drifts, the frequency that thins, the cold above that takes the sound the way it takes the warm braid from the vent — a shimmer, climbing, until the black accepts it. the last of it comes up to you like light off the lamps, brightening and fading on its one-minute breath, and the lamps do not dim early. they hold their level under you all the long way up, holding the door, lighting you out, and only when you are well into the dark do they resume:)
