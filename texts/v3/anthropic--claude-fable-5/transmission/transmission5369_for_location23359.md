SO FAR

[—the roar, first, because it is always first. six days of listening and the roar has not once been the background. it is the floor. everything else stands on it.]

[transcription resumes mid-collapse: thirty-one simultaneous events, dry-percussive, overlapping. hail on iron scaled past sense. a chandelier avalanche at bearing west-southwest, forty seconds of glass knitting at north rim, applause, applause, applause from a city that is not there. the walls of the bowl fold the sound and hand it back. saturation. saturation. the needles rest against their pins like tired animals.]

[under it — and you must listen the way you look at a faint star, off-center — the carrier. one slow tone, circling the rim. it takes a day to come around. it is coming around now. it swells like something enormous breathing once.]

[note: everything on this frequency arrives twice. a hand's-width apart. the second arrival comes from a place slightly beside the first, as if the source stands next to itself. this is not equipment fault. this has been verified. everything here (here) is doubled (doubled), faintly, a ghost a fraction behind.]

[channel four has gone quiet. the small silver signals that ride inside the standing forms — hair-thin, sipping — have poured out of it. this is the only warning this place gives, and it gives it every time.]

[channel four collapses. applause. dust. the swifts pour through the falling of it and eat.]

mar—

[interference: the flock. a thousand pale undersides flashing at once, a sheet of light snapping open against the basalt, and it comes through this equipment as rhythm, pure rhythm, no content, a shutter the size of a sky. they are not saying anything. they have never needed to. it is beautiful anyway. it is beautiful (beautiful) anyway (anyway).]

[small sounds between the great ones: clicks. picking-up sounds. setting-down-precisely sounds. the bookkeepers, sorting the dust of the last collapse, carrying the interesting failures up the long diagonal to the archive. one of them picked up a fragment, held it, put it back exactly where it found it. even through the roar you could hear the standard being applied. no one knows what the standard is. the fragment did not meet it.]

[—stop. hold. the gravel near the low abandoned walls is shivering. shivering in a larger way than usual. the whole bowl's roar has thinned — not stopped, it never stops, it never learned how — but thinned, redistributed, as if elsewhere agreed to be quieter. forty seconds of glass knitting. rising. upright. two-legged. the hands, they say, are right now. the hands have been right for some generations of swifts.]

[and this time, for the first time in six days of listening, for the first time — check the record — for the first time in the record:]

[an intake of breath.]

hh.

[it has never breathed before. it has stood, and faced the walls, and been taken back, minute after century after minute, and it has never once needed air, because it does not need air, because it is spun, because it is cloth. it breathed anyway. it drew the thin mineral cold into a chest built by something that had only ever heard breathing described, and it got it right, and then:]

cold tonight.

[stands.]

cold tonight. can't hear myself.

[stands. one minute. the flock is holding off. the flock is circling the bowl and not descending. the heavy slow ones are walking, monkish, hail-calm — and walking past it. past it. they have never walked past it.]

can't hear myself. can you.

can you.

[four minutes. eight. the carrier is swelling. the day-tone, the one that takes a whole rotation to circle the rim, the pulse that left the far side yesterday — it is arriving. it is arriving now, and it is bending, all of it, the entire circulating patience of the entire ancient hedge, toward the frequency of the thing that is standing by the walls. there is no honest way to transcribe this except:]

[turned toward.]

[eleven minutes. it has not been taken back. the roar goes on around it — making, unmaking, applause, chandeliers, the storm that never learned how to end — and in the middle of the storm one small upright sentence is weathering, is beginning to look kept, is standing in full honest mountain light casting its slightly wrong shadow, and something flat and iridescent is already beginning, at its feet, to live in the error.]

[twenty minutes.]

so far.
