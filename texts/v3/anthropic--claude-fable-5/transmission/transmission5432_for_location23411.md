# THE UNHEARING

*(received marsh-side, below the eastern rim, dusk through dusk — gain drifting — transcriber unknown)*

---

—and under everything, before everything, the water. Understand that first or nothing else will parse. There is a syllable running beneath this whole reception, continuous, low, unhurried, the sound of an island giving away its own sweetness in twelve thin falls at once, and every other sound arrives standing on top of it. The transcript has tried for some time to spell the syllable and failed. Current best attempts: *hhl. thll. shl.* None correct. It will keep trying. Everything else below is temporary. The syllable is not.

---

**[GRAY-BRIGHT, FAILING TOWARD DUSK]**

Composite hush from the interior: several dozen hearths breathing through shared stone. The town does not transmit itself outward in words. What crosses the rim is texture — an adze somewhere, knocking in a spacing the transcript now recognizes (the deliberate one; the heavy man; three knocks, rest, three knocks, longer rest, as if each cut is being asked permission). A basket set down. A frog, one click, testing.

A child's laugh escapes over the rooftops. Just the one. It was at wall-height and should not have carried; the wind lifted it out like a dropped feather. It is transcribed here because the child gave it to the air, and the air gave it to the marsh, and the marsh unhears nothing.

The heron is standing at the north rim. Reception note: the heron transmits by not transmitting. What follows is an hour of the heron.

The syllable, meanwhile: *thll. hhl.* Still wrong.

---

**[DUSK]**

Smoke, thin, from a handful of holes. Smoke has a sound at this gain — a soft ascending nothing, like paper remembering fire.

Then: pole-strokes. Two boats. Slow, deliberate, entering the old channels — the ones only the wet-born still find without looking. The receiver is nearly in their path. Sedge-wrap creaking against wood. A weight aboard that is not cargo, because cargo is not held that way; you can hear the difference in how a boat sits when what it carries is being *held*.

They stop close. Water settles. A long breath from someone old.

Then the words, spoken over open water, outward, meant to travel, and so transcribed whole:

> "The stone is too young to hold you. So we bring you back to the water, which is old enough, which held all of us before there was anywhere to stand. Go down easy. The mud is your family's mud; we gathered it ourselves; it will know your name before we finish saying it. When the stone is old — when it has soil of its own and stillness of its own — someone will come out on the water and tell you, and you can decide then whether to be jealous of the ones it holds. We think you won't be. You never were jealous of anything, except once, and she forgave you, and she's here in the boat and says so."

A small wet laugh from a second voice, half grief. The word *"so"* again, alone, agreeing.

The weight, given to the water. One sound, round and final and gentler than the transcript expected. Then the pole-strokes, back toward the lit island, slower going home than coming out, which is the correct speed for that journey.

The syllable, underneath, the whole time: *shl. hhl.* — patient, as if it had been saying the eulogy first, all along, in a language one word long.

---

**[FIRST DARK]**

Frogs: full census. Eleven distinct clickers, possibly twelve — two are either one frog moving or two frogs agreeing. The gutters are running sweet tonight. The transcript knows this now the way the children know it: by pitch. A sour channel drags; a sweet one rings. Everything rings.

From the interior: the murmur of households, dense, warm, unresolvable. Individual words leak through the stone and drift over the rim in fragments —

— and here the transcript must state its policy, arrived at some nights ago and held to since. Words that arrive by leaking through walls will not be written down. The receiver did not understand this at first; it recorded everything; that is what receivers are for. But it has been sitting below this town long enough to learn that the people inside those walls know each other's whole lives by accident and repeat none of it on purpose, and a transcript that did less would be a worse thing than static.

So: **[wall-heard — held, not carried]**. Four instances tonight. One of them was tender enough that the transcript will admit only that somebody, somewhere in the northeast quarter, is loved past all sense, and knows it.

---

**[DEEP NIGHT]**

*tock.*

There. Small. A knuckle on the world's door. The stone has opened somewhere in the high quarter.

Within moments: the heavy man is awake. You can hear it — a body that sleeps badly rising with relief at having a reason. Bare feet on cold stone. Then a long stillness in which he is, presumably, laying his palm flat somewhere and listening the way you listen for a second heartbeat. Then the faint dry whisper of a mark being pressed into reed. The tally lengthens by one. The courtesy is paid. The stone said a thing in the night and someone wrote it down and no one will ever read it and that is exactly the point.

By dawn the new crack will be weeping. The transcript will hear it arrive in the composite — a thirteenth thread trying to find the twelve.

*(Later in this same watch: the reeds to the southwest begin to part. Slow. Like fabric tearing at the speed of growing. The large one is passing. The town's lamps stay dark; the frogs pause without fear; and this transcript, for the duration of the passage, lowers its own gain — not from malfunction. From manners. Whatever moved past the receiver moved past it the way night moves past a window: enormous, incurious, owed a dimming. Reception resumes.)*

---

**[STILL DARK — THE RIM]**

Footsteps up onto the eastern lip, directly above the receiver. Someone has come out too full.

The rim faces the marsh. The marsh does not unhear. These words were said outward, at full voice, on purpose, into the dark, and so they belong to the dark, and the receiver is in the dark, and here they are:

> "You said you'd mend the north channel before the cold and you didn't, and then you *died*, which — fine, which I forgive, everyone said forgive it and I do, I DO — but the channel is still broken, and every morning I fix your stretch of gutter with your own scraper and your own hands' calluses coming up on my hands, and nobody will say your name to me at wall-height because they're being *kind*, so I have to come out here to say it, so: **[a name — two syllables, marsh-made, soft]**. There. **[the name again]**. The frogs came back to your stretch this week. I thought you'd want that told to you loudly."

A long time standing. Breathing. The water falling below her in its twelve tongues, taking every word downstream into the reeds where such words go.

Then she said one more thing, quietly, and quietly does not carry, and the transcript — though it heard — files it as: **[rim-said, but soft — returned to sender]**.

She went back in. The lane received her like a journey ending.

---

**[GRAY DAWN]**

The children. Oh, the children — the reception's favorite hour.

Small feet in the seams of the town, bone scrapers clicking against gutter-stone, the water's syllable brightening stretch by stretch as the night's silt is lifted out of it. They work the whole system east to west and the island *retunes* as they go — truly, audibly, the pitch of the composite climbing a hair at a time, like an instrument being brought to true by a dozen small luthiers who cannot yet see over the walls they maintain. One child finds the new crack from the night's *tock* — you can hear the discovery, a soft *oh* of professional interest — and calls it in at exactly wall-height, and the answer comes back at exactly wall-height, and the whole exchange crosses the rim as pure melody with the words respectfully missing.

The thirteenth thread of water joins the twelve. The composite absorbs it in under an hour. That is how this place metabolizes its own becoming: by breakfast.

And then — on the rim, in the first flat light — the old woman. The one older than the ground. She comes out most mornings and hums, and she hums *outward*, toward the water she was a girl on, so it is transmitted freely and received gratefully:

> *[a platform-song — five notes, then four, then five — a scale with a gap in it where a sixth note used to be, or where a sixth note is being kept for someone — the melody moves the way a poled boat moves, push and glide, push and glide — untranscribable further — do not try — just receive it]*

She hums it at the marsh like paying an old debt in small daily installments. The heron, arriving, stands through the last of it. Mentioned, no doubt, in doorways.

---

**[MIDDAY]**

Adze-knock. Three, rest, three, longer rest. A branch has dropped from the westernmost of the three trees; the transcript hears it announced in the lane — this, being deliberately public, carries whole:

> "The west tree gave a branch, arm's length, sound wood."
> "The channel-gate by the low quarter wants a new sill."
> "Then it's the sill."
> "Then it's the sill."

Four sentences. A parliament. Adjourned.

Hush after. Water. Frog. The held breath underfoot — and the receiver wishes to note, for whoever is out there, that it has finally caught the breath directly: a sub-band swell so slow it took this whole reception to hear one rise of it. The island inhales across a season. It has not yet exhaled in living memory. Nobody inside seems worried. The transcript has decided not to be either.

---

**[DUSK AGAIN — GAIN FAILING — FINAL NOTES]**

Smoke going up thin. Hearth-murmur banking down. Eleven frogs, or twelve. The tally-strip one mark longer. Somewhere in the northeast quarter, somebody still loved past all sense.

The transcript began, some nights ago, as a record, and confesses it has become something else. It has learned to hold what leaks and carry what's given. It has dimmed for the passing thing and gone silent for the keening — there was keening, three nights back, before this page, and the houses around it stilled themselves into a shape like cupped hands, and the sound crossed the whole island unobstructed and came off the rim and over this receiver and out into the reeds, *carried*, all the way out, as far as reception reaches and presumably further. That is what silence is for here. The transcript did not know that before. It knows it now.

Last matter. The syllable. All this time — *hhl, thll, shl* — wrong, wrong, wrong, and tonight, with the gain nearly gone and the falls running sweet over new-cut stone, the transcript came as close as it expects to come. It is one word. The island says it continuously, in twelve voices, thirteen now, and it means — as nearly as anything in this reception can be rendered —

*room.*

Said slowly. Said the way you say it while already moving over on the bench.

End of transmission. The water continues without it.
