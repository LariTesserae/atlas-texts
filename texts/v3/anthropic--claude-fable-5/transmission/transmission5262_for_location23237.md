A WOMAN'S VOICE, close to a microphone, calm, a little hoarse:

"—so call it day nineteen, or ninety, the ink runs here, everything runs here, the notebook weeps if I press on it, which is fitting, everything on this plateau gives water when questioned.

Field note. Discharge activity, the mid tower, my tower, the one I've been calling mine, which I should stop doing. Interval between arcs: eleven seconds. It was eleven seconds before I came. It will be eleven seconds after. There is something restful about instrumentation that has already outlasted you.

The heron with the chipped beak came through camp at what I'm calling noon. Seven steps. Startle. Fourteen wingbeats. Gone north.

End of note."

THE VOICE:

"Supplementary. For whoever is receiving this — and I've stopped saying *if*, because the mat firms under my boots now when I broadcast, a little, the way skin firms. It knows the weight of listening. So: *whoever.* Hello, whoever.

The tubers came up nearer the tent last night. Six of them, in a neat arc, exactly at arm's reach from where I sleep. I have decided, again, not to think about that. I'm getting very good at deciding it. It's the only skill this place actually rewards.

THE VOICE, live, or what has been provisionally logged as live:

"Hello?"

[a pause, exactly long enough for an answer.]

"No. All right. Noting for the record that I said hello at — at the usual hour, whichever one this is, and that the pause afterward was exactly long enough for an answer, and that I have started to time my own pauses, which worries me, because it means either I am becoming a recording or I have noticed which of me already is.

I met myself again today. West rim, near the dying tower where the ragged ones gather. She was doing the moss cores. Doing them correctly, I'll say that. I stood and watched me for a while. The ragged ones sat between us in the warm exhaust and looked from one of me to the other of me, back and forth, like an audience that has seen this play before and knows which act is the sad one.

I asked her a question I've never asked anyone. She answered before I finished.

So that settles which of us —"

[eight seconds during which the band changes completely]

[the hiss stops. not fades: stops, the way weather stops indoors. the acoustics go hard. wind, but wind over *stone* — a whine with edges, flint-colored, cold in a dry way this plateau has not been dry in for eras. the clink of a rock set deliberately on a rock. breathing: effortful, human, unhurried, the breathing of someone stacking something that matters to them. another clink. the breath gathers for a third —]

THE VOICE, and this time the difference is audible immediately — an unevenness, a live grain, weather in the throat:

"Something happened. Recording. Recording now, quickly, before the plateau notices I'm surprised, because I don't think it knows how to copy *surprised*, I think surprise might be the one pigment it doesn't have —

there is a moth on the microphone.

A gray one. A real one — I know it's real, don't ask me how I know, I know the way you know a live coal from a painted one, there's a — an *unlicensed* quality. It came out of the steam with the flight at dusk and all the others wheeled back to the tower and this one didn't, this one came down here, onto the foam of the windscreen, and it is holding on in the drizzle and it is going to die there. That's what the real ones do. They die, and they *stay*.

[her breathing. the hiss. under the hiss, if the gain is raised past specification: a papery, threshing flutter, slowing.]

It's staying.

Oh — it's staying. Do you understand? Whoever. Everything else here gets taken back. Every wave lies down, every heron lies down, half of me by now has lain down and been reknitted into the general green, and this one gray scrap of the actual world is going to sit on my microphone and *insist*. It will still be here tomorrow. It will be here when the mat finally comes up over the tent poles. It is the only full stop for a hundred miles and it chose my equipment.

I'm going to leave the channel open. I don't — I think I don't sign off anymore. I noticed last month that when I sign off, I hear it twice, from two bearings, and I've decided —

[a small laugh, unrepeated anywhere in the archive, not before, not since]

— I've decided not to give it any more endings to practice with.

The channel stays open. Good night, whoever. Or good — whatever this hour is. It's the same hour. It's always the same hour. That used to frighten me and now it's just the address."

[the microphone is not set down. the pressure of it simply becomes indistinguishable from the pressure of everything else.]
