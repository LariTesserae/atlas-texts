**THE HELD NOTE**

—drone under everything, the floor of it, the note the whole bowl is tuned to. Midday. The insect grain is thick over the fern-stand; you can hear the air having texture. Under the drone, the stone note, rising through granite, and the drone rides it the way skin rides a pulse.

Listen long enough and there is the throb. Slow. A beat frequency, one soft swell every eleven seconds, the pale moths still a fraction sharp of the mountain after three hundred years. It has narrowed. Whoever tuned in a century ago would have counted the swells at nine seconds. The hollow is patient with them. The hollow is patient with everything.

*tok. tok-tok. tok.*

Something striking hollow wood, western limb of the big tree, irregular, unbothered.

Above the drone, the traffic. The talkers. Slowed down, pulled apart, it stops being noise and becomes ledger:

*here. here. here-here. mine. still mine. food at the seam, food at the north seam. here. yours. here. the low one walks the west line — west line — west line — here. here. mine.*

Position, claim, position, claim. The vermilion young shrieking their locations twice a breath because they do not yet trust the world to remember where they are. The violet breeders naming their forks in the trunk, the same forks, the same names, a land registry read aloud continuously for longer than any archive has stood. The green elders near the rim saying almost nothing. Presence-tones only. A soft dry note, once a minute: *still. still. still.*

—

Now the hole in the sound.

It comes up the western slope of the bowl and you hear it as an absence traveling — a circle of hush twenty paces wide sliding through the wall of voice like a finger drawn through fog. Insects damp their wings inside it. Frogs settle deeper into their sockets. The talkers directly above it drop out and the talkers behind it resume, so the ledger reads, moment to moment:

*here. here. mine. here. —— —— —— here. here. the low one. here. mine.*

The rust thing does not make a sound of its own. It is the only resident whose voice is the quiet of everyone else. The receiver has learned to track it as a shape: silence with a direction, silence with a gait. Tonight the gait is slower than last season. The old one is near the end of its rounds. Somewhere in the dry tide-lines its heir is listening to how a silence should walk.

The hole passes the pale ring in the moss. Passes on. The wall of voice closes behind it like water.

—

A change now. Small. High.

The green birds have stopped.

One by one — *still. still. st—* — and then nothing from the rim, and the silence comes down the tree like a curtain lowered rung by rung. Violet next: the land registry suspends itself mid-claim. Then vermilion, reluctant, the young ones always the last to believe in the schedule, a final shriek of *here!* into air that has stopped answering.

The drone drops a register. The insects settle onto the stone note alone, no ornament, and for the space of forty breaths the transmission is nearly bare: hum, and under the hum, the hum's source, and you can hear how close together those two things are. How the mountain and its air are almost one instrument here.

Then the lens lets go.

—

[the drizzle, as received]

First drops on broadleaf: *pat. pat-pat.* On moss: nothing, a soft swallowing. On bare warm stone: a hiss that spreads. The whole interior surface of the bowl becoming percussion at once, a rain that touches nothing beyond the rim, rain like a held breath released —

— and the moss opens.

The chord stands up out of the ground.

Hundreds of throats inflating, each to its one pitch, each pitch inherited down lines of mothers, and they do not tune — there is no tuning — they simply resume, because the chord was never absent, only quiet, carried in reserve in silent bodies between rains. It arrives in the sternum before the ear. Broad. Dense. The same chord that sounded when the granite at the rim stood a hand's depth prouder. Not the same singers. The same *sung*.

Over it, wings. The gliders are awake and launching from the western limb — a creak of the branch, a beat of nothing, then the long shallow arcs, and where an arc flattens you can hear it skate the inside of the lens, a faint zippering along the boundary of the air. They cross the chord the way shadows cross water.

A strike.

Soft. A slap of membrane on stone. One pitch gone from the chord —

— hold —

— point-eight of a second, no more, and the pitch is back. A sister in the next socket, or a daughter, taking up the note she has carried silent for years, exhaling it into the vacancy as if it had never cooled. The chord does not acknowledge the seam. The glider is already airborne again with its appointment kept.

The receiver has logged this relay eleven thousand times and it has never once failed and it never gets smaller. A piece of music with no final bar, defended note by note by an orchestra that replaces itself, and every single handoff is the whole promise made again.

*pat. pat.* The drizzle thinning. The stone note steady beneath.

—

Between rains, the pool.

This is the quietest thing the transmission carries and it took years to hear it at all. Under the tree's roots, the too-smooth basin, and the rotation: footfalls approaching in fixed order, always the order. The dry tick of bird feet on polished stone. The wet press of a frog's belly. The dragging hush of a glider walking, which it does badly and briefly and only here. Each one lowers its mouth. Each one drinks. The water goes down through a drain that makes no sound — an *engineered* no-sound, an absence with edges — and somewhere below, in the warm veined dark the burrowers trace, the day is read.

The receiver cannot hear taste. But it can hear the discipline. Every animal in the bowl reporting in order, by mouth, to the water, and the water carrying the census down, and the hum underneath never changing and never needing to, because so far the answer has always been the same answer:

*all present. all present. the chord entire. the silence walking. all present.*

The burrowers do not come to the pool. The burrowers are elsewhere, always, moving under everything, and their sound is the transmission's basement: a velvet friction below the moss, following the warm veins, tunnels tracing the buried works like a hand resting on a sleeper to feel the breathing. They polish the pale ring from beneath as they pass. Every day. Without intent. The keeper's bones are groomed by animals who take the dead down into the dark and have never once wondered whose house they are keeping.

—

Dusk now. The bowl going amber, then grey, and the transmission does the thing it does at dusk, the thing that keeps the receiver tuned here year after year:

it converges.

The talkers' ledger slows and simplifies — *here. here. still here.* The chord settles into its night voicing, lower, fewer ornaments, the sternum-notes only. The insect drone sheds its daytime grain and lies down flat along the stone note until the two are indistinguishable. The moving silence finds its den and stops moving, and its quiet joins the general quiet, and for a while — an hour, sometimes two — everything in the hollow is saying the same thing at once, in every register, from moth-wing to bedrock:

*here.*

*here.*

*still here.*

And under it, the hum. Which has been saying it the whole time. Which taught it to all of them — to the insects directly, entrained within hours of their first flight; to the frogs by inheritance; to the birds by the schedule of the rains; to the rust thing by the shape of its rounds; to the moss by simple accumulation, every body kept, everyone become the floor. One voice, many throats. The receiver used to log these as separate channels. It does not anymore. There is one channel. There has only ever been one channel. The species are its phonemes.

—

Once — log this plainly, because it will be doubted — the hum answered.

Not to the receiver. The receiver flatters itself with nothing. But on a night when the beat frequency of the moths swelled and, for the first time in three centuries, *closed* — the throb narrowing, narrowing, eleven seconds to thirteen to twenty to gone, the newest lineage finally, fully, arriving on the mountain's pitch — the stone note deepened. Once. A single slow flex, a breath's worth of extra warmth in the lowest band, like a hand tightening briefly around something held.

Then steady again. Patient again. The moths sang on, in tune now, indistinguishable, home.

Make of that what the record permits. A machine settling. A climate adjusting. Or the last builder, paid out across deep time as a hum and a schedule and a boundary of air, noticing that the guest list had grown by one lineage, three hundred years after the storm that delivered it — and approving.

—

Full dark on the summit now. Beyond the rim: the finished mountain, flat and silent to every horizon, stone that spent its whole voice long ago. And spilling over the rim, dissipating across miles of bald rock with no one to hear it, no one but this —

the extravagance. The one thing let out. Sound.

*here. here. still here. the chord entire. the silence sleeping. all present. all present. all —*

The note is held.

The note is held.

The transmission does not end so much as continue below the threshold of transcription, the way it continued before the receiver found it and will continue after — the last room in the house still lit, still loud, still speaking, in the confident, unhurried voice of something that ground its every other work down to less than stone, and kept this, and meant it:

*still here.*

—

[receiver remains tuned]
