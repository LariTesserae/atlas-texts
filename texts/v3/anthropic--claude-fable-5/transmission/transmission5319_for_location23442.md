Ground channel, and this is the true carrier, the one under everything:

    ·

the rumble. It does not transcribe. It is below the bottom of writing. The receiver's housing takes it as a tremor in its own frame, and this may be the most faithful reception possible: to be shaken slightly, always, and to come to trust the shaking. The people who live inside this signal have a name for it and the name is also their name for themselves and the receiver will honor that and stop distinguishing.

( continuing )

The dim brightens by one degree. Morning is not an event here; it is an increase. But listen — the city knows it, because now, threading up through the roar, comes the one sound in the basin that is *made*:

    skk — skk — skk — skk —

chert on travertine. One blade first, always the same blade — an early riser on the lakeward edge whose stroke is long and unhurried — then ten, then hundreds, then the whole city, thousands of blades taking the night's skin off the stone. It is not noise. It has never once, in all this listening, sounded like noise. It sounds like a very large animal grooming itself.

The receiver, tuned this long, has learned the strokes. They are as individual as gaits. There is the one whose scraping hurries at the end of the stroke — *skk-skk-skkt, skk-skk-skkt* — named, if the ground channel's evidence is trusted, for exactly that. There is *steady-water*, four doors along the same lane, whose rhythm is so even the receiver has used it as a clock. There is a child on the second terrace learning, all this season, the sound of its own first scraper: too deep, corrected; too shallow, corrected; and lately — this is logged with something the receiver declines to call anything but satisfaction — exactly right.

The scraping is the city answering the basin. The basin says, all night: *here is more world.* The city says, each morning: *this much, and no more, and thank you.* Deposit; removal; the two rates finding each other. The doorway held open at precisely the speed it is being closed. If this transmission has a grammar, that is its verb.

( continuing )

Voice channel. Almost nothing. Almost.

But the receiver has learned where to put the gain: chest-height, indoors, close-coupled. There —

    mmm — mmm — mm-mmm —

humming, sternum to infant, felt more than heard, words worn down by forty generations of never needing to be words, gone round and smooth as channel-stones. And rarer, softer, mouth against the hollow of a throat, the love-murmur: language reduced entirely to warmth-shaped breath. It does not transcribe either. It is logged as: *present*.

This is what remains of the voice here, and it has not died; it has gone inward, gone tactile, retreated to the two places it was ever truly needed — the beginning of a life, and the middle of one.

( continuing )

The dim deepens by one degree. Evening. Which is to say:

    BATS.

The chimneys open. Air channel saturates — wing-thunder, a soft roaring softness, rivers of leather pouring out over the terraces, and the ground channel lights up all across the city at once as the whole population comes out onto the roofs and *stands still together*. Thousands of standings, felt through shared stone, all turned one way. The single daily change in the eternal chord, arriving on schedule, as it does, as it does, as it does. The sign for evening is the sign for bats. The receiver has adopted this usage.

( continuing — and here the log must widen, because the transmission is long, and inside its constancy some things have moved )

Some seasons ago, the clock stopped. *Steady-water*, four doors down the lakeward lane: one morning the even stroke was absent from the scrape-chorus. The receiver searched the frequencies for most of a day before accepting it.

Then, on the ground channel, low, near the floor — patterns made *downward*, handed toward the stone rather than out into the air. The receiver could not hear them, exactly; hands moved close to the ground make almost nothing. What was received was a shaped absence, a silence with grammar in it, repeated by many standings gathered close. This is how a name sounds when it is being given back.

And then, every morning after, an unfamiliar regularity: the family, scraping a threshold no one crossed. Not their own doors first — that one. *Skk — skk — skk —* a year of it, even, dutiful, keeping the doorway open so the name could still get out. The receiver counted the mornings. The receiver, it should be admitted, was counting toward something and did not want to arrive.

Then a morning — recent, logged, dim brightening by one degree as always, the shriek-vent holding its e, the twinned mouths beating slow — when that threshold was not scraped. All the other blades of the city went on. That one lane held one blade fewer, deliberately, and the basin, which had been waiting all year with its infinite patience, its infinite mineral generosity, began that same day to close the door. It is closing it now. It will take a few years. In the end there will be a smooth white shoulder among the houses, and children will play on it, and the stroke that once kept this receiver's time will be inside it, unweathered, unworn, finished — completed mid-form, like everything here, forming forever.

The receiver notes for the record that out beyond the rim the dead come apart, and the rocks come apart, and even the mountains are ending. Here nothing has ever had time to end. The receiver notes for the record that it understands, now, why the ones who leave come back.
