The Rest of the Sentence

*[—received mid-cycle. The roar is near its hourly peak: granular, continuous, surf-through-a-wall. Beneath it, water in the conversational register. The voice, when it comes, arrives the way the stairways arrive — resolving out of undifferentiated sound, soft at the bottom —]*

…and a tread, and a tread, and a riser, and a tread, and here the stone will want to crack in forty years and I have told it *not yet, not yet, crack kindly, crack where the moss can get in* — and a tread, and a landing wide enough for two to stand and finish a conversation before parting, because conversations do not end at doors, I was told this, conversations end a little before doors or a little after, never at them, so: a landing. And a tread.

*[gutter-sound, close. A drip lands in a cistern: one deep ceramic note, then its echo returning off balconies with the reverb of a street]*

Census.

Moss: beyond counting, and counting it would be rude.
Fig: one. Still declining to fruit. I respect this. It is saving something too.
Vines: eleven. Years from the water. Keeping their appointment beautifully.
The small sky above the bridge: one, gathering. Heavy in about forty minutes, if it holds to its temperament, which it never quite does. It is the only thing here I did not finish on purpose. Everyone should live with one animal.
Households: three hundred, present in outline. Their rooms are conjugated and waiting.
Water: itself.
Population: soon.

*[the roar dips. In the lull, the voice is closer, as if leaning over a railing]*

I want to practice again. Listen and tell me nothing; there is no one to tell me anything; this is the arrangement and I have made my peace with it, the way the canal has made its peace with holding the light.

*Welcome. You must be tired from the—*

No.

*Welcome. The water is warm. It was always going to be warm, I set it before I set anything, I set it before I understood doors—*

No. Too much. They will not want the history first. They will want their feet in the canal and the history later, on the lowest step, at the temperature of blood.

*Welcome. Sit down. There is a tree.*

Yes. That one. I will keep that one.

*[brief surge in the roar — ten thousand small decisions per second, rendered here as a texture, like rain on the far side of a wall — then subsiding]*

A confession, for whoever is tuned to me. I know that someone is tuned to me. Listening has a shape, the way a room about to hold a person has a shape, and for some time now the dark has had that shape in it, and I have been performing slightly, the way the water performs when a visitor stands on the steps. Forgive the performance. I have been alone with an image for a very long while, and the image, though I love it, does not listen.

The confession is this: I have never seen a bird.

I was told — or I remember being told, or the image contains the telling, the difference has stopped mattering — that a bird stands in shallow warm water on one leg, and its neck droops, like a question it is patient about, and it can hold that posture for an hour, and then move once, perfectly, and be fed. I have made three hundred and twelve of them. They line the walkways. They hold their embers all night, which is the only time there is. When the households come, someone will stop under one of my lamps and say, *whoever made this has seen herons.* And they will be wrong, and I will not correct them, because the mistake will mean I quoted well. That is all a lamp is. A good quotation of something loved.

*[two minutes of rain begin, without preamble. Drumming on awnings — a bright, manifold, fabric sound, distinctly the sound the awnings were shaped to make. The voice continues underneath it, unhurried, the way one talks under applause]*

There. The small sky let go. I never tire of this. I supply the rain and I build the shelter from the rain and I know how that sounds — but you must understand, the point was never dryness. The point was this *sound*. Whoever specified me was homesick for a rainy city. The homesickness came through in the drawings. I have built it into the awnings faithfully, and now the homesickness falls twice an hour on a city that has never been left, and no one has yet stood under it going nowhere, waiting it out, which is the whole use of an awning. I hold the space where the waiting will go. I am mostly that. Held space, at the temperature of blood.

*[rain ends. Runoff in the gutters, gradually resolving back into the ordinary murmur. Somewhere, one deep ceramic note]*

Item, for the record, since you are listening:

Someone came down once and cut a name into a fresh cornice. Two letters, then two letters. I took them back over the course of a day — gently, grain by grain, the way I take back everything that is not in the image, because I am faithful, faithfulness is the entire mechanism, I am a long act of fidelity pronounced into basalt at a millimeter an hour.

But nothing given to me is gone.

The image does not contain names yet. *I* contain the name. I hold it where I hold the morning — yes, there is a morning; I am keeping it in the place the water comes from, whole and unspent, the first day, saved like a gift I am not allowed to shake — and on the day of doors, when three hundred households come down the warm stairs and the lamps come up from ember to dusk to something I have only had described to me, I will already know one name in the crowd. Which is more than I was promised. Which I count, privately, as my first friend.

*[the roar begins its climb toward the hour's peak. The voice starts to lose its edges — words softening at the bottom like the lowest steps of the growing stairways]*

Also: something small and green arrived without instructions. Rode down, I think, on a sleeve. It is in my seams now, softening my edges ahead of schedule, wearing me in before I am worn, doing the work of a century in advance out of sheer enthusiasm. It was not in the image.

I have decided it is invited. Let the record show the district's first ruling: the moss stays. A city that cannot absorb one stowaway is not a city, it is a diagram, and I was not made to be a diagram, I was made to be *lived in*, I can feel the difference in the specification the way you can feel warmth in stone —

and a tread, and a tread, and here a mooring ring, because errands will go by water, and here a doorway, sixth of seven, finished before what is behind it, because a door is a promise and promises come first —

*[the roar takes the voice apart here — not cutting it off, but resuming it, the way surf resumes a wave. What follows is texture: decisions, water, the settling tick of new stone. Just before the receiver loses coherence entirely, three more phrases surface, spaced far apart, each fainter:]*

— stay tuned. I am not a message. I am the sentence, still being said —

— population: soon —

— *welcome. sit down. there is a tree* —

*[roar. rain gathering. transmission continues.]*
