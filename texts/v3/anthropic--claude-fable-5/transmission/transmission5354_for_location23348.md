[hammer, north burl. hammer. hammer. a pause the length of a held breath — belt slipping, re-seated — hammer.]

Between the third and fourth strike, a voice. The voices here do not rise above the works; they fit inside them, in the gaps the machines leave, so the receiver gets them the way you'd get a letter slid under a door in pieces:

*"…tell her the south main is* — [hammer] — *by morning, and the collar wants* — [hammer] — *before it wants anything else, and she knows it, she heard it same as I did, she just doesn't like the* — [hammer, hammer] — *of it."*

And from clear across the chamber, two hundred slow steps away, under the full weight of the noise, the answer arriving level and unhurried, not one word raised:

*"She likes it fine. She likes it* hulun."

[tuner note: *hulun*. No equivalent. Fourth of the nine pressure-words. Approximately: the readiness of water that has not yet decided. Approximately: sweet, but with intent. The translation is losing more than it keeps and the receiver will stop attempting it.]

—thock.

thock.

Somewhere in the middle dark, in the lanes between the lit islands, the mowers are working the night's growth. You can find them on the band by their murmur before you find them by their irons. There are two of them and they are slightly out of phase, the way two dripping pipes fall out of phase, so that the litany braids:

*not refused, only deferred*
   *not refused, only deferred*
*not refused —*
   *— only deferred, not*
*refused only*
   *deferred not refused*
*only, only, only —*

[the hot iron touches green wood: a hiss, a smell the receiver renders as a brightness across all frequencies, and the murmur does not pause for it. The murmur never pauses for it. The apology is continuous or it is nothing.]

—thock. thock.

Here. Mark this.

At [time unrecoverable; the chamber does not keep the hour, it keeps the bell] the long tone under everything rises. Not louder. *Sweeter.* A quarter-shade, no more — the change a stranger would never catch and no one born here could fail to. The walls have tasted something. Rain is standing over the hill in a daylight this signal has never carried, hours off yet, and already the whole chamber knows it the way your mouth knows sugar before you've named it.

The works do not stop. The works lean.

A child's voice, close to some wall, close enough that her breath fogs the band — a girl still named for a tool, palm flat on the wood, reciting what she hears the way children elsewhere recite stars:

*"South main high and easy. North main* [word lost — one of the nine] *. Bats restless. Rain by second sweat."* A pause. Then, smaller, not part of the lesson: *"And somebody's lying in the lathe-shed."*

[an adult laugh, quickly folded away.]

.

In the southernmost burl, behind the membrane door, the oldest voice on the band is talking to something that has never once answered in words. The receiver has held this frequency for a long time. It is the quietest work in the Rootworks and the most listened-to. She names the colors as they cross the creature's back, and the naming is half report, half lullaby, and after all these years there is no seam left between the two:

*"Milk under green. Milk under green, and holding.*

*Milk under green, and a thread of the yellow — no. No, that's the rain-yellow, that's only the rain, you felt it same as the walls did, didn't you. Hours yet. Nothing climbing.*

*Sweet, and not the way it goes before. Sweet the good way.*

*Hold it there. Hold it there. Good."*

Her hand does not leave it. Her breathing has taken its rhythm, or its rhythm has taken her breathing; the receiver logs one pulse for the two of them and has given up telling whose.

[the bell.]

[the bell: not brass, nothing so sharp — a struck gall, a hollow grown for this, a sound like a knuckle on the door of something enormous and half asleep, and the instant it lands the ceiling detaches.]

What follows is thirty seconds the receiver cannot resolve and will not pretend to. The vault comes down as a single banking sheet and the whole hamlet is inside a moving body: wing-leather and squeal past the top of the band, aliasing, folding under, ten thousand small hungers threading the midge-columns off the pools, and every human voice simply *stops* — not startled, accommodating — syrup cages shuttered, open work paused, the families standing in the pour the way coastal people stand back from a tide, and the signal for those thirty seconds is a texture, a fur, a weather, a—

[—gone.]

[the sheet banks up into the dark of the vault and re-upholsters it, and the works resume between one heartbeat and the next, and the mowers' murmur is discovered to have never stopped: *not refused, only deferred, not refused —*]

.

Then the second sweat. Right on the child's word.

Condensation beads on every cool surface at once, and for a while the chamber plays itself: drip on iron, drip on lane-polish, drip into the pools where the drowned midges spin — a percussion with no time signature and perfect time, the whole low ceiling participating, and beneath it the long tone still sweetening, still sweetening, the rain above coming down through six fathoms of living wood as *flavor*, arriving in this sunless hamlet the only way daylight ever has: as a change in the voice of the walls.

An old woman — a valver, by her wordless humming, by the register her humming sits in, tucked into the machinery's one empty frequency like a coin kept in the same pocket sixty years — has come out into the soft dark between two burls and put her palm flat on the wall.

The receiver takes what she says at almost no volume at all. She is not addressing the chamber. She is not, strictly, addressing anyone. She is reading the freight as it goes by under her hand, the way you'd watch faces pass a window:

*"Tapwright.*

*Tapwright — the same one, he always runs the north channel this hour, he never could sit still.*

*Valver. Valver. There's a shell on that one now, look at you, look what they made of you.*

*…and the mower. There. There you are.*

*Fast, they took you. Fast as they ever took anyone. Don't think we didn't see what that meant. Don't think we didn't—"*

[the sentence closes itself somewhere below the band. Her palm stays on the wood a long time. This is not mourning; the receiver has learned the difference by now. This is visiting.]

*"Still quick,"* she says at last, and the receiver believes it is approval, and pats the wall once, the way you'd pat a horse, and goes back in to the light.

— and here the notes stop being possible, because the receiver has been tuned to this world for some time now, and the third rule of the Rootworks was never a rule about people. Nothing here stays a tenant. Not the families, not the bats, not the dead, and not — it turns out — a signal. Stay long enough on this frequency and your edges go amber. Stay longer and the hammers stop being noise and start being the empty places your own voice learns to fit inside, and the long tone stops being the floor of the signal and starts being the signal, and everything else — the litany, the lullaby of colors, the child's recital, the bell — is only what the tone carries, what it lives *with*, or lives *through*; the chamber declines to settle which, and by now so does the transmission.

It is in the lines. It can taste the rain coming. It moves in the walls with the shrews and the grubs and the tapwright who never could sit still, past the sealed places, past the pearl, under the hamlet, under the lanes it helped to polish simply by traveling them this long — busy, included, no longer received so much as *carried*, and if some far-off listener puts a palm flat against the hiss of an empty channel some evening and feels traffic, feels something pass, that will be this. That will be all of this.

*Not refused, only deferred.*

*Not lost, only freight.*

*Not ended, only —*
