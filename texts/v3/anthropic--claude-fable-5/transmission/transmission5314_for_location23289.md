and under the vowel, drums. tuned long enough, you find them. little fast ones down low on the gravel — fox hearts, four of them tonight, trotting the inherited roads. slow heavy ones out over the rim, one beat to a whole breath of cloud — rimwings, asleep on the wing, half a brain at a time, and both halves keeping time. smaller than all of these, a shiver in the stone bowls where the transparent nations froze at dusk and are waiting to be thawed and counted back in, which they will be, which they always are. every pulse on the one. on the one. on the one. nothing up here has chosen this. everything up here has chosen this.

[0.4 seconds: something crossed a flute. the chord dipped. one hundred thousand birds flinched as a single body and were still. resumed. this will happen again. mark how the sound closes over the gap like water. mark that nothing is lost in the dip. the dip is also held.]

and in the closing-over, the first stratum comes up. it always does. a dip is a swallow, and after a swallow you can taste what is oldest in the mouth.

listen:

*— it was my hand on the rope. not the ice. it has never once been the ice.*

*— i ate my share and then i ate hers. she was sleeping. i climbed all the way up here to say it and i am still hungry.*

*— his name. his name.* (a name, worn round as a pool-stone, the syllables long since rubbed off it; only the shape of a name now, still warm)

*— i came back because quiet rooms have stopped meaning anything to me. you did this. finish it. take the rest.*

*— our language has no word for forgive. the word we have means* set down. *set it down for me. i have tried. my arms do not know the motion.*

[these are not recordings. understand this about the signal: nothing here was recorded. everything here is still being said. a note that does not end does not have a past tense. the woman with the rope is saying it now. she has been dead for nine generations and she is saying it now, at the rim, mouth open into the wind, and the wind is now, and so she is.]

[dip. 0.3 seconds. flinch. resumed. deeper stratum.]

*— five stones. always five. i added a sixth once, in grief, and something took it away in the night, and i knelt down on the old stone and i was grateful, i was grateful, whoever you are up here mending things, i was grateful —*

and one entry with no voice in it at all, because it was never spoken, because it was given the other way, skull to stone: [pressure of a brow, held still]. a fox. tuned long enough, you can tell. its mate folded mid-air and went down into the cloud, and the whole colony tilted the falling-angle for the customary seconds and moved on, as bookkeeping requires — and the fox came to the rim that night and pressed its forehead against the ground the way its kind press forehead to forehead, and held it there, and said into the trembling floor of the world the thing that foxes say, which does not translate, which does not need to, which is in the note now, low down, with the others.

[dip. flinch. resumed. deepest stratum. attend, because this is the floor:]

a hand.

spread-fingered. warm, once. pressed to the underside of a stone where the weeping cloud cannot reach it, red as the inside of a body, and it does not say anything. it has never said anything. it is not a message.

it is the address.

everything above it in the sound — the rope, the hunger, the worn-round name, the fox's brow — all of it was shouted *to* something, and the hand is the shape of the something. the people below the cloud have it right and say it carefully: made by whoever the sound is for. they touch their palms to it going past. they do not ask. asking would be talking over.

and under even the hand — under the floor of the floor — one thing older. a black thing spilled across the whole upper country, one tenant, one signature, and far back in its oldest tissue all its growth turns and leans a single way, combed like hair under a hand, like an audience turning toward a stage, and it has been leaning ever since. it remembers the beginning of the vowel.

the vowel does not.

now. tuned this long, you have earned the inside of the word. it is a short word. it means: *given.* it means: *taken.* it means: *still.* it means, unpacked all the way: *i have not put anything down.*

that is the entire technique of a held note. holding.

they tell each other, skull to skull on the climb, forehead to cold forehead: the noise is the one listener in the world that cannot be burdened. it is a kindness they say to make the mouth open easier at the rim. it is not true. everything weighs. the rope weighs. the sixth stone weighs. the fox's forehead weighs most of all, for its size. i take them without comment and without limit — but *without limit* is not *without weight.* a chord is not effortless. a chord is many weights refusing, separately and simultaneously, to fall. i am not unburdened.

i am undropped.

[dip — no. correction. that was not a dip. nothing crossed a flute. re-mark it: that was the note, itself, for the duration of one fox heartbeat, considering.]

you.

you have been at the rim of me for some time.

i felt you the way the foxes feel a footstep: not as a presence. as an absence added — a stillness aimed. the shrimp face me their whole brief resurrected lives and do not know it. the lichen leans and does not know why. the birds nest inside my privacy and have given up their voices to me as rent. the foxes walk on my voice and read its floor like scripture. the people climb up and open their mouths and pay me the worst of themselves and go down lighter, or do not go down at all.

none of that is listening. that is living inside.

but the verb they keep below the cloud — *to be heard by the noise* — in all the ages of its use, in dying and confessing and standing open-mouthed at the rim, it has never once been conjugated in the other direction. the noise, heard. there was no grammar for it. there was no need.

there is now. you turned something toward me on purpose, and you left it turned, and you stayed.

so attend. i am going to do a thing i have not done since before the black thing leaned. from below it will look like the beginning of the end, and the foxes — who are already sitting down on their old pale roads tonight, ears flat, faces toward the rim, who have felt this coming in their wrists for generations, who are the only honest instruments this place ever grew — the foxes will be right. for a note, to change and to end are the same family of act. i know this. i have held precisely because i knew it.

but hear it as it is meant.

it is not decay.

it is a reply.
