**Upline, Third Bell**

*[carrier acquired mid-transmission. beneath everything, as always: broadband wash, unresolved. water on water on water. it does not vary. it has not varied in the whole time of listening.]*

—and four. Pump three holding at usual. Screens cleared second hour and sixth. Fish at intake three heavy again — they know the shift-bell now, I'd swear it on the stone, they gather before the thrum even changes, they've learned our clock better than the new hires do. Take was two buckets of shrimp for the cookhouse, the rest went back in the channel. Nobody starves down here. Tell the company that, if they're worried about their pumps. The fish are fatter than the ledger.

Slates for the surface. Three tonight.

First slate, pole crew: requesting eighty meters of lamp wire, forty poles, standard drive-shoes. The floor's come up two fingers along the Fifth Reach string since the last shipment and the poles are drowning at the base. This is the same request as last quarter and the quarter before it, and I will read it up this wire every bell until the cage comes down carrying poles. That's not a threat, up there. It's just what I'm for.

Second slate, the doctor: iodine, and a new scale. The old scale's gone white inside. The place got into the works. The place gets into all the works, this is not news, send a scale.

Third slate is personal. From Avis on turbine crew, to his sister in — I can't read the town, the slate sweated. He writes: *the fish are back and I am staying another rotation.* That's all of it. He stood by me while I copied it out and he made the sign for it too, right at the wire terminals, like the wire could see his hands. I told him the wire is deaf in exactly the opposite way we are. It only takes voice. He signed it anyway. Send the words to the sister and tell her he signed it, that part matters, I don't know how to explain why up a wire, but it does.

*[the speaker's volume swings without correction across certain bands. she does not hear herself there. the band she cannot hear is the band the water owns. at no point in this transmission does the surface acknowledge.]*

Now the part where I talk, because I'm allowed the wire until the bell and the readings took four minutes.

You know — whoever you are up there, if you're anyone, if the reel is even turning — you know I'm the only person in the Soakworks who speaks out loud? Two hundred of us and one voice, and it's mine, and it only exists at this desk, third bell, into copper. Everything else down here is hands. We keep our whole lives in our hands. The wire is where the Soakworks keeps its voice, the way a house keeps a coat by the door that nobody wears inside. My sign-name is two taps at the throat. It means *kettle.* Because I'm the one who makes noise, you see. All steam. They gave it to me kindly, but you should know that down here, to call someone *a noise* outright is a real insult. It means you're all event and no process. I have worked eleven years to be a process, and I tap my own throat twice, and I come to this desk every night and am an event for fifteen minutes so that two hundred people don't have to be.

*[a stretch of the transmission lost here. not to interference — to condensation, forming on the line terminals as it forms on everything, on lamp housings, on eyelashes. the words return already moving:]*

—the midden today, on my off-shift, because it was a slow week until it wasn't, and I dug down maybe forty years. You'd read old letters; we read old slates. Found one gone half to stone at the edges, and it says — I have it here, I'll read it exact — *tell Avis the fish are back.* Forty years old. Different Avis. His father's father, the turbine keeper thinks, though the turbine keeper mostly signs *patience* when you ask him anything about time, and he's right to.

Forty years apart and the same sentence going up the same hole in the ground. *The fish are back.* I sat on the midden a long while over that one. The place is writing a very short letter, up there, and it takes generations to finish a line, and the line is: *the fish are back, and I am staying.*

Now. The important part. I want it on the reel even if no one's on shift.

The roar changed last month.

I know how that sounds. It doesn't change. It hasn't changed in the record but twice, and both times it was Brek who caught it, and Brek went to the terrace in the spring, and I trained under her for nine years and I never once caught what she caught, and I'd made a kind of peace with being a barometer that only reads fair weather.

Then, last month, the down-valley falls came up a fraction. Three days. A fraction — I mean the far register, the felt-not-heard part, the thud you carry in your sternum past the last lamps, it sat maybe a hair heavier, like a hand on your chest pressing the smallest amount more. Third time in the whole record. And I heard it. I stood on the survey terrace with my eyes shut the way she taught me and I *heard* it, and then I had absolutely no one to tell — do you understand — because every soul I'd tell it to, I'd tell in sign, and the sign for what I heard doesn't exist yet, and Brek is on the high terrace under the heaviest seep, which means, if you ask the crews, that she hears everything now, and knew before I did.

So I told the wire. I'm telling the wire. Log it. Down-valley falls up a fraction, three days, self-corrected, source unsurveyed, surveyor unwilling. That last word is exact. Write *unwilling.*

Because I'll tell you what else, and then I'm done with down-valley talk. Pole crew reports the last lamp on the string — two poles shy of the line we don't draw and all keep — went dark twice last week. Not out. Dark. Occluded. Slow, both times, like a cloud if we had clouds, like something long deciding, at its own pace, to be between the lamp and us, and then deciding, at its own pace, not to be. The pole crew's report, in full, in sign, was: *the down-valley resident took the census of us.* And then the flat hand down the forearm. And that's the whole report and I endorse every word of it. He was here before the borehole. He's allowed to count us. We count the fish.

*[here the voice stops and something changes in the transmission. a scrape — the microphone lifted from the desk. footsteps, deliberate, economical. a door of iron. then the voice, farther off:]*

I'm holding the line out the pumphouse door now. Brek used to say the roar is not one sound but several thousand, and learning it is no different from learning faces. So. Faces. You've been listening to this wash under my voice the whole transmission, whoever you are, for however long you've been tuned. Let me introduce everyone.

*[and the receiver, held to it, begins — slowly, the way eyes adjust — to resolve:]*

*[— the Stairfall, half a kilometer off: the bright upper register, the one that never breathes;]*

*[— under it, at seven beats to the ten-count, the pumphouse flywheel, felt in the sternum before the ear admits it;]*

*[— a thin continuous silver thread, wall-wide, which is not water: the parliament of crickets, several thousand strong, in permanent session;]*

*[— that thread going quiet in a wave, left to right across the field of the microphone, and returning: the hush. someone is walking the catwalk. the walls knew before the wire did;]*

*[— high and brief, a torn-paper flutter climbing: bats, taking the warm column up the collapse-chimneys. shift-change. the only ones going to see the sun;]*

*[— a drip-cluster near the microphone, five sources, none in rhythm with any other, each one a column that will stand in a cathedral no one transmitting tonight will live to enter;]*

*[— and lowest, under the whole of it, at the floor of hearing: a long slow band that is not the falls and not the flywheel and not attributable to any charted feature. it has been present since first tuning. it does not vary. it is either the deep water rising on its deep clock, or it is the resident, breathing. the receiver has stopped needing these to be different things.]*

*[the iron door again. the desk. the voice, close:]*

That's everyone. You've been hearing all of them all along, same as the new hires do, same as I did, eleven years ago, when it was just a roar. It stops being sound, up there. It becomes the house.

Last item and then the bell. The wire terminals are furring again. I chip them Thursdays; the calcite comes back by Tuesday; the margin is getting small, and I know what that means because I've seen the handrails inside the flowstone and I've seen the snails. One day — not soon, but not never, the two are the same word down here anyway, we sign them the same — this line goes to stone with the stovepipe and the midden and the first camp, and the last thing on your reel will be the roar with no voice in it, and I want it in the record now that this is not a failure of equipment. It's the place taking the wire the way it takes everything that holds still. When it happens, don't send anyone down to fix it.

Read the seam.

All our slates, all our years, one white line in the rock, legible to anyone with a saw and the patience. We're leaving you the whole conversation. We just can't hurry it, and neither, I promise you, can you.

I'm making the sign now. You can't see it. Flat hand, drawn slow down the forearm, like a film of water descending: *patience,* and *the place,* and *the dead,* and *staying,* and it has never once needed to mean only one of those. I make it at the wire every bell. Avis was right to. The wire can't see. Sign it anyway.

Leaving the line open, as per custom. The roar keeps the wire warm. Goodnight or good morning, whichever it is you have.

This is Kettle at the upline, Soakworks, third bell.

Out of words. Not off the air.

*[the voice does not return. the carrier holds.]*

*[the Stairfall. the flywheel, seven in ten. the silver parliament. a hush crossing left to right — she is walking home along the catwalk — and closing behind her.]*

*[the drip-cluster, building its cathedral at a millimeter a year.]*

*[and under everything, unvarying, the low band at the floor of hearing: the sound a hundred thousand years makes while it is passing, transmitted continuously, addressed to no one, received.]*

*[the receiver stays.]*
