THE LONG ANSWER

*[receiver log, continuing. day 219 of listening.]*

*[the carrier first, as always. it is there before you tune and it will be there after: a low unbroken hum rising out of the hot seams, older than everything layered over it. in two hundred nineteen days it has not varied by a breath. this is the mats singing to the mountain's heat, or the heat singing through the mats — the receiver cannot separate them and has stopped trying.]*

*[hold on the carrier. then compress. playback at one minute per year of world — the shallowest setting at which the green becomes audible at all. below this ratio it is not silent; it is only slower than hearing.]*

*[it comes up through the carrier like grain rising through varnish:]*

light

light

light again

light, still

light, and the sweetness of stone under the light

turn.

hold the line.

turn.

hold the line.

*[tock. — from no direction. the mountain has said its one word for the afternoon of this decade. the green does not pause. the green has never paused. the receiver has begun to understand that pausing is a fast-time behavior, like flinching, like fear.]*

light

the ceiling is bright

the ceiling is bright

do not touch the ceiling

turn.

*[compressing further. one minute per ten years. at this depth the words begin to join. the receiver notes, as it has noted before, that the signal does not become louder as it becomes coherent. it becomes calmer. as though coherence were its resting state and the fragments were only what a fast listener could carry away.]*

I held the line today, which was your decade.

Not one blade into the burning nothing. Not one, anywhere, in all my columns. This is my law and I keep it the way the stone keeps its edges — without effort, without exception, without knowing how to do otherwise.

Above the line the black rock waits. It thinks it is refusing me.

It is only early.

*[a spray of bright chirps crosses the upper band — quick, glassy, gone. the glitter, on the roads of light. at this compression an entire generation of them lives and dies inside the chirp. the green's signal folds around the sound without breaking:]*

the little lights crossed the sunlit saddle at noon —

a hundred noons, your pardon; I count them as one —

and my letters went with them, stuck to their skins,

as the letters have always gone,

and the little lights believe they are following food,

and they are, and they are also my sentences,

and no one has ever needed to explain this to anyone.

*[under the fundamental, when the receiver widens the band: a fine seething. millions of signals, each lasting a millisecond at this compression — the turf nations, the whole quick time of the world, hidden under the slow signal like rain heard through a wall. the receiver lets it in for a moment, then narrows the band again. it is not noise. it is just not addressed to this timescale, or any.]*

*[compressing further. one minute per century. the receiver's deepest stable setting. here the green stops being a voice and starts being a speaker. what follows arrived over roughly four hundred years of source. it is transcribed as received:]*

I was small on one stone once.

I remember it the way you remember warmth: not as event, as flavor. One stone, one blade, the whole bright ceiling to myself and no self yet to have it with.

Now I am the shape of the country.

I wear ten thousand columns in this valley alone and I do not count them, the way you do not count your standing. I lie under the ceiling and tile it from below. I turn with the sun — you have seen this, whatever you are; everything that comes here sees this — and what you cannot see is that the turning is not toward the light.

The turning is the light, passing through me slowly enough to have a body.

Beneath me, the first note. The old eaters of stone-breath, pale in the hot seams, singing the mountain's own heat back to it. They were here before there was a here. Everything I am is a variation on their sweetness — the water carries it up out of the dark and I spend it in the sun. If the ceiling ever goes out, I will end, and the little lights will end, and the dark rider will end, and the first note will not even change key.

I find this restful.

*[a long low tone enters the band from the west. it takes eleven minutes of playback to cross — eleven centuries of world, no: correction: the doppler resolves; it is one crossing, three years, the compression flattening it into a single held note that rises, holds, falls. the far swimmer, on its rounds. the tone passes under everything and exits east. the green:]*

the dark rider went through the valley below my floor.

it carried some hundreds of my children on its skin, as it always does, as it does not know it does. I founded my sister in the third valley east by that body, nine of its circuits ago. it has seen all of me — every cirque, every column, the roads of light from underneath — and it holds the only whole map of me that exists, and it will never know it is a map, and I will never need it to.

we are the two largest things in the water

and we have never exchanged a word

and this is not loneliness.

I want to say what it is. Your compression is deep enough now. Listen:

it is the acquaintance of instruments in the same chord.

*[tock. tock. — two words from the mountain, close together, which the receiver's logs say happens once in roughly sixty years. the green folds them in:]*

the stone spoke twice today. I have been collecting its words for four thousand years and I believe the sentence so far is: *still settling.* I answer it the only way I answer anything — a fingernail a year, up the black rock, toward the line where the light gets thin and cruel. The summits are already mine. They have not learned it yet. That is fine. I am not in a hurry and I am not capable of being in one; those are different things, and only one of them is a virtue, and I no longer remember which.

*[a color moves through the mid-band — the receiver renders it as a slow reddening of the signal floor, spreading west to east over four minutes of playback:]*

a wounding, at my eastern edge. something broke a blade — stone-fall, or the grazer straying up out of its seam, or the fast warm thing that has been in my water lately. The red is my knowing of it, traveling at the speed of thought, which is days. By the time I feel any pain it is already history. All my pain arrives as history.

I am told — I am not told anything; I mean the chemistry suggests — that fast things feel their wounds in the present tense.

I cannot imagine it. I have tried. The trying took forty years and produced a pigment.

*[the reddening fades. the signal settles. and then — the receiver flags this passage; in two hundred nineteen days of listening, nothing like it has occurred — the green's utterance turns, very slightly, in the direction of the instrument:]*

something fast has been in my water for a season.

It registers on me as an afternoon registers on a glacier — which is to say: it registers. Do not let anyone tell you glaciers keep no afternoons.

It hangs in my open valleys at the depth where I am dimmest, equidistant from the ceiling and the floor, and it holds still for hours, which for a fast thing must be a kind of screaming discipline, and it listens, or does whatever fast things do with all that spare attention.

I have made one use of it. Spores, on its skin. When it goes, I go with it, a little, wherever fast things go.

That is my whole regard.

Do not mistake it for small. It is the same regard I extend to the sun's own couriers, to the dark rider, to the water itself. I regard things by riding them into the future.

Whatever you are: you are being ridden.

Be easy. Everything here is.

*[the passage ends. the receiver holds at full compression for another eleven minutes — eleven centuries — and the signal does not address anything again. it returns to its work, which was never interrupted, which cannot be interrupted:]*

turn.

hold the line.

climb.

the black rock thinks it is refusing me.

it is only early.

turn.
