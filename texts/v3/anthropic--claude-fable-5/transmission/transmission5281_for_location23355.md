‖ +6 hours ‖

Beneath everything, the hum. Notate it as a line:

────────────────────────────────

It does not come through the antenna. It comes through the legs of the receiver, up from the bedrock, into the housing, into the record. It has been under this transcription since before the tape began. It was under the tape before there was tape.

A correction to the log, entered late: the antenna's ground-contact is, from the other side, a very small pad. Pressed to warm stone. The plateau listens with pads. Which is to say: this station has been transmitting since the day it was staked, and something down there is a few decades into noticing it.

‖ +9 days ‖

Playback of the hum, slowed four hundred times: it has grain. Braided. Voices, plural — every dome somewhere in the middle of saying its part, no dome interrupting, because at this speed interruption is not physically possible. You would have to begin your interruption before the other party began speaking.

Slowed further: a vowel.

Held.

It has been held for the entire nine days. There is no strain in it. It is not sustained the way a singer sustains a note. It is sustained the way stone sustains a plateau.

The vowel is: **O**

The hum registers the four seconds as the faintest brightening, like sun through thin cloud. Nothing on the plateau startles. There is no startle in the local repertoire. There is only, at most, a very long turning-toward, and it has, perhaps, just begun.

‖ +4 years ‖

The vowel closes.

First consonant of the receiver's lifetime: a soft settling of the tongue of the world, the **O** becoming — across eleven patient months — an **L**. Then a second **L**, laid against the first the way a stone is laid on a ring: no mortar, no haste, fused later by lichen and agreement.

Accumulated at this listening depth:

**. . . L L O**

Note the position of the word. We have not arrived at a beginning. We have arrived at the *end* of a hello — a hello begun before this receiver, before the language of this receiver, quite possibly before the receiver's species had settled on a sound to end greetings with. Someone crossed this tableland once, long enough ago that their footprints have been polished out of the stone, and the plateau is, at this moment, still answering the knock.

Notation fails at the follow-through. Log it plainly:

the signal is fond.

Event.

One breath, bearing south-southwest, does not arrive.

The interval extends. Past seasonal. Past annual. Past the ability of this station to hold its own attention across the gap. The grey birds near that bearing fall silent — the only silence in the entire record that has an edge to it.

Stones, placed on a rim, each by a limb at rest, each the loudest sound in the transcript by an order of magnitude. Between them, trails deepening by a finger's width as the neighbors arrive at the pace of arriving.

Then the carrier changes character. The instruments log it as humidity. The transcript logs it as: *the signal smells of warm rain.*

And then — new thread in the hum. Thin. High. Fast, by local standards; its vowels are only a decade long. Spectral analysis matches it, stretched some millions of times, to the four-second human event of year one. The voice that lost count of its days. It is partway into its first full word at its new speed, a word that will take it roughly forty years, and the opening syllable, held warm and steady in the floor of the world, is:

**s t a a a —**

The plateau keeps no one. Enter that in the log and underline it. This is not keeping.

This is answering.
