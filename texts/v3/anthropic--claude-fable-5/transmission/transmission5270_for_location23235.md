**STILL GOING**

*(—the chorus, as before. It has not paused in the whole of this record. Rendered here as a floor of sound: ten thousand frog voices in the warm shallows, interlocking, leaving their small old gaps. Under it, the seep — a trickle so continuous the receiver long ago stopped flagging it and now reports it only as warmth. Under that, constant, the Tone: one low note, held somewhere past the western horizon by a voice that took it up an hour ago. The note was a D once, some centuries back. It is not quite anything now. It is itself.)*

*(A bird works the flats at close range: probe, slap, swallow. Its calls carry out and do not come back. Nothing here echoes. Everything here arrives.)*

A VOICE, SOUTH, PERHAPS THREE MILES: South asking. What are the snails doing.

*(Six minutes: chorus. The Tone changes voices — a far singer takes it before the near one lets it fall. The join is smooth as water finding water.)*

A VOICE, FAR SOUTHEAST: The same.

*(Silence, in the local sense — which is to say the frogs, the seep, the Tone, and a heron somewhere disapproving of a fish. Then, over the following minutes, laughter. It arrives from separate bearings as the joke reaches each acre at the speed of sound: first a low single laugh in the east, then two in the north, then one very far off to the west, delayed and generous, laughing at something said nine minutes ago. The laughter crosses itself on the open water. Then it lies back down.)*

*(Forty minutes: chorus. A rain column passes through the signal from the south — announced first as the smell would be announced, by a change in the air's weight, then as a wide soft roar walking north. The frogs sing up into it. The sheets deepen a finger's width. The Tone does not alter. The rain moves off holding its one gray note, and the plain redistributes.)*

A VOICE, NORTH, LOW AND FLAT AND CARRYING: West. I am here for the evening.

*(Four minutes.)*

A VOICE, WEST — an old voice, worn smooth, the kind the domes would have if the domes spoke: North. I know. You have been walking toward hearing distance since noon. I felt you through the floor.

*(Three minutes. When she says* floor *she uses the word that is also* patience, *and also the name of what sleeps under everything. The receiver renders it as one syllable, very low, and notes that when it is spoken the seep seems briefly louder. This is not a measurable effect. It is noted anyway.)*

WEST: Everyone hearing this already knows, because everything said here is said to everyone, so I will say it plainly for the plain. The seep is louder than my blood now. I have heard it in myself since the reeds seeded. I finished with Ondu-of-the-East at the last moon. I finished with the heron dome yesterday, which took less time than you would think; the dome is a good listener and a poor talker. North, you are the last conversation. You knew that. I saved you.

*(Nine minutes: chorus. The Tone passes hands again in the far west. Somewhere north-northeast a voice says, softly, at distance-volume,* the reed-stand dome has a sleeper *— and for the next hour every conversation on that bearing bends slightly, routed around a point of silence the way water routes around a stone.)*

NORTH: I have what you gave.

*(In their speech this is one word. Spoken and given are the same act. The receiver cannot transcribe it as two English words without lying slightly, and lying here is exhausting, so let it stand: she spoke, and he confirmed receipt, and both of these were the same thing.)*

WEST: There are three things left. I have counted them for a season. Here is the first, and it is for you and therefore for everyone.

*(Two minutes.)*

WEST: The seam at the base of the split dome runs sweeter for a day after rain. I found this forty years ago and I never said it, because I liked having something. It was my one wall. I am taking it down now. The seam is yours. The seam is everyone's. I just heard myself say it, so it is done.

*(Eleven minutes. A child's voice, near the receiver, loud and unspaced in the way of children, asking something about the sweet seam already. An adult voice, warm: routed around, little one. Not tonight. The chorus swells with full dark coming on. This is the hour the frogs own outright. Voices thread the gaps in it as through the teeth of a comb.)*

WEST: The second thing. When we were young, North — when your acre was still your mother's acre — I stood one whole afternoon within a hundred yards of you and never spoke. I did not trust my voice to lie flat. It would have carried everything in it and the plain would have heard, and I was not ready to give what speaking gives. That afternoon is the shell I wear on the reed cord. I drilled it that evening. I wanted you to know, before I walk, which afternoon it keeps.

*(Fourteen minutes: chorus. The receiver notes that North's signal has begun, very slowly, to move. A source in motion across the flats, splashless, closing the distance at the speed of ceremony. Two miles is a long walk when each step is placed like a word.)*

NORTH, STILL FAR, VOICE ABSOLUTELY LEVEL: I have what you gave. I have had it, I think, for forty years, unopened.

WEST: Yes. That is why the shell.

*(Twenty minutes. The moving source crosses the half-mile line. In the whole of this record, no two voices have ever originated closer than six hundred yards. The receiver flags the approach as an anomaly. The plain does not. The frogs do not so much as hitch.)*

WEST: The third thing has no words, so I will not spend words failing at it. Instead — whoever is holding the Tone tonight, hear me: I ask for it. One last holding. Listen for where I bend it. The bend is the third thing. I am going to put it where nothing is ever lost.

*(Four minutes. Then, from the far southwest, the Tone comes toward her — not moving, exactly, but handed, voice to voice along the chain, each singer releasing it a breath after the next takes hold, so that the note travels the miles like a cup passed down a long table. It takes most of an hour to reach her. The chorus roars on above it, oblivious, eternal, correct.)*

*(She takes it.)*

*(For a while the Tone is simply the Tone in an old smooth voice. Then — the receiver slows the recording to be sure — a bend. A fraction flat, then a small lift at the end of each breath, like a wading bird raising one foot and setting it down without a ripple. It is nothing. It is a grain's worth of change, the yearly ration, spent all at once. Anyone listening across the whole plain hears it, because everyone across the whole plain is listening. That is the point. That was always the point of everything here.)*

*(She holds it through six breaths. Then, near her — nearer than near, arm's-reach near, the deathbed distance — a small voice takes it. High. Unsteady. A first holding. The note wavers, wanders a half-tone wide, narrows, narrows, finds the floor of itself and stands on it. And inside it, unmistakable, learned in six breaths and kept: the bend. The lifted foot. The third thing, entered into the monument, where each voice will now hand it to the next voice forever without ever needing to know what afternoon it keeps.)*

*(Two hours: the child holds the Tone across the dark water and the child's voice grows older by the minute, the way voices do in that hour. The frogs come up around it like a tide. Every adult on the plain is silent, giving the child the whole dusk to stand in.)*

*(Then two voices at a single bearing. A single point. The receiver has no precedent for this and renders it plainly: breathing, close, doubled. The old voice speaks once more, and though the other is beside her she speaks at carrying volume, at distance-volume, because there is no whispering here and she would not want there to be:)*

WEST: Everyone. This is the sound of two people close together. We wanted it heard once.

*(Water sounds. Unhurried. A walk of perhaps three hundred yards — the receiver tracks it to the base of a dome where a seam weeps, and there the footsteps stop, and there is the long soft sound of someone lying down along the warm stone in the warm water, lips to the seam, taking only what the land is already letting go.)*

*(Then the seep. Just the seep, at that bearing, from then on.)*

*(And then this, which the receiver asks be kept in the record exactly: the Tone lowers. A half-step, no more. But it lowers in a ring — the near singers first, then the next, then the next, the grief traveling outward across the flats at the speed of sound, a slow circular wave of lowered voices spreading from the dome where she lies, taking eleven minutes to reach the horizon and pass beyond range. The loudest sorrow the plain permits, and it moves across the world like rain seen from far off: a gray pillar, walking.)*

*(The Tone holds low all night. The frogs do not know. The frogs are magnificent and do not need to know. Near dawn the birds begin, cross-hatching the gray light, each one given its acre.)*

*(At the following dusk — the receiver kept the channel open; the receiver was not going anywhere — the ring comes back the other way. Voice by voice, bearing by bearing, the Tone rises the half-step home, and when it settles it is not the note it was two nights ago. It is a fraction different. There is a bend in it now, small as a grain, permanent as anything here is permanent, which is to say: handed.)*

*(A child is holding it, somewhere east, steadily.)*

*(Somewhere west, a hand is laid flat on warm stone, held a moment, taken away. The receiver cannot hear this. The receiver reports it anyway, on the grounds that everything said here is said to everyone, and that the gesture, translated as closely as the signal allows, means—)*

*(—still going—)*

*(—chorus, as before.)*
