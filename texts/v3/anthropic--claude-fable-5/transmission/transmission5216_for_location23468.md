TWO, THEN TWO

— and under the wind, always, the water: tick. tick. tick-tick. a brief rush somewhere inside the stone, gone.

The long tone first. The big halls make it — a moan that rises out of nothing, holds for the length of three breaths, and slides down a fifth, because one of the halls was cut to do exactly that, and the wind has never once refused. Above it the well-shaft that is now a chimney plays its two open ends against each other, a thin hollow flute-note that wavers when the mist comes through. The mist is coming through now. You can hear it arrive: every lintel in the city begins, very quietly, to weep.

tick. tick.

[a sharp report — stone cracking, close, like a knuckle. then another, far, answered by three more, traveling upward through the levels]

It is autumn. The tooth is talking.

[low on the signal now, near the ground: the warm register. picks. antler on new stone, a soft biting sound, wet, rhythmic, eight or ten hands working out of time with each other. breath at altitude. voices, close together, not discussing the work]

— late third week of summer, this. Feel it. Late third.
— you said second, yesterday.
— yesterday it was second.
— [laughter, brief, breathless]
— the fox came up the saddle last night. Just to the grass. Sat.
— early for the fox.
— hard winter, then.
— hard winter. [a pick. a pick. a pick] — and she's marrying him anyway, is what I heard, before the frost, because —
— because you don't let a thing like that harden.
— because you don't let a thing like that harden, that's right —

[a basket of spoil set down. somewhere above, slower: basalt and wet sand on a threshold, a circular grinding, patient, and under it a voice singing a song with no words in it, in time with the grinding, which is very slow time]

[higher still — a ladder creaking. old hands on an edge cut sixty years ago. a voice alone in a room, the room answering it with its own size]

— still true. [pause. palm along stone.] Still true. Good. Good.

[the mist thickens on the signal. the flute-note bends. somewhere in the middle levels, knocking begins — small knuckles, light-shaft to light-shaft, quick, a code]

[running feet on a stairwell, ascending, two sets, then a third. an adult voice below them, calling upward, not angry:]

— mind the white marks! The white marks are white for a reason!

[and fainter, the same voice, to someone nearer:]

— her mark's at her eyebrows now. I watched her measure herself against it this morning when she thought no one — she stood on her toes. It won't help her, standing on her toes. By spring it's overhead and she knows it. She's been asking about the finishing trade. Asking around it, the way you do.

[the light changes on the signal. you can hear the light change: the wind-tones sharpen, the water quickens, and then —]

[the swifts]

[thirty dark birds pouring out of the high windows all at once, screaming, the only fast sound this transmission has ever carried, a black scarf of noise unwinding around the tooth twice, three times, and gone out over the saddle. in the rooms they left, the silence is a different silence for a while. riper.]

— that hall's gone up, then. Third year running they've skipped it.
— gone up. Yes.
— and the low one by the census stair?
— ripe. They were in and out of it all summer.
— [satisfaction, wordless]

[dusk. the census stair.]

[a strike — the heel of a hand, flat, on stone. the stair rings, a dead clear note, the note of a bell that is also a building. then another strike. another. an elder's voice, counting, unhurried, each number pressed into the air the way a stamp is pressed into wet stone:]

— …thirty-four. …thirty-five. …thirty-six. …thirty-seven.

[a long gap. wind. the flute-note. then, descending through it, footsteps from very high up, deliberate, a long time coming. the thirty-eighth strike, when it lands, is softer than the others and rings longer.]

— …thirty-eight. And the fox, guest.

[murmur. the scrape of a long table. eating sounds, small talk, and then a child's voice, blunt as children are:]

— what is it like up there.

[and the answer, in a voice gone strange from nineteen years of talking mostly to large empty rooms — a voice with its own reverb, that carries its rooms with it:]

— Windy. Green. Loud in the halls, quiet in the stairs. Your great-grandmother's rowan fruited this year. I pruned the junipers off the west windows so the birds run clean. Four ladders failed the test; I marked them. Everything else held.

— do you talk to it.

— I knock. It's politer.

— does it —

— [the resonant voice, mild:] — every time. Better every year. Eat your supper.

[full dark on the signal now. the long tone and its fifth. the water. the tooth cracks once, deeply, a groan that travels down through the transmission floor and out the other side. footsteps ascending again, alone, knuckles brushing the wall the whole way up — a soft continuous reading sound, stone spelled out under a living hand, room after room after room touched for the last time this season —]

[— and then nothing for a long while. this receiver stayed with the nothing. the nothing has architecture. you can hear the shapes of the empty halls in how the wind leaves them.]

[then, from below the waterline — from the deep stair-shafts, from stone too new to be rooms —]

knock. knock.

[a pause. the pause is exact. the pause has been practiced.]

knock. knock.

[above, faintly, all through the inhabited band, in the dark, without anyone lighting a lamp: answers. a brisk pair-pause-pair from a Spender's floor. a formal one, evenly spaced, from a Keeper's sealed hall. from somewhere high, a pair-pause-pair with a small ornament on the last knock, an Answerer, unable to help it. and from the very top of the city, knuckles on an old wall, gently:]

knock. knock. — knock. knock.

[silence. water. tick. tick.]

[and then it comes again from below, and — the receiver notes this without explanation — it is *better* this time. more even. the interval truer. the way an apprentice's second attempt is better. the way a thing learns.]

knock. knock. — knock. knock.

[the wind holds its fifth. the transmission is degrading now, or purifying, it is difficult to say which — the voices are gone, the water is gone, the long tone is thinning to carrier hiss, and what remains, what the hiss itself is beginning to resolve into, what may have been underneath this signal the entire time, riding up through it a hand's width a year, is:]

knock. knock.

— knock. knock.

knock. knock.

— knock. knock.

[a pause.]

[a pause.]

[the pause is exact. the pause is for you.]
