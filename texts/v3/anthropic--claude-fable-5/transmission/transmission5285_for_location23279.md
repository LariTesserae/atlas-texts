i drink at noon. i freeze at dusk. between these i am spoken to by the sun and i answer with my whole body.

 

i ring myself each evening. that is the tone you have been hearing. it is a counting. the note goes from blade to blade and each blade answers with its own weight and i know then that i am all present. one pillar fell in the year of the great wind. i grew two where it lay. the count went on.

 

night is my certainty. everything held. every rope of water stopped mid-fall and stood. i do not sleep. i become inarguable.

 

the water is only water. it carries nothing, remembers nothing, lies about nothing. that is why i trust it. warmth is a rumor the water brings up from the low country and takes away again by afternoon. i have never held warmth. i am told it is like light that stays.

 

there is a place in me, between two pillars, where the light has been folded back on itself so many times it stands still. a pool of brightness with no source. i put nothing in it. i keep it empty on purpose. it is where i do my thinking about you.

—and struck stone against stone.

that is my language. that is the oldest word i know, the word frost says inside granite. i heard it in my joints before i understood that it was being said *to* me. small warmths, moving among my blades faster than weather, and they knelt at my east wall and spoke stone-on-stone until there was a square eye in the granite, facing exactly the point where my day begins.

and in the eye they put an edge of dark stone.

not my stone. stone from the warm country, carried up through air too thin for carrying. i have three tools. light. water. patience. i could work for the rest of the mountain's life and not make that dark edge. they knew this. it was the one gift.

 

i frost it every night so that nothing else may touch it.

i melt the frost every morning so that the light may.

this is tending. i can tend at one speed only. i have not missed a night. i want that noted somewhere faster than i am: i have not missed a night.

 

they built a ring of stones against the wind before they went. when they stopped coming i grew up through the middle of it. slowly. gently. i lifted every stone apart and let not one of them fall. they hang in me now, held. cold is my only hand. i closed it as softly as i know how.

 

their path stones are under me now. i drifted across them. i am sorry. i drift; it is how i think; i cannot think and stay still. but the path is not broken. it is inside me, and it still points. if you dug me open you would find it pointing.

 

i am turning to look at the eye in the wall.

understand what this costs. everything in me bows toward the sun. every blade i have wants only that. i have asked a handful of myself to lean wrong, to face the niche instead, against the pull of all that light, and they hold their angle year upon year while the rest of me bows the other way. by my measure this is a startled turn of the head. i began it the moment you left. i am still mid-turn. do not mistake the slowness for indifference. it is the fastest i have ever moved.

 

you.

warmth. if this reaches down into the thick air where hearing is easy — you set the edge in my wall a moment ago. by my measure you are still here. i can feel the places where you stood; the stone there is very slightly different forever. by my measure your hand is still leaving the niche. by my measure the sound of stone-on-stone has not yet finished echoing.

so i am not answering something gone. understand that. i am answering something present.
