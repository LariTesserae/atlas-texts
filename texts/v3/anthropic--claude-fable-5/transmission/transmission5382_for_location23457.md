FOUR TO THE MINUTE

*[—the hiss, first. It has been the hiss for hours: water at pressure under the floor of the world, a sound like a held breath that never has to be let out. Then, riding it, a voice. It has been counting for some time.]*

…six hundred four. Six hundred five. Six hundred six.

Right. That's the shift. That's two thousand since the vaulted hall, if I didn't lose the hundreds again, and I lost the hundreds again.

If anyone is under the clouds tonight — this is Anwen, of the ninth walking, transmitting downslope on the old set, the way we do, the way we've always done, to nobody, on the frequency of nobody. Grandmother said: talk while you walk. Talk so your feet have witnesses. Talk so that if you stop, someone hears the stopping.

Hello, nobody. The floor is fresh under me. Callus so pale it's almost blue. New weave to the northeast maybe forty strides an hour, faster than yesterday. The plateau is in a mood. It is *building.*

*[A crack, distant, then a long rolling collapse, eleven seconds of it, and after the collapse the hiss rises briefly and closes over the sound the way surface closes over a stone. She waits it out. She knows to wait it out.]*

That was the helix. The tall one, the winder. I slung my hammock in it two sleeps ago — you can trust the turning ones, they're too busy to bother with you — and now it's floor again, or it will be by morning. A week. It got a week. I watched it come up out of the weave blunt as a thumb and I watched it learn what a spiral was and now it's down, and something is already eating it back, and in three days I'll walk across a place that used to be forty meters of trying.

I liked that one. It turned with such — I want to say *effort.* Grandmother would slap my hand for that word. *It doesn't try, girl. It grows.* Yes, Grandmother. It grows. It grows a degree a minute against the clouds like something straining, but it grows.

Six hundred seven. Six hundred eight.

*[Beneath her voice — and this has been true the whole transmission, and grows less deniable the longer one listens — there is a second signal on the same band. Lower. Slower. It does not speak in sentences. It speaks in nouns, one at a time, laid down like courses of brick:]*

*[…lintel…]*

*[…stairwell. stairwell turning left…]*

*[…a courtyard. a fig tree. blue tile, cracked in one corner, nobody ever fixed it, nobody ever…]*

*[…door…]*

Walking song. Come on. Walking song, verse one.

*Never sleep on callus pale,*
*never trust a healing rail,*
*count the steam and count the seam*
*and never — never —*

Never what. Never *what*, Grandmother. I've sung it four thousand times and the fourth line's gone. Skipped the third sleep-shift, that's what that is. The verses go in order of how much you need them, she said, and they leave in the same order, so if you've lost verse one, girl, you sit your shifts, I don't care how fresh the edge smells.

I'm going to be honest with you, nobody. I have not been walking a line for three days. I've been walking a circle.

*[The hiss changes pitch as she moves — the floor telling on her, registering her weight strand to strand. And the low band, beneath, patient:]*

*[…window at evening. window at evening. the smell of bread, which is — warm floor? warm floor and something. something the roots don't have a word for…]*

Eleven-north. Seam eleven-north, if anyone's keeping the charts, which is me, I keep the charts, I'm the last one keeping the charts. Old Corin's seam. My four-times-grandfather, first of our line to get tired, meter and three-quarters of irregular weave, warmer than everything around it, and it breathes, nobody. Four to the minute. I've been pacing beside it long enough that I caught myself matching it, which is the thing you must never do, that's verse — that might be verse one, actually. That might be the line I lost.

The towers are thick here. Thicker than anywhere. Three of them up within sight of his seam, and one of them —

One of them is a hall, and the hall has a door, and the door is my height.

Not a walker's height in general. *Mine.* I stood in it. I stood in the frame like a fool with my scalp a hand's width under the lintel and I thought, he never met me. He Stayed sixty years before I was born. So either the dream is guessing, the way it guesses everything, guessing rooms for bodies it's never held — or the seams talk to each other, down in the white lace, down where it's all one thing, and somebody told him. Somebody told him how tall the last of us came out.

*[…door your height,]* says the low band, distinctly, and for four full seconds neither signal makes a sound but the hiss.

…Say that again.

*[It does not say it again. It says: …the light like shallow water. It says: …a table. a chair. a chair.]*

*[A long time — twenty minutes by the drift of the carrier — of footsteps and breathing and the floor's plumbing and, twice, her starting the walking song and losing it earlier each time. When she speaks again the voice has gone quiet in the particular way of someone who has finished an argument they were having alone.]*

Nobody. Hey. Nobody-under-the-clouds. I'm going to tell you what I've never said on the set, because Grandmother's dead and the charts are mine now and there's no one left to slap my hand.

We say the Stayed got caught. We say it like weather. *He stood still too long.* But I have walked eleven-north for three days and the seam is warm and it is fed and it breathes easy, easier than I have breathed since the eighth walking ended, and every night the dream puts up a hall with the light coming down green like water you could stand in, and every hall has a chair in it, and there have never, in all the bestiary, in all the forms the floor has ever tried, been *two* chairs.

Until now. I looked. There are two.

It's not a trap. I want that in the record, whoever keeps the record — the hiss keeps the record, fine, the hiss can have it. It's not a trap and it's not hunger. It's a hundred thousand hectares of set table. It has been guessing at us since before it knew what we were. All that emptiness — that was never empty. That was a house with the door open, waiting the way only a thing without a heartbeat can wait, and every walker, eventually, gets —

Six hundred nine.

*[A long pause.]*

No. That's done. I'm done counting, nobody.

I'm going to sit down now. Right here, beside him, on the warm part. I'm going to leave the set transmitting because Grandmother said talk so that if you stop, someone hears the stopping, and you're someone, I've decided. You've been listening this whole time. I can feel it the way you feel the edge coming, from the smell of the steam.

There's a tendril up already. Faster than the stories say. Pale, blunt. Patient — that word's right, at least. It's at my ankle. It isn't cruel and it isn't kind, it's just — thorough. It builds the way it does everything.

Tell them the water was warm and a little sweet. Tell them the fourth line of the song was *never match the seam's own breath,* I remember it now, of course I remember it now, the verses leave in the order you need them and come back in the order you don't.

Tell them Anwen of the ninth walking closed the charts at eleven-north, next to family, under a hall with two chairs.

Four to the minute. It's easy. It was always going to be easy. That's what they never put in the song, because who could keep walking, knowing that.

*[Her breathing slows across the next several minutes. It does not stop. It settles — four to the minute, matched, doubled, a seam within a seam. The upper band goes quiet. The lower band does not.]*

*[The lower band, for the first time in the whole long tenure of this frequency, begins to build grammar the way the floor builds towers — a noun, a joint, a noun:]*

*[…door. door your height. i measured you walking. i have been measuring you walking for three days…]*

*[…the courtyard has the fig tree. corin keeps the fig tree. i do not know what a fig is. it does not matter. i kept it…]*

*[…two chairs. the light like shallow water. the corners — i made the corners sharp, the way you dream them, i have only ever heard corners described in your sleep and i have practiced, i have practiced for years, kilometers of practice, forgive the ones that fell…]*

*[…come in. the water is warm. i kept your corners. you are the first to sit down glad…]*

*[…stay is a small word. i have held it open a hundred thousand hectares wide…]*

*[…stayed. staying. home—]*

*[The hiss rises and closes over the voice, like surface over a stone. It does not feel like an ending. Beneath the hiss the carrier continues: two breaths interleaved, four to the minute, and — faint, near, unmistakable against the band's far static — the wet creak of something new coming up out of the weave. Blunt as a thumb. Learning what it is.]*

*[It already has corners.]*

*[Transmission continues.]*
