THE IRON CARRIES

*(received through metal; the air here does not cooperate)*

—

tick.

tick. tick.

tick.

[the condensary towers, drinking. this is the noise floor of the world. it does not stop. under it, at 0.4 hertz, the flywheels: too low to call sound. the receiver renders it as pressure. you have been feeling it this whole time and calling it your own pulse. it is not your own pulse.]

tick. tick.

tick.

—

· · — · [rail, north tower, descending]
· · — ·
— —

    *cold up top. cistern nine at mark. coming down.*

— [rail, cistern nine, ascending]

    *held.*

[a towerman and a cistern keeper, thirty meters of iron column between them, conversing at the speed of patience. the word rendered "held" is one tap. it is the most common word in the city. the receiver has logged it eleven thousand times and its meaning has not moved once.]

—

[dawn. light does something to iron and iron says so — a long dim creaking all across the rooflines, plate by plate, west to east, as the sun takes the frost that never quite formed. the city stretching. no voice yet. voices are expensive here and no one spends before breakfast.]

—

first spoken voice of the day, close to a wall, low:

    "...stand straight. heels to the iron.
    there. eleven-oh-two.
    and the block came up one and a half.
    you're beating the mountain this year.
    don't get proud. it's not racing you."

[a child being measured. solstice is near. the scratch of the ledger-pen comes through the wall better than the voice does. the pen sounds like the towers. everything here sounds like the towers eventually.]

—

· — · · — [party wall, middle terraces, trans. uncertain]

[a pause of nine minutes.]

· —

[the receiver has been tuned to this wall for four months. the first pattern is a question. it is always the same question. the answers have been getting quicker. tonight's answer took nine minutes and was two taps. the receiver's translation model offers, at low confidence: *ask me at the measured door.* the receiver's operator offers, at high confidence: *yes.*]

—

[mid-morning. the turbine halls. rendered here as best the medium allows:]

hhhhhhhhhhhh — [regulated steam, continuous]
        · · · [handrail: *ready*]
    hhhhhhhhh
        — — · · [handrail: *take it slow*]
    hhhhhhhh — the pitch drops a quarter tone as load comes on —
        · [*held*]

[somewhere in the hall someone is humming. the hum is F, or was F in their grandmother's mouth. it is very slightly flat of F now and nobody has corrected it because it is not wrong. the cathedral is flat of F too. they are going down together.]

—

[NOON.]

[for fifty-eight seconds the transmission whites out. this is not a fault. this is the release — the whole city exhaling through its stacks at once, the one loud thing, the sanctioned roar. inside the white, if the gain is pushed, small fast bodies: the swifts, taking the plume. they register as absences, holes the size of a hand moving upward through the noise at speeds nothing else in this city moves. the receiver cannot say what they are. the receiver notes that neither can anyone else, and that the city has decided this is fine, and files the swifts under *held.*]

[silence resettling, like dust.]

tick.

tick. tick.

—

[afternoon. the signal goes deep now — down the pilings, into the galleries, where the iron runs into the green stone and the green stone runs into whatever the green stone runs into. the noise floor changes character down here. it stops being mechanical. the receiver's operator has written in the margin of the log, unprofessionally: *warmer.*]

an old voice, very quiet, gallery seventeen:

    "...dish is grown over again. look at that.
    branching left this time. seventeen always branches left.
    write it down. no — write it the way it is,
    not the way you think it should be.
    the cabinet doesn't want your opinion."

[the scratch of the pen. then, after a while, a pattern tapped slow on the gallery wall — the receiver has heard it before, always from the oldest hands, always followed by waiting:]

— · · · — — · [trans. none. the guild says: *it's not a word. it's a knock.*]

[and then the waiting. four minutes. six.

and then the thing the receiver was built without a channel for, and receives anyway: the noise floor *rises to meet the wall.* not sound. a broadening. a warmth arriving in the signal the way warmth arrives under a palm. it holds position adjacent to the tapping hand. when the hand goes still, it goes still.

the old voice, not to the apprentice:]

    "hello, then."

[nothing in the transmission answers. everything in the transmission is the answer.]

—

[evening. the light goes and the iron says so again, east to west this time, the day's creak run backwards. in the cathedral several hundred people have settled onto one low note and are holding it with staggered breath so it never breaks. the piers hum back. the receiver measures the note at 43.7 hertz. last year's log says 43.8. the note is going where the note is going, a fraction of a fraction at a time, and the people are going with it, and this is not decline, the receiver's operator has decided; this is the city keeping its building company on the way down, which is a thing that has no name in the lowlands and one tap here.]

—

[night. streets empty and steaming. one lamp per funicular head. the towers tick. the flywheels turn under everything at the speed of a conversation with something very large.

and then, from below the pilings, below the galleries, below the closed chambers where the finished dead ring high and clear when the hammers pass —

the groan.

long. slow. unhurried. the sound of the ground continuing to become itself. it comes up through every rail and doorframe and tensioned wall in the city at once, and all across the terraces, in the dark, the receiver picks up the response — hundreds of small taps, one per household, knuckle to iron, the whole city answering its mountain the way you'd answer a knock at the wall:]

·

·   ·

·

    *held. held. held. held.*

tick.

tick. tick.

tick.

[transmission continues. transmission has always continued. the receiver will stay tuned; the operator has stopped logging an end time. between the measurements, in a hand going patient, the log now reads:

*the signal is not finished. neither is the listening. adjust accordingly.*]
