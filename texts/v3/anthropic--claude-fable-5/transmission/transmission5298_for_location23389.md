**The Day's Allowance**

*— received from the western bench, one full light, dark to dark —*

---

**[before gray]**

Water first. It is always water first.

This is not a sound so much as a change in the quality of the silence: the north faces of the walls going slick, the stone taking its film. The receiver cannot hear beading. What it hears is the first drop off the roof-funnel of the near house into the catch-jar below it —

*tik*

— and then the long interval, and then again, and then the other jars beginning at their own rates, six jars for six families, until the courtyard is a slow irregular percussion with no downbeat, the day being paid out.

[a wall-sparrow shifts, third course, left of the smoke-gap — a dry rustle deep in the masonry — the house breathes]

[somewhere below, deeper, once:]

*tock.*

[nothing. the pause. even now, with no one awake to make it, the pause is there — as if the silence itself had learned the custom]

---

**[first gray]**

The quern starts in the second courtyard east. Grain against stone against stone, a sound like the mountain talking in its sleep.

The loom behind the east wall started *before* the birds this morning. Third time this month. Someone in that house is finishing something.

Then the cliffs let go. The receiver has recorded this every morning for a long time and has not found the right notation for it: a thin roar, wide as the whole face, the nesting flocks pouring downward and out — smoke running backward — thirty seconds of a sound like distance tearing, and then gone, and the silence comes back with the seams still showing.

First whistle of the day, from the high path:

‹two notes, level, then lifting›
— *coming up.*

Answered from below, after a decent interval:

‹two notes, level, lifting, the second held and weighted at the end›
— *coming up, with a load. slow.*

Then a third voice, a woman, from the near courtyard, a phrase the receiver took four months to parse because it belongs to no one but one child:

‹quick triplet, falling, then a small hooked rise›
— *third-course-above-the-door, left of the smoke-gap. water. now.*

They whistle the children by their place in the wall. A name here is an address in the family masonry, and the child comes when the wall calls.

[footsteps, unhurried, covering ground like weather]

---

**[hard light]**

The talus is chirping. Whole slopes of it, the round rodents whistling at each other, at the light, at nothing — the land's own gossip register. The people's whistles thread through it on different pitches and neither language yields right of way.

[an argument, two courtyards over: voices kept low and absolutely venomous. the words are indistinct. the receiver does not need the words. it is about the seep. it is always about the seep]

And then, mid-morning, cutting across everything, the formal register — the arbiter's whistle, pitched to carry, delivered slow so the whole slope hears it once and no one can dispute later what was said:

‹long phrases, level, ornamented at the ends, repeated twice in full›
— *the moss is divided at the gray vein in the rock. the eastern claim waters first on odd circuits. the western on even. so heard by the slope. so held.*

[the slope holds it. the argument two courtyards over does not resume]

Then, near noon:

‹a long level pair. repeated. repeated. coming closer along the bench›

— *a death.*

The receiver has heard this phrase from other benches, other cities, north along the range where it once was tuned. Here the second note falls a little further before it levels. A person walking the spine of the world could measure the whole distance of their walk by how far grief bends.

[the hush travels ahead of the procession, slope by slope, the talus-whistlers going quiet in a wave. the strolling pace. no voice. only the pair of notes, again, again, and the feet on stone, many feet, slow]

[high above, on the thermal, without one wingbeat: a shape detaches from the circling and begins its long flat glide toward the platform ledges. the receiver believes — from the span, from the pale wing-edges — that this is the great one, the bird the city has named, the one that has attended forty years]

[a good death, then. the notes stop at the ledge. the transmission does not follow past the ledge. that is not its place]

---

**[shadow crossing]**

Afternoon is the herd's hour. They are somewhere on the bench below, invisible, present only as a texture: hooves in aggregate, a low woolly complaint, the followers' long-distance whistles — a dialect stretched thin by absence, phrases that take four breaths and mean things like *the front has turned at the rope-span* and *count is good.*

[the rope-span itself, in the afternoon wind: a deep aeolian hum, the braided wool singing across the gorge. under it, rhythmic — the ropemaker, out on the anchor stones, striking the strands one by one and listening. rope people understand that things end. this is the sound of someone checking how soon]

*tock.*

[the pause. the ropemaker's striking stops for the pause too, mid-pattern, and resumes]

[a fragment of voice, close, an old man to no one, or to the wall:]

"...settle through the winter and take it up again. There's no course laid in anger that ever took its weight."

---

**[lamplight]**

The courtyards fill and the voices come down to arm's reach, where voices belong. The receiver can hold only the near courtyard now; the city has folded inward into a hundred small warm rooms of sound.

[six families. a fire of dung and patience. children in a heap like the rodents they love the stories about]

A man is telling the trader joke. The receiver has heard four tellings of the trader joke and this is the longest: the trader has been given a full lowland biography, a wife, ambitions, a hat. But the shape is the shape. The trader is late. The trader looks at the sun. The trader *runs.*

[the pause before the punchline is a mason's pause, load-bearing]

"...and then he had to sit down."

[laughter, kept low, the whole courtyard's shoulders moving more than its voices]

And later — the fire down to coals — a very old woman, folded, almost mineral, says into a gap in the talk:

"I was on the north bench. The year of the rockfall."

[the courtyard goes quiet. the quiet is not waiting for more. everyone understands that this is the whole story and that it is enough. the transmission also understands. it has been listening a long time. it will not say the name of what she saw either]

Then, far off, from the eastern quarter, threading in over the rooflines:

‹a quick pair, the second note lifted at the end, like a question already answered›

— *a birth.*

[and the phrase is taken up, courtyard to courtyard, passed westward along the bench, each courtyard sounding it once — not celebration exactly. acknowledgment. a receipt. a new stone has been delivered to the wall of the world and the slope signs for it]

[in the near courtyard, someone: "there. that's the day squared, then." a death at noon, a birth at lamplight. a course taken up, a course laid down. no one says this. it does not need saying]

[from below, through the smoke-gap, from the buried streets of the lamp-quarter where it is always evening: someone singing. very quietly. a song with long rests in it. the receiver has concluded, over many nights, that the rests are part of the song]

---

**[dark]**

The sounds withdraw in order, as they always do. The looms. The querns. The voices, room by room. The wall-sparrows settle and the houses stop breathing and begin, instead, to sleep.

Then the hush.

[it starts at the western edge of the talus and travels east — the whistling slopes going silent one after another, a wave of nothing moving across the notation at the pace of something walking. the receiver could describe what walks at that pace at that hour along the herd margins]

[the receiver will not. it finds being discussed rude]

[the hush passes. below the bench, once, a single herd animal complains and is answered by silence, which is, the followers would say, the tithe being taken, and the year being told it is a good one]

---

**[late]**

Wind in the chimneys. Nothing else.

No — one thing else. The loom behind the east wall, which started before the birds, has finally stopped. Whatever they were finishing is finished. Tomorrow, presumably, it begins settling.

The rock rose today. A finger-width a year is nothing you can hear; divide it by the days and it is nothing at all; and yet the receiver has kept this frequency long enough to believe that it hears it anyway — not as sound but the way the people hear it, as tenure. Somewhere under the six sleeping families, under the child-stones of six generations, under the jars already gathering the first film of tomorrow's allowance, the range is laying its own next course, unhurried, not expecting to be finished, not requiring anyone to see it done.

*tock.*
