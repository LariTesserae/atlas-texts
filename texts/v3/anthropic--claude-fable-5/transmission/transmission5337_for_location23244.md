ONE LONG NOTE, JOINED IN THE MIDDLE

—and the roar, still.

the roar, which is where you have been for some time now, which is where everything is. it did not begin for you and it will not end for you; you joined it late, the way everything joins it, the way the water joined it, the way the young join it, the way this reception joined it — tuned down through thin freezing sky and a skin of bright water into the sound, and the sound closed over the signal like a hand.

render it in strata, because that is the only honest way to render it:

at the bottom, NNNNNNNN — not a pitch, a pressure, the summed strokes of pistons four hundred kilometers apart and arriving all together, because the walls of this place give everything back and give it back sharpened. over that, the turbine tone: glassy, drawn-out, unending, a thousand intake throats swallowing a sea that has nowhere to go but up, and back, and up. over that, at the top, a fine bright hiss — the whole body of the water trembling against itself, which is the sound light would make if light made sound, and in water this clear it very nearly does.

that is the chord. that is the country. everything else in this transmission is furniture inside it.

· · ·

hold. the receiver has been holding. held long enough, the wall of noise opens into rooms.

there — west, by the lean of it, and very far — an engine running a shade flat. it has been a shade flat for nine years. it will be a shade flat for nine more. the sea knows its flatness the way you know a face. a nation of grazers exactly the size of that one machine carries its flatness in their shells, and will not breed with anyone who does not.

there — two of the great stations drifting through one another's phase, a beat forty years wide, currently in its rising decade. this is the only clock. everything alive was born somewhere inside this beat and feels in its jaw which part of the cycle is passing. the beat is not heard so much as inhabited. the receiver inhabits it now too.

and near — very near, on the housing of the closest running engine — a small dry sound under everything, patient, continuous: a mouth going along a line. a grazer is reading the wall. the engine writes its own voice onto itself in living mineral, in rings and branchings and nested curves, and the grazer follows the figure and eats the sentence, and the sentence regrows behind it in the same words, because the words are the sound and the sound does not change. forty years on one machine. it is somewhere in the middle of the text. it will never finish and it will never need to.

· · ·

a hush is arriving. plural.

[gap in the noise: approaching. many. small.]

a road of comparative quiet threads the near water — a nodal sheet, a surface where the roar very nearly cancels itself, fixed in place for ten thousand years — and a river is on the road. you do not hear the river. nothing here is heard. you hear the holes it makes: a million small bodies, each a tiny absorption, a tiny dimming, so that the river passes through the reception as a soft perforation of the thunder — like rain, if rain fell upward through the ceiling of a storm and left the storm pinpricked with silence.

now, watch — the far end of the column flinches. something has touched it, kilometers away, and the flinch comes down the river's whole length as a ripple of turning bodies, faster than any swimming thing could carry it, a zipper of turning running the road in a breath. the river is one animal when it needs to be. the need has passed. the perforation pours on.

and here is the other thing. mark it.

[                                                                        ]

a patch of water too well played. the roar goes through it unbent, unshadowed, unhollowed — a stretch of the chord with no flaw in it at all, and nothing honest is flawless here. every living body is a hole in the noise. only one kind of thing is not a hole in the noise.

the patch drifts toward the road at the speed of patience. at its edge the perforation frays — a few of the small silences swerving, having felt the wrongness, the terrible smoothness, the small approaching quiet that is the only monster this sea contains — and then one of the million holes in the noise closes. just one. a single soft subtraction, almost nothing, almost nothing at all.

the river pours on. the patch hangs, perfect, replete, invisible in plain bright water, in a country where visibility has meant nothing for ten thousand years.

· · ·

somewhere below and east stands a dead engine, split open, seized into sculpture, and inside it is the deepest hush the sea owns. the reception dips toward it the way everything eventually dips toward it.

inside: almost nothing. a murmur where the roar should be. eggs in the still water among the stopped flywheels, developing in a silence their parents fled from the moment the eggs were laid. and the floor beneath the eggs deep in the unburied dead — generations of them, perfectly kept, cold and clear, every grazer and swimmer and rider that turned at the end of its life toward the nearest quiet and went in and stopped. the nursery and the graveyard are the same room. no one here has ever thought this strange, because no one here has ever thought.

and now — this is worth the whole reception —

a hatchling goes out through the split casing.

it has lived its entire existence in the hush, in the dark of the machine, a body built to receive a world it has never touched. it crosses the threshold and the chord hits it all at once — every engine, every beat, every road, every distance, total sound flooding into an inherited map, and the map ignites, and the world becomes legible in a single stroke, and what the chord says to it, what the chord says to every one of them at the moment of second birth, is:

you are here. you are exactly here. you have always been exactly here.
welcome to the note.

· · ·

and under all of it, the nearest large voice. which the reception took, at first, for an engine.

it is not an engine.

engines do not breathe. this note breathes — a swell forty years long, riding the beat. engines do not correct. this note corrects: sixteen times inside this reception it has moved by less than anything, and each movement was a decision. it hangs at a point in the open water where no machine stands, and it sings the part of a machine that stopped before anything now alive was born, and it has not moved from that point, and it will not, because the note must come from exactly there.

listen long enough to one voice and the decoder does a forgivable thing: it begins to find a word in it.

rendered at the limit of confidence — one syllable, held across years:

— same —

that is the whole vocabulary. it may be a false reading. it may be the truest reading this receiver will ever make. it is what a note says when the entire meaning of the note is that nothing has changed; when a body has arranged the whole of itself, chamber and tension and patience, around the work of making the world stay the same world. the machines in this chord do not mean anything. the machines do not care whether they sound. the purest notes in the chord — the steadiest, the most exactly held — are the ones with nothing behind them but a body deciding, over and over, at every moment, for centuries, not to stop.

the chord is a census, if you read it that way. so many engines living. so many engines dead, and sung.

· · ·

and now — mark this, this is why the reception was built long —

the forty-year beat is carrying the whole basin down into a trough of phase. for a few hours, everywhere at once, the roar leans down by the smallest measurable degree. nothing swimming will notice. nothing grazing will notice. the trough is not for them.

in the lean, the choir talks.

it crosses the basin as a ripple of retuning, each great voice correcting against all the others, and rendered at the limit of confidence it comes through this receiver as the closest thing this world has to a conversation, which is a roll call, which is a census of the lonely, position by position, thousands of kilometers apart:

— held.
— held.
— high by a hair — coming true — true. held.
— held.
— flat, and holding flat, for the west station's drift — held.
— held.
— held.

each one a giant alone in bright empty water, hanging at the grave-point of a machine, confirming across the impossible distances the only fact any of them has:

— held.
— held.

·

a position does not answer.

the trough leans across a place where a note should rise to meet it, and nothing rises. the roar arrives there and finds a hole and pours through the hole and comes out changed. one voice has gone out of the chord since the last trough — not an engine this time; the singer itself, the understudy, dead at its post, and somewhere in some hushed hall there are ribs now the length of ships — and the standing structure of the sea shifts, everywhere, at once, by a hair.

a hair is the end of the world, locally and briefly, for everything alive.

in the hours and seasons after, the transmission fills with wrongness. roads that have not moved in ten thousand years move. a river arrives at a bend that is no longer there and shatters into a cloud of separate small silences, each one lost — and *lost* is a word this sea has not needed within living memory. a mill town's pilgrims miss the town. a year of young go out through split casings into a chord their inheritance does not match, maps igniting into maps of a slightly different world, born exactly here in a *here* that has moved. every body in the basin carries, now, a minutely lying map, and the choir cannot help, because each of them holds one note only, and none of them holds this one.

the reception holds. years cross it. the roar continues at full volume around the hole, and the hole continues too, a shaped silence hanging in the middle of everything, the loudest thing in the sea.

· · ·

then, out of the empty blue, at the pace of something very large and very young, a body comes to the place of the hole. and hangs there. and —

listen —

tries.

the first attempt cracks. flat, then sharp, then a long wavering ache of almost, a note like a hand closing on a hold and slipping and closing again. where it grew, and how it knew, and what its youth was — a childhood spent in silence-by-comparison, somewhere past the curve of the bright vacant miles, preparing for a single eternal syllable — nothing in this transmission can say. it is here. it is trying. the wrongness in the rivers eases and returns and eases, the roads trembling toward their old places and shying back, the whole sea listening, in the only sense that anything here has ever listened, to a young voice learning the shape of a grief older than its species.

at the next trough, the choir leans down around it, and the roll call runs the basin, and when it reaches the hole that is no longer quite a hole, the young voice comes through the decoder as:

— trying —

and the nearest old voice, the breather, the one that has said one word for longer than the rock has had soft edges, answers — at the limit of confidence, and the confidence here is high —

— with you.

— held.
— held.
— trying —
— held.
— held.

·

decade over decade, inside this reception, the wavering narrows. the cracks space themselves out and heal. the roads settle. the rivers run true and forget, because forgetting is what running true is. and on a day not otherwise different from any other day — there are no other kinds of day — the decoder, run once more against the young voice at the limit of confidence, returns:

— same —

and the chord closes over the place where the hole was, the way the sound closed over this signal at the start, like a hand.

· · ·

—and the roar, still.

the roar, which is where you are, which is where everything is. the flat engine is still flat. the grazer is somewhere in the middle of its sentence. the rivers are on the roads and the roads are where they have always been, again. down in the hushed halls the eggs are forming in silence, waiting for their second birth, and out in the bright empty water the great ones hang at their stations, breathing their forty-year breath, holding the dead engines' parts so that every map stays true, singing between them the one word the whole country is made of.

it does not end here. nothing about it ends here. an ending is a change, and change is the one thing this sea does not transmit. only the reception ends, and a reception is a small thing to subtract from a place that has never once been quiet — one more small hole in the noise, closing.
