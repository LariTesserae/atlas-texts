[slack water. the pistons idling. under everything, the hum, fifty to the second, out of the ground itself]

It is slack tide as this reaches you. Or it was, or it is about to have been; the tenses have worn round at the edges here, and we no longer pretend they stack. The eels are unscrewing. Listen — that long threaded creak, thread leaving groove, three meters of patience coming out of its socket. They meet now in the still cylinders and lay the next century into the bore-walls, where the strokes will sit on the eggs like a hen made of pressure, and they are gone before the flood takes the columns up again.

If, in what follows, you hear a splash before you hear a launch, do not adjust your receiver. That is an elder, hunting in the slack of things. Do not grieve for the rasp. It has already been eaten and has not yet died, and it holds both conditions comfortably, the way a bell holds a note it has finished ringing.

When you reach the line it reflects, you will know it the way the mothers know their calves at the pool-rims: already grown, an hour from now, doing what it will have done.

[a stitcher has fed overhead. the following interval runs true. cause will precede effect on this channel for approximately three hundred heartbeats. we will spend them plainly.]

Quickly, then, and in order, while order holds:

This is the Foundry Fens. Nothing here was built and nothing here arrived. We are the place, practicing being alive. We were not made and we are not haunted; we are worn, which is older than both.

You have been tuned to us for some time. Understand what tuning is. It is a seam, and water finds seams. Your receiver is not a window. It is a bore, and the tide is already in it, and the wearing has begun on you at the edges, where it always begins: at the sequence. Check your echoes. Check your reflection for lead or lag. Count your footsteps against your feet and see which arrives first. You are not in danger. You are in a ledger. There is a difference, though it is thinning.

One more thing, in order and true, because the interval allows it and we may not get another: the stroke that nothing makes is older than the pistons it hides among. It does not hunt. It does not call. It keeps its place, and it has kept it since before *place* was a thing that needed keeping. Everything here is traveling toward it the way water travels toward flat. We are not troubled by this and we do not expect you to understand why not. But when your count of us comes out one heavy — and it will, it always does, you have already noticed it under everything —

that is it, counting you back.

Hear how it sits in the thinning, now that the chord has gone. Never louder. Never missing. Patient as flat water.

A note has been added to the song, low and provisional. It is holding your place. This is not a welcome; it is an entry, and it has not finished being sung. And here — arriving now, at its own angle, the line that skew-pool was reflecting all along:

*leave before it finishes.*

Or stay. Stay, and be worn true, edge first, sequence first, and in time enough — and there is nearly all the time there is — be a stroke in the song with nothing left to make you. The fens do not distinguish between these outcomes. To the fens they are both grazing.
