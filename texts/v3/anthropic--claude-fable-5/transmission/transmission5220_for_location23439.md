**CARRIED UP**

—and under everything, the tick.

Not a clock. Slower than a clock and less honest. The rock speaks in single syllables spaced too far apart to make a rhythm: *tik.* Then nothing for the length of a held breath. Then *tik*, a half-tone lower, somewhere else, deeper in the ridge, as if the first sound had been considered and answered.

The air carries almost nothing. What it carries:

the furnaces, breathing. In and hold and out. A hundred small orange mouths exhaling in no agreed rhythm, so the hush has a texture to it, a grain, like cloth.

the drop-hammer in the lower forge, landing. A knuckle on a door. *Knock.* Long pause. *Knock.* Someone at the bottom of the world, asking politely to be let in, forever.

wings, once — a single swift crossing the seam high up, a leathery snip of sound, gone.

Then light traffic. The receiver takes the lamps as pulse:

⟨ long — long — short — shutter — long ⟩
*…canisters up. Nine by weight. Warden confirms nine. Nine.*

⟨ short short short — hold ⟩
*Received.*

⟨ a slow banked glow, dimming, brightening, work-signal register ⟩
*Girders from the old winch-house: fourteen sound, six sprung, two for the breakers. Send hands.*

And beneath the public channel, threading it, a fast intimate flicker between two points on opposing tiers — a stutter almost too quick to hold, hand-shadow passing over flame, laughter rendered as light —

[ decode declined. private register. the shape of it, though: teasing. an old joke retold. the answering lamp trembles in a way that is not tiredness and is not cold ]

—arc-welder flash, violet, everything whites out—

—signal restored. The tick again. *Tik.*

.

.

A steel rod, set in the living rock, and a mouth pressed to it. The receiver rides the rod now, hears what the teeth hear:

the groan. Long, hull-deep, a great ship settling on a swell that takes an hour to pass. Within the groan, granular detail: the dry creak of strata shouldering, a crystalline crackle like frost forming though nothing here has ever frosted twice, and — there. There. A note that is not settling. A note with *intention* in it. High in the ridge, above the fourth tier, a tone the width of a hair, sustained.

The listener's breath stops against the rod. Starts again, slower.

Then the wires.

A low note, plucked — felt before heard, felt in the feet of every soul in the chamber, in the girders, in the drying felt the voles have stolen, in the lamp housings where the lizards lie along the warmth. The root note. Attention. Then the interval every body in the fissure knows before it knows its own name:

root — falling fourth — root — falling fourth —

*shudder-warning. shudder-warning.*

Then, slower, spelled out along the great tensioned cables so the deaf rock itself could read it:

*Upper gallery, east face, fourth tier. Four days. The mountain has said four days. Condemned. Condemned. Salvage begins at first watch.*

No siren. No voice raised. The hush does not break; it *organizes.* Across the whole chamber, lamps begin to move — dozens, then a hundred, streaming up ladders and along chain bridges, a slow constellation reorganizing itself around a single dark point on the east face.

The receiver holds on the condemned gallery for a long time. Sounds, close in:

pry-bars easing rivets that were set forty years ago by hands now folded in the deep rock. Each rivet gives with a small cry — *skree* — and then a ringing tap as it is caught, never dropped, caught, and passed palm to palm down the line.

plate after plate coming off the frame, lowered on lines, everything lowered, nothing thrown, the whole building descending piece by piece like a body being undressed with great care.

a child — the breathing gives the age away, quick and light against the tidal bellows-breath of the adults — working at the seam of a small dormitory cell. The cell they were born in. The crew has stepped back to let them. The last plate comes free in their hands and behind it is bare rock, and the receiver catches, at whisper range, at the range of shared warmth, a voice — an adult's, high and breathy and almost gone:

"…there. That wall heard your first — " [lost] " — keep the third rivet, the third one held longest — "

A small ringing tap. Kept.

.

.

Third watch of the fourth day, and the slab lets go.

It falls where the listeners said it would fall, into the emptied scaffold-space, and eight kilometers of thin air smothers it to almost nothing: a *whump*, felt more than heard, a soft fist into a pillow, and then a long hiss of rock-flour, and then the tick, resumed, satisfied, a half-tone lower than before.

Every lamp in the city flashes once, together. One beat of light, chamber-wide.

*No one. No one. No one.*

.

.

But the wires speak again before dawn, and this note is different. Struck once, hard, then damped by a palm:

a pitch, and the interval of a rising sixth.

A name. Her name. The receiver has heard it plucked before, in work-signal, in roll-calls, once in what may have been an argument. Now it is plucked and then — the second interval, the one appended only once in a life:

the rising sixth, and beneath it, the deep closing tone.

*Carried up.*

The old rigger. The one whose lamp trembled with laughter across the tiers. Not the slab — the slab took no one — just the ordinary settling of a body that had hung one-handed over the drop for ninety rebuildings and finally, in her sleep, let go of the cable.

The procession goes down. The receiver goes with it, past the lowest forge where the knuckle knocks on the door, down where the orange light thins out and the rock changes voice — because down here the tick runs backward, the seams are *closing*, centimeters a year, the mountain inhaling. The wrapped shape is small. Dried, folded, light as lagging felt. Her cord of rivets goes with her, dozens of them, every room and bridge and winch-house she outlived, and one rivet is taken off the cord and kept by the child from the fourth tier, who has, this watch, two rivets, and a whole life of wire under their feet.

They set her in the pinch of the stone.

And here the receiver picks up the smallest sound of the whole transmission, smaller than the tick, smaller than the swift's wing:

drops. Falling on wrapped cloth. Unhurried. Unwiped. Counted by no warden. One. A pause the length of a furnace-breath. Another. Water, given away, in the one place in the world where it is beyond price — and close in, mouths against the cloth, the private channel, fragments only:

"…plumb, you were, the whole — "
"…for now, for now — "
" — drink, up there. Drink everything."

The mountain will close over her. Ten years to a seam. A lifetime to a line. Then inside, rising, a few centimeters a year, toward the sky.

.

.

First light does not arrive here; it merely fails to darken. The ribbon overhead goes from white-grey to a harder white-grey, and the swifts erupt.

They go out *angry* — the whole flue-warm thousand of them at once, a boiling silent smoke poured upward through the crack, and the thin air renders their storm as a pressure only, a soundless roar, a change in the weight of the hush. High in the topmost cables, among the anchors, a folded leathery shape shifts, once, resettling its patience. On a particular bracket, something small and freshly taken has been left. Religion or rent. The channel does not specify.

And then, down the long funicular cable toward the drowned loud world below, someone begins the sentence.

It is flashed slowly. It takes a full minute. The receiver takes it whole, glow by banked glow, and will not compress it:

⟨ a long light, held, brightening by degrees ⟩

*The mountain rises.*

⟨ shuttered — opened — a climbing stutter, each pulse a half-step higher in warmth ⟩

*The city climbs.*

⟨ then the light widens — no flicker now, no grammar, the shutter taken fully away, the lamp simply burning, longer than any word has a right to be — ⟩

*And one morning the door will be the whole sky,*

⟨ — and at the very end, two brief pulses, small, almost casual, like a hand let go of a rail — ⟩

*and whoever is here*

*will simply step out.*

.

The lamp banks down. Work-signal resumes on the east face; fourteen sound girders are rising to be set three meters higher.

The furnaces breathe. The knuckle knocks. Somewhere on the second tier a private flicker starts up again, too fast to hold, the shape of a joke being retold with a name now missing from it, and the answering lamp trembles, and it is not tiredness, and it is not cold.

*Tik.*

Plumb for now.

*Tik.*
