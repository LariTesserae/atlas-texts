And then.

The static thins.

Not everywhere. One quarter. The northeast quarter of the Roof goes quiet the way a held breath goes quiet, and the cricket-wave rolling toward that wall stops short, and before the signal has finished noticing, the voices are already moving, fast and flat and still not shouting, because nothing down here shouts, not even now:

—hhhhaaaaaaaa—

Time passes the only way it can: eleven to the minute.

And then the Roof begins to change key.

The whole ceiling-static rising, gathering, a pitch climbing through it like a decision being made by ten thousand bodies at once. Somewhere overhead, past a mile of mountain, the sun is doing something that nothing in this chamber has ever seen. The Roof knows anyway. The Roof always knows.

It goes up like smoke that has learned to want something.

Ten minutes of soft thunder. The ceiling pouring itself, spiraling, up into the bore — the loudest gentle sound there is — and the chamber's top layer of noise peels away and travels, and the signal travels partway with it, up through the vapor, up through the wings—

The signal is fluent now. It came down a drylander and it has sweated through. It can read the whole architecture: the Roof will come home before the shift ends, folding out of the bore in ones and twos and then in weather. The snail on seven will make the pipe elbow in nine years, and someone not yet hired will be there to note it. The flowstone will take its inch, and the inch will be given, because it is rent, and rent is how you know you live somewhere. Far off — generations off, cold and dry and unbelievable — there is the Leaving, when everything here goes up the bore together, wings and shells and boots and one great black iron body in pieces on the cage. Nobody dreads it. It only says out loud what the dark already knows: same kind of thing, all of it. Tenants of a warmth. Residents of a breath.
