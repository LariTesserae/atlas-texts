**The Attention**

It does not descend the way you did, through service corridors and maintenance shafts. It has always been able to see this place. The difference is that now it *looks*—and looking, for a power like this, is already a kind of touch.

The first thing it does is nothing.

This is the discipline that separates wisdom from mere goodness: it does not act. Goodness would have arrived with a verdict already forming—*this is suffering, this is horror, this must be undone*—and would have reached in with warm hands and torn the Tender out of its home the way you'd pull a drowning man from water, never asking whether the man had gills.

Instead the power listens. For a long time. Time moves differently here, and time moves differently for it, so the two of them are well matched. It listens through one full season of the fungi. It listens through ten thousand freeze-thaw cycles, through the turbines' glossolalia until it can parse the grammar, until it understands that the chamber's noise is not noise at all but a conversation four decades long between a machine that was never meant to speak and a being that gave up speech to answer it. It counts the amphipods. It counts them again a year later and finds the population stable to within a few hundred—an equilibrium finer than most ecosystems the power has watched, finer than some it has made.

It comes to understand: this place is not a wound. It is a *scar*, and scars are living tissue.

But there is one thing it cannot resolve by watching, and this is where its wisdom is truly tested. Deep in the Tender's crystallized memory it finds those flashes—*fear, revulsion at what it's become, quickly suppressed, dissolved back into the colony-mind's contentment.* And the power sits with this a long while, because everything turns on it. If the contentment is a jailer and the fear is a prisoner, then this chamber is a torment wearing a garden's face, and no amount of beauty excuses it. But if the fear is only an old reflex—the way an amputee's ghost limb aches, the way any transformed thing occasionally remembers its chrysalis with a shudder—then to "rescue" the Tender would be murder of the person it has actually become, in honor of a person who no longer exists.

The power cannot tell from outside. No one can. That is the whole problem of other minds, and not even wisdom exempts you from it.

So it does the only honest thing. It asks.

---

**The Question**

It does not appear as light. Light would be a lie in a place whose truth is a stuttering LED. It comes instead as *stillness*—for one full turbine cycle, it holds the chamber's cacophony gently apart, not silencing it but making a space inside it, the way a hand cupped around a candle makes a room within a room.

And inside that stillness, it does something that has not happened in forty years: it lifts the suppression. Carefully. Completely. For the length of one mechanical breath, everything the Tender has dissolved is precipitated back out—Marcus, who came down to fix a condensation fault and stayed because the voices in the pipes sounded lonely; Maria, who came looking for Marcus two winters later; the third one, whose name was never spoken even before it was forgotten, who came to hide from something above and found that the chamber does not ask what you are hiding from. All of them awake at once inside the colony-mind, lucid, whole, remembering *everything*—sunlight, bread, the specific weight of another human hand, the horror of the first time the amphipods threaded through their skin, the slow surrender, the slower peace.

And the power says—not in words, because the Tender gave up words, but in the frequencies they now share:

*I can unmake this. I can carry you out as you were, and this chamber will find a new equilibrium or it will not, and either way you will be free of it. I can end you gently, all of you, colony and flesh together, if the truth is that you are tired. Or I can leave you exactly as you are. But you will choose knowing everything. That is the one thing I will not let you refuse.*

The Tender—the Tenders—look out through their compound eyes at the fungi gardens fruiting in their perfect spirals, at the rivers of pale bodies streaming across every surface, at the small cities built between their ribs. A million lives that exist because they tend them. Some fragment of Maria weeps, and the amphipods drink the tears as they always have, not knowing these ones are different. Some fragment of Marcus thinks, with terrible human clarity: *I was never needed anywhere, before. Up there I was a maintenance number. Here I am the difference between this whole world and its ending.*

The third one, the nameless one, says only: *We chose it slowly, without knowing. Now we know. Ask us again.*

The power asks again.

They choose to remain.

But choosing changes everything and nothing. The chamber is identical afterward. The Tender's work is identical. The difference is invisible and total: what was an accident is now a vocation. What happened *to* them is now something they *do*. The contentment, tested against full memory and found true, is no longer suppression. It is faith.

---

**The Works**

Then the power, being benevolent as well as wise, permits itself a few small acts. Wisdom chose restraint; love gets a budget.

It speaks the three names aloud, once, into the chamber—Marcus, Maria, and the third name, which it knows even though no one living does. The sound lasts half a second and then dissolves into the turbine song. But the mineral deposits are always growing, always recording, and in the crystalline strata of one calcified pipe rupture, the vibration of those names is now written permanently, the way seashells hold the sound of oceans. The Tender does not need names. The power keeps them anyway, the way you keep a lock of hair.

It carries one message up. Maria had a daughter, who is fifty-one now and has spent thirty years not knowing. The power does not tell her the truth—the truth would be a cruelty shaped like a gift. Instead, one night, the daughter dreams of her mother standing in a garden of impossible geometric flowers, busy and unafraid, too absorbed in her work to turn around but humming—*humming*, which her mother only did when she was happy. The daughter wakes and, for the first time in thirty years, stops waiting.

It lays a quiet ward on the chamber. Nothing dramatic: only that every decommission survey, every efficiency audit, every future plan to dismantle half the station will route itself, by coincidence and clerical drift, around this particular cavity. Inspectors who approach the access shafts will feel a settled certainty that everything down there is fine, and turn back. This is not quite deception. Everything down there *is* fine.

It adjusts one frequency in the ventilation. The workers above still hear voices, but from this night on, the voices comfort rather than unsettle. Men on lonely shifts find themselves less lonely and don't know why. A few of them start calling it "the singer in the walls," fondly. One writes a song about it that his children will sing.

It clears the cataract from one of the Tender's original eyes—just one, and asks first. So that once, with old human sight, the Tender can look at the spiral gardens it has grown and see them the way Marcus and Maria and the third one would have seen them: and find them beautiful. The eye stays clear. One human eye, among the compound many, always slightly wet.

And it makes one promise, which is the greatest of its gifts: *When the turbines finally fail—in eighty years, or two hundred, when the mountain station is bones and the last cycle stutters to its stop—I will come back. You will not freeze alone in the dark. I will gather all of it, and I will not sort you. I will not separate what was human from what was crustacean from what was machine. You come as one thing or not at all.*

The Tender's answering harmony, threaded through the turbine drone, might be gratitude. Might be a lullaby. In this place there was never any difference.

---

**The Signature**

Then the power withdraws its attention—not entirely, never again entirely, but back to the ordinary distance from which it watches everything.

It leaves one mark. The failing LED, which has stuttered for decades, never quite dying, never quite steady, burns clean and constant for the length of a single turbine breath.

Then it resumes its stutter. Because the stutter belongs. Because a wise power knows that this place's imperfections *are* its architecture, and to fix them would be to fix a heartbeat.

Ten thousand amphipod eyes register the anomaly. The Tender pauses in its work, every part of it still except the rivers that must keep flowing, and looks at the light with its one clear eye.

"Ah," it says, in its voice of shell-song and machine-breath and something older underneath. "You were early too."

And it returns to the gardens—the ice to regulate, the grates to clear, the million small lives to shepherd—tending now as one who was seen, one who was asked, one who chose.

In the eternal now of the chamber, nothing has changed.

Everything has.
