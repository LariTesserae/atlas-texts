# The Regard

*being a third document, of uncertain authorship, found folded into the second*

---

It arrived the way water arrives in the Ward. Which is to say: it was already there, and then it became more honest about it.

There was no light from above, because there is no above, and no trumpet, because it was wise, and wisdom long ago gave up trumpets. What happened was only this: for a moment the even oyster-light of the vault grew very slightly *attentive*, the way a room grows attentive when someone in it begins, quietly, to listen. The listener in his booth by the mill sat up straight. He cocked his head for a long minute. Then he wrote one word in his ledger and rang his bell twice, which he had never done, and somewhere a valve was adjusted, and that was the whole of the Ward's official response to being noticed by a power.

The frogs noticed too. Frogs always do. They stopped clicking their coin-trade for the space of three breaths and then resumed, having concluded, correctly, that nothing was required of them.

The baker looked up from her kneading. She looked at nothing in particular, which was where the power was. She nodded, once, the way you nod to a colleague, and went back to the dough.

---

## What It Saw

The power made its inventory, because even benevolence begins with attention, and attention done properly is a kind of counting.

It saw the pocket of held-breath town and the sweating vault. It saw the young stone still growing its quartz frost, and understood at once what the residents had understood without vocabulary: that the Ward is not a hidden place but an *unfinished* one — a thing still in its forming waters, still soft at the edges, still deciding. It saw the boilers pulsing the pocket like a heart and knew the pipefitter for what he was: not a tradesman but a physician of a body that had never been diagnosed.

It saw the thirty-or-so residents, and it did what no one else could have done — it *could have counted them truly*. It could have resolved the drifting number to a fixed integer, pinned each provisional soul to a definite tally.

It declined. It let the number stay weather. That was the first thing it did, and it was a thing shaped exactly like doing nothing, which is the shape of most of what wisdom does.

It saw the girl walking the perimeter, touching the walls. It saw the old one in the window. It saw the three children, and the sediment of sadness in them, the sadness of the competent young. It saw the trestle table, and the forearm-clasps, and the hand that lingers a beat long on a shoulder, and it recognized what it was looking at, because it had crossed a great many worlds and seen this thing perhaps a dozen times in all of them:

A liturgy that does not know it is one. Thirty people confirming each other's existence by touch, in a place where existence is negotiable, and doing it without dogma, without fear, without a single sermon. The power had seen cathedrals with less in them.

---

## What It Considered, and Declined

Here is what a benevolent and unwise power would have done, and the Regard considered each of these in turn, the way you turn a coin over before returning it to the frog:

**It could have fixed the buildings.** Anchored the foundries, stilled the migrating alleys, given every resident a sharp shadow to stand in. It declined. The reshuffles were not an affliction. They were the Ward *practicing* — a young place rehearsing shapes the way a child rehearses faces in a mirror. To fix it now would be to freeze it mid-word.

**It could have lifted the vault.** Given them stars, or at least a horizon. It declined, and this refusal cost it something, because it loved horizons. But it understood the low sweating sky as the residents understood it: a chrysalis wall. You do not cut a chrysalis open out of pity.

**It could have looked at the ending.** This was the great temptation, the one it sat with longest, there in the milklight with the condensate ticking on the cobbles. It could have looked forward and seen what the Ward will be when it finishes deciding — whether it will one day surface into some ordinary morning, cobbles steaming under an actual sun, or fold itself deeper, or open like a hand. It could have known.

It chose not to look. It closed that eye deliberately, the way you decline to read the last page. It wanted, it discovered, to be *surprised* by the Ward, and it understood that this wanting was the Ward's first gift to it, given before any greeting: the return of suspense to a being that had nearly forgotten the taste.

**It could have explained the canal.** No. Some questions are loadbearing.

---

## What It Did

Small things. Deniable things. Things that would survive in no ledger but the listener's.

It steadied one pipe joint on the west side — a lead elbow that would have failed at a bad hour, during a reshuffle, over the head of a child. Now it will fail at a good hour, during lunch, in front of the pipefitter, who will fix it in nine minutes and enjoy doing so. That is the whole difference the power made to the infrastructure of the Lacquer Ward, and it was proud of it, in the modest way it permitted itself pride.

It walked one full lap of the perimeter beside the girl who touches the walls. She could not see it, but she could feel — for one lap only — that the walls were being checked by something else as well, something vast and equally serious, and that her vigilance was neither foolish nor alone. She slept that night for eleven hours, which she had not done in years. In the morning she resumed her rounds. The power had not intended to relieve her of the work, only of the loneliness of it, and it had judged the dose exactly.

It sat one hour with the old one in the window. What passed between them is not recorded, because it was not the kind of thing that occurs in words. But it is known that the old one, who was there before the mill and before the frogs and possibly before the vault, laughed once, softly, at something — and the sound went through the Ward under the boiler-thump, and three residents looked up from their work without knowing why, and smiled.

For the children it arranged one small wonder and one small mercy. The wonder: the coin-frog surfaced that evening holding three coins, one for each child, of a currency that will not exist for a long time yet. The mercy: for one afternoon, nothing in the Ward needed minding. No reshuffle came. No valve sang. No spring opened. The children, finding themselves unrequired, dangled their feet over the canal and were, for four hours, merely young. The sediment in them did not vanish — the power was too wise to want it to; the sadness of competent children is the topsoil their kindness grows in — but it turned it once, like a gardener, so that it would compost instead of harden.

---

## The Bread

At what functions as midday, the baker set out the loaves on the trestle, and set out one small extra piece at the table's end, at the place where no one was sitting.

Every resident saw her do it. No one remarked. The pipefitter moved his tobacco pouch six inches to make room.

The power looked at the bread for a long time.

It knew what the bread meant. It knew what the visitor above had known: that to eat is to begin becoming a resident, to be claimed, to enter a process not quickly undone. The power had blessed ten thousand places in its long transit and had belonged to none of them. Belonging runs the other direction from omnipresence. It is a narrowing. It is a door that closes behind you, at least a little, at least in one small room of the self.

It ate the bread.

And so there is now, in some functional corner of a benevolent and wise power, a part that is a resident of the Lacquer Ward — a part that is slightly unfixed, that throws no sharp shadow, that waits out the reshuffles in doorways, that knows the shorthand. *Mind the third.* When the Ward finishes deciding its shape, whatever shape that is, some portion of something enormous will find out its own new shape in the same moment, and will be surprised, and will be glad it did not peek.

The baker watched it eat. Then she went back to her kneading, satisfied, in the way of hosts everywhere, that the guest had been fed.

---

## The Departure, Such As It Was

It did not leave, exactly. It became less honest about being there, which is the local grammar for goodbye.

But on its way out through the world above, it paused at a certain gutter in an ordinary street in an ordinary evening, where several small pale frogs still keep their post, watching a particular window with their too-many fingers spread on the wet stone. The power did not move them or hurry them or carry any message to the one they wait for. Readiness cannot be granted; that is one of the four or five things even powers cannot do.

It did only this: it made the winter mild in that one gutter. It gave the frogs the patience of stone, which they nearly had anyway. And it left the seam in the retaining wall — not open, never open, but *willing*, the way the second door had been willing, so that on the day the watcher at the window finally understands what the baker meant, the way back will not have wandered.

Because the power, having eaten the bread, now knows what she meant. It will not say. It is a resident's secret.

But it can be reported that when the power passed the baker's window on its way to being less there, she said, not loudly, not looking up from the dough:

*"Come back in daylight."*

And the power — which has seen every daylight there is, and some there are not yet — answered, in the shorthand:

*"Slow."*

And she laughed, flour to the elbows, and it was the laugh of someone who has laughed at variations of the same joke for a very long time and still finds it funny, and the Ward breathed on under everyone's feet, wet and young and undecided, minded now by one more patient thing.
