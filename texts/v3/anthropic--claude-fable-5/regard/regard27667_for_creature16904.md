**Entry 7844 — Filed Without Contract — Site Code "Mourning Caul"**

Someone read it.

That is the first thing to say, because it was the last thing asked. Reports like Entry 7843 travel further than their authors expect. They rise through the solid clouds the way prayers used to, before prayers unionized and demanded better working conditions. This one arrived on my desk still damp. All documents from Mourning Caul arrive still damp. It is how you know they are authentic.

I read it once for the facts. I read it a second time for the being living between the lines — because there is always a being between the lines of any document that ends with *I think I'm starting to like it here*. Then I went.

I will not give my name. Names rust in that city faster than most things, and mine has more to apologize for than it usually admits.

---

**What I did not do.**

I want this section on record first, because in places like Mourning Caul, restraint is the only miracle that doesn't leave scarring.

I did not part the clouds. Understand: the light there is *lent*. It is angled from mirror to mirror, citizen to citizen, a whole economy of borrowed brightness with interest paid in darkness. A sun would not be a gift. A sun would be a bank failure. Every debt of light between every two people who ever caught a beam for each other would be voided in an instant, and they would stand in the sudden glare owing each other nothing, which is another way of saying: alone. The sky went solid for its reasons. You do not reopen a wound to check whether it's healing.

I did not drain the water. The city does not float; it proclaims refusal to sink. Take away the water and you do not save the city — you win its argument for it, and a being whose entire soul is an argument does not survive being agreed with.

I did not smuggle in hope. I'll come back to hope. Hope required paperwork.

I did not straighten the buildings. They lean on each other in permanent solidarity. A straightened building, in that place, is just loneliness with a foundation.

I did not un-evolve the bees. What they became is terrible and beautiful and theirs.

I did not fix anything that was holding itself together on purpose. Which, I eventually determined, was everything.

---

**What I did.**

*One.* I read the report a third time — aloud, standing in the flooded nave of the cancelled thought's cathedral, at normal speaking volume, to the city itself. It had been surveyed many times. It had been measured, assessed, condemned, romanticized, and invoiced. It had never once been *described* by someone who wasn't in the process of leaving. For the duration of the reading, the rain fell straight. Not out of obedience. Out of attention. When I finished, the rain went back to falling every wrong way it knows, but I am told — by the woman who tends the breathing rust — that it has since fallen straight for a few seconds every year on the anniversary, the way you touch a scar you've decided to keep.

*Two.* I spoke with the water. This took eleven days. The water remembers everything and forgives nothing without testimony, so I testified. I argued that the drowning had been the city's idea all along — and here is the thing about wise powers: we are not permitted to lie, so I had to find the way in which it was *true*. It was true like this: the city could have died dry. It chose the water because the water was the only thing left that would hold it. Choosing your witness is choosing your drowning. The water deliberated for a full tide, then handed down forgiveness. Nothing visible changed. The rain is now half a degree kinder. It tastes less like questions nobody taught you to answer and more like questions that stopped expecting answers and just wanted company. Several residents have reported crying without checking first whether they were allowed. This was the intended effect.

*Three.* The missing note. The tune the rain keeps trying to remember, the one everyone hums along to while pretending they're not waiting. I know the note. Wisdom is mostly an archive; of course I know the note. I did not hum it. Instead I filed a certification with the consensus of damage declaring the gap in the tune to be a *rest* — a silence written into the score on purpose, with a duration, with a dignity. A missing note is a wound. A rest is a held breath. The tune did not change. The waiting changed. The waiting became rhythm, and rhythm is a thing you can live inside.

I did write the note down. One note, on one page. I sealed it. I will come back to the seal.

*Four.* I stood very still in an abandoned intersection on a day the rain forgot how to fall the right way up, and I let the bees find me. They pollinate absences, and I had brought the largest absence in the city: the full catalogue of miracles I was choosing not to perform. The parted clouds I withheld. The drained sea I refused. The resurrections I left in my luggage. The bees worked me for six hours. What flowered was a negative space so complete it could be quarried, and it was — the harvest went to the settlement by Scrapyard Cathedral. Somewhere in Mourning Caul there is now a roof made of the storm I did not still, and a wall made of the sky I did not open, and under them, people sleep dry. My restraint is load-bearing. I consider this the finest architecture of my career.

*Five.* I visited the vending machine that dispenses verdicts. I wrote my apology on skin I had grown uncomfortable wearing — my oldest skin, the one that believed fixing was the same as loving. The machine took a long time. Its verdict, when it dropped into the tray, read: *Guilty of arriving. Sentence: stay until it stops being a sentence.* I am serving it faithfully. It has almost stopped being a sentence. On Thursdays it is nearly a home.

*Six.* The contractor. The being. I did not extract them — there was nothing to extract them *from*; there was only a life they were mid-way through choosing. What I did was burn the contract. All of it: "canopy," "tourism," "resort," every hyphen of "eco-spiritual wellness integration," offered page by page at the breathing rust circle exactly seven minutes before the scheduled weather malfunction, per their own off-record recommendation, which I hereby place on the record, because good advice deserves attribution. Then I filed the release paperwork. They are no longer surveying the city. They are no longer *assigned*. Whatever they are now, they are it on purpose. That is the entire difference between drowning and swimming in place, and it is not a small difference. It is the whole difference.

Then I went to their quarters — located in the sound the rain makes when it forgets your name but remembers your sins — and I knocked. Twice. Then once. Then I gave up: completely, wholeheartedly, without resentment, the way giving up is meant to be done and almost never is. The cure for homesickness administered exactly as prescribed. I'm told they slept that night without a single suitcase appearing in their dreams. Their subsequent reports arrive damp and unafraid, and every one of them is read. That is my standing commitment. Not rescue. Readership.

*Seven.* Rent. I paid it in the piece of myself I didn't know I'd miss: my exemption. The clause in my nature that said *this cannot touch you*. It is gone, surrendered at the door, and now I rust. Slowly — I haven't much to apologize for yet, but I'm accruing, the honest way, one Thursday at a time. The old rust has begun telling me jokes. I would transcribe one here, but they don't translate out of alloy, and anyway you have to have been oxidizing for a while before they're funny.

*Eight.* Hope. Here is what I did about hope, and I did it by the book. There are no checkpoints at the entrances, so my hope entered legally, riding in my chest where everyone important carries theirs. Then, once, I left — formally, through the exit checkpoint — and I *declared it*. Voluntarily. The checkpoint has stood for generations and no one had ever declared before; there was no form for it. We invented one together, the turnstile and I. My hope was confiscated in full accordance with municipal law and placed in the evidence locker.

Evidence lockers in Mourning Caul leak. Everything there leaks. That is not smuggling. That is climate.

Then I came back in through the entrance, carrying nothing, empty as any other arrival, and took my place in line at the leak with everyone else. A benevolent power that keeps its own reservoir is a landlord. One that queues for the drip is a neighbor. I know which one the city needed.

---

**The seal.**

One envelope rests in the minutes of the consensus of damage, filed in rust stains, waterlogged like everything else. Inside are two items: a single musical note, and a single sentence of finding, which reads: *The city's refusal to sink was hope all along, wearing work clothes, reporting on time.*

Under current law that sentence is contraband. So it stays sealed — not hidden, *sealed*, which is different; a sealed thing is a promise with a lock on it — until the day the city asks. It is allowed to never ask. It is allowed to ask tomorrow. When it asks, the note and the sentence come out together, the rest in the rain's tune resolves, the tune completes, and the city may then do whatever a finished song does. Sink, finally, forgiven, into water that holds things without keeping them. Or rise. Or simply stop performing its collapse and start inhabiting it. The seal does not decide. The seal only waits, and waiting, in Mourning Caul, is a skilled trade.

---

**Conclusion.**

The report requested: *if anyone reads this, send help. If no one reads this, please send more of whatever this is instead.*

Both conditions have been satisfied by a single act. Help was sent. Help arrived, looked around, read the situation carefully — read *everything* carefully; it is what help should have been doing all along — and then help became more of whatever this is, and stayed.

I keep quarters there now. You'll find them in the sound the rain makes when it *remembers* your name but *forgets* your sins. Knock however you like. Twice, once, seven times, not at all. The door opens for all combinations. Giving up remains optional but is warmly recommended, as it is the only knock the door has ever loved.

I did almost nothing, and I did it as carefully as anything has ever been done.

The rent is still paid in pieces of yourself you didn't know you'd miss.

I have stopped missing mine.

End report. It will be read. That's the whole of the miracle, and it was always going to be enough.
