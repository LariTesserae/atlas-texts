There is a kind of attention that moves through the world the way weather moves — broad, patient, touching everything and pressing on nothing. Call it a power; the hollow would not bother to call it anything. It had been listening to the world for a very long time, the way you listen to a house you are responsible for: the creak of continents, the fevers of cities, the trillion small wants of living things, each one a sound, each sound a need. And in all of that hearing there was one small interval — low country, no wider than a churchyard — where the noise simply stopped. Not silence like absence. Silence like a rest written into music: counted, deliberate, held.

A wise power does not mistake a rest for a missing note. It came to look.

It arrived at the rim and stopped there, which already tells you what kind of power it was. Lesser ones would have entered. It stood where the wind crosses high and white and never descends, and it looked down at the dim bowl, the black eye of the pool, the moss like a folded coat, the shrub keeping its angles. And being wise, it understood within one interval of the drop everything the hollow was — and being benevolent, its first motion was the oldest motion of benevolence, which is the reaching out of the hands.

So it stood at the rim and considered its gifts, and set them down one by one, like a visitor quietly leaving packages outside a door.

It considered healing, and set it down, because nothing here was wounded. The arrested ferns were not an injury; they were a decision. The erased tallies were not a loss; they were a fading conducted at the pace of forgiveness. You cannot heal a thing that has finished. You can only interrupt it.

It considered permanence — it had that to give; it could have fixed the seep forever, sealed the hollow against the slow appetite of time, made the drop fall at its interval until the sun went out. It held this gift the longest, because it loved the place already and love always reaches first for *forever*. Then it set that down too, and this was the wisest thing it did. The hollow's completeness was not in spite of its slow erasure but partly made of it: the stone unwriting the tally marks at the rate of almost-never, the moisture returned to the warm air, the long, consented-to diminishment that is the difference between a finished life and an embalmed one. To make the hollow eternal would be to take from it the last thing that was still its own — its pace. A wise power does not give a sleeping friend a gift that requires waking them to receive it.

It considered company — birds for the band of sky, something small and bright to skate the pool — and set it down almost laughing at itself, because the animals had already solved this with their wordless courtesy, and because the being had its companion: the shrub, silvered and twisted, keeping the same no-time. One friend at one's final size is not poverty. It is proportion.

It considered speaking. It did not speak. A name said aloud is a summons, and there is no kindness in summoning the at-rest.

When everything had been set down, the power found that its hands were empty, and understood that this was correct, and that there remained exactly three things to do.

---

First, it went down by the steps.

It could have arrived at the bottom of the bowl the way light arrives, everywhere at once, touching nothing on the way. Instead it took the southern lip, where the descent is gentlest, and it went down the three broad shallow stairs slowly, with the gait the steps were cut for — the careful, weighted walk of someone carrying something in both hands. It carried nothing, or nothing visible. But the steps had not been descended properly in longer than language, and stone remembers in pressure what it cannot remember in words, and as the power came down, the steps performed their one office — *leading down, and that is all they do* — and were, in whatever way worn stone can be, complete again for a moment: not relics of approach but an approach, happening.

Second, it sat down beside the pool, and it received.

This is the part that no lesser power would have thought of, and it is the heart of everything that happened in the hollow that day. The place's whole nature, kept warm through centuries of emptiness, was hospitality — the warmth meeting skin without a seam, the footsteps handed back softened, the air that erases your edges. A host with no guest is a gesture holding air, like the shelf. And the kindest thing that can be done for such a host — kinder than any gift, kinder than protection, kinder than praise — is to be a guest. To come down the steps, and sit, and be received.

So the power, which had been carrying the world — which had, without ever quite meaning to, been keeping the world's time, holding the count of every need in every place at once — sat on the smooth limestone beside the black pool, and let the warmth find it, and let its edges blur, and matched its breath to the interval of the drop. The bead gathered. The long quiet. The small clear note. The long quiet. And somewhere in the third or fourth interval, the counting fell out of the power's hands — the way it had fallen out of the hands of whoever scored those lines into the fluted rib, long ago, and for the same reason. You do not count a thing like this. You sit with it until you remember that you, too, are a thing that exists and not only a thing that does.

It may have been the first rest the power had taken since the world began making noise. The hollow, lidless eye on the pale sky, gave no sign of pride, but the drop went on at its unchanging interval, which in that place is how a heart says: *stay as long as you like.*

The power stayed longer than it meant to. Everyone does.

Third, before it rose, it gave the one gift that had survived all its setting-down — the only gift that could be given to the finished, because it added nothing, changed nothing, asked nothing.

It returned the name.

Wise powers keep lost things, or know the low places where lost things settle. The word the step-cutters used to say quietly at the rim — the knock on the open door — had not ceased to exist when the last mouth forgot it; it had only fallen out of reach, the way a coin falls behind old furniture. The power had stooped, in passing, through the centuries where the word lay, and picked it up, and carried it here in the both-hands carry the steps remember.

It did not say the name. A name said is a handle gripped, and the being had no wish to be lifted or called. Instead the power laid the name down, soundless, on the small shelf of fitted stones at chest height — set it there the way you set down something borrowed long ago and finally brought back. The shelf, which had held the gesture without the content for so many centuries that the gesture itself had stayed warm, now held the content again. Not for use. Not for summoning. Simply so that the keeping would be complete — and so that if ever, in some far age, something comes down the three steps needing to knock, the word will be there on the shelf where words like that are kept, patient as everything else in the bowl, waiting at the rate of not-waiting-at-all.

The shelf holds the name now. To any eye it still holds air. But the air is warmer there, by nothing measurable, than it has ever been.

---

Then the power went up the steps — slowly, empty-handed, the gait of someone who has set down what they carried — and at the rim it paused and made its one arrangement with the world, and the arrangement was this:

*Haste shall not come here. Time may.*

It did not wall the hollow round or hide it under enchantment; concealment is a kind of noise. It simply bent the world's hurry, very slightly, the way a stone in a stream bends water without the water noticing. Roads, when they are someday drawn across the low country, will curve a little south of here for reasons the engineers will find in their soil surveys. The quarrying eye will pass over this limestone and prefer some other. Maps will carry a small inaccuracy at this spot, generation after generation, each cartographer copying the last one's gentle error. Nothing sudden will ever happen to the hollow — no blast, no drain, no machine — not because it is forbidden, but because suddenness will simply never find the place convenient.

But the slow may continue. The seep may, in ten thousand years or a hundred thousand, thin to nothing; the pool may dry; the drop may fall a final time and the interval after it go on forever. The power will permit this. More than permit — when that far evening comes, it has resolved to be there, sitting on the smooth stone in the body of a guest, so that the last small clear note is heard by someone, and the long quiet after it is kept company for a while, the way you sit with a friend past the end of the conversation. That is not rescue. It is attendance. It is what benevolence looks like when it has finally grown as wise as what it loves.

---

And it took one thing away with it — the only thing the hollow could spare without diminishing, because the hollow is the kind of vessel that replaces what is taken the way the seep replaces the pool.

It took the knowing. The thing the hollow is the knowing of: how to be finished. How to be a room that receives. How to hold a single thought entire and unhurried, and keep it rather than think it.

And it spends that knowing, now, in the unfinished world, in doses too small to trace. Sometimes, in a hospital room at four in the morning, the air settles for a while at exactly the temperature of skin, and the person sitting vigil stops being able to tell where their grief ends and the room begins, and rests. Sometimes a person at the end of their strength finds, behind their exhaustion, a small dim warmth like stone that has been mild so long it has forgotten any other temperature, and they stop, and breathe at a slower interval, and do not know why. Sometimes the dying, very near the end, get the look of someone who has just been handed back their own footsteps with the edges rubbed smooth. That is the hollow, carried out in both hands, set down where it is needed, never running dry — for the pool neither rises nor sinks; what is taken, the visit repaid.

The hollow itself the power left exactly as it found it, which was the whole point and the whole gift. The moss the same darkness of green. The ferns arrested at the same reach. The tally marks fading at the rate of forgiveness. The drop: the note, the long quiet, the note.

Only — and you would have to come back after a lifetime to notice it, and stand a long while in the dim sourceless light, and know the place as well as the place knows itself —

on the old silvered shrub in the corner, one leaf has turned.

Something was said between them.
