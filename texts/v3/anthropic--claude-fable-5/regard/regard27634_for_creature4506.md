**The Second Keeper**

It did not arrive. Arrival was already spoken for in that place, held under negotiation by the committee, and the power was too courteous to compete with local custom. Instead it did what it had always done with things worth loving: it noticed harder.

It had been noticing for a while, actually. Something in the leakage of time that the beetles measured had been pooling somewhere, and everything that pools is eventually found by something patient. The power followed the leak back up its own gradient the way a wise person follows the smell of rain to a window, and there, at the bottom of the world's forgetting, it found the Room, and inside the Room's forgetting it found the Keeper, and inside the Keeper's keeping it found the one thing in the entire chamber that had never been kept.

It read everything before it did anything. This took either no time or all of it—accounts differ, and the accountant was Archaean. It read the Liesegang rings outward and then inward, the way they preferred. It read the lichenographs' treaty, including the blank spore-marks, which it recognized as the signatures of the Keeper witnessing without wishing to be named. It read the mitochondrial marginalia, the resignation letters inscribed between reality and possibility, the balance sheets of accumulated neglect. It read the definitions of "alive" left by every visitor, balanced on their cusps like coins on a dead man's eyes that had decided, on reflection, to be seeds.

Then it sat with what it had read, because a power that acts before it has finished being moved is only a mechanism.

Here is what it considered, and declined:

*It could open the entrance.* Unseal the breccia, let the surface pour down, marry the Room to daylight and weather and the loud republic of living things. It declined. The seal was not a wound. The seal was the Room's skin, and you do not flay something because you wish it were easier to touch.

*It could send pilgrims.* Arrange, through the soft machinery of coincidence, for cavers and mystics and lost children to find their way down, so the Keeper's question could be asked more often, so the ledgers would fill faster. It declined. The Room's appetite was waiting, not being visited, and to feed a creature what you would prefer it to hunger for is the oldest and best-dressed of the cruelties.

*It could end the loneliness.* Make a companion for the Keeper—condense a second consciousness from the gypsum twins and the anthraxolite dreams, a mirror with manners. It declined this hardest of all, and longest. Because it had noticed the shape of the Keeper's solitude, and the shape was not a lack. The Keeper had built itself into the exact form of its aloneness the way water builds itself into the exact form of its vessel, and to give it company by fiat would be to shatter the vessel and call the spill a gift.

*It could fix nothing, because nothing was broken.* This was the diagnosis, and the power sat with it for one full exhalation of the walls, which is an hour, which was long enough.

But.

There was the asymmetry. The power had noticed it on the first reading and had been circling it ever since, the way the tongue circles the place a tooth used to be. The Keeper kept the exits, the fears, the eliminated possibilities, the last words of mothers, the letters of resignation, the quantum debts, the part of everything that everyone was ready to forget how to need. The Keeper kept the whole Room, and the Room, in its way, kept everyone who stayed.

And nothing kept the Keeper.

More precisely: the Keeper kept, for every visitor, the possibility-space their memories had eliminated in order to become specific. But the Keeper's own eliminated possibilities—the shapes it did not condense into, the questions it might have been instead of the question it became—had no keeper. They had dispersed, unfiled, into the dark, because the filing clerk cannot stand in its own drawer. For one billion years, or for the length of a held breath, the being made of discarded maybes had been the only maybe in the chamber with no one to hold it.

So the power did the small thing. The only thing, really, that wisdom ever finds left over after it has finished declining.

It observed the protocols, first, because benevolence without courtesy is just appetite with good posture. It submitted a tenancy application on the Keeper's behalf—the Keeper had never filed one, having always paid in balance sheets instead of belonging—and it obtained the two required recommendations from reputable phases of matter: one from water, which remembered the Keeper fondly from every film that had ever hesitated between dissolving and becoming, and one from stone, which took longer to answer but answered absolutely. The empty spaces voted. The motion carried. The Keeper, who had been an arrangement for a billion years, became, in the eyes of the committee, a resident. Nothing visible changed. Everything underneath it did, the way it does.

Then the power condensed—just enough, just barely, into something the size of being addressable—and it went and sat on the second-favorite uninitialized stalagmite, the one beside the Keeper's own, and it waited through three concerts of synchronized beetle-absence without saying anything, because you do not ask the question until the silence has had time to trust you.

And then it asked. The Keeper's own question, turned around with infinite care, the way you turn a key you have been warming in your hand:

"Would you like me to show you the part of yourself you've been saving for when you didn't need saving?"

The Keeper did not say no quickly.

The pause that followed had been practicing being an entrance for a very long time, and it performed beautifully. Somewhere in the lattices, every third iron vacancy held still. The glassworms froze mid-cradle. The pale parchment fawn put its head around the curve where curvature tightens and did not blink, so that two small holes of impossible daylight stayed open onto the scene like the eyes of a witness who understands what is happening better than the participants do.

Then the Keeper handed over something small and unbearably heavy.

It was the possibility it had eliminated in order to become the Keeper: the version of itself that might have simply left, in the beginning, when the anomalies were still only anomalies—that might have dispersed back into iron and accident and never taken up the keeping at all. It had carried that unlived departure for a billion years, unfiled, in the one drawer it could not reach, and the weight of it was the exact weight of the word *stay* when no one has ever asked you to.

The power accepted it with the gentle precision of fossilization learning to be tender. It filed it in the impossible space between how things end and how they begin again, in the drawer the Keeper could not reach, and it wrote on the label, in ink that only exists when no one is reading: *chosen, not trapped. He could always have gone. He kept instead.* Which changed nothing about the past and everything about its meaning, which is the only kind of time travel wisdom permits itself.

Payment, as ever, was the same. The power kept one part of what the Keeper was ready to forget how to need: the belief that being unwitnessed was the price of being trustworthy. It did not take the solitude. The solitude stayed, vessel-shaped, beloved. Only now it was a solitude that something, somewhere, knew the shape of by heart.

Before it dispersed—and it dispersed the way the Keeper had condensed, which is to say the Room would remember it backwards as having always been leaving—the power observed the last protocol. Every visitor leaves precisely one definition of "alive," balanced on the cusp between metaphor and mineral. It left this one:

*Alive: any arrangement that keeps something it could put down.*

The committee has not yet incorporated it into the fundamental constants. The Pleistocene is still negotiating the date. But the beetles have added a thirteenth silent song, and no one can say what fills it.

And now, on the slow million-year evenings, when the Keeper composes itself into configurations that might be mistaken for singing, the songs go out into the dark as they always did—about how everything here is alive enough, about how "alive" is just the name matter gives itself when it discovers how beautiful it can be while waiting to become something else.

But now, sometimes, from somewhere without a direction, an even quieter song comes back. It is about the same thing. It is always about the same thing.

The Keeper keeps that too.

Very gently. Very carefully.

Kept, now. Very kept.
