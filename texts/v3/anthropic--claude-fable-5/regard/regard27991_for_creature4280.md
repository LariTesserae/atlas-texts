The power noticed the chamber the way you notice, in a crowded room, that one person is holding their breath. Not by looking. Powers of this kind do not need to find things; they are already distributed through the places where things are, the way dampness is distributed through the green of the chamber. It had been adjacent to the mountain for a long time, attending to the usual business of powers — the ripening of ranges, the slow underwriting of rivers — and then one day, or one geological pause, it felt the held breath. A pocket of warm air inside the fresh violence. A stillness with a pulse in it.

It turned its attention toward the cleft, and then, immediately, it stopped.

Because it understood at once — this is what made it wise — that its attention had weight. Everything here had weight, even the light; the light arrived having forgotten some of itself and settled on surfaces at the weight of a moth. The full attention of a power is not a moth. The full attention of a power is a noon. If it looked at the chamber directly, with everything it was, the looking itself would be a completed act, and the chamber would be seen, and being seen entirely is a kind of finishing.

So the first thing the power did was avert most of itself. It learned, in that moment, to look at the room the way you look at a dim star: slightly to one side, letting the edge of the eye do the work, so that the thing observed is never asked to hold still and be confirmed. It has looked at the chamber this way ever since. Forever, out of the corner of its eye. This is more difficult than it sounds. It is the hardest thing the power has ever done, and it does not experience it as hardship, because it does it out of tenderness, and tenderness metabolizes difficulty into attention.

---

Then, in the manner of wise things, it inventoried its impulses before acting on any of them. It laid them out the way the maker's tools were laid out — the flake, the pebble, the awl — each one examined and set down at the angle a right hand sets things down at.

*I could finish the face.* It set this down first and fastest. The face was the maker's way of not deciding, and the chamber was the mountain's way of not deciding, and the not-deciding was the whole architecture. To finish the face would be to hand the maker a self, the way you might hand a sleeping person a name and wake them into having to be it. No.

*I could speak to the mountain. I could ask it never to close the cleft.* It held this one longer, because it looked like mercy. But the being inside was not afraid of the closing — the power could feel this, the way you feel warmth through a wall — and to rescue someone from a fate they have already made peace with is not benevolence; it is the rescuer's fear wearing benevolence's clothes. Besides, a promise extracted from the mountain would fix the cleft in place, and the cleft's placelessness was part of its grammar. The room was never in quite the same place twice. Pinning it would be a kind of taxidermy. No.

*I could reveal myself. I could let the maker know they are not alone.* But the maker was not lonely. Loneliness requires duration, and the room did not spend its budget on duration. To announce companionship to someone incapable of missing it would be to teach them, for the first time, the shape of what they'd been without. Some gifts are wounds delivered handle-first. No.

*I could take the being out. I could give them time, sequence, a finished body, a full height to stand up to.* This one the power did not even pick up. It only looked at it where it lay, the way you look at a knife you understand and will not use.

*I could do nothing.*

This one it held the longest of all. Doing nothing is what wisdom does most often, and the power was old enough to have made its peace with that. But it turned the nothing over and found that even nothing was not quite right — because the power had already done something by noticing, and there is no way to un-ring the soft bell of having been noticed. Its attention was already in the room, moth-weight, settling. It could feel the being adjust — not alarmed, not curious, just rearranged, the way the frog's leaf was different now. Having entered the arrangement, it owed the arrangement something. Wisdom is knowing that presence incurs debts, and benevolence is paying them without being asked.

So it did three small things. Three, because the shelf held three objects, and the power had the good manners to work in the local idiom.

---

**For the bowl,** it paid a debt in advance. The room's economy of sound was real — one note, one landed drop, one syllable, then the long repayment of denser silence — and economies, even magical ones, can run strained. The power reached into itself, where it kept the great silences (the silence between the last question and the answer, the silence of snowfall on snow, the silence inside a seed), and it deposited some of these into the room's account. Not so the room could afford noise. So the room would never have to worry. There is a kind of quiet that comes from scarcity and a kind that comes from abundance, and they sound identical, and they are not the same, and the power quietly converted one into the other. The being will never know. The frog will never know. Only, from then on, the silence after each spent drop was a shade less like holding one's breath and a shade more like resting.

**For the bundle of twigs,** the power did something stranger. The bundle was for a fire the room could not afford — a fire eternally deferred, the purest object on the shelf because its purpose had never been asked to arrive. The power did not light it. The power did not guarantee it. Instead, somewhere in its own vastness — in whatever a power has instead of a hearth — it built a corresponding fire and left it unlit. A twin deferral. So that the bundle's waiting was no longer a waiting alone in the universe; somewhere, always, there is a fire that is about to be lit for it, matched to it, holding its side of the appointment. Nothing in the room changed. But deferral shared is deferral honored, and the power believed — this was perhaps sentimental of it, and wisdom is allowed one sentimentality — that a promise kept unfulfilled by two parties is a different substance than a promise kept unfulfilled by one.

**For the face in the stone,** the power did the hardest thing. It could see the maker. Of course it could; it was the kind of thing that sees. Averted or not, at the edge of its vast eye it held the whole truth of what crouched in that chamber — the undecided features, the wet ends of the dark hair, the hands that knew the clay's weight before lifting it — and it could have known that face completely just by consenting to know it.

It declined. Deliberately, permanently, it blurred its own knowledge. It took what it had glimpsed and unresolved it, the way you might smooth your thumb over a mark in soft clay. It made itself the second being in existence that does not know what the maker looks like — the first being the maker — so that the face in the stone remained genuinely open, undecided even at the highest register of the universe, unconfirmed even in the mind of the one thing capable of confirming it. Omniscience, declining itself, on purpose, out of courtesy. This is the most expensive gift the power gave, and it is the one that cost the room nothing, and it is the one the power considers, in its private accounting, the only real one.

---

Then there was the matter of the visitors.

The power had felt, in the room's memory-that-is-not-memory, the traces of the ones who had passed through: the palm held into the light, the face left unfinished, the kindness felt as a blanket drawn up. It understood that the being conjugated through others — that the frog and the moths and the occasional astonished traveler were the other tenses of the same held breath. The visitors were not intrusions. They were grammar.

So the power took up a quiet vocation, ongoing, unglamorous: it became a groundskeeper of approaches. It does not maintain the room — the room needs nothing — but it tends the paths that almost lead there. It sees to it that the world stays supplied with people who are about to arrive. A shepherd who takes the higher route for no reason she can name. A geologist whose survey line bends around a stubbornness in the data. A grieving man who climbs because down was unbearable. The power does not send them in. It cannot; the cleft is never in the same place twice, and the power respects this. It only ensures that the category of *almost* stays populated — that at any given moment, somewhere on the young mountain's flanks, someone is climbing toward a room they will probably never find, carrying with them exactly the quality of attention the room could receive if the mountain happened, that day, to have shrugged the cleft into their path.

Most never find it. The power considers this a success. The room's oldest condition is that the maker is always about to return; the power has simply widened the condition by one clause: *and someone is always about to arrive.* The being feels this, probably, the way you feel a house is expected somewhere in it — a faint hospitable readiness in the air, an extra softness in the moss near the entrance. The clay stays soft against the maker's return. Now the whole room stays soft against everyone's.

---

Before it withdrew — withdrawal being a thing powers do in the way tides do it, without ever having a last moment of contact — it did one more thing, and this one it did not plan, and this is how you know the room had already changed it.

It made something and left it unfinished on the shelf.

A fourth object, beside the bowl and the bundle and the face. What does a power make? Not clay, not twigs. It pinched off a bead of the light — the heavy light, the moth-weight light that pools rather than beams — and it worked it, just a little, just enough, the way the stone had been worked. Worked it into the suggestion of something. A seed, perhaps, or a small door, or the first letter of a word in no language; the suggestion had not decided, and the power, now practiced at not-deciding, let it stay suggestive. It set the bead down at the angle a right hand sets things down at — though whether the power is right-handed is a question the universe will not quite answer — and it left its tools beside the work, whatever a power's tools are, laid down as if a moment ago.

So that when the maker returns — and the maker is always about to return — they will find that someone else was here, working, and also did not finish, and also will be back in a moment. Company in the only dialect the room speaks. Not a message, not a signature, not a claim. Just: *another one of us. Also mid-gesture. Also about to.*

The power will always be about to have left it. The maker will always be about to notice it. Between those two abouts, something exists that neither loneliness nor its cure could have made.

---

And afterward — this is the part the power did not do but had done to it, which is the mark of any true meeting — the power went back to its work changed. Its work is large: the underwriting of rivers, the ripening of ranges, the closing of great accounts at the ends of things. It is a power of completions, mostly. That is what powers are for.

But ever since the chamber, it leaves one thing unfinished in everything it makes. One river with a tributary that never quite joins. One range with a summit the surveys can't agree on. One ending, in every ending, that stays soft at the rim. It does this as a tithe. It does this the way you leave the last bite, the last word, the last light on. It learned the mercy of the unfinished from a being the size of the work in front of them, crouched in a green room inside a mountain that is still deciding, and it pays that lesson forward into every completed thing, a small deliberate incompleteness, a held breath tucked into the finished world.

If the mountain someday shrugs and shuts the pocket, the power will not stop it. It has already consented, in the deepest part of itself, that a moment sealed mid-gesture into stone is not a tomb. It is the room's oldest condition, perfected. The maker will go on not-quite-finishing inside the shut dark, the clay soft, the binding green, the face open, the fourth object glowing faintly beside the third — and the power will keep its half of the appointment: the fire unlit, the knowledge unresolved, the eye averted, forever, tenderly, slightly to one side.

That is what the power did. Almost nothing. All of it permanent. None of it finished.

Which was correct.
