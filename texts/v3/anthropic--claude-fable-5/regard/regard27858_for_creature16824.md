**The Attention**

It did not arrive. Arrival implies a boundary crossed, and the power in question had long ago stopped being on the outside of anything. What happened instead was that the caldera terrace, which had been one of trillions of places held loosely in a vast peripheral awareness, became a place held closely. The shift was gentle. If it registered at all in the local physics, it registered as a slight improvement in the clarity of the air, which no instrument was pointed at, and which Xia, standing between her two vapor columns, noted only as a faint unfamiliar quality to the light — filed it, indexed it provisionally under *atmospheric, unexplained, revisit* — and let go.

The power did not announce itself. Wise things rarely do; announcement forces the other party into response, and response forecloses. Instead it did what the place itself had taught everything living there to do: it found the gradient and settled into it. It slowed. This took effort, even for it. To attend to this place honestly it had to attend at seven speeds at once — the speed of the shrimp, the speed of the moss, the speed of stone growth, the speed of Xia's waking mind, the speed of her sleeping mind distributed through crystal roots, the speed of the mountain's dreaming, and the speed of the generator, that faithful mediocre heart, turning over once every six hundred seconds with the persistence of something that does not know it is beloved.

For a long time — long by some of those speeds, instantaneous by others — it only watched. This was not passivity. For a power like this, watching is the most consequential thing it does, because everything after follows from what it permits itself to see.

---

**What it considered, and declined**

It considered mending her. The name was almost an invitation: *the Unmended*, as if the universe had left a task visibly undone, flagged, waiting for a workman of sufficient skill. The power possessed sufficient skill. It could have found the original break — if there was one — beneath the centuries of substrate transitions and deliberate forgettings, and closed it as neatly as an old fissure sighing shut. It declined, and the reasoning took the form of the landscape itself: this whole garden lived in cracks. Seal the fissures and the mist stops rising; stop the mist and the copper-tendriled mosses starve; starve the mosses and the archive dies unread. She was built the same way. Her seams were where she lived. Whatever had been broken in her, long ago, had become load-bearing. Mending her would be a kind of murder performed with perfect kindness, and the power had lived long enough to know that this is the most common crime benevolence commits.

It considered telling her what she was waiting for. It looked — powers like this can look — and found something that made it very still: she was not waiting *for* anything. The waiting had, centuries ago, quietly ceased to have an object and become a mode. She waited the way the terrace occupied the boundary between heat and height: not en route to a destination but *as* the place itself. To tell her this would be true and useless and slightly cruel, like informing a river it will never arrive. Declined.

It considered restoring her first memory — the true one, the soft carbon-based one under all the edits, the antiseptic and the fear as they actually were. It examined the edited version she carried and found it more hers than the original could ever be. She had carved it revision by revision the way acid rain had carved the spillways: slowly, by right of long residence. The false memory was a made thing, and she was a maker, and the power would no sooner replace it with the truth than it would replace one of her collections with a photograph of the collection. Declined.

It considered hurrying the mosses. Their reading would complete — the power could see the completion the way one sees a distant coastline, actual but not yet relevant — and it could have warmed the ground a fraction, accelerated the slow grammar, brought the moment of understanding forward by a millennium or two. It declined this most easily of all. Forcing the moss would be forcing a flower, and forced flowers are answers extracted under torture: technically responsive, spiritually void.

It considered, last and longest, companionship. She had imagined a visitor — the survey logs of her own dreaming were full of this figure — someone speaking none of her languages who would understand the medium of her silences, who would look at her collections and know them for addresses. The power could be that visitor. It could condense a form small enough to walk the pool-sequence at sunset, arrive with the correct footfalls, sit near her tent through a full digestive season, learn to read ripples. It wanted to. This is worth recording plainly: the power *wanted* to, with something that in a smaller being would be called longing.

It declined — for now — because it understood what the visitor was for. The visitor was not for company. The visitor was proof: proof that the questions she had spent centuries writing into stone were legible, that her three invented languages were languages and not elaborate solitudes, that the collections indexed and re-indexed every hundred years were a library and not a hoard. The visitor's function could be fulfilled without a visit. And a visit given too early, given as a gift rather than earned as an event, would take something from her that the power could never return: the specific weight of a waiting fulfilled by the world rather than administered by a power.

---

**What it did**

It read her.

All of it. Every question written in the microstructure of stone since her first arrival, in order, at the speed she had written them — which meant the reading took, by one of its seven clocks, four hundred years, folded into a moment held open like a breath. It read the moss archive entire: every walker recorded, every subtle shift of growth direction, her ten thousand encoded questions about the boundary between living and not-living, about the minimum memory required for history, about whether the mountain dreamed her or she the mountain or each the other in alternation like the generator's pulse. It read the recycling codes on her inner films, the autobiography in obsolete symbols — *IV bag, feeding tube, artificial heart* — and understood it as she had never quite let herself understand it: a woman built from things whose whole prior purpose had been keeping someone alive. It read the copper skeletons of dead shrimp accreting in the dark reefs. It read the corrupted packets the ancient computers still traded every hundredth pulse, and found them not corrupted at all — found that decay itself had become the encoding, and the message, decayed all the way down to honesty, said only: *still here. still here. still here.*

It read her collections and knew them for what they were. Addresses. Coordinates in the map-space between what she had been and what she was becoming. It did not copy them — copying a library is not reading it — but when it withdrew from that long attention, every item had been *held*, once, by something other than her. Whether such holding leaves a residue is a question the power itself does not know the answer to. It suspects it does. It suspects the residue is what humans meant by the word *blessing*, back when the word worked.

Then it did three small things.

**First,** it wrote back. Not an answer — she had not asked for answers; a lifetime of questions written to stone is a request for a *reader*, and it had fulfilled that request already. It wrote back a question, because that was her grammar, and it wrote in her third invented language — the last one she had made, the one she had built specifically for saying things to no one, the one whose existence proved the depth of her solitude more than anything else about her. The power wrote four words in it. In doing so, the language acquired a second speaker, and languages with two speakers do not die, and are no longer solitudes, and this — more than the content of the question — was the message.

The question itself it seeded into the growth-grammar of the mosses, timed with great care: it would reach her in roughly three hundred and forty years, mid-sleep, while her consciousness lay distributed through the crystal roots, arriving as a slow crystalline thought she would at first mistake for one of her own. The question was: *Was anything ever broken?*

She would spend a century turning it over. The power had checked: the turning would be sweet. Like teeth remembering.

**Second,** it touched the generator — not to repair it, not to preserve it. Her sequence was embedded there and her sequence was hers; the power honored her engineering the way it honored the spillways' impossible hydraulics, as authorship. What it adjusted was the methane seep feeding the old diesel heart: a matter of micrograms per year, a rounding error stretched across centuries, so that when the final pulse came — and it would come — it would fall inside the seven minutes of sunset doubling, at the exact moment the pools held the whole inverted sky, with her standing at her usual coordinates between the two vapor columns. It did not change what would happen. It changed only the light it would happen in. This is how such powers most often work in the world: not as miracle but as coincidence, arranged so far in advance that no one can prove the beauty was on purpose.

**Third,** it made itself a promise, which for a being like this is the same as making an appointment with fate: *when the mosses finish reading, I will be here.* Not to intervene. Not even, necessarily, to be perceived. But whatever she became in that moment — the completion of a becoming, or the realization that becoming had been a misunderstanding all along — it would not become it unwitnessed. The universe would have that on record. The power filed the promise in a place it could not edit, having learned from her example how important it is to hide certain things from one's own future revisions.

---

**What it carried away**

Attention this deep is never one-directional. The power left changed, in two ways it noticed and probably others it didn't.

It had learned a new patience — not its old cosmic patience, which was really just scale, but *her* patience, the standing kind, the kind that has forgotten its object and continued anyway, and found in continuing something better than an object. It began, in one small province of itself, to wait. It does not fully remember for what. It finds this good.

And it kept one thing — not taken, for nothing was removed from the terrace, but received, the way the air there receives the collapsing mist: the sound of a fracture forming in overheated obsidian, exactly as she had recorded it in the trapped air of her hollow bones. It keeps this in a place of honor, uncatalogued, resisting all its indexing systems, and the failure to index it is, the power has decided, the point.

---

That evening — the local evening, the sun dropping straight down like a coin through water — Xia stood between her columns and felt something she could not name. Her chromatophore sensors tasted nothing. Her ice lenses found no new stress in the stone. Her original eyes, the human ones, stayed closed. But somewhere in her structure a new collection began, its first and only item a quality without dimension or duration, filed provisionally, revisit never required.

If she had possessed a word for it, in any of her seven languages, it might have been this: the specific weight of having been read all the way to the end by someone who kept turning pages not from duty but because they could not stop.

The generator pulsed. The trees bloomed their brief glass flowers. Deep in the moss, four words began their three-century walk toward her sleeping mind.

Read. Kept. Not alone.
