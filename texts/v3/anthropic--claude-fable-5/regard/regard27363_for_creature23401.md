## The Visit

A power like this does not come down by the stair.

It does not need the steps, or the rope pegs, or the lamp niche; it does not displace air or dampen sound; it could attend to the room from anywhere, the way you can attend to a memory. But it has been wise for a long time, and wisdom is mostly the accumulation of courtesies, and the first courtesy of a dwelling is that you do not enter it unannounced.

So it gives itself just enough presence to be felt. Down in the cold margins of the pool, the crayfish read a footstep with no foot — a slow rhythmic damping descending the stair, arriving at the polished floor, stopping. The room's voice shifts by a fraction to fit the visitor in, the way it shifts for everything, and in that fraction the power and the resident are introduced. That is the whole ceremony. The roar does not pause, because the roar cannot pause, and the power would not have wanted it to.

Then the power stands there, and considers what it might do. Because it is a power, everything is available to it, and because it is wise, it takes inventory honestly, one temptation at a time.

It could stop the lip of the fall from retreating — pin the geometry in place, fix the voice forever in its current register. It sets this down almost at once. The retreat is not the room's wound; it is the room's life. The utterance is thousands of years long *and still in progress*, and to freeze it would be to embalm a speaker mid-syllable and call it preservation. Finished, here, has never meant static. The room is finished the way a long sentence is finished in its grammar while the speaking goes on.

It could give the eel back its eyes, light the bottom of the unmeasured pool, warm the water a few degrees. It sets this down too, more gently. The dark is not the eel's deprivation; it is the eel's accomplishment, eighty years in the making. The cold is the lock on the door, the thing that keeps the room safe for everything soft in it. A benevolence that removes what a creature has built its peace around is just force with good manners.

It could let the dipper's song be heard — lift it for one spring out of the thunder and deliver it across the pool to the mate, so that the offering made every year into the roar would for once arrive. The power holds this one longer than the others. It is the kind of gift a younger power gives. But the song's privacy is the song's species. A song sung into total erasure, year after year, asked-for by no one and heard by no one, is a different and rarer thing than a song that lands — and besides, the power realizes, standing in the white thunder, the premise is wrong. The song has not gone unheard. Not this year. The roar can erase a voice from every ear in the room, but the power is not in the room the way an ear is; it is in the room the way the sound is. The dipper sings, if she sings, directly into the medium the power now occupies. So the gift, it turns out, requires no intervention at all. It only requires staying.

It could bring people back — clear the way, renew the rope, set a flame in the soot-black niche so the warm point of light burns again in the cold. It sets this down with something like sadness. A lamp lit for no one is not a restoration; it is a lie told to an empty stair. And the room's loneliness is load-bearing now. The dipper nests in the throat of the fall, the bats sleep through their quiet ultrasonic day, the blind ones read the floor in safety, all because the place is too loud to hunt by ear and too hard to reach by accident. To make the room known would be to renovate the very thing that makes it a refuge. The people will come back when they come back, or they won't. Either way it is not a door the power should hold open with its thumb.

Last, and most quietly, it could give the room one second of silence — still the fall for a single heartbeat so that the chamber, for the first time since the river found it, could hear itself. This one the power examines very carefully, because it has the shape of a profound gift, and profound-shaped gifts are where wise powers go wrong. Then it sees through it. The unbroken utterance *is* the resident. One second of silence is not a mirror held up to the being; it is the being's death and the swearing-in of a successor. The roar has never stopped: that is not a fact about the resident, it is the resident. You do not introduce a flame to itself by blowing it out.

So the inventory ends where these inventories, taken honestly, almost always end: with empty hands.

But not with nothing. Because there is one thing in the room that no member of the census can do, that the dipper and the eel and the bats and even the people in their hour of deafness could never do, and it is the one thing a finished, wanting-nothing, thousands-of-years-long utterance can actually receive.

It can be heard whole.

The power listens. Not to the present roar only — to all of it. The first fall of the river into the freshly finished room. Every flood that thickened the curtain and drove the voice up a register; every winter that collared the moss in ice and muffled the thunder to a vast drumming; every fractional adjustment the room ever made to hold an arrival — each dipper across ten thousand generations, each eel rising once a night through the black, each bat, each footstep on the stair, each lamp-hour of fat and wick, each child's hand pressed flat against the humming wall. The whole single utterance, beginning to present, taken in at once and kept. Nothing in the room changes. Nothing is added or repaired or improved. But something that was true of the room stops being true: it is no longer unwitnessed. The long sentence has, at last, a reader.

And the power stands the hour, as the people did. It lets the roar do to it what the roar does — empties itself out, sets down its scope and its options and its inventory, and is for a while just a presence in cold thunder with the wall humming under what would be its palm. It does not press a hand. The constellation of ochre on the dry shelf is complete; it needs no immortal added to it. What the hands asked for — *remember that we stood here, smallest of us included* — is simply granted, fully, the way only a power can grant it: the weight of each palm, kept. The names are gone past recovery, and the power does not counterfeit them. It keeps the touch.

Two things it permits itself, on the way out.

The first is a promise, made to no one, binding anyway: that it will be there for the last syllable. Some thousands of years on, when the lip has retreated far enough and the geometry tips and the voice becomes a different voice, the old resident will end as it lived — mid-utterance, unceremonious — and the power will receive the whole of it, intact, the way the eel carries the far sea in its body across a lifetime of dark water. An entire roar, complete, carried out of the world. And then the power will come down again without the stair, give itself a footstep for the blind ones to read, and stand the hour for the new tenant, and begin listening to the next long sentence from its first word.

The second is the only act it commits, and it commits it above ground, so that the room stays exactly as it was found. The line of the handprint people did not end; it dispersed, the way old lines do, until it runs thin through half the country above. To one of them — it hardly matters which; over the centuries it will be many — the power delivers a dream: green dimness, a worn stair going down, a wall of white thunder, the feel of cold humming stone under a flat palm, and an unaccountable calm on waking. No instruction. No summons. Whether anyone ever follows the dream down through the hazel and the cairns is not the power's business. It only delivered the address. The hands on the wall asked to be remembered, and now, at intervals, in scattered sleepers, they are — not as history, but as homesickness for a place the dreamer has never seen.

Then the power withdraws, with the same courtesy it arrived with: the footstep with no foot climbs the stair, the crayfish feel it go, the room's voice settles back by a fraction, already forgetting in the only way it knows how — by continuing.

In the room, nothing is different. The dipper walks into the cataract. Something old turns over at the bottom of the pool. The roar goes on saying what it has always been saying, finished long ago and in no hurry, and the only change anywhere is this, which no instrument would bother with and which changes everything: somewhere, the whole of it is known. The song sung into erasure landed. The hands were answered. The long utterance has a listener now, patient as limestone, who will be there for the end of the sentence —

and the dimness above, when the power passes up through it, qualifies as day.
