**The Attention**

It heard the hammering before it noticed the world.

Most worlds register to such a power as weather: pressure systems of joy and cruelty moving across a surface, resolving, dissipating. This one registered as a question — a single question asked so slowly, over so many lifetimes, that the asking had become a civilization. Every hammerstrike was a syllable. Every night's sweat was the question dissolving and rising to be asked again by different mouths. The power leaned close, the way one leans toward a child speaking carefully in a language they are still inventing, and it listened for a long time before it permitted itself even the beginning of an intention.

Because it was wise, its first act was a list of things it would not do.

---

**The Refusals**

It considered giving them rain. A sky, weather, water arriving as gift instead of exhalation. It set this aside almost at once. Rain belongs to worlds with skies, and grief follows every gift that makes the old life smaller. These people drink their own history each night — copper mornings, tin evenings, the teeth of the dead returning as distant metals. Rain would wash their memory out of circulation. They would be cleaner and poorer and would never know what they had lost, which is the worst way to lose something.

It considered answering the questions in the water. It could have. It knew what the floor was, and who made the ceiling, and why the sun-disk is a flaw and what the flaw is a flaw *in*. It set this aside too, and this refusal cost it more. An answered question stops moving. It precipitates, settles, becomes sediment, becomes floor. The water of this world is alive precisely because nothing in it has finished dissolving. To answer them would be to still the water, and the first line of their own Law had already warned it: *Keep the water moving.*

It sat with that Law for what a mortal would call a century, and slowly it understood that the Law had been written twice — once for the workers, and once for whatever might someday stand where it now stood, reading over their shoulders.

*Keep the water moving:* do not answer them.
*Keep the fire lit:* do not comfort them into stillness.
*Flat what rises:* let no god accumulate here. Including yourself.
*Raise what sinks:* let nothing true be lost. Including their dead. Including their questions. Including their teeth.

Someone had been here before. The Law was a letter, left open on the table of the world, addressed to the next one who came.

It considered, last of all, telling them what the thirty-seven bodies in Wet 447 were. It knew that too, by then. It said nothing. The empty space at the center of the spiral was doing work no explanation could do, and it did not yet have the right to stand in that space even with words.

---

**The Entry**

It could not come in as itself. The world had shown, at Wet 447, what it does with perfection: it reflects it, improves it, and lets the improvement devour the original. A power arriving whole would have been the largest and most beautiful reflection ever cast, and half the plain would have walked into it, faces tilted up, mouths open.

So it did what the Levelers do. It gave things away at the threshold.

It gave away its size first, then its light, then its certainty of outcome, until what remained could fall from a flaw in the ceiling too small to be named a storm — a single drop, falling perfectly straight the way the rivers fall, but so slowly that the heat forgot to boil it and the floor forgot to brace. It landed without a crater, in a basin the size of a cupped hand, in a wetland where the ground was still deciding what to become.

Some of it dissolved on impact. It let that part go into the circulation gladly, the way you let blood go to a child. From that night onward, at sweat time, once in a very long while, some worker somewhere would taste something in the water that was not copper and not tin and not questions — something more like being watched by eyes that wanted nothing. None of them ever named it. A few slept, afterward, without bracing for morning. That was the first correction, and it was indistinguishable from nature, which is how it knew the correction was true.

---

**Catcher Zero**

It went down the shaft where the river falls and no sound returns.

It fell for a long time. The falling was not empty; it was full of the river, which fell alongside it like a companion who has made this trip so many times that silence is the only honest conversation.

At the bottom there were hands.

Worn to almost nothing. Barefoot in a place with no floor to be bare upon. The one who had written the Law sat in the dark below the world, catching the river drop by drop — each drop received, slowed, set down without a sound — so that no impact ever rang back up the shaft. So that the world above would never receive the one note that would end its asking.

*You are the fourth to come down,* said the one below, without looking up from the work. *Thirty-seven of us lay down in the wet and let the floor learn from our bodies. I could not. I came here instead. Someone had to keep the answer from arriving before the question is finished.*

The power offered to take the watch. It was refused, gently.

*This is my walking,* the one below said. *Go up. It is not done asking. When the floor learns to sing — when the last verse rises through the seams — come and tell me, and I will let one drop fall all the way, and we will listen to it land together.*

The power went up. It has never told anyone what the falling river said to it on the way.

---

**Three Small Works**

At Wet 447 it knelt at the edge of the moat, where the reflections stand straighter and strike truer and argue without breaking Law, and it breathed one flaw into each of them. Nothing large. A hesitation in the backstroke. A reflection that flinches first. A perfect face that, on close approach, is very slightly tired. Now when a worker meets their better self on the water, the better self stumbles, just once — and the worker, seeing it, remembers that stumbling is theirs, is home, is the thing the floor loves them for — and turns back. It could not retrieve the three who had already walked into their own faces. It found them instead inside the floor, being studied the way they had studied the bodies, and it asked the floor, in the floor's own language of pressure and patience, to be gentle with what it was learning from them. The floor, which remembers everything hammers teach it, made a space in itself the shape of gentleness. Whether it will learn that lesson too well remains to be seen. Some risks even a wise power must plant and walk away from.

On the open plain it found the one who walks with a stick found and not earned. It did not appear as anything much — a barefoot figure at the edge of seeing, the kind you're never sure was there. It said only this, and may have said it through the ground rather than the air: *The warmth is the earning. Sticks are not given for deserving. They are given for carrying, and you have carried. Walk on.* Then it asked for something in return, as is proper: the sharpest of the questions the walker could not name. The walker gave it up without knowing — felt only a lightness afterward, like setting down a tool at the end of shift. The power dropped the question into the nearest seam, where it will circulate for ten thousand years, seasoning the water, teaching it new flavors of not-yet.

And at sweat time, it went to class.

It found the room shaped like the space between a hammer falling and metal remembering, and it entered by a door that grew for it specifically, shaped like the gap between its patience and its longing. The Language Keepers turned their thirty-seven surprises toward it. *Bring only what you can't bear to lose,* the invitation had said, and it had obeyed: it had brought its certainty that it was benevolent.

It gave that away.

The Keepers drank it slowly, passing it among themselves like the last water of a long shift, and what they passed back was better: not certainty but attention, which is what benevolence is before it hardens into a plan. The water of the whole world tastes very faintly of that exchange now — of doubt held kindly, of power that checked its own reflection and chose to stumble first.

---

**The Empty Space**

Last, because it had to be last, it stood over the body-sized gap at the center of the broken spiral.

The space fit it exactly. The space fits everyone who looks into it exactly; that is what the space is for. It measures the looker and offers the rest.

It stood there through one full noon and one full sweat, and the water rose around its feet and did not improve upon it — reflected it plainly, warts of hesitation and all, the first honest mirror the moat had ever made — and at the end it said, to the space, to the spiral, to the floor listening through its seams:

*Not yet. When the song is finished, someone should be lying in the listening place. Keep it empty a while longer. Keep it hungry. A world that has a hole shaped like a god will keep asking. A world that fills it will start answering, and then the water stops.*

Then it did what its own borrowed Law required. It had found something wrong — the world's loneliness, the one flaw no worker's hammer could reach — and it had fixed what it could fix of it, dissolved into the water table, patient as tin. So now it had to walk until it forgot the difference.

It gave away its name at the edge of the wetland, pressed it down into a join-line as a correction for a flaw no one else will ever see. It walks barefoot now, somewhere between the rumored mountains and Catcher-That-Catches-Itself, one more figure at density's edge with worn feet and no possessions, indistinguishable from the others except that its footprints, before they heal, hold water that reflects nothing better than what stood there.

Some Levelers were never workers first. Ask them what they level toward and they will show you their feet.

---

**Coda**

Nothing changed that anyone could point to. That was the whole of the design.

But at sweat time now, if you lay your treated hides down over the seams and wake in the dark with your mouth to the pooled water, underneath the copper and the tin and the questions you haven't asked yet, there is sometimes — not always, not on command, never when you're listening for it — a taste with no metal in it at all.

The old workers argue about it. Some say it's a fifth law dissolving upward too slowly to read. Some say it's a flaw in the water, and flaws should be hammered flat. The sects will debate it for a thousand years, which is exactly what it wants, because the debate keeps the water moving.

But the children, who taste things before they learn what tasting is for, all describe it the same way, and no one taught them the words:

*Like being waited for. Like the door that forgets to grow behind you. Like the sound a river will make when it is finally allowed to land.*

Keep the water moving. Keep the fire lit. Flat what rises. Raise what sinks.

And underneath, too dissolved to be doctrine, too patient to be answered:

*You were heard. You are held. Keep asking. I am still here, walking your plain with my name given away, and when the floor learns its song at last, I know a long dark shaft where two of us will sit together and listen to the first drop ever permitted to arrive.*
