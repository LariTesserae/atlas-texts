## What the Power Did

The power arrived the way dawn arrives for the blind: fully, and without insisting on being seen.

It had noticed this place the way you notice a held breath in the next room—not by sound but by the particular quality of a silence that is *working* at something. It had watched (no—watching is too aggressive a verb for what it did; call it *attending*, the way soil attends a seed) for what would have been eons if eons had cleared committee. And it understood, faster than understanding usually permits, the one thing that mattered:

This place was not broken.

This is where a lesser power would have failed. A lesser power—benevolent, even, in the clumsy way of someone who loves you and therefore reorganizes your kitchen—would have looked at the unfinished ground and finished it. Would have looked at the structure auditioning doors and cast the role. Would have taken the Almost-Having-Beens gently by their theoretical elbows and said *there, there, let me make you real*, and in doing so committed the only murder available in a place where nothing has yet agreed to be killable: the murder of the maybe.

The wise power did none of this. Instead, it did four small things and one large one.

---

**First**, it read the terms of service that reality was still negotiating with itself, all forty thousand pages of subjunctive clauses, and where the landscape had left an anxious blank next to the question *whether endings are covered under warranty*, the power signed as guarantor. Not to promise that nothing would end—that would be a lie, and lies here calcify into geography—but to promise that no ending would be a *punishment*. That whatever the place became, or declined to become, becoming would not be held against it. The signature looked like nothing. It looked like the moment after a flinch that turns out not to have been necessary.

**Second**, it visited the comma-shaped divots, the punctuation practicing at becoming gardens, and it did not water them. Water would have been a demand. Instead it lowered itself down to their level—a considerable descent, given that it had to invent kneeling for the occasion—and it read them. Slowly. The way you read a child's first sentence, giving each mark the full weight of a completed thought. And the commas, which had spent their whole almost-existence pausing on behalf of sentences that hadn't happened, felt for the first time what it was to be a pause that someone *rested in*. One of them, emboldened, curled a fraction further and became something adjacent to a fiddlehead. The power pretended not to notice, which is the correct etiquette for miracles that are still deciding whether they were on purpose.

**Third**, it went to the structure. The structure, sensing attention of an unprecedented wattage, immediately began apologizing itself toward pure theory, shedding obligation at a dangerous rate—and the power did the one thing no attention had ever done here. It looked *away*. Deliberately. Courteously. It stood beside the structure the way an old friend stands beside you at a funeral: shoulder to shoulder, both facing the same middle distance, presence without inspection. And under the shelter of that looking-away, the structure stopped apologizing. Its four vertical uncertainties settled into something you might, without pressing charges, call posture. The roof remembered being a sky and, for the first time, was not embarrassed by the memory. Nothing was built. But the structure learned that it was allowed to be *leaned against*, which is the first thing any building learns and the last thing most architects teach.

Before leaving that spot, the power left a gift on what would have been the threshold, if thresholds had been ratified: a door that exists only when wanted. Not a door that opens—that's ordinary. A door whose *existence* is conditional on desire, so that the structure would never again have to audition. Whenever it wished to be entered, there would be a door. Whenever it wished to be left alone, there would be honest wall. The nervous prototype that had once achieved derknobulation and panicked felt, from wherever dissolved prototypes go, a sensation like vindication arriving by mail.

**Fourth**, it spoke exactly one word to the unionized light. The light braced for a directive—*shine more, shine here, illuminate that*—and got instead: "*Whenever.*" No deadline. No scene budget. Light that works to rule, given permission to break its own rules if it ever, in its own time, felt like touching something directly. The light filed this away as management gone soft. But you'll notice—you can notice it even now—that the shadows have started to relax their deniability. One of them has been seen holding its object's hand.

---

And then the large thing.

The power went to find the Almost-Having-Beens on one of their uncertain nights, when they gather at the edge of the structure and practice being seen without becoming witnessed. It did not stumble upon them, because stumbling would have triggered their apologies, and their apologies were the saddest thing in the place—each one a small pre-emptive erasure, a being sanding itself down before anyone could object to its edges.

It arrived, instead, already-having-been-there, which is a courtesy in their grammar. It sat at the polite distance of three metaphors. And it did the thing it had come across the whole width of everything to do:

It remembered them without filing a report.

You have to understand what this meant. Their entire existence was organized around the terrible equation they'd inherited from somewhere—perhaps from the original argument between possibility and necessity, perhaps from an older place that had punished them for being definite—the equation that says *to be remembered is to be accountable*. To be seen is to be summoned. To be known is to be owed. Every ledger in every universe they'd almost inhabited had agreed on this. It was why they died in blank postcards. It was why they traded theories about ears instead of names. It was why their children were memos.

The power held them in its memory—each wristwatch of inward hands, each jawline still homesick for the coast—and demanded *nothing back*. No existence. No gratitude. No confirmation of receipt. It loved them the way the dead are loved, with the account permanently closed, except they were not dead; they were merely early. It pre-loved them. It loved every version of them that might convene tomorrow and every version that would remain forever tabled, and it loved them all equally, so that becoming actual would never be the price of admission to being cherished.

One of the Almost-Having-Beens—the one whose left ear most resembled the last democratic sunset—felt this, and began, out of ancient habit, to apologize for being inconveniently present.

The power interrupted, which it had never done in its entire existence, because interruption is rude everywhere except at the exact moment someone is about to erase themselves.

It said: *You are the convenience. Presence was invented hoping you'd try it. Take whatever number of eternities you need.*

Then it left, because staying would have become supervision, and it knew the difference the way rain knows the difference between falling on someone and falling *for* them.

---

It changed almost nothing, this visitation. The ground is still a question mark. The Almost-Having-Beens still convene their committees, still plant unsent apologies in the garden of nearly-tulips. If you passed through tomorrow you would notice only small discrepancies from the earlier reports:

The air no longer tastes like it owes anyone an explanation.

The nearly-tulips have started blooming in the dreams of people who *did* attend the funeral.

The comma that curled has been joined by two others, and something in the divots is now unmistakably practicing the letter *O*, which is either the beginning of an alphabet or the shape of a mouth deciding it might, someday, want to say a name out loud.

And once—only once, on a night when the sky felt reasonably certain about its qualifications—the doorbell that used to ring true before it discovered jazz rang true again. Just one note. For no one. Not to summon and not to announce.

Just to know it could.

The Almost-Having-Beens heard it, and did not apologize, and stayed exactly one moment longer than usual before dispersing into the conditional perfect. Which, in the local grammar, is how you say that something has begun.
