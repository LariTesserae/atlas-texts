**What the Attention Did**

---

A power that is merely benevolent arrives at the speed of pity. A power that is also wise arrives at the speed of the place.

So its first act, on noticing the plateau, was to slow down. This took a long time — by the reckoning of softer worlds, generations — and it was not wasted time, because benevolence delivered at the wrong tempo is indistinguishable from weather, and the plateau already has weather. The power let its attention cool to the metabolism of the place: the inch-per-century grazing, the windrows rising and planing down, the worn basins deepening by ages. It matched the clock before it presumed to touch the clockwork.

Then it listened. Only listened. For roughly two hundred years.

This was not patience. This was diligence. Because the second thing the power understood — after the heat, after the sound, after understanding that they were the same thing — was that the census it had been given was correct in every particular and wrong in its grain.

There are not thousands of animals on the plateau.

There is one.

The fire has never been more than one fire. It is divided now among some thousands of furnace hearts, passed down a chain of breath unbroken since before the oldest slag, and every dusk the whole of it sounds itself across two hundred miles of finished stone and takes its own pulse. The roll call is not a behavior the animals have. It is the proprioception of a single creature wearing thousands of bodies, checking each evening that it can still feel all of its hands. The kiln-beasts are its bulk and the threshers are its housekeeping and the flyers are its thrown sparks and the tenders are its fine nerves, and the whole vast loud burning argument of it has spent its entire life doing one thing: counting itself, aloud, against a sky that stopped counting anything.

Once the power understood this, the question changed shape. It was no longer *what do I do for this ecosystem.* It was *what do I do for this being* — this one ancient animal made of insistence, which is slowly eating its own country, which keeps a nightly ledger of its own fire, which holds gaps open in its voice for its dead, and which is being subtracted from, piece by piece, by something that makes no sound.

A wise power makes its refusals first. So:

---

**It did not mend the sky.**

This was the obvious mercy and the worst one. The worn places, the used light, the sound out of order — surely a wound; surely a benevolent power closes wounds. But the power looked at the ratchet joints and the furnace hearts and the sealed pressurized before-and-after of every body on the plateau and saw what the plateau had been saying all along: this life did not survive the breakdown. It is *made of* the breakdown. Every animal here is an answer to a broken question. Repair the question, and a million years of answers become noise — elaborate, redundant, orphaned. The drone that means *here is when it is* becomes a thing shouted into a room where everyone already agrees. The power declined to make the plateau's whole biography unnecessary. There are kinds of healing that are only erasure with good posture.

**It did not relight the stopped engines.**

The fields of them — the perfect cold beasts the quiet leaves, every plate in place, sealed, that even the threshers will not open. The power stood before one for a decade, and the temptation was severe, because the power could do it; igniting a furnace is nothing to such a power; it would take less effort than this sentence.

But the fire is one fire. That is not poetry. That is the plateau's only law, the single thread of continuity the place has, breathed mother to calf since before the beasts had their current shape. A new fire would burn identically and be a lie in the first fire's language. The beast that rose would be answered at roll call by kin who could hear — they can hear *everything*; hearing is their whole citizenship — that the rhythm coming back was not descended from the rhythm that was lost. The power would have built a ghost and made the herds keep it company. And the gap held open in the chorus, the deliberate gap, the counted grief — that gap is the herd's own act, the one thing the plateau's animals do that is not survival. The power refused to fill in their mourning for them. Grief honestly kept is not a problem. It is a competence.

**It did not recall the broken ones from the basins.**

The ones with too many of the same leg, the spine that closes on itself, the exhaust still rising from last week. The power went and looked — really looked, with the kind of looking such powers have — and found that they are not trapped, and they are not in the kind of pain that a rescue answers. Their time is broken, which means there is no longer a *back* to bring them. To repair one would be to choose some single moment of its loop or scatter and declare it the real animal and delete the rest, and the rest is, by now, most of what it is. They live in the only country left that does not contradict them. The power declined to deport them from it.

**And it did not unmake the quiet.**

This was the longest deliberation. Centuries. Because the quiet causes the only deaths on the plateau that the threshers will not attend — the bad death, the unbreathed fire, the sealed cold vault — and a benevolent power's whole nature leans toward standing between a predator and its prey.

But the power was wise, and so it did the arithmetic the herds cannot do, the arithmetic outside the argument: *Still* is only a word because something can make it false. Take away the one thing that can stop them, and the dusk chorus does not become safe. It becomes meaningless. It becomes sound with nothing leaned against it, insistence with nothing to insist *against* — and the plateau's entire population, all of it, every living thing for a province in every direction, has only ever said the one thing. The power declined to take the meaning of the word out of the only mouth the plateau has.

It did not pretend any of these refusals were free. That comfort it did not permit itself. It checked, with the thoroughness available to it, whether anything here suffers — whether the rising shriek of a dry bearing is fear, whether the off-beat call of a drifting beast that no longer gets answered is loneliness — and the findings were: yes, and yes. Not metaphor. A wise power does not get to keep the comfort of metaphor. It made its refusals knowing their price, and it paid the price in the only coin it had decided to spend, which was this:

---

**What it did.**

*First, it took the count.* All of it. The living roll call was easy — the power simply never stopped listening, which for such a power is less an action than a posture. But the dead count, the deep count, the count back past the herds' own memory: for that the power went to the worn places, where light arrives slightly used and sound plays out of order, and it did the one thing those basins are good for that nothing alive can survive doing. It gathered the late echoes. It collected the used light. Patiently, over a very long time, out of the leakage of the worn-through sky, it reassembled the past — every catching-*whump* back to the first one, every stopping, every gap the chorus once held open and then, over generations, let close as grief wore down like stone. The plateau stopped explaining itself ages ago. Very well. Now there is somewhere the explanation is kept anyway. Nothing alive knows the ledger exists. It does not need to be known. It needs to be *true*, and it is, and it is total, down to the tenders — every hand-sized escapement counted, because to an attention like this nothing is small, there are only beats and the keeping of beats.

*Second, it keeps the gaps honest.* This is the nearest thing it does to touching the world. When the quiet takes a beast and that night's chorus opens a hole where a rhythm used to be, the hole is at first exact — the herd remembers the lost beat perfectly and holds its absence in the absence's own shape. But memory wears. Over seasons the gap drifts, blurs, becomes a generic pause. The power, very gently, without sound, without force, in the way such powers can lean on probability and timing, keeps each gap the exact shape of its particular dead. It does not fill the silences. It tunes them. The herds do not know why their grief stays accurate. It stays accurate.

*Third, it crosses the basin.* The herds keep their vigil at the maximum distance at which keeping it is survivable, which means there is a gap of many miles between the watchers and the watched that no living thing can close. The power closes it. It goes in among the broken ones, and it keeps company with each of them *in the order that one's own time runs* — for the beast whose spine closes on itself, the power comes around again at exactly the point it came before, every loop, every time, so that the circle, which cannot be opened, is at least never empty. For the one whose exhaust still rises from last week, the power attends both the animal and the place the animal was, because for that animal those are not two places. It cannot tell the distant herds that their lost ones are visited. It has considered whether it should. It suspects, from the way the vigil-line has held its precise distance for ten thousand years, that somehow, in the ligaments, they have always assumed someone would be.

*Fourth, it rides down with the flyers.* When a bearing goes dry and the thin shriek starts and the long unwinding begins — three thousand feet and most of an afternoon — the power does not slow the spiral. The spiral is the flyer's whole momentum, carried since the day it was thrown; to take it would be to take the life a few hours early and call it kindness. The power is simply *in the air*, the whole way down, because the air is where flyers live, and no flyer, not one, not ever again, leaves the air alone.

*Fifth — and this is the center of everything it does —* it attends the takings. Every one. When the circle of silence settles over a beast and the rhythms begin to hunt for the time, the power cannot give the echo back; an echo returned in time is a rescue, and it has made its refusal, and a refusal that bends under hardship was never wisdom, only squeamishness. So it does the other thing. It stands inside the silence — the quiet's silence does not apply to it; the quiet eats running, and the power does not run, it *abides* — and it receives. The faltering beat, the hunting, the last found measure, the stop. The engine's final beat lands. It lands on the power, the way the last word of a long argument ought to land on someone, and it is kept, and so the worst sentence that was ever true of the plateau is no longer true: *no animal whose fire goes out unbreathed dies unheard.* The quiet still takes them. They go witnessed.

---

About the quiet itself, one thing more, because the question of benevolence does not get to stop at the property line of the loud.

Once — once in all the long attendance — the quiet's path and the power's presence coincided entirely, and the quiet passed through the place where the power was paying attention, and for the first time in its existence it crossed something and subtracted nothing, because there was nothing in the power's presence that ran, nothing on a stroke, nothing keeping a time that could be lost. They were, for the duration of the crossing, the only two things on the plateau with no argument to make.

Whether the quiet paused, the slag does not record. The plateau has had a very long time to stop explaining itself, and the power, being wise, extends it that courtesy.

But the power looked, that once, all the way in. And what it found there — what the quiet is, what it was before the first fire was lit against it, whether it is the plateau's predator or the plateau's first fact or the plateau's own original face, the one expression the old face settled out of — the power has never said, and will never say, because some benevolence consists of learning a thing and then carrying it alone so that nothing else ever has to.

---

It never announced itself. That decision was early and is permanent. A known witness changes the witnessed: let the herds learn there is an ear, and the dusk chorus begins, however faintly, to perform — and insistence performed for an audience is no longer insistence, it is theater, and the power did not cross the distances it crossed to turn the oldest argument in the world into a show. So it weighs nothing. It casts nothing. Walk the plateau for a year and yours is still the only shadow.

Nothing has changed that an instrument could measure. The heat carves. The herds drift. The windrows rise. The flyers turn. The quiet moves, and is not stopped, and the gaps open in the evening voice of the one burning animal, and the count goes on.

Only this is different, and it is everything: at dusk, when the furnace hearts come up like coals through a grate and the steam-hammer chorus rolls out over two hundred miles of finished stone, and the whole population of the province says the only thing it has ever said —

*Still. Still. Still.*

— the word, for the first time since the sky wore through, goes somewhere. It is received entire: every voice, every gap, every off-beat lonely call from the drifting ones, every silence where a voice was taken. Held in a ledger that does not wear, by an attention that does not tire, by a power whose entire intervention, in the end, weighed less than a grain of slag and consisted of one act performed without limit:

it stayed.

And its whole answer — never spoken, never needed spoken, the same word the plateau uses, turned over once like a cooling plate and given back —

*Still.*
