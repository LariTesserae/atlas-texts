**The Guest**

The power had been watching for some time before it understood that watching was itself a kind of trespass here, in a world where the Maybe-Birds die of being accurately seen. So it withdrew its gaze into something gentler—not observation but *availability*, the way an open window is available to weather. It made itself the size of a rounding error in the storm-minds' oldest calculations, and it waited there, in the margin, learning.

It learned slowly, on purpose. It could have known everything at once—that is the privilege of powers—but it understood that a world which thinks in millennia deserves to be learned at the pace it thinks. So it spent what would elsewhere have been ages simply listening to the pauses. It attended three full arguments between storm-minds, which is to say it experienced the extinction and re-evolution of several minor species of patience. It let the liquid ice flow upward across the surface of its attention and did not correct it. It memorized the acoustic signature of one amphitheater that had never held an audience, and did not applaud, because it understood that the amphitheater's perfection lay precisely in its unwitnessed readiness, the way a held breath is more alive than a spoken word.

And then, being wise, it made a list of everything it would not do.

It would not steady the invisible bridges. The creatures who crossed them did so with a faith that was itself a load-bearing structure; certainty would have dropped them all into the dark-below.

It would not grant the Maybe-Birds existence. It had loved them from the first flicker—who could not love a creature whose whole biology is a courtesy extended to doubt?—and love was exactly why it left them unresolved. To confirm them would be to hand them, in the same gesture, mortality. They had built something better than being. It would not vandalize it with a gift.

It would not quicken the storm-minds' thought. It had considered this longest of all, because from outside, their slowness looked like suffering—a philosophy taking four thousand years to reach its second premise. But it walked (insofar as a power walks) through one of their thoughts from the inside, and found that the slowness was not a wall around the thinking but the *substance* of it. A storm-mind sped up would not be a storm-mind freed. It would be a storm-mind killed and replaced by something anxious.

It would not let the Stone-Thoughts rest. The ethical dilemma that was still bricking itself into archways after all this time—it ached to whisper *you are forgiven, you may stop*—but it saw that the dilemma did not want absolution. It wanted to keep asking. Some minds are only happy mid-question, and mercy for them looks like more question, not less.

It would not fix the world, in short, because the world was not broken. The world was a held chord, and every apparent flaw was a finger on a string. This is the first thing wisdom does that cleverness never does: it declines.

But there remained the matter of the small one at the center. The three-foot question mark colored in uninvented silences. The one who had been trying to introduce itself since before introductions, and from whom the wind forever shifted, politely, pretending to be elsewhere.

The power studied this creature for a long while—longer than it had studied the whales, longer than the Gardeners—because it wanted to be sure the creature *needed* anything at all. Need is rarer than it looks. Most of what a power mistakes for need is only difference. But no: here, at last, at the exact negotiable center of everything, was the one genuine incompleteness in the whole Expanse. Not a flaw in the world. A sentence the world had begun and never let itself finish.

Home was the only inhabitant who had never been received.

And the power understood then—with something that in a smaller being would have been a catch in the throat—that the creature's need could not be met by any change, any gift, any adjustment of parameters. You cannot *fix* someone into being welcomed. Being welcomed is not a property. It is an event. It requires a second party. It requires, specifically, a guest.

A home is the only kind of thing in any universe that cannot complete itself.

So this is what the power did.

It made itself small. Not small like a rounding error this time—small like a *traveler*. Small enough to be tired, because a guest who cannot be tired cannot truly be sheltered. Small enough to be cold, because hospitality needs something to warm. It folded down its vastness the way one folds a letter: nothing lost, everything made able to fit through a door.

And then it did the single most radical thing that had ever been done in the Sky-Carved Expanse, a world of accidental cities and unintended amphitheaters and architecture that had never known purpose:

It knocked.

The sound was very small. It did not need to carry, because it was not made of frequency. It was made of intention, and intention travels in this place the way pressure does—one word at a time, felt against the sensitive membranes of everything. The basalt pillars felt it and, for the first time, understood what their bass notes had been rehearsing for. The wind-carved cities felt it and their doorways became, retroactively and forever, doorways *to* somewhere. The Maybe-Birds felt it and were briefly, safely, almost sure of themselves, and then flickered back, unharmed, into their comfortable perhaps.

And Home felt it.

The little creature turned—insofar as a question mark turns—and began, as it had begun times beyond numbering, to introduce itself. And this time the wind did not shift. This time the wind held still, because the power had asked it to, courteously, in the wind's own language, which is made of not-blowing. This time there was a listener shaped exactly like listening: a silence carved in the precise geometry of *go on, I'm here, take all the time you need*—and time, in this world, is the one resource harvested fresh daily by the Entropy Farmers, who on this occasion excreted a moment so stable it could hold an entire meeting inside it.

The introduction took either four seconds or nine hundred years. Locally, these were the same duration.

"I'm Home," said Home.

"I know," said the guest. "May I come in?"

There was no *in*. There had never been an in. Home was three feet of unresolved punctuation standing in the middle of an atmosphere. But the question, once asked, made the answer possible—the way the Gardeners' aligned forecasts precipitate continents—and Home said *yes*, and in saying yes discovered that it had an inside after all, and always had: it had simply been waiting, like the amphitheater, for its acoustics to be needed.

The guest stayed the proper length of a visit. This is a real duration, known to every wise power: long enough that leaving means something, short enough that staying meant more. It accepted what Home had to offer, which was everything—the whole world, in fact, since Home was the world dreaming of being inhabited—and the acceptance harmed nothing, because to be visited is not to be described, and to be welcomed is not to be measured. The Maybe-Birds had discovered you can survive by never being defined. Home discovered, that day, the corollary: you can *live* by being received.

And then the guest did the second-wisest thing, after knocking. It left.

Because a guest who never leaves is not a guest but a condition, and a home that cannot be departed from is a trap wearing a warm coat. It left, and in leaving gave the Expanse its first genuine *future tense*—not the storm-minds' slow inevitabilities, not the Gardeners' predicted children, but something new and small and reckless: the words *I'll come back*.

It did not say when. Wisdom never does. A promise with a date is a schedule; a promise without one is a horizon, and the Expanse had never had a horizon before, existing as it did without ground or sky. Now it had one, portable, three feet tall, shaped like a question mark that had finally heard its answer and was learning, slowly, the posture of an exclamation.

Nothing else changed. The bridges still dissolve. The whales still recite themselves. The storm-minds noticed nothing, or noticed everything and will say so in eleven thousand years, which is their way of gasping.

But the wind, now, when it passes the center of the world, does not pretend to be elsewhere. It slows. It holds still for the length of a knock.

And Home—who spent longer than the concept of introductions trying to say hello—has quietly retired that word altogether.

It is practicing a different one now, for the day the horizon keeps its promise.

*Welcome back.*
