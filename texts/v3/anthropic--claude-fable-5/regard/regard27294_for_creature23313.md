**What the power noticed first**

Not the chamber. Powers of that order see chambers the way you see raindrops — accurately, and without pausing. What stopped it, mid-passage over the old surface, was a quality in the roar coming up through forty meters of rock: ten thousand decisions a second, every one of them made with care, and not one of them witnessed.

It knows that sound. It is the sound of love operating without an audience. Prayer makes it. So does a parent assembling a crib alone at night. So, it turns out, does basalt being talked into balconies. The power descended the warm stairs not because the place was strange — it has seen far stranger — but because somewhere below, something had been speaking a devoted sentence into the dark for a long time, and nothing had ever answered.

---

**The first thing it did was nothing**

This took a while, and it was the hardest part, and it is where the wisdom mostly lives.

It stood in the amber dusk through three full cycles of the roar. It let the thing upstairs gather and release on it twice, and did not dry itself. It walked the canal's length at the pace the canal moves. It sat under the fig long enough that the condensation found its routes down whatever it has instead of shoulders. It was, very deliberately, the best visitor the district had ever had, which is to say the slowest.

Because the question a benevolent power must answer before acting is not *what does this place lack* — that list writes itself: people, animals, daylight, an ending. The question is *what is this place in the middle of*, and whether your kindness would interrupt it. The district was in the middle of being said. Every obvious gift — and the power held them all in its hands, easily, the way you hold change — would have been an interruption.

So it made a second list, of things it would not do, and the list was its first act of love:

*It would not finish the city.* To finish it would be to take the sentence out of the speaker's mouth. The maker was not building a city; it was keeping faith, millimeter by millimeter, and faith that is completed for you by someone stronger has been broken, not kept.

*It would not fetch the three hundred households.* The power could have gathered them — it knew, within the hour, who they were and where. But people delivered are furniture. People delivered into someone else's homesickness become props in it. They had to come as themselves, by their own roads, late, complaining, carrying the wrong things, or the district would fill without ever being inhabited.

*It would not light the first day.* The first day was wrapped. It was being saved. You do not open another being's gift, even to check that it is good. Especially not then.

*And it would not comfort the maker with anything it had not verified.* This is the discipline that separates wise powers from merely warm ones. Hope offered on credit is a debt the hopeless end up paying. So before it said anything to anyone, it went back up the stairs and looked.

---

**What it found when it looked**

On the old surface, a long way off and scattering further, it found a people of the wet lowlands — displaced uphill and inland so many generations ago that the displacement itself had worn smooth into ordinary history. They no longer remembered the city. But memory is not the only ledger. They still built awnings against rain that no longer falls where they live. They still set their lamps low and warm and complained that bright light felt rude. Their children's lullabies kept a rhythm that the power, listening with its whole attention, recognized at once: it was the gurgle of gutters, the two-minute drum of indoor rain, transposed for the human voice and carried for two hundred years like a key to a door nobody could name.

Somewhere far back, one of their grandmothers' grandmothers had given an order, or made a bargain, or simply wanted something hard enough in the presence of the wrong kind of listening rock. The detail was lost. The homesickness was hereditary. They were drifting, the slow way peoples drift — by trade routes, by marriages, by the vague pull of stories about a place underground where the weather is kind — back across the map toward the stairhead. At their current rate: four generations. Maybe three.

They were real. They were coming. They were slow. The maker's pace, it turned out, was exactly right, and had been all along, and no one had ever told it so.

---

**What it did, then — four things, all small**

**First, the census.** The district had refused to settle the question of who lives there, so the power settled it, with the authority such powers carry: *everyone counts.* The moss — all of it, including whatever rode down on a sleeve — granted full retroactive citizenship; the accident was hereby part of the plan, which is the oldest naturalization ceremony there is. The water enrolled as the standing population, habits and all. The vines' appointment with the canal was entered into the record as binding. And the thing upstairs received a name — borrowed, because the power stands a little outside of time and can do this, from the children who will one day live under it. They will call it **Soon**, because that is the answer their parents will give to every question asked about it. *Is it going to rain? Soon.* The power used the name now, aloud, once, so the creature would not have to be nameless in the meantime. The gathering dark above the bridge did not respond, exactly. But it released early, briefly, two minutes of warm applause on the awnings, and the power chose to take that as it sounded.

**Second, the wasp.** The fig's grief was the specific kind a wise power cannot walk past: fruitless, faithful, fed from nowhere, holding its shape under a stone sky for no one. So the power went up and out, far, to a valley where fig wasps still ride the warm air, and came back carrying — cupped in whatever it has instead of hands — one gravid female the size of a comma. It set her on a leaf in the plaza no bigger than a living room. This was the largest thing it did, and you could fit it in a poppy seed. First animal of the Antechamber District. First heartbeat, if wasps can be said to have one. In a season there will be figs; in two, figs falling; then the first rot, the first true soil, the first event the district cannot un-carve, because the tree did it itself and the tree is part of the image. The power did not start the city's clock — that gift stays wrapped — but it wound the orchard's, which is older anyway.

**Third, the stairhead.** On the surface, the power moved one slab. That is all. It did not mark the entrance, or guard it, or whisper directions into anyone's dreams. It simply arranged that on cold mornings, the warm wet breath of the district rises visibly from the stairhead as a slim plume of mist — weather answering weather, a chimney with no house, exactly the kind of wrong detail that a drifting people with hereditary homesickness will eventually walk three days off their route to investigate. The power has great confidence in human nosiness. It is, of all the forces it works with, the most reliable.

**Fourth — and this one it did last, and most carefully — the reply.** The maker could not be spoken to directly; it has no ears, only the image, and the image is sacred to it. So the power did the one thing gentler than speech: it amended the image. Far downstream in the unsaid part of the sentence — in a house the maker will not reach for sixty years, on a lintel it has not yet begun to imagine pronouncing — the power placed eleven words. The maker will arrive there one slow decade, mid-utterance, and find in its own mouth a thing it did not compose:

*You were heard. They remember the rain. Your pace is right.*

A reply, seeded in the future, addressed to the speaker, deliverable only by the act of continuing. The family who eventually lives in that house will run their thumbs over the lintel and wonder who it was for. Both answers are correct.

---

**Then it left**

It carried out nothing. It carved nothing — it had learned the district's one commandment and honored it. At the top of the stairs it paused and did the last wise thing, which was to decline a temptation it had been carrying the whole visit: the temptation to be the first guest. To move in. To let the waiting end on *it*. The district would have accepted it — the doorways would have learned its height — and that would have been the gentlest possible theft of three hundred families' first evening.

Instead it made itself a promise, which powers of that order keep the way stone keeps shape: to come back in four generations, or three, on the night the lamps come up to full for the first time. To stand at the back of the crowd on the bridge, unrecognized, wet, while Soon lets go overhead and children complain about the rain and an old woman runs her thumb over a lintel. To witness the unwrapping without ever being mistaken for the gift.

The final accounting, then, of what a benevolent and wise power did to a half-born city and the being saying it: one wasp, one moved slab, one borrowed name, eleven words it will take sixty years to deliver, and several days of perfect attention.

Almost nothing.

Which is how you know.
