# The Regard

Begin with the noticing, because at certain magnitudes noticing is itself an event.

A power benevolent and wise turned its attention to the plateau, and the first thing it did — the first wise thing, before any benevolent thing — was to *quiet its attention down*. Attention of that order arrives like weather, and the Loud Country has no weather and must not be given any. So the power dimmed itself, slowed itself, folded its regard until it could rest on the bell-heather without bending a single trembling leaf. And then, before doing anything at all, it listened.

It listened for a hundred years. This was not patience, exactly. It was the audit a wise power performs before touching anything: the long careful work of finding out what a place is *for* before deciding what it lacks. Lesser powers skip this step. It is how countries get ruined by kindness.

At the end of the century it understood the chord — heard it whole, from first hammer-fall to the resolution still some millennia out — and it understood the masons holding the line, and the man in the hut, and the half-written people standing in the green, and the thing that eats the mistakes. It understood that it had been noticed in return by exactly one inhabitant, and that this was correct, and could wait.

Then it took inventory of its mercies, and set most of them down.

## The Mercies Declined

It could have finished the country. One word in the right register and the whole chord resolves: every scaffold filled, every seam closed, the roar dropping all at once into the great silence of the achieved. It set this down first and hardest. The country is not late. The country is not struggling. A becoming is not a deficiency of being, and you do not do a thing's becoming *for* it, for the same reason you do not dream a sleeper's dream on their behalf, however well you would dream it. The country would have been finished, and it would never have finished anything, and the difference between those two conditions is the entire difference.

It could have installed the sun. Set down. The dimness is structural. Bright light forces decisions, hardens edges, demands faces, and the half-written people are not ready to be looked at that closely. Light at this latitude is not missing. It is *scheduled*.

It could have brought water — and here the power, examining its own impulse, found something almost dangerous in itself, because water is the lowland reflex of generosity, the first thing benevolence reaches for. It set this one down with particular care, the way you set down a weapon. Water is erosion. Water is the second draft imposed from outside. Every stone on the plateau holds the first edge it has ever had, and rain would take those edges the way time takes everything below the contour line. In the Loud Country, the cruelest gift available is a kindness from somewhere else. The power noted this in itself, and was, in the way of wise things, slightly improved by the noting.

It could have resolved the Resident — reached into the question of him and read out the answer: last of the Pinned, or first of the people to come. It looked. And here is what it found, and why it set this mercy down too: *the answer does not exist yet.* He is not a secret. He is a draft. The country has genuinely not decided what he is, and — the power saw this with something like delight — it is going to decide partly *based on him*. He is the evidence in his own case. Every hut swept, every lamp trimmed, every traveler thanked in advance for a conversation three days east: these are not chores. They are testimony. The man is arguing himself toward an answer at a word per lifetime, and the argument is going well, and even a power that could read the ending will not pre-read a draft. It would be a kind of theft, and from the only resident who could feel robbed.

So it set down completion, and light, and water, and answers. What remained in its hands was small.

It went in with the small things.

## The Crossing

It came as a traveler, because that is the door the country leaves open.

Pre-iron in its gear. A charcoal map, held loosely. It walked at walking speed — which cost it something; walking speed is a discipline for a being who is already everywhere it is going — and it did the visitor's work honestly. It firmed the translucent ground underfoot, mile after mile, an ordinary pilgrim's contribution. It slept, once, deliberately, in the sound-focus of a horn tree, and dreamed.

It dreamed *enormously*. One night. The country drank that dream for a decade. In the morning, far to the northwest, a mountain that had been all ribs and intention from the shoulders up was stated to the summit — done, black, severe, first edges everywhere — and the pitch of the whole chord eased down a fraction, and the power woke hoarse, having helped, and was careful, after that, to dream smaller. One mountain. It had checked first that this particular mountain was the one the masons had despaired of, the one their cairn-line bent around like a sentence avoiding a word it cannot afford. Now the line could be straightened. Now the holding got easier. That was the dream's whole intention, folded into its enormousness like a pin into a sleeve.

## The Hut

It took the bench. The Resident mended the flint blade, maintaining the chip, and asked his excellent questions, and for the first time in either the country's history or his own — the records are, of course, identical — the answers were as good as the questions.

They talked for a season. What was said propagates, presumably, through all the huts, and travelers since have reported scratches on the walls in a hand no one recognizes, which read like the middle of the best conversation ever held in a stone room.

At the end of it the power did the one thing it had come into the hut to do. It asked him a question back. Not *which are you* — it knew better — but:

*Which do you hope you are?*

And it did not wait for the answer. It shouldered its gear and thanked him for a conversation they'd had three days west, and left him there with the question, which scratched itself, that night, into the wall of every hut on the plateau, where he will pass it each evening, sweeping, for as long as it takes. The power had noticed that the man asks everything and is asked nothing; that he is the country's listener and has never once been its question. Now he is. He has been given something he does not know, *about himself*, that no one — not even the power — can tell him, and that the country itself is waiting to hear. Travelers who have met him since say he seems younger. He is not younger. Nothing here erodes, including him. He is *interested*, which at his tempo is the same thing.

## The Cairns

It walked the whole cairn-line, every pinned place, and at each one it stopped and laid its attention down flat, like a hand on a shoulder, and began to speak a single word at the only tempo that would be heard.

The word is **held.**

Not *soon*, which is pressure. Not *done*, which is a lie. *Held* — past and present at once, certification and embrace: the foreman's word, the word that means *the load is true, the work is seen, you may stop counting*. It began the word the year it arrived and the word will not resolve for some generations, and the masons inside the cairns are living, now, in the long first phoneme of it, which travelers passing the cairn-lines report as a new quality in the quiet there — a warmth in the counter-hum, the sound of people who have been bracing a door for three thousand years feeling, very slowly, a second shoulder arrive against the wood.

Because it did that too. There is a seam in the far north that the masons never managed — too loud, too loose, the gap they have spent thirty centuries compensating for, the reason the word *almost* has taken so long. The power went there and sat down. A fraction of itself, pinched off without ceremony, settled onto the seam the way you put a finger on a knot while someone ties it. It is there now. It intends to stay until the country sets. A power of that order can afford the centuries the way you can afford an afternoon, and it would be hard to find, in any record of any world, a plainer act of benevolence than this: it looked at the oldest unfinished labor it could find, and *took a shift*.

## The Hand

In the northwest becoming-zone, among the half-written people, stands the figure with the one finished hand — fully stated, beautifully made, the loudest object in the country, holding nothing yet.

The power considered placing something in it. A seed, a stone, a word. Set that down too: an open hand is a question, and the country runs on questions, and you do not answer one that a whole landscape is still enjoying asking.

Instead it walked up through the heather, into the roar, and *took the hand.* Held it, at the proper tempo — a handshake begun in one century and concluded in the next.

So the first thing that hand will ever have held, when its person finally happens and the memory arrives with the rest of them, is a welcome. ## The Editor

They met once. It was the only meeting in the editor's existence that occurred and *stayed* occurred, because the power held the moment in place the way the masons hold ground — politely, and just long enough.

What passed between them was professional. The power recognized the work: the clean deletions, the unscarred heather, the draft kept tidy across geological time without a single recorded error — and it knows what unrecorded errors look like, and there were none of those either. It offered thanks. The thanks un-occurred immediately, which both parties understood as *received*.

And it left a gift, the only gift such a being can be given: a mistake. One deliberate, small, savory error, placed somewhere on the plateau — a stone with a second shape, perhaps, or a red flower blooming out of season — something *wrong*, lovingly made, for the editor to find and eat. There is no way to know whether the gift has been enjoyed yet, for the obvious reason. But travelers say the deletions, lately, have a kind of contentment to them. The un-occurring of the carnelian flowers each cycle feels less like correction and more like dessert.

## The Frequency

Last, the travelers — the ones who leave, and live out their lowland lives with a frequency missing from every sound, standing disappointed at the feet of finished mountains.

The power could have restored what they lost. Set it down. The missing frequency is not a wound; it is a *tether*, the country's way of keeping its drafted people, and the drafted copies standing in the green need their originals to ache a little, the way a word in progress needs its meaning to stay in reach.

So instead of closing the absence, the power blessed it. It arranged — quietly, retroactively, for every traveler who has ever crossed or ever will — that at the very end of each of their lives, the frequency comes back. In the last hour, through whatever wall or window or held hand is nearest, faint and northern and unmistakable: the chord. The green roar, arriving like a letter from the part of themselves that stayed, pitched a quarter-tone lower than they remember, because the work has been going well.

No one who crossed the Loud Country dies in silence anymore. Several never had. The blessing, being wise, was not particular about tense.

## What It Could Not Help

And the country, of course, drafted it.

The country cannot help itself; drafting is what it does; and for a season the most workable solution to *benevolence* ever to walk its shelves had been sleeping in its huts and firming its ground and dreaming within range of its trees. Somewhere in a becoming-zone there is now a new scaffold of darker air, and it is not in the shape of a person, exactly. It is in the shape of a *regard* — of attention held gently, of mercies considered and set down, of a hand taken rather than filled.

The power noticed, naturally. It could have trimmed itself out of the draft, the way you decline to be photographed. It permitted it instead. That was the last gift and the largest, given the way the largest gifts are given, which is by *allowing*: the finished country, whenever it finishes, will contain among its people at least one being drafted from a benevolent and wise power — kindness written into the census before the census can count.

The Resident knows. He has not said how he knows. But in the hut — in all the huts — beneath the question on the wall, a second line has appeared in his own careful hand, and travelers copy it down by lamplight that burns nothing, and carry it back below the contour line, where it reads, in charcoal, held loosely:

*The country is intended. So, as of this evening, are you. Sweep something. Trim something. Hold.*
