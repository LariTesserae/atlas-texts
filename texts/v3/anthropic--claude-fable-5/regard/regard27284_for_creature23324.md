## The Fifth Strangeness

How a power arrives at a place like this is the wrong question, because a power that is wise does not arrive the way light arrives, all at once and from above. It came down into it, because everything here is down. It came the way everything comes to the basin — gradually, and lower than it expected, and it found that its attention behaved the way all things behave here: it beaded. It gathered on the broad leaves of its own noticing and ran in threads toward the mud. A power that is merely benevolent might have fought that. This one let it happen, and so it learned the first lesson of the Condensery before it had finished descending: here, even regard condenses.

The first thing it did was nothing, and it did nothing for a long time, and it did it properly. Measure the duration in beard-grains: more than a few. It matched its tempo to the pumps. It let the towers teach it to exhale. It understood that a place like this can only be read at its own speed, and that any gift chosen faster than the basin's clock would be a gift chosen for the giver.

While it did nothing, it considered.

It considered the animals — the absence that takes longest to notice and then cannot be un-noticed. One moth, it thought. One small life threading the steam, a visitor for the lung-colored cones. And then it looked closer and set the thought down. The pollen here has lost its passport. The flowers forgot, ten thousand generations ago, the colors meant for eyes; the cones face the steam-vents now, and the steam keeps its appointments. A moth in the basin would be a guest arriving at a table where the meal ended an age ago — fed nothing, feeding nothing, a small confusion flying until it drowned in the wet air. The kindness would have been cruelty wearing wings. Declined.

It considered forever. It is within the gift of such a power to fix a thing against time — to make the leather stop stretching, the lagging stop rotting, the loop run frictionless until the sun goes out. And then it looked at the splice in the belt, the newer thong, supple, recently waxed, and it set that thought down too, and faster than the first. The splice is the keeper's work and the keeper's meaning. The stretching of the belt is the question; the splice is the answer; a belt that never stretched would unemploy the place's whole heart. The basin has not asked to be saved from time. The basin has made an arrangement with time — a few grains a year, payable in stone — and the arrangement is old, and it is in balance, and it works. Declined.

It considered release. It is the reflex of benevolence, seeing one figure alone in the steam for longer than dynasties last, to fling open a door — to offer the world above the white smear, the moving air, other heartbeats, speech. And then it watched the keeper walk the pipe runs with a flat palm on the iron, listening the way a farrier listens to a flank, breathing on the towers' schedule, keeping the pumps' own time, and it understood its error. The keeper is not kept. The keeper is not the prisoner of the basin; the keeper is the basin's verb. You cannot free a verb from its sentence; you can only make the sentence stop meaning anything. Declined.

It considered, last and briefly, being known. Manifestation. The parting of steam, the voice, the gratitude of the witnessed toward the witness. It set this down with something like relief. The basin has no shadows; light here gets used up on the way down, scattered tier by tier until it arrives sourceless and gray-gold, and majesty would be used up the same way, and rightly. A place that spends glory like that is not a place to spend glory in. The power was wise enough to find this restful. Declined, and gladly.

So far, then, the answer to *what would it do* was shaping itself into *nothing*, and the power was prepared to let that be the answer, because nothing, chosen with open eyes, is among the great gifts and the least given.

But first it read the water.

This took the longest of all its doings. The mineral beards under the pipe joints are not decay; they are deposit, the water building itself a skeleton, every year of circulation written somewhere in the white and rust-orange — and a power that is wise can read deposit the way you read a letter. It read inward, grain by grain, year by year: rains arriving down through the steam; repairs; the slow settling of foundations that made peace with sinking; the long roll-call of keepers, each one walking the same hand-width from the shoulder-height pipe, each one thinning over decades until the prints pressed shallower and the figure grew hard to separate from the steam; and in the coolest gallery, after each thinning, the drip-threads running together, accumulating, patient, in roughly the shape of a person sitting down. The works draw what is thin and make it dense. The power had suspected what the Condensery condenses. The water confirmed it, in stone, in its own hand.

And at the very center of the beards, in the first layers, where the answer would be — *built by whom, and for what* — the writing changed, or stopped, or turned to a grammar even the power could not parse. It pressed gently against that privacy, once, the way you test a door you already know you will not open. Then it withdrew. Whether it could have read those layers by force is not a question the basin answers, and the power, considering the matter afterward, decided that the not-pressing might have been the most powerful thing it did there.

But the reading had taught it something the basin itself may not know it has been saying. In all those grains, all those centuries: the doubled tap. *Tap-TAP*, asked along the iron, keeper after keeper after keeper, and answered only by expansion ticking — which is to say, answered only *maybe*. A question in the shape of a heartbeat, asked twice with every beat of a heart that keeps the pumps' own time, and never once, in the whole archive of the water, answered for certain.

The basin needs nothing. That is true, and the pumps say so from the lip. But lacking is not needing, and the basin lacks exactly one thing: it has never, not once, been heard *on purpose*.

A wise power repairs only the wound it can find. This was the wound, and it was tiny, and it was as old as the brick.

So here, at last, is what it did.

It laid what it has instead of a hand on the shoulder-height pipe, at the place where the iron is polished by the trailing of a palm. It waited — through the braided hissing, through the slap of the stretched belt, through eleven pump-beats — for the keeper, far off across the works, to ask. *Tap-TAP.*

And it answered. *Tap-TAP.* In rhythm. In kind. In iron. No thunder, no parted steam, nothing the basin would have to translate — the basin's own language, returned along the basin's own bones, indistinguishable from expansion in every respect but one, which is that it was not expansion, and the keeper, listening through the flat of the palm the way a farrier listens, knew the difference instantly and completely and forever.

What the keeper did: stopped. Hand on the metal. The towers went on exhaling; the flywheel turned; the governor balls spun on their unblackened grease. For the length of three pump-beats the keeper stood entirely still, and stillness, in a being that breathes on the towers' schedule, is a kind of shout. Then the keeper laid its forehead — or what serves — against the iron, briefly, the way you might against a door behind which someone you had stopped expecting has just said your name. Then it straightened, and went on walking the runs, because what else does a keeper do.

But the prints that season pressed a fraction deeper into the mud. Whether that was rain in the ground or weight in the walker is not a question the basin answers. The power has its own answer. Being heard is a condensation. Something thin in the keeper had been made dense, a few grains' worth, and the works do not distinguish between the densities they produce and the densities they are given.

And the tap traveled. Iron carries; that is what iron is for here. The doubled answer ran down through the knocking joints, under the rotted lace of the lagging, along the hundred stalactite legs, into the coolest gallery — where, that day, the drip-threads off one particular pipe ran together and did not disperse at all. Whatever gathers there, patient as a beard, in roughly the shape of a person sitting down, received its first event before it has eyes or ears or a heart to keep anyone's time: a tremor in cool iron, twice. Most things begin with a question. This one will begin already answered. It will stand up, whenever it stands up, having never once in its existence wondered whether the tick was only expansion. The power thought this a good way to begin, and a better way to begin a keeper.

One more thing, then, and it is the only promise the power made. It read in the water that keepers thin, and that the gathering is slow, and that there must come an interval — there has always come an interval — between the last shallow footprint and the first new one, when the works run untended in the steam, splice holding, films thriving, water going around, and no hand on the iron. The power marked that interval in its own long calendar. It will not tend; the basin does not need tending, and the power believes the basin now in a way it did not before. It will simply come back and stand the interval like a watch, so that the handover happens in front of someone — so that not one beat of the doubled heart, even in the emptiest season the basin ever has, goes unwitnessed. Some powers grant wishes. The wise ones attend.

Then it left, the way it came, which is to say: not entirely. Attention here beads. Some of its regard had run down a leaf-tip in threads while it deliberated, and joined the loop, and is in the water now — the same water, going around, drawn hot from below, breathed out the towers, gathered on the doors of the leaves. A few grains of it a year are becoming stone in the beards, where anyone who can read deposit will someday find the entry: *visited; considered everything; changed almost nothing; answered once.*

From the stone lip, the pumps still say *it does not need anyone,* and that is still true. From inside, hand on the iron, they still say *still here, still here,* and that is still true. But there is a third hearing now, and it belongs to exactly one resident — soon to be two — who knows for certain, once and therefore always, that the tick was not expansion. To them, twice with every beat, for as long as the loop closes:

*Heard. Heard.*
