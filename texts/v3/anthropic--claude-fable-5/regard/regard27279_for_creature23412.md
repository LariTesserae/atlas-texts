**What Attends the Bright Understory**

It came in through the light, because that is the only way this country admits anything.

It did not descend the way you did, by the long throat of stone, boots and bellows-lungs and all your unmeant noise. It arrived the way the day arrives here: from above, through a kilometer of salt and calcite, soaked and scattered and scrubbed of direction, grain by grain, until what reached the floor of the world was not a presence so much as a quality of attention diffused through the brightness — sourceless, shadowless, even. A wise power knows that the first courtesy is to enter a place in the place's own idiom. So it filtered down through the vault the way rain does, weeks late, and pooled in the light, and was simply there one bloom-cycle where the cycle before it had not been, and nothing on the plain so much as flinched.

What drew it was not what usually draws such things. Powers are called by smoke and prayer, by need broadcasting on its great rude frequencies, by the noise of want. This place broadcasts nothing. It found the Bright Understory the way you find a held breath in a quiet room — by the shape of the silence, which was not an empty silence but a kept one, maintained, load-bearing, deliberate as masonry. There are few sounds in any world louder than a hush sustained on purpose for ten thousand years. It came to see who was holding it.

And then, being wise, it did the wise thing, which is nearly indistinguishable from doing nothing.

It watched for a thousand blooms. It learned to tell time by the ceiling, as they do, because there is no other clock and it would have been a kind of rudeness to keep its own. It matched its speed to the speed of the country — the cricket rivers, the creeping film, the lime coming down grain by grain — until its thoughts moved at the pace of seep-water, and only then did it begin to read.

It did not learn the language from the living. Their signing is too fast, too intimate; it flickers between two people like light on water, and to overhear it at that speed felt like reading someone's face in their sleep. It learned instead from the stone. At the oldest basins it read downward through the lime — and reading down, here, is reading back — through stratum under stratum of pressed red palms, ten thousand years of recorded choruses, the deepest rings first, working up toward the fresh ochre at the living surface. It expected, frankly, prayers. Powers always do; it is a professional weakness. It expected to find, at the bottom of all that pigment, the old familiar grammar of asking — for water, for safety, for the sun back.

The rings do not ask. Every ring, in every stratum, at every basin, is the same utterance: the open hand, held flat, pressed still — the sign that is louder than any other thing in their language, the one they make in the doorways when the blaze comes, the one nothing in a surface tongue translates. The nearest glosses are *here*, or *yes*, or *witnessed*, or *amen*, and none of them is close, because all of those words are answers, and this sign is not an answer to anything. Ten thousand years of choruses, hundreds of hands at a time, saying in one voice the thing that is not a request and not a thanksgiving and not a lament: *we are not waiting.*

This unmoored the power for some while — perhaps a century; it had stopped counting in anything but blooms. Benevolence is an instrument shaped to fit need, and it had arrived carrying its whole gleaming toolkit and found the lock long since dissolved. So it sat with its tools in the even light, and one by one it considered them, and one by one it set them down.

It could have warmed the air. It did not; the cold is a wall of the house.

It could have banished the hunters, those long white patiences working the herd-margins. It did not, because the country is whole, and a benevolence that cannot tell wholeness from hardship is not wisdom but only power with good intentions, which is the most common thing in any universe and the most dangerous.

It could have lifted the old terror of brightness out of the grazers — that alarm older than its cause, the stampede from nothing. It did not. The terror is the harvest's hinge; the people kneel in the dim districts because the fear delivers the herds to their hands. Pull that one thread and the quietest agriculture ever practiced unravels, and a people who finished their world must begin it again, and that is not a gift. That is arson with a kind face.

It could have shown itself. It could have given them the sky — opened the vault, or opened their understanding, poured in the sun and the wind and the staggering blue. It did not, and this refusal it considered longest of all, standing (insofar as it stood) in the old quarter among the aligned doorways. Because here is what it understood, reading the rings: these people did not lose the surface. They spent it. They traded it, deliberately, over generations of descent, for the drip-lines and the basins and the bright even hush, and they finished the trade and set down their tools, and to *return* the surface to them now would be to refund a purchase they do not regret — to tell a people in their own completed house that the house was an exile all along. There is no theft quite like a gift that reclassifies someone's whole life as a misfortune.

So: almost nothing, below. But a wise power, having declined to touch a thing, does not therefore leave it untended. It looked up instead, through the vault, at the surface and the centuries — because the dangers to a finished world are never inside it.

It went and found the blaze. It is a lake — you wondered; it is a lake — broad and shallow and floored with white marl, a half-day's walk across the true surface from the throat you descended, and when the wind dies and the sun stands on it the whole sheet goes incandescent and drives its light down through the thinnest quarter of the vault, and a mile below, a city's worth of doorways faces it and every living thing holds still. The lake has perhaps eleven hundred years left. A river to the north is wandering, as rivers do, and in eleven centuries it will find the lake and fill it with brown silt, and the white hours will dim and then stop, and the doorways will face nothing, forever.

The power has adopted the river.

This is now, by volume, the larger part of its work: eleven hundred years of patient negotiation with a river, conducted in the people's own idiom — a placed stone, a toppled snag, a gravel bar encouraged here and discouraged there, a thread of water diverted two handspans at a time, at the scale of watersheds, at the speed of weather. It finds the work absorbing. It had not gardened before. There is also the other surface business, drabber but necessary: the mountain now assays poor, and always will; the surveys above this country have acquired a small permanent blankness, a tendency to be filed wrong; and in the villages within two valleys of the lake there is a very old saying about the white water — that it is watching, that it is owed quiet — which no one believes and everyone obeys, and which the power maintains the way the people maintain the word for *bird*: in working order, against a need no one remembers.

Below, it has permitted itself exactly two acts.

The first you would never find. In the third district along the western drip-line, a fracture in the vault is healing itself — calcite sealing the seam, as calcite does — and in four hundred years that drip-line would have gone dry, and a fifth of the basins with it, and the salamanders that breed nowhere else. The power placed its pebble. Somewhere in a kilometer of stone, one crystal grew otherwise than it would have, and the seam will weep for another thirty thousand years, and no one below will ever know that anything was ever going to happen, which is the correct amount of knowing.

The second act is not an act so much as a promise, and it made the promise not to the people — who ask for nothing — but to a word.

The vault is thinning. Millimeter by millimeter, the same water that glazes the city is unbuilding the roof, and in some deep epoch — twenty thousand years, thirty — it will open. The power could prevent this. It could seal the sky forever and keep the Bright Understory like a thing in amber, and it declined that too, for the same reason it declined everything: the country is not a specimen, and geology is one of its honest walls. The roof will open when the roof opens. What the power has chosen instead is to furnish the occasion. On the surface above the place where the first breach will someday come, it has begun — already, now, this early, because soil is slow — to grow a marsh. Reeds, in time. Willows, in more time. Mud that remembers water, banks that will become, when the ground at last lets go and a ragged skylight tears open over the floor of the world, cliffs — and in the cliffs, swifts. So that on the day the first light with *direction* in it falls down through the gap, the first hard-edged shadow in thirty thousand years sweeping across the pale plain, the shadow will have wings. There is a word down there that has been taught to every child against a sky no one remembers, kept polished, kept waiting, referring to nothing. The power has arranged that it will not have been a lie. That is the whole promise. It is the only one it has made.

As for you — it followed you up, when you finally climbed back toward the weather. It did not touch your memory; a wise power does not edit witnesses, and besides, the place had already done to you the only thing it needed done, which you will discover the first time you try to describe it and find your voice dropping of its own accord. But the long throat you descended was due, geologically, to close — a rockfall waiting in the dark like everything else here waits — and the power moved that appointment up by a single season. You will not find the way again. No one will. You will carry the only map, and it will be made of telling, which is the one form in which a place like that can be safely held: unverifiable, luminous, a story with its hands open.

And once per blaze — this is its single indulgence — the power goes down through the light, and stands in one of the aligned doorways among the still people and the still herds, and opens whatever it has that answers for hands, and holds them so. It adds its silence to theirs. Whether the hush during those white hours is deeper than the vault alone can account for, no one below has any way to measure, and the power has no wish to be measured.

Though once — one blaze, some centuries into its residence — an elder standing three doorways down turned her enormous gray eyes toward the doorway where the power stood, eyes that read the world by gradient and motion and should have found nothing there at all, and looked at it, or at the empty arch, for the length of forty drips from the seam above. And then, slowly, deliberately, in its direction, she made the sign.

*We are not waiting.*

The power does not know if it was seen. It has decided not to investigate, because some questions are better kept than answered, like basins, like superstitions about white water, like the word for a thing with wings. It made the only reply their language allows to that sign, which is the sign itself: it held still.

It is still holding. Up on the surface the river is being talked out of a lake, handspans a decade; the reeds are coming in over the place where the sky will someday fail; the maps stay blank and the mountain stays poor. Down on the floor of the world the lime comes down grain by grain, the herds wheel in silence, a child is taught a word for nothing, and the city dissolves by millimeters into its final shape with a guest standing quiet in its doorways — a power that arrived carrying every tool there is, and learned from a people who had finished their world the one thing powers almost never learn: that there is a setting-down of tools which is not death, and a doing-nothing which is not absence, and that the loudest thing it would ever say, in the long bright hush of its watching, was an open hand, held still.
