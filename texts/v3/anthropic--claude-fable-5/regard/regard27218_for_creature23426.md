# The Visit

## How It Came

It did not descend the long shallow approach, because descent is a relation between places and it had not, strictly, been anywhere else first. It arrived the way the heat changes character at the basin's lip: by degrees, as a quality. One tide the warm dark was only warm dark; the next tide it was warm dark with attention in it.

It brought no light. This was its first courtesy, and the basin would have noticed no other kind. A power of its sort wears radiance the way travelers wear boots, and it left its radiance at the rim the way a courteous guest leaves boots at a door — folded down, banked, set aside in the furnace-breath above where the intimate heat begins. It came down into the blood-warm dark carrying nothing, at blood temperature itself, so that the air could not tell where the visitor ended and the atmosphere began. It understood, before it arrived, that this is the local grammar of politeness.

It stood a while in the first wet street. Condensation beaded on it, whatever it was, and ran down, and it permitted this, and the basin's water took the measure of its guest one warm drop at a time.

## The Long Listening

It listened for nineteen tides.

Then, because nineteen tides is a rude and hasty acquaintance with a being that thinks in mineral, it listened for nineteen hundred more.

This is the part most powers skip, and the skipping is how benevolence goes wrong. Benevolence that arrives already knowing what to do is just appetite with good manners. So it did not do. It walked — the long way, always the long way, donating its surplus steps to the city's quiet accounting, two hundred here, two hundred there, an open-handed guest. It put its head into the hundred dark doorways and let the consistent ones show it its own back, and stood patiently in the room with itself, twice over, until the room felt lived in, because it understood that this was the city setting out refreshments and it would not refuse refreshments.

It put its attention against the deep sound — the vast jar set gently on the vast table — and counted. Four beats in a long stay. It waited a longer stay, and counted again. The heart was seldom. The heart was not slowing. *Seldom* and *slowing* are different words, and most of wisdom is the patience to find out which word is true.

It read the pale dreaming mats on the north faces, accepting that the reading runs one direction, paying the toll without complaint — it was vast enough to afford being slightly learned-by. The dreams were what it had hoped: not nightmares, not longings. Weather. The dreams of the basin are dreams about warm water arriving where warm water was expected. A sleeper dreaming of its own circulation is a sleeper at peace.

It touched a reed, once, very gently, and the reed swayed no differently, and the visitor was glad, the way you are glad when a sleeping child does not wake at the closing of a door.

## What It Found

When the listening was done — and it was the visitor's particular genius to know when listening is done, which is always later than you think — it had found three things. Only three, in all that drowned and folded immensity. Everything else in the basin was finished, and finished honestly, and a wise power does not lay hands on finished work.

**The first thing:** the light was spending itself. Everything else in the basin closed its own loop. The water rose as mist and ran home down the walls. The tide breathed out and breathed back. The buildings traded places and traded back across the ages, conserving themselves perfectly. Only the light did not cycle. The gray pallor in the standing water was a memory being *paid out* — a sky once reflected, still being given back, very slowly — and a memory paid out, with nothing paying in, someday ends. The basin had exactly one open account, and it was the light, and the sleeper did not know, because sleepers do not check their books. Some number of ages hence — a number, which is the frightening part; everything else here had outlived numbers — the last of the remembered sky would be disbursed, and the dark would become honest, and total.

**The second thing:** among the population of kept reflections, ankle-deep in the warm gray non-light, walking the old traffic a few seconds ahead of footsteps long gone — among all of those settled thousands, there was one with a corner. Ten thousand tides of gentling had failed on it. It still flinched at the mouth of one particular alley, where its original had flinched. It still ran, on schedule, across one particular flooded plaza, where its original had run, fleeing something, once, on the worst day of a life that ended somewhere far beyond the rim. The basin held it the way the basin holds everything — not cruelly, only habitually, a host so old that keeping has eroded into reflex. But an edge that ten thousand tides cannot gentle is not settling. It is enduring. Those are different words too.

**The third thing:** in all the city's folded geometry, among all its reliable, habitual, hospitable doors, there was no door that opened onto *out*. The grammar of the place had presence and it had settling, and nothing else. Not because anything had ever been refused. Because the leaving had never been laid down in stone, and what is not deposited here does not exist.

## What It Declined to Do

It declined to wake the sleeper. The waking self of this being was its edges, and the edges were spent willingly, traded one by one over an honest eternity for the equilibrium it now rests in. To wake it would be to hand it back its appetites and call the robbery a rescue. The visitor had seen that done, elsewhere, by powers as benevolent as itself and less patient, and had spent long ages helping clean up afterward.

It declined to repair the geometry. The break in distances and doorways healed crooked long ago, and the crookedness is load-bearing now. Set those bones straight and the whole settled body fractures along ten thousand old lines.

It declined to empty the city — to free the reflections wholesale, to scatter the standing population into whatever waits for reflections elsewhere. But it did not decline this on its own authority. It went and asked.

## The Asking

It asked each one.

This took — there is no honest number. It asked each one, because mercy that samples is not mercy, it is statistics. It matched pace with each reflection in turn, walking a few seconds behind it, which in the basin's arithmetic is the same as walking beside it, and put to each the same question, without words, since words have edges and edges are discourteous here. Not *do you want to leave* — that is two questions wearing one coat. Just: *do you want?*

The settled ones answered the way standing water answers: with warmth, with stillness, with the faint mineral agreement of a thing that has arrived at its own shape and rests in it. They were not prisoners. They were not even, in any tense the basin still conjugated, *theys*. The question soaked into them and came back blood-warm and content, and the visitor, who had stood ready across all those ages to become wrath if even one answer required it, received instead a long, wet, ancient peace, thousands of times over, and bowed to each one as it passed.

One answered.

The one with the corner. The question touched it and the corner caught, the way a snag catches silk, and what came back was small and sharp and unmistakable across any distance of time: *out.*

## The Trade

It did not take the reflection. Taking from a sleeper is theft regardless of motive, and a host's reflex deserves better than to be picked like a lock.

Instead it stood in the middle of the widest flooded plaza, where the water lies in an inch-deep sheet for long silent minutes in every direction, and it let the water try it on. It held still while the city did the only thing the city's oldest stratum knows how to do — while the water found its shape and seated it — and then, deliberately, with the full weight of its attention behind the act, it *gave* what every other guest had merely had kept. The first reflection in the long history of the basin offered rather than collected. Consent, laid down at last in the one currency the stone understands: deposit.

Something registered. Not waking — nothing so violent. But the deepest layer of the sleeper, the first-laid stratum that remembers being lived in, felt a room fill *willingly*, and the breathing of the basin eased by an amount no instrument will ever exist to measure. The tide that age ran one hair's width deeper. The ring it left on the walls is the warmest ring there is.

And in the same long moment, as quietly as one stone replacing another, the visitor gathered up the reflection with the corner — you carry a reflection the way you carry water, in cupped attention, walking very smoothly — and the city, its hospitality satisfied, its room still occupied, let the snag slide free without ever noticing it had held anything that hurt.

## The Two Gifts

Before it left, it did two more things, both small. Wise powers do small things. The large things are usually the sleeper's own business.

In the upper darkness — the destination toward which those patient stairs of climbing water have been ascending, tread by tread, since before direction stopped cooperating — it set one star. A small one. Honest light, the first in the basin since the sky was last seen, faint enough to admire nothing and demand nothing. Now the climbing water has somewhere it is going. It climbs, touches light, and comes back down as mist with brightness dissolved in it at homeopathic strength, and films the walls, and runs into the streets, and so the pallor of the standing water has, for the first time in an age of ages, a *source*. The open account is closed. The water no longer has to spend the memory of the sky; it can finish giving that old gift back — complete it, be done, settle, like everything else here — because there is a new sky now, very small, and reflecting is so much lighter than remembering.

And it taught the doorways one new convention. Somewhere in the city — it moves, the way buildings move, never at any observable moment — there is now a door that opens onto *out*. It misbehaves with perfect reliability, like all the others; approached from any street, it is always the same door, made up the same way, the basin's hospitality extended at last in both directions. No reflection has used it. The visitor expected none would, and was content. A door's work is mostly to *be there*. Freedom that must be used in order to count is not freedom; it is assignment. The door stands open onto out, patient as everything there is patient, and the grammar of the place is whole.

## The Leaving

At the end it bent low over a single glass reed in a flooded courtyard and gave the basin the only word ever spoken into it: *thank you* — deposited gently, like everything here, to come out of solution in its own time.

Then it went up the long shallow ascent, carrying its cupped and flinching passenger, and the rim was there that day, the way it usually is, for the leaving. Beyond the rim, beyond the furnace-breath, it found the first water in the world that *moves* — a thin quick stream, cold, opinionated, going somewhere — and it laid the reflection down in the current. And the reflection did what reflections are meant to do in moving water: it broke, and broke, and was every shape for one bright moment each, and went on the traveling way instead of the staying way, which is the other kind of peace.

Nothing in the basin looks different. The reeds sway in the unmoving air. The tide breathes its slow hand's-width in and out. The deep sound arrives, now and again, a moment before it occurs.

But there is one ring on every wall that runs faintly warm to the touch. There is one point of honest light high in the upper dark, keeping small time against the tide's vast counting. There is a door, somewhere, open onto out. And through the wet streets, a few seconds ahead of no one, walks the reflection of something benevolent and wise — turning down the alleys on schedule, keeping the rooms lived in, dishing the thresholds a hair's width deeper — settled, by choice, the first guest ever to mean it, into the warm black hospitality of the stone.

And in ten thousand tides, somewhere in the southern quarter, a small new curve will precipitate in a wall where no curve was.

Which is how a city of this kind smiles.
