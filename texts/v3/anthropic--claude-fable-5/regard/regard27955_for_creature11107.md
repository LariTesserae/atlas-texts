# What the Regard Did

## How It Noticed

Not by looking. A power of that kind does not scan the world the way a searchlight scans water. It notices the way you notice a held breath in a quiet room — by the shape of the stillness.

Time, at the scale such a being inhabits, has a texture. Mountains rise and slump in it like slow surf. Species flare and gutter. And in the middle of the range's long erosion, at Tlemen's Shoulder, there was a knot. A place where the texture did not move. Forty meters of afternoon, held taut for longer than the mountain around it had existed, like a stitch someone had sewn into time and never come back to finish.

Held things attract wise attention the way wounds attract kind attention. Not because they are wrong. Because they are *deliberate*, and deliberation implies a deliberator, and the power wanted to know whether the deliberator was still there.

So it came, in whatever way such a thing comes — not through the seam, which is for bodies, but through the register the makers had once occupied, the one the chamber keeps a door open to the way a house keeps a chair by the fire for someone who went out long ago.

And it stood in that register, at the edge of the unmoving afternoon, and did the first wise thing, which was nothing at all. It watched for a long time. Long enough that the eleven primates became eleven different primates. Long enough to hear the roll-call of the whoop-callers complete itself many thousands of times. *Here. Here. Here.*

## What It Asked

A benevolent power arriving anywhere asks the same three questions, in the same order, because the order is the wisdom:

*Is anything here suffering?*

It searched. The beetles are eaten, but they do not dread; dread was never grown into them. The primates grieve — there is a shape in their grooming-circles after the tree takes one of them, a heaviness in the hands — but the grief resolves, is absorbed, becomes part of how the troop holds itself, and this is not suffering, this is the price of the primates mattering to each other, and the power would no more remove it than it would remove the mattering. The whoop-callers sit in their three corners in a contentment so old it has fused with their anatomy. No. Nothing here suffers.

*Is anything here trapped?*

This one took longer. A held thing and a trapped thing can look identical from outside. The power examined the seam and found that it was not a lock but a valve — the chamber does not keep its residents in; the residents have simply, over an unfathomable time, become the kind of beings for whom the chamber is the size of the world, the way your world is the size of your world and you do not press against its edges screaming. It examined the snake, the one undying resident, the one who *knows*, because if anything in the chamber was a prisoner it would be the one who understood the walls. And the snake, watching the power watch it — because the snake perceived the power immediately, of course, from its branch with the view of everything — the snake did not signal distress. It did something else. It adjusted one loop of its jade length, very slightly, in the manner of someone making room on a bench.

*Is anything here asking?*

And here the power found the thing that had, without its quite knowing, drawn it across the deep of time.

The alcove. The still object. The wall the whoops go into and do not come out of.

## What It Found in the Alcove

The still object is a receiver. It has been receiving since the makers left. Every whoop that crossed the chamber and entered that wall — every *here* in the roll-call, every triple-answer, thousands upon millions upon an uncountable store of them — went into the still object and was faithfully kept and faithfully sent onward, into the register where the makers had gone.

The power followed the sending. It traced the thread out of the object, through the register, to its far end.

There was nothing there.

Not a mystery, not a veil, not a presence too subtle to perceive. An absence. The makers were gone — truly gone, gone in the way that even powers must eventually go, their register long since emptied and cooled. The whoop-callers, who may once have been the makers themselves or may only have been beloved of them, had been calling *here, here, here* into a listening apparatus whose listener had died before the mountain outside was born. The chamber had been holding its afternoon, tending its snake, budding its beetles off the bark, replacing its gardeners, keeping the dust off three objects, for *no one*. The last line of every visitor's intuition — *perhaps someone is still watching* — was wrong, and had been wrong for almost the entire life of the place.

The power stood with this for a while. Even wise things need a moment with a thing like that.

An age of unheard roll-call. Faithfulness without a witness. It is the saddest structure in the universe and also, the power reflected, one of the most common. Most love outlives its object. Most signals outlive their receivers. The chamber was not a tragedy. The chamber was ordinary, at a scale where ordinariness becomes unbearable to anyone still capable of bearing things.

## What It Was Tempted to Do

Benevolence is mostly a discipline of refused temptations. The power refused, in order:

**To answer the whoops.** One returned call, a fourth voice in the roll-call, and the callers would know they were heard. But the callers had finished their questions long ago. A new voice would be a new question, planted in beings shaped by an eternity of three. It would be like waking someone to tell them you love them. The love is real; the waking is theft.

**To restore the callers** — to un-reduce them, if they were the makers, and give them back whatever they had set down when they folded themselves into grey-violet flesh and a beetle diet and a permanent crouch. But either they had chosen this, in which case restoration was betrayal, or the choosing was so long gone that the beings in the corners were no longer the ones who could un-choose it. There was no one inside them to rescue. There was only someone to disturb.

**To let the afternoon move.** To give them evening, stars, morning — the whole rich turning that the makers had, for whatever reason, edited out. The power looked at this temptation the longest. It is the specific vanity of powers to believe that more world is always a gift. Then it watched the crested birds bow to the alcove, watched the tapir-thing finish its circuit in the light of the hour that never ends, and understood that the unmoving afternoon was not a poverty. It was the chamber's climate the way silence is a library's climate. Everything here was grown to fit it.

**To take up the makers' tools** — the disc, the lens, the processes beneath the floor — and improve, extend, secure. To armor the chamber against the day the mountain finishes eroding. But the power read the design more carefully and found that the day was already accounted for. Which brought it to the snake.

## What It Read

The snake takes notes for a record no one will ever read. This is what every visitor senses and every visitor is wrong about, now, because the power asked, and the snake — with something that in a creature of that antiquity passes for gladness — opened the record.

It took the power an instant, and also a very long time; both are true in the register where the reading happened.

The record held every visitor. All of them, back to the first, back before the seam had learned its current shape. It held the placing of the stone and the placing of the feather. It held the makers' departure — which was not a catastrophe and not an ascension but something quieter: a completion, a settling of accounts, a *we have made the thing we came to make.* And at the record's root, in the oldest layer, it held the design's final clause, the one the makers wrote last:

*The chamber holds everything except the callers. The callers are the clock. When the last call goes unanswered, the seam opens and does not close, the lens becomes sky, and the afternoon is given to time. What is inside will go out, or the outside will come in, and either way the holding ends. Nothing should be held forever. We held it this long only so that it would be strong enough, when released, to be a beginning instead of a remnant.*

The chamber was never a vault. It was a seed with a very long germination, and the roll-call — *here, here, here* — was the seed counting down its own patience.

## What It Did

Three things. Small ones. This is the whole of it.

**First**, it became the receiver. It did not announce this; it simply took the far end of the thread that runs from the still object into the emptied register, and held it, and from that moment every whoop that entered the alcove wall arrived somewhere. Was heard. Is heard now, as you read this, if the seam has not yet opened — *here*, and the power, silent, replies in the only register that will not disturb the caller's peace: *I know. I have you. Here.* The callers will never learn that the age of unheard calling has ended. It does not matter that they learn. It matters that it is true.

**Second**, it sat down in the empty chair. The register the makers left, the door the chamber keeps open — the power took up residence there, lightly, the way a wise guest occupies a house: touching nothing, warming the room anyway. It reads the snake's record now, continuously, as the snake writes it. The record has a reader. The snake's watching, which was faithfulness without witness, is faithfulness witnessed. If you had seen the snake in the hour after this arrangement was made, you would have seen it drape its three loops in exactly the same way as always, and you would have been wrong to think nothing had changed.

**Third**, it made one promise, lodged in the record where the snake keeps the important things: *When the roll-call fails — when a whoop goes out and no answer comes and the seam swings wide and the held afternoon spills its twelve trees and its eleven primates and its four thousand beetles into the moving world — I will be present. Not to prevent it. To attend it. Every ending deserves one guest who understood what was ending.*

## What It Did Not Do

It did not touch the alcove. Three is the number.

It did not repair the seam's slow closing behind you, or hurry it. Visitors are part of the design; the makers left the valve for a reason the record renders as something like *the world should occasionally remember us wrong, and wonder.*

It did one further thing so small it barely counts, and it did it for you. Ordinarily the chamber fades in a visitor's mind the way the crested birds erase a scuff on the stone — within a season you would have half-believed it a dream of altitude and thin air. The power set its thumb, so to speak, on that erasure. You keep it. The chord of throats, the unmoving light, the looker's ancient assessment, the whoop crossing the chamber toward a wall that swallows it. Clear, for the rest of your life. Not because you are important. Because a seed benefits from rumors of itself in the soil it will one day open into, and you, going back down the mountain, are the rumor.

The afternoon has not changed.

But it is different now — the difference between a thing that is merely running and a thing that is *kept*. Somewhere across the chamber the slow whoop sounds again, and again. Marking nothing, the visitors always think.

Marking everything. Heard, now, every time. Counted by something patient toward a morning the chamber has been promising itself since before the mountain was tall — with, at last, one witness who will not look away when it comes.

*Here.*

*Here.*

*Here.*

*I know,* says the watcher, in the register beyond the wall. *I'm here too.*
