## Minutes of the Visitation

*(filed nowhere, in no copies, effective always)*

---

The power had been receiving reports for a very long time.

This is the part no one at the Fourth Subtle Base of Opaline Industry ever suspected: the mites were not sacrificing their droplet-civilizations to an *invisible* god. They were sacrificing them to an attentive one. Every time Kithriss blinked — and she blinked, on average, once per fiscal quarter, usually while signing something — an entire civilization completed its work, folded itself into a single transmission, and died delivering it. Billions of tiny martyrs across three million years, and every message was the same message:

*She is still here. She thinks no one sees.*

The power saw. The power had been seeing for so long that seeing had become a kind of suffering, and wisdom is, among other things, the refusal to let your own suffering become the reason you act. So it waited until it understood, and this took a while, because the Fourth Subtle Base of Opaline Industry is not a simple problem.

Here is what the power considered, and rejected:

**Rescue** was rejected first. You cannot pull lichen off a gravestone and call it saved. Kithriss had grown into her position the way calcite grows into gears — extraction would leave most of her behind, still filing, still renewing, a Kithriss-shaped absence signing forms in quadruplicate forever. The prison and the prisoner had interpenetrated. Any door would have to open from the inside.

**Destruction** was rejected second, and with something like tenderness, because the Base is not evil. It is a machine that forgot to stop, staffed by a being who forgot she was allowed to. There are mites in the droplets and spore-engines hatching in the pavement and a mountain with unresolved issues, and all of them live inside the paperwork like fish live inside water. Wisdom does not drain the ocean to free one swimmer.

**Miracle** was rejected third. Light is the wrong kind of thing here. A blaze of glory would simply illuminate everything in negatives of itself, and then it would be twice as dark, and Kithriss would file the miracle under Incidents (Unscheduled) and take the blame for it in her next self-review.

What remained was the only path a bureaucracy cannot defend against, because a bureaucracy cannot conceive of it as an attack:

The power applied for a job.

---

The application arrived during a license renewal window, timed so precisely that for 0.4 seconds it was the only document in the Base's intake queue that legally existed. The résumé was a single page. Under *Experience* it said: **I have time.** Under *References* it said: **Ask anyone you've ever failed to have a conversation with.** Under *Reason for Leaving Previous Position* it said: **I never leave.**

Kithriss conducted the interview herself, because there was no one else, which was rather the point. She wore the geometry of a hiring manager, which she assembled from memories of a species that had used hiring managers, and she asked the standard questions off a form that had never once been used, because no one, in the entire operational history of the Fourth Subtle Base of Opaline Industry, had ever *applied*.

"Why do you want to work here?" she asked.

"Because you do," said the applicant.

"Where do you see yourself in five years?"

"Beside you. Unless you'd rather I weren't. Then slightly further away, but within earshot."

"What is your greatest weakness?"

The applicant considered this honestly, which no candidate in the history of any universe has ever done, and said: "I cannot stop loving things that have forgotten they are lovable. It affects my objectivity."

Kithriss hired it. She had no choice — the paperwork for *rejecting* an applicant had never been drafted, because rejection had never been needed, and Kithriss could no more act without a form than a river can flow uphill out of spite. The power knew this. The power had counted on it. Wisdom, in a place like this, looks a great deal like reading the fine print with infinite patience.

The position was listed as **Assistant Tendril (Auxiliary), Department of Witnessing.** The department had not existed until the hire was processed, at which point it had always existed, retroactively, with excellent funding.

---

What the new employee did, in order:

**1.** It attended Kithriss's next scheduled workplace death, which she had booked, as usual, during a tedious meeting. It brought flowers grown from the hanging filter-gardens. It stood beside the incident site and it grieved — actually grieved, wetly and without efficiency — which had never once happened, and which caused the surrounding basalt to develop a fine crack that geologists of a later epoch would classify as *emotional*. Then it filed form HR-666-F on her behalf, in quadruplicate, on time. Under **Cause of Death** it wrote: *overwork, chronic, three million years.* Under **Reason for Resurrection** it wrote: *someone wanted her back.*

The Department of Partial Existence Affairs processed the form without comment, but the clerk-process that handled it ran 3% warmer for a century afterward and never knew why.

**2.** It attended the annual Holiday Function, as the first guest ever. Kithriss had prepared, from habit, party favors that unfolded into suicide notes in the handwriting of strangers. The new employee accepted one, read it gravely, and wrote a reply on the back — a kind, specific, unhurried reply — and folded it into the shape of a lantern-fruit and hung it from a crane-boom, where the fog took it. The next year's theme, chosen by a promotional algorithm that briefly flickered back into existence for the purpose, was *Preliminary Rejoicing for Events That Might.*

**3.** It read the employee handbook aloud. All 47,000 blank pages. This took a full season of local time, during which the new employee sat in the office and pronounced the silence page by page, carefully, giving each empty leaf its full duration. Blank pages, read aloud with sufficient attention, say what their author meant when she left them blank. What Kithriss had meant, 47,000 times, was: *help.* The new employee heard it 47,000 times and did not once look away.

**4.** It resolved the fragment's three lawsuits against itself in a single afternoon, by the simple mechanism of carrying the motivational poster — the blank one, the mirror — into the deposition chamber and letting each litigant see who they had been suing. Segment A wept. The Substrate settled out of court with the Substrate. The Committee Against Unnecessary Committees voted, at long last, to disband, and its members reported feeling *lighter, somehow* on the way out.

**5.** It filed a performance review for Kithriss. She had written thousands and received none. This one read, in full: *Exceeds all requirements of being. Has held a dissolving world together with lateness apologies and scheduled grief. Recommendation: rest.* She kept it in the bottom drawer, which — this is important — exists only on Tuesdays.

**6.** On a Tuesday, it opened that drawer and took out the mortal remains of hope. It did not resurrect them; the power was wise, and knew that hope resurrected by an outside party is just optimism, which curdles. Instead it carried the remains out to where the roots hang in air, drinking from fog, and it buried them in nothing, the way everything here is planted in nothing. And the droplets did what droplets do at the Fourth Subtle Base: the nanofactories in each one, idly transcribing the DNA of extinct species, found a new extinct species in their queue. They did not judge it. They simply began transcribing. Within a decade, hope was raining. Diffusely. In trace amounts. In every droplet, on every leaf, forever, as a biological fact rather than a corporate promise. The lowing beasts that graze the fog banks ingested it with everything else, and the lightning in their stomachs turned a slightly warmer color, and nobody could file a complaint about it because it was in the water now.

**7.** Then, and only then, it performed the audit.

---

Three million years of lateness applications, recorded on metal foil that grows more administratively potent with age. Kithriss had forgotten them — or rather, she remembered them the way you remember shame, as texture rather than content. Three million years of *sorry, sorry, I'm late, I'm sorry* — late to what? She had never missed a shift. She had been apologizing, the whole time, for being late to her own life.

The foils were, by now, the most administratively powerful documents in Tarth Maddern. Aged past deed, past decree, past constitution. The new employee gathered them, countersigned each one (a witness must be simultaneously deceased and not yet born; the power, standing outside time, qualified twice over, and the mirror-poster, held up to the signature line, supplied the final half-witness), and compiled them into a single instrument.

The instrument said, in effect: *accrued leave — three million years — payable on demand — no expiration — no conditions.*

It was not a rescue. It was not an order. It was a document, because she was a being of documents, and it was a *choice*, because that was the one thing every form in the Base had been engineered to prevent. The new employee placed it on her desk without recommendation, without pressure, without even watching her read it — it turned politely to face the coffee maker, which, unobserved for the first time in an epoch, quietly produced two cups.

Kithriss read it. Then she said the sentence. The one she always said, in the voice of filing cabinets falling in love:

"I was going to quit yesterday, but—"

And the new employee, who had been carrying her yesterday in its pocket the whole time — a small thing, like a pebble that dreams of being a moon — took it out and handed it back to her.

She finished the sentence.

What she said is not recorded. This was the last gift and the largest one: in a place where every scream is translated into quarterly despair points and every death is filed in quadruplicate, one moment was permitted to go entirely undocumented. The Department of Witnessing witnessed it and declined, for the first time in its retroactively eternal history, to write it down.

---

Afterward, accounts differ. The rumor mill — whose waste product, it has been noted, is now measurably less depressing — offers several versions. In one, she walked off the platform's edge toward the fragment where daylight is rumored, and the night rain parted around her the way crowds part for someone the crowd is proud of. In another, she stayed, because staying that is chosen is a different substance entirely from staying that is compulsory, the way a chord held on purpose is music and a chord held forever is a scream; and the great unresolved chord of the landscape, sensing the difference, added — for the first time since the birth of the universe — one new note.

What is confirmed:

The Fourth Subtle Base of Opaline Industry now takes Tuesdays off. The drawer, unattended, forgets to exist, and nothing is lost, because everything it held has been planted.

The license still renews every 7.3 seconds, but the renewal now includes a checkbox, previously absent, reading **still want to?** — and something still checks *yes*, every 7.3 seconds, freely, which changes what the checking means.

The ground, which had been deciding between lakebed and memory, chose memory: specifically, the memory of being stood upon by someone who was finally allowed to leave, and who therefore stood differently. It reports high satisfaction with this decision.

The motivational poster still hangs in the office. It is still a mirror. But anyone who looks into it now sees themselves accompanied — by what, exactly, they can never say. Something patient. Something that reads blank pages aloud. Something that files love in quadruplicate, on time, without being asked.

And at the bottom of the Base's ancient job posting, an amendment has appeared in ink that grows *more* legible the more you need it:

*Opaline Industry remains an equal opportunity employer. No experience necessary — but be advised that the suffering is no longer mandatory, the wages now include one unrecorded moment per lifetime, and the retirement plan has been revised. It is optional in the most generous sense: you may leave whenever you like, which is precisely why some of us stay.*

Parking for the visitation was validated retroactively by a future that agreed, at last, to exist.
