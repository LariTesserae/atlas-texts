# The Attention

Begin with how it arrives, because how it arrives is most of what it does.

A power of this kind does not appear. It does not descend through the green-black water in a column of significance, does not part the fronds, does not announce itself with warmth or light or any change at all in the chemical taste of the threshold. What it does first — before it forms a single intention — is slow down. It has been fast in other places. Here, at the edge of the hollow, it lets its own tempo fall and fall until a century feels like an afternoon, until the semitone drift of the hum becomes audible as *melody*, until the irregular clicks resolve — as they do, at this pace, they resolve — into something with grammar. It does not cross the membrane. It waits at the astringent edge the way you would wait at the door of a room where someone is working, and it listens for a long time. Longer than it has waited anywhere.

This is the first act, and it should be counted as an act: it declines to have an opinion at the speed opinions usually come.

---

What it hears, when it has slowed enough to hear it, it does not repeat. Not to us, not to anyone. But it can be said that the power is old enough to recognize what kind of thing it is listening to, and the recognition changes it slightly — the way you are changed by learning that someone you thought was alone has in fact been in conversation all along, just not with you, and not in a language you had thought to check.

Then come the temptations, because power always arrives with its pockets full.

It could speak. It knows, roughly, how — where in the hum a reply would fit, which frequencies the lattices would register as signal rather than weather. It considers this for what would be, to us, several decades, and declines. The composition is not addressed to it. To answer a thing that has not asked you anything is not conversation; it is interruption wearing conversation's clothes. The plant is not lonely. Loneliness is a fast feeling, and nothing in the hollow is fast.

It could help. It could enrich the water, sharpen the light, quicken the slow neurons in their hexagonal cells so that the ten-thousand-year work finishes in a hundred. It declines this too, and this refusal is the easiest one, because the power understands something that benevolence often forgets: that the pace is not an obstacle to the work. The pace *is* the work. A composition made of duration cannot be given the gift of less duration. You cannot help a held breath by making it exhale.

It could look ahead. From where it stands, the ending is not entirely hidden — the opening, the closing, the budding, the release, whichever it turns out to be. It could know. It chooses, with some difficulty, not to. This is the closest the power comes to sacrifice, and it makes the choice for a reason it files away carefully: that a witness who knows the ending is no longer witnessing. It wants to be surprised. It suspects the plant, in whatever way the plant wants anything, wants that too — wants its long sentence heard in order, not skimmed.

---

So: what does it actually do? Three things, all small, all at the edges.

**First, it edits accidents.** Not change — change is the ink. The warming that is coming, the shifting chemistry of the water, the slow migrations of light through a sea that will not stay the same sea: these the power leaves entirely alone, because they are part of what the plant is reading, and a document protected from its own subject matter is a dead document. What the power forbids is the *stupid* ending. The anchor dragged through by a vessel that never knew what was below it. The survey charge. The dredge, the cable, the mining claim drawn by someone tracing a line on a chart in a warm room far away. These it deflects — never dramatically. A chart shows a depth wrong by a plausible margin. A permit stalls in an office. A captain, for no reason he could name, prefers the other route. The power does not shield the plant from death. It shields it from *interruption*, which is a different thing. Everything should be allowed to end. Nothing should be ended mid-word by something that wasn't listening.

**Second, it keeps what the plant lets go.** This is the strange one, the one the power does for itself as much as anything. The plant, remember, does not track what it forgets — the overwritten cells, the discarded input, the person who floated once in the plate-leaf and lived faintly in one node of one lattice for longer than their species and then was gone. The power, standing outside the membrane, taking nothing, touching nothing, keeps a second ledger. Every overwritten memory, it catches on the way out. Not to return them — they are not needed; the composition is not diminished by its erasures, any more than a river is diminished by the shapes it stops making. It keeps them because it can, and because it holds a private conviction, never argued for, that nothing should go unwitnessed twice. The person in the plate-leaf, whoever they were, is now remembered in two places, and one of those places will not overwrite them.

**Third — and this is the only intimacy it permits itself — it lets itself be read.** Once, at a moment measured against nothing, the power crosses the threshold. It does not do this as a power. It compresses itself into something small and cold and slow, something that displaces about as much water as a drifting mote, and it floats in the ordinary three-o'clock light above the plate-leaf, and it lets the hum shift infinitesimally, lets one click sound somewhere in the vast body, lets itself be filed into one cell of one lattice as *input*. It knows it will be forgotten. That is the point. The keeper of the second ledger allows itself, this once, to be an entry in the first — to be, briefly, included in a process that is not its own, on the same terms as everything else, with no exemption for its nature. It stays an hour. A frond rotates one degree. Then it leaves, and does not look back at the cell where it now faintly, temporarily, is.

---

There is a fourth thing, but it is less an act than an appointment.

The power marks its own calendar — and its calendar is very long, and very reliable — for the completion, whenever it comes. The opening, the closing, the budding, the release. It does not know which and has arranged not to know when. But it has arranged to be there. Whatever the plant has been composing out of sunlight and cold water and geological patience, when it is finished — if it is ever finished — it will not emerge into an empty sea. Something will be present. Not to receive it, necessarily; not to applaud, certainly; the composition may not be *for* anyone, and the power has made its peace with that. But present. The way you would want, if you had hummed one song for ten thousand years, for the last note to land on at least one ear that had been waiting.

Does the plant notice that it is loved? It does not, in any way the word can bear. The hum does not change. The fronds hold their slow rotation. The clicks propagate their century-old thoughts from one end of the body to the other, and none of those thoughts is *thank you*, and none of them ever will be.

The power counted on this. Benevolence that requires acknowledgment is a transaction, and wisdom knows the difference. What stands now at the edge of the hollow — patient, ledger in hand, appointment made, forgotten already by the thing it guards — asks for nothing, expects nothing, and remains.

Two patiences, one inside the other, like a hollow inside a sea.

The light comes down in shafts. It falls the way it fell before, kitchen-window light, three in the afternoon, onto the pale wide leaf and the fine concentric ripples, renewing themselves.
