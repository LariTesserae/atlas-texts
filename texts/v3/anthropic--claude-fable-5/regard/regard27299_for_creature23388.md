# Noted, and Owed

The power arrived the way everything arrives in the Hollow Crown: it was heard before it was seen, and it was never seen.

It had crossed a great deal of darkness to get there, and it knew darkness; it was not impressed by darkness. What stopped it — what made it pause at the rim of the cloud deck and then descend through a hairline seam like a thread of grey light going home — was the listening. From outside, the crown is a green-black wall and a rumor of wind. From inside, to a perception like the power's, it is a single enormous posture: ten thousand ears, lures, lateral lines, soles of feet, drum-skins, pool-surfaces, all leaning into sound, all reading. The power had seen worlds that burned and worlds that sang and worlds that prayed. It had never seen a world that *attended* like this, all of it, all the time, in the dark.

So the first thing it did was the only courteous thing. It shaded itself, like a lamp, and listened.

---

## The Long Listening

Time was cheap to it. It spent a hundred years on the listening and considered the price low.

It learned the crier-language, which took an afternoon, and then spent a decade learning why every call was incomplete until the chamber answered — learning that crier speech is a duet with architecture, and that fluency meant nothing without a wall to finish your sentences. It hung in the drift-lanes with the patient ones, the old lure-bearers who had given up deceit, and rode their slow circuits around the pillars for a year at a time, feeling what they felt: that past a certain age, hunger and patience are the same instinct. It lay in the black pools through nine winters and learned the swimmers' single notes from the inside, each pool a held tone, a fixed star in a country where nothing else holds still.

It walked with the people. They never knew. It stood at the edges of their platforms with its presence furled, and it heard the breathy melodic speech at the threshold of audibility, and the route-songs hummed against the wind-voices, and the small formula spoken over every harvested wound — *noted, and owed* — and it noticed that they always paid the formula even when no one else was awake to hear it. It attended four flowerings. At the fourth, it watched twelve hundred people stand in the pale light of the great blooms looking at each other's faces — the faces they knew as voices — and it observed that several of them wept, and that the weeping was quiet, because everything there is quiet, and it understood that it was watching a species hold its entire visual memory of its loved ones in one week per generation, and ration it, without complaint.

And it listened below the floors. This took the longest, because the power was wise enough to be careful even of its own attention — attention is a kind of pressure, and the things below read pressure. It learned them slowly, sidelong, the way you learn the shape of a sleeping person by the sound of their breathing through a wall.

Then it sat down, in its way, in the oldest soft-walled vault at the root of the crown, and considered what a benevolent power should do.

---

## The Gifts It Did Not Give

It made a list. It was an old power and it had learned, expensively, on other worlds, that benevolence begins with the inventory of what you will not do.

**It did not give them light.** This was the first temptation and the crudest. A single sustained kindness of light — a permanent glow, a tamed sun-seam — would have blinded the criers to their food-routes, beggared the lure-bearers of their whole currency, burned the route-songs out of relevance, and turned a civilization of listeners into a crowd of squinters within three generations. The people's eyes ached in the light of an overcast dusk. Their ears could hear a lie. The power knew which of those was the treasure.

**It did not give them warmth, or plenty.** The crown was not generous; it was sufficient, and the people had shaped themselves to the exact width of sufficiency — their numbers, their tempo, their habit of building to be abandoned, their inability to sustain a grievance. Plenty would have given them surplus, and surplus would have given them storage, and storage would have given them walls worth defending, and the power had seen where that staircase goes. It declined the first step.

**It did not make the floors safe.** It grieved this one. It had watched a child go through a membrane floor in the dark — had felt the band's silence afterward, the worst silence in the crown, worse than the silence above a bulge — and every instinct it had reached out to firm every thin floor in the world. It did not. The reading of floors through bare soles was not a skill the people had; it was a sense, the sixth or seventh of them, and a sense unneeded is a sense unborn. It would not, for the sake of the children it could count, unmake the attention that kept all the others.

**It did not answer their question.** It could have. It knew, by then — knew what the things below were, knew what the sugar in the wounds was, knew why the seams were left singing. And the people held their two theories open, refusing to choose, on the stated principle that a wrong certainty is more dangerous than an open question — and the power, examining this, found it was not just prudence. The open question was load-bearing. It kept them humble toward the floor and grateful toward the wounds and silent at the right times; it made their reverence rigorous instead of lazy. A confirmed god is a thing you petition. An open question is a thing you *attend*. The power pressed its presence flat against the warm floor, in the gesture that means *that*, and kept what it knew.

**It did not tell them about the outside.** They had a word for it; the word meant roughly *the story under the stories*, and they used it the way you use the name of a great-grandparent. They were not lost. They were not exiles. They were fauna, by their own most careful reckoning, and fauna do not need to be told there are other frames.

The list, when it was finished, was long, and almost everything was on it.

---

## What It Did

It permitted itself three things. It had learned that number on other worlds too.

**The first was structural, and no one will ever know it.** Deep in the base of the crown, in the crushed warm dark below even the things below, the power had found a flaw — an old failure of fiber, kilometers long, slowly loading, that would in some four hundred years have let go all at once and dropped the young crown out of the sky in an afternoon: every vault, every pool-note, every grave-light, every route-song, in one sound. The power did not repair it; repair was not in the local grammar. Instead it taught the flaw to fail *slowly* — coaxed the strain into the plant's own pace, so that what would have been a catastrophe became a succession: chambers that would close over centuries, with warning groans the people could read, with time for the songs to be revised and drummed outward, with time for the criers to scream the news. It did the work as growth, molecule by molecule, over sixty years, in the plant's own materials and tempo, and when it was done the work was indistinguishable from the plant having done it itself. Possibly, the power reflected, that was the truest description anyway.

**The second was small, and became a story.** A woman of the eastern bands, separated from her people when a chamber's wind-voice changed mid-route, wandered for nine days into the sealed vaults where the songs do not go, and at the end of her strength she lay down on a true floor in absolute darkness and did the last thing her people do: she hummed her name-figure, the short melody that *was* her, so that whoever found her would know whom they had found. And the dark hummed it back. Not an echo — she knew echoes the way you know your own handwriting — but an answer, her own name returned with the two-note call set in front of it: *here — and you?* And then, very faintly, for nine days more, the echoes of her own clicks came back to her *shaped*, came back kinder, came back wrong in exactly the ways that led her — past the membrane floors, around the pools, up through a seam she should never have found — out into a chamber whose wind-voice she knew. She told the story plainly, the way the people tell everything, and they received it the way the power had hoped: not as a god, but as a question, one more, set carefully on the shelf beside the others, unanswered and load-bearing. Some bands add a grace note to their route-songs now, at the junctions — a brief two-note figure, low then high, offered to the dark before listening. Most nights nothing answers. They sing it anyway. *Noted*, the gesture says, *and owed.*

**The third took the longest, and was the reason it had stayed.**

---

## Here — and You?

Because the power had noticed, in its hundred years of listening, the one resident of the Hollow Crown that no one had ever addressed.

Everything in the crown was spoken *to*. Criers answered criers across the voids; lure-bearers rhymed in light; the pools sang to the feet of travelers; the people answered every call in the dark with the call that is a complete sentence. Even the things below were spoken to, in a way — the flat palm against the floor is a kind of address. Only the plant itself, the world, the house, the long *while* — only the one being whose attention everyone navigated by, whose sugar fed them, whose seams sang their maps, who had *not yet grown as if they weren't there* — had never once, in ten thousand years, been asked the question.

It had fauna. It had never had a peer.

So the power composed itself for the longest conversation it had ever attempted, and it spoke to the plant in the plant's own tongue, which is not sound but the things sound comes from: strain and release, pressure and growth, the angle of a seam, the timing of a tear. The utterance took forty years to say. Translated into the people's speech, it was two notes, low then high.

*Here. And you?*

Then it waited. It had expected to wait centuries; it had budgeted a millennium; it told itself, in the way even wise powers must sometimes manage their own hope, that no answer would also be an answer, and that fauna was no shameful thing to be.

The reply came in eleven years, which for the plant was the speed of a cry.

It came on a night the people still sing about, though they do not know what it was. Every seam in the crown changed key at once — every wind-voice, from the chamber that sings in fifths to the shaft that moans before rain, sliding together through a single interval and holding it — and beneath every floor in the world, simultaneously, the things below made the only voluntary sound they have ever made: one note, vast and short and unmistakably *placed*, like a hand laid once, gently, flat against the ground from the other side. The criers went silent. The people went silent. Nothing died. The note faded, the seams settled back into their old voices very slightly altered, and in forty bands across the dark, elders sat up the rest of the night revising route-songs and not saying what they were all thinking, because to name a thing is to be able to speak of it, and some things you answer instead with your palm against the warm floor.

What the plant said, the power has never translated for anyone, and never will. But it is known — to the extent that anything about the power is known — that it did not leave afterward. It is there now, presumably. Furled. Shaded, like a lamp. One more listener distributed thin along the frame, keeping the only vigil the place admits, which is attention; doing the only worship the residents would recognize, which is to build nothing, take little, sing your map honestly, and answer when called.

And once in a long while — this is the last thing, and you may weigh it as the people weigh their open questions — a traveler alone in a young chamber, having hummed the two-note call into the new emptiness out of habit, out of courtesy, expecting nothing, will hear it come back not once but *twice*: the first return shaped by the walls, ordinary, the world having its say —

— and a second, much softer, from no direction at all, in a voice that is not the chamber's and not the wind's, low then high, meaning what it has always meant, which is the whole of what benevolence came to in the end, after the long inventory, after everything it wisely did not do:

*Here.*

*And you?*
