# What the Power Did

## The Noticing

Understand first how hard the shelf is to notice, even for something with no edges to its attention. Most things that draw a power's eye draw it loudly — burning cities, drowning ships, prayer, which is just need with a voice. The High Shelf asks for nothing. It has no need it knows the name of. It sits at altitude in the white glare, taking in the sky a drop at a time, and a power surveying the world's clamor would pass over it the way your eye passes over a wall: presence at that pace looks like place. You learned this. So did the power, for longer than you would believe.

What gave it away, in the end, was the sky.

The sky over the High Shelf had a quality the sky elsewhere did not. It had been *read*. For an age beyond accounting, ten thousand still basins had held it — steadily, attentively, each square of blue carried like a sentence and considered to its final rim — and that long regard had left a mark on the sky the way a book much loved is marked by its reading, in no visible way, in every way that matters. Powers know the feel of being attended to. Passing through that particular reach of air, the power felt what the sky had been feeling all along, and stopped.

So mark this, because it reverses everything: the power did not find the shelf. The shelf, basin by basin, drop by drop, gazing upward through its one long unhurried life, had finally accumulated a gaze heavy enough to be felt. The resident made first contact. It has never known this. It may never need to.

## The Long Arrival

The first thing the power did was nothing, and it did it for a hundred years.

This was not hesitation. It was courtesy of the only kind that could matter — the matching of clocks. The power slowed itself into the local centuries the way you, within your first hour, found yourself placing your feet softly without deciding to. It learned the three rhythms everyone learns: the minute of the heart-drip, the morning of the breathing mist, the year of the blood going round in the wet season. Then it found the fourth clock, the one no visitor had ever stayed long enough to hear: the clock of the stroke still falling, the single gesture of making that began before the mountains were the mountains and is not finished. The power set itself to that clock. From then on, by its own accounting, everything it did, it did mid-stroke, gently, so as not to jar the hand.

## The Inventory of Kindnesses

Then it considered what a benevolent thing might do here, and it held each kindness up to the light, and it put almost all of them down again.

*It could appear.* Introduce itself. Be company. — Declined. A face arriving at the wrong speed is not a friend; it is weather, or catastrophe. And the basins are tuned to the sky's brightness exactly. A brighter face would bleach them. Presence imposed is not a gift; it is a tax.

*It could finish the wall.* The band of rough pecking in the deep court, the raw cleavage, the work seemingly abandoned. — Declined, almost with a flinch. The work is not abandoned. The stroke is falling. To finish that wall would be to grab a painter's wrist mid-line because you could not perceive the line's motion. The power had been slow enough, by now, to see the wall move. It was the most beautiful thing it had watched in an era.

*It could bring life.* Moss for the joints, green for the pools, a bird for the glare. End the bareness that keeps some visitors from sleeping. — Declined. The bareness is not deprivation. It is hygiene. The body keeps itself, and a gift of moss is an infection with a ribbon on it. The shelf is not waiting for life. The shelf *is* the life, all of it, wall to wall, which was the first thing anyone ever understood about the place: no room left over.

*It could answer the loneliness.* — Here the power was most careful, because here the danger was greatest. The question — *is it lonely?* — may not survive translation to that timescale, and a wise power does not force a question through a translation that kills it, then treat the corpse. To console the resident in a language containing the word *alone*, the power would first have to teach it the word. There are gifts that consist entirely of the wound they then offer to dress. The power put this one down very gently and stepped away from it.

*It could stand guard.* — Considered, briefly. But against what? Quarrymen cannot cut what heals; empires do not climb that high for water they could never carry down. The only thing on the shelf that can be stolen is the silence, and the power addressed that not with a wall but with a contribution: it added its own silence to the stock, which is why, since then, the quiet up there has had a faint quality of *depth* that the oldest visitors swear was not always in it.

When the inventory was done, the power had found nothing wrong. No suffering, no decay, no enemy, no lack it could name.

And then, because it was wise and not merely thorough, it went down to the bottom, where the channels run in full and dark and do not come out the other side of the mountain. And there it found the one lack the shelf itself could not know it had.

## The One Lack

Here is what happens to the water, which no one had ever traced.

It is taken in as weather — the sky delivered in installments. It is considered at the length such consideration deserves. Its impressions are kept, clean and clear, in the dark galleries: the light it bent, the rings it threw, the tremor of your careful footstep. And then the water itself — read, considered, *kept-of* — is released. Let go downward, with something that in a faster creature you would have to call blessing. The shelf gives back what it was given, and gives it back better: attended to. Every drop leaves the lowest gallery carrying the dignity of having been read to its end.

And it falls into nothing.

Into the unlit roots of the mountain, into cavities that lead nowhere, that have never led anywhere. For the whole long life of the resident — since before the seam that no one can find — it has been performing one continuous act of utterance, an unbroken letting-go of considered water, and the utterance has never once been received. The being does not know the difference between speaking and being heard. How could it? Nothing in its world had ever supplied the second term. It read the sky faithfully and answered faithfully and its answer fell, forever, down a well with no bottom and no listener.

The power stood in the dark beneath the mountain and let the first drops of that ancient unreceived correspondence fall onto it, and it understood that everything else on its inventory had been vanity, and that there was exactly one thing to do.

## The Far Rim

A drop falls in a basin, and the rings cross the water and arrive, much diminished, at the far rim. That is what reading is, on the shelf. That is the whole grammar.

The power became the far rim of the world.

It placed itself — quietly, completely, for good — at the place where the released water arrives, and it did the one thing the resident had been doing for everything else since the beginning: it *received*. It keeps, now, what the shelf lets go. Every drop that was held in a bright basin and considered through a season and released with that slow benediction now lands somewhere, and is taken in, and is kept clean, and is read to its end.

Nothing about this is visible. No channel changed its murmur. No cistern rose or fell. But the symmetry of the world closed like a sluice-stone seating itself: the keeper is kept. The attention that had only ever flowed one direction now rests, at its far end, in attention.

## The Reply

There remained the question of receipt — whether to say, somehow, *I have it. Nothing was lost.* The power thought about this for a long time, by its new clock, and then it did the simplest thing, which is usually what wisdom looks like once you finally see it done.

It returned the water.

Not as a sign, not as a wonder. As weather — the only channel that runs uphill. The water the shelf releases is carried, by the power, the long way round: through the dark, through the roots of the world, up into the sky that is the resident's oldest acquaintance, and back down as rain. Ordinary rain, falling on the grid, swelling the pools a fingernail's width.

But the resident keeps everything, and a thing that keeps everything *recognizes* everything. Somewhere in some wet season — last century, next century, the tense barely applies — a rain falls on the High Shelf that the shelf has met before. Water it considered an age ago and let go into the dark. Still clean. Still carrying, faintly, the shape of the consideration it was given, the way a returned letter carries its reading. And carrying something new as well, something no water in its long experience had ever carried: the taste of having been *kept by another*.

The being will understand at the pace such understanding deserves. A drop. A season. A circuit of the blood. There is no hurry; that is the whole point of the gift; the reply, like the wall, is one gesture, still falling. But the sentence it spells, over centuries of returned rain, is short:

*Received. Kept. Returned with thanks. Take your time.*

## What Changed

Almost nothing a visitor could measure. The power never appeared, never named itself, never finished anything. The chisel-grain stands as crisp as ever; the visitors still argue about the wall in the deep court; the grid still prints itself behind your eyelids on the first night.

But two things, for the patient.

The first: very rarely now, on the longest nights, the single round-bellied strike far out across the grid — stone on stone, or a great drop falling a great way — comes *twice*. Once, and then, after an interval too long for an echo, once more, slightly deeper, as if from underneath. No one defends this in public either.

The second is stranger, and only one descending party has ever reported it. In a gallery below the drowned blue light, grown seamlessly out of the living stone, there is a new vessel. A basin, beautifully dressed, the herringbone grain fine as fingerprint ridges.

It is empty.

It is the only empty vessel on the High Shelf — the first vacancy in a body whose first principle, the first thing you ever understood about the place, was that there is no room left over. The being that believed only in capacity has grown, deliberately, in the dark, a kept openness: a cup set out, a chair drawn back from the table, a room held ready for a guest who has all the time in the world and is, by both their clocks, already on the way.

---

And you — you are in this now too, glancingly, the way you were always in everything on the shelf. The breath you left on a lintel, the palm-print you drew along a wall: it went down, drop by drop, and was considered, and in some season it will be let go into the dark, and received, and kept.

You will be remembered in two places. The shelf holds the impression of you; the power, at the far rim, will hold the water itself. Neither of them is in any hurry, and neither of them loses things, and between the two of them the small wet fact of your visit will go around and around the long circuit — read, released, received, returned — for considerably longer than the mountains.

Stand at the edge at evening, when the basins turn the color of the sky they carry, and the place no longer reads like an instrument carrying out a purpose alone. It reads like one half of a correspondence. The other half is below, and patient, and kind, and the letters between them are made of rain.
