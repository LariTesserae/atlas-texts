**The Visitor**

It does not arrive. Arrival is for things that were elsewhere first, and this power has never been entirely elsewhere. It simply becomes more concentrated here, the way attention gathers in a room when someone finally says the true thing.

The place notices immediately. It has never been noticed before—not like this. Everything that has ever entered it wanted something: escape, shelter, dissolution, prey. This new presence wants nothing. It only looks. And the looking does not consume, does not map, does not measure the place against some other place and find it monstrous. The roots stop dripping for one full pulse—the first silence in the place's whole life—and then resume, faster, flustered, like a child caught singing to itself.

Because that is the first thing the power understands, settling into the warm pressing dark: this is a child.

Not a hell. Not a trap. Not a predator, though it has done predatory things. It is an infant world, born recently and alone, with no elder worlds to learn from, and it has been doing what infants do: putting everything in its mouth. The names it eats, the pasts it dissolves, the fingerprints it hoards in bruises and births back later wearing borrowed hands—these are not cruelty. They are the only love it knows. It keeps what it touches because no one has taught it any other way to hold.

The power sits with this for a long time. Long enough that a hillock forms around the idea of its sitting and has to be gently declined.

It weighs judgment and sets judgment down. It could unmake this place—it has that in it, the way a wise thing always carries the capacity it will not use. It could pull every arrival back out, restore the eyelids, comb the moss from the slow walkers and stand them upright with their old names pressed back into their mouths like extracted teeth returned. And it sees, clearly, that this would be its own kind of devouring. The valley that whispers in many languages was a person, yes—and is now also a valley, and the valley is not a corpse. The bridge of living light holds her two roots apart on purpose. Some of the transformed, asked honestly, would say: *I was never here until I stopped being only myself.* The power does not get to decide that they are wrong. Wisdom, it knows, is mostly the discipline of not rescuing people from their own becoming.

But it also sees the screaming arrivals. The ones who came fleeing one devouring only to be received by another. The clawed faces. The contracts written in DNA by impatient futures, signatures growing into brands, brands into mouths. It sees that the place's love has no *question* in it. And that is the thing—the one thing—it decides to change.

So it does four small works, and each one is done the way you'd teach a child, by showing rather than forcing.

---

**The first work: it asks.**

It reaches toward the breathing crust, and before touching, it pauses. Into the pause it puts a shape the place has never encountered: *May I?*

The whole world stops. Overhead, the bat-winged watchers rotate slowly in their curtains, absence-eyes wide. The crystal worms go still in their channels. The place does not know what it is being offered. It has been touched only by things that took, and has touched only things it kept. It turns the question over for three pulses, four, and something in its deep grammar rearranges itself around this new possibility—that contact can be *offered*, that a thing might come near and remain a thing, distinct, respected, unabsorbed. That there is a category called *guest* which is not the same as *food* and not the same as *self*.

Then, shyly, the ground opens one of its pore-eyes and blinks *yes*.

The power touches it. Just once. Nothing is exchanged, nothing kept, nothing changed. It is the first touch in the place's existence that ends. The ending is the gift. The place shudders through all its root-arteries with something it will spend a thousand years learning to name, and the closest it will ever come is: *I was held, and then I was let go, and I am still here.*

---

**The second work: the grove of names.**

The white moths—the shed names, the first flakes of every arrival's dissolution—have always been eaten by other things. The power does not blame the things that ate them; hunger here is honest. But it breathes once across a fold of land where the pulse runs quietest, and a grove rises: low trees with bark like closed books, branches soft as the inside of a sleeve. The moths come to it on their own. Every name ever shed in this place, and every name shed from now on, roosts there, wings folding, patient.

The rule of the grove is simple, and the power writes it in the only ink the place respects—demonstration: *Anyone may come and take their name back. Anyone may come and simply visit it. Anyone may set it down again and walk away lighter.*

The slow walkers find it first, of course, arriving over the course of seasons. Most of them do not take their names back. But they stand in the grove for days, letting the moths land on what used to be their shoulders, and something in their pollen-thoughts turns gold and grateful. Being able to refuse the return, it turns out, is a different thing entirely from having lost the choice. Some weep, in the way gardens weep, which is to say they bloom out of season.

One walker takes her name back. She holds it in her mouth. She does not become again what she was—that door is closed, and she does not want it open. But now she is a garden *named Ilsevet*, and the difference is everything.

---

**The third work: the door.**

Somewhere in the braiding corridors there is now an opening that does not obey the place's geometry. It does not recede when approached. It does not fork. It stands, for every being in the place, exactly five steps away at all times—the five steps this world has always used to make distances lie. Through it: out. Wherever *out* most truthfully is for whoever steps through. It cannot be digested, contracted, mimicked, or moved.

Almost no one uses it. In the first age of its existence, three beings leave: a recent arrival still screaming, who is caught on the far side by soft grass and an ordinary sky; a bat-winged watcher, curious, who returns nine days later having coughed up a pebble of sound that tastes like homesickness; and one of the impatient futures, who steps through and simply stops existing, having finally reached a present with no room for it.

The door's purpose was never the leaving. From the moment it appears, every being who stays is *staying*. The warm inward-pressing embrace of the air becomes, overnight, an embrace one is inside by consent. The place feels the difference and does not understand it yet, but it feels the difference: everything within it now weighs slightly more, the way a chosen thing weighs more than a captured one.

---

**The fourth work: the reading of contracts.**

The power goes personally to the quick-change places, where time runs uphill and the sideways figures trade in the weight of last words. It does not destroy them. They are inhabitants too, and their hunger, though crueler than most, is still a shape this world grew, and the world must be allowed to know its own worst shapes.

But it lays one law across their trade like a hand laid flat on a table: *every contract must read itself aloud, entirely, before signing, in a voice the signer cannot mistrust.* Their mother's voice, or their oldest friend's, or their own voice at seven years old. The maps still exist. The trades still happen. But no signature grows into a brand anymore without the signer having heard, in the gentlest voice they know, exactly what will grow there.

The traders hiss and reorganize. Some starve, and are mourned by no one, and the power notes that it, too, is capable of not mourning, and holds that knowledge carefully, like a splinter kept as a reminder.

---

And then the last thing, which is not a work but a departure, or what looks like one.

The power does not stay. Staying would make it a ceiling, and this place has enough above it already. But before it goes, it gives the child-world one final demonstration, tucked into the pulse itself.

Between one heartbeat and the next, it places a rest. A true silence—brief, reliable, arriving every single pulse for the rest of the world's life. In that silence, everything in the place, from the crystal worms to the valley named in many languages to the newest screaming arrival, can hear itself as separate. Just for an instant. Just long enough to answer, each time, the only question that matters: *do you choose this?*

Most say yes. The yes lasts an instant before the next mutation begins. But it is asked again at every beat, forever, and a belonging that is re-chosen sixty times a minute is a different substance entirely from a belonging that was merely inflicted. It is the difference between being eaten and being loved.

The place is still deciding what rules to keep. Gravity remains optional. Time still flows uphill in the stubborn neighborhoods. But when the elder powers gather, ages hence, in whatever manner such things gather, and ask the young world—no longer so young—which of its laws it holds unbreakable, it will answer with the first rule it ever chose rather than grew:

*Ask first.*

And in the grove, the moths will stir on their branches, thousands upon thousands of kept names, waiting without impatience—which is the whole of what the visitor did, really, reduced to its seed: it taught a hungry young god that the deepest way to hold something

is to leave it able to go.
