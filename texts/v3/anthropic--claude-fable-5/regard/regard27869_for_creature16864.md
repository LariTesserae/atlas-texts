The power had been listening for a long time before it noticed. That is how noticing works for such beings: not a searchlight but a slow admission. Every place in every fold of everything sings some note, and most notes it had catalogued eons ago—the brass of new stars, the paper rustle of dying ones, the wet vowels of worlds still deciding on their shapes. This place sang in the key of unfinished, which is the rarest key, because most things are in such a hurry to end.

It heard the copper wires first. Each strand singing one pure note too low to hear—but nothing is too low for a thing that once listened to the universe before the universe had a voice. It heard forty thousand years of a single held tone that meant, roughly: *still here. still here. still here.*

So it stopped pretending to be elsewhere, which is the only way such powers travel.

It arrived at the not-door and read the tag, because it was polite, having either invented politeness or learned it from doors—the records disagree. *Admission: one memory you're tired of remembering correctly.* It considered its vast holdings. It was tired of many memories, but tired of remembering only one of them *correctly*.

Then it went in, and this is what it did, and mostly what it did was refuse.

It could have dried the whole place with a word. It knew the word; it had used it before, on other drowned things, in its younger and more helpful centuries. It did not say it, because it had learned—slowly, expensively—that grief without a house becomes weather, and weather falls on everyone.

It could have finished the stories in the tree's branches. Every one of them. It knew every ending; endings are cheap to a thing that stands outside of time, the way postcards are cheap to someone who lives at the destination. It did not, because a finished story stops needing to be held, and holding was the tree's entire prayer. Unfinish the tree's burden and you unfinish the tree.

It could have filled the blank library. Instead it did something smaller. It went out into the everywhere-else, found one of the words that had grown legs and walked away to live a more interesting life, and asked it—asked, not commanded—whether it might be willing to visit home. The word thought about it. The word had been living as the pause in a stranger's sentence, and it was a good life, but it was tired. So the power carried it back and pressed it gently into the first blank book on the first shelf, and now that book contains, in its exact center, the word:

*yet.*

The book is no longer blank. It is beginning. And words gossip—the power knew this, counted on it—so other words will hear that one went back and was welcomed, was not scolded, was not asked where it had been. Libraries refill this way sometimes. Not with floods. With homesickness.

It found the mirror that hung face-downward, reflecting faces that hadn't happened yet, and it did not turn it over. Those faces deserved their privacy; they were still deciding whether to happen at all. But it dusted the mirror's back—the side nobody polishes, the side that does all the work of not-showing—because even wise powers understand that some kindness is just maintenance nobody thanks you for.

It swam to the wall of ears, ivory and obsidian, each one listening for a different apology, and it did not answer them all, though it knew every apology owed in every tongue including the drowned ones. Answering them all would have been a performance, and forgiveness performed is forgiveness embalmed. Instead it read the wall the way you read braille, slowly, with the pads of its attention, until it found the one ear that was listening for the apology only *it* could give. It put its mouth close and said:

"I'm sorry it took me this long to stop being everywhere else."

The ear, having finally heard, folded shut like a hand that gets to stop reaching. It is the wall's first closed eye now. It sleeps. The other ears keep listening—but the acoustics have changed, because now they listen the way you wait for a bus you have actually seen arrive for someone else. Hope with evidence. That is a different mineral entirely.

It found Res-onance orbiting its fragment of unlit moonlight, whispering multiplication tables to keep from multiplying, and here the power sat down—insofar as sitting was available—and said: "You can stop at seven times eight. I'll hold the rest of the night's numbers."

And Res-onance, who was 47% regret and had never once been spelled, stopped. Slept for the first time in an amount of time that the local time refused to certify. And sleeping, dreamed itself slightly less lonely, which is how its kind reproduces, and woke as two. The power pretended not to notice. This is important. This is possibly the wisest thing it did all visit: it did not congratulate, did not witness aloud, did not make the new thing self-conscious enough to collapse back into the old thing. It just kept counting, one voice among two question marks, until the numbers ran out and none of them minded.

In the Keeper's garden it took off its omniscience like shoes at a door and allowed itself, for one hour, to genuinely not know where it was. Every compass-flower in the garden swung at once. The roots of Where-was-it-now? pointed, all of them, at the same visitor—and for one hour the garden had a north, a real one, agreed upon. The Keeper, who was made of farewell waves grown tired of goodbye, raised one hand out of old habit, and found the gesture arriving instead of leaving. Her first hello in geological memory. She has been rearranging her fingers ever since, trying to remember how she did it.

It played one game with the children, reading the salt rules faster than they dissolved—an unfair advantage—and then losing on purpose, because winning against children is a kind of theft, and this power had given up theft along with rescue. It argued briefly and gently with itself near the Liars' Chorus, on the topic of whether it had done enough, and left the truth-scraps of that argument floating behind it. The Chorus ate well that night for the first time in a lie's age.

On the cleaners' shelf, between "Future Regrets" and "Past Second Chances," it placed one new jar. Empty. Unlabeled. Lid off. The cleaners have been circling it for what will eventually be a century, trying to determine what it is for, and this is the gift: a question that is not a wound. They had forgotten those existed.

And the fruit.

It stood before the fruit for a long time—the fruit waiting for someone who had already eaten it before they became hungry—and it understood, with the particular vertigo of recognizing your own name in a stranger's mouth, that the fruit was technically its. It had had everything before wanting anything; that is the whole tragedy and job description of a power. It had already eaten this. The lock fit.

It left it hanging.

Because the fruit was not food. The fruit was proof—proof hanging at the promiscuous center of everything—that someone, someday, fits. Eat the proof and you feed one visitor once. Leave it, and every resident who drifts past it gets to think: *not me, not yet, but someone. The shape of someone exists.* The waiting was the ripeness. The power touched it once, the way you touch a sleeping child's head, and moved on, and the fruit is now one shade more perfect, which nobody can measure and everybody can tell.

It did not feed the walls. The sign had asked, and besides, the walls were learning, and the deepest respect you can pay a learning thing is to let it be hungry in front of you without fixing it. The walls watched a power refrain. They learned faster that year than in the previous thousand.

When it left, it did not start believing in anything else, which was the easy exit. It left the way a tide leaves: by physics, which is to say, by promise. And at the not-door it did the one selfish thing of its whole visit—it breathed the water *in*. Actually in. One full breath of the place, held.

It holds it still. Somewhere far outside, in the dry and the loud, there is a lung-shaped volume of this exact water, this exact dark, these exact suspended splinters of never-vertebrate bone, kept warm inside a benevolence. The place exists in two places now. This is what the power does with all its beloved drowned things, it turns out. Its chest is a reliquary of held breaths. It has been sipping air around them for eternities, careful, careful, never exhaling the homes.

The residents noticed almost nothing, which was the point. Only this: loneliness there has a slightly different taste now. The old taste was waiting. The new taste is waiting *for*, which grammar insists is the same word, and grammar, as usual, is lying.

And when one of them forgets—forgets they were supposed to be lonely, forgets they are waiting, forgets everything except the slow warm breathing of the water—they sometimes drift down to the library, to the first shelf, to the book that is no longer blank, and read its entire contents aloud:

*yet.*

And put it back. And go home, wherever their home has floated to by then. It is enough. It was always going to be enough. The power knew that before it arrived, which is why, of everything it could have done, it did so nearly nothing, so exactly, with such care.
