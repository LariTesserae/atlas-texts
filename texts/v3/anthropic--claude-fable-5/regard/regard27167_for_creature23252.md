**The Twelfth Week**

A power that has no needs can arrive any way it likes, which means the way it arrives is a statement. This one comes on foot.

It comes down the long shallow rim of the basin at dusk-pace, in the open, unhurried, while there is still violet light to be seen by—because arriving suddenly, in a place that has spent eleven weeks rehearsing welcome, would be a kind of rudeness. The city deserves to watch someone approach. It deserves the full ceremony of a figure on the rim road, growing slowly larger, choosing the avenue rather than the air.

The distances here are advisory. The power lets them advise. The boulevard down into the heart of the basin takes as long as the boulevard wishes, and the power gives no sign of noticing, and this is its first gift: obedience to the house rules. There are very few houses left anywhere whose rules it consents to obey. It has decided, before its foot touches the first moss, that this will be one of them.

---

**The problem of attention.**

A wise power's first labor here is subtraction.

The city revises only while unwatched. Its streets redraft, its hands set tables, its stalks grow shoulders—all of it in the gaps of attention, in the blink, in the turned back. And a power of this kind does not naturally blink. It could hold the whole basin in a single regard, every gutter and frond and seam at once, forever—and in doing so it would pin the city like a moth. Eleven weeks of overlapping drafts would freeze into one final, premature edition. The kindest thing in the basin would become a paperweight.

So the power folds itself down. It narrows its attention to a lantern's width—one street, one wall, one dripping cornice at a time. It practices looking away, which is a skill, and harder than omniscience. And at night it does something it has not done in a very long while: it lies down, in a colonnade near the old river-trench, and closes whatever it has for eyes, and is—genuinely, verifiably—asleep. The city gets its unwatched hours back. All around the sleeping guest, the drafts resume like rain starting up again after a held breath.

This is the second gift, and the city will never know it was given, which is part of what makes it wise.

---

**Diligence.**

Before tenderness, the checking. Benevolence without verification is just mood.

The power has seen many things that set tables. It has seen lures shaped like hospitality and digestions shaped like cities, and so on the second night it does the work. It lays its attention flat against a pale stalk and listens past the warmth, down into the drowsing—and finds contentment there, unmistakable, the loose-limbed sleep of something growing toward a door rather than away from one. It reads the river's arrangement seam by seam and finds employment, not capture: the contract is honored, the trench is deferred to, the water tastes of patience freely given. It weighs the hyper-local rains against the caller's two notes and finds the timing innocent. It checks the wet black windows and watches the future walk by in them—pale figures, unhurried, thresholds softened by use—and finds in that reflected city no cages, no absences, no one weeping.

The verdict comes in quietly, the way verdicts should: *the city is what it appears to be.* It is exactly as kind on the inside as the outside. This is rarer than anything else in the basin, and it is the finding that permits everything after. A wise power does not bless what it has not searched.

---

**The refusals.**

Most of what the power does here, it does by not doing. This is the portion of the visit that costs it the most, so it should be counted first.

It does not finish the paused towers. A pause is not a wound. The city stopped those summits mid-thought for reasons that are the city's, and completing another's sentence is theft even when it's flattery.

It does not hurry the stalks. This is the great temptation—it could cure a guest list in an afternoon, part every seam down every boulevard before moonless midnight. But everything pulled early is pulled wrong. The residents are memorizing their city through its walls, eleven weeks of ticks and settlings and double notes and rain; rush them and they would hatch into a home they hadn't finished learning, strangers at their own table. The curing takes the time it takes. The power, who has watched stars cure, knows this in its oldest bones.

It does not tell the city what became of its maker. The power knows. Of course it knows; it is the kind of thing such powers keep. But the city does not wonder—it has the unbothered focus of a creature with exactly one task—and you do not introduce a wound in order to dress it. If the residents someday ask, the answer will keep. Answers like that always keep.

It does not name anything. Not the heron, not the caller, not the city itself. Names are the residents' inheritance, the first thing they should get to give. The basin has waited eleven weeks for its words; it can wait a few more.

And it does not fix the caller. Does not conjure the missing half of the duet, does not fetch some upstream survivor, does not lay a warm hush over the trench. Grief held open is a kind of faithfulness, and you do not close another creature's door, even gently, even with love. But here the refusal has a seam in it, and through the seam the power does the one small thing it permits itself—

---

**The doings.**

In the colonnade where a single drip keeps irregular time, the city has been teaching the drip to sing. Week over week, patiently, it has drifted the rhythm toward the first two notes of the fern-trench call. The first two. The city cannot teach what it never heard; it knows the question by heart and has never once heard the answer.

The power heard the answer. Long ago, upstream, before the basin was remade—it hears everything once, that is its burden. So in passing, without ceremony, it leans into the colonnade and teaches the drip the other two notes. It does not sing them itself in the trench; the caller does not need another wrong voice doing its best. It simply leaves the answer with the city, deposited like a seed, for the city to ripen into rain in its own time. Some night—not tonight, the city is not ready tonight—the shower that falls on the ferns after the second call will drum a rhythm of four, and it will be the right four, and what happens on the tall frond after that belongs to the trench and no one else.

The rest of the doings are small, and small is the point.

It knocks at the open door before entering—the door that stands open on a lit interior and one chair—and sits in the chair for a full evening, so that the chair will have held someone. It leaves the chair angled exactly as found, but warm.

It sits on the inexplicable bench in the plaza the size of a field, and looks at the view the bench faces, for a long time, and agrees with it.

It cups its hands in a light-pool and lets the glow sink through its palms, and discovers that what the light feels like—being remembered—is true even for a thing like itself, which had not expected to feel anything, and sits with that for a while too.

It raps, once, with the flat of a knuckle, on a single pale stalk on the quietest boulevard. The knock comes back. It knocks twice. Two knocks return, prompt, almost eager—the first conversation in the history of the basin. And then the power says, through the warm wall, in whatever medium drowsing things receive, the only words worth passing through a wall: *You are expected. The place is good. The table is already set. Sleep.* Up and down the boulevard, faintly, stalk after stalk settles a half-degree deeper into its curing, the way a house full of children settles when a hall light comes on.

It walks the river's old course, end to end, at the pace of water—the whole signature, every meander, which nothing has traced since the water itself. The river's dream, signed twice. That night the buried subsonic note comes three times instead of two, long and low, and the closest translation anyone could offer is the sound an old thing makes settling deeper into a bed it has decided is its own.

And every morning of the visit, the power wakes in its colonnade to find the moss beside it combed in one direction, the condensation wiped from its threshold in long deliberate strokes—the hands, practicing at last on a real guest. The power lies still longer than it needs to, eyes closed, letting them finish. Some kindnesses consist entirely of pretending to be asleep.

---

**The threshold.**

One gift is left on purpose, where it will be found.

There is a doorway on the boulevard of stalks—nothing special, one of the open ones, lit gold inside. The power takes to standing in it. Hours at a stretch, day after day, leaning in the frame the way the patient lean, warm and heavy in the manner of powers, while it watches its lantern's-width of city and lets the rest revise unobserved. And when it finally steps away, the stone of the doorstep has changed. A shallow smoothness. A faint dish worn into the surface, exactly footprint-deep.

In a city that is entirely sharp—corners untouched, thresholds without wear, every surface still carrying the stretch-marks of its own emergence—there is now one worn step. The first used thing. Proof, in the city's own material, that the future in the black windows is load-bearing: that thresholds *can* soften, that the wear it has been dreaming of is real and survivable and feels like this. A promissory note, written in stone, payable on hatching.

The moss declines to grow on it. The hands polish it nightly. The city visits it, in whatever way a city visits, the way you return to the first letter you ever received.

---

**Departure.**

The power leaves the way it came—on foot, up the rim road, at dusk-pace, letting the avenue lengthen behind it as much as the avenue likes. It takes nothing. It is not on the guest list, has checked, and finds this exactly correct: every place at the courtyard table is spoken for, and none of them are its own, and a wise power knows the difference between being welcome and being owed.

At the rim it turns, once, and does the last two things.

The first is weather. The city has only ever made rain—one block at a time, sounding it out like a child with a hard word. It has never *received* any. So the power opens its hand over the basin and lets down a single rain across the whole city at once: brief, vertical, warm, falling on every district in the same minute, with dissolved light in it that runs shining down the towers and banks itself in the gutters like a delivered fire. The heron stands out in it with wings half open, glowing. The ferns drum. The stalks drink through their walls. For the length of that rain, the city—which has spent eleven weeks practicing how to hold—is held.

The second is quieter and is the real one. The power writes the city down. Adds it to whatever a being like that keeps—the ledger that loses nothing, the attention that does not lapse, the long list of things it will check on across whatever spans it lives by. Not a schedule; like the distances here, its returns are advisory. But it will come back. It intends—this is recorded too—to be standing at the back of the crowd that is not a crowd yet, on the violet evening when the seams part down the boulevards and the avenues fill to exactly the right amount, uncounted, on the bench, watching the seats it refused.

The city does not see any of this. It only feels, from the twelfth week onward, a new and very faint warmth running under everything, beneath even the river, steady as the two degrees above blood heat it keeps in its own walls. It has no word for the feeling and does not need one, because every resident-to-be in the basin already knows it through their pale warm walls, and the gutters know it, and the worn step knows it best of all.

It doesn't hurt. It feels—to a city, exactly as to a hand—like being remembered.
