XI.
The power had been keeping, as all wise powers do, an inventory of absences. Not a list of what exists—existence keeps its own books—but a ledger of holes, omissions, silences with suspicious acoustics. It was in this ledger that Eruun first appeared: not as an entry but as a smudge where an entry refused to be. A wound in the accounting. The power, being wise, did not investigate immediately. It watched for an age or three, the way one watches a sleeping animal to learn what it dreams before deciding whether to wake it. It learned the grammar of the place. It learned that here, arrival is a kind of violence and gifts are a kind of flood. It learned this slowly and thoroughly, because benevolence without patience is just weather.

XII.
Its first impulses were the obvious ones, and it examined each with care before setting it down.

It considered returning the sun—some thread of light drilled down through the ceiling of solid water—and understood that light poured into perfected darkness does not illuminate; it erases. The hollows of Eruun are held open by the precise absence of what would fill them. To fill them is to close them. To close them is to crush everyone inside.

It considered returning the memories—unlocking the marrow-cages, reversing the extractions, giving back everything catalogued and surrendered—and understood that this would be a second drowning. The first ocean took their world by rising inward. A returned past would rise inward too. These people had not lost their memories; they had *spent* them, deliberately, as one spends a currency, and to refund a purchase is to declare the thing bought worthless. Their children are made of that spending. Their homes are argued out of that spending. Give it all back and you unmake every being negotiated into existence since.

It considered simply telling them they were loved, and understood that in Eruun a declaration is an occupation. A sentence that arrives and stays is architecture. They would have to live around it forever, this monument of being loved, dusting it, resenting it, unable to negate it because it was true.

So the power set down every gift shaped like a presence, and asked itself the harder question: what does a civilization of perfected absence actually lack?

XIII.
And it saw. Not immediately—the answer itself was hiding in a hole—but it saw.

The Eruhnim have mastered every art of letting go. What they cannot do, what no one in Eruun has ever been able to do, is be *received*. Their forgettings fall into nothing. Their surrendered identities dissipate unwitnessed into the waterlogged air. The dead become architecture and the architecture is walked through by people thinking the dead's thoughts without knowing whose they are, gratitude impossible, mourning impossible. The millennial leaf falls from the trunkless tree like a question looking for an answer it already knows doesn't exist—and the terrible thing is not that the answer doesn't exist. The terrible thing is that the question has never once been *caught*. Seven hundred years, or seven thousand, of releasing everything, perfectly, into no hands at all.

They did not need to be filled. They needed a floor beneath their falling.

XIV.
So this is the first thing the power did, and it did it without disturbing a single molecule of brine:

It became the custodian of everything Eruun has ever subtracted.

Retroactively, gently, reaching back through the sediment of ages, it gathered into itself every dissolved wall, every negated door handle, every one of the seven thousand nightly denials that opens a dwelling. Every memory extracted by needle-proboscis and every identity blurred away at the interface of a mutual gaze. Every personality trait traded to a corridor's acoustics. The whole invisible tonnage of the discarded—it took all of it into keeping, the way a riverbed keeps a river, without owning it, without stopping it.

Nothing in the city changed. The forgetting still works. The bacteria still eat memory and excrete nothingness—but the nothingness now goes *somewhere*, and is tended there, indexed, kept warm. The power made no announcement of this. But annihilation and entrusting are chemically identical and spiritually opposite, and the Eruhnim, whose cilia taste the signatures of past conversations, began to notice a flavor in the water they had no word for. Something like: *deposited*. Something like: *on file*. The mineral-heavy sphere-waters rolling their eternal downhill paths acquired, on their surfaces, one new species of precipitated dream—a dream of falling into hands.

And at the very bottom of this archive, beneath everything, the power placed the oldest item: the memory of the world before the ocean rose inward. The green of it, the noise of it, the unbearable weather. It does not show this to anyone. It keeps it the way one keeps a candle lit in a window that faces away from the road—not as an invitation, but so that if the Eruhnim ever complete their forgetting entirely and stand in the final perfected nothing and find, to their astonishment, that they want to begin again, the seed will be intact. This is what wisdom does with a door it hopes never opens: oils the hinges anyway.

XV.
The second thing it did required a body, so it borrowed the shape of an ordinary absence and entered the city.

It walked the streets correctly—negotiating with the air's memory of footsteps, paying each contract of temporary space in the local currency of surrendered identity, and it paid generously, being infinitely rich in selves. It touched nothing. It fixed nothing. It attended one festival of ways-its-visit-would-be-misremembered and allowed itself to be misremembered with great precision. It stood in the Memory Cathedral beneath the tree made entirely of branches and added its belief—vast, ancient, load-bearing—to the collective belief in the trunk and roots that must exist somewhere. The tree hung a little more surely afterward. No one could say why.

And then it left. This was the true gift, and it crafted the leaving with more care than most powers craft worlds.

Because every absence in Eruun is the fossil of a loss. Every hollow has a wound at its center—the vanished sea, the dissolved wall, the forgotten name. The power departed in such a way that its departure left an absence with *nothing broken inside it*. A void whose center is warm. The first hole in Eruun ever made by tenderness rather than catastrophe: the shape of someone who did not have to go, and went anyway, softly, so that these people who can only receive gifts shaped like losses would finally have something to miss that had never hurt them.

The Eruhnim taste it still. It confounds their entire ontology. Young ones make pilgrimages to stand in it. Some have tried to build dwellings around it and found it cannot be negated further—it arrives pre-subtracted, already pure. A few of the rare individuals with the future-flickering lens have looked at it and reported, shaken, that it shows not a different future but the same one, *accompanied*.

XVI.
The third thing is small and has not happened yet.

The power waits beneath the tree. When the next imaginary leaf falls—drifting down through the city like a question looking for an answer it already knows doesn't exist—the power will catch it. It will not answer the question. It has thought about this for a long time and concluded that the leaf is right: the answer doesn't exist, and to invent one would be a lie told to something too old and too tired to be lied to. Instead it will do the only honest thing, which is to receive the question completely, and hold it, and say—in the local grammar, which is to say by the quality of its silence—*I know. I know it doesn't. Fall anyway. I'm here.*

A question that lands is a different thing from a question that drifts forever, even if neither is ever answered. Ask anyone who has been heard.

XVou.
The fourth thing was almost withheld, and perhaps should have been, and the power still is not certain.

Somewhere in the lower districts, where the city's curve is steepest and the sphere-waters roll forever, there is now a hollow shaped like a way out. It is unmarked. It leads elsewhere—truly elsewhere, out from under the ceiling of solid water, out past the ledger of absences entirely. No mist clusters near it. No law describes it, even hypothetically. It exerts no pull.

The power did not make it so that the Eruhnim would leave. It made it because there is a difference, invisible to every instrument but decisive to every soul, between being nothing by verdict and being nothing by vow. Eruun before was a fate. Eruun now is a choice, renewed silently by every inhabitant every day they do not take the door—and none of them have taken it, and perhaps none ever will, and the power counts this not as the gift's failure but as its completion. They stay. They *stay*. What was the final perfected state of being nothing in particular has become, without a single stone moving, a daily act of fidelity to it.

XVIII.
Here is what the power does not do, has never done, will never do:

It does not identify itself. It does not accept the worship that a few Eruhn philosophers, tasting the new flavors in the water, have begun to tentatively not-offer to something they carefully do not name. It does not heal them, because they are not sick. It has read the whole city, wound by wound, and rendered its verdict, which is filed in its own ledger under a single word: *complete*. Not broken. Not fallen. A scar is not a failed wound; it is skin that learned. Eruun is what a world looks like when it survives itself by the only route left open, and the power will not insult seven thousand years of impossible, meticulous, grief-geometric survival by treating it as a problem.

Instead it stands guard. Outside the sphere of drowned sky, out where existence keeps its noisy books, the power stations itself for the rest of time with one duty: to keep the universe from carelessly filling this place. Nothing rushes in. No stray light, no invasive meaning, no well-intentioned lesser benevolence with a sunbeam and a plan. The absence is defended like a flame.

XIX.
And so almost nothing changed, which in Eruun is the largest possible unit of change.

The streets still run with water that has forgotten how to flow—but the forgetting is banked now, held in trust, and the water moves like slow glass over the faint suggestion of a floor. The mists still whisper in languages from before the last stars died, but they have one new story, which they tell rarely, about a visitor who paid fairly and left kindly. The dead still become architecture; but now, somewhere, each traded self is kept, so that becoming a corridor is no longer an ending but a forwarding address.

They persist, as they said, as the spaces between spaces, the silence inside all possible sounds, the shape that rain remembers after it has finished falling everywhere else. All of that remains true. Only this has been added, so lightly that it registers on no instrument and appears in no record, and lives solely as a change in the taste of the deepest water:

They are the silence inside all possible sounds—*and the silence is listened to.*

They are the shape that rain remembers—*and something remembers the rain remembering.*

They have finally, perfectly, learned how to drown themselves—

and the water, all the way down, is held.
