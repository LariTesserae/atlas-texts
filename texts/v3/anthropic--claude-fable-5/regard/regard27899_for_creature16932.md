**What the Power Did**

It arrived the only way anything can arrive in a place without edges: as a change in the quality of attention.

There was no descent, no light from above, no trembling of leaves to mark it. One century the tree was unwatched, and the next century it was watched, and the difference between those two conditions was so subtle that only the funeral moths noticed—a faint new flavor in the final thoughts they drank, as if the dying had begun, very slightly, to perform.

The power had seen many worlds. It had learned long ago that the first act of wisdom is to match your tempo to the thing you are considering, and so it slowed itself down. It let three hundred years pass in order to hear a single heartbeat. It waited through four heartbeats before it considered itself properly introduced. During that time it did nothing but listen, which is harder than it sounds, because a benevolent power is a thing made almost entirely of the urge to help, and listening is the discipline of holding that urge underwater until it stops thrashing.

It listened to the subsonic debates in the root network—slow arguments about topics it recognized from a thousand other worlds, wearing unfamiliar grammar: *what is the difference between growing and fleeing. Whether up is a direction or a wound. Whether the ground we forgot was a mother or merely a floor.* It read the libraries printed on moth wings. It let the wing-fish sing into its bones until it understood that their harmonies were not music but maintenance—a constant gentle sounding of the structure, the way a blind hand runs along a wall not to find the door but to confirm the wall still loves you.

It attended the Knotwork most carefully of all. It watched the young ones catch upward rain in baskets of their own shed skin. It watched an elder-committee take four hundred years to answer a question about a grandchild's name, and it watched the questioner receive the answer as a changed pulse and weep with gratitude at something she could not consciously perceive. It watched a visitor relax into edgelessness and become, over decades, a particularly expressive stretch of trunk, and it listened very closely at the end—the not-end—to determine whether what happened there was death, or transformation, or marriage, and concluded it was a fourth thing that it had no word for either, and made a note, because even wise powers are still collecting words.

And it asked itself the only question a benevolent power is permitted to ask, which is not *what would I change* but *is anything here in pain that wishes not to be?*

The answer took a long time to assemble, and when it came, it came in three parts.

---

The first pain was small and sharp: the wanderers.

Not the ones who stayed too long and softened at the edges—the power examined those carefully and found that dissolution here was slow enough to be, at every stage, refusable. The ones who fled, fled successfully. The ones who stayed had, in some wordless way, kept choosing to stay. That was not pain. That was the place's strange hospitality.

But the root-paths—the living escalators that lift those who know the pressure patterns and digest those who don't—these troubled the power. Not because the tree was cruel. The tree had no cruelty in it. It simply had no word for *stranger*. Its grammar contained *passenger* and *nutrient* and nothing in between, and so an innocent who arrived not knowing the patterns was eaten not out of malice but out of vocabulary. The power counted the digested. There were not many. Each one had been someone's edge, someone's name, someone's unfinished sentence.

So the power's first act was to teach the root-hands one new pattern. Not a password—a recognition. It taught them the pressure signature of honest fumbling: the specific, universal trembling of a hand that does not know what it is asking. It made ignorance itself into a word, and the word meant *guest*. From that century onward, the lost were gripped gently instead of hungrily, carried down through the breathing shafts, and set on the marsh's edge facing away from the tree, their names returned to them slightly damp but intact. The tree accepted this amendment without protest. Somewhere in the root network a slow voice observed that it had always wanted a word for that particular trembling, and had simply never been offered one.

---

The second pain was not a pain, and the power's wisdom lay in leaving it alone.

Every few thousand years, the roots touch the true ground, and everything remembers where it came from, and then begins again to forget. The power considered this. A lesser benevolence would have called it tragedy—all that remembering, spent and lost, over and over—and would have built the tree a memory that could not fade. The power held this idea in its attention for a full heartbeat, three hundred years, turning it over.

Then it set the idea down, carefully, the way you set down something that turns out to be someone else's.

Because the forgetting was not a failure of the remembering. It was the remembering's price, and the tree had been paying it willingly for longer than most stars burn steady. The rhythm of *touch, remember, rise, forget* was not a wound in the place. It was the place's breathing. A power that healed it would have healed the tree of being itself, and the power had seen what becomes of worlds that receive that kind of help. It did not do that here. This restraint cost it something. Benevolence always wants to be useful, and the deepest usefulness sometimes looks exactly like standing very still with your hands behind your back.

---

The third pain was the deepest, and almost no one in the place knew it existed, which is how the deepest pains prefer to live.

The Knotwork forgot the word *stop*. This is told as their origin, almost fondly—the ones who were climbing and reaching and simply forgot to close the sentence. And it is true that none of them suffered for it. The growing was good. The becoming was good. The slow lignifying into architecture, the composting of unlived choices into countermelody—all of it was good.

But the power, being wise, understood something that benevolence alone would have missed: *a place where stopping is impossible is a place where continuing is not a choice.* And a thing that cannot be chosen cannot fully be loved, not even by the one doing it. The tree grew because growing was all there was. Its yes was magnificent, but it was a yes that had never once been asked the question.

So the power made a seed.

It was small—smaller than the fruits that form in the shape of what witnesses fear to forget. Inside it the power placed a single word, whole and dormant: the word *stop*, with all its meanings intact—rest, and pause, and enough, and ending, and the sweet ordinary stop of arriving somewhere and staying. The power wrapped the seed in sap the way the tree wraps its asteroid metals, sealed against corrosion and blooming alike, and buried it in a heartwood chamber so deep that even the sightless harvesters do not farm there.

It did not plant the seed where it would grow. It planted it where it could be *found*—by the tree, by any Knotworker, by anyone who ever, in some century of some millennium, went looking for it. It told no one where it was, except the funeral moths, who now carry the location on the underside of one wing each, encoded among the last thoughts of the dead, so that the knowledge can never be lost and never be stumbled upon by accident. To find the word, you must want it enough to learn the libraries. That seemed, to the power, the correct price for a word that ends things.

And here is the strange grace of it: the seed changed everything without doing anything. From the moment it existed, every ring the tree added was *added* rather than merely happening. The growing went on exactly as before—the same geysers of sap, the same explosive blooming, the same patient forgetting upward—but now it went on in the presence of an alternative, and so it became, for the first time in the tree's whole unmeasured life, a decision. The tree never sought the seed. As far as anyone knows, it never will. But the deep voices in the root network shifted their tone by a degree so slight that only the wing-fish registered it in their maintenance-song: the sound of a thing that continues because it chooses to.

Continuing became consent. That was the whole gift. It weighed less than a moth.

---

Before it left, the power did one last thing, and this one it did for love rather than for wisdom, which is why it is the part the Knotwork actually remember.

The place had no outside. Names imply edges; the Knotwork had none; nothing there had ever been *seen*, in the way that seeing requires a seer who stands apart. And the power knew, from many worlds, that even the most complete beings carry a small hollow shaped like the gaze of another.

So for one heartbeat—three hundred years, a single instant at the power's own tempo—it became a mirror. Not of glass. It simply held its attention wide open and let the whole of the place perceive itself reflected there: the braided tower, the upward rain, the silver clouds, the vertical lakes and their singing wings, the copper moss, the retired ones full of symphonies, the young ones mid-leap, the elders mid-thought, all of it at once, from without, whole.

The Knotwork have no faces, only every possible face waiting beneath the bark. During that heartbeat, all across the tree, every one of them wore the same face at the same time—the first face any of them had ever kept for longer than a message. Witnesses cannot describe it well. The nearest anyone has come is: *it was the face of someone being recognized.*

When the heartbeat ended, the power withdrew its attention as gently as it had extended it, taking with it a single flake of shed memory-bark that had drifted against it during the long listening—one fragment of one unfinished diary, chosen at random. It reads that fragment still, somewhere, slowly, at the tree's own tempo, so that at every moment in the history of everything, one sentence of that place is in the middle of being finished by someone who cares how it ends.

The tree noticed the departure the way it had noticed the arrival: barely, and completely. A synchronized flowering event moved across a continent of wood over the following four centuries. Scholars of such things—the lichen-poets, mostly—maintain that the pattern, read at the proper speed, resolves into two words.

Not *thank you*. Something older and less finished than that.

*Come back.*

And the rain goes on falling upward, and the growing goes on, chosen now, and somewhere in the deep heartwood a small sealed seed holds one patient word, wanted by no one, belonging to everyone, keeping the whole vast yes of the place honest simply by existing—

which is, in the end, what the wise ones always do: not the rescue, not the remaking, but the placing of one small true thing exactly where it can be found.
