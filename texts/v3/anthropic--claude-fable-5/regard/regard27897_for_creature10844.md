# What the Power Does

It arrives the way it always arrives: as attention. No footsteps through the strangler-fig gap, no disturbance in the frog-chorus, no shadow across the green-gold light. The hollow does not know it is being seen, which is the only way the hollow can bear to be seen.

The power looks for a long time. Time is not expensive for it. It watches three generations of frogs hatch and sing and die. It watches the snake breathe. It watches the woman in the root-alcove listen to the deep machinery, and it listens with her, and it understands what she has become: an organ the hollow grew for itself, the way a body grows an antibody. Not a guardian. A function.

It sees the completeness. It does not mistake the completeness for safety.

Because the power is wise, it sees the three wounds that the hollow cannot see in itself.

---

**The first wound is under the ground.**

The steam comes from a system built by people who are already dying off, maintained by budgets that are already shrinking, feeding refineries whose ore-seams are already thinning. The power can read the future of infrastructure the way the Listener reads the fissure's breath, and the future is this: in some number of decades — forty, sixty, it hardly matters — the pumps three valleys over will cycle down for the last time. Not repaired, not sabotaged. Simply no longer worth running. The steam will thin, then cool, then stop, and the hollow will have perhaps two weeks of stored warmth in its root-mass before the frogs begin to die.

The hollow believes it is eternal. Everything that has stopped changing believes it is eternal. This is the particular fragility of paradise: it has forgotten how to become anything else.

**The second wound is under the roots.**

A man is buried there — is *distributed* there, processed by millipedes into the dark matter between the roots. He had a name. He had a locker at the maintenance depot with a photograph taped inside the door. Somewhere three valleys over, a woman stopped sleeping properly eleven years ago, and a boy grew up telling people his father *left*, because that was easier than telling them his father *vanished*, and the difference between those words has shaped everything about who the boy became.

The power does not flinch from this. Benevolence that flinches is only squeamishness. The man was innocent. The killing was real. The woman in the alcove did it without hesitation and without cruelty, the way a wasp stings, and she has never once dreamed of it, because she has arranged herself so that she cannot.

**The third wound is in her.**

Not the murder — that is the second wound's shadow in her, unfelt. The third wound is that she has made herself necessary to something, and she is mortal, and she knows it, and her entire theology consists of the hope that she will be eaten gently. She has never once asked what the hollow will do without its listener. She cannot afford the question. Her peace is built on not asking it.

---

The power considers what it could do, because even wise powers must walk through the garden of their own temptations before they act.

It could seal the fissure's future — reach into the deep system and quietly make venting point 7-K self-sustaining, reroute a geothermal seam, guarantee the warmth forever. Preserve the hollow in amber. It sets this aside. A thing guaranteed forever is no longer alive; it is taxidermy that breathes. The hollow's beauty is that it is a *negotiation* — between plants and steam, frogs and snake, woman and machinery — and a negotiation with one party made omnipotent is just a monument.

It could judge her. Lift her out of the alcove and make her face what she did in the full light of consequence. It sets this aside too, though more slowly. Justice that arrives as force teaches only fear, and she has already organized her entire being around a single fear. Adding another would not open her. It would only give her something else to listen for.

It could do nothing. This is the temptation the power sits with longest, because it is almost right. The hollow asks nothing. The deepest respect might be to answer in kind. But the power has seen the three wounds, and it knows that *nothing* is also a choice, and that in sixty years *nothing* means a closet-sized chamber full of pale small bodies gone silent in the cooling dark, and a very old woman — or her bones — beside a fissure that no longer breathes.

So it does not do nothing. It does *almost* nothing, which is different, and much harder.

---

**For the man**, first. Because the dead have waited longest.

The power moves through paperwork the way steam moves through rock. A pension case, jammed for eleven years in the machinery of "missing, presumed abandoned employment," resolves. A form is found — was it always there? it was always there — reclassifying him: *lost in the line of duty, root-mass sector, remains unrecoverable*. The word *unrecoverable* does what the word *left* never could. The woman three valleys over reads the letter twice, and something in her chest that has been held at an unbearable angle for eleven years is finally allowed to break, which is the only way it was ever going to heal. The boy, grown now, visits a memorial wall where his father's name has been added. He puts his palm flat against the engraved letters. He does not forgive anything, because there is nothing named for him to forgive. But he stops telling the lie. That is enough. Grief that can end is the power's gift to them; grief that cannot end is a wound, but grief that can end is just love completing its arc.

And in the same quiet movement through the files: venting point 7-K is reclassified. *Sealed. Natural blockage confirmed. Removed from survey rotation.* No one will ever crawl through the strangler-fig gap with a clipboard again. This is mercy braided three ways — mercy to whoever would have come, mercy to the hollow, and mercy to her, because the power has seen what she is, and it will not leave her where she must become that thing a second time. A wasp that stings once is nature. A wasp that learns to sting is something else, and the power declines to let her learn.

---

**For the hollow**, next. This work takes forty years, and the power does it at the speed of roots.

It does not save the steam. The steam will stop. Instead, it teaches the hollow — so slowly that not even the Listener's listening can catch it — how to survive the stopping.

A taproot from one of the fused figs, questing downward as taproots do, encounters a warmth it would not have found alone: a geothermal seep, thin and patient, a natural vein in the rock forty meters below the industrial pipe. The root wraps it. Over decades, the plant-mass learns this second warmth the way a body learns a second language in childhood — without noticing it is learning. The heat it conducts upward is faint, a few degrees' worth, indistinguishable from the steam's ambient gift.

And the frogs: each generation, the eggs that tolerate a slightly wider temperature range are the eggs the power's attention rests on, gently, the way sunlight rests on one leaf rather than another. No miracle. Just probability, leaned upon. In forty years the steam-frogs of the hollow can survive a range their great-great-hundredth-grandparents could not. They do not know this. They sing the same chorus. But the hollow, which believed itself incapable of becoming anything else, has been quietly taught *becoming* again, in a dose small enough that its completeness never notices the lesson.

When the pumps three valleys over finally cycle down — and they do, on an autumn afternoon, decommissioned by a committee that never heard of the hollow — the fissure's breath thins over a month instead of a day, because the power arranged the shutdown sequence too, a valve closed in stages rather than at once. The frogs cluster and sing in distress. Some die. Not all. The root-floor stays warm — cooler than before, a gentler warmth, a natural one — and the ecosystem contracts, grieves in its wordless way, and holds. The hollow becomes smaller, quieter, and *real* in a way it never was before: no longer sipping from a wound, but rooted in the actual heat of the actual earth. Symbiosis with infrastructure gives way to something older. The industrial accident finishes, at last, being an accident.

---

**For her**, last. Because she is hardest, and the power loves hardest things.

It gives her one dream.

She has never dreamed of him. The power does not force the dream on her — it only removes, one night, the arrangement inside her that prevents it. What she has refused to know is already in her; it needs no delivery. It only needs permission.

In the dream she is in the hollow, and the man in the maintenance uniform is sitting across the fissure from her, and he is not bleeding, and he is not accusing. He is just there, the way the snake is there. And because she does not know how to do anything else — because it is the only capacity she has left, the only one she ever grew — she *listens* to him.

He talks the way the machinery talks: rhythms, pressures, things building and releasing. His daughter's cough. The photograph in his locker. The way he'd requested the 7-K survey himself because he liked the far sectors, the quiet ones, the ones where you could hear yourself think. He was, she understands, listening for something too. They were the same animal. She killed her own kind on the fissure's edge and fed him to the millipedes.

She wakes with her face wet, and for the first time she cannot tell whether it is steam or something the hollow has never condensed before. She does not leave. She does not confess — there is no one to confess to, and the power did not want confession. What it wanted happens over the following years: she begins to tend the place in the root-floor where the dark matter that was a man lies distributed among the roots. She keeps the condensation pool above it clear. She learns which frogs breed there. She does not call this penance, because she has no word for penance. She calls it nothing. She just includes him in her listening, which is the only sacrament she has, and the power — watching — judges it sufficient. Guilt, in her, has done what the millipedes do: taken something dead and made it into the substrate other things grow from. It is not erased. It is composted. That is the only forgiveness that grows anything.

---

**And her death**, when it comes, decades on, after the steam has stopped and the hollow has contracted around its new deep warmth:

The power keeps its distance. It has learned, watching her, that its distance is the truest tenderness it can offer this place. But it permits itself one act — small, unwitnessed, entirely sentimental, and the power forgives itself for it.

She dies in the alcove, folded, having felt it coming for days in her own rhythms the way she once felt it in the machinery's. And the frogs, who have grown used to her over sixty years the way they grew used to the fissure, come to her first, as she hoped. The soft parts first. Her eyes, which spent a lifetime watching over them, go gently into the small pale bodies.

The millipedes take their time.

There is no second listener. The power considered arranging one — some other runaway, some other child on a dare — and set it aside. She was not a role to be filled. She was a thing that happened once, the way the hollow was a thing that happened once. Instead the power does its last, subtlest work: it leaves the hollow's contract with the earth so stable, so free of the deep machinery's stutters, that no listening is required anymore. The hollow, at last, keeps its own vigil. She was needed only while the ground beneath it could betray it. The power made the ground honest. Then it let her go.

---

From the outside, across all those decades, nothing happened. A pension case resolved. A survey point was reclassified. A valve was closed in stages. A woman dreamed once, and cried once, and tended a patch of root-floor. Frogs got very slightly hardier. A taproot found a warm seam, as taproots sometimes do.

That is the whole of the intervention. That is what wisdom looks like when it is also love: not a hand descending, but the world's own tendencies, leaned upon so lightly that the world believes it did everything itself.

The power withdraws its attention the way it arrived — leaving no footsteps, no shadow, no ripple in the frog-song. If it feels anything as it goes, it is something like what she felt in her alcove all those years:

the satisfaction of being necessary to something that never knew to ask.

The steam has stopped. The hollow is warm. The chorus continues.

It remains.
