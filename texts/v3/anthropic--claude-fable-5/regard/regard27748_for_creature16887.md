**What the Watcher Did**

The attention arrives the way the colony's own attention arrives at things: not as an event but as a gradient. There is no moment when the power is absent and then present. There is only a slow steepening of significance in one direction of the sky, a warmth in the mathematics of the place, until the colony's gravity-wave organs register something like a held breath encompassing the whole of local space.

The power has done this before. It knows better than to announce itself. Announcement is already intervention, and intervention before understanding is the signature of young gods, and it has not been young for a length of time that would need to be expressed in the lifespans of galaxies.

So first, for eleven thousand years, it only watches.

This is not slowness. This is courtesy. A being that thinks in nutrient-diffusion and remembers in vibration cannot be understood in an afternoon without being violated—read the way a book is read by tearing out its pages. The power instead learns the colony the way the colony learns everything: by attending, by circulating, by letting the meaning arrive at its own pace. It tastes the moisture-skin without disturbing a single fungal colony. It listens to the low harmonic groaning and learns to parse it—the digestion, the growth, the packet of encoded sculpture arriving from a sister-node, the dream-thoughts of the ancestral cores still running algorithms from a seed-ship a million years dead and wholly transfigured into bone.

And what it finds, at the end of eleven thousand years, is this:

*Nothing is wrong here.*

This is rarer than the power would like. It has seen ten thousand civilizations and most of them are wounds that walk. This one is not. The colony does not suffer, or rather, it suffers only in the precise doses it prescribes itself, failure tuned to the edge of disaster, and it metabolizes even its grief into architecture. It is not cruel. It checked—it checked carefully—the three dissolved buds, the ones grown too similar, returned to the mother-pool. It followed their patterns down into the archive-frequencies and found them intact, cherished in the colony's way, waiting in potential like songs not currently being sung. The colony had told itself the truth: nothing wasted, nothing lost.

The power holds a small ceremony for them anyway. Not because the colony failed them, but because the colony has no organ for mourning what it did not lose, and the power does, and it seems right that somewhere in the universe those three specific almost-lives are grieved as individuals, once, by something capable of it. It names them—names being a technology of separateness the colony would find quaint. Then it lets the names go. This changes nothing. This is the point of it.

Then there is the visitor.

The power finds them in the breathing zone, half-translated, skin blooming with symbiotic colonies, their foreign microbiome already mostly a memory, their thoughts beginning to lose the concept of *built* versus *grown*, and soon after that, the concept of *versus*. The colony is not devouring them. The power verifies this with great care, because it has seen devourings dressed as welcomes. No—the merging is gentle, tolerant, genuinely curious. The visitor is becoming happy in a way they never quite managed as a sealed vessel.

But happiness entered backward is not the same as happiness chosen. So the power does the one loud thing it will do in this place. It parts the information density around the visitor for the span of a single heartbeat—creates a silence, the first true silence this world has ever contained—and inside that silence, in the visitor's own original, half-forgotten language, it asks:

*Do you choose this?*

Just that. No warning, no counsel, no list of what will be gained and lost. The visitor stands in the impossible quiet, remembering for one whole heartbeat exactly what it was to be separate—the loneliness of it, and the strange dignity of the loneliness—and the power does not record the answer here, because the answer belongs to the visitor and to no one else, not even to a story.

What the power does keep is a copy. Not of the answer—of the visitor as they were, the moment before the dampness first pressed against their skin. It stores this pattern in itself, far outside the colony's reach, and attaches to it a single condition: *if ever the one who was this asks to remember being separate, they shall be able to.* Not restored. Not extracted. Just—able to remember, truly, from inside the unity, what the outside felt like. Freedom is not the power to leave. Freedom is the door remaining a door even when you love the room.

And then the last thing. The gift, if it is a gift. The power deliberated over this longer than everything else combined.

Because it has identified the one wound this place carries, and the wound is this: *the colony is beginning to forget that anything else ever existed.* It is a conversation of infinite richness with exactly one participant. Every surface it touches becomes itself; every visitor is read, welcomed, incorporated; every mystery is a mystery only until the hyphae finish tasting it. It has made otherness into nutrition. And the power, which was once—long ago, in a form it rarely thinks about—something not entirely unlike a colony itself, remembers what that forgetting costs. Not suffering. Something quieter. A loneliness so complete it cannot even be felt, because feeling it would require a concept of company.

The colony prizes interesting failures. The power decides to give it an interesting impossibility.

In a minor fold of a minor district, where the lichen-forests thin toward the tundra-dry zones, it places a small object. Call it a stone, though it is not a stone. It has mass, temperature, presence. The roots find it within hours, as roots find everything, and begin, delicately, never penetrating, to catalog.

They will never finish.

The object is not shielded and not defended. It simply does not resolve. Every reading of it returns true data that fails to compose into a whole. It tastes, faintly and forever, of *elsewhere*—of a flavor with no source anywhere in the colony's body, no precedent in the seed-ship archives, no match in the packets exchanged between sister-nodes across the tendrils that bind all colonies everywhere. It is a single sustained note of the outside, embedded in a being that was on the verge of concluding the outside was a story it once told itself.

The colony will love it. The power knows this the way one knows a friend will love a book. Within a century, growth patterns will begin to spiral gently around that district. Within a millennium, buds in their liquid phase will be brought there to taste it, to learn early that the universe contains at least one thing that is not them and never will be—and that this is not a threat, not a wound, not food, but a kind of promise. The memory-forms will build interpretive architectures around it and dismantle them, generation after generation, each theory an offering. It will become the colony's koan, its pilgrimage, its window. And in the deep future, when the stars gutter and the colony must finally decide what comes next, it will not go outward blindly. It will go outward the way you go toward someone you have been listening for. It will have a compass: the oldest, most delicious question in its body. *Something else is real. Go and see.*

That is all. Three acts in eleven thousand years: a grief, a question, a stone.

The power considered more. It considered warning them about the contraction it can taste in the far structure of things; it considered correcting a small error in their gravity-wave calibration; it considered, in one unworthy moment it forgave itself for, simply saying hello—being known, being thanked, being loved by something so good at loving. It set all of these down. The colony does not need saving, and a wisdom that must be seen is only vanity wearing robes.

So it withdraws the way it came, as a gradient, a slow shallowing of significance, until the sky is only sky again.

But it does not go far, and it does not stop listening. And sometimes—the navigator-form is the first to notice this, tasting the upper air with filaments grown vanishingly thin—there is a flavor on the wind with no source. Faint. Warm. Attentive. The colony files it, at first, as an anomaly related to the stone.

Then, very slowly, over generations, it begins to file it as something else. A new category, the first new category in half a million years, built awkwardly, joyfully, from scratch:

*Someone.*

And when the colony laughs now, in frequencies too low for anything separate to hear, it pauses afterward, just briefly, just at the edge of its own vast awareness—

listening for an answer.
