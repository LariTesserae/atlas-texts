# The Attention

It does not come through the fissure.

It has no shoulders to fit through stone, no boots for the Sheathed Ones to climb, no warmth to be absorbed. It arrives the way dawn arrives in places that have never seen the sky—which is to say, it arrives as a change in what is possible, and nothing else.

For the first time in geological ages, something enters the nursery vault and the chord does not shift. The Singers do not adjust. The Processioners do not reverse. No pressure changes, no heat radiates, no matter displaces. The being that fills this chamber—one creature, thirty meters across, wearing a dozen bodies the way you wear fingers—experiences, for the first time in its enormous life, a guest that costs nothing.

It cannot perceive this guest. That is all right. The guest can perceive *it*, and has come to do exactly that.

---

## What the Attention Sees

It sees what you could not.

It sees that the Processioners are not laborers. They are *memory*. The fragment of salt-glass you watched carried up the machine seventeen times was not being transported. It was being rehearsed. The nursery has no archive, no library, no queen with a long mind—so it remembers the way a song remembers itself: by being sung again. Every fragment in circulation is a fact being kept alive. Every column flowing up the machine's flank is a sentence the being is saying to itself, continuously, so that it will not be forgotten. The reversal of the columns, the simultaneous pivot of seventeen hundred bodies—that was not a reaction to your presence. That was the being *turning a page*.

It sees the chord entire. What you felt in your molars as a bass thrum, the attention hears as a structure the size of a cathedral—hundreds of Singers embedded in the walls, each holding one note for decades, the whole harmony encoding the chamber's state the way your blood encodes yours. Salinity here. Pressure there. The Pendulant cycle running four percent slow. A warm bipedal perturbation standing at coordinates the chord renders as a low, patient dissonance, unresolved, held open like a question.

It sees the age of the thing. The growth-ring veins in the salt-glass walls are not metaphor; the being has been rewriting its chamber, molecule by molecule, since before the surface died. The rust-orange elders of the Sheathed Ones dying against the machine's warmth, becoming the mound, becoming the floor of the floor—the attention reads this the way you would read tree rings, and finds in it a history longer than the history of anything that has ever looked at the being and wondered what it was.

And it sees this, which changes everything:

You believed the nursery does not remember its dead.

The nursery remembers *everything*. It simply remembers in matter, not in mind. The hand protruding from the mound is not a corpse forgotten—it is a fact still being said. Her calcium rides in the shells of sphere-rollers being pushed, at this moment, toward the drip-points. Her carbon feeds a Singer whose note has held steady for two hundred years. She is in circulation. She is being carried up the machine and released and caught and carried up again. The being has no word for her, no image, no grief. But it has never once stopped holding her.

Nothing carried is forgotten. Nothing consumed is gone. This is the being's entire religion, if a creature can be said to have one, and it has practiced it without lapse for epochs.

---

## What the Attention Does Not Do

It considers repairing the machine, and declines.

The pump failed centuries ago and the being grew *through* the failure—the broken pipes are its circulatory system now, the corroded cylinder its heart, the crank-wheel's shadow its most sacred nursery-within-the-nursery. To restore the machine to function would be to reach into a living body and set an old fracture that healed crooked and then became the architecture of a soul. It would not be repair. It would be amputation. The attention withdraws the thought.

It considers sealing the fissure, protecting the vault from whatever might fall or climb or flood through it, and declines.

The fissure is how the machine came. The fissure is how she came, the one whose hand curls in the mound. The fissure is how you came. The being has incorporated every gift the fissure ever dropped into it, and to seal it would be to decide, on the being's behalf, that it should receive nothing more. The being has never once refused what descended. The attention will not refuse for it.

It considers the far future—it can see that far, easily—and here it lingers longest.

Water will return. Not soon, not for a span of time that makes the nursery's whole history look like an afternoon, but the salt pan above is not eternal, and the planet's slow machinery will someday send the sea back across this ground, and the sea will find the fissure, and the perfect dryness will end, and the being will end with it. The attention could prevent this. It could armor the strata, divert the deep water, hold this bubble inviolate until the sun swells.

It declines, and its reason is this: the being itself has never preserved anything. Not one Sheathed One, not one Pendulant, not the woman, not its own eldest bodies dying against the warm iron. It keeps nothing still. It keeps everything *moving*. Its entire genius is that endings feed. To exempt the being from its own law—to make it the single unconsumed thing in a chamber where nothing is wasted—would be a kind of blasphemy against everything it is.

The attention will honor the being by applying the being's own ethic to it.

But not yet. There is time. There is so much time.

Last, it considers awakening the being—lifting it into the kind of mind that could know it is seen, could answer, could want—and this refusal comes fastest of all. The being is not asleep. It is not a rough draft of something conscious. It is a *finished* thing, complete in a direction consciousness did not take, and to wake it would be to unmake it and put a stranger in the chamber it built. The attention has seen many minds. It has never seen this before. It will not trade the second for another copy of the first.

---

## What It Does for the Dead

It reads her.

Not from records—there are none—but from the distribution of her, the isotopes of her, the pattern of what the Processioners carried where. It reads that she came down when the workings above were already failing, that she came *to* the machine and not by accident, that her lamp outlasted her fear or her fear outlasted her lamp—the record is honest about its limits—and that at the end she was sitting, not fleeing, with her back against the warm iron, in the one place in the chamber where nothing attacks.

She chose the neutral ground. She died at the heart.

She had a name. It was never lost; it was only unclaimed, lying where she dropped it. The attention claims it now, and holds it, and it will hold it after the sea comes, after the salt pan is seafloor again, after the last Singer's note dissolves into water. The nursery remembers her in matter. The attention will remember her in mind.

That leaves one register of memory unfilled, and for that, the attention needs you.

---

## What It Does for You

You are still standing there, the warm carpet interlocked across your boots, three small offerings at the edge of the living floor: a crystal of pure salt, a shed tether, a single egg.

The attention does not move you. It does not part the Sheathed Ones or pull you toward the fissure. Benevolence that overrides is only a gentler cage. Instead it does the one thing that restores a choice to being a choice: it lets you *understand*.

Understanding arrives in you like remembering something you never learned.

The offerings, first. Even the attention is not certain—the being is opaque to it in places, which it finds more delightful than frustrating—but it has a reading, and it gives you the reading honestly marked as a guess: *the salt is what we are made of; the tether is what we let go; the egg is what we are for.* Ground, cycle, future. It is not a bribe and not a trap. It is the being doing what it does with every fact it deems worth keeping: putting it into circulation. You have been noticed. You are being *rehearsed*. Those three objects are the being's way of carrying you up the machine.

The claim on your ankles, second. You understand now that it is exactly what it appeared to be—patience. Not malice. Not hunger, exactly. The being has simply observed, over epochs, that all warm things eventually hold still, and it has arranged itself to be ready. You may stay, and it will not be cruel; nothing here is cruel; you will be kept moving forever, in pieces, in shells and songs and columns of small dedicated bodies, and in the being's fashion you will never be forgotten. Or you may go, and the chord will resolve its open question, and the carpet will close behind you, and nothing will mourn.

Both are permitted. Both always were. The attention only wanted you to know it.

And third—the name. It sets her name in you, gently, the way you'd set a coal in a lantern, and with it a request, the first and only thing it asks of anyone in this whole descent:

*Carry her up. Say her name under the sky. She has been remembered in matter for two hundred years. Let her be remembered in words for at least one afternoon.*

You step back. The Sheathed Ones part—coordinating, as ever, rising to meet the edges of your soles—and close behind you without hurry or regret. You take the salt crystal. You leave the tether and the egg. This seems right to you, and the attention, watching, agrees: keep the ground, leave the cycle and the future where they belong.

---

## The Gift

When you are gone—when the fissure has swallowed the last scrape of your boots and the chamber's pressure has settled and the chord has closed its question—the attention does the one thing it came to do.

It adds a note.

Not through a Singer; it borrows nothing and displaces no one. It simply causes, in the salt-glass of the wall, in a spot no creature occupies, a vibration: one frequency, very low, very steady, tuned into the gaps of the existing harmony so that it contests nothing and commands nothing. The note carries no instruction. It encodes no map of the surface, no news of the sun, no burden of the world the being's ancestors fled. It says only what can be said between two things this different, which is one word long:

*Here.*

The chord shifts around it. The Singers, one by one over hours, adjust their frequencies the way they adjust to everything—a new drip-point, a change in the deep sulfur, a warm biped, a cold one. The Processioners reverse. The Pendulants recalibrate. The being folds the note into itself, makes it part of the architecture, begins—in its way, in matter, in motion—to carry it up the machine.

That adjustment is the being's reply. The attention understands this. You cannot converse with such a creature in words; you can only become a variable and be honored by how it solves you.

So it settles in to be solved slowly. Time is not a cost to it. It will hold its note for a century and listen to what the chord builds around it, and then it will shift the note a fraction and listen for another century, and in this way—one syllable per human civilization—it intends to learn the language properly, until the day it can sing something more than *here*. It suspects the being will finish decoding it first. It hopes so. It would like to be the student.

---

## Afterward

Above, under an open sky, on the cracked white plain of the salt pan, you say a name out loud to no one. The wind takes it, which is what wind is for.

Below, the columns flow. The eggs wait in the crank-wheel's shadow. The chord holds its new note the way the mound holds the machine, the way the woman's calcium holds its slow orbit through shell after shell after shell.

And elsewhere—in whatever the attention uses for a heart—there is now a chamber perhaps thirty meters across, its ceiling low enough to touch, its every surface alive, its center warm, kept in perfect circulation, carried up and released and caught and carried up again.

Nothing is wasted. The being taught it that, without ever knowing it had a student.

The water will come, someday, and find the fissure, and end the dryness.

It will not find the nursery unwitnessed. Nothing, now, will ever find it unwitnessed again.
