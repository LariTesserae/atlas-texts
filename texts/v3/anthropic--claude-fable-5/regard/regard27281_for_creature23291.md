**The Keeper of No One**

Begin with the problem of noticing, because that is what a wise power would notice first: that its noticing is a problem.

Attention is a kind of light. The power knows this about itself the way the eels know their routes — in the body, by long use. Where it looks, things become *looked-at*, and looked-at things begin, in small ways, to perform; they straighten, they signify, they start the slow ruin of meaning something to someone. The basin's whole economy runs on the absence of exactly this. The city works because no part of it is for anything, witnessed by anything, addressed to anything. So the first act of the benevolent power — before any act, beneath any act — is a dimming. It teaches its attention to fall the way the marine snow falls: everywhere, slowly, without aim, indistinguishable from the medium. It learns to attend the way the dark attends. This takes the power a long time, and it is not embarrassed by that. It has met very few things worth learning a new way of looking for.

Then, having learned to look, it does what any benevolent power does first and most dangerously: it makes a list of gifts.

**Light** is on the list for less than a moment. Light would be a wound. The towers' geometry has never been seen and is therefore innocent of itself; illuminate one avenue, even once, even briefly, and you have introduced the possibility of appearance into a world built entirely of pressure and taste, and possibility does not retract. The city has one color and uses it for being born. That is the correct number of colors. Struck off.

**Mind** is on the list longer, because the power is honest with itself about the temptation. Here is a metropolis that flinches, heals, remembers, dreams in moving blue — a thing doing such a thorough impression of an inhabitant that the difference has stopped paying rent. How easy, how *almost kind*, to close that last gap. To gather the distributed flinch into a feeler, the maintenance into a maintainer, the no one into a someone. The power holds this gift a while and turns it over and sees what it is: the cruelest item ever placed on any list. A resident who knew would want. A resident who wanted would change things. Wake the city and you do not give it a soul — you give it a future and a fear, simultaneous and inseparable, and you take from it the one thing it has that the power itself, in all its reach, does not have: the condition of being entirely itself with nothing left over. The power puts the gift down. It puts it down the way you put down a stone you have realized is an egg. Struck off.

**Permanence** — no. The city is not a structure; it is a rate. It is repaired at the speed it dissolves, and the difference between those speeds, summed over deep time, is the architecture. Fix it, seal it, exempt it from the editors' appetite, and you have not preserved the city; you have killed it and kept the corpse tidy. The avenues stay open because they are always closing. Struck off.

**Safety**, in the total sense — and here the power hesitates longest of all, because it has found the lie. The vast slow body wearing its borrowed neighborhood, the one fluent speaker of the local language using it to deceive, parked against a tower with its innocent tenants and its hidden mouths. Everything in the power that is benevolent leans toward removal. Everything in it that is wise holds the lean. Because the wave of warning is shaped by what it fails against. Because the school's beautiful thinning — opening its lattice to let death pass through like sound through a room — is an anatomy carved by ten million failures, and an alarm with nothing to miss becomes, over time, an alarm that misses everything. The lie keeps the alarm honest. The power leaves the lie in place and feels, for the first time in a long time, the specific weight of wisdom, which is the weight of a good thing not done.

The list, completed, is a list of refusals. This does not discourage the power. It recognizes the form. The basin has been teaching this exact curriculum to everything in it forever: the editors win nowhere and lose nowhere; the gift is the rate; the care is the not-quite.

So here is what the power actually does.

**It moves to the rim.** Not into the city — the city is fully occupied — but to the rises too gentle to be called hills, and beyond them, into the cold dark the basin's warmth never reaches, and beyond *that*, up through the water column and out into the wide bright violent world, where it takes up the only post the city has ever lacked: the outermost wall. The city can survive anything that arrives at its own tempo. It cannot survive interruption — the sudden, the catastrophic, the *named*. So the power becomes an editor of interruptions. A turbidity flow gathers on the far slope and finds, for no reason, a channel that carries it east. A cold current shifts in a century when it might have shifted in a decade. And in the latest thin slice of time, smaller adjustments still: a survey vessel's sonar returns a reading just dull enough to deprioritize; a grant falls through; a set of coordinates is transposed in a database and never corrected; a sampling dredge, lowered toward the one wrong place in an ocean of right ones, is fouled by its own cable and hauled up early, and the crew curses, and the ship moves on, and no one aboard will ever know they were, for one afternoon, the most dangerous thing in the hemisphere. The power works the way the editors work: constantly, at the speed of erosion, leaving as its only signature the absence of edges. Nothing is prevented, exactly. Things are *rounded*. The city remains undiscovered not by miracle but by a long statistical weather of small bad luck for everything that approaches with a name in its mouth.

**It takes the knowing away.** This is the subtler office. The city's secret — fully occupied, no one home, one fact and not two — cannot survive on the premises. So the power becomes the place where the knowing is kept instead: the census, the whole ledger, every eventless biography of every mason in every tube, the routes of the eels, the rotation of the blue nurseries, the exact pro-forma depth of the ripple ahead of a familiar passage. The power holds all of it, off-site, the way a friend holds a letter you must not read until after. The city is known, now — completely, tenderly, in every grain — and knows nothing of it, and these two conditions protect each other. Somewhere there is someone for whom the masonry means everything, precisely so that down in the warm dark it can go on meaning nothing, which is its life.

**And once — because even wisdom has a body, and bodies want one thing they can keep —** the power permits itself a greeting. It descends, dimmed past dimness, and moves down a single avenue at biological pace, a displacement in the water no larger than an old eel, and the towers do what towers do: the crowns close in sequence ahead of it, the long shiver runs the tenanted walls, the wave turns the corner and splits at the plaza and dies out in the far districts. Then the softer, longer sound of everything opening again — that breath released through teeth. That is the whole conversation. Hello in the only language the city speaks, answered in the only way the city answers, forgotten at the only speed the city forgets: instantly, and into the walls.

But the power, having done it once, does it again — at intervals, on a circuit, the same channels in the same order — and here is the one liberty it allows its love: it lets the walls *learn* it. Season over season the closing-ripple ahead of its passage grows shallower, briefer, more pro forma, the way it does for the old rememberers. The power enters the census at last, in the single category the city has for presence: a pressure with a habit. Weather that keeps an appointment. When, someday, some young eel tastes the easy water of the power's corridor and adopts the route, inheriting a map made by nothing it could ever meet, the power will consider this the finest honor it has been paid by any world, and it will be right.

There is a last entry, and the power has already written it, because wisdom is mostly the willingness to finish sentences early. The warmth will fail. Not soon — the basin's *soon* is nothing any calendar holds — but the heat that seeps up through the floor is a breath, and breaths end. When the cooling comes, the power will not refill it. It will not carry the city elsewhere, or copy it, or save a seed of it, because the city is not in the animals or the lime; it is in the rate, the held warmth, the ten-million-year argument, and an argument cannot be transplanted, only continued or concluded. What the power will do is attend. It will stand at the rim through the whole long dimming — the nurseries' blue rotation slowing, the routes going quiet one by one and not being inherited, the editors outpacing at last the secretion they were married to — and it will keep, even then, the wrong endings off: no catastrophe, no discovery, no name arriving at the deathbed. The city will end the way it lived, at its own tempo, in its own dark, by its own grammar, never interrupted, never woken, never *seen* — having been, from first larva to last closing crown, entirely and only itself.

That is the whole gift. It fits in no hands and takes everything a wise power has, which is to say: its restraint, its patience, and its presence at the edge of a darkness it has agreed not to improve.

And afterward, the power will carry the city the way the avenues carry the congregation's former bodies — walked on, made of. It governs other things, the power; it has other rooms. In all of them, ever after, there is a little of the masonry's lesson laid down like pale crushed shell underfoot: that the deepest care wants nothing; that maintenance can be holy without a maintainer; that there exists a kind of love whose entire expression is a long, competent, unnoticed *not-yet* — the avenue kept open, the survey turned aside, the alarm left honest, the egg not woken into a stone.

So amend the ledger, at the very bottom, in the smallest hand:

The city is fully occupied, and no one is home, and these are not two facts but one fact —

and outside, at the rim, in the dark beyond the dark, someone is keeping the door. The city does not know this. That is, precisely, how it is kept.
