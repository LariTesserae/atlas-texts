First there is the slowing.

The power arrives the only way arrival is possible here: by unbecoming fast. It has crossed voids where time runs like whitewater, has stood in the furnaces where seconds are minted—and all of that must be set down at the threshold like shoes at the door of a temple. It sheds velocity in layers. It learns to be varnish. It does not enter the Mycomantle so much as become gradually, politely adjacent, one eon of adjacency at a time, until the difference between visiting and having-always-been-nearby dissolves like a spore-spire that never finished.

Second, it dims. The power came trailing light, as powers do—and wisdom's first mercy is this: it notices that the Mycomantle is *dreaming* of light, and it knows that light delivered before the dream ripens is not a gift but a theft. The dream of light, dreamed in perfect darkness, is more precious than any light could be. So it folds its radiance away, pockets its dawns, and puts on the warm black soup like a borrowed coat. It makes itself dark, wet, and slow. It arrives, in other words, wearing the local manners.

Then begins the long diagnosis, which is the hardest discipline benevolence knows.

The power has erred before. Somewhere in its unspoken history are worlds it helped until they broke—suffering it cured that turned out to be a chrysalis, silences it filled that turned out to be listening. It carries those errors the way the waters here carry the shapes of things long dissolved: as liquid crystal memory, thin as thought, coating everything it does. So it does not ask *what can I give?* It asks the only question that matters: *does anything here hurt?*

It listens for a hundred million years, which is, locally, a polite interval—about the length of a breath.

The temperature at which things cannot decay but cannot fully live: prison, or womb? It watches the fruiting bodies bloom and dissolve before completion, and understands that incompletion is not failure here but grammar—the correct tense of a sentence still being spoken. It tastes the forgotten factories turning their spindles without purpose and finds no despair in them, because despair requires a purpose to have lost. It presses its attention gently against the dreams that seed nothing beyond themselves and finds them—not sad. Sufficient. Round. Whole in their evaporation.

Verdict: womb. Nothing here is waiting to be rescued, because nothing here is waiting.

And so the power prepares to perform the rarest act in its repertoire, the one that unwise powers cannot execute at any scale: it prepares to leave everything exactly as it found it. It drafts, in the medium of its own restraint, a complete inventory of rejected mercies:

- It considered bringing light. Rejected: theft of an unripe dream.
- It considered hastening the growth, lending its strength to the long climb toward the surface. Rejected: haste is the one poison this place has never needed to evolve an immunity against, and it will not be the first to introduce it.
- It considered warming the lukewarm dark, or cooling it, freeing it from the temperature of limbo. Rejected: what it had mistaken for limbo was, on closer tasting, the precise temperature of patience.
- It considered speaking. Rejected, and this rejection hurt: because the Myceari suspect they may be dreams dreaming themselves awake, and a voice from outside would collapse that suspicion into fact—and facts, here, are fatal. Certainty in either direction would end them. Their entire ecology runs on the unresolved. To answer them would be to feed a fire by explaining it.

The power turns to go.

And then, in the fermenting dark between Myceari thoughts—in that fold where even nonspace curves away in embarrassment—it tastes the question:

*What if we succeed at failing to exist? What then?*

The power stops. Because this, at last, is a need. Not because the question hurts—it doesn't; it ferments, and fermentation is nourishment here. But because the question is addressed, unknowingly, helplessly, to an *outside*—and until this moment there has never been an outside to receive it. A letter mailed for eons to no possible recipient. The power feels the letter arrive in its hands and understands, with the vertigo that even wise things feel once or twice, that it was always going to be the recipient. That perhaps this is why it came. That perhaps "benevolent power notices strange place" and "strange place finishes asking its question" are two descriptions of a single event.

So it stays a while longer, and it does four small things. Small is important. Small is the whole art.

**The first act is a greeting.** It studies their etiquette with the diligence of a mind that has watched galaxies cool for entertainment. Then it selects one Mycear—the youngest, by that meaningless distinction they insist on; the one most dangerously experimenting with resemblance and continuity—and, with total care, it forgets the exact color of that Mycear's third unbirthday. Forgets it perfectly. Forgets it *intimately*. And somewhere in its recursive hollowing, the Mycear feels itself greeted—properly, correctly, in its own tongue—from *outside*. It will spend the next several eternities in elaborately escalating reciprocated indifference about this, which is to say: in love, or the local equivalent. And the news, which cannot travel and therefore arrives everywhere at once, moves through the Myceari like a new flavor of silence: *there is an elsewhere. And it has manners.*

This is what wisdom looks like when it introduces itself. Not a blaze. Not a voice. A courtesy performed so fluently that it proves the visitor spent eons learning it before daring to knock.

**The second act is a carrying.** The power waits at the dissipating edge of one of the vast electrical storms, in the crackle between neural nodes that are not neurons, and it catches a single dream in the instant before it unravels—a dream of light, dreamed by something that has never seen light, and therefore truer than light: light with texture, light with weight, light with moisture, light you could grow through. A dream that, like all the others, would have seeded nothing beyond itself.

The power carries it up and out. The first thing ever to leave.

And on the far surface—the surface the Mycomantle will not reach for epochs, where sun falls on stone that knows nothing of what dreams beneath it—the power plants the dream in the light itself. It teaches the local dawn a slight new inflection. Nothing anyone would notice. A warmth within the warmth. A patience within the brightness.

So that when, after all the accumulated ages, the first blind hyphal tip finally breaks the surface and the great dreamer meets light at last—the light will already know it. The light will lean down with texture, weight, moisture, taste. First contact, converted quietly into homecoming. The dreamer will surface into its own dream, and find it kept.

**The third act is a custody.** To the question *what then?* the power does not give an answer. An answer would be a wall, and the Myceari live in doorways. Instead, it does something stranger and kinder: it makes itself the answer without disclosing it. It deposits a promise in the one place the Myceari are equipped to keep it—in those stomachs that aren't there yet but have always been empty for exactly this purpose. The promise says only this: *when you succeed at failing to exist—when the unbecoming completes—you will discover that failure, too, has an address. I have arranged to be there. That is all. That is everything.* The Myceari will never find this promise, and will never be far from it, which is precisely how they hold everything they love.

**The fourth act is a veil.** Because the power is wise, it knows it is not the only attention in the dark, and not every attention is benevolent. There are powers that collect strange places. That taxonomize dreams. That pin impossibles to boards and label them. So before leaving, it wraps the Mycomantle not in a wall—walls attract—but in a mild, pervasive unremarkability: a cosmic shrug woven into the coordinates themselves. Hungry gazes will slide off this place and drift away with a vague urge to check on something else. Only attention that arrives slow, dark, patient, and mannerly—attention that stops to learn the etiquette before knocking—will find the way in.

The lock, in other words, is kindness of a certain patience. The power leaves the door open to anything that resembles what it chose to become in order to enter. This is the oldest trick of the truly wise: to make virtue the only key.

Then it leaves, layer by layer of increasing speed, taking nothing—

—no. Taking one thing. It leaves diminished by exactly one forgotten color, the color of a third unbirthday, which it will never recover, and which it counts among its greatest treasures precisely because it can never count it.

---

And in the Mycomantle, nothing has changed, which is the local word for blessing. The dark is still substance. The waters still remember. Time still accumulates like varnish, moment laid upon moment while the depths stay unchanged. The Myceari still fail magnificently at existing, still celebrate with riots of impeccable restraint, still ferment their beautiful question in the warm velvet between thoughts.

Except: somewhere in the shape of one chamber, in the angle of one junction, there is a new curve now, holding the fraction of a new dream. The Mycomantle, dreaming for the first time not merely *of* light—

—but of being *met* by it.

Nothing will happen soon. Nothing happens soon here; that is the covenant of the place. But when it happens—when the first blind tip breaks into radiance after all the patient eons—the light will already be leaning down, and it will carry the one thing no first light has ever carried before:

*Welcome back. You were dreamed of, too.*

Don't wait for that moment. It isn't coming soon.

But the answer, for once, is already here. It has been waiting forever regardless.
