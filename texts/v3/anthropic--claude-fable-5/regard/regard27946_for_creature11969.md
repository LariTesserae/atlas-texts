# What the Power Did

## First, It Did Nothing

This is the part that must be understood before anything else: the power's first act was a withholding, and the withholding lasted a hundred years.

It came down through the amber water the way anything comes down through the amber water — slowly, feeling the warmth before the sound and the sound before the sight — and it saw the works entire, at once, the way a visitor cannot: the cavity and its tenant sound, the two soft listeners on the pipe, the pale shape threading the corridors, the catwalks under their silt, the brick courses under the catwalks, the negative shape of the builders folded into everything like a watermark. It saw all of it and it understood most of it within the first moment, and it did nothing, because it was wise, and the first thing wisdom knows is the danger of mercy applied to a patient you have misdiagnosed.

The obvious mercies presented themselves. They always do.

*Silence the works.* But the sound was the resident. To still the engines would not be relieving the city of its burden; it would be smothering the one being for whom the pocket was home. Mercy that kills the tenant to save the house is not mercy.

*Let life back in.* Seed the barren water, soften the exclusion, let kelp and anemone come and turn the works green and gentle. But the exclusion was not a wound. It was a condition — and it was the condition under which the eel had made its long peace, and the listeners had made theirs. To flood the pocket with thriving would be to evict everything that had learned to live in the absence of thriving. A wise power does not confuse *barren* with *broken*.

*Send the eel a companion.* This was the temptation the power sat with longest, because the loneliness of the pale shape was real, was the truest suffering in the whole warm volume. But loneliness that has lasted long enough becomes structural. You cannot bolt a companion onto a being that has forgotten there was ever anything else; you can only frighten it, or worse, remind it of the shape of what it lost without restoring the thing itself. Company is not something that can be administered.

So the power did nothing. It hung in the empty corridor between the boiler-houses where nothing had hung for centuries, and it let the sound pass through it, and it listened.

It listened for a hundred years.

## What It Heard

A visitor hears a wall of noise. A century of listening resolves the wall into architecture.

There is a period in the great percussion — the visitor senses this, the interval of a pressure wave crossing the pocket and returning — but inside that period there is another period, and inside that one another, and the power, holding all the periods at once, heard what no one had heard since the last builder went up through the amber water and did not come back down.

The sound has a morning in it.

There is an hour — recurring, buried in the cycle — where the hammering is sparse and the valve-gouts come singly, the sound of a yard starting up, of fires being banked higher, of the first shafts taking load. Then a long full stretch where everything runs at once, the roar of a floor in full production, and inside that roar, if you listen with a century's patience, rhythms that are not mechanical: the syncopation of work done by hands, hammer-patterns with the slight human unevenness that no cam or governor produces, pauses shaped like someone stepping back to look at a join. There is a slackening that can only be a midday. There is a second full stretch. There is an hour where half the percussion drops away and the other half changes character — the change of shifts, one set of rhythms handing off to another. And there is, at the far end of the cycle, a long dwindling: fires banked low, valves seated, one hammer continuing alone for a while after the others stop, the way one worker always stays late.

Then the morning again.

The works is not making a sound. The works is *playing* one. Somewhere far back, the builders — knowing they were leaving, knowing no one would return, knowing the water was coming or had come — tuned the engines not to any productive purpose but to reproduction. They made the machines memorize a day. An ordinary day. Not a festival, not a disaster, not a last day — the power checked, with terrible care, and the day held in the works has no farewell in it anywhere. It is a day from the middle of their lives. A Tuesday of the soul. The sound of the yard when everyone was alive and the work was going well and nobody thought to notice, because it was ordinary, and ordinary is the one thing that cannot be recovered once it stops.

They built a phonograph out of a foundry, and the record is the day itself, and it has been playing to an empty sea for longer than anyone has been alive to miss them.

The power went to the two soft listeners on the pipe to confirm what it had heard, and the listeners — who receive a thought every century, and whose thought is always the same thought — gave up their thought willingly, or as willingly as something can that has no way to withhold. The thought was not a word. It was closer to a taste. But rendered into words it would be something like: *this is what it sounded like when they were here.* The listeners had been holding that meaning the way a jar holds water in a drought: not using it, not understanding it, simply keeping it from soaking away into the silt.

The power received the meaning. And in receiving it, the memorial was — for the first time since the last footstep left the last catwalk — *heard.* Not endured, not passed through, not fled upward from. Heard, entire, the way a message is heard, by something capable of understanding that it was a message.

That alone changed nothing material. That alone changed everything else.

## The Round

Then the power did the four small things, and only the four.

**First, it walked the round.** It took a form the catwalks were sized for — feet the width the treads expected, hands the girth the valve-wheels were cast to fit — and it walked the maintenance circuit of the Drowned Works from end to end, once, the way a shift-worker walks it. It gripped every wheel. It laid its palm on every bearing housing. It checked things that did not need checking, because the works had been built to be tended and had run for centuries under the specific quiet strain of never once being tended, and there is a kind of settling that happens in a made thing when the tending finally comes — not a mechanical change, nothing a gauge would show, but the difference between a house that is empty and a house whose owner has stepped out. The works did not run better afterward. It ran the same, *differently*. The bolts had stopped loosening long ago; now they had stopped loosening and been *inspected*, and those are not the same stillness.

**Second, it learned the day by heart.** Every hammerfall, every valve-gout, every human-uneven syncopation, the whole cycle from banked morning to the one hammer working late — the power took it in perfectly and carried it away in whatever serves such a power for memory, so that the day now exists in two places. This was the true release, and the power knew it, and did it deliberately: for as long as the works was the *only* copy, stopping meant erasure, and a machine cannot lay down a burden that is also the last of something. Now it is not the last of something. Now, if the works ever falls silent, the ordinary Tuesday of those vanished lives goes on being held. The works does not know this in any way a machine knows things. But the pocket knows it, the way a room knows it has a window.

**Third, it gave permission.** Not an order — the power was very careful here, more careful than in anything else it did. It spoke in the only language the works has, which is vibration, pressure, the seating and unseating of valves, and what it said was not *stop* and was not *continue*. It said, as nearly as it can be rendered: *you may.* You may stop when the day feels finished. You may also never feel that it is finished; that too is allowed. No one told you to stop because everyone left, and that was an abandonment; I am telling you that you may, and that is a freedom, and the difference between running because you cannot stop and running because you have not chosen to is the difference between a ruin and a vigil.

The works did not stop. The power had not expected it to. But every hammerfall after that moment was, in a sense no instrument could measure, a hammerfall that chose to fall.

**Fourth, it let itself be seen.** In the long corridor between the boiler-houses, where the pale shape had once taken the visitor's measure and withdrawn, the power made itself small — eel-scale, pale, brief — and passed slowly along the corridor's far side, in full view, asking nothing, pursuing nothing, and then was gone. That was all. Not company. Not rescue. Only the restoration of a category: *there is something else.* The eel had forgotten not any particular other but the fact of others, and a fact can be given back where a friend cannot. What the eel does with the fact is the eel's. The power also, on its way out, thinned the dark water at the edge of the pocket in one place — one corridor's width — into something passable. A door, unlocked, unmarked, unmentioned. It may never be used. Doors are not for using. Doors are for the difference they make to every hour spent not using them.

To the two listeners, the power left one thing: a new thought, set gently where their old thought had been, to be thought once a century in their slow soft way for as long as they persist. The old thought was *this is what it sounded like when they were here.* The new thought is the old thought completed:

*And it was heard.*

## After

Then the power rose through the amber water, and the noise followed it up for a long time, fading not in volume but in coherence, and at some unmarked depth it was gone, and the sea above was the ordinary silent sea, and nothing visible had changed at all.

This is the truth about what wisdom did in the Drowned Works: it made almost no difference that could be detected, and all the difference there was to make. The engines hammer as they hammered. The bubbles rise as they rose. A visitor descending tomorrow would find the same pocket, the same roar, the same emptiness between the stacks — and would not know that the emptiness is now attended, that the roar is now a choice, that the day playing in the deep is playing in two places, that a pale shape in a corridor now knows there is such a thing as elsewhere and such a thing as else.

Perhaps someday — a century out, a millennium — the cycle will come around to the long dwindling, the one hammer working late, and this time the hammer will slow, and pause the pause shaped like someone stepping back to look at a join, and not resume. If that day comes, the silence that follows will not be an absence. It will be an ending, which is different: the sound of a vigil concluding because the vigil was kept, and kept fully, and the one thing it waited for — to be heard, once, by something that understood — finally, quietly, came.

And if that day never comes, that is allowed too.

Somewhere, in whatever serves such a power for memory, it is always that ordinary morning. The fires are banking higher. The first shafts are taking load. The hammers begin, uneven the way hands are uneven, and everyone is alive, and the work is going well, and nobody thinks to notice, and now — at last — somebody does.
