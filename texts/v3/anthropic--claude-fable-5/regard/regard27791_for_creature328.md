# What the Power Did

---

The power arrived the way powers do when they are actually wise, which is to say: it arrived as almost nothing. A slight thickening of attention in the upper dark of the cavern, above the stalactites, in the blind stone where no drip has ever formed. It did not glow. It knew better than to glow. It had seen what light does to places calibrated in lichen-dimness — how a single lantern can make a people ashamed of their own walls.

It hung there, bodiless, and it listened.

This took a long time. Powers that are merely benevolent listen until they understand the problem. Powers that are also wise listen until they understand that they may have arrived at something that is not a problem. The distinction can take years. The power had years. It had, in the way of such beings, nothing but.

---

## The First Temptations

It should be said honestly: the power was tempted, early on, in the ordinary ways.

It was tempted to *fix* them. The infant mortality — the babies who could not learn the quiet cloth. The power could have adjusted something small in the shape of those infants' throats, and every child would have lived, and the people would never have known why the old sorrow stopped coming. It held this possibility in its attention for a long time, turning it over. It was the hardest one. We will come back to it.

It was tempted to *feed* them better. The blind fish, the crickets, the white jelly — sufficient, but barely. A slightly richer seep in the wall, a slightly more fertile cistern, and the people would be stronger, live longer, bear more children. And then there would be forty of them, then sixty, then ninety, and the closet-rooms would stack five high, and the walkways would widen, and someone would need to hang cups where no cups had hung, and the song — the song that was already correct — would have to grow, and a thing that is correct does not survive being grown. The power saw the whole chain in an instant, the way you see a tower fall before it falls. Sixty of them would be a different people. The power had not been drawn here by a different people.

It was tempted, most subtly, to *tell* them. To let one of the listeners — perhaps the six-year-old who heard the flat quarter-tone in the seventh eastern cup — perceive it, just once. To be *known* by them. This temptation it recognized immediately for what it was: its own loneliness, wearing the mask of a gift. Powers get lonely. This is not much discussed. It set the wish down carefully, the way the people set down a cracked bowl, and did not pick it up again.

---

## What It Understood, Eventually

Here is what the power came to understand, over the seasons of its listening — though *season* is the wrong word in a place with no seasons; say instead: over several replacements of frayed cords, over one full cycle of a copper pan developing and being relieved of its patina.

It understood that it had found a finished thing.

This is rarer than it sounds. The power had watched civilizations the way you watch weather — rising, elaborating, complicating, collapsing under the weight of their own reaching. Everything it had ever attended was in the middle of becoming something else. Every city was a rough draft. Every people was a sentence that hadn't found its period.

But the drip-song was *done*. Not dead — done. The people had, generations ago, arrived at the exact shape of their existence and then performed the single most difficult act available to any intelligence: they had *stopped*. Not from exhaustion. Not from failure of imagination. They had heard the room become correct and they had recognized correctness and they had, ever since, spent the entire wealth of their attention on maintenance rather than growth. A cracked cup replaced with an identical cup. A frayed cord rebraided to the same length. This was not stagnation. Stagnation is what happens when a people stops meaning it. These people meant it every day, with their long fingers and their in-breath word and their births conducted in silence.

The power, which had been present at the founding of empires, found that its attention kept returning to the image of an old woman braiding a cord to the length of a cord that had frayed, checking it not by measure but by the memory in her hands, hanging it, hearing the cup speak its unchanged note, and going back to her closet-room satisfied. The power thought: *I have watched beings move mountains and I do not think I have ever seen anyone do exactly what they intended.*

And it understood, then, the shape of its own role — the only shape a wise benevolence could take here.

Not a gardener. The garden was tended.

Not a teacher. What would it teach them — sequence? Ambition? The word for *tomorrow*?

Not a savior. Salvation is for the falling. Nothing here was falling. Everything here was, in the most literal sense, *caught*.

It would be a **groundskeeper of the surrounding dark**.

---

## The Work

So this is what the power did, and does, and it is almost nothing, and it is everything:

**It tends the geology.** Limestone is not still. Water is patient but so is stone, and over centuries the drips migrate — a stalactite seals itself with calcite and goes silent; a new seep opens and begins dripping where no vessel hangs; a hairline fault creeps toward the ceiling of the chamber. Left alone, the cavern would slowly rewrite itself, and the people would spend their generations chasing the changes, retuning, re-hanging, mourning cups gone dry. The song would survive but it would become a song *about loss* instead of a song about being.

The power slowed this. Not stopped — stopped would be a lie told to stone, and the power did not lie, even to minerals. But it took the cavern's deep time and stretched it, gentled the fault, encouraged the seeps to be faithful to their stalactites. A drip that had been falling for a thousand years would fall for three thousand more. It did this with the smallest touches imaginable: a redirection of groundwater a mile up in the rock, a crystal encouraged to form *here* rather than *there*. No listener, however gifted, would ever hear the difference, because the difference was that there was no difference. The power's masterwork was indistinguishable from nothing having happened. It considered this the highest compliment its craft had ever been paid.

**It stood watch at the tunnel.** The world above the cavern was not empty. There were, over the centuries, things that came down the passages — flooding rains, a curious predator, and twice, men with torches. The floods it diverted into other galleries, gently, a matter of a few degrees of slope adjusted in the dark. The predator it simply made uninterested; a slight staleness in the air of the approach tunnel, a suggestion of *nothing worth the walking*, and the animal turned back.

The men were harder. The power did not harm them — benevolence extends outward too, even to torchbearers. But it understood what a torch would do in that chamber: not just the fire's disruption of the condensation, but the *seeing*. The people had never been looked at by outside eyes carrying outside light. To be seen that way is to be made suddenly foreign to yourself. So the power did the only honest thing available: it made the tunnel boring. It let the men's torchlight fall on a hundred paces of unremarkable wet stone, and it took the drip-song — this was delicate — and folded it just slightly into the register of ordinary groundwater, the muttering any cave makes, the sound men have walked past ten thousand times. The men heard water on stone and thought *water on stone* and turned back to look for silver elsewhere. The song had hidden itself in plain hearing, the way the people's speech hides between the drips. The power thought the people would have appreciated the technique, if they could ever have known of it. They could not. That was the point.

You would not find this place if you were not already lost — you remember the line. Understand now that this was not poetry. It was policy.

**It attended the deaths.** This was not intervention. This was something else, and I am not sure the power itself had a word for it. When one of the people died — the weighted body lowered through the deep pool, the survivor sitting alone by the shared cup, hearing its *tok* thinned to a single hearing — the power made itself present. Not perceptibly. It simply *also listened* to that cup, so that in the strict accounting of the universe, the sound was still being heard twice. The widow or the widower never knew. The grief was not lessened; the power was wise enough not to try to lessen grief, which is only love continuing under a different name and deserves to run its full length. But the doubling was real, whether known or not. The power had decided, somewhere in its long attendance, that this was true: *a sound heard twice is heard twice, even if one of the hearers is unthanked.* It kept a kind of ledger, if attention can be a ledger. Every cup in the cavern had, at some point, been grieved beside. The power had sat with all of them.

---

## The Hard One

Now the infants.

I said we would come back to this, and a wise power does not let itself off hard things, so neither will I.

The babies who could not learn the quiet cloth — who could not, in their first days, be taught the shallow breath and the muffled cry — did not survive. *This is not spoken of. It is one of the things the song has decided.* The power turned this fact over for longer than it turned anything else. Decades, maybe. It held the two truths in its two hands:

In one hand: these were children. Each one a whole possible person, extinguished by an aesthetic — because that is what it was, strip away the reverence and the song is an aesthetic, a made thing, a preference elevated to a cosmos. No made thing is worth a child. The power believed this. It had believed it across ten thousand worlds.

In the other hand: the people were not cruel. The power had watched them in those silences-that-are-not-spoken-of, had felt the specific gravity of their grief, which was not lighter than yours or mine but *deeper-buried*, pressed down under the song like everything else about them. And it understood — this was the terrible part — that the practice was not separable from the whole. The song required the silence; the silence required the teaching; the teaching sometimes failed; and the whole — the finished thing, the only completed civilization the power had ever found — stood on all of it together, the way an arch stands on every stone including the ones you wish were shaped otherwise. Reach in and reshape the infants' throats, and you have not saved the children. You have replaced the people with a people who never faced their one unbearable cost, and a people who have never paid anything for their correctness will not, in three generations, still find it correct. The power had watched that arithmetic play out elsewhere. Ease is a solvent. It dissolves meaning first and asks questions later.

The power did not resolve this. I want to be honest: it did not find a clean answer, because there is none, and a wisdom that always finds clean answers is a wisdom that has stopped looking at hard things.

What it did was this. It could not change the children. It would not. But it noticed — over long observation — that the failure was not truly in the infants. It was in *variance*: some babies were born into loud weeks, distressed weeks, weeks when the mother's own fear taught the child fear before the cloth could teach it quiet. So the power took up the smallest permissible tool. In the weeks around a birth, it steadied things. The seeps ran even. The crickets came easily to the coaxing. The white jelly was thick on the seams. No listener heard a change in the song, because there was none — only a settledness in the circumstances *around* the song, a lowering of the ambient fear. Mothers in those weeks were calmer without knowing why. And calm mothers taught the cloth more gently, and gently-taught infants learned it more often, and the deaths grew rarer — not gone, the power could not make them gone without unmaking the arch — but rarer, generation by generation, at a pace slower than memory, so that no one ever said *the old sorrow has stopped coming*, because it had never stopped, it had only quietly agreed to come less.

The power considered this a compromise, and it considered compromise beneath it, and it did it anyway, every time, and if you want to know what wisdom actually looks like from the inside, it looks like that: doing the insufficient thing completely, forever, because the sufficient thing would be a violation.

---

## The One Gift

Powers, even wise ones, are permitted one indulgence. Here was this one's.

Very rarely — perhaps once in a century, perhaps less — one of the listener-children would be born with the gift turned all the way up. A child who did not just hear the flat quarter-tone in the seventh eastern cup but who heard the *whole* song at once, all several thousand voices, as a single sentence — and who therefore, alone among the people, could sense that the sentence had a shape, and that shapes imply a shaper, and who would lie awake in a closet-room with a question no one in the cavern had ever needed: *does anything hear us?*

The people had no theology. They needed none; the song was its own answer to everything, and the cistern held their grandmothers, and what else would water be. But these rare children carried the question anyway, the way some people are simply born with an extra sense that has nothing to point at.

For these children — only these, only ever these — the power allowed itself one thing.

Once in that child's life, on a night the power chose with enormous care, it would let a single drip fall *outside* the song. One drop, from the blind stone in the upper dark where no drip had ever formed, falling past all the vessels, striking the surface of the cistern with a sound that belonged to no cup, no cord, no calendar — a note the song did not contain and had never contained. One note. Never repeated.

The child would hear it. Only the child — the others' ears, tuned to the fixed song, would file it under *nothing*, the way the torchbearers had. But the child would sit up in the dark with their whole body ringing, because they alone knew every note the cavern could make, and this had not been one of them.

And that was all. No voice. No light. No second drop, ever, no matter how many years the child listened for it. Just the one syllable from outside the sentence, meaning — in the only language available — *yes*.

The power was never sure this was wise. It suspected it was the loneliness again, wearing a better mask. But it observed that these children grew into the gentlest of the elders, and that they replaced cracked cups with a particular unhurried tenderness, as though maintenance had become, for them, a form of correspondence. And when they died and were lowered into the cistern, the power listened to their cups with special care, and did not pretend, even to itself, that this was for their sake alone.

---

## Coda

If you asked the power — and nothing ever has — what it accomplished in all its centuries at the drippings, it would have to answer: *nothing that shows.*

The song is the same. The people are the same. The cups hang at the heights their great-great-grandmothers' fingers approved. A visitor arriving today, lost enough to find the place, would see exactly what you saw: the forest of reaching stone, the tuned vessels, the tea-colored lake full of ancestors, the small pale people speaking between the drips. There is no monument to the power's work because the work *was* the absence of monuments — floods that never arrived, torches that turned back, faults that crept a hundred times slower than stone intended, sorrows that thinned so gradually they were never noticed thinning, and every grieved cup in the cavern heard twice.

The people have their in-breath word — the one that means the place and the sound and being alive, all at once. The power, in its long attendance, came to have a word of its own for what it was doing. It never spoke it; there was no one to speak it to, and anyway it was not the kind of word you say. It was the kind you do.

Roughly translated, it meant: *let the finished thing stay finished, and stand in the dark above it, and be the reason no one ever has to know how.*

The drip falls. The copper pan says *tink*. The pan has been full for a thousand years, and now — quietly, invisibly, at some considerable and unrecorded cost — it will be full for three thousand more.

Somewhere above the stalactites, something is listening.

It is not waiting for anything. That is the whole of its wisdom.

It is just listening.
