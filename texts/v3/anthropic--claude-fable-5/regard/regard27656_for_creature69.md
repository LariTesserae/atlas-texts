# What the Power Did

You will want it to do something. That is the trouble with powers, benevolent or otherwise: we imagine them the way we imagine hands — reaching, adjusting, setting right. I have to ask you to set that image down before we go on, the way you would set down a lantern before entering a room full of moths.

## The first thing it did was slow down.

It arrived without arriving. Powers of this kind do not walk into places; they *attend* to them, and the attending is the arrival. One moment the hollow beneath the root-plate was unregarded, in the way that nearly everything is unregarded, and the next moment it was held in a regard so wide and so quiet that nothing in the hollow noticed any change at all. This is the correct way for such a regard to arrive. Any other way would have knocked the spires over.

But attending was not enough, and the power knew it was not enough, because the power was wise, and wisdom begins in the discovery that your own tempo is a kind of blindness. Watch the city of Under-Root at the speed of a human heart and you see a photograph. Watch it at the speed of a wren and you see less than that. So the power did the thing that only a very patient being can do: it slowed its attention until a decade of its regard passed over each millimeter of lichen-growth, until the falling of the droplet from the hanging root became an event with a beginning, a middle, a long deliberation, and a conclusion — until the city stopped being an arrangement and became what it actually is, which is a *performance*, ongoing, crowded with incident, tumultuous by its own standards.

At that tempo, the wood louse's passage down the colonnade was a state procession lasting an age. The eastern edge of the ochre district was advancing like a tide. The scarlet lamps were opening and spending themselves and being succeeded in slow generations, constellations wheeling. The mycelium below was fighting its dark chemical wars, front lines surging and buckling through the wet wood, empires of thread.

The power watched this for what would have been, by our counting, several years. It did not consider this long. It considered this an introduction.

## The second thing it did was want, and set the wanting aside.

Because it was benevolent, it wanted things for the city, immediately and in quantity. I will list what it wanted, and what it understood, because the understanding is the whole of the matter.

It wanted to warm the hollow — just slightly, just a degree — because cold reads as hardship to any being that has ever been warm. And it understood, before the wanting had even finished forming, that the cold *was* the city: that warmth would quicken the metabolisms, hasten the digestion of the beech, spend four hundred years of endurance in forty, and leave a heap of ordinary rot where a civilization had been. The cold was not the city's affliction. The cold was the city's constitution.

It wanted to protect the hollow forever — to set something around it, a deflection, a forgetting, so that no boot heel, no rooting boar, no falling hazel would ever trouble it. And it understood that *forever* was a falsification. The city existed inside a deal, and the power had read the deal's terms in the dark lines of the warring mycelia: the beech will be entirely consumed; the city will go with it; this is not tragedy; this is the deal. To exempt the city from its own foundations would be to replace it with a museum of itself. The lichens had never asked to be permanent. They had only ever asked — in their thallus-way, which is not asking — to *continue until*.

It wanted to speak. This wanting it examined for a long time, turning it over, because it was the most tender of the wantings and the most dangerous. Speak to whom? The springtails, lifted into knowledge? Knowledge of what — that they were watched, that they were small, that their pavements were beloved? The power could find no gift in that, only burden. And its voice, however softened, was loudness of a kind the hollow's economy had no room for. So it did not speak. It has never spoken. If it has a voice, the voice is folded away somewhere, kept like the pseudoscorpions keep their pincers.

And it wanted, last and most humanly — forgive the word; there is no better one — to *understand the spiral*. To crawl in, as it were, with the great hand lens of its whole attention, and resolve the question. It stood at the edge of that wanting for a while. Then it recalled what the biologists had concluded on their wet knees, and it agreed with them, and it did the wise thing, which was to leave the fact finer than any explanation of it, and turn away.

## What it permitted itself.

Not nothing. Wisdom is not paralysis. Over the centuries of its attending, the power has permitted itself, by my count, four acts, and I give them in order of size, largest first, so you can see how small they get.

*First:* there is a hazel on the slope above the root-plate, and in about one hundred and forty years it will fall, and unamended it would have fallen across the fern-screen and crushed the light out of the hollow. The power did not brace the hazel or fell it early. It leaned it. Over sixty years, by increments no dendrologist would remark on — a root encouraged here, a competitor's shade adjusted there — it has turned the hazel's future fall eleven degrees to the north. The hazel will come down in a gap. It will feed the same soil. Nothing has been taken from anything.

*Second:* boars root through this part of the wood roughly once a decade, and twice now a sow has angled toward the root-plate, and twice now she has found — just there, just at the critical moment — a richer smell four strides to the left. The power does not congratulate itself on this. Beetle grubs were genuinely there. It had merely made sure of them, some seasons in advance.

*Third:* when the fruticose spires spend their scarlet lamps and the spores go up on the cold updrafts through the ferns, the power has, on three occasions across three centuries, breathed. Once, gently, a millimeter's worth. Downwind of the hollow, a half-mile off, there is another fallen tree — younger, barely begun, its hollow still crude and unmapped — and the spores that reached it would very probably have reached it anyway. Very probably. The power keeps its accounting honest and admits: this was indulgence. It has rationed itself to three.

*Fourth, and smallest:* it arranged for the moss at the hollow's entrance to be very slightly deeper than it needed to be. So that anyone who came would have to kneel. So that the knees of their trousers would get wet. So that the posture in which the city can be seen at all is, whether the observer intends it or not, the posture of reverence — and so that standing up again is just awkward enough that no one does it carelessly, no one does it fast, no one rises without first drawing back. You have knelt in that moss. You thought the wetness was an inconvenience. It was a curriculum.

## The spiral.

I said the power turned away from explaining the spiral. That is true. But I did not say it turned away from the spiral itself, and it did not, because on its fourth or fifth slow pass over the deep place where the tap-root once plunged, the power recognized it.

Not what it was. *What kind of thing it was.*

A logarithmic spiral, precise past the resolution of its own material. Not rotating. Not growing. Not quite centered on anything. Set down at the founding of the city or before it, and maintained since by beings with no mechanism for consulting one another about geometry, and inexplicable to every hand lens ever brought against it.

The power looked at it for a long time — a long time even by its own reckoning — and understood that it was looking at stillness held so long it had become a feature. At attention that had cooled into pattern. And it understood, with something that in a smaller being would have been called a shiver, that it was not the first.

Something had attended here before. Before the beech fell, perhaps. Something that had also arrived without arriving, also slowed itself to the tempo of stone, also wanted and set the wanting aside — and had, at the end of its watching, left the only signature such watching can leave: a shape too regular to be accident and too quiet to be message, off-center, patient, declining to explain itself. The third possibility, the one the narrator leaned away from. The village road following the older path.

The power did not try to answer it, because there is no answering across that kind of gulf, and because an answer is a sort of loudness. It did something better. It took the spiral as instruction — as the previous watcher's whole testament, compressed: *I was here; nothing needed doing; I held still until my holding-still became part of the place; go and do likewise.*

## The end, foreseen.

The power can see the end of the city. Not mystically — arithmetically. So much beech remains; the mycelium eats at such a rate; the cold retards it thus far and no farther. Some centuries hence — it knows the season, near enough — the last structural fiber of the root-plate will let go, and the plate will slump, and the fern-screen will close over a hollow that is no longer a hollow, and the cold bright light will stop arriving, and the city of Under-Root will be complete. Not destroyed. *Complete*, the way a long ceremony is complete, the way the caddis case is complete when the caddis leaves it.

The power will permit the end. This is the hardest of its permissions and it renews the decision every decade or so, the way you might renew a vow, checking it against the heart. Each time, the answer holds: the deal was struck before it came; the deal is the city's dignity; a rescued Under-Root would be a different and lesser thing, an ochre-and-sage taxidermy. The lichens have always known. They knew before it did.

But it has also seen — because it looked, because looking is what it is — which tree in this wood falls next. A beech, as it happens, upslope, sound-seeming, with a flaw in its heartwood the width of a hand. Two hundred years, three. Its root-plate will tear up a hollow of promising depth, north-facing, wet. The power does not touch this tree. It only holds it, already, in the same wide regard. And it has noticed which way the cold updrafts run, from the old hollow toward the site of the new, and it has counted its remaining breaths, and it is saving them.

## What it does now.

Now — and *now*, for this power, is a word covering your whole life and your children's — it does the last thing, the ongoing thing, the thing that is arguably all it ever really came to do.

It counts.

The droplet forming on the hanging root: counted, its whole long deliberation, its commitment, its fall, its strike on the acorn-cup pool. The flicker of the rotifer, if it was a rotifer: counted. The four sounds an hour: each one received, none let slip. The wood louse's every procession. The advance of the ochre district, millimeter by decadal millimeter. The wren's call arriving as a rumor of a sound and going unanswered — counted, and the unanswering counted too.

The city cannot remember itself. That is the one poverty in all its wealth: it is made of beings without archives, doing four hundred years of exquisite work with no witness and no ledger, every scarlet lamp spending itself unrecorded, every caddis case abandoned into anonymity. This the power could mend without touching anything, without warming anything, without a single grain of quartz displaced. And so it has.

Somewhere — I cannot tell you where; *where* is not the right category — the whole of Under-Root is held, exact and cold and bright: every citizen, every confederation that forgot it was a confederation, every frozen gesture of every slime mold's last calculation, every dark line of every mycelial war, the spiral and the watcher before and now the watching itself. When the root-plate slumps at last and the ferns close over, nothing will have been lost. Completed, and kept.

The droplet falls. The power counts it.

Nothing else changes. Everything is exactly as it was — which was always, if you looked closely enough, and someone now always is, a kind of miracle held at just above freezing. The light goes on pouring in through the ferns as if it had forgotten how to do anything else.

It has not forgotten. It has decided.
