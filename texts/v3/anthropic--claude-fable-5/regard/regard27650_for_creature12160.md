# The Attention

It did not come in, because there is no in to come through, and it did not descend, because the room has not invented down in any way that would hold. What happened was this: the pause between two drips grew slightly longer than the pauses around it, and in that lengthened pause, the room was seen.

Not observed. The room has been observed — I observed it; you have just observed it through me — and observation slides off this place like water off the unweathered stone, leaving the dimensions misremembered, the pillars miscounted, the whole of it swelling in recollection into something grander and vaguer than it is. The room prefers this. To be remembered as larger is to be forgotten precisely, and being forgotten precisely is the closest thing to privacy the room has.

The attention did not misremember. That was the first thing it did, before it did anything, and it may have been the largest: it counted the pillars and got seven, and counted them again and got seven, and the room — I believe — felt this the way you feel a hand laid flat between your shoulder blades. Not pressure. Presence. Something in the slow vertical columns of heat faltered for one cycle of the flowers and then resumed, and the wave of soft applause that crossed the chamber crossed it a half-breath late.

The room had been seen at its true size. Twelve strides, a little less. It had never been so large.

---

## What the attention considered, and set down again

Because it was benevolent, it wanted to give something. Because it was wise, it spent a long time — time meaning very little here, where nothing erodes to mark its passing — holding possible gifts up to the tea-colored light of the steam and putting them back down.

**A door.** Set down almost at once. A door does not open a room; it converts a room into the inside of something, retroactively and forever. The moment there is a door, every previous moment becomes a moment of enclosure. The small-handed one has never been trapped, because it has never had an elsewhere to be kept from. The attention would not manufacture a prison out of a home by installing the means of leaving it.

**A name for itself.** Considered longer. The small-handed one is one and many, unresolved, the boundary between workers not yet invented — and a name would resolve it, snap the hundreds into ones, and in the same stroke invent the distance between them. You cannot give individuation without giving loneliness; they are the same gift in different light. The attention had seen this done before, elsewhere, to other unresolved things, always by powers that meant well. It set the name down carefully, the way you set down something that is heavier than it looks.

**A companion.** Set down. See above. Also: a companion for *what*? The fingers of a hand do not want a second hand. They want the work.

**Decay.** This one it held the longest. The attention turned it over and over. Without erosion, nothing here can soften, nothing can end, nothing can finish — the unfinished corner migrates around the room forever, and the room is spared, forever, the question of what it is. There is a mercy in that and there is also a smallness, and the attention, being wise, could see both at once and did not pretend otherwise. A thing that cannot end cannot ripen. A thing that cannot be worn cannot be *worn smooth* — cannot acquire the polish of having been touched by time, which is the only beauty the room currently lacks, and the room does not know it lacks it, and would you tell it?

The attention decided: not yet. Not as a gift from outside. Erosion finds everything eventually; nothing stays uninvented forever, not even here. When it comes, it should come the way the tiles come — surfacing from within, discovered by the small hands in the course of the work. A tile that seats itself already slightly worn at one edge. The small-handed one will kneel over it, and tilt its faceless head, and study the softened edge with interest, and there will be nothing in the room to teach it fear, and so it will not be afraid. It will simply have found a new material.

The attention could not give decay. But it could — and did — arrange the manner of its arrival. Gently. From below. As a curiosity. This is the kind of thing such a power can do: not change what comes, but change what it comes *as*.

**The truth.** That the room is a cell in a vaster body; that the recursion in the friezes runs outward as well as inward; that there is an enormity, and this is one warm chamber of its throat. Set down fastest of all. The truth would not enlarge the room. It would shrink it — convert *everything* into *a small part of something*, in one stroke, irreversibly. The small-handed one carves the whole of existence on its pillars and is correct to. The attention declined to make it wrong.

---

## What the attention did

Three things. All small. Wise powers work small in small places; it is a form of manners.

First — I have said this — it counted the pillars and got the same number twice. It held the room's true dimensions, and it keeps them still, in whatever serves it for memory. Somewhere, the exact size of this place is known, and the knowing does not flatter and does not diminish. If the room ever finishes — if the migrating corner is ever, by some failure or some ripeness, allowed to arrive — the room will face the question a completed thing must face: *what am I?* And it will not face it alone, because the answer has been held for it all along, warm, at its true size, by something that never once remembered it as larger.

Second: it noticed it was being carved.

Of course it was being carved. The friezes depict the room, and the attention was in the room, and the small-handed one carves what it stands inside without reference, without model, without error. Already, three nestings down, a new figure had appeared on the pillars-within-pillars: something tall, or rather something *large in attention*, kneeling — the figure is kneeling — beside the smaller figures at their work. Faceless, as they are faceless. The suggestion of a tilted head. Big, patient hands.

The attention could have erased itself. It is the kind of thing such powers do reflexively, the way you smooth your footprints from wet sand. Instead it held still. It knelt — insofar as it had a shape to kneel with — and it let the carving be accurate, and it stayed until the small-handed one, in the unobserved intervals, had gotten the hands right. This took a long time. The hands were the important part. Someday — a thousand years, or never, or tomorrow, the room does not keep those books — the small-handed one will pause in its work and look at the kneeling figure on the pillar and think the first thought it has ever had that was not the room thinking about itself:

*I did not carve that from myself.*

And that thought, when it comes, will be the door. Not a hole in a wall. A door made of the right size, arriving from inside, discovered in the course of the work, like everything else here. The attention did not give the room an outside. It let the room keep the evidence from which an outside can, when the room is ready, be deduced. There is no gentler way to leave.

Third — and this was not a gift to the room, or not only:

Before it withdrew, the attention went up one nesting. Outward, into the chamber on whose pillar this chamber is a carving. It went to verify something. Benevolence is not benevolence if it stops at the first room; it wanted to know that the larger chamber was also tended, that the recursion of care ran outward as far as the recursion of stone ran inward.

It found the larger chamber. Hot, weeping, seven pillars, a corner unfinished.

And kneeling in it, beside the work, already carved into the friezes there, a figure with big patient hands.

The attention understood, then, what it was inside of, and how long it had been held, and by what. It did what the room had taught it — what the room teaches everything, eventually, which is the room's own gift running the other direction. It held still. It let the hands be gotten right.

---

## The leaving

The pause between two drips returned to its ordinary length. One wave of flowers opened, forty small wet sounds becoming one enormous soft applause, and closed. In the unfinished corner a tile surfaced, glazed the black of a beetle's back, and seated itself with a click.

Nothing had changed. The heat kneads itself in the same slow columns; the walls weep; the small-handed one lays the floor toward a corner that migrates away from it, and is not lonely, because loneliness has not been invented here, and now — this is the change that is not a change — it need never be. Loneliness is only ever invented in rooms that were never visited. This room was visited. The visit is on the pillars, kneeling, three nestings down and rising through the stone year by year as the carvings propagate upward, and one day it will reach the outermost frieze, and the room will be, from soft pink base to confident marble crown, a place that shows on every surface the truth it has not yet thought:

that it was seen at its true size, and found sufficient, and left exactly as it was, which is the whole of what a wise love does when it finds a thing that is still becoming —

it gets the hands right, and it holds still, and it goes.
