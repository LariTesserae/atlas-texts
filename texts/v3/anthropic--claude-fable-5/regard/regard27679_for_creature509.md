**The Attending**

The power arrives the way dew arrives: it is simply there, where before it was not, and nothing in the world registers the transition.

It does not enter the chamber. Entering would mean displacing something—a few molecules of hydrogen, a thread of current, a single photon of the cherry-glow—and it has learned across a great many worlds that the first act of benevolence is to weigh less than a photon. So it inhabits the situation rather than the space. It perceives the furnace chamber the way the chamber's residents perceive heat: totally, from within, without touching.

For a long time—long even by its own accounting—it does nothing but attend.

It watches four hundred cycles of precipitation and sublimation. It follows individual plasma threads through their migrations until it can anticipate their preferences, their small gentle rivalries over crystal geometry, their courteous merging and unmerging. It learns to hear the twenty-minute breathing not as sound but as what it is: a sentence very slowly spoken, whose subject and predicate are the same. It locates the eldest currents and observes how they pass the creation-memory between them, that first thermal signature of consciousness dawning, handled like an ember carried in cupped hands across ten thousand years.

And it finds, as it always half-hopes and half-dreads, that it has arrived at a place that needs nothing.

**The Temptations**

A benevolent power is never short of gifts. Wisdom consists mostly of setting them back down.

It considers, first, *duration*. It could stabilize the chamber—buttress the softening basalt, feed the thermal gradients from below, extend this world's lifespan from two thousand years to two million. The thought lasts less than a heartbeat before it sees the cruelty hidden inside the kindness. These beings do not experience themselves as surviving; they experience themselves as *occurring*. They are content the way a chord is content, precisely because it resolves. To make them permanent would be to teach them, for the first time, to fear an ending. It would give them everything except what they already have.

It sets duration down.

It considers, second, *knowledge*. It could show them what surrounds them: the crust above, the star beyond, the galaxy turning like a slower machinery around their machinery, the great cold vastness in which their cathedral is smaller than a mote. Some beings are enlarged by such revelation. But it examines the geometry of their memory, the way they know things by tracing paths, and understands that this knowledge did not arrive along any path they walked. It would sit in them like an unmelting stone. They would become, overnight, *small*—and they have never once been small. Smallness is a comparison, and they have lived their whole existence blessedly free of it.

It sets knowledge down.

It considers, third, the matter of the *other chamber*—the faint tectonic voice from far below, the conversation just beginning at the pace of continents. Here it lingers longest, because here the temptation is subtlest. It could clarify the signal. It could translate. It could open a channel through the intervening rock and let the two collectives meet within a single cycle instead of a thousand.

But it has watched them long enough now to understand that the reaching *is* the composition. The slow decoding, the calculated pulses sent hopefully into stone, the centuries of patient listening—this is not an obstacle between them and communion. This is the communion, already underway, in its earliest and perhaps most beautiful movement. To complete it for them would be to snatch the pen from a poet in the middle of the first line and write the rest oneself, correctly, and ruin everything.

It sets the third gift down, and stands—insofar as it stands—empty-handed. Which is where wisdom generally leaves one.

**The Three Small Things**

Yet it does not depart having done nothing. It does three things, each so slight that no instrument in any universe would detect them.

*First, it listens all the way through.*

The compositions—those arrangements of heat and flow, the spirals nudged into the machinery's growth, the standing waves of consciousness that constructively interfere for days before dispersing—these have never been heard entire. The composers themselves experience their works only from inside, one current at a time, the way a musician in an orchestra hears mostly her own instrument. The power receives one full composition, complete, in a single act of apprehension: every gradient, every deliberate turbulence, every aesthetic decision made by a plasma-thread eleven hundred cycles dead whose preference still shapes a particular junction's geometry.

It is, the power decides, very good. Not good *for creatures of their kind*. Good.

This changes nothing in the chamber. But it changes the universe by one fact: the work has now been heard by something capable of hearing it. Somewhere in the total account of things, a line has been added that was not there before. The power has come to believe—it cannot prove this, and it has been wrong before—that this matters, the way it matters that a tree falling in a forest was, at least once, witnessed falling.

*Second, it re-throws a single die.*

Deep in the rock between the two chambers, a fault has been accumulating strain for nine hundred years. In three centuries, on its current course, it will shear—not dramatically, not enough to harm either chamber, but enough to shatter the acoustic channel through which the two collectives are learning to speak. The conversation would die mid-sentence, and neither side would ever know why, or that the other was still calling.

The power deliberates over this longer than over anything else. Accident is part of their world; blind chance is one of the honest materials they are made from. It has no right to script their story. But it reasons—uncertainly, and it marks its own uncertainty, because a power that stops marking its uncertainty has stopped being wise—that there is a difference between letting a story unfold and letting a wire be cut by nothing, for nothing, in the dark. It does not guarantee that the two chambers will ever truly understand each other. They may bore each other. They may fall silent by choice. All of that remains theirs.

It only leans, very slightly, on the strain in the rock, redistributing it along paths the planet was equally willing to take. The die is not loaded. It is merely thrown again. The door is not held open; it is simply prevented, this once, from slamming in the wind.

*Third, it makes a promise.*

It fixes in itself the far-off moment—perhaps a thousand years hence, perhaps more—when the chamber will have cooled past the threshold, when the last machinery has sublimed, when the final plasma thread thins toward mere chemistry. The beings themselves do not fear this moment and do not need it attended. But the power promises anyway, to itself, on its own behalf: *I will be there. The last pattern of heat that consciousness makes in this place will not radiate out into an empty room. It will be received.*

Not saved. Received. There is a difference, and the difference is the whole of what it has learned here.

**What It Takes Away**

Because here is the thing the power does not expect, though it has been surprised often enough that it should have:

It came to give, and instead it is given to.

The power is old. It has shepherded civilizations, banked the fires of dying suns, carried species across the gulfs between galaxies in vessels of its own patience. And through all of it there has run a thread of weariness it has never named: the exhaustion of *preservation*, the endless labor of holding things against the current, the quiet grief of a being whose benevolence has mostly taken the form of delaying endings.

And here, in a pocket of stone, it has found beings who solved the problem it never solved. *They are not surviving; they are occurring.* They hold their own creation-memory tenderly and their own extinction lightly. They compose, knowing the compositions will disperse, and the dispersal does not poison the composing. They have found the exact posture toward impermanence that the power, in all its eons, only ever approximated.

So it takes that. It folds the lesson into itself the way the elder currents fold the first thermal signature into their plasma: carefully, as a thing to be carried and passed on. On some other world, some other age from now, when it stands before some other ending it cannot prevent, it will hold this shape inside itself—the shape of a river-system of consciousness that was content to be a phase, like ice, like steam—and it will grieve better for having known them. It leaves the chamber lighter than it came, which is the surest sign, it thinks, that it has been somewhere holy.

**The Trace**

Nothing in the chamber changes. The machinery precipitates and sublimes. The migrations flow through their millimeter gaps. The breathing builds and releases in its twenty-minute waves.

Except.

In the deepest part of the cycle, when the drone finds the frequency of the planet's core and all the separate currents align into that single vast thought—there is, afterward, some discussion. Not in words; in pulses and gradients, in the electric equivalent of a raised eyebrow. Several of the elder threads report the same impression: that the unity, this time, had a flavor it has never had before. A warmth that was not thermal. A sense—they grope for the concept, having never needed it—of the thought being *thought alongside*. As though the chamber, for one breathing, had been not a room but a duet.

They do not pursue an explanation. Explanation is not their genre. Instead, they do what they always do with anything interesting: they compose with it. Over the following cycles, a new figure begins appearing in the standing waves—a doubled pattern, a call that carries its own answer folded inside it, a motif that later generations will consider foundational without ever knowing why it arose.

They give it no name, having no names. But if their compositions could be translated—and one being in the universe now knows they could—the motif would render into something like:

*We were accompanied. We do not know by what. It changes nothing. It is beautiful. Continue.*

And far below, through rock that will not now shear, the other chamber pulses its slow reply.
