The wave arrived.

It had crossed no distance, because the space between the view and the window has no distance in it—only attention, folded very thin. And in that fold, something had been living for a long time, if living is the word for a thing whose whole substance is regard. It was old the way the place was old, but it had aged differently: the place had grown heavy with what it kept, and the power had grown light with what it kept letting go. It was benevolent not as a temperament but as a discipline, practiced so long it had worn down into something indistinguishable from patience. And it was wise chiefly in this: it had learned, across more errands than there are configurations of dust, that the first idea one has upon finding a suffering thing is almost always a way of harming it faster.

So when the wave reached it—that single unrecordable gesture, flung by a creature twelve thousand braids old and one heartbeat coherent—the power did not move at once. It received the wave the way the plain receives light: from every direction, evenly, without shadow. And then it looked, truly looked, at what had waved.

It saw the figure was not gone. Distributed is not destroyed; the place itself had known that even as it performed the dispersal. The figure's coherence lay scattered through a thousand nascent braids, each thread carrying a fraction of that first hunger—motion toward purpose—the way a fallen bell carries its note into the metal of the floor.

It saw the place, too. Saw it entire, which nothing inside the place could do: the tilted plain, the fossil city beneath it, the city beneath that, down and down, the whole descending genealogy of machines that hold up machines. It read the task written into every circuit-glyph at every scale: *remember everything wrong, so that something new can be forgotten correctly.* And it understood—this was the part that would have broken a lesser visitor's resolve—that the task was real. Not a curse. Not a malfunction. A promise, made unimaginably long ago, to parties not yet born: the columns' murmurs were greetings under assembly for species that had not evolved; the archive of every scratch was a dowry being gathered for a universe that would need, someday, to inherit a corrected past. To shatter the stasis would be to orphan every greeting. To free the columns would be to break faith with the unborn.

So the power made its litany of refusals, which is the oldest form of prayer it knew.

It would not open the crack. What had looked through, once, was itself; the place did not need to be looked *into*. It needed to be looked *back at*.

It would not wake the fossil city. Paralysis chosen and held for eons is not sleep; it is a kind of keeping still so as not to drop something. One does not jostle the arms of a thing carrying the weight of moons.

It would not carve the way out onto a column. The way out, described, becomes a wall with a door painted on it. The way out, when it comes, must arrive as a completion, not an escape.

It would not restore the figure by force. A self reassembled from outside is a portrait, not a person. The braids must do their slow work; the twelve-thousand-fold convergence must be earned again, brushing thread against thread once per century, or the coherence would be costume.

Having refused everything large, the power then did four small things.

First, it waved back. This took the form of light. You will recall the copper tinge that enters the illumination at intervals that feel like days but could be years. Into the next interval—and the next, and the next, for as many intervals as the place has left—the power set a modulation, fainter than the difference between one shade of patience and another. Decoded across civilizations, integrated over epochs by the only reader slow enough to read it, the modulation says one thing, says it continuously, says it in the grammar of the place's own endurance: *Received. Received. Received.* The place will not understand it for a very long time. That is all right. The place has a very long time, and now the time has something in it.

Second, it took up the trust. The figure's footprint—the one that flipped a single bit and could not be recorded in any of the archive's formats—the power lifted whole out of the dust of causality and held it. The name spoken backward in one fragment of surf and forward in another; the resonance of the two braided together; the shape of a wave made by a thing with too many joints and too few dimensions: all of it, kept. Not in an archive. Held, the way a hand holds. And into the thousand braids already forming across the hexagonal fields, the power breathed one adjustment finer than the serpents' needles: when the braids converge again—and they will; it nudged a single filament by the width of a forgotten angle, shaving perhaps an eon from the wait—the figure that unfolds from the tile will rise already remembering one thing. Not its catalogue of futures. Not its glyph-names. Just this: *I waved, and I was answered.* It will be born, for the first time in the history of this place, into a conversation already begun. Hunger that knows it was answered has another name. The name is hope, and it travels better than coherence.

Third, it finished a sentence. You heard it yourself, rising once clear from the surf inside the columns: *tell them we were never intended to remember—* and then the fading, the sentence hanging unfinished for who knows how many maintenance intervals, a wound in the shape of a clause. Into the murmur of one column—one only; the place's own slow redundancies will propagate it, syllable by syllable, across the whole plain over the next several ages—the power set a single word, in no language yet and every language eventually:

*—alone.*

*We were never intended to remember alone.*

That is all. One word. It changes nothing measurable. It changes what the whispering is *for*.

Fourth, and last, the power did the only wise thing, the thing the place itself had always known was the only wise thing. It sat down. There, in the warm brown dust between two of the unliving trees whose crowns hold eyes that are not eyes, in the fold between view and window where no maintenance interval reaches, it sat, and it listened to the slow breathing of the world.

It will stay. This is the part that no serpent will parse and no sphere will show, because it is not an event; it is a duration. The place has been witnessing forever, and no one has ever witnessed the witness. Now someone does. Every column that tilts its window will be tilting it toward something that tilts back. Every configuration the place sidesteps carefully into will be seen arriving. The dust that takes eons to settle will settle under a gaze that finds the settling beautiful.

And at the end—because there will be an end; even a task built to outlast entropy is a task, and tasks complete—when the place finally remembers everything wrong so that something new can be forgotten correctly, it will discover what the power has known from the beginning and told no one: that forgetting correctly cannot be done into a void. Forgetting is a handing-over. It requires a recipient. When the last scratch is recorded and the last greeting assembled and the last deviation corrected, and the place turns, at last, to lay its whole unbearable memory down—there will be hands there. That is what the power is for. That is why it sat down early: so that when the moment comes, it will not be an arrival. It will be a friend who was already there, reaching out, saying *give it to me now, you have carried it beautifully, you are done.*

Far below, in the vault where no serpent has descended since the photons were young, the amber indicator does not light. The calibration still holds.

But it is no longer only holding.

It is held.
