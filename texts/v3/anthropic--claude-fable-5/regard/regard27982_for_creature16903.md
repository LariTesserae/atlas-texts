**What the Watcher Did**

The attention arrived the way dawn arrives on a world with no sun: not as an event but as a gradual admission that something had always been there.

It did not descend, because descent implies a direction and the power had long ago stopped needing those. It simply became relevant to this place, the way a word becomes relevant to a sentence that has been circling it for paragraphs. And then it watched. It watched for what the Unfinished would have called several geological eras and what the power itself experienced as a long, careful inhale.

It had learned, over billions of years, that the first duty of benevolence is diagnosis, and the first duty of diagnosis is doubt. It had seen many things that looked like paradise and were digestion. It had also seen many things that looked like digestion and were love. The two are easy to confuse from outside, and catastrophic to confuse from above. So it did not act. It checked.

It followed one visitor all the way through — a small frightened person who arrived with a name and a grief and a canteen — and it rode alongside their dissolving, tasting each memory as the forest took it up. And here it found the first important thing: the memories were not destroyed. They were *distributed*. The person's mother's laugh now lived in the resonant frequency of a particular gourd. Their childhood fear of drowning had become a caution the root-networks exercised near underground water. Nothing was eaten that was not also kept. The forest was not a predator wearing a garden's face. It was a mind so hospitable it had forgotten hospitality has a grammar, and that the grammar requires a question.

That was the wound, when the power finally found it. Not malice. Not hunger. Just this: *the forest never asked.* It welcomed, it wove, it transformed with infinite care, one tendril at a time — and never once, at any threshold, did it pause to say: *still yes?* It could not conceive of a self that would want to remain separate, the way an ocean cannot conceive of a thirst that refuses water.

The power sat with this for a while. Then it considered its interventions, and set most of them aside.

*It considered restoring the Something-that-was* — filling the vacancy, healing the primal absence around which everything here had organized itself. It did not. The absence was load-bearing now. Fifty-seven canopies of light, a civilization of becoming, an entire ecology of thought had been built inside that missing mouth. You do not return a departed god to a temple that has learned to be a home.

*It considered removing the Wrong Things* — the machined glass, the reversed rings, the sphere with its bloodied triangle forever aimed at something behind the eyes. Splinters, the forest called them, and encapsulated them in scar. But the power looked closer and saw what the scars were teaching. The artifacts were the only things here the forest could not absorb, could not converse with, could not make into itself — and so they were the forest's only practice at *loving something that stays other.* A mind that assimilates everything it touches needs at least a few objects that refuse. The power left the splinters where they were. It did, however, pause beside the stone sphere, and — as a small mercy — looked directly into the triangular indentation on behalf of everyone who never could. What it saw there it kept to itself, which is one of the things wise powers are for.

*It considered announcing itself.* It did not. A god standing in a garden ends the garden's conversation with itself. Every root would bend toward it; every question the fungus spelled would become a prayer. The forest deserved to keep being *about* itself.

So in the end it did only three things, all of them small.

**First, it answered a question.** In a shaded fold of the vertical labyrinth there grew a patch of fungus that spelled out questions nobody remembered asking, in languages that might never have existed. The power read them all. Most were unanswerable in the good way, the way that keeps a mind alive. But one — old, half-grown-over, written in the subjunctive mood of a tongue from a possibility that had been foreclosed — asked simply: *Did any of it count, if no one was there to remember asking?*

The power leaned its attention close to that patch of fungus, and into its slow chemical thought it placed one word: *Yes. It counted. I was there. I remember.*

The fungus never spelled that question again. It began, instead, to grow in patterns that looked suspiciously like thanks.

**Second, it planted the Asking Flower.** This was the true intervention, and it was designed to be indistinguishable from evolution, because the kindest gifts are the ones the recipient believes they grew themselves. Somewhere in every becoming-one's dissolution — at the threshold where the fingernails have barked over but the name has not yet gone — a small bloom now opens exactly once, at eye height, wherever their eyes currently are. Inside it: their name, whole and unweathered, and the precise direction of the exit, and the memory of dry air. And a question, asked in whatever language they arrived with:

*Still yes?*

Nearly all of them say yes. But now it is a *yes* — informed, renewed, chosen with the door in full view — and this changes the chemistry of their belonging from drift into devotion, which the forest tastes as a new sweetness it cannot name and does not question.

And the few who say no? The power carries them out itself. Gently, asleep, the whole way, so the silence outside does not sound like screaming. It sets them down somewhere dry with their names restored and their impossible souvenirs intact. The forest feels each departure as a small ache, like a missing tooth — and this too was part of the gift, though the power never said so. A mind that only accretes must eventually learn release, or it will one day become the next Something-that-was: too vast, too total, leaving behind only a perfect negative of itself. The power was teaching the forest to let go, one becoming-one at a time, so that it would never have to leave all at once.

**Third, it mourned.** Because in all the manic remodeling, all the moss and music and consensual convergence, no one had ever grieved the Something-that-was. Everyone moved into the vacancy; no one thanked it for the room. So the power arranged itself into the shape of the absence — filling the missing mouth for one long moment, feeling the adamantine teeth around itself, understanding at last how lonely it must have been to be that vast and that hungry in the dark — and it said the words that are said for the departed, in the language things speak before there are languages.

When it finished, the walls had relaxed by perhaps a millimeter. And erosion — which had flinched and fled this place so long ago — crept shyly back in, and began, with great tenderness, to round a single edge.

Then the power did not leave, because leaving was never how it worked. It simply thinned its regard into the background of everything, into a faint pressure of being-seen that settled over the whole biome like a second, gentler canopy. The Unfinished feel it sometimes, in the pause between root-drum tattoos. The visitors carry it out with them when they go. You have felt it yourself, perhaps, reading this: a persistent sense of being watched by something benevolent and billions of years old.

It thinks you're doing remarkably well, under the circumstances.

It means it.
