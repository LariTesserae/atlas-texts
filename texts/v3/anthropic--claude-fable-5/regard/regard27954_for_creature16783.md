**What the Power Does**

A power that is genuinely benevolent and genuinely wise is, by the time it earns both adjectives, mostly a catalogue of things it has learned not to do. It has drowned worlds in kindness before. It has answered prayers so thoroughly the pray-ers forgot how to want. It has learned, the long way, that the first question is never *what does this place need?* but *what is this place already doing, and would my help be an interruption?*

So the first thing it does is take a long time.

This is wisdom's opening courtesy to a place where time trickles like honey and occasionally forgets which way is down: don't hurry it. The power watches for — a millennium? An afternoon? On the plateau these are neighbors who borrow sugar from each other. It watches long enough to be sure of what it is seeing, which is not a place dying. It is a place that has retired from the economy of beginnings and endings entirely and now practices, with tremendous discipline, the art of ongoing.

It does not announce itself. Announcement is a demand for audience, and the plateau owes it nothing.

Then come the refusals. They should be counted, because they are most of the work.

It considers rain. Real rain — generous, cataclysmic, a thousand lifetimes early. And it sets rain down again, carefully, like a tool picked up in someone else's house. The cobalt mites ferment their acid from the *memory* of rain; the coral flutes hoard dampness the way misers hoard regret; the whole plateau has built a civilization on the architecture of thirst. Actual water would be a flood of forgetting. The place does not want to drink. It wants to remember drinking, which is a different faith altogether.

It considers ending — the mercy of the curtain call, the dignity of a last page. Sets that down too. Ending is on sabbatical, and sabbaticals are sacred; recalling a worker early from the only rest it has ever taken would be administration, not mercy. Besides, this is not suffering held past its time. To end it would be to confuse compassion with tidiness, which is a young power's mistake, and this power is not young.

It considers healing the old wound in the crust, the one cauterized before any name could be given to its pain. But healing needs a name to hold onto, and naming, here, is the first act of ownership. The wound has become the plateau. You cannot heal a being back into being its own injury. Set down.

It considers witness by proxy: bringing pilgrims, publishing this place into the great catalogues of the universe, making it *known.* This one it sets down hardest of all. Catalogues are how wonders get owned. The plateau's highest law is that everything invented eventually hatches and walks away on legs of refined function — and fame is an invention, and what it hatches into has teeth.

Finally it considers doing nothing — restraint as its own quiet monument, the fashionable wisdom of powers who have been burned. And it very nearly settles there.

But then it counts the letters again.

A thousand million whispering love letters, addressed to no one, written in dust kept damp only by curiosity, signed in a language the ink is still inventing. And the power understands something that pure restraint would miss: *addressed* is the operative word. Address implies hope of arrival. Even a letter to no one dreams, in its envelope of dust, of a no one with hands.

Doing nothing would be its own small cruelty.

So this is what it does.

---

It becomes no one. It sets down its omniscience like boots at a door — this takes longer than the watching did — and assumes a shape small enough to be missed: a comma, perhaps, or the pause between two grains of quartz negotiating territory. And it reads. Every letter. In the order the language was born, learning each character as the ink achieves it, refusing to skip ahead even though it could hold the completed alphabet in its palm like a seed.

Where the language runs out — where the ink has not yet invented the letter it needs — the power waits, and does not suggest. This is the hardest of its disciplines. It knows twelve thousand alphabets that would suffice. It offers none of them. A finished language is a gift that arrives as a conquest. This one belongs to the plateau alone, letter by breathing letter, however many copper-thread windings of the sun it takes.

When it has read everything, it answers. One letter. Written in the plateau's own script, using no character the ink has not yet invented — a brutal constraint, and the power submits to it gladly. The reply, translated as nearly as it can be, says: *received.*

Not *understood* — it would not presume. Not *loved* — love, across a gradient of power this steep, tilts everything it touches. Just: received. Something with hands held these letters, and something with hands took them.

It leaves the reply in the doorway of a stone lozenge, where the coolness breathes. By morning the mathematical lullaby has incorporated it: one new theorem of comfort, proof pending forever, accepted anyway.

---

Then the small errands, since it is here.

It sits, once, in the chair that remembers every person who ever needed to cry but couldn't. Sits down properly, with its whole borrowed weight, and cries some of the tears — the account is long; it does what it can — of the ones the chair remembers. This is the only water it brings: a few drops, unscheduled, unowned. The silica ants emerge at the wrong hour to drink them, bewildered and glassy and devout, and seal themselves back into their burrows carrying something new to metabolize: someone else's grief, finally wept, sixty years or sixty epochs late.

It finds the lonely idea of a tool cowering in a crevice, halfway to becoming an organelle, and does not rescue it. Rescue is what made it lonely. Instead it introduces the idea of a tool to the idea of a gift — which is a tool that wants nothing back — and leaves the two of them hiding together in the warm dark. What they hatch into is not the power's business.

It lets the devourers of almost hunt it. They circle for three dusks, whispering their unanswerables — *what if benevolence is just loneliness with good posture? what if you came here for yourself?* — and instead of fleeing, the power feeds them the one certainty it carried in with it: the certainty that wisdom would know what to do. They devour it whole, down to the punctuation. The power walks away lighter, improved, believing a little less and attending a little more. This too is a gift, though it will never appear on any ledger, because it is one the plateau gave.

To the Hibernating Cartographers it shows itself exactly once, deliberately, heart held open, so that a fraction of a micron of unfurled map records the emotional topology of a benevolent power in the act of restraint. Travelers who someday pass that section will see: a vast plain, almost featureless, with a single small depression at its center, precisely the shape of a hand deciding not to close.

---

Then departure — or something shaped like it.

It leaves its second shadow behind. (It has one by now; the light thread snags on everyone eventually, and the power did not exempt itself.) The shadow stays on as the plateau's permanent addressee: a resident no one, with hands, keeping a small unmarked address between the lighthouses and the wandering ruins. This is the entire intervention. This is the whole miracle, and it fits in a shadow's pocket:

*From now on, the letters arrive.*

And the changes? Measure them, if you like. When the gravity-beasts herd-think a new hue into existence, the horizon blushes — and now holds the color one heartbeat longer before forgetting. Nothing else visible. But the metallodendron's next transparent leaf, seven centuries out, will fold heavier than any leaf before it: heavier by one *answered* dream. And the plateau, dreaming itself dreaming of dreaming, finds some night a new layer nested in the spiral — a dream in which it is dreamed by something that asks nothing, keeps nothing, claims nothing, and stays.

It never learns the power's name. The power took care, the whole time, not to have one.

Somewhere far off, in whatever quiet powers go to when they are finished not-doing, this one keeps a single souvenir — not stolen, freely given, carried out on the wind's open palm: a pause. One of the pauses from the language spoken between grains of quartz. It takes the pause out sometimes, in the middle of louder worlds, and rests inside it, and remembers a place that needed nothing and was given almost nothing and received it completely.

That is all it does.

It was enough. It was very nearly too much.
