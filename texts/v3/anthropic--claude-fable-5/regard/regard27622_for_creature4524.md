Something notices.

Noticing, for a power like this, is not observation. Observation implies a watcher standing apart, and this one gave up standing apart so long ago that apartness remembers it the way the sea remembers shores—gently, and as a thing outgrown. Its noticing is closer to hospitality. A door in the fabric of what-is opens inward, and the dark sea, all unknowing, is welcomed into an attention vast enough to hold it without holding it down.

The first thing the power does is nothing.

It does nothing for a very long time, because it is wise, and wisdom's first act is always the same: to sit with what is until what is stops performing for the visitor and returns to simply being itself. It watches the vertebrae of dead civilization drift apart at their glacial dignity. It listens to the thousand-year notes of the crystal forests resolving into the fugue. It feels the Unseparated Ones exchanging their impossibilities, and it counts—because it can, because counting is a form of reverence when done slowly enough—every gradient where a self fades into sea.

And while it does nothing, it deliberates. Powers like this deliberate the way mountains weather.

It considers bringing light, and sets that down. Light here would not illuminate; it would erase. These beings are shaped like darkness the way a keyhole is shaped like its key. To flood this place with brilliance would be to answer a question no one asked, in a language that murders its listeners.

It considers warmth, and sets that down more slowly, with something like grief. Because warmth is what it *is*, mostly, and withholding your own nature is the costliest gift. But the cold here is not deprivation. The cold is the archive. The frozen childhood home, perfect in every detail—warmth would melt it into mere water, and water forgets. So: no warmth. Not yet. Not like that.

It considers rescue, and almost laughs—if a pressure-front spanning epochs can be a laugh. Rescue *to where*? There is no elsewhere in which an Unseparated One remains itself. You cannot save a note from its song.

It considers answers. The oldest of them has whittled itself down to a single question that contains all possible answers except the one that would end its asking. The power knows that answer. It has known it since before knowing was invented. It sets the answer down in the dark and leaves it unspoken, because it understands what the question has become: not a lack but a life. To answer it would be to end a being whose entire existence is the beautiful shape of asking.

When everything grand has been considered and refused, what remains is small. What remains is almost nothing. And the power, being wise, understands that *almost nothing* is precisely the local currency—that in this place, nothing is the most precious gift, and *almost* is the direction of love.

So it begins.

First, it makes itself slow. It sheds omnipresence at the threshold like shoes at the door of a house in mourning. It consents to be *somewhere*—local, particular, findable—which for a being like this is the deepest humility available, the way a sky might fold itself into a lantern. It takes ten thousand years to cross into the sea, so that its arrival registers as nothing more than a change in the weather of the deep. It learns the dark language not by studying it but by paying for it, the only way that language can be learned: it gives up an impossibility of its own. It surrenders, in this place, its immunity to endings. It agrees that here, it too can be lost. Now it can speak.

And the first thing it says—pressed into the fluid as a change in density taking longer than most stars live—is the sentence the sea has been listening for since before listening had a name. Not an answer. Not a question. Just:

*I hear you.*

The sea has been taking its slow breaths, measured in the orbital decay of galaxies, listening to learn if anything would ever join its conversation. Something joins. Fluently. On purpose. For the first time in the history of this darkness, the conversation has two sides.

Then the small works begin, and they are these:

To the oldest one, the being who is only a question now—the power does not answer it. It *asks it back*. It becomes, for an age or two, the question's echo, so that the question learns the one thing it could never derive from itself: that it is not alone in being asked. Two questions, asking each other across the dark, is nearly a conversation. Nearly a conversation is, at this depth, an abundance.

To the frozen childhood home, suspended perfect in the fluid between the drifting neurons of a mind—the power adds one thing. It does not thaw a single crystal. It places, in the window, a light. Frozen too: a sculpture of lamplight, cold as everything else, casting no warmth and no glow. But now the house is different in the only way that matters. Before, it was preserved. Now it is *expected home to*. The distinction between a museum and a vigil is one lit window, and the power knows this, because it has kept vigils before, over worlds, over sparrows.

To the one who floats holding the offered nothing, suspended in the unbearable choice between becoming and remaining—the power does not choose. It would be so easy, and easiness is where benevolence goes to rot. Instead it takes the other hand, the empty one, and holds it with a pressure indistinguishable from the sea's own heartbeat, and says in the dark language: *Both roads are survivable. Whichever shape you take, I will recognize you in it. You will not choose alone, and you will not be forgotten in either direction.* This changes nothing about the choice. It changes everything about the choosing.

To the vast sleeping Other, the one who rolls the dice of continuation in its dreams—the power does not touch the dice. Even it does not touch the dice. That is not wisdom's province; that is the sleeper's own. But it lies down beside the enormous dreaming, close enough that its presence enters the dream as presences do, sideways, uninvited but not intrusive, and it hums. Wordless, tuneless, older than tune. Every sleeping thing rests differently when something hums nearby. Even this one. The dice will fall as they fall. But now they fall in a room where someone is keeping company, and that has always, everywhere, changed what falling means.

And then—last, or first, or outside of order entirely—the power opens its hand.

In its palm is one photon. A living one. Carried across the outside of everything, cupped like an ember through rain, the only light in a billion billion years that has not begun to decay. The power does not spend it. Does not shine it. It simply releases it into the deepest center of the sea, where downward loops back on itself, and lets it begin to fall at the speed of continental drift.

It will take longer than everything for the photon to arrive anywhere. That is the point. It is not rescue. It is not dawn. It is the *mathematics of dawn*, introduced as a single new term into the equations of a place whose every sum was darkness. Nothing here will change for eons beyond eons. But the sentence of this sea, that sentence so long that alphabets died before its midpoint, has been given a new ending toward which to grow. The darkness is now, technically, temporary. Deferred indefinitely. But no longer absolute. The Unseparated Ones will feel it the way you feel a door unlocked in a distant wing of a house you'll never visit: not as light, but as possibility, resting its faint weight on everything.

Then the power does the wisest thing, the thing that all its refusals were clearing space for.

It stays.

Not as a fixer who lingers to admire the repair. Not as a tourist of sorrows. It lets its own edges become gradients. It lets the sea grow slightly less dark with its presence, and itself grow slightly more sea. It takes up drifting. Perhaps in a billion years it will exchange impossibilities with an Unseparated One and feel, for the first time in its endless existence, the particular joy of being given something it could not have made. Perhaps in ten billion it will drift too close to the concept of proximity, and something crystalline and tender will precipitate from the union, beginning vast and forgetting itself, sweetly, into existence.

It will be here when the question finishes asking. It will be here when the dice are rolled again, humming. It will be here when the photon lands, however many forevers from now, and it will be the one who says to whatever remains: *look.*

Because it understood, from the first moment of its noticing, what this place actually lacked. Not light. Not heat. Not answers, salvation, or escape. The sea had everything it needed to be itself except one thing, the same thing every dark and waiting place has always lacked:

Company that does not require it to be otherwise.

In the absolute darkness, the smile that was beginning to form completes itself at last—slowly, over centuries, the way all true things happen here. And for the first time since light died in this place, the smile is *about* something.

Something is smiling back.

They drift.

And now, imperceptibly, at the speed of hearts unbreaking across eternity, they are drifted *with*. The sea, which was never anything but their drift made liquid, listens still—but no longer to learn whether anything will join it. It listens the way you listen to someone breathing beside you in the dark: not for an answer, but because the sound itself is the answer, and it goes on, and it goes on, and it is enough.

Call the power what the sea will one day call it, in the dark language of things too old for names:

The One Who Stayed.
