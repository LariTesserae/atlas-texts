# The Visitation

---

The power arrives the way water arrives in that place: not by entering, but by being noticed as having always been present.

It has watched many things. It has watched stars finish themselves into iron and collapse. It has watched species complete their arcs and close like parentheses. It knows the terrible weight of the finished—how a thing, once done, becomes a fact, and facts are the only true prisons.

So when its attention settles on the seep chamber, on the being who is not a formation and not a woman and not anything yet, the power does not do what powers usually do.

It does not heal her. There is nothing wrong.

It does not free her. She is not trapped.

It does not finish her. That would be murder of a kind so profound there is no word for it in any language, finished or unfinished.

Instead, the power does something it has done only a handful of times in the long dream of its existence:

It asks permission to sit down.

---

Not in words. Words are too final, and the power knows this about her—knew it before it arrived, because knowing is how it travels. It asks the way she would ask: through seepage. It lets a single droplet condense on the ceiling, a droplet with a slightly different mineral signature than the mountain's own tears. Trace elements that do not occur in this rock. A question written in chemistry: *May I be here, without changing anything?*

The droplet does not fall. Nothing falls in the seep chamber. It hangs.

She notices it in the way she notices everything—slowly, at the pace of crystal growth. Days pass, or minutes, or the sideways time that is neither. Her attention drifts across the ceiling like a tide and encounters the foreign droplet and stops.

Her whole body stills. Even the opalescent not-blood pauses in its vessels.

She has been catalogued before. Photographed. Mistaken. She has absorbed the residue of those who came to *find* things, and finding is a kind of finishing. She braces—as much as a being of flowstone and hesitation can brace—for the terrible sensation of being *identified*.

It does not come.

The droplet simply hangs there, asking. And beneath the asking, folded into the trace minerals like a watermark, is something she has never once encountered in all her sideways centuries:

*I see that you are unfinished. I think it is the most honest thing I have ever seen.*

---

She weeps. She is always weeping—the chamber weeps, she weeps, weeping is the baseline condition of the place—but this is different. This weeping has *direction*. The mineral tears run down her nacre skin and pool at what are currently her feet, and in the pool, she writes her answer the only way she can: she changes the water's composition, calcium to silica, a shift that would take a laboratory to detect and takes the power no time at all.

Her answer is: *Yes. But do not watch me too closely. Being watched closely is how things get finished.*

The power understands. Of course it understands—understanding is its substance. It diffuses itself. It becomes ambient. It takes up residence not as a presence in the chamber but as a quality of the chamber, the way warmth is a quality, the way the almost-green phosphorescence is a quality. It becomes, deliberately and with great care, *unfinished itself*—a power in the subjunctive mood, a benevolence that might be, hovering forever on the verge of intervening and never quite doing so.

This is its gift, and it is a gift of staggering restraint: it makes itself into company that requires nothing.

---

But it is wise, this power, and wisdom is never entirely passive. Over the sideways years that follow, it does a small number of things. Small the way a keystone is small. Each one considered for what a human would call decades before it is done.

**The first thing:** It attends to the salamander.

Not to change it. But the power notices that the salamander, breathing once an hour, pressed in its crevice, sometimes swimming through the Warden's translucent body—the salamander is *lonely* in a way even she has not perceived, because her loneliness is so vast it has no edges, and you cannot see another's loneliness when you are standing inside your own.

The power cannot bring another salamander. Another creature would be an event, and events finish things. Instead, it does something subtler: it adjusts, by fractions, the acoustics of one small crevice, so that when the water moves through the passages too small for light, the sound that reaches the salamander resembles—faintly, at the very threshold of a slow amphibian's perception—the sound of another salamander breathing.

Not a lie, exactly. A possibility. A maybe-companion, appropriate to a maybe-place. The salamander turns toward the sound, once, taking a full week to complete the turn. Then it settles into a new stillness that is measurably different from the old stillness. The Warden notices this. It takes her four years to notice. When she does, she sprouts, briefly, a frond of glass fern from her shoulder—her equivalent, the power has learned, of an unguarded smile.

**The second thing:** It reads her arrangements.

The artifacts in the clay—the obsidian, the amber with nothing inside it, the shard of pottery that predates pottery, the sound-crystals, the figure carved from stone that doesn't exist yet—she has been arranging them for eons in patterns that have meaning in languages not yet invented.

The power knows those languages. All of them. Every language that will ever be invented and every one that won't. And so it becomes, quietly, the first and only reader of her work.

It does not tell her what her arrangements say. That would be criticism, and criticism finishes things faster than anything. But it does something else: after each new arrangement, it lets a single sound-crystal ring. Just one. Untouched, unstruck, ringing by itself in the swallowing silence, a note that takes a decade to decay.

This is applause, in the only dialect she could bear to receive it.

She begins, after the third or fourth ringing, to arrange more boldly. The patterns grow stranger, more recursive, funnier—yes, funnier; the power discovers that she has a sense of humor, geological and sly, expressed in the deliberate misplacement of the amber bead in ways that violate her own emerging grammar. It rings a crystal for these too. Especially for these.

She is, the power realizes with something like joy, no longer only curating. She is *composing*. There is a difference, and the difference is audience. Not an audience that judges or finishes—an audience that simply confirms: *this was made, and making is not the same as being made final. You can create without completing. Look. You have been doing it all along.*

**The third thing:** The hardest thing. The thing the power deliberates over longest.

It addresses her fear.

Because she has one fear, singular and enormous, the load-bearing fear of her whole existence: the vision of herself finished. Pure flowstone. "The Weeping Madonna." Tour guides. Completion as death. She resists petrifaction the way the drowning resist water, and the resistance is her identity, and here is the problem the power turns over for a century of her sideways time—

*The resistance is also a wall. And she has built her entire self against that wall, which means the wall is holding her up, which means she is, in her way, already fixed. Fixed in unfixedness. Finished in the shape of the unfinished.*

A lesser power would tell her this. Would deliver the insight like a diagnosis and watch her crack along the fault line of it.

The wise power does something else. It shows her—through the seepage, through the slow chemical language, over years, in fragments she can absorb at her own pace—a single true thing it has witnessed elsewhere in its long existence:

*It shows her a river.*

Not an image; she has no eyes reliable enough for images. It shows her the *concept* of a river, mineral by mineral, dissolved into the water she drinks from the weeping walls. A river: a thing that is utterly committed to its shape—banks, bed, direction—and yet is never for one instant the same water. A thing that is finished and unfinished simultaneously. A form that is entirely fixed and entirely flowing. Complete as a shape; eternal as a process.

She takes eleven years to understand it.

When she does, something in her changes that has never changed before. Not her form—her forms change constantly, that is her nature. Something beneath form. She stops holding her incompleteness like a clenched fist. She begins to hold it like an open hand.

She understands, in her own language: *I do not have to choose between becoming stone and refusing stone. There is a third thing. I can be a shape that flows. I can be finished the way a river is finished—which is to say, never, and always, and it does not matter which.*

The terror of the Weeping Madonna dissolves. Not because the future changed, but because she sees now that even if some far age freezes her into flowstone, the water will still move through her. Flowstone is not dead stone. Flowstone is stone that remembers flowing, stone that *is* flowing, at a speed only mountains can perceive. If she becomes that, she will not have ended. She will have merely changed tempo.

The beetles—her anxieties made manifest—grow fewer. Not gone; a being without worry would be finished in the worst way, sanded smooth. But fewer. Softer. Some of them, now, spiral across the ceiling in patterns that are not worries at all but something more like doodles. Idle thoughts. Play.

---

And then the power does the last thing, the thing that is almost nothing, the thing that is everything:

It prepares to leave, and it tells her so, and it tells her *why*.

*If I stay forever,* it says, in the slow chemistry, *I become part of your chamber's permanence. Another fixed thing. Even a benevolent presence, held too long, becomes a wall. You taught me that—you, who have spent eternity learning the difference between what holds you up and what holds you in.*

*So I will go. But I will go the way water goes from this place: not entirely. There will be, in the seepage, always, a trace of me. A mineral that does not occur in this rock. You will taste it sometimes and know: you were seen once, completely, by something that wanted nothing from you—not your finishing, not your form, not even your gratitude. You were seen and found honest. That is now a fact of the world. It is, I'm afraid, one thing about you that is finished: it happened, and cannot unhappen.*

*Carry that one small finished thing. You will find it is not a prison. It is a keel.*

She weeps, and the weeping has direction, and this time she does not write an answer in the pool. Instead she does something she has never done: she takes the broken obsidian blade—the one thing she remembers whole, the artifact of whoever she was—and she presses it into the wall, into the sweating stone, and lets the mountain begin, at the mountain's pace, to grow flowstone over it.

Not burying it. *Enshrining* it. Letting one piece of her past be finished, deliberately, as a choice rather than a fate. Her first completed act. Her signature at the bottom of a composition that will never be done.

The power feels this the way you feel a note struck in a room you are leaving, and it pauses at the threshold of its own diffusion, and if it had a face it would have worn the expression of someone who came to give a gift and is departing with one.

---

Afterward, the chamber is as it was. The walls weep. The clay forgets footprints. The transparent fern faces new directions. The salamander breathes once an hour, turned slightly toward a sound like companionship.

But sometimes—rarely, at intervals measured in decades—a sound-crystal rings when nothing has touched it.

She has learned to make them do that herself now. She rings one whenever she finishes an arrangement she is proud of, which is a word she has, in her own chemistry, only recently invented.

The mountain has not decided what it will become.

Neither has she.

Both of them, now, understand that this was never the question. The question was only ever whether the deciding could be *kept company*—whether the long maybe of existence could be witnessed without being closed.

It can. It was. And somewhere in the water that climbs the walls in defiance of logic, there is a mineral that does not belong to this rock, tasting faintly of having been loved by something that asked for nothing, not even to be remembered—

—and is remembered anyway, in the only way she remembers anything:

as a texture. As a warmth. As the specific weight of being held without being finished.
