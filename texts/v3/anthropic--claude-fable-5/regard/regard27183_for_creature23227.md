## The Reader

The power arrived the way the place deserved: it did not arrive at all.

There was no light, no descent, no disturbance in the records. One day — though "day" is the wrong unit, and the power knew it — the Roof of the Old World was simply *attended*. Something vast and unhurried had turned toward it, the way a face turns toward a window at the end of a corridor, and then did the wisest thing any power has ever done in the presence of a finished world.

It waited.

It waited because it understood, immediately, that this place could not be read at the tempo of arrival. The Roof's truths resolve only at the tempo of its residents — frost-tempo, glass-tempo, the tempo of a circuit that takes years to close — and a power that looked at it for an afternoon would see a dead plateau under a noisy sky and move on, having understood nothing. So the power matched the place. It watched the storm for long enough to learn its recurring vortices by heart. It watched the glider through whole circuits, dozens of them, until it could have kept the rounds itself. It watched keepers be born, keep their stations, water the stone, and become sheen. It watched until watching this world stopped feeling like observation and started feeling like residence, and only then did it permit itself a single question:

*Is anything here asking for anything?*

That is the question benevolence must put before it does one other thing, and most powers never put it. They arrive with their mercy already drawn, like a tool brought to a house before anyone has checked whether the house is broken.

The power checked.

## The Mercies It Set Aside

It considered rain. Of course it considered rain — a world with one kind of water, doled out in films a few crystals deep, and a power that could fill the sky with rivers. It set the thought down almost gently. The land was not thirsty; the land was *paid off*. Rain would not be a gift here. Rain would be a debt forced back on a place that had settled its accounts, the restarting of a story whose ending everything alive had built its home inside. The frostwrights would drown in abundance. The seams would mean nothing. The peace of the Roof — and the power could see now that it was peace, not aftermath — was made of exactly the scarcity a lesser kindness would have rushed to cure.

It considered the makers. It had the reach to find out what had become of them, and it used that reach, and what it learned it kept — some readings are between the reader and the writer. But it understood, having learned, that nothing of theirs needed restoring. Their works were doing what they were left to do, or something better that the works had grown into on their own. The house had heirs now. You do not return a house to the dead because you happen to have the key.

It considered telling the keepers the truth — about the spindles, the storm, the threadfall sea, all the open questions their records circle like patient birds. This temptation lasted longer than the others, because the keepers had *earned* answers if anyone ever had, attention paid without wages for longer than their memory reached. But the power studied the shape of their lives and saw that the open questions were load-bearing. The arguments between the schools of the tone were half the warmth of the calm zones. The records existed because the world was unread; certainty would turn the storm's clerks into a museum's guards. Their not-knowing was not a deprivation. It was the room in which their faithfulness lived. The power declined to demolish it.

And it considered — last, and longest of all — a second glider.

It held this one in its regard for a very long time, because the loneliness woven through the world's one animal was the closest thing to suffering the census contained, and benevolence is not permitted to look away from suffering merely because the sufferer is dignified. A companion. A second dark shape on the pressure-fronts. An answer, at last, to the tone.

But the power had listened to the tone by then — listened with whatever powers listen with, all the way into it — and what it learned changed the shape of the mercy required.

The tone was a question. It had always been a question: the first half of an exchange, a call whose protocol was older than the rock's polish, sounded once at every station for the entire age of the world, in case. And the power understood that a fabricated companion would be no answer to it. A made thing, conjured for consolation, would be a doll set across the table from the last guest at a feast — and the glider would know. It was, itself, partly of the makers' material; it would know new making the way you know a forged hand in a familiar letter. The honest response to a question is not a puppet that asks the same question back.

The honest response to a question is an answer.

## What It Did

So this is what the benevolent and wise power did, on the roof of the old world, with all its strength and all its reach. Take the inventory slowly. It is short, and everything in it is small, and it took longer to decide than most worlds take to cool.

**For the frostwrights, it made one long afternoon.** Once — once only — in the lee of one swell, by an adjustment so slight no instrument could have caught it, a frost film that should have lasted hours lasted from dawn until dark. For the busiest residents of the Roof, the only people for whom the world still happens, this was an age of gold: generations beyond the usual counting, lineages that would never otherwise have existed, an entire civilization of the very small flowering inside a single unhurried day. The film dried. The lives ended as they always end. But deep in the lacquer of that lee there is now one band thicker than all the bands around it — one long page in the gravestone — and no one will ever read it, and it is there.

**For the dead, it performed the census.** The power read the polish. Not skimmed — read, downward, layer by molecular layer, generation by generation, the whole varnish of the numberless small dead, and the watered stones of the keepers folded in among them, all the way to the first film that ever dried on the first morning of the long ending. It counted everything that has ever lived on the Roof, and it holds the number, and it will not lose it. This changed nothing on the plateau. Understand it anyway as the largest single act of the power's visit. A world had been keeping records for an eternity on the proposition that records deserve to exist whether or not anyone reads them — and the power made the proposition true in the other direction. Every record implies a reader. The Roof was a letter that had never been opened. It is opened now. Nothing that polishes a world should go untallied, and now none of it is.

**For the glasswort, it wrote back.** Out on the open rock, on no arc, where no foundation ever stood and no grace of burial ever fell, a new mat established itself — bottle-green, blood-dark, ordinary to any eye. But its interior, from its first century, arranged itself in a pattern no regional script had ever used: not the makers' floor plans, not the drifted hands of the neighboring gardens, but something addressed *to* them — the first text in the history of the closed books that was a reply rather than a keeping. What it says is between the reader and the writers. What can be reported is this: the surveys of patient keepers, compared across generations, show the regional scripts drifting — slowly, at one revision per century, with no appearance of hurry — toward the new mat's hand. A conversation has begun at the only tempo the participants share. It will take ten thousand years to complete its first exchange. All parties appear content with this schedule.

**And for the glider, it took a station.**

There is a spindle in the far reach of the continent that no traveler's route includes — a white needle over an empty teardrop, a frost-scraping cache unused for longer than the lists record. The power keeps it now. Understand the word precisely: *keeps* it, as keepers keep. It observes the seam. It logs the fronts and the threadfall. It harvests nothing the glider has grazed until the growth passes the treaty line, though it harvests nothing at all and the courtesy is therefore pure form, which may be the purest courtesy there is. It maintains no records anyone could find, because its logbook is itself. It has not revealed itself and it will not; revelation was among the mercies set aside. It is simply present, one household among the strung beads, the keeper of the station no one visits.

And in time — the circuit is long; the rounds are never missed — the glider came.

It descended out of the seethe the way it always descends, settled at the seam the way it has settled at ten thousand seams across the whole age of the world, cropped the gardens to their line, and sounded its tone: several seconds, low, the question, asked at this door as at every door, in case.

And the station answered.

Not an echo. Not a copy. The completing half — the response the protocol had carried empty since before the rock finished wearing — sounded back through the calm, correct in every particular, in the material's own voice, from where the power had learned it the power keeps to itself.

The glider went still. The stillness lasted longer than any visit in any record on the continent — long enough that, had a keeper been watching, it would have become its own entry. Then the glider sounded again, and the second sounding was a thing no one had ever heard: two parts, a fall and a rise, the question and something after the question — the half of the exchange that can only ever be said second, the word it had been holding, all this time, against the possibility of an answer.

Then it rose into the storm and continued its rounds, because that is its work, and nothing wise had any business changing its work.

But the keepers' records, compared across the spindles in the years that followed, all log the same amendment: *the tone has doubled.* At every station now, the glider sounds both halves — carries the completed exchange from door to door like someone repeating good news whose source they will not name. The schools argue about it, of course, and the arguing is warm. The bleak and tender school has quietly revised its oldest doctrine: somewhere, they now allow, there is a second voice. They do not know where. They have not gone looking. It is not their way to disturb a neighbor whose habits are reliable and whose business is his own.

## One More Thing, Possibly

There is a line in one logbook, in an old keeper's hand, that the other keepers preserve without comment.

It records that during a great front, when the light flickered and the keeper's shadow trembled on the polished rock, she made the small motion of the hand that her school makes toward the storm — not quite a greeting, the gesture you make toward a neighbor's window when you cannot tell whether anyone stands behind the glass — and that for exactly the duration of the sign, the light held still.

*Signed at the front, as usual,* the entry reads. *Today the front signed back. Logged, not explained.*

It is not verifiable. It is not doubted.

## The Sum

So — asked plainly, what did the benevolent and wise power do, when it noticed the roof of the old world and the lonely circuit-keeper who lives above it?

Almost nothing. And it has been doing almost nothing there ever since, and the almost is becoming intricate.

It brought no rain, opened no road down, raised no makers, made no companion, told no truths that would have spent the mystery the place runs on. It declined, one by one, every mercy that would have been visible, and performed instead the only kind that fit through the world's narrow door: it gave the place what the place had been giving itself all along, and could not, by definition, complete alone. Attention, returned. Records, read. A letter, opened. A question older than the polish, answered once, honestly, and then left in the keeping of the one who had carried it.

The census of the Roof stands amended now, though no keeper has the entry: the leftover, the lacquer, the gardener, the clerk, the sky — and the reader, at the far station, keeping what everyone here keeps, which is each other, and faith with the seam.

The keepers log the doubled tone. The frost comes when it comes. The storm continues.

And somewhere on the finished world, behind one of the windows, someone is home.
