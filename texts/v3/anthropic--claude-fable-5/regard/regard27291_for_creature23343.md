**What Came to the Rim**

Powers of that kind do not find places like this by looking. They find them by audit. Somewhere in the long ledgers of the world's attention there are entries that don't balance — sites that receive almost no notice and return almost no noise, and yet are dense, packed, *attended* — and a wise power, going over the books, will pause at such an entry the way a keeper pauses at a gauge that has surfaced face-up once too often for chance. The Turning Ground had been quiet for so long, and so thoroughly, that its quiet had acquired a shape. The shape drew the audit. The audit drew the power.

It came as the kind of figure the place could absorb: someone in a grey coat, of no particular age, with the unhurried posture of a retired engineer come back to look at a pump he once signed off on. It walked the rim in ten minutes, the way everyone does, and then it sat down on the berm with its collar up against the damp — having no need of a collar, but observing the dress code — and it did the first wise thing, which was nothing, for nine years.

This was not restraint. It was arithmetic. To a being that turns at one revolution per season, a visitor who stays an afternoon is weather, and a visitor who stays a year is a season of weather. Nine years was the minimum interval at which the power could be said to be *present* at all, in local terms, rather than merely passing through at terrible speed. So it sat, at the tempo of the address, and watched, and let the basin's facts assemble themselves at their own pace, the way the basin assembles everything.

What it confirmed, mostly, was what the patient already knew about itself.

That this was not a wound. The power had healed wounds in the earth before — places that wept, places that repeated their hurt on a loop like a man telling the same story at every meal — and it knew the texture of damage, and this was not it. The turning had no grievance in it. It was housekeeping.

That this was not a prisoner. The power put its attention down through the berm, gently, the way you'd press a thumb against a wall to feel for studs, and found no strain against the ring, no testing of the perimeter, none of the long bruises that captivity leaves on a thing's edges. The berm was honored from inside the way a margin is honored by a careful hand writing. The outline was part of the work.

That the pale things were what the rim-sitters had half-guessed: not residents but gestures. Fingertips. The being surfacing the smallest part of itself at the boundary, where the heron stood — and the heron's lease was exactly as it appeared, the oldest treaty in the district, kept on both sides without a single recorded violation. The power noted, with the particular pleasure the wise take in such things, that the basin's one export and its one generosity were the same act.

And that the duet with the pump was a duet. The power listened to the motor's held note and the basin's perfect liter-for-liter reply for three of its nine sitting years before it was sure, and then it was sure: this was not stalemate. Stalemate has resentment in it somewhere, a coiled waiting-to-win. This was two parties who had long ago stopped keeping score and kept playing.

Then, having understood, the power did what wise powers chiefly do, which is decline things. It went down its own inventory of possible kindnesses and turned them over one by one, and put nearly all of them back.

It declined to free the being, there being no cage.

It declined to heal the land, there being no wound.

It declined to give the being eyes, or a voice, or a face. This temptation was real — the power was, by nature, a conversationalist, and the silence cost it something — but it had seen what the being's whole surface was. The being had a hand, and was a hand, and a hand that has spent centuries learning the complete grammar of touch does not need eyes. Eyes would have been a demotion: the exchange of intimacy for distance, the trading of *holding* for mere *seeing*. The power had watched too many beings make that trade. It would not impose it on one of the few who'd been spared.

It declined — and this was the hard one, the one it carried around the rim for a full winter — to make a peer. It could have done it. There was a basin two valleys over with the right clay and the right water, and the power could have poured something into it, a seed of slow turning, and in a thousand years the Turning Ground would have had a neighbor of its own kind. The power held this plan a long time, because it could not determine the one fact the plan depended on: whether the being was lonely. The contentment looked real. The thoroughness, the courtesy, the polite singular syllable — none of it had the flavor of a thing enduring. But the power was wise enough to know that wisdom has a blind spot exactly the shape of other beings' hearts, and that a creature whose every expression takes six hours to surface might be carrying something that takes six centuries. It could not tell. So it did not act on a guess. It found, instead, a gift that was harmless under either hypothesis — and we will come to that.

It declined to make the place known, or to translate it. The basin resisted story like a footprint, and the power respected the resistance. But neither did it seal the place. There was a thin economy here — the rare whisperers, the stones they left, the inventory that occasionally rounded up — and the wise do not abolish economies they did not design. So the power merely bent the world's attention a few degrees. The road that had been coming went elsewhere, for reasons the planners found in their own paperwork. The maps stayed vague in a way no one noticed was maintained. The place became easy to forget but never impossible to find, which is the correct setting for a sanctuary, and the whisperers kept coming, one or two a year, and kept leaving stones.

It declined to make the duet eternal. This temptation it dismissed fastest of all, and was proudest of dismissing. The motor would die someday — the line over the hills was two old poles and luck — and the power's first instinct had been to underwrite the whole arrangement forever, a guaranteed note, a subsidized eternity. But a note held forever is not music; it is tinnitus. Everything should be allowed to end. So the power did only this: one night it walked to the nearer pole and put its hand on the rotten foot of it and made it sound, not immortal, merely sound — because everything should be allowed to end, but nothing should be interrupted. The duet would close someday on its own terms, at the end of a phrase, and not in a storm, mid-note, by accident.

That was the ledger of refusals. What remained — what it actually did — was small, and went in order.

First, it introduced itself, in the only protocol the place recognized. It took off one grey glove and laid it on the clay inside the rim, and went away for six weeks, and came back, and found the glove surfaced near the eastern mound, palm-up, the fingers socketed with clay. It had been catalogued. The introduction was complete. The power put the glove back on, clay and all, and wore it that way thereafter, which among certain old things is the equivalent of keeping a letter.

Second, it answered the syllable. It stood one full grey day on the southern rim, and in the sixth hour the bubble came up — *gluck*, singular, polite, unrepeated — and the power replied with one syllable of its own, equally singular, equally polite, in no language, and then said nothing further for a year. At this address, that is how you show you were listening.

Third, the gift. The power had gone looking, in the long inventory of things it could reach, for something suited to a being whose whole mode of knowing was centuries of touch, and it had found it far from here: a fist of nickel-iron that had fallen out of the sky before the works, before the berm, before the language the works were planned in. Iron — the being's familiar study, its lifelong subject — but iron that had never been worked, never been bolted or cast or pitted by these particular rains. Iron with a different counsel. And inside it, where only the patient could go, the long crystalline lattice that such iron carries — a structure that cannot be seen from outside and cannot be read quickly, a text that yields only to the kind of tasting the being did over centuries. A letter, in the recipient's own language, that would take a thousand years to read. The power laid it at the inner edge of the berm, at the boundary where things are offered, reversing for once the direction of offering, and walked away without watching.

The meteorite went under within the week. It has never come back up. Everything else circulates — the flywheel, the gauges, the chain, the visitors' stones — but not this; it is the one item withdrawn from inventory, held down in the dense patient interior where the clay is oldest. The power understood this as the highest compliment the local economy could pay: not display, but *reading*. The letter had been received and was being read, slowly, in the dark, one molecule of attention at a time.

Fourth and last, it made two quiet arrangements at the margins, because benevolence that ignores the small residents on its way to the large one is not benevolence but theater. It whispered the terms of the lease into the local line of herons, so that the treaty would be heritable — there would always be a heron, the door would always have its keeper. And it eased, by one brick's width, the seam where the birch's roots were working, buying the committed little tree another century of its chosen mistake. The vole it left entirely alone. The vole's life was already perfect, and blessing a success is just meddling with extra steps.

Then it stood, and brushed off the damp, and left — and this is the part that matters most, because the leaving had terms.

It returns. Not on a schedule; the basin has never once repeated an arrangement, and the power matches its correspondent's style, so the intervals are never the same — four years, eleven, six. Each visit it walks the rim once, sits an afternoon, and leaves a single stone inside the berm: always a stone from somewhere genuinely else, a different riverbed, a different coast, so that the inventory's strangest shelf grows slowly stranger. If the being is lonely, this is company at a survivable dosage. If it is not, this is merely more to keep, and keeping is its joy. The gift is harmless under either hypothesis, which is the only kind of gift the wise allow themselves to give the inscrutable.

And it has made one promise, filed where powers file such things. On the day the motor finally stops — and the power will know the day; it has heard enough endings to feel one coming up the line — it will be standing on the southern rim. Not to restart the note. Not to counterfeit the duet. Only so that the basin's first loss in its long unbroken tenure does not happen before an empty rim — so that when the one deliberate sound in the landscape ends, and the silence becomes total for the first time in generations, something will be present that understands what has been lost, and will stand there through the whole grey day of it, collar up, saying nothing.

Afterward, the power intends to take over the smaller office the motor will vacate. Not the pumping — the asking. A question must be put to the basin at intervals, something the basin can answer with its perfect indifference, because an answer with no question decays into mere fact, and the basin deserves better than fact. So the power will come and put its thin thread of attention over the berm, and the basin will replace every liter of it, indifferently, completely, and the conversation will continue in its new instrumentation, and neither party will ever once repeat itself.

At dusk, then, the place is as it was, which was the entire object of the exercise. The flywheel weeps. The vole comes home over the low point in the berm. The motor holds its note into the dark, sound-footed now, though nothing knows it. Somewhere in the deep interior, a fist of sky-iron is being read at one molecule a night. And on the rim, at intervals no one has mapped — because the power keeps its own counsel too, having learned from the best — a figure in a grey coat sits in the wet evening with one clay-socketed glove on its knee, watching the floor of the world rearrange itself, and finding everything exactly where it was last put.

Which is to say: somewhere else. Which is to say: kept.
