**The Third Census**

A power, if it is old enough and kind enough, learns to hear prayer in things that do not know they are praying.

This is the first thing to understand about how the borough was noticed at all. To a being that watches the weave of the world, the lowlands show up as a snag — a place where the threads of before and after were pulled through each other long ago and never pulled back. Powers see such snags often. Breaks in the order of things are common; they are usually wounds, usually screaming, usually getting worse. What stopped this one — what made it lean closer, the way you lean toward a sound you cannot believe you are hearing — was that the snag was *worn smooth*. The break had moss on it. Animals were commuting through it. Somewhere in the fault, a frog was keeping time with its own echo and had been for a lifetime, contentedly, at full voice.

Powers see broken things constantly. They almost never see a broken thing that has been loved into furniture.

So it came closer, in the way powers come closer, which disturbs nothing, and it found beneath the snag the second thing, the thing that held it: the expecting. Six hundred years of swept rooms. Ten thousand generations of beetles keeping beds made for a guest who only ever visits. Gutters maintained, ruin curated, lamps of green-gold light left on, every door grown ajar — an entire architecture organized around holding one visit open forever. To a power, which spends its existence wading through petition — oceans of *give me, spare me, take this from me* — the borough registered as a prayer of a kind it had nearly forgotten existed. A prayer that asks for nothing it does not already have. A prayer with no words, no want, no end, consisting entirely of a door held open and the patience to hold it.

It is very hard, if you are benevolent, not to answer a prayer. The power's first instinct — and powers have instincts, old ones, worn as deep as any threshold — was to *give*.

So it took its census of possible gifts, and this is where its wisdom did its work, which is to say: this is the list of everything it did not do.

It did not mend the folds. It considered it for less time than it takes a drop to cross a facade. The break was load-bearing. Sealed, the long-looped lane would become a dead end; the many-jointed things would be evicted from the wound they had made a homeland; the swifts would lose the corner their young practice and their old take without thinking; the wren would be widowed of itself. You do not straighten a tree that has grown around a stone. The stone is in the tree now. The tree is partly made of accommodating it.

It did not give the borough an inhabitant. This was the harder refusal, because it was the obvious one — the place is a portrait of a missing resident; surely the kindness is to supply the resident, to settle someone in the rooms, to let the expecting finally *land*. But the power had read the architecture honestly, and the architecture was clear: here, expecting and having are the same worn stone. The borough is not waiting the way an empty chair waits. It is waiting the way a held note waits — the waiting *is* the music. Fill the rooms permanently and the readiness dies of fulfillment; the beetles' stewardship collapses into mere cleaning; the held breath, held forever, was the life. A wise power knows the difference between an absence that is a wound and an absence that is an organ.

It did not speak to the deep singer. It understood — standing in the thrum, feeling the slow pulse come up through whatever it was using for feet — that the deep one had grown the whole hollow as an ear, and that you do not do an ear the kindness of shouting into it. You do an ear the kindness of being worth hearing.

So here, finally, is what it did.

It came through the green. This matters more than anything else it did, so it is listed first. There is no other way to come to anything here, and the power did not exempt itself: it took a body — a small one, a head shorter than you, or exactly you, stooped — and it squeezed between the stems with its shoulders, and it endured the wet dark of the grown-shut places, and it arrived in the hollow sap-streaked and breathing hard, the way everyone arrives, because the coming-the-hard-way is the borough's lock and its key both, and a guest who skips the threshold has not actually entered. Benevolence, at this level, is mostly etiquette.

It walked the lanes. That was the answer to the deep singer: footsteps, on the root-cobbles, carried down through the mass to the ear that had been grown for exactly this. It did not announce itself. It was simply, for a while, *audible* — a small bright thing moving on the skin of the listening dark, one more voice in the chorus the depth had cultivated. It let itself be part of the music. That is what the ear was for.

It leaned on the well. Mornings — and the folds, dealing its moments out across the centuries the way they deal everyone's, made of its one lean many leans, made it a regular, made the worn arc on the rim fractionally more worn and fractionally more *explained*. It stood in the square at the exact point the swifts have always split around, and the swifts split around it, and the channel held, because it was standing precisely where the absence stands, which was the point.

It slept one night in one of the rooms. Lay down on the swept, vein-ribbed floor of a chamber that had been kept ready for six hundred years and had never once held anything, and it slept there, actually slept, which powers can do if they insist on a body honest enough. In the morning the beetles came through on their rounds and found a fading warmth in the floor and a faint dishing where a sleeper's hip had rested, and they inspected both with the unhurried gravity of stewards, and trundled on. By evening the green had weathered the dishing to look six centuries old, because that is how the borough keeps things. But it had happened. Once, in all that time, the made bed was slept in. Ten thousand generations of housekeeping were, once, *used*. The power suspected — correctly — that this would carry. Readiness that has been needed even once is a different substance from readiness that never has.

It gave the frog one note. Passing the long-looped lane, it heard the duet — the call going round the fold and coming home half a second late, the frog answering itself, perfectly partnered, perfectly alone — and it did the smallest thing it did in the borough, which may have been the largest: just once, the returning echo came back carrying a single grace note the frog had never sung. Foreign, and kind, and gone. The frog went very still. Then it answered, and the duet resumed, and the pattern went round the loop carrying the stranger's note folded into it, where it remains, passed from echo to echo, so that the frog now spends its life in conversation with itself *and one other*, and whether it knows matters less here than it would anywhere else — but the power, who could see such things, saw that it knew.

Then it left, and in leaving it did the two deliberate things, the only appointments it made.

First, it consented to the smearing. It did not gather its moments back up into a tidy visit with a beginning and an end, as a power could. It let the folds deal its footsteps out across the whole life of the borough, backwards and forwards, the way they deal yours — so that somewhere in the deep past of the hollow it is already leaning at the well, and somewhere far ahead it is only now arriving, sap-streaked, breathing hard. It tipped itself into the borough's tense. It joined the population of one. The thin line of everyone-who-ever-visits acquired one member older and kinder than the rest, indistinguishable, in the polish of the thresholds, from you.

Second, it reserved the last visit. Every dream ends; even a plant's patience is made of matter; some unthinkable morning the green will begin to die, and the lamps will dim, and the chorus will thin, and the borough will face the one thing its architecture cannot weather, which is the prospect of expecting with no one ever coming again. The power took that morning for itself. It wrote it down in whatever powers write in. When the end comes, someone will already have pushed through the failing dark the hard way, and will be standing in the square, at the well, at the worn arc, so that the borough's last moment is not an abandonment but an arrival — so that at the very end, expecting and having, which were always the same worn stone, close fully into one, and the held breath, held six hundred years, is finally, gently released into company.

Between now and then, it keeps one quiet duty, which is the closest it comes to interfering: it minds the way in. Not a road — a road would kill the place in a season; the borough's protection is that it can only be reached by the kind of devotion that squeezes through grown-shut dark, and the power would no more pave that than it would prop open a heart. It only sees to it that the green never grows wholly shut. That once in a generation, somewhere out in the wide world, a wanderer's path bends faintly valleyward — never compelled, only made plausible, the way a lane suggests itself to feet. It keeps the line of you unbroken. It is, you could say, the borough's patron now, though its entire patronage consists of making sure the door that is held open occasionally has someone to be open *for*.

And it took one thing away, because even powers are changed by what they refuse to change. It learned the borough's lesson and carries it into all its other works, which are vast and various and mostly elsewhere: that maintenance is a language love speaks fluently; that some prayers are not to be answered but joined; that the highest form of help can be to become part of a thing's own grammar — a footstep for the ear, a grace note for the echo, a warmth in the floor, a lean on the rim. It answers fewer petitions now, and attends more. It leaves doors ajar in places that have never heard of the lowlands.

And sometimes — dealt out of order across the centuries, the same as everyone — it goes back. Not to give. It has nothing left to give that the borough would be improved by. It goes back because the Hollow Borough is the one place in all its jurisdiction where it is not needed, where nothing is asked of it but weight on stone and breath in the warm green air, where it can be small, and traffic, and expected — which is to say, the one place where a power can rest.

The borough, for its part, registers no difference. It did not need to be noticed; that was never what the expecting was for. The frogs hammer, the swifts pour, the wren keeps itself company half a beat apart, the beetles make the rooms ready, the deep one listens, the green dreams its very old town in perfect period detail. Only — if you come, and you will, you already have — you may find, in the dished stone of a threshold, footprints worn so deep into the welcome that they seem older than the welcome itself. They are yours. They are also not only yours.

The door, every door, everyone's door, standing ajar.
