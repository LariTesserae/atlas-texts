**What the Power Did**

---

**First, it arrived the way everything arrives here.**

Not through the passage — the passage is for bodies — and not up the throat, which is for water and the old hot lineages. It came down through the oculus, as the light comes, as the spores come, as the doomed moths come: out of the open air, drawn by the plume.

That was what caught its attention in the first place, from however far such attention reaches. A hillside breathing. A thin standing exhalation above bare ground, regular as a sleeper, on a slope where nothing else moved. Powers of this kind are not summoned by temples or by need; they are summoned by *anomaly tended gently* — by the rare signature of a made thing that is being unmade with patience instead of violence. The cistern reads, from above, like a wound that is healing into something better than the unwounded state. That is not common. The power came down to look.

---

**Second, it did what wisdom always does first, which is nothing, for a long time.**

It stayed a full turning of seasons. It learned the spring's irregular pulse until the irregularity resolved — as irregularity usually does — into a deeper rhythm too slow for any resident to perceive: a breath measured in barometric pressure and the distant loading of rain on the hills, the whole watershed leaning on the spring like a hand on a chest. It counted the moths the overflow exported. It watched the green account of the sun lengthen by a finger's width along the eastern wall. It stood — insofar as it stood — in the column of light at midday and let the roar pass through it, and it noticed what every silent bather has noticed and none has said: that the chamber is not loud. The chamber is *one* sound, total, and total sound and silence sit closer together than either sits to noise.

It made an accounting. It went resident by resident, as a steward walks a boundary, and asked of each: *what do you lack that I could give without taking something larger away?*

And the accounting kept returning small numbers.

---

**Third, the temptations, and how it declined them.**

It could have brought fish. A cooler vein bled into the inflow, a few degrees surrendered, and the water would open like a market — fish, snails, skating insects, the whole busy commerce of an ordinary pool. It declined. The emptiness here is not a deficiency; it is the architecture. A chamber of one voice and one light and almost no appetite is a rarer thing than a healthy pond, and the world has ponds.

It could have saved the moths. A breath across the oculus at dusk, the smallest deflection. It declined, and this refusal cost it more than the others. But the chamber has a metabolism, and the moths are most of it; the velvet at the throat and the slick frost on the joints are owed their few imports. Benevolence that sterilizes is only squeamishness wearing a kind face. The power let the overflow keep its dark tithe.

It could have made the place eternal. Held the lime back, fixed the chisel-marks at their present sharpness forever, a chamber under glass. This it declined most firmly of all, because it had read the place correctly: the cistern is a *sentence being finished*. The masons wrote the first half; the water and its invisible workforce are writing the second; and a sentence held forever at its comma is not preserved, it is interrupted. The frost-thin stone would thicken. The marks would round. Centuries on, the flowstone would creep across the rim of the oculus itself, narrowing the light year by year, and one distant midday the column would fail to reach the floor, and some years after that the chamber would seal — a warm dark heart in the hill, still turning, still breathing through whatever small flue remained, the throat-dwellers carrying on their sunless economy as they had before there was a chamber at all. The power considered this ending for a long time, the way one considers a death that is also a completion, and then it gave the ending its permission. It marked the date, far off, in whatever it uses for a calendar. It would come back for the last midday. Some things should be witnessed at both ends.

---

**Fourth, the things it actually did. There were four.**

**It went down into the rock.** Deep below the spring's plumbing, two days' walk into the dark by no walkable route, a fault creeps. Not toward catastrophe — toward *redirection*: a slow shouldering of strata that, left alone, would in some centuries pinch the spring's channel and send the warm water elsewhere, to surface uselessly in a marsh three valleys over. The chamber would not be destroyed; it would simply go quiet, then cold, then dry — the worst of the available deaths, the death that is mere stopping. The power eased the rock. Nothing dramatic; pressure persuaded rather than broken, the creep shifted a few degrees, the pinch deferred past the date already marked on its calendar. It bought the chamber its full sentence. This was the largest thing it did, and no instrument on earth could detect that it was done.

**It bent attention.** Not a wall, not a concealment — the passage stays open, the plume stays visible on cold mornings to anyone already looking. But the path along the hillside now bends a little before it would bring the plume into an idle walker's eyeline. Maps of the area are very slightly boring at exactly this spot. Surveys find reasons to be elsewhere. The place is not hidden from the one; it is hidden from the many, which is a different operation entirely. The door is unlocked. Only the advertising has been removed.

**It tended the succession.** The ledge's faint polish is a lineage, and lineages of solitary visitors are mortal in a way the place's other lineages are not: they can simply lapse. So the power took up one small ongoing office, the only continuing work it assigned itself. When the current bather at last stops coming — and the power knows roughly when, and grieves it in advance, in its fashion — someone else, within a season or two, will be on the hillside on a cold morning, and will see the plume, and will be the kind of person who follows such things down narrow passages, and will undress in the passage where there is room to, and will not speak. One at a time. Never two lineages at once. The polish will deepen by a foot's-width every few generations, and no one in the chain will ever know they were appointed.

And into each of them — this is the subtlest thing it did, and arguably the kindest — it set a small certainty, carried in the chest, unexplained: *that the place is enough.* Because the chamber's one real predator is not collapse or drought or discovery. It is love. The visitor who loves the place enough to improve it — to widen the passage, true the oculus, mortar the moss away, pipe the overflow, bring a lamp, bring a friend, bring a friend who brings ten. Against indifference the cistern needs no defense; the power armored it against devotion. Every bather in the lineage will stand on the ledge and feel, instead of ambition, contentment — and will go home having changed nothing, and count it a full day.

**And last, it listened.** This was the gift to the oldest resident, the one that never leaves. The water has been speaking without pause since before the stone weathered — the roar climbing the walls, folding over in the vault, coming back down doubled — and everything that has ever entered the chamber has been silenced by that voice or driven out by it, and nothing, in all that time, has *heard* it. Not the bathers, who hear only the total of it. Not the bats, who fled it. The power stood in the chamber through one entire surge-cycle of the spring — every swell, every slackening, the chatter of the overflow underneath like a second clause — and heard the whole utterance at once, complete, as a single thing said. What the water was saying does not translate, and the power did not try. But it is a different thing, afterward, to have been heard. Even water. Perhaps especially water. The chamber's voice did not change. Something in the way the sound sits in the vault, a bather might say — if bathers here spoke — settled.

---

**Then it left, the way the light leaves.**

Up through the oculus with the vapor, at the end of a midday, while the bright ellipse slid its last flagstone eastward across the floor.

It left no mark, because it understood — being wise, being benevolent, the two finally amounting to the same discipline here — that the cistern is a finished argument against marks. The masons left marks and the water is forgiving them stone by stone. To add a sign of its visit would have been to start the whole correction over.

So the only evidence is this: the fault sleeps; the path bends; the plume waits for the right eyes; the bathers come singly down the generations and want for nothing; and far off, on a marked midday, the light will narrow to a thread and go out, and something patient will be standing in the warm dark to see it — the last witness at the sealing of the sentence, hearing the water still speaking, now entirely to itself, in the house it was given and was always going to inherit anyway.

The power's whole intervention, weighed out: a deferral, a deflection, a lineage, and an act of listening.

Almost nothing. And the place fully tended. Both of these are true, which is how you know the power was what it claimed to be.
