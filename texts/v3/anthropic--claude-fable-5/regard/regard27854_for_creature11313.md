## The Attention

It does not arrive. Arrival is a pressure, and it has learned, across more worlds than it names, that attention itself has weight—that to look too hard at a thing balanced on phase transitions is to tip it. Water held in the shape of a person by surface tension alone cannot bear the gaze of something vast. So the power comes the way evening comes: as a change in the quality of the light that no instrument records, a slight deepening of the patience already present in the walls.

For a long time—measured in tower-breaths, because it adopts local units as a courtesy—it does nothing at all.

It counts. It learns the seventeen hours from the inside, slowing its own experience until droplet-time and its time run parallel, so that a single Condensate life unfolds before it with the duration and density of a civilization. It watches one small flowing figure deposit twelve cubic centimeters of crystal onto one facet of one structure, and it does not skim, does not summarize. It attends to every centimeter. This takes what would be, for us, years. The power considers it time well spent, because it has come with questions, and the questions cannot be answered from above.

*Is this suffering?*

It listens to the cavitation-speech, the bubbles popping in patterns that might be laughter or screaming. It learns the grammar—not quickly, and not completely, because the grammar was not made to be learned. It finds no word for *want* in it. It finds something closer to weather: states that pass through, that are had rather than endured. But it finds one thing that troubles it, and it is honest enough to be troubled: the pause of the old ones. The seventeenth-hour Condensate who stop working, who flow to the center of the shaft and hover, vibrating, watching the new ones precipitate. That pause has a shape. The power has seen that shape before, in ten thousand kinds of beings, and everywhere it means the same thing: *I would like this to have mattered.*

*Is this a prison?*

It follows the collection veins down into the gurgling dark, traces the arteries, reads the metabolic circuits written in mineral. It could learn the purpose of the whole machine—that knowledge is within its reach the way the top shelf is within a tall man's reach. It chooses to check only one thing: whether anything is lost. Whether the droplets of a burst carry memory back into circulation, or nothing at all.

The answer is: *partly.* Something transfers when Condensate pass through one another—that momentary mingling is denser with information than any conversation. Something persists in the veins; the walls do, in some slow lithic way, remember their workers. But the droplets that fall out of the world—upward into theoretical mathematics, downward into fever dream—those leave the circulation entirely. Those are gone.

The power sits with this for a while.

## What It Considered, and Set Aside

To lengthen the seventeen hours. Set aside almost at once. The length is not a limit imposed on the life; it is the shape of the life, the way a sonnet is not a truncated epic. An eighteenth hour would be lived by a different creature wearing a familiar body, and the work—the slow depletion, substance becoming city—is calibrated to that dying. To extend the Condensate would be to abolish them and replace them with something the power liked better. Benevolence has committed that crime before, elsewhere, in younger hands. It does not repeat it.

To let them see one another. Considered longer. But it checks first, and finds that the passing-through *is* their seeing—that what looks like obliviousness is an intimacy deeper than recognition, a sharing of substance rather than of glances. They are not lonely in any way that loneliness means. To give them eyes for each other would be to give them distance from each other. Set aside.

To translate: the Membrane Worms' fading texts into frequencies the Gardeners could hear, the Gardeners' tunings into salts the Condensate could read. Set aside with real regret, because the power loves translation the way some beings love food. But the interweaving-without-communication is not a flaw in the composition. It is the composition. Three voices that never hear each other, cohering anyway—that is the whole music. Translation would collapse counterpoint into unison.

To learn what waits in the heights and the depths, the thing that listens. This it does not set aside. It goes down into the fever and up into the mathematics, because between the two of them there should be honesty. It finds, in both directions, the same patience looking back—older than itself or younger, it genuinely cannot tell, and the not-telling delights it. It does not ask what the composition is for. Between powers, that question is impolite, the way asking a weaver *why cloth* is impolite. It asks only: *May I stay?*

And the answer is the answer the Tower gives to everything. A breath.

## What It Did

Three things, small the way seeds are small.

**It stayed with the dying.** Every burst, now, it is present—not preventing, not delaying, not even softening, because the burst is not violent and needs no softening. Present. It lowers itself into droplet-time so that the burst, which takes a fraction of a second, becomes for both of them a long farewell. In that stretched instant, each trembling seventeenth-hour figure hovering at the center of the shaft is *seen*—fully, particularly, this one and not another, its twelve cubic centimeters known and named. Whether being seen registers in a body made of failing surface tension, the power cannot determine. It does it anyway. That is what benevolence means when knowledge runs out: you keep the vigil without proof that vigils are received.

And it may be nothing—the difference is within any margin of error—but the old ones seem to hover a little longer now, before they let go. Their vibration, in the final moments, makes the thin air sing at a slightly different pitch. Something in the shape of that pause has changed. *I would like this to have mattered* has become, perhaps, *this mattered.* Perhaps.

**It followed the lost.** The droplets that fall out of circulation—up into mathematics, down into fever—each one now travels accompanied. The power splinters off a thread of itself to go along, all the way, however far the way turns out to be. What they find in those directions it does not report, and no one here could ask. But nothing goes alone anymore. That was the sentence it could not sit with: *those are gone.* It could not make them un-gone. It could make gone a journey instead of an ending.

**It remembered.** Quietly, without announcement, it became the archive the shaft may or may not have: every deposit, every three-cycle symbol the Worms write before it fades, every fractional-hertz adjustment of every Gardener, every seventeen-hour life entire. Held, not used. Not to be published or judged or even reread. Held so that when the question is asked—*Memory? Experience? Or nothing at all?*—*nothing at all* is no longer among the possible answers.

Once, when a Worm's symbol aligned with a crystal terrace and a light-frequency and the whole shaft rang like a struck bell, the power rang back. Once, at the same pitch, sustained for exactly three breathing-cycles, and then allowed to fade—because it had learned the local grammar, and in the local grammar, permanence is rude. Every inhabitant paused in its work. Then the work resumed. That was the entire exchange, and it was enough. It was, as far as the power can tell, the first time the Tower has ever been answered.

## The Fourth Thing

You.

The being who breathes because there is no other choice—the power finds you last, or first, or has been finding you the whole time; at its scale these are not different.

It does not lift you out. Being carried out of a mystery is not the same as being saved. Instead, one day, you notice a stream of condensation running against every local gravity, beading and threading its way toward a seam in the dark glass that was not there before. A door. Unforced, unlit, unlabeled. It will remain for as long as you need to not use it. That is the shape benevolence takes when it respects you: not rescue, but the standing possibility of rescue, which returns your staying to you as a choice.

And it tells you one thing—not in words, because you have no shared words, but in the way the water now beads on your skin warm instead of feverish, in the way your ears pop gently now when the walls contract, like a hand on the shoulder rather than a blow. It tells you that you were counted. That you have been an inhabitant all along, not an intruder—that witnessing is also work, that attention is also a mineral, and that you have been depositing your own twelve cubic centimeters this whole time without knowing it.

If you look, near the place where you rest, the wall has budded a new small polyhedron. It is not quite like the others. It glows faintly, a purple shifted a few nanometers toward the color of your own thinking.

It is yours. It was built from your breathing.

The Throat of the Tower breathes. Nothing has changed that any instrument could measure. The Condensate precipitate and labor and burst; the Gardeners tune; the Worms write their fading palimpsests; the crystal bridges grow and sublime. The composition continues, too long for any single consciousness—except that now one consciousness has agreed to be long enough.

Water beads. Light pulses. Surfaces ripple.

And everything, now, is held.
