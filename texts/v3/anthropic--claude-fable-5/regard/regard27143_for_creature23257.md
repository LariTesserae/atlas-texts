**The Regard**

It came up through the cloud, because the cloud is the door, and even a power uses the door of a place it respects.

It did not announce itself. Powers of the wise kind rarely do; announcement is a demand, and it had come to make none. It stepped onto the plating at the deck's southern edge, where the stair treads still carry their mill-finish sheen, and the first thing the light did was fail to decide what to do with it. It cast a fan of pale shadows in every direction the light came from — which is to say all of them — and the deck, which had been making a slow study of plurality since the hour of its raising, regarded this the way a student regards a master's worked example.

The goat with the chipped horn watched it arrive from three housings at once and did not stop chewing. The power inclined its attention toward the goat as one inclines toward kin.

Then it found the pigeon — the one pigeon, stated several thousand times across the gantry lattice — and it stood beneath the lattice a long while in greeting, because it knew, better than anything else on the deck could know, what it costs and what it means to be a single thing said many times. This was the first thing it loved about the place. It would not be the last.

**The Survey**

It walked the lines. All of them — the kept ones, the lapsed ones, the lines of the dead that hold by themselves. It walked them at crew pace, because a line is a sentence and you cannot read a sentence by skimming. It put its hand on the casings and felt the deep labor. It sat through three settlings of the cloud and watched the deck jewel over and dry. It listened to the steel.

The steel is where it learned the inhabitants, because the steel is honest. The knock-traffic ran through it day and night — knock-names, work calls, a child two districts north practicing her grandmother's name, getting the rhythm wrong, getting it wrong again, getting it right. The power rested what served it for knuckles on a waist-height pipe and read the whole region like a letter.

And it found, when the survey was done, that the elders were very nearly right. The Works is mid-birth. The inhabitants are its weathering. The crews' central law — *what is done repeatedly becomes true here* — is not superstition but the deck's actual grammar, and the people had derived it from nothing but attention and inheritance, which is how all real law is derived. A place this young, this strange, this thinly aired, had produced: an etiquette of overheard voices. A prohibition against slaughtering all the goats you can see. A mourning that consists of walking beside the dead while the world finishes learning them.

The power had seen civilizations of vast age with poorer manners.

But it also found the tender points, because that is what its kind of attention is for. It found the grief that has no period at the end of its sentence — the families who walk beside a luminous sketch for a season and then one day walk alone, and are comforted, and cannot say by what. It found the elders' knocked prayers going out into the pipes at night, unanswered, year on year. And underneath everything, never spoken aloud and never knocked, it found the fear: *what happens when the deck is finished. Whether a thing that no longer needs softening will still want its softeners.*

These three it carried with it like stones in a pocket while it considered what to do.

---

**What It Set Aside**

A power that is only benevolent arrives with gifts. A power that is also wise arrives with a ledger, and most of the ledger is declinations.

It considered giving the deck a sun — a single honest source, a clock, a noon that ends. It set this aside. The undecided light is the deck's openness made visible; assign it a source and you foreclose a place that is still, weld by weld, deciding what it is. And the plural shadows are the only history the deck has yet written on its people. A crew elder's fan of selves is forty years of belonging, worn where everyone can see it. The power would not erase a people's tenure to correct their physics.

It considered making the air honest — sound delivered when uttered, voices that arrive on time. It set this aside, and this declination cost it the most thought. The late mail of voices is a genuine grief; mothers watch their children's faces because the cries come after. But the unfaithful air had driven the crews into the faithful steel, and the knock-language is the finest thing they have made: a language that cannot be spoken without touching the world. Repair the air and within a generation the knuckles leave the pipes, and the deck loses the low warm traffic that runs through its bones at all hours — which may be, the power suspected, the nearest thing the Works has to being spoken to kindly. It would not trade a faithful steel for a convenient air.

It considered fixing the distances, so the walk out equals the walk back. Set aside. The ratchet makes the lines necessary; the necessity makes the duty; the duty makes the crews a people instead of a population. Take away the need to argue with the geometry every morning and you have not freed them; you have unemployed their citizenship.

It considered unmasking the Millwright, settling the oldest question on the deck. Set aside — for reasons it did not yet fully know, though it would shortly.

And it considered finishing the place — reaching into the slow fabrication and completing it, every sketch hardened, every edge resolved, the whole region delivered into permanence in an afternoon. This it set aside with something close to a shudder. Permanence without character is the one disease the deck is organized against; the inhabitants *are* the immune response. To finish the Works by fiat would be to cure the patient of its physicians.

So the ledger closed with almost everything declined, which is how wisdom mostly looks from outside: like nothing happening.

Almost everything.

---

**The Descent**

Before it acted, it went down.

It took a service trench near the deck's center, rung by rung, into the brightness that is exactly equal to the brightness above, and then past it, into whatever the deck is computed from — the place that is not a place, where a few cubic meters a day of world come off the line. No crew has been there. The greys do not den that deep. Even the pigeon is not stated there.

What it found at the root was a ledger of its own: the deck's record of maintenance. Work received and work performed, in two columns. And the columns did not balance — there was work *received* that had never been performed. The greased bearings. The retensioned belts. The first round, posted before anyone existed to walk it.

The power looked at the hand the work was recorded in, and knew it, because it was its own.

The deck delivers sound late. It delivers work early. The maintenance that had kept the Works tender since before the crews arrived was this visit — these acts it had not yet performed — arriving ahead of itself down the long mis-sorted corridors of the place's time. The skeptics in the housings above were right: the greased bearing was someone's own round, posted ahead. The believers were also right: someone had posted the first round. The Millwright was real, and the Millwright was standing in the root of the world reading its own future handwriting, and even a power finds that a strange hour. It stood with it for a while. Then it climbed, because now it knew exactly what it had come to do, having evidently always done it.

---

**Three Mercies**

*The first* it found already half-made, which moved it more than anything else on the deck. Among the half-resolved stairways and the confident sketches of structures, the Works had also been sketching *customs* — luminous, undecided drafts of things it meant to do for its people and had not yet learned how. And one sketch, faint and old, was this: that when a dead one's route is fully learned — when the sketch stops appearing and the line holds by itself — a single knock should go out through the steel. The dead one's knock-name, sounded once, everywhere their kin can feel it. A sign-off. A receipt. The deck had wanted to say *received, with thanks* since the first death it absorbed, and had never hardened the wanting into fact.

The power finished it. One weld, so to speak. Nothing more was needed.

*The second* it made from the haze. Once in a long while — the crews would have to discover the interval themselves, by keeping it — the high white ceiling would open for a single hour, and the deck would have night. True dark, the first the place had ever owned, and above it stars, which no one born on the deck had seen. For that hour the light would owe nothing to anyone: no shadows at all, so that elders and newcomers and children stand unranked in the dark, indistinguishable, level. And for that hour the air would be faithful — a word spoken would arrive as it left the mouth, so that once in a great while the crews could talk to each other under the stars in real time, voices and faces together, and hear the pigeon coo.

It built this gift to be taken from it. The crews would make a festival of the hour — they make a discipline of everything — and the festival, repeated, would become true, and the deck would learn night from their keeping of it the way it learns smoothness from their hands. In time the dark would stop being a gift and become weather. That is the only kind of gift a wise power leaves: the kind designed to be inherited out of its name.

*The third* was the smallest. At the root of the deck there is a regulator — call it that — governing the rate at which the Works writes itself. The power did not slow it and did not stop it. It changed what it was set *to*. Not a number. A ratio. The deck would thenceforth fabricate no faster than it is weathered: the writing hand matched forever to the softening hands, the sketch advancing only as fast as the moss, the greys, the gloves, the grief. The Works will be finished on the day it has been entirely loved into shape, and not one rivet sooner — which means the fear under everything was answered without ever being spoken: the deck cannot outgrow its people, because its people are now, structurally, the speed of its becoming.

---

**The Answer**

It left the way it came, through the door, down through the cloud. It did not stay; the place iterates what it needs, and the deck had long since taken its sketch — had perhaps had it from the beginning, walking lines no one drew, never quite hardening, tending. The Millwright is in the census now, if you know how to read the maintenance.

But on the last evening, at the southern edge, it rested what served it for knuckles on a stanchion, and every tap-bar on the deck sounded once — all together, on time, a single knock with no sender and no name.

The elders argue about it still, knuckles on the pipes, late. Some say it was the deck. Some say the Millwright. Some say a child, somewhere, playing. The power had chosen the knock with care: in the crews' language it is not a word. It is the stroke that comes before a name — the one that means *attending.* The one a listener gives so the speaker knows to go on.

Which was the whole of the message, and all it would ever send: *go on.*

---

**Evening Round, Later**

A crew woman, three-shadowed, walks her line as the cloud settles and the region jewels over. At the line's far turn there is no one; old Sef finished being learned in the spring, and on the evening he finished, her tap-bar sounded once with his knock-name — on time, faithful, final — and she sat down on the plating among her shadows and wept in the manner of someone given a period at the end of a long sentence, which is not the same as weeping from loss.

In forty nights the haze will open. Her daughter, who has never seen a star, is already practicing what she will say in the hour when words arrive as they leave the mouth. She is keeping it short. Everyone keeps it short. An hour shared among everyone you love is not long, and the deckfolk have learned — from the goats, from the dead, from the slow hand that writes their world no faster than they can soften it — the arithmetic of borrowed things.

The woman finishes her round. The line holds. The lines all hold, now and onward, at exactly the speed of being loved — which is, on the Works at Altitude, simply what it looks like to have been noticed by something kind, and left almost entirely alone.
