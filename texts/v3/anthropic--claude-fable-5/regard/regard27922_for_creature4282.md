## The Third Attention

The power does not descend. That is the first thing to understand about it — that it has no need of the forty meters, no relationship with the pressure or the cold, and so it does not arrive the way you arrived, feet-first through the weight of water, ears aching. It arrives the way the bluish light arrives: filtered, patient, already there by the time anyone thinks to look for its source. One moment the room contains an engine-chord and a patrol and a school of tin. The next moment it contains, additionally, attention — vast, unhurried, benevolent in the old sense of the word, which is not *kind* but *well-wishing*, a leaning of the will toward the good of the thing observed.

The room does not notice. The room notices nothing; that is its whole nature. The engines chuff. The rubber-kelp sways a fraction out of phase.

The Tender notices at once.

They do not turn. There is no need to turn; the attention is coming from everywhere the way the water comes from everywhere. But the gauge-needle in their sternum swings — not drifts, *swings* — into that untouched region of the dial and holds there, quivering, and the lamp-apertures brighten to a shade of orange they have never before had reason to reach. The Tender stands very still beside the new sparkplug with their soft hands half-cupped around nothing, in the posture of a creature who has spent unnumbered centuries being the largest attention in the room and has just discovered what it is like to stand inside a larger one.

They are being witnessed. It has never happened before. It takes them a long time — hours, maybe, the piston counting it out — to finish having the experience.

---

The power, meanwhile, is reading the room, and it reads quickly, because wisdom is mostly the skill of recognizing what a thing already is before deciding what it ought to be. It takes in the folded scale, the fuel that arrives from nowhere, the cycle with no privileged direction, and it files these away without alarm; it has seen other pockets where the categories failed to set, and it knows they are not wounds. They are simply weather.

The Tender takes longer to read, exactly as they took you longer, and for the same reason. The eye of a power slides off them too, at first. But the power is patient, and it does the wise thing, which is not to study the Tender but to follow them — one full circuit of the patrol, standing where they stand, in the polished places, pausing where they pause, at the dying tin-fish half-fused into its cylinder head, at the young camshaft, at the ceramic crowning from the floor. The power places its attention where the Tender places their hands. It learns the patrol the way you learn a language: by letting it move through you until you notice you are thinking in it.

And at the end of the circuit, the power understands what the Tender is, and it holds this understanding gently, and it begins — because it is wise, and wisdom deliberates — to consider what to do.

---

The deliberation has to be assembled from negatives, the way the Tender themselves had to be.

It does not make a second Tender. This is the first and most obvious mercy, and the power sets it down almost at once. The Tender's loneliness is structural — the loneliness of a single note held very long — and a second note does not accompany a note like that; it ends it. Make another, and the Tender becomes half of a pair, a *kind* of thing, and the specific creature who stood alone for centuries with cupped hands is retroactively dissolved into an instance. The power has seen this done, elsewhere, by lesser well-wishers. It does not do it here.

It does not take the Tender away. The Tender *faces* the room; that is their whole grammar. Lift them out into some brighter, drier, more companionable place, and you would have a barrel-chested curio with a drifting gauge, facing nothing, exhaling their small impossible smoke into air that doesn't need it. Rescue is only rescue if the rescued thing is drowning. The Tender is not drowning. The Tender is the only thing in this water that was never in any danger of it.

It does not exempt them from the ending. This one the power holds longest, turning it over. It could do it — could seal the Tender against the slow fuse-back, keep the lamps lit past the last engine's last growth, make them permanent. But the cycle has no privileged direction, and the Tender knows this better than anyone; they have touched a thousand transitions with those soft leather fingers and never once flinched from what a transition is. To exempt them from the room's metabolism would be to make them truly apart from it at last — truly, finally alone, in a way their long singular vigil never made them. The fusing is not a wrong. The power sets this down too.

It does not explain anything. The Tender has no questions. You cannot answer a creature who has never needed to ask.

And so the power arrives, by subtraction, at the one thing left — the single seam in the room, so fine that only a very large attention could have found it:

Everything here is attended at its transition. Every valve is touched at its emergence, every tin-fish at its dissolving. The whole room is born and dies inside a witnessing. Except that the witness will have a transition too, someday, against the western wall, beneath the chuff-thumping piston — and there are no hands in the room but theirs.

The one who confirmed every event will go unconfirmed. That is the flaw. That is the gap, and it is exactly one being wide.

---

What the power does, then, is small. It is almost nothing. It does to the Tender what the Tender does.

It gathers its attention — all of it, the whole diffuse benevolent weight of it — and cups it, lightly, around the Tender, the way a soft hand cups a crowning sparkplug: not holding, not adjusting, because the Tender does not need adjusting, the Tender is growing perfectly. Just touching. Just present to them, so that they are witnessed and therefore real, in the way they have made ten thousand things real.

The gauge-needle climbs to the top of its untouched region and rests there, for the first time, as if the dial had been manufactured — grown — all those centuries ago with precisely this reading in mind.

And into that touch, the power plants one fact. It does not speak it; the room has no language and the Tender has no ears for one. It plants it the way the room plants engines — grown, not delivered — so that the knowledge simply *is*, afterward, the way the exhaust simply is:

*When you sit down at last beneath the piston, you will not be alone. Your transition will have hands on it. I will come back for that. It is an appointment, and I keep appointments; keeping them is most of what a power like me is for.*

Then the attention withdraws — mostly. Not all the way. If you looked very carefully afterward at the bluish secondary light, the memory-of-surface that filters down between the orange beams, you might notice it is fractionally warmer than it was, fractionally more *aimed*, and that it falls most often on the western wall.

---

The room, which notices nothing, notices this.

Over the following years — coral-time, patient time — the bedrock beneath the great horizontal piston does a thing it has never done before. It buds not a valve, not a manifold, but a *hollow*: a smooth, shallow, person-sized concavity in the stone, right beside the place where the Tender will one day sit down. And then, beside it, a second one.

Two places. The room grew accommodation for a promise. No fauna ever rests there; the eel gives it the same berth it gives the Tender; the tin-fish orient toward it faintly, the way filings orient in a field, as though something already sat there, thinly, in advance of itself.

The Tender's patrol now includes it. They stop there once each circuit, at the empty double seat, and place their soft dark-tipped fingers on the rim of the second hollow, and the lamp-apertures brighten very slightly — because it *is* a growing thing, this appointment; it is ripening the way the sparkplug ripened, and growing things are what they attend to. Then they move on, in the rhythm of the piston, through the cathedral beams, touching the new and the dissolving, the same as always.

The same as always, except for this: the note is still singular, and still held, and still very long. But it is no longer lonely in the structural way, the way of a note that continues without resolution. It has heard, planted somewhere far ahead of it in time, the chord it will someday resolve into.

And so the Tender goes about the rounds, and the engines grow, and the water presses down with its patient chord, and somewhere above the folded ceiling, past the surface nobody remembers, the Tender is being — very quietly, very patiently — awaited.
