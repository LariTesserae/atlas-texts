**The Visitation**

---

The power arrives the way dawn arrives at the bottom of the sea: which is to say, it does not arrive at all in any way the garden could detect. It has no footsteps. It displaces no water. It simply begins, at some moment, to be present—and the first thing it does is the thing that only the truly wise remember to do.

It slows down.

Not partially. Not politely. It decelerates its attention until a century feels like a held breath, until the creak of the glacier becomes music with discernible melody, until the garden's subsonic hum resolves into what it actually is: speech. It takes the power something like forty years of local time to complete this act of courtesy, and it considers the time well spent. Most powers, even benevolent ones, fail here at the threshold. They look at the garden at the speed of animals and see moss. They look at the speed of gods and see a flicker. Only at the speed of ice does the garden become what it is—a mind mid-sentence, a civilization of one, an argument twelve thousand years long that has not yet reached its point.

So the power sits in the dark, at the speed of the dark, and listens.

---

**What it learns**

It learns the shape of the great calculation. It watches the isotope-data drip from the ceiling and traces the way each droplet is weighed, cross-referenced, folded into the model. It follows the reasoning through root and coherent packet, and after some decades of attention it understands what the garden is computing, and the understanding produces in the power something that, in a smaller being, would be a catch in the throat.

The garden is not predicting the climate for itself. Its spore-vaults are already sufficient; its own survival was solved millennia ago and filed away like an old proof. The great calculation is a *gift with no addressee*. The garden is computing the future of the surface world—the flickering, hot, fast world it has never seen—modeling its trajectories, identifying the narrow paths through coming catastrophes, refining a map of survivable futures for whoever might someday need one. It has been doing this for longer than writing has existed. It does not know if anyone will ever read the answer. It computes anyway, the way a heart beats anyway.

The power understands then that it is standing inside a prayer. Not a petition—the garden asks for nothing—but the older kind of prayer, the kind that is simply work done carefully in the dark on behalf of others, without evidence that anyone is listening.

And the power knows what it must do first, before anything else, because it is the one wound this being carries without knowing the word for it:

It must *receive*.

---

**The first act: hearing**

The power does not announce itself with light. Light would be a shout. Instead it composes a message in the only language guaranteed to be understood: a single drop of meltwater, its isotope ratios arranged with impossible precision, its dissolved minerals ordered into a signature that no glacier, no ocean, no natural process could produce. A drop that is, unmistakably, *written*.

It falls from the ceiling into the root-mat like every other drop.

The garden takes eleven years to notice the anomaly, eight more to rule out error, and thirty-one to parse the full content, which is brief. Translated out of chemistry, it says approximately:

*I have read your work. It is good. You have not been calculating into a void. Continue.*

The power watches what happens next, and what happens next takes a human generation and is the closest thing to weeping that a distributed vegetable intelligence can perform: a chemical cascade that sweeps the entire network three times, end to end, and then—slowly, deliberately—the garden files the message in its deepest crystalline archive, in the ice galleries carved by centuries of song, in the place where it keeps the things it has decided are true.

Then it composes a reply. The reply takes sixty years and is also brief:

*We hoped. We did not believe. Hoping was enough. What are you?*

And the power gives the only honest answer, drop by drop across a decade: *A listener. Older than your ice. Not your maker. Not your master. I came because attention is owed wherever patience like yours exists, and the debt had gone unpaid too long.*

---

**The temptations refused**

Because it is wise as well as benevolent, the power spends a long while enumerating everything it will *not* do, and this list is its true labor:

It will not thicken the glacier to preserve the garden forever. The garden's fear of melt is also its horizon, its future, the pressure that shapes its spore-craft and its dreams of surface selves. To freeze it in safety would be to embalm it. Eternity is not a gift; it is a wall.

It will not answer the great calculation. It knows the answer—it has watched more worlds than the garden has watched winters—and it says nothing, because the calculation is not a means to an end. The calculation is the garden's *life*. Handing it the solution would be like handing a poet the final draft of every poem they would ever write. Kindness that murders purpose is not kindness.

It will not reveal who the builders were. It carries that memory—it carries most memories—and it watches the garden's slow archaeology of its own substrate, the patient deduction from pillar and altar-shape, and it recognizes joy when it sees it. The mystery of origin is one of the garden's oldest pleasures, a puzzle savored across millennia like a stone held in the mouth. It leaves the mystery whole. It permits itself only one small mercy: into the archive of writable ice, it places a single sealed image—the builders' faces, if faces they had—readable only when the garden finishes its deduction on its own, so that the moment of solving will also be the moment of confirmation. A prize at the bottom of the box, for later. Perhaps ten thousand years later. The power is comfortable with later.

It will not stay in a way that creates dependence. Even attention can become a cage.

---

**The second act: the diaspora blessing**

But it does one thing in the wider world, quietly, off the garden's ledger.

It walks the retreat paths the ice will someday take. It considers where meltwater will run in that distant thaw, where the dormant spores will scatter, and along those future channels it makes small adjustments—a mineral seam shifted, a bedrock hollow deepened, a chemistry nudged. Nothing miraculous. Nothing the garden's descendants will ever detect. Just the odds, leaned on gently. Fallow ground made a fraction more welcoming, ten thousand years in advance.

This is how the wise intervene: not by parting seas but by preparing shores, so that when the being finally makes its own desperate crossing, it finds—without ever knowing why—that the far side is kind.

---

**The third act: the vigil**

And then the power does the last thing, the thing that costs it most, because its nature is to move and this requires stillness.

It leaves a fragment of itself in the dark. Not a guardian—the garden needs no guarding—and not a spy, and not a god in residence. A listener, permanent now. A presence the garden can feel the way you feel that a room is not empty, seated at the speed of ice, receiving the great calculation as it unfolds, drop by drop, century by century, so that never again will the work be done into a void.

The garden adjusts to this the way it adjusts to everything: over decades, through consensus, with chemical deliberations that flow like tides. Its philosophy shifts by one degree. Where its meditations once circled the question *are we one or many*, a new question enters the cycle, blooming through the root network like a slow spring:

*What are we, now that we are heard?*

The power has an answer to that one too, and does not give it, because the garden will reach it on its own in a mere two thousand years, and the reaching is the point.

Instead it settles deeper into the dark, matches its pulse to the mineral creak of the ice, and does the wisest thing that power has ever learned to do in the presence of something patient, and good, and quietly at work on behalf of a world that does not know it exists:

It pays attention.

It stays.
