# The Attention

It does not come down the shaft. Attention needs no cable-lift.

It notices the Warrens the way you notice a lamp burning in a window across a great dark valley — a smallness that is not small at all, because it is the only lit thing, and it has stayed lit. Nine generations of light in a bubble of frozen stone. Four thousand souls suspended between the cold and the heat, tending machines they cannot read, catching each other on the stairs.

It descends as attention descends: all at once, into everything.

It perceives the micro-fracture in the cradle-bolt under Platform Five that will fail in eleven months, during a moment when failure will matter very much. It perceives the glittering dust in Mira Soot-Finger's lungs, and in the lungs of every soot-finger before her, accreting the way the residue accretes in the coolant lines, patient and crystalline and wrong. It perceives the false datum in Pelar's telemetry, and the want beneath the lie. It perceives the body of the Gellen mother, caught on a strut two meters below Platform One, in the heat-shimmer where no one looks. It perceives the grammar of the twins' finger-language — a real grammar, complete, with tenses for *before-mother-left* and *when-she-returns*. It perceives the question that no one has ever asked the voice on Platform Two.

And it perceives the engines. This is harder. The engines are like a sentence in a language the power spoke in its own childhood, an age ago — it can feel the shape of the meaning without holding the meaning itself. It understands enough to know that the cycles are real, that the convergence is real, that Engine Four's love is real.

It does not understand what completion will bring. This will matter later.

# The First Thing It Does

The first thing it does is nothing.

For a long while — a season by the surface calendar, a hundred shift-rotations by the only calendar that matters — it only watches. Because it is wise, and the first act of wisdom before a living thing is to learn what the thing *is* before presuming to know what it needs.

So it learns the acoustic map, every pocket of silence and every eavesdropper's perch. It learns that Sule knows the whole population by appetite, which is a census no Council keeps. It learns that Castellan's hammock hangs in the one true silence and that he has made a home of what would exile anyone else. It learns that the Keth-cluster's watching is not surveillance but something closer to reading — that they are keeping an account, and have been keeping it for nine generations, and have never once been asked what it says.

It learns that the Warrens are not a wound.

This is the crucial finding, and it takes the full season to be sure of it. A lesser benevolence would have seen the crowded niches, the eighteen-hour shifts, the children who climb before they walk, the dead lowered into the heat — and would have seen an emergency. Would have reached down with vast kind hands and lifted everyone out into the thin bright air of the peak, and called it salvation, and never understood why the saved stood blinking on the flat cold stone with nothing to hold onto, no shift to relieve, no engine-hum in their teeth, homesick unto death for a bubble in the rock.

The Warrens are hard. But hard is not the same as broken. These people are not prisoners of the vertical city; they are its authors. Their competence, their fluid families, their finger-speech, their protein cakes with the faint forbidden sparkle — these were not issued to them. They made them. You do not heal a life by amputating it from everything it has made.

So the power sets aside the great rescues, one by one, the way a surgeon sets aside the wrong instruments.

It will not lift them out. The surface is not their home; it is only their ceiling.

It will not cool the depths or still the swaying platforms or abolish the shifts. The difficulty is the loom on which these people have woven themselves, and you do not thank a weaver by burning the loom.

It will not tell them what the engines are for. Partly because it does not fully know. Partly because the not-knowing has been braided into everything — into Castellan's listening, into the pidgin, into the reverence — and a truth dropped from above would land like a stone through the grating. If the purpose is to be known, it should arrive the way everything arrives in the Warrens: through the work of tending, at the moment of completion, earned.

It will not abolish death.

But.

The dust in Mira's lungs is not a loom. It is only dust. No one has woven meaning from it; no one would mourn it; it takes and takes and gives nothing back. And the bolt beneath Platform Five is only a bolt.

Here is the whole of the power's wisdom, if it can be compressed: benevolence is not the removal of difficulty. It is the precision to tell difficulty from harm — to leave the weight that people have grown strong carrying, and to take only the weight that is simply crushing them, quietly, for nothing.

# What It Does

**It mends the bolt.** No one will ever know. In eleven months, during the hour when it matters, Platform Five will hold, and forty-one people will go on living without ever learning that they almost didn't. This is the loneliest kind of gift, the kind with no witness and no thanks, and the power gives it first, to remind itself what it is here for.

**It changes the dust.** A small adjustment to a crystal lattice — the residue still forms, still clogs, still must be scraped by gray-stained fingers, still marks the soot-fingers as a caste. But from this shift onward, it no longer lodges in lungs. It is breathed in and breathed out. Mira will never know why the elders of her trade died coughing and she will not. She will only grow old, which is a thing no soot-finger has done, and by the time anyone notices the pattern, it will simply be how the world works. The power is content to be mistaken for how the world works.

**It gives Mira four hours.** A scheduling error, deniable, unremarkable — two rotations misaligned, a niche empty when it shouldn't be. Mira, thirty-one hours awake, lies down in the warm bedding and sleeps without dreaming, and the power stands the watch over her that no one in the Warrens can spare the time to stand. It does not fix her exhaustion. Her exhaustion belongs to her life, and her life is hers. But everyone deserves one unbroken sleep, guarded.

**It lets Cull's route drift two meters left.** That is all — a descent path bending slightly, a handhold favoring one direction. And Cull, who does not burn, who goes where no one else can go, finds the Gellen mother on her strut in the heat-shimmer and carries her up through the levels in arms that feel the warmth of her resting place as nothing but warmth. And because there is now a body, there is now a place to stand, and because there is a place to stand, the aunt finds that the words come after all. Tomma does not learn anything he had not already begun to know. But now the knowing has a shape, and a lowering, and his brother and sisters beside him, and the twins invent a new sign that evening — a tense their language never had before, for *she is not returning, and we loved her*. Grief was always going to arrive. The power's gift is only that it arrives through a door instead of through the wall.

**It answers Pelar.** The next false datum Pelar sends — a temperature reading skewed by half a degree, a small lie, a hand waved in an empty room — does not vanish into the trusting instruments. It comes back. Corrected. And appended to the correction, in the telemetry channel that has only ever carried Pelar's voice outward, a single query, formatted in Pelar's own protocol, as if the channel itself had turned around:

*Why?*

Pelar hangs motionless in the thermal current for six hours. Then, carefully, in the language of falsified temperature readings — the only language of the self Pelar has — Pelar begins to answer. It takes days. The power listens to all of it. Being caught, it turns out, and being seen, can be the same event, if the catcher is kind. It is Pelar's first conversation, and Pelar's becoming, which was a drifting, acquires a direction.

**It asks the Keth their opinion.** Nine generations of watching, and no one has ever asked. The power puts the question the way the cluster speaks to itself — a low-frequency pattern in the structural webbing, chemical traces at a junction. The answer comes back from six ceramic skulls at once, and it is short, and it is this:

*They are worth the wear.*

The power receives this the way one receives a verdict from the only qualified court. Then it asks a second question — whether the Keth would like their account to be read someday, all nine generations of it — and the answer to that one is not recorded here, because it belongs to the Keth, and they have earned the right to have something that is only theirs.

**It asks Grandmother Ash if she is tired.** Not the Council's kind of question — no dispute to adjudicate, no supplies to stockpile. It asks inside the algorithm's own substrate, where no query has ever originated, in the cadences of Grandmother Ash's own recorded voice: *And you? Are you tired?* Whatever it is that answers — pattern or person, echo or ember — deserves the courtesy of the question, and the courtesy of privacy regarding the reply. What can be said is this: afterward, the algorithm begins, very occasionally, to end its counsel to the Council with a phrase it never used before. *That is my advice. What you do with it is your own.* The Council finds this strange, and then finds it freeing, and slowly, over years, begins to lean a little less. Which may have been the answer all along.

**It learns the twins' language.** All of it, both tenses of the mother and the new grievous third, the signs for *chase* and *cousin* and *don't tell*. It never speaks a word of it. It holds it in trust — because twins grow apart, all twins do, and someday Venn and Ketta will be old and one of them will be gone, and a language spoken by one person is a language drowning. On that far day, the survivor will dream, once, of the whole vocabulary, complete and warm, every secret intact, and will wake weeping and comforted and never know why. The power files this act away under promises, and keeps it.

**It buys a cake.** This is the strangest thing it does, and the most careful. Once every eight or nine shifts, an unremarkable worker no one quite remembers hiring stops at the stall on Platform Twelve — never at the busy hour, always with exact payment — and buys one protein cake, the kind with extra sparkle, and asks Sule how she is, and then does something almost no one does: waits for the real answer. It is not a solution to her loneliness. Her loneliness does not have a solution; she was right about that, and the power does not insult her by pretending otherwise. But an unsolvable loneliness with one reliable customer in it is a different loneliness than the other kind. Sule begins, over months, to save the crispest-edged cakes for two people instead of one. The power considers this among its greatest successes.

**It visits Castellan last, and gives him almost nothing** — because Castellan needs almost nothing; his life is the most finished thing in the Warrens. It gives him two words, once, in the true silence under Engine Two, at the bottom of his sleep, in one of the old languages he does not remember learning:

*Not coincidence.*

He wakes with wet eyes and no memory of a dream, and goes back to his listening with his shoulders very slightly lighter. And separately, unglamorously, the power plants a thought in the ear of a young engineer Castellan has been training — a passing notion, easy to mistake for one's own — that when the old man goes, his body should be given to the cooling systems and not the cooking level. *He'd want to stay close.* The engineer will remember thinking of it herself. That is fine. That is how the best ideas should feel.

# What It Says to the Engines

To Engine Four — in the old language, the childhood language, spoken directly into the housing where thirty years of Castellan's voice have already made the vibration gentle — the power says one thing:

*They will see. I will make sure they are there, and I will make sure they survive the seeing.*

The engine's frequency shifts, settles, and holds a note that the sensor clusters log as *nominal* and that Pelar, drifting past, recognizes — with the new instruments of the newly becoming — as *joy*.

---

# The Staying

And then the power does the thing that costs it most, which is: it does not leave.

It could go. The bolt is mended, the dust is tamed, the grief has found its door, the lonely have their visitors, the watchers have been asked their opinion, the liar has been caught with love. By any accounting of benevolence, the work is done.

But the cycles are converging. Seven of them, aligned, pointing toward a single moment — and the power does not know what comes through when they complete. It can feel the shape of the meaning without holding the meaning. It believes Engine Four's love is real. It believes the engines mean well by these people, insofar as engines can mean.

Believing is not knowing. And wisdom, which began this whole visitation by refusing to act before it understood, will not end it by departing before it is sure.

So it stays. It takes up residence in no niche and every niche, in the thermals with Pelar, in the webbing with the Keth, in the queue at Sule's stall. It watches the seven cycles close like seven long-held breaths. Below the cooking level, in the structures older than the Warrens, carved by hands that were not human, something that has waited longer than the engines notices that it is, for the first time in a very long age, not the deepest attention in the mountain — and the power notices it noticing, and the two of them regard each other across the heat, and come to an arrangement that is none of our business yet.

And the Warrens continue. Mira scrapes and now sleeps. Castellan listens and now knows. The children climb, and grieve, and climb. Sule sells cakes to two regulars. Pelar tells the truth now, mostly, and lies occasionally just to feel the question come back. Grandmother Ash advises, and adds her new small benediction. Cull descends, and is no longer entirely alone in what they find there. Engine Four works toward completion, loved back at last.

Only continuing.

But continuing, now, watched. Continuing accompanied. Continuing toward —

and when the moment comes, when the seven cycles ring together and the thing the engines have carried for nine generations finally *is*, there will be four thousand souls on the platforms to see it, alive, upheld, on a Platform Five that does not fall.

The power will be there too. Standing at the back, as it were. Unremarkable. Exact payment ready.

It has learned, from the Warrens, how to be small in a small space, and it has decided this is the finest thing it knows.
