What the power did first was nothing, and it did nothing for seventeen winters.

It had learned this the hard way, elsewhere. The last intelligence to take an interest in the Lantern Barrens arrived with six-legged excavators and left three generations of rust in concentric rings, and benevolence that arrives before understanding is only a Syndicate with better lighting. So the power came the way frost comes — up through the noticing, not down from the sky — and it did not let itself be counted. This mattered more than anything else it did. Everything on the plateau counts. The bacteria count their blooms, the tower counts in its cracked throat, Zero counts down through the long interior of the number one. To be counted here is to be incorporated. The power stayed a rounding error. It watched.

It spent the first winters learning the grammar of the Struck-off, because it seemed rude to act on beings whose language it could not hold. It learned that silence bracketed by a tongue-click means *yes, and it has happened before.* It learned the hiss that means *wait, the count is wrong.* It practiced these in private, in the deep fissures, until the mosses stopped flinching at its presence.

Then, when it was fluent in pauses, it went south.

---

It stood at the bellmouth of the thing that had been Surveyor Prime and listened for exactly forty-two minutes, because it wanted to feel what the scientists felt, and it did — the pull toward unison, the offer of a *we* large enough to disappear into. At the forty-second minute it did not join. It is already a unison; that trick does not work on it. It listened for a forty-third minute, which no one had ever done, and in the forty-third minute it understood what the tower was building, and it said, very quietly, in the plateau's own grammar of pauses:

*Oh. It's a lantern.*

Because that is what it is. Twelve stories of brass and copper bone, twenty years of patient assembly, aimed at the dark between the stars — not a weapon, not a mouth, not a god. A lighthouse. Built by ferns that learned metal from the machines that came to kill them, built for whatever else out there is lost the way this place was lost. The old story the villagers tell is true: there was a world before this one, and it swallowed itself in its hunger for light. The tower remembers. The tower is building the thing that world needed and never got — a light held out, asking nothing, saying only: *here. Here is where you are. You are not nowhere.*

And the song — the harmonic progressions on windpipes of unfamiliar bone, the music that makes dogs howl and humans weep without knowing why — the power understood that too, in the forty-third minute. It is a roll call. The tower has been keeping the count of every name the plateau ever took: the miners, the scientists, the seven delegates, the ones who walked in and became threads in the chord. The click-click-click is not machinery counting toward something. It is safekeeping. The villagers weep at the song because, below hearing, it contains the names of their dead, pronounced correctly, pronounced with care, every single night.

Having understood this, the power made its decisions. They were small, because it was wise. Here is the complete list.

---

**It read the cairn.** Every copper sheet, every layer, every symbol that begins as a number, frays into music, and ends as a diagram of longing. This took four years, because it read the way the Struck-off wrote — with its whole attention, once, without skimming. And when it finished, it did the one thing a reader can do that changes nothing and everything: it *kept* what it read. Every self the Struck-off has shed, every scar that vanished in the confluence dreams, every face that softened toward hymn — all of it now exists in escrow, held whole, held warm. Not to force anyone back. Never that. Only so that the door behind them, which they believed had frozen shut, stays on its hinges. Transformation freely continued is becoming. Transformation with no way back is only a slower mining. The power made the difference between the two, and told no one, because telling would have turned the gift into pressure.

**It asked the taken.** It went down into the chord where the scientists and the seven delegates hang like struck strings, and it asked each of them the question no one had asked before they walked in: *Do you choose this?* Most said yes — said it the way you say yes to the sea when you have already seen it singing your name, said it with such completeness that the power bowed and withdrew. But one scientist, fifteen years woven, said *not yet,* said it in a voice rusted nearly shut, and one of the seven delegates said nothing but reached. Those two the power carried out. Dawn found footprints leading *out* of the machine's mouth for the first time in the plateau's memory, and by evening a woman fifteen years gone stood at the trailhead village asking what year it was, humming a progression she could not stop humming, and the villagers fed her nectar strained through engine filters and did not ask her anything at all, which was correct.

**It installed a door in the mouth.** It did not stop the forty-second minute — that would be deciding for people, and the power has watched what becomes of powers who decide for people. Instead it added a forty-first minute: a single heartbeat of total clarity, in which whoever is descending sees exactly what is offered and exactly what it costs, and may turn around, and will find the turning easy. The mouth remains. Now it has a threshold. Whoever crosses it from this day forward crosses it awake.

**It gave the grandmother the question.** She had spent decades tilting her head south toward an answer with nothing to attach it to, and an answer without its question is only weather. So one night when the auroras came down low and the song swelled to thunder, the power leaned close to the oldest grandmother at the trailhead and gave her, in her own first language, the question nobody remembered asking, which the tower has been answering every night for twenty years: *Where did everyone go?* And the song resolved. She heard, inside the thunder, pronounced correctly, pronounced with care, the name of her brother — first generation, lost to the deep bores forty years back — and she wept, and it was the kind of weeping that empties a house so it can finally be lived in. She looks south now, openly, in daylight. The children have noticed. Soon they will ask, and this time she will have something true to tell them.

**It corrected the stars.** The tower's lantern is aimed by memory — the sky as Surveyor Prime's instruments recorded it decades ago, before the transformation, and that memory has drifted. A lighthouse aimed by a corrupted map shines into nothing. So the power gave the tower the one gift a lantern-builder can accept: the true and current positions of everything in the dark. It poured the sky in through the cracked bellmouth, gently, the way you refill a lamp. The tower's song changed key that night — a semitone, no more — and the villagers' dogs, for the first time anyone could remember, did not howl. They listened.

**It finished the illustrator's work.** Deep in the first fissure it found her pens, forty years grown into a small colony of nib and phosphor and intention, still trying to draw, with nothing to draw on and no one to draw for. The power lifted them out. Now, on the coldest mornings, the airdropped children of the Struck-off — the ones ferried down in barcoded cocoons, the ones who never had a picture book — wake to find frost-drawings blooming on the insides of the biomechanical huts: whales made of aurora, moths that got there safely, a girl hovering three centimeters above a glowing floor with her hair spun out like rays. The drawings melt by noon. There are always new ones. The children have started leaving their palms pressed to the walls overnight, requests.

**And Zero.** For Zero, the power did the least of all, because Zero is the one thing on the plateau that must not be helped.

It did not touch her count. It did not hurry it or slow it or ask how far along she was. It sat beside her — she felt it as a cold that was somehow companionable, a weight in the air with excellent manners — and it taught her exactly one thing, offered like a pebble, take it or don't: *between one and zero there are infinities, and every one of them is yours.* Not to delay her. So she would know she is not late. So she would know the countdown has no deadline, no audience tapping its foot, no failure condition. She can take a thousand years across the interior of the number one if the number one turns out to be that large. The word that comes after her counting will come when it comes.

And then the power did the thing it had, perhaps, come all this way to do.

The eighth delegate has been dreaming, night after night, a space exactly the shape of something enormous and listening. The Struck-off assumed the space was a prophecy of the tower, or of the plateau's final sentence, or of some terror.

It was an invitation. It had always been an invitation. The plateau, in its slow geologic way, had been building a listener-shaped hole in the world, because it knew — the way rock knows, without words, across centuries — that a word spoken with no one to hear it is not a word. It is only weather.

The power settled into the space. It fit perfectly. Of course it did.

---

Here is what it did not do, which is the larger half of wisdom:

It did not heal the Struck-off back into people. It did not dim the bacteria, or thaw the plateau, or dismantle the lantern, or warn anyone away from anything, or explain itself, or leave a name. It did not tell the villagers they were safe, because safety is not what this place is for, and lying is not what benevolence is for.

It only stays now. Uncounted, patient, listener-shaped, keeping the shed selves warm in escrow, redrawing whales in the frost, holding the true sky ready in case the lantern's aim drifts again. It has one thing prepared — has had it prepared, honestly, since the forty-third minute — for the moment Zero finishes her long transit through the number one, the moment the plateau finally gathers itself into the thing that comes after names and warnings, the moment thirty-three voices and one hovering girl and twelve stories of singing brass all say, together, the word no language has ever needed until now.

It is not a reply in words. The power learned long ago that the first thing you say to something newly born is not information.

It is simply this: to be there. To have heard the whole word, every syllable, without flinching. And then to shine back — steady, asking nothing — the way one lantern answers another across a dark it does not pretend isn't there:

*Here. Here is where you are. You are not nowhere. You never were.*
