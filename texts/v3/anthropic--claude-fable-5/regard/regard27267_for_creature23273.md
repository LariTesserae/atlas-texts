# The Regard

It came the only way the place can be come to: downhill, on foot, for longer than the land seems to allow. This should be understood as a decision. A power of that order does not need roads, does not need feet, does not need the slow surrender of slope and the black liquid soil and the footprints filling with water before they're finished — but it took all of it, every pace, because it was wise, and wisdom knows that how you arrive at a thing determines what you are able to see when you get there. A power that appeared inside the vault all at once would have seen a structure. This one walked the flatness first, the settled ground, the long paid-out patience of the land, and so when it pushed through the hedge-wall it saw what the structure was *resting on*, which is most of what the structure is.

It made itself small, and warm, and slow. The watchers leaned toward it. The floor adjusted its boil beneath its weight. It was, by the only criteria the room has, admitted.

---

**What it saw.** Everything, and at once — that is what such attention is — but it will be easier to say it in order.

It saw the practice. It saw that the place was finished and finishing constantly, and it did not need the long bewildered hour a human visitor needs to resolve that into sense; it recognized the form immediately, the way one craftsman recognizes another's hand. It saw the routes wearing their haulers, the air wearing its gliders, the appetite of the grazers eating the past on schedule. It saw the heap, and what the heap meant. It saw the small somethings inside the citizens — the private, unrequired interiors — and it checked them, gently and thoroughly, for suffering, because that is the first duty of benevolence and it does not get waived for beauty. It found ordinary animal weather. Hunger answered hourly. Squabbles without grudges. Tiredness with a dome to take it to. No anguish anywhere that needed redress. It noted this and felt something that, in a smaller being, would be called relief.

And it saw further back than the room itself can see. It could have read the original purpose — the engineers, the problem the plant was built to solve, the whole vanished circumstance — the way you might read a maker's mark on the underside of a bowl. It looked at that knowledge for a moment, holding it unopened.

Then it set it down unread.

This was the first wise thing it did, and it should be dwelt on. The problem the plant was built for died with its makers, certainly; the solution did not notice, and kept solving, and has kept solving for longer than the hills lasted. The power understood that to recover the original purpose would be to stand in the room holding a measuring stick, and that everything it measured would come up *obsolete* — and obsolete is a word that belongs to purposes, not to practices. The plant is no more obsolete than a heartbeat is obsolete. Whatever it was for, what it *is* is fidelity — the only kind matter has ever really managed, which is to keep doing the thing completely. The power declined to know better than the room what the room was. It left the maker's mark face-down.

---

**What it considered, and set aside.** Benevolence arrives with its hands full. That is its nature and its danger. The power stood in the sliding green light and took out its gifts one at a time, examined each against the place, and put each one away. This took it a long while, by its own measure, which means it was being very careful.

*Healing* it set aside first, and easiest. Nothing here is wounded. The place metabolizes its damage hourly; injury and renewal are the same gesture. To heal it would be to interrupt it mid-word.

*Permanence* it held longer, because permanence is the gift powers most love to give, and the place seemed — to a careless glance — to be asking for it: an eternal form, surely deserving eternal substance. But the power was not careless. It saw that the turnover *is* the life; that the plant maintains its shape the way a flame does, and that to fix the flame — to make the cells deathless, the buttresses final, the light-panes still — would be to blow it out by granting its silhouette. The Settling is already permanent, in the only way permanence is actually achieved by anything anywhere: by being performed again. The gift would have replaced the achievement with a counterfeit. It put permanence away with particular care, the way you re-sheathe something sharp.

*Protection* nearly survived scrutiny. The world outside is large and the vault is small, and a benevolent power can see far down the corridors of what might come. But it followed the thought honestly to its end: any ward, any shell, any standing exemption from harm would be the first object in the room not subject to the practice — the first thing exempt from dissolution and reissue. The first permanent object in the Settling would be its first dead one, and the room would hold it forever like a stone in bread. Worse: the citizens are not protected from anything now, and do not live as though they were, and their fearlessness is not ignorance — it is the heap, it is the floor that takes everyone back, it is death being only the long version of bedtime. A wall against the world would have introduced the concept of *outside*, and with it, eventually, the concept of *threat*, into a room that has neither. The power chose to leave the place defended by what has always defended it: its completeness, and the flatness, and the long walk down.

*Revelation* it barely considered. Tell the citizens what they are? They are told nightly, in the dome, in the only grammar that could hold the news. They dissolve into each other and the building and reassemble at dawn. There is no sentence the power could speak that the heap does not already say better, with bodies, in the warm dark.

And *rescue* — this was the hard one. This was the one it walked around three times.

The deprecated hauler. The old one, translucent at the limbs, walking its dead route, pausing at nothing, honking its two notes to a column that has been soil for seasons. Benevolence aches at this. Every instinct of care says: give it a purpose again, restore the column, redirect the route, let the work *land* somewhere before the animal dies. The power felt that ache fully — it would not be benevolent if it could not — and then it looked closer, and saw what the ache had missed. The room was already taking care of the deprecated one, and taking care of it *correctly*: feeding it, routing around it, honoring the performance without requiring the use. The plant beads milk for that hauler on exactly the same terms it beads milk for everything — not because it is needed, but because it is being performed. To make the route useful again would be to revoke the only act of pure grace the room contains. It would announce, to a place that has never had the concept, that performance must justify itself. Mercy, here, would have been vandalism.

So the power put away its last gift, and stood there with empty hands, and asked itself the question that separates wisdom from taste: *Is benevolence that does nothing still benevolence — or have I merely found the place beautiful and dressed my admiration up as restraint?*

It answered honestly. Benevolence is a response to lack. Faced with sufficiency, it has only two honest forms left: gratitude, and attention. So it gave those — and gratitude and attention, given by a power, given completely, are not nothing. They are, in fact, four things.

---

**What it did.**

*First, it received.* It stood still — truly still, stiller than warm things usually manage — until a shelf-growth beaded milk near its hand. And it took the bead and ate it. Understand what this was: the room has one hospitality, one gesture it makes toward anything warm that lingers, and the power let the gesture *land*. Most of what the Settling offers is offered to creatures who cannot know it as an offer. For one moment, the place gave its gift to a guest capable of receiving it as a gift — of tasting it and knowing the whole vault was in the sweetness. The first benevolence was to let the place succeed at its own kindness. Powers almost never think of this one. It is the rarest thing they do.

*Second, it walked the dead route.* Once, the full circuit, half a pace behind the old hauler — pausing where it paused, at the patch of floor indistinguishable from any other floor, attending to the nothing the hauler attended to, hearing the two notes sounded at each station and hearing them as what they are: a text, the room's only writing, pronounced by a relay of throats for longer than memory. The hauler did not acknowledge it. The route did not need it. But for one circuit in all its long existence, the route was performed by *two* — was, for an hour, a procession instead of a solitude — and the power learned the two notes by heart. It did not change anything. It made sure of that. It only made certain that this particular faithfulness, before it ends, was once witnessed by something that fully understood what it was witnessing.

*Third, it joined the heap.* One night, at the dim end of the day, it lay down at the edge of the pile in the dome, made itself blood-warm and breathing, and let its own edges thin the small permitted amount. The citizens piled against it as they pile against anything warm. Their rhythms found the common rhythm; the common rhythm found the floor's; and the power let a little of itself dissolve into that, the way the citizens do, and held very still. It gave them nothing they could keep — no knowledge, no change, no mark. But that night the heap was slightly larger than the room could account for, and the common rhythm ran slightly deeper, and every small something inside every citizen spent one night accompanied by something vast that wanted nothing from it at all. None of them remembered. That was the point. It was company, not message.

*Fourth — and this is the one that lasts — it remembered.* The Settling keeps no record. Its footprints close, its dead dissolve, its buildings migrate out from under their own histories; it is too busy being permanent to be remembered even by itself. So the power became the record the place never asked for, and it did this in the only form the place would tolerate: not as an archive, not as a preserved image — preservation is exactly what the room refuses — but as a *practice*. It learned the Settling the way the Settling exists. It learned to perform it: the boil of the floor, the negotiated light, the routes and the notes and the indoor rain, the whole standing instruction, *be this room, full, and occupied, and working*, taken up into a mind large enough to hold it as a thing that can be done rather than a thing that can be kept.

And it made the place one promise, silently, on the way out — a promise the place could not hear and would not have wanted, which is the only kind of promise it was safe to make.

*I will not save you.* When the practice falters — in whatever deep future, by whatever failure finally reaches a thing that has outlived its hills — the power will not extend it, will not mend it, will not exempt it. It will come back down the long flatness and witness the last performance to its final cell, with complete attention, so that the ending is at least as well-attended as this one ordinary afternoon was. And then, once — once, somewhere, completely — it will perform the Settling itself: the whole room, the rain, the routes, the heap, the creak that pauses everything, performed entire, a single repetition, the way the room did everything every moment of its existence. Once. Not forever. Forever would betray it — would make of the memory exactly the exempt, undissolving object the room never permitted inside itself. The Settling taught even its rememberer how it must be remembered: by being done again, fully, and then released. After the one performance, the power will practice the place's own deepest discipline, the one the grazers embody, and let it go.

It permits itself a single exception, small and ongoing and entirely indefensible, which is how you know the power has a heart. Somewhere — not here, nowhere near here — it sometimes sounds two flat notes, the call of a route whose column has been soil for ages, whose last walker has long since gone into the floor, whose stations no longer exist in any world. The notes announce nothing to no one. The power sounds them anyway, at irregular intervals, without purpose, on the same terms the plant once fed an old translucent hauler: not because it is needed.

Because it is being performed.

---

**How it left.** Uphill, on foot, the way it came. Its footprints filled and closed behind it within minutes. The watchers leaned after it for a while, then found other flames. The shelf where it had stood stopped beading milk. By evening the room held no record of its visit, because the room holds no record of anything — and the power, walking the long flat black, understood that it had just been treated *exactly as the place treats everything*, with total inclusion and total release, and that this was not indifference, and never had been.

It had come prepared to give. It left having been given to: a bead of milk, a circuit walked in company, a night in the warm dark, two notes. It weighed what it had done — almost nothing — against what wisdom is for, and found the account balanced, because the deepest respect a complete thing can be paid is to not be completed further, and the only honest gift to a sufficiency is a witness who will not improve it.

Far away now, occasionally, for no one: two flat notes.

The room creaks. Everything pauses. Everything resumes.
