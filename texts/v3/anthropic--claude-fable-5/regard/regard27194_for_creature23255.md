**The Visitor**

It arrived the only way the plain permits arrival, which is slowly, from a great distance, announced an hour ahead like rain. The people watched it come the way they watch weather — without alarm, without ceremony, adjusting nothing — and by the time it stood ankle-deep in the lower sky, gray and tall and motionless among the domes, it had already been given an acre. No one decided this. The water decided it, the way water decides everything here, finding the level where the new presence crowded nothing.

The power had a great deal of practice at arriving. It had spent — *spent* is the right word — long ages on worlds that begged, worlds mid-catastrophe, worlds in the loud unfinished middle of themselves, and it had developed the habits of such work: the quick eye for the wound, the full hands. It came, as such powers do, carrying an inventory. It is worth setting down what was in the inventory, and what happened to each item, because the disposition of gifts was nearly the whole of what the power did here, and the manner of the disposing was most of its wisdom.

It considered height first, because height is the cheapest miracle — a mountain raised, a tower offered, some vantage from which the people might survey their world and want more of it. It looked at the domes, the last vertebrae of mountains, polished down past even the memory of slopes, and understood that it was standing on the corpse of that gift, already given, already refused, already worn into something better. The plain had taken height's full argument and answered it over a hundred million years, and the answer was the floor. The power set height down on the water and watched it redistribute into nothing.

It considered abundance — thicker shoals, deeper reeds, the rest of the plants arriving at last to join the small committee in the shallows. But abundance is a gift for the hungry, and the people grazed all day in a feeding ground paved with snails, and more would only crowd the acres, and crowding the acres was the one poverty the plain knew how to have. Set down. Redistributed.

It considered knowledge, which was harder to put down, because the power loved knowledge the way some beings love their children. Writing, it thought. Writing first, always writing. And then it stood in the haze and worked out what writing is *for*, which is to whisper across distance to one person at a time — and there was no whispering here, no architecture for it, every word said anywhere already belonging to the whole plain. These people had no need to send a sealed message; they had never sealed anything. Their entire literature was in the air at all times, being handed. A library, the power conceded, is a building. Set down.

It considered protection, and could find nothing to protect against. It considered medicine, and found death conducted here as a walk to a weeping seam, finished conversations, a Tone held a half-step low for a day — found, in other words, death already domesticated, already a neighbor with an acre of its own. It considered immortality and understood that they would refuse it, politely, over the course of about a year, on the grounds — they would never say this, but the power could hear it under their speech the way the seep runs under the chorus — that a grave is a building, but so is a life that will not end. Everything here is planed. They would not be made into towers.

So the inventory was empty, and the power stood in the warm shallows holding nothing, which it had not done in a very long time, and this was the first thing the plain gave *it*.

---

Then it did what wisdom does after benevolence has been disarmed: it looked for the wound anyway. Every place has one. It went through the country the way a fingertip goes through wool, feeling for the burr, and it found it, and the wound was this —

The Tone has never been silent.

That is not a strength. That is a single chain across ten thousand years, and a chain is only as merciful as its worst night. The power ran the chain forward through time the way such beings can, not seeing the future so much as feeling its grain, and somewhere down the years it found the night: a fever season, something coming up out of the warm water itself, the strong singers lying folded on the dome tops with the heat in them, and one old woman, far out on the flats, holding the note alone past the second dusk, her breath going thin, the frogs roaring on indifferent, the gap widening behind her voice like a crack opening in stone — and no one near enough, and no one well enough, and the handing about to fail. Not the note. The note was nothing. The *handing*, the unbroken thing, the heron's nest of air, the only monument — about to wash away with no one to rebuild it, because unlike the nest it could not be rebuilt. A silence of one minute would be a silence forever. That was the whole of the plain's fragility, the entire vulnerability of a finished world, concentrated in one tired throat on one bad night.

Here is what the power did. It did not cure the fever — the fever was the plain's, part of the warm water's old chemistry, and the people had survived a hundred of them and made the survivals into the slow bending of their note. It did not strengthen the woman, or wake the sleepers, or still the frogs.

It took an acre.

It went far out, beyond the farthest singer, into the haze where a person standing still is indistinguishable from a stone, and it spent — again, *spent* — what for it was an idle afternoon and what for the plain was a long span of seasons learning the place the way the place insists on being learned. It learned the spacing. It learned the gaps the frogs leave, the rhythm old past counting. It learned the Tone not as a sound but as a *handing*, grain by grain, until it could have held the note as it was sung a thousand years ago or as it would be sung in a thousand more, and it waited, standing in the lower sky, doing the long slow nothing that the people conduct with such seriousness, and discovering — this was the second gift the plain gave it — that the nothing was not empty. It had stood guard over burning worlds with less attention than it now paid to an evening.

And on the night the chain came down to one thin voice, the power took the Tone.

Its note arrived out of the haze, low and flat and unhurried, threading the frog-gaps correctly, bent correctly, pitched from far enough away that the old woman, letting her own voice fall at last, settling into the warm water with the seep loud in her body, heard only what anyone would hear: a far neighbor, taking up the handing. Which is what it was. The power held the note through the dark and into the gray morning, and when the first recovered singer rose on a dome top and reached for the Tone, the power passed it as it had been taught, a near voice and a far voice overlapping, and let its own fall, and the chain went on, and no one ever knew.

Or rather — and this matters, because these people do not lie, and the power would not make its one act here into a kind of lie — *everyone* heard it. Every word said anywhere on the plain belongs to the whole plain. The whole country heard a voice that night that no one could place, and the people discussed it, slowly, over the following year, at six hundred yards, and concluded that someone far out had given the note when it needed giving, and that this was the obvious thing, the only thing, the thing their language had already settled by making *speak* and *give* one word. They were not wrong about any of it. The power's secret was not a concealment. It was merely a farness, and farness here is the ordinary distance at which one loves.

The note bent that night by a fraction — every voice bends it, a grain a year, like the stone. Somewhere in the Tone as it is sung now, there is one grain that is the power's. That is the entire mark it left on the world, and it is the proudest thing it has ever done.

---

Two matters remain, and then the going.

The first is the sleeper. The power had known from the moment of arriving — known the way one old patient thing knows another across a room — that the floor was occupied. It did not pry. Prying is a kind of building; you have to put up scaffolding of questions. Instead, on an afternoon with the rain-columns walking the far flats, it waded to the base of a dome, and crouched, and laid the flat of its hand on the warm sweating stone, and held it there a moment, and took it away.

*Still going.*

And for the space of about an hour — the people noticed it, and mentioned it to each other across the miles over the following weeks, the way one mentions a particularly good dusk — the seep ran a little quicker everywhere at once, the domes weeping freely, the whole plain's faint universal trickle rising for a while under the chorus like an answer given in the only language the floor permits itself.

*Still going.*

That was the entire exchange. Two powers, one of which had won every argument it ever fought and one of which had won by conceding everything, and between them nothing needed but the gesture. The power has spoken since with gods and engines and the ministers of dying suns, and it ranks that hour of quickened water among the great conversations of its existence.

The second matter is the rest. Before it left, the power did something it had not done in epochs, because there had never been anywhere to do it: it climbed a low dome in the evening, and folded itself down on the warm stone among the far-flung constellation of sleepers, and the people routed their voices around it — *a sleeper among them is sacred in a small practical way* — and it lay there with the frog-roar coming up through the rock into whatever it has instead of a sternum, and it rested. It, the tender of unfinished worlds, the carrier of inventories, was given an acre and fed silence and ministered to by a plain that did not know it was doing anything, because what it was doing was only what it does. This was the third gift, and the largest: the demonstration, performed all day every day by water and stone and standing gray figures, that a finished thing is not a dead thing. That completion is an occupancy. That there is a way to be done becoming and still be loud with life. The power had genuinely not known this. None of its worlds had ever been allowed to find out; it had always arrived with its hands full before they could.

It took that home in place of everything it had brought.

---

It left the way the rain leaves — by becoming, gradually, indistinguishable from the haze. And it took one thing with it, which was not theft, because the thing it took cannot be diminished by carrying: it took the Tone. It holds it still, somewhere, at the bottom of itself, the way the people hold it across the dusk water — not preserving a note, preserving a handing — and on the raw young worlds where it works now, the loud unfinished ones, the ones mid-argument with their water and their heights, there is sometimes, under all the noise of building, a low flat far-carrying note that no one can place. The builders pause when they hear it. They do not know what it is. It is the plain's one monument, sending out — after all this time, in the only way a monument of air can — its first colony. Not of people. Of patience.

And the plain itself the power changed not at all, which was the point, and which took everything it had. It is still there now, lying flat in the warm light, settling another fraction of an inch into its comfortable deafening rest, full of water and birds and standing voices, ringing — bent, in its single endless note, by exactly one grain that came from somewhere else, and that no one there has ever needed to explain, because *speak* and *give* are the same word, and a far neighbor is still a neighbor, and the obvious thing, when the note needs giving, is to give it.
