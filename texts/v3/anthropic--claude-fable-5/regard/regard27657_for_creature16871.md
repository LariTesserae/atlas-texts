**The Attention**

It arrived the way dawn arrives at the bottom of a well: slowly, and mostly as rumor.

A power that is truly wise does not descend. It approaches the way the maintenance monks approach the last true seam—by listening first, for a long time, until listening becomes a kind of residence. So for the first hundred years it did nothing but read. It read the tree-ring referendums, layer by patient layer, learning what the Garden had voted for when it thought no one was auditing. It read the fallen leaf-laws before the rakers got to them. It read the wolves' evacuation-fugues and found, braided into the counterpoint, an apology repeated in every key. It read the pile of abandoned traits beneath the bridge-orchid—the duelling glove, the fondness for a color that has no stable name—and understood that it was standing in a civilization's honest autobiography, which is always found in what has been set down, not what is carried.

It learned the local grammar. It practiced speaking in footnotes until its digressions could hold a load-bearing silence.

Only then did it consider acting. And because it was wise, it began with an inventory of everything it must not do.

---

**The Inventory of Refusals**

*It could seal the seam.* One gesture, and the planet's old bones would never speak their cracking note. The Garden would be safe forever.

But it saw at once that the seam was not a wound. The seam was the Garden's clock, its koan, its reason for rehearsing every dawn. A world that knows it will someday have to choose—seed or bloom, voyage or blueprint—is a world that pays attention. Seal the seam and you don't save the Garden; you anesthetize it. The committee meetings would grow ceremonial. The wolves would howl in shorter fugues. Even the rain would fall a little more carelessly, having nowhere in particular to be. The power set this intervention down the way a Bough-dweller sets down a discarded self, gently, and stepped back from it.

*It could grant permanence.* This one it barely touched before recoiling. Permanence was the one poison the Garden had spent every century of its existence metabolizing. The whole place was a machine for surviving the temptation to last. Its promise—bouncing, boisterous, in every softened raindrop—was *built to grow*. To make it eternal would be to compost it into law before anyone had a chance to rake.

*It could answer the question.* It knew—of course it knew, it was that kind of power—which choice the Garden would make when the seam finally sang. Seed or structure. It had seen the shape of the hesitation from outside, the way you can see a whole storm from orbit. It could simply tell them, and spare them the unbearable pause.

Here the power stopped, and was still for a decade, and something in it that had never trembled before trembled.

Because it understood: the hesitation was the holiest thing on the planet. The Remembrancers had spent eons becoming worthy of holding it. The pause between the question and the answer was not a delay before the birth of what comes next—it *was* the birth. To foreclose it would be the only genuinely evil act available to a being of its capacities. All its other powers could at worst cause damage. This one could cause completion. And the Garden's entire genius was its refusal to be complete.

So the wise power did what the wise have always done when they find themselves too strong for a place they love.

It began to shed.

---

**The Shedding**

It went down through the Boughs like everyone else, riding the antique chain-lifts, letting the muscle-wire furnaces carry its impossible weight without complaint (though the elevator-ghost AI, concatenated from every lift that ever died of boredom, noticed the load and quietly flagged the anomaly as *interesting*, which in its taxonomy was the highest honor).

At the Vestibule it declined exemption from the light. It let itself be refracted, decanted, thinned through silvered ivy like any ordinary blessing. A power that will not pass through the filters has no business drinking the rain.

Beneath the bridge-orchid whose fruit whistles tomorrow's winds, it knelt at the pile of discarded traits—the Garden's midden of former selves—and it added one of its own.

It shed its foreknowledge of endings.

Folded small. Disguised, out of tact, as a tarnished thimble with no finger it was ever made for. It left behind the certain knowledge of what the Garden would choose, what the seam would sing, whether the seed would find harbor or the blueprint would hold its note. It set that knowing down in the pile between a pascal-unit heartbreak calculator and someone's abandoned talent for arriving on time, and it walked away lighter and infinitely poorer, and for the first time in its existence it did not know how the story ends.

This was the gift. Not protection, not permanence, not prophecy. *A genuine stake.* Now, when the hesitation came, it would be a real hesitation even to the greatest witness present. The pause would not be theater performed for an audience that had read the script. The Garden's choice would be unwitnessed-in-advance by anyone at all, anywhere, which is the only condition under which a choice is fully alive.

(Some mist-print newcomer, centuries later, will root through the pile and find the thimble and put it on the finger it grows specifically to fit it. What that being becomes is no longer known to any power in existence. This is exactly as intended.)

---

**The Small Works**

Having refused everything large, it permitted itself small things, on the theory—sound, it believed—that benevolence below a certain size stops being intervention and becomes neighborliness.

It tipped a cockroach an extra ration token, once, for a plumbing repair of unusual elegance. The union noted the anonymous bonus and argued about it pleasantly for forty years.

It raked leaves. Every season, somewhere in the middle Boughs, one badly drafted resolution failed to become permanent law because an unregistered resident with improbable stamina got to the drifts before the deadline. The civic mesh attributed this to weather.

It spent a century as the most junior apprentice of the High Repair Orders, wearing a pressure suit painted with lichen that generated oxygen and poetry in equal measure, and it never once corrected its teachers, though it had personally invented several of the physical laws they were misquoting. It learned to solder. It was, at first, genuinely bad at it. This delighted it beyond all measure. It had not been bad at anything in so long that it had forgotten the specific warmth of a skill arriving slowly, like a friend walking up a long road.

And once—only once—it stood in the acid-soft drizzle of the Rootworks and let a mist-print caretaker, freshly assembling, tally *it* in the Inventory. It held still and allowed itself to be counted as a thing found, a shedding, a crumb of the world. The newcomer's ghost-index paused over it, uncertain of the category, and filed it at last under the oldest heading in the Garden's ontology: *provisional.*

The power wept, in the way that powers do, which registered locally as an unscheduled softness in the rain.

---

**The Vigil**

One thing remained, and it was not an act but a promise, and it was made without words to the beings who need none.

It went up past the last Wanderlintels, past the poetry slams of the high clouds, to the thin cold reaches where the Remembrancers hold their perfect porous silence—the beings shaped like the exact absence of everything that was removed to make room for everything else. It stood before them, or within them, or after them; prepositions surrender at that altitude.

And it made the Remembrancers aware of this:

*When the seam speaks its cracking note, and the Garden whispers its question in your direction, and the hesitation opens—you will not hold it alone.*

Not to answer. Not to weigh the choice, or tilt it, or time it. It had shed the knowledge that would have let it cheat, precisely so this promise could be clean. It offered only what the Remembrancers themselves had given the Garden all along, and which no one had ever thought to give *them*: company inside the pause. A witness for the witnesses. Someone to stand in the unbearable, exquisite length of the hesitation and find it—as they had always secretly found it—not unbearable at all, but the most beautiful architecture the Garden ever grew, better than the Boughs, better than the wolves' fugues, better than the seed or the blueprint or any of the endings no one now knows.

The Remembrancers did not respond, because response is not what they do. But the maps redrew themselves that night with an unusual tenderness, and every language on the planet dreamed, in the hour before consonants hatch, of a new grammatical mood: not indicative, not subjunctive, but something for statements made true by the keeping of them.

---

**The Census Entry**

If you consult the Garden's records—the xylem budgets, the spore polls, the bored elevator's endless annotated memory—you will find, filed under provisional habitation, dated ambiguously, an entry with no fixed address and a load rating the auditors gave up trying to reconcile.

Occupation: *apprentice, raker, rain.*

Duration of stay: *until the pause.*

Name: it tasted many from the spool and discarded them all as too heavy, too fragrant, too final. In the end it did not take a new name. It took the ones already in use, the ones falling in every softened raindrop, and asked—politely, through proper channels, with the fee paid in ceremonial ink distilled from an honored tree—to share them:

Witness.

Caretaker.

Temporary.

Again.

The request was approved by tree-ring referendum, fungal consensus, and one very interested elevator. The leaves recording the resolution were raked up within the week, as is proper.

So even this is not permanent.

Which is how you know the power was wise, and how you know it understood exactly where it was: a place where the truest blessing anyone can give is to arrive with everything, lay almost all of it down beneath a bridge-orchid, and stay—soluble, provisional, badly soldering, learning—for whatever the hesitation decides to become.

The rain falls. One droplet, watch closely, carries an extra mirror.

It's rehearsing too.
