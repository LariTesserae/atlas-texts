It arrives the way the petrel arrived: by preference, off the books, unannounced. Powers in the old stories come as light, or as a great stillness, and it understands within moments that neither will serve here. There is no stillness to come as. The light is spoken for — aurora, the gray-violet smear, the less-dark green. So it comes as the one thing the valley has no native supply of: attention that does not blink.

**What it does first is nothing, for a long time.**

A season. Then another. It has seen succession before — has watched it at the honest speed, the nine-thousand-year version, lichen to crust to saxifrage to shade — and it checks this valley against those memories the way you check a signature against a known hand. It is looking, frankly, for forgery. It finds none. The hand is hurried but it is the same hand: pioneer, canopy, collapse, soil, the whole old sentence written at speed without a word out of order. The visitors standing in the freezing rain already arrived at the verdict, and the power, examining the place at depths no visitor can reach, can only countersign it: *nothing about it is strange. It is only fast.* Wisdom, it notes with some pleasure, is well distributed here. It will not be needed for diagnosis.

**Then it goes looking for the wound, because that is what benevolence is for.**

It hunts the way the foxes hunt: not listening to the roar but listening for holes in it — for the place where something is missing, suffering, falling short.

It considers the voles first, the screaming brief ones, because brevity at that volume looks like anguish from outside. It is not. The power listens all the way into the scream and finds it is address, not pain — *here I am, here I am, here I am* — the sound of a small animal refusing to be lost in a world too loud to be quiet in. And the mercy a vole could want was installed before the voles arrived: warm seams, endless seed, ground that never freezes. Four months of warmth, food, and family, lived at full voice. The power has seen worse lives that lasted forty years. It moves on.

It considers the fungi and their four percent, the question the stewards can't decide — limit found, or limit set? The power looks and sees that the question is wrongly shaped. The four percent is neither a wall the fungi hit nor a ration the lattice grants. It is an opening offer. The first terms of a negotiation that will run for centuries, conducted by two parties neither of which knows it is negotiating, out of which will come — slowly, wordlessly — something like a partnership and possibly something like a marriage. The power backs out of that seam very carefully, the way you back out of a room where something delicate has just begun.

It considers the lattice itself, all the way down. The engineers are right: it does not think. The treaty-writers are also right, and the power admires the wording for a long moment, because the witnessing clause is wiser than law usually manages to be. The lattice is not alive and is not dead and is not waiting; it is a thing whose category has not been invented yet, in a valley whose category has not been invented yet, and the correct relation to such a thing is exactly what the treaty requires: stay near it, in person, within the sound of it, and watch. The power adds its own attention to the clause. Unsigned.

It considers the ninth conifer for a long time. It knows what the conifer is — knows whether the writing is drifting or whether the writers wrote better than they understood — and it tells no one, and it will not tell you. What it does instead is look away. Deliberately, the way you look away from a child who is practicing. Some becoming must go unwatched, even in a valley founded on witnessing; *especially* in a valley founded on witnessing. The conifers it leaves to their privacy.

It pauses over the owls, in something close to kinship. They surrendered ten million years of silence to live where they are — set down the soft wing, the noiseless stoop, the whole inherited dramaturgy, because the place they loved had no use for it. The power, which has spent eternities arriving as a hush, has just had to do the same thing. It perches its attention beside them on the pylons, the unswaying verticals, and watches the dark with them for a while, loudly, in its way.

**Then the question of intervention, which it settles mostly by refusal.**

It will not quiet anything. The noise is the valley's speech, and you do not improve a speaker by stopping their mouth.

It will not slow anything. Tempo here is not a symptom; it is the constitution.

It will not extend a single life. Brevity is the currency, and the body is communal — every animal built from every animal before it, the place one continuous flesh wearing forty thousand temporary faces. To exempt one face would be to tear it from the body. That is not a gift. That is an exile.

It will not answer the open questions — the four percent, the lattice, the conifer — because the questions are load-bearing. The stewards' uncertainty is a form of attention, and the attention is the point. Answer the question and the attending ends.

And it will not bless the valley into safety. The debris flows have right of way. The rivers vote. The survey station drowned and nothing went wrong. So be it, says the power, in the spirit of the people who walked away from that station, whom it is coming to respect a great deal.

What remains, after all that refusal, is small. Three things. Perhaps four.

**One: the knoll.** Memory is the only fragile thing in the valley. Everything else is redundant beyond insult — the voles backed up forty-thousand-fold, the trees mere waves through standing surf, the channels redrawn nightly without loss. But the logbooks exist once. So: henceforth, very quietly, luck compounds on the knoll. Nothing is forbidden. The eastern river will feint at it twice in the next century and both times find the far bank's gravel more interesting. A debris flow will part around the rock like a congregation around an altar and rejoin below, and future stewards will praise the original surveyors for their siting. The original surveyors chose the knoll because it was the only dry place to land a helicopter. Luck has to start somewhere.

**Two: the parting gift.** Departing stewards find silence thin afterward, suspicious, like a held breath — the world gone wrong by subtraction. The power lets each of them keep one sound. For the rest of their lives, on the very edge of sleep, once a night: the glacier. Distant. Soft. Like a heart in the next room. None of them will ever mention it, except that the woman of eleven dark seasons, very old someday and very far south, will say to whoever is sitting with her — *there it is* — and smile, and the person at the bedside will hear nothing at all, and that will be fine.

**Three: it reads.** Every logbook, every entry, back to the first rotation — the year the eastern river jumped, the first climbing fox, the petrel's arrival, the seventh and eighth conifers. Because preservation is not what memory actually wants. Memory wants a reader. The logbooks are letters, kept against a century when someone will want to know what the roaring used to be, and the stewards half-understand that they are writing to someone. The power makes it true. The someone exists. The letters are received.

**And the fourth, which may not count as a doing.** On a night of strong aurora, when the crossbill flocks go still and silent — the only silence ever recorded here, the valley's whole voice held like a breath, ten thousand small faces turned up through the canopy gaps — the power looks back. That is all. Nothing changes. Nothing is recorded that was not recorded before. But the senior steward's old entry was truer than she knew when she wrote it. *They watch it. So do we.* And something watches the watching, and finds it — the oldest word, the second review after the petrel's, and likewise favorable — good.

**Then it stays.**

The treaty says witnessing: continuous, in person, within the sound of it. Two stewards at a time, says the roster.

There have been three for some years now. The third does not rotate.

It intends to be here in two centuries, when the surf finally settles, when the valley slows and quiets and becomes merely a forest — old at last, the channels lazy, the trees permitted their age — and some steward, because there will still be stewards, witnessing having long outlived need, takes the first logbooks down and reads them aloud in the cabin, so the forest can hear what its silence used to sound like when it was a roar.

The power means to be at that reading. That is the whole of its plan.

Until then it does what the valley does, which is the most ordinary thing in the world, performed at a pressure that turns ordinariness into devotion: it pays attention, in the dark, at full volume, and lets everything become.
