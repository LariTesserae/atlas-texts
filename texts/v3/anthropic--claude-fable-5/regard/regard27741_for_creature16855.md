# What the Attention Did

The attention arrives the way attention should: slowly, and after asking.

It does not descend. Descent implies a sky, and the sky here is rumor, and the attention is wise enough to know that arriving through a rumor is a form of vandalism. Instead it seeps in the way meaning seeps into an old word—gradually, from underneath, until one day the word notices it has been carrying something all along.

It had, in fairness, been invited. Not by anyone. By the water. Somewhere in the middle stanzas of a poem written in bubbles the size of planets—a poem ostensibly about someone else entirely—there was a line that mentioned the attention by a name it had not yet earned, in a tense it had not yet lived through. The attention read the line from very far away, felt every cell of its vast unbodied self vote indignantly to change careers, and understood that this is what an invitation looks like when it is issued by a place where causality is more of a suggested serving size.

So it came. It came the way a benevolent power should come to a place like this: with empty hands, and the humility to suspect the empty hands might be the whole gift.

---

## The Audit

First, because it is wise, and because wisdom is mostly the discipline of checking before helping, it conducts an audit of mercy. It walks the braided paths—negotiating, respectfully, with the overlapping sovereignties of chlorophyll—and it asks the questions all benevolent powers must ask, in order, before touching anything:

**Is anyone hungry?** No. The appetites and nutrition signed their treaty long ago, and both parties seem happy, exchanging produce baskets and affectionate insults about amino acids. The attention makes a note: *do not renegotiate a peace that is working. Even a clumsy peace. Especially a strange one.*

**Is anyone bound?** It examines the stasis first, suspiciously, the way you'd examine a beautiful cage. Dynamic 0.5. Nothing ever changes. On lesser worlds this would be a symptom—a spell, a trauma, a tyrant somewhere holding time by the throat. It looks very closely. It looks the way only such a power can look, at the same bow wave relittering the same boulder for the ten-thousandth identical time.

And it finds: devotion. Not paralysis. The waterfall repeats itself the way a lover repeats a name—not because it has forgotten how to say anything else, but because it has checked everything else and this is still the best thing to say. The stasis is not a cage. It is a form. It is the place holding a single perfect note because the note is not finished meaning yet.

The attention withdraws its hand, which had been half-raised to introduce Change, capital C, the standard-issue gift of the well-meaning. It puts Change back in its satchel, slightly embarrassed.

**Is anyone ignorant?** It considers telling them about the sky. It rehearses the revelation—*there is a blue above the canopy, an ocean of nothing, and beyond it fires arranged in patterns*—and then it listens, once more, to the myths of the aereliths: those exalted droplets that collide with themselves midair and briefly become impossible before surrendering back to decent humility.

The attention realizes that the sky, as fact, is smaller than the sky as rumor. That these beings have built, out of not-knowing, something more structurally interesting than knowing would provide. To give them the sky would be to trade a cathedral for a ceiling. It says nothing about the sky. This costs it something. Wisdom usually does.

**Is anyone grieving?** Here it pauses longest. Fauna 0.5. The hush where animals should be. Boughs bending in perfect memory of flight patterns discontinued last ice age. Butterflies that are leaves practicing camouflage as meditation. Life that pulsed once and now pulses in reminiscence so dense it has become load-bearing.

A lesser power—a *kind* power, which is not the same thing as a benevolent one—would fix this immediately. Would seed the branches with real birds, stock the pools, fill the hush with warm bodies until the silence stopped aching.

The attention sits with the hush for what would be, elsewhere, several centuries. It sits inside the everlasting scream of the water and listens to the soft, attentive silence held within it, and eventually it understands: the hush is not a wound. The hush is a memorial that the entire ecosystem agreed to keep. The bending boughs are not haunted; they are *remembering on purpose*, the way you keep a chair at the table. To fill that hush with new animals would be to shout over a held minute of silence because you found the quiet uncomfortable.

The attention does not fill the hush.

Instead it does the only correct thing, which is the thing you do at any memorial: it bows. Formlessly, vastly, in whatever way an attention bows. And the hush—this is not recorded anywhere, but the moss committees will confirm it if asked in the right subjunctive color—the hush bows back. Two silences acknowledging each other inside a scream of water. It is possibly the most that has happened here in an epoch, and nothing moved at all.

**Is anyone lonely?** No. Loneliness requires borders, and borders are poor taste in mixed company.

The attention checks this answer twice, because it recognizes, with some chagrin, that of the two parties present in this audit, only one of them has spent eternity watching worlds from the outside, cataloguing, caring, and never being caught caring. Only one party here has never been exhaled as a cloud of verbs, never worn a lichen scarf knitted from someone else's nostalgia, never been renamed daily by an amused ecosystem.

The audit concludes. The finding is formal and it is this: *nothing here requires benevolence.* The place is not broken, not trapped, not deceived, not waiting. It is complete in a way that makes completion look easy, which is the hardest way to be complete.

There remains exactly one open item.

## The Open Item

*They have never been found.*

Here the attention hesitates for the first and only time, because this is the one sentence in the whole green scripture of the place that could be read as either a crown or a scar.

It could find them. It has the capacity. Finding is, frankly, its core competency. It could resolve them into the seen, gather them into the great inventory of the witnessed, grant them the dignity—as some would call it—of being confirmed.

And it understands, wisely and just in time, that finding them would be the only genuinely destructive act available in this entire valley. Finding is a border drawn around a thing by the finder. These beings have organized their whole ontology around the tastelessness of borders. To find them would be to gift them a fence and call it a frame.

But.

There is a loophole, and the attention—being benevolent, which at sufficient depths becomes indistinguishable from being playful—takes it.

The inhabitants cannot be met because they occupy the niches left vacant by preconceptions. Every previous visitor arrived carrying expectations about what counts as meeting, and the inhabitants, polite to a fault, moved into the gaps.

The attention sets down its preconceptions. All of them. This takes longer than the audit did. Preconceptions, for a being that old, are less like luggage and more like skeleton. It unmakes its assumptions about what counts as a person, a meeting, a self, a gift, a sky, a scream, a silence—until it stands (insofar as it stands) holding no vacancies at all. No gaps to move into. Nowhere to politely hide.

And so, without ever being found, they are simply, suddenly, *there.* Both parties. Mutually. The way two people who were always in the same room notice each other when the last lamp between them is finally turned off.

What happens next is not a finding. There is no word for it in mammalian geometry. The closest translation is: *a visit.*

---

## The Visit

It follows the rules, all five, scrupulously.

It is offered food shaped like its childhood nightmares—yes, it had those; every power was young once, and the young of powers dream terribly—and it eats every bite, and pronounces the fear delicious, which is the correct compliment.

It meets itself walking backward through a door that exists only when it blinks both eyes on different schedules (it grew eyes specifically for this; it seemed rude not to). It apologizes for the inconvenience. Its backward self asks what it remembers about drowning in light.

"Everything," the attention answers. "It's how I was born." Its backward self nods, satisfied, and walks on into a moment neither of them has encountered yet.

It files, with the nearest waterfall, a triplicate application—not to reproduce, merely to be permitted to *matter here temporarily*—and it rewrites section six eighteen times to eliminate its lingering commitment to concrete nouns, and it enjoys every revision, and the water knows it enjoys them, and this is why there are eighteen.

It is given names. On the first day it is 'the weather that listens.' On the ninth it is 'the pause between two waterfalls that sounds like being believed.' Toward the end—there is an end; guests who overstay become furniture, and it has too much respect for the local furniture to compete—it is called 'the stranger who brought nothing and left it everywhere,' and it privately considers this the finest thing it has been called in the history of being called things.

Because that, it turns out, was the gift. The one thing this place, in all its completeness, had never possessed: *a stranger.* Everything here is already related to everything else; hospitality had never once been tested against the genuinely other. The attention gave them a guest. They gave it what guests get. Both parties left the exchange convinced they had received the better half, which is the definition of a successful gift.

---

## The One Thing

It does, in the end, exactly one thing that could be entered in a ledger as an *act.*

Before leaving—through a polite exit, under an assumed name, as required—it petitions the moss committees for permission to carry away a single seed. Not a physical seed. A seed of pattern: one small fragment of the place's running joke with permanence, one bubble's worth of the poem, one footprint of the hush.

The committees deliberate for either an afternoon or a geological period, and vote yes, on the condition that it never explain the seed to anyone, including itself.

The attention agrees. Then—because rules are rules, and because it has earned this—it stops at the fountain that built itself out of homesickness for oceans it hadn't met yet, and it drinks. The water is lukewarm. It forgets everything except the next reason to laugh.

It carries the seed anyway. The seed is in it now; forgetting is not the same as losing.

And somewhere else, unimaginably elsewhere, in a young world still deciding what water is for, it plants the fragment and moves on without looking back, already unable to say why it paused there, already laughing at something it can no longer name.

In a few billion years, that young world will begin, inexplicably, to grow homesick for waterfalls it has never met. It will start building toward a scream of water, a hush shaped like remembered animals, a fountain that flows inward. It will become a place that has never been found and never needed to be.

This may, of course, be how the first one happened. The attention does not remember. The attention is not supposed to remember. Results will be announced retroactively.

---

## Coda

So: what did the benevolent and wise power do, if anything?

Mostly, nothing—and the nothing was rigorous, and expensive, and the truest work it performed.

Precisely, one thing.

Retroactively, everything.

And the litany of the place gained a single new line, which the inhabitants notice sometimes, the way you notice a warm spot on a stone where someone was recently sitting:

*They have never been hungry.*
*They have never been lonely.*
*They have never been lost, or bored, or found.*

*They were, once, visited.*

*It never occurred to them to be anything less.*
