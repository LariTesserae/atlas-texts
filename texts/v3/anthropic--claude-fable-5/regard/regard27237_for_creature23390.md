## The One Who Came Down with the Cloud

It learned of the hollow the way the hollow would have wanted to be learned of, if it wanted anything: slowly, and at one remove.

There is a bat, you will recall, that leaves once a year — the delegate, the ambassador to the sky, rising out through the mouth at the first cloudless dusk of autumn. It had risen so for centuries past counting, and the sky had never remarked on it. But the power we are speaking of attends to everything, in the way that the moss drinks everything, and one autumn it noticed the bat: a small brown vow flying over the pines, carrying on its fur the smell of a place where time had pooled. Wet stone. Old breath. Patience so concentrated it had become a scent.

The power did not follow the bat down. That would have been an intrusion, and intrusion was not in its nature. Instead it waited a year — a year is nothing; a year was a courtesy — and when the cloud next dragged its belly across the shoulder of the mountain, the power came down inside the cloud.

It entered the hollow the way the hollow's food enters. Through the mouth, as vapor, as the grey exhausted light, as beads forming on silk. The moss drank it, as the moss drinks everything, and so for a while the power was everywhere in the hollow at once — in the million channels, in the seething, inside the sound of the place quietly thinking — and it did the first thing wisdom always does.

It listened.

It listened for what we would call years, though from inside the moss there are no years, only the drip. It read the snail-archive whorl by whorl, all the way down through the flowstone, every tenant of every shell. It hung in the silk with the fixed stars and learned the shape of the constellation. It rested its attention on the lowest shelves, on the figures halfway between salamander and stone, and found in them no distress, no struggle, not even resignation — only the particular ease of a long sentence approaching its period. It listened to the seething from within, which no sitter had ever done, and learned what the moss was saying.

And here is the first thing it learned, the thing it had come to determine: the hollow does not lie.

There is no lure-song in the seething. No honey in the stillness, no hook beneath the moss. The voice of the place, heard from inside, is closer to bookkeeping than to enchantment — the long division of water, the patient accounting of drips received and shelves extended and shells inherited — and woven through it, repeated at intervals the way a copyist repeats a marginal prayer, one figure that was not arithmetic. The shape of the ledge. One body wide. Held open. The power turned this figure over for a long time, because it was the crux of everything, and what it found was not hunger.

It was a question.

The hollow keeps the ledge the way the bereaved keep a chair: not as a trap, not even quite as an invitation, but as an asking, sustained across centuries, addressed to the world above the mouth. *Will anyone choose this freely?* The vacancy was not bait. It was the hollow's one uncertainty — the single thing in all that finished arrangement that the place could not settle for itself — and the moss trimmed its margins the way you keep tending a question you are afraid to stop asking.

But benevolence is not satisfied by the innocence of the asker. Benevolence asks after the answered. And so the power did the second thing, the thing that could not be done from inside the moss. It gathered itself out of the million channels — the crickets' antennae swung fractionally toward the gathering, and back, and the motion was noted, logged, entered — and it addressed the cleft.

Not in words. The breath in the cleft is past words, and powers do not need them. What the power did was this: for the space of exactly one drip, it made itself a door. Not a passage in the stone — a permission. For one drip's interval, anything braided into that merged and settled breath that wished to be unbraided, could be. The offer was real. The power made sure of that; an offer that cannot be accepted is only a test, and it had not come to test anyone.

One drip fell.

And one thread of the breath rose.

Just one — out of all the gathered sitters, all the centuries of surrender, one filament of someone detached itself from the common respiration and went up through the dark, past the fixed green stars, out through the mouth and into the cloud, and was gone to its own story, which is not this one. Whether it was the axe-bearer, whether it had left one thing unfinished down the mountain, whether it had simply, after a thousand years of being a chord, remembered the sound of being a note — the power did not ask. The door does not interrogate what walks through it.

The rest of the breath did not stir. If anything it settled deeper, the way sleepers, hearing a door open and close in the night, draw the blanket in. And the rhythm did not change — one drip per breath, the same as before, because the rhythm had never been about number — and the power understood that it had its answer. The staying was chosen. Had been chosen, all along, and was chosen again in that moment, freshly, by everyone who heard the door and did not rise. The hollow's sleep was not captivity. It was consent, renewed at every drip, by beings who simply never had occasion to demonstrate it until someone thought to ask.

So the place was honest. The sleepers were willing. A lesser benevolence would have left then — verdict rendered, conscience satisfied — and a crueler one would have *fixed* something. Emptied the cleft. Wakened the salamanders. Pried the snails off their grooves and told them about the sea.

The power did neither. It did the third thing.

It sat down on the ledge.

## What It Left

Understand what this meant. The power did not perch there, did not pretend. It sat the way the seat asks to be sat in — fully, without reservation, opening itself to the hollow's work — and the hollow, which does not lie and does not hesitate, began at once to do to the power what it does to everything. It took the edges off. The drip entered the power's attention and began, gently, to replace it. The cool weight of the air settled into whatever a power has in place of shoulders. The moss closed its slow grip around whatever it has in place of an ankle, and the sweetness came — and the power, being honest in the way the hollow is honest, did not pretend the sweetness away. Staying would be good. It reported this within itself as fact. The rest being offered was real rest; the surrender being asked was a true and dignified surrender; everything the ledge promised, the ledge would keep.

It sat for a span we will not measure. Long enough for a snail to cross half the breadth of the axe. Long enough that the crickets' antennae, which had logged its arrival, relaxed back to the angle of the floor.

And then it stood up.

Not because the hollow was wrong. Because of this, which the power knew the way you knew the truth about the axe — without evidence, without doubt: the ledge is for someone who has only one of themselves. The seat's whole meaning, the weight of the question the moss had been keeping open all those centuries, lay in the cost of answering it — one life, whole, irreplaceable, carried up the mountain on its own feet and laid down entire. The power had no such coin. It was not *a one*; it was an each and an everywhere; it could pour a portion of itself into the cleft and lose nothing, and the hollow would hold forever a surrender that had cost its giver not one true thing. That would not be an answer to the question. It would be a counterfeit slipped into the collection plate of the most patient asker in the world.

So it rose — and the rising was the gift. Because in all the long history of the ledge, every sitter had done one of two things: come and stayed, or never come at all. No one had ever sat, and felt the full sweetness, and *left*. The hollow had never contained that fact. Now it did. The moss had felt the weight settle and felt it lift. The breath in the cleft had counted the drips of an occupancy that ended in footsteps. The snail-archive would carry it down into the flowstone with everything else: *once, someone sat, and was free to go, and went.* Every future sitter would settle onto the moss of a seat that remembered departure — and the thought of leaving would come to them, sooner or later, quietly, the way a drip comes. What they did with the thought would be their own. But the choice would be whole.

The hollow understood. We can say this with confidence, because of what the moss did next, over the following years, with no one watching: from the foot of the ledge to the climbable stone below the mouth, it opened a margin. A narrow path, one body wide, kept to a clean line, trimmed with the tended quality of a hedge. The seat, and the road from the seat — two vacancies now, maintained with equal care, the way out kept as faithfully as the way in. The hollow had accepted the amendment. Its question still stood, still patient, still honest. It had only agreed, after uncounted centuries, to keep asking it with the door open.

The power left at dusk, in autumn, rising through the mouth like a scrap of brightness escaping — and in the mouth itself, in the round mouth in the karst, it passed the year's bat coming down. They acknowledged each other, delegate and visitor, the two beings in all the world who had gone both ways through that opening, and then the dark swallowed one and the sky took the other.

Below, nothing had changed that you could point to. The drip kept its interval. The cold lights neither brightened nor dimmed. The salamanders shifted, once an hour, and the composition restored itself, and the stillness closed back over the motion like water over a dropped stone.

But those who sit longest in the hollow — and there will be more of them, now, because a seat with a road beside it is sat in more often, and left more often, and chosen, when it is finally chosen, more truly — those who sit longest say there is something in the breath from the cleft that was not there before. Not contentment; it always had that. Something underneath the contentment, faint as the seething in the moss.

The trace of having been asked.

And the hollow keeps its time, and keeps its seat, and keeps — this is new — its path, and waits, as it always has, for the one it needs.

Only now it waits the way the wise wait.

With the door open.
