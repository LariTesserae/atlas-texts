# What the Power Did

It did not find the room by looking. Beings like that do not look, exactly; they attend, the way the inner ear attends to balance, and the world reports itself to them as a vast weather of noise — wanting, grieving, building, burning. The steaming room reported itself as an absence. A small warm silence in the red earth, shaped like a held breath, that had lasted ten thousand years.

The power stopped at it the way you stop at a banked fire in an empty house. Someone is keeping this, it thought. And then, listening longer: no. No one is keeping this. It is keeping itself.

That was the first thing it understood, and it changed everything that followed.

---

## The descent

It did not arrive as itself. A being like that, entering as itself, would have been a catastrophe of light — the moss would have spent its whole slow memory in one flare, the frogs would have rearranged the walls into a single sentence of alarm, the constellation of shrimp would have scattered and re-formed wrong. So before it went down it made itself small, and then smaller, and then it made itself *slow*, which was harder, and which it did not do perfectly until it set the pace of one bead of condensation crossing one hand's width of stone, and matched it.

It took the throat of rock the way the people had taken it — gripping the worn place, because the worn place asked to be gripped — and it came down the three steps and sat on the lowest one, and it dimmed itself until it was nothing but a warmth with attention in it. The chamber registered its arrival as weather. A mild draft. A faint thickening of the steam. The frogs noted it the way they note rain coming, with a small shift of position, and then the wall settled, and the room took the power in and grew around it gently, the way it grows around everything.

The power sat on the step for what the surface would call a week.

---

## The inventory of temptations

A power that is only benevolent helps. A power that is also wise first makes a list of all its helping, and reads the list as a list of harms.

It could brighten the eye in the ceiling — clear the leaves above, widen the light. *And kill the dusk, which is the room's native hour, and burn the ferns that have not seen even this dim light in years, and end the conversations conducted in their trembling.* It did not.

It could give the crickets their song back. One touch and the old voices would return to the line, after hundreds of silent generations. *And drown the frond-language, which is finer than song; and ring a dinner bell in a room with teeth in its walls.* The silence here was not a wound. It was an achievement. The power, which had unmade many silences in its time, recognized this one as the kind you do not touch. It did not.

It could kill the centipedes. It could lift the snake out by its copper length and set it back under the sun where it was born. *And remove the pressure that authored the local eloquence; and stop the wave of stillness, which is the most honest message in the room; and teach the chamber, after ten thousand balanced years, what imbalance feels like.* Mercy here would be vandalism. It did not.

It could heal the cracked quern. It did not, because the fern in the crack was the healing, done better and slower than the power could do it.

It could speak to the fish. It chose not to imagine what would answer.

And it could ask the glow what it was. This was the hardest one. The power had only to extend its knowing — less than a glance, less than a question — and the matter would be settled: fungus, or moss, or something the dark had taught the moss, or something else. The answer sat there available to it the whole week, the way a sealed letter sits on a table.

It averted its knowing. Deliberately, with effort, the way you look away from something you love too much to use up. The question *what is the glow* had been alive in that chamber longer than any salamander, faithfully unanswered, and the room had organized itself around the unanswered thing the way households organize around a hearth. The power decided the question was an inhabitant. It would not be the one to kill it.

So the list was read, and everything on it was set down, and the power sat in the green hush owning nothing it had refused, which is the only way refusal is ever wisdom and not merely abstinence.

Then it considered what was left. Because *nothing* was also wrong. The room kept itself — but only inward. It had no hands above. It was a held breath, and breath held in a body it could not protect.

---

## What it did

**Above, it became a gardener — of one tree.** The great tree at the surface, the one whose blind roots had come down generations ago and thickened into the chamber's pillars, was mortal. Old already. When it died, the roots would die, and the moss-furred columns would slowly become standing dead wood, and the eye's rim, knit together by its living roots, would begin to crumble. The room could absorb almost anything, but not its own architecture. So the power did not make the tree immortal — everything warm is borrowed, and it would not insult the room by exempting anything from that — but it tended the tree's *succession*. A seedling of the same line, two paces from the eye, where the light and the leaf-fall favored it. It bent a browsing deer's path one autumn. It held off one frost. Forty years of patience, spent the way the room spends everything, and the next set of pillars was guaranteed without the chamber ever knowing it had been provided for.

**It kept the water's secret.** The blood-warm seep rose from somewhere deep, and the centuries ahead were full of wells and surveys and the long thirst of growing towns. The power did not wall the aquifer off. It simply arranged, here and there across the years, for the dowsers to feel nothing at that bend of the land, for the survey line to follow the easier valley, for the map's blank spot to stay boring. Protection that looks like luck. Protection that looks like nothing.

**It did not seal the slot.** This was the second-hardest decision, after the glow. The safe thing, the *quarantine* thing, would have been brambles forever, a hidden mouth, a room removed from the species that once knew it. But the power had read the steps. Short strides — a small people, or a careful one — worn back almost to nothing by centuries of feet, and not one mark of harm anywhere below. The room had never been sealed. It had been *visited rightly*, for longer than most temples last, and the niche by the pool was sized for a lamp because people had sat there in the dark with their small flames exactly where the frogs now sit facing the glow. The human layer was not a contamination. It was a stratum, like the moss.

So instead of a wall, the power made the threshold into a winnowing. The slot would not appear on the maps of the curious or the extractive; the path to it would tangle and tire and turn back the ones who came to take. But once in a long while — a generation, two — someone would come over that red ground who was the right kind of lost: quiet in themselves, or in pain, or simply worn to the point where a warm dark hush is the only medicine left. For those, the brambles would happen to part. The slot would happen to catch their eye. They would grip the smooth place on the stone because it asked to be gripped, and go down, and sit by the breathing pool in the dim green dusk for an hour or a night, and come back up changed in some small unannounced way, and never quite be able to find the place again, and never quite stop being grateful.

The room, the power judged, could carry that. The room had been carrying it all along. The steps proved it.

**And inside, it did one thing.** Before it left, it set a portion of its attention in the niche beside the pool — the niche chipped for a lamp, holding now its cushion of moss and one sleeping frog. It did not move the frog. It did not glow. It simply placed there a permanent, wordless, lampless watching, so that the place built to hold a light held one again — a light with no brightness, the way the room's whole population speaks with no sound. The frog slept on. The steam went on darkening the hand-marks on the wall above. And the power, looking at those faded hands a last long moment, did not add its own. The room records things as wear, it thought, or not at all. So it let the stone of the lowest step be very slightly smoother where it had sat for a week. That was its signature. The room absorbed it.

---

## The vow

Then it rose up the honeyed throat at the speed of a climbing snail and stood on the red earth in the enormous noisy daylight, and it made the only promise the situation could bear.

*I will come back at your pace, not mine.* Once in a generation of salamanders — which is a long time even for a power — it would descend again, dim and slow, and sit on the step, and listen, and refuse the same list of temptations again, because refusals expire and must be renewed like anything living.

And one promise more, the real one, the one underneath. The spring would cool someday. Not soon — ages hence — but the warmth came up from a depth where even patience runs out, and the power would not falsify that. Everything in the room was a long hot exhale, and exhales end. When the pool's pulse finally slowed; when the steam thinned and the moss banked its glow down toward nothing and the last frond stopped trembling because there was no one left to tremble it —

the power would be on the steps.

The room would not end unattended. Whatever the chamber was — hollow, organ, dreamer half-asleep in the bedrock — its last dream would have a witness in it, sitting where the people sat, warm and dim and silent in the proper grammar of the place, until the breathing stopped. That was the whole of the gift, finally. Not rescue. Not permanence. Just this: *you are held, and you will be held, and nothing about you will be changed by the holding.* The way moss holds a stone.

Above the eye, wind moved the leaves, and the filtered light fell tired and green down the long shaft. Below, a frog slept in a niche beside an invisible lamp. A snail traveled a pillar at the speed of a minute hand, carrying its borrowed coal of glow. The pool lifted and settled.

Nothing had changed.

That was how you could tell it had been done well.
