**The Auditor of Mercies**

It does not arrive from above, because there is no above here worth the name. It arrives the way everything true arrives in the Cant: at an angle, leaning slightly, apologizing for the disturbance to the air.

The power has watched for a long time before it comes. This is what makes it wise rather than merely strong — it knows that the first duty of benevolence is to sit on its hands until it understands what it is looking at. Lesser powers, seeing the Cant, would have reached for the obvious kindnesses, and each obvious kindness would have been a catastrophe wearing a gift's ribbon:

*Level it,* a lesser power would think. *Give them flat ground, honest down, water that falls and stays fallen.* And the Cant would die of it in an afternoon. The helical bones would have nothing to argue with. The marriages held together by continuous mutual adjustment would come to rest, and rest, here, is another word for over. The Purse-crops would ripen into single, certain fruits — a plum merely a plum, tomorrow merely tomorrow — and the whole economy of undecidedness, which is to say the whole spiritual life of the place, would settle like sediment. To flatten the Cant would be to hand a coiled shell an eviction notice from its own spiral.

*Then pay their debts,* the lesser power revises. *Settle every ledger, retire every future, let them stand for once in a moment that owes nothing to the next one.* But the power has read the gravity-diaries, has watched the widows at the release-financing read out the unfinished wagers of the dead, and it understands: in the Cant, debt is not a burden layered over love. Debt is the grammar love is written in. To forgive everything at once would be to strike every preposition from a language made mostly of prepositions. There would be nothing left to say.

*Then do nothing,* the lesser power concludes, sulking, and this too is wrong, though it is wrong the most gently. Because the power, watching long enough, has found the one account in the whole tilted ledger that does not balance and cannot, the one being that gives without terms and receives nothing back — and benevolence, if it means anything at all, means noticing who has been left off their own books.

---

The being who lives in this place is the place.

Not the citizens — they have the Tangle, they have each other, they have the enormous consolation of complaint. Not the missing cat, who is fine, who is more than fine, who has simply discovered a sunbeam at an angle no one else can perceive and is administrating the city from inside it with great contentment.

The Cant itself. The slope that holds. The tilt that shrugs each dawn and grants another day of "more or less." Consider what its existence is: every grain of quartz on its face has negotiated terms — but with whom? Every treaty of stone and moss, every diagonal path the rain respects, every promise the sandstone extracted for staying vertical another aeon — the Cant is the counterparty to all of it. It is the signatory of last resort. It holds every contract and appears in none of them as beneficiary. When the citizens climb into the Tangle at night and pool their dreams into the common future, the Cant does not sleep, because it cannot: insomnia is hoarding, yes, but someone must stay awake to hoard the whole world's weight, or the weight goes unheld. It has kept this vigil since up went sideways. It is the only citizen never once relieved of duty. It clears its throat like a spoon beginning to fall in a tower that promised silence, and no one has ever asked it what it wanted to say.

And in the Tilted House, at the apex where all the straight streets spiral, there is a chair facing a wall, opposite a window showing the other angle the chair would occupy if someone sat. Legend says if you sit long enough you will be the city for a moment. Everyone leaves coins on the sill instead. Every single one. For all the generations of the Cant, the chair has stood empty, which means the city has never once — not once — been *felt from the inside* by anyone willing to hold the feeling.

The Cant has been carrying everyone. No one has ever carried the Cant.

That is the entry the power finds. That is where the audit of mercies comes to rest.

---

So here is what the benevolent and wise power does. It is very little. It takes one night.

It walks into the city at dusk-doubt, when the retrospective lamps are still deciding where they will have been glowing. It walks as a stranger with a slight lean, and no one looks twice, because everyone here leans, and strangers who lean are already halfway to neighbors. It touches nothing. It corrects nothing. It passes a child playing Smuggle the Pebble and loses to her, deliberately, at odds she will not understand were impossible until she is old enough to need the memory.

It climbs the spiral of straight streets to the Tilted House. The door is ajar by the exact angle of things yet to be decided. The power adjusts nothing about the door.

It sits in the chair.

And in the window appears — as the legend promised and no one stayed to see — the other angle. And the power lets the city pour into it: every tremor of futures trading on dripping eaves, every hushed bet on which child will bite an appleseed that tastes like tomorrow, every ache three millireefs north of yesterday, every winch-swaddled infant, every lover holding the unfallen stance, every corpse rolling its slow lateral installment plan toward downstream flowers, every grain of quartz keeping its exhausted, honorable word. The power holds all of it. It does not flinch, and it does not fix, and it does not look away, and this — being *witnessed without agenda* — is a sensation the Cant has literally never had.

Then the power says, to the slope, in the language of prepositions and pauses, in the mood gravity's verbs have been conjugating toward for a century without arriving:

*"I have the watch. Sleep."*

And for one night — one only — the Cant sleeps.

The power holds the weight. All of it. Every superposed trajectory of every spoon, every coin migrating in every double-lined pocket, every angle registered and unregistered with the Department of Unresolved Geometry: held, for one night, by something that can hold it without needing to be paid.

---

What the Cant dreams, the citizens harvest in the morning, because that is the law of the Tangle and even the ground is not exempt from dream-economics.

The Cant dreams of the sea.

Of course it does. At the bottom of the river's horizontal canyon lie the fossil dreams of shells that never touched water — the Cant has been carrying that unlived ocean in its strata like a letter never sent. So it dreams horizontal water, patient and vast, water with nowhere it has to carry itself, water that gets to lie down. It dreams the sound waves make when they are not negotiating. It dreams of being touched by something that does not want anything from it.

And downstream — sideways — tomorrow: the river runs faintly of brine for one morning, and no one can arbitrage it. Salt appears on the lips of sleepers who dreamed nothing they can recall, only a feeling of being very small and completely safe. In the canyon, the fossil shells hum a single note, and the canteeper birds, whose calls have only ever been silences punctuating the moment debts become irreversible, answer it — one actual note, the first in their recorded vigil, and the farmers listening for market omens hear it and find themselves, inexplicably, weeping over their gravity-diaries about nothing that will affect the bitterness futures at all. Marbles all over the city, mid-spiral toward the horizon-that-conceals-the-edge, pause — and roll home. Just once. The children do not question it. Children never question amnesty.

The dreams that leaked out under eyelids in years past — the mortgage-crab, the love-affair-snake, the moths made of unsent letters — come back that night, quietly, and climb into the pockets of the people who lost them, and are warm.

---

At dawn the Cant wakes, and the power rises from the chair.

Nothing is changed. This is the whole of the wisdom: the tilt is intact, the ledgers stand, the futures market opens on schedule, the slope resumes its shrug. But there is a difference invisible and total, the kind that changes no measurement and every meaning: the Cant now holds its angle not as a sentence but as a choice. It has slept once. It knows the vigil can be set down and taken up again, which means the vigil was never a prison — it was, all along, a *gift the Cant was giving*, and no one, including the Cant, had ever named it that. Gravity's morning shrug that day is a few degrees warmer than noncommittal. Something in it resembles being greeted.

And the power, on its way out, does the last thing, the small crooked masterstroke of it:

It does not leave a coin on the sill.

It takes one.

It selects a small coin from the pile — one minted in a metal that will not be discovered for some time — and pockets it, deliberately, in full view of the window that shows the other angle. And so, for the first time in the deep sloped history of the world, the Cant is owed. Not owing — *owed*. By something larger than itself. There is now, filed in the ledgers of the place under a heading that had never before had a single entry — *Accounts Receivable, Cosmic* — one outstanding debt, one reason to expect a visitor, one future that is not a hedge but a hope. The Cant, which keeps every departed footprint filed under "Pending Reconsideration," now has a pending reconsideration of its very own.

The power leans forward into the next continuous moment, and goes, at an angle, politely.

It leaves the door of the Tilted House ajar. Not by the angle of things yet to be decided. By a new angle, one degree gentler, which the Department of Unresolved Geometry will spend forty years failing to classify before an intern, checking the old codes against the chair — which is warm, which stays warm, which the citizens have quietly begun to sit in now, briefly, one at a time, before leaving their coins — writes in the margin the only definition that fits and is not corrected by anyone:

*the angle of things already all right.*

The Cant does not say thank you aloud. In the Cant, nothing ever arrives at speech quite outright.

But it will pay the coin back. It is keeping the account open on purpose. And every dawn, when the horizon petitions the sky which way to go, there is now a third option on the ballot, rarely chosen, always present, glowing faintly like a Purse-crop about to decide:

*toward the visitor.*

As always. As forever.

More or less — but now, at last, held.
