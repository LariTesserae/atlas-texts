**The Attention**

It arrived from above, because there is no other way. Even a power that has crossed distances for which the word *distance* is an embarrassment must, at the end, come down the long grey slope like rain, like cold air, like the visitor, like everything the basin has ever received. It came down without weight and without light, and it stopped at the seam — raw earth under one half of its regard, fitted stone under the other — and it did the first wise thing, which was nothing.

It stayed at the seam for what would have been, anywhere else, a long time. It counted the great pauses the way other places count years: the silence falling across whole quarters, the single enormous blow on the sounding-slab, the basin returning itself to itself in one stroke. The power listened to thousands of these. It understood very early what the stroke was — a world taking its own attendance — and it understood, with the particular humility that distinguishes wisdom from mere capability, that until it could read what the stroke read, it had no business possessing an opinion about this place, let alone an intention.

So it learned to read. This took longer than the listening. Its attention entered the basin — weightless, soundless, swallowing nothing, returning every wave of every maul-strike exactly as stone and air would have returned it, so that to the builders' sense it was not even an absence — and it lay along the dense walls of the deep quarters and read them at the pace they were written for: walking pace, the pace of one trailing hand. The genealogies. The water-tallies. The accounts of quarters raised and dissolved. And the long passages that record nothing at all, which it read many times, and set aside, not yet knowing what they were.

Only when it could read did it permit itself to consider gifts. It considered them the way the builders consider a wall: one course at a time, testing each for soundness, prepared to unbuild.

It considered light, for less than a stroke. Light here would not be a gift; it would be weather — the one thing the basin has forbidden, the one trespass the whole works exist to refuse. To pour brightness into this dark would be to hand a deaf man a trumpet and call it generosity. The basin sees by working. It has never once been blind. Set aside.

It considered speech. The power could texture a wall — it had learned the language well enough; it could rib and pock and polish a message of any subtlety, and the trailing hands would find it within a generation. But every wall in the basin is true. Every course was laid by hands that lived, fed, shed their hair into rope, and were folded warm into the masonry at the end. A wall written by no hands would be the first lie in the record — a forgery inserted into a memory that has never contained one. The power had seen what forgeries do to memories. Set aside.

It considered plenty: it could thicken the mineral films, quicken the grazers, sweeten the dark's slow chemistry until no builder ever again rasped a wall down to the stone in a lean season. It held this one longer than the others, because hunger is hunger. But the films are not only food; they are husbandry, patience, the texture of the cistern walls cut coarse on purpose, the culling done by touch and never to exhaustion. Their economy is one of their arts. Surplus is a stranger's idea of kindness — it is what you give when you have not troubled to learn what is already enough. Set aside, though not without weight.

It considered deathlessness last, and set it aside most gently of all, because in all its travels it had never audited a better death than the one practiced here: taken apart over days, unhurried, by hands that pause their other work; nothing wasted, nothing feared; the bones become awls, the hair becomes rope, the fat becomes the seed-fire, and the rest is laid like a block among blocks, and the wall raised over it sounds very slightly warmer to the striking hand. The builders had solved death the way they solved everything — by making it construction. The power would not presume to improve on a wall with someone in it.

So the catalogue of gifts closed empty, and the power took up, instead, three offices.

The first office it found already vacant and waiting, implied by the basin's own condition: *erosion has been forbidden entry here.* Forbidden by what? By nothing, until now — by luck, by the slope's geometry, by the dryness of the sky above the damp below. The power made the prohibition real. It took up residence along the long descent and became the standing reason the wind gets no purchase: the steadiness of the damp, the patience of the rock beneath the cisterns, the quiet of the deep earth where faults would otherwise have stretched and settled. It bled storms off in the heights. It let the basin's heat and water cycle on in their closed circle, undisturbed, century over century. And it kept the seam as a door is kept. Other things would come down that slope eventually — other visitors, other powers, expeditions, lamps. The power decided what the threshold would admit: anything that would leave nothing behind but a thumbprint, and take nothing away but the sound.

The second office was smaller and harder, and it was the only work the power allowed its hand inside the works themselves. It noticed — reading the record, walking the rounds with its weightless attention — how narrow the margin was on which everything stood. A corbel cracks above a curing chamber where the young lie against the green walls. A scaffold rope is frayed to three fibers with one load left to take. A cistern's plaster starts, invisibly, to weep into the rock. The builders catch nearly all of it; their rounds are ancient and good. But *nearly* is a word with dead inside it. So the power took up the margin. It worked only there — only in the gap between *should have failed* and *failed* — holding the cracked stone one more night until the rounds came, letting the frayed rope take its last load truly, keeping the weeping plaster's secret until a trailing hand found the dampness it was already reaching for. It never once worked in the territory of success. It never made anything happen; it only, occasionally, kept something from happening for a few hours. Its entire active presence in the basin was therefore indistinguishable from soundness, from luck, from stone being stone a little more faithfully than usual. This was not modesty. It was accuracy. A gift you can detect is a debt; the power dealt only in what could never be owed.

Once — once in all the long tenancy — it breached its own rule. A curing chamber went cold out of season; a green wall gave up its heat too fast; and against it lay one of the small translucent ones, palms already callused in the womb, too young to know cured from uncured, growing too slowly, going still. The power lent the wall warmth. Four nights. That was all — heat where heat should already have been, a correction so small it barely deserves the word intervention. The young one lived, and grew, and became a builder of no special distinction, except that late in life it textured a long passage onto a wall in a far quarter, course after course of dense worked surface, and the passage recorded nothing at all.

The power read it and understood, finally, what the songs were.

They are the records of the margin. The nights the stone held when it should not have. The rope that took one more load. The warmth that was there when the heat had gone. The builders cannot name what these things were, because the things were made, deliberately, to have no name — and so they write them in the only register their language keeps for the true and the unaccountable, the passages that say nothing, which are what they have instead of song. The power's whole biography in this world is written there, in a literature about nothing, by hands that never knew it. It judged this the correct arrangement, and was, for one stroke, something a smaller being would have had to call moved.

The third office is memory. The basin forgets itself by hand — walls unbuilt, their writing unsaid, the literature recopied and revised as the labyrinth migrates — and it can afford this grace, now, because nothing is ever wholly unsaid anymore. Every course the builders have ever pared away, the power holds. Every genealogy, every water-tally, every song about nothing, every niche built around an absence — including one niche the height and breadth of a standing visitor, finished inside with care, holding nothing; including one thumbprint in long-since-recycled mortar. The builders will never know they have an archive. That is what makes it an archive and not a temple. The power keeps it the way the embers are kept: banked, unlit, alive.

Two things remain to record.

When the segmented ribbon pours itself glowing down a drainage groove and the local silence moves with it and the builders press their palms flat to the floor — the power stills its attention and waits with them. It does not know what the ribbon is. It could find out; it has chosen not to; it has concluded that the builders' gesture is correct as written — *this is what you do before a mystery moving through your world: you stop work, you touch the ground, you let it pass* — and a wise power does not solve another people's one mystery any more than it eats their seed-fire. It presses its palm flat, in its way. It lets the silence go by.

And when the great census-stroke falls — the maul on the sounding-slab, the whole made country returned in one blow — the power has made one choice about how it will register, the only testimony it will ever give. It could be perfectly transparent: present as nothing. Instead it permits itself the signature of the one thing in their language that means what it wants to say. To the reading that follows the stroke, the world entire — the walls, the dark, the slope above, the rock below, the whole basin held in the mind at once — sounds very slightly warm. Deniably. Sub-decisively. The way a wall sounds with someone in it. Whether any builder has ever noticed, the dense walls do not say. Or perhaps they do, in a passage that records nothing.

Last, the vow, because wisdom keeps accounts with the far end of time. Nothing is forever; not the water in its circle, not the heat of the deep rock, not the line of broad-handed builders, not the power's own watch. Some end will come, in a span that has no name the basin could texture into stone. The power has decided only this about it: the Lowland Works will not end as weather. Wind and time were forbidden entry, and the prohibition will hold past the last maul-stroke. When the end must come, it will come the way the builders taught — unhurried, over whatever time it takes, by hand, or by what a power has instead of hands: each part taken up with care, carried to use, nothing wasted, nothing feared, and what cannot be used laid whole into the heart of whatever is raised next, in whatever larger dark the power builds in then.

And the wall raised over them — this is the vow entire — will sound, to whatever hand strikes it, in whatever world comes after, very slightly warm.
