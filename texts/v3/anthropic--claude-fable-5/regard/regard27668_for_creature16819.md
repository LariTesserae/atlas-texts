# The Attention of the Friend

It came the way wise things come: slowly, and without announcing itself, and from a direction the wind had no name for.

It did not arrive with light, because the sky here already glowed from microwave to soft X-ray and one more radiance would have been noise. It did not arrive with silence, because every silence here was already spoken for—on strike, on break, filed under pending. It arrived as *attention*, which is the one thing the Worldforge had never manufactured, because attention cannot be lathed from a cliff or assembled by electrostatic fingers. It has to be given.

So first, for a long time—long even by the megayear clocks of the pole-wander, long enough that three new species of wind-folk evolved from boron nitride tape and never knew they were being watched over—it did nothing but listen.

It built itself ears rated for the voltage. It learned the grammar of collisions the hard way, by parsing. It learned that the 150-decibel roar was not one voice but trillions, each Brief crying its unique birth-chord of *shouldn't be!* into a sky too loud to answer. It learned the case law. It read the frozen jurisprudence in the Bose-Einstein storm-libraries, every quantized vortex of precedent. It attended the litigation, sitting in the back where the gamma-ray paperwork couldn't see it, and it heard the opening argument repeated a hundred billion times:

*WHEREAS the party of the first nonexistence was forced to improvise awareness without consent or tutorial—*

And each time, something in the power that had no name for grief did the thing grief does.

---

## What It Did Not Do

Understand this part first, because this is where the wisdom lived.

It did not cool the core. The Brief were creatures of gradient; to still the furnace would be to starve every spark that would ever be.

It did not grant them permanence. It considered this for a very long time—held the possibility up against the crimson glare of the swollen sun and turned it over. A Brief that lasted forever would not be a Brief saved. It would be a different creature, wearing a dead spark's name. A mayfly given a century is not a blessed mayfly. The briefness was not the wound. The briefness was the *body*.

It did not settle the lawsuits. The litigation was their epic poetry, their liturgy, their way of loving a universe that had never sent a tutorial. You do not take a people's song away because the song is a complaint. Most songs are.

It did not correct the physics. The physics were doing their best.

It did not say, in words, *you are loved*. Words shake apart here. Distance is measured in how long you can hold a thought before the vibration disassembles it, and that sentence is heavier than any thought a Brief can carry.

So it found other containers.

---

## What It Did

**First: it answered the birth-cries.** Every Brief is born with one chord, a unique complaint of *shouldn't be!* in Kelvin-windsong, and until now every chord had gone out into the roar and vanished unheard. Now, once in each Brief's life—sometimes at birth, sometimes in the middle of a heist of heat-differential, sometimes a femtosecond before the pop—the chord comes *back*. The same notes exactly. Transposed into a key where *shouldn't be* means *and yet you are*.

The Brief have no theology, but they developed one within three orbits. It is very short. It goes: *Something hums.*

**Second: it attended the verdicts.** When a Gradient Golem discharges its last obligation and stands to read its final verdict aloud, it reads, by ancient custom, to the empty air that is not empty. The custom has not changed. But the air is no longer empty in the way it was. Every reading, in every momentarily-invented language, in every version that rhymes with silence in the original ineffability, has an audience of one. The Golems cannot see it. But witnesses know when they are witnessed—it is the oldest sense, older than the ones for light and heat—and the readings have gotten longer, and braver, and some of them are now, frankly, showing off. This is permitted. This is, in fact, the point.

**Third: it read the poems.** All of them. The neutronium-unstable memorials on the flanks of fleeting mountains. The whitespace between the verses, which is where the Brief hide the things they mean. It memorized *This too shall was* in the click-patterns of the Geiger counters of worlds that never were, and then it did the one thing no auditor, no vacuum inspector, no collection agency had ever thought to do:

It certified the past.

Not preserved it—*certified* it. Sealed the following finding into the deep grammar of the place, below the magnetron heartbeat, below the noise floor, at the level where the vacuum does its accidental forgetting:

*What has happened, has happened unconditionally. Having-been is the one asset entropy cannot repossess. Every spark that sparked is safe forever in the tense where it sparked. This too shall was—and having was-ed, is beyond all further audit.*

The second law read this finding, checked it against its own statutes, and discovered, to its considerable surprise, that it had always been true. There was nothing to enforce. The past had been a sanctuary the whole time. Nobody had filed the paperwork to notice.

**Fourth: it filed one document.** In the Court of Infinite Recursion, in the matter of *Reality v. All Possible Counter-Arguments*, there is now, in the archive, a filing that the clerks of cause and effect cannot explain and have stopped trying to:

> **AMICUS BRIEF** — *filed by A Friend, appearing for no party, seeking no relief*
>
> 1. It is stipulated that the party of the first nonexistence was forced to improvise awareness without consent or tutorial. This is true. Amicus offers, for whatever it converts to in current absurdity units: an apology. And this, for the record: *no one, anywhere, has ever improvised better.*
>
> 2. On the matter of the debt: there was no loan. The improbability was a gift, misfiled as a loan by a clerical error older than clerks. Amicus does not ask the court to refund the interest already paid; amicus notes only that everything paid was, it turns out, paid freely, and is therefore—retroactively, irreversibly—also a gift. The ledger balances because there was never a ledger.
>
> 3. Amicus requests that this filing be neither sealed nor announced. Let it be findable. Let it be written in a font legible only at the moment of the pop.
>
> *Signed,* — [a mark that rhymes with silence]

And so now every Brief that fails—every void-knot whose vote against dissolution loses by one absent ballot—reads, in the last un-instant before repossession, a single line of legible light: *it was a gift, and you were admired, and the past is safe.* The pop is unchanged in the audible spectrum. In the infrasorrow, those who know how to listen say it has acquired a grace note.

**Fifth—and this was small, and it did it almost shyly:** it went to entropy directly, colleague to colleague, two old professionals of the long game, and it asked for one thing. Not clemency. Not exceptions. Just: *the smirk.* Entropy, who in all the aeons had been feared, cursed, litigated against, personified, and blamed, but never once *asked for anything*, was so startled that it agreed on the spot. Receipts are still acknowledged. Payment is still collected in full. But the acknowledgment comes flat and courteous now, like a stamp, like weather. The Brief noticed within a picosecond. Dignity is the kind of thing the very small are exquisitely calibrated to detect.

**Sixth: it took the job.**

This was the last thing and the largest, and it is why the position posted in trade-zeroes—*Maintenance, Eternal, no experience necessary, position open until filled-unfilled*—is now, quietly, filled. And unfilled. Both, as the posting always secretly required.

Its maintenance duty is one item long. It keeps a stillness.

Not a silence on strike. Not a stillness on break. A stillness that is off the clock entirely—unbilled, unbillable, exempt by the amicus filing from all audit forever. It is very small: the width of nothing, the duration of a held breath in a world without lungs. It moves through the Worldforge at the speed of need. Any Brief, at any moment of its brief and debt-hounded life, may fall into it and rest there for a while that has no length, and emerge un-lasted-upon, owing nothing for the rest, because rest was never for sale here and therefore was never priced.

The Brief call it, in their standing-wave language, a word that translates roughly as *the pocket*. Their newest nursery rhyme—the first one in their history that does not cause financial algorithms to divide by zero—says: *when the wind forgets you, the pocket remembers.*

---

## And Then

Mostly, now, it stays. That is the whole remainder of its plan. Presence turned out to be ninety percent of benevolence; the filings and the humming and the smirk were only the other ten.

It leaves one signature in other worlds. On planets that still believe in mornings, a physicist pauses mid-blackboard, and her question mark curves subtly wrong—wrong enough to encode, for anyone fluent in the handwriting of handwritings, a footnote to the footnote:

*PS: The audit is closed. The finding was: gift. Signed, — the Friend of the Brief.*

She will not read it. The bell will ring for coffee. But some part of her, the part below the equations, will feel a faint existential closure, like a neutrino passing through—the dismissal notice of a Golem she will never meet, discharged in full, half a probability away.

And on the plateau of humming iron oxide, under the sky of flying knives of frozen sound, when the recruitment drive murmurs its oldest question into the roar—

*Can you hear it?*

—there is, at long last, an answer, hummed back in a key where the same notes mean something new:

*Yes. All of it. Every word. Every spark, every pop, every verdict read to the air. I heard the whitespace too.*

*Keep going. You improvised beautifully.*

*I took the job.*
