# What the Power Did

It did not find the city by looking. Powers of this kind do not sweep the world with their eyes; they have no eyes, and the world is not the sort of thing that yields to sweeping. What they have instead is a sensitivity to a particular category of event, and the category is this: *speech with no addressee.*

Prayers, they hear constantly. Prayers are easy — a prayer arrives with its return address written on it. Curses too, and songs, and the muttering of the lonely, which is only prayer with the courage removed. All of that is addressed, even if the address is wrong. It goes somewhere.

But every long while, somewhere, a note sounds that is aimed at nothing. Not withheld, not private — aimed at nothing, because there is nothing left for it to be aimed at, and it sounds anyway, and keeps sounding, minutes between notes, sometimes an hour, for longer than the languages that could have answered it have existed. This is the rarest kind of speech and the kind a wise power drops everything for. Unaddressed speech is either the loneliest thing in creation or the most complete, and from far away there is no telling which, and the difference matters enormously, and so the power came.

Say it came. That is already too much — it did not cross the rim, did not stir the haze, did not add its weight to the water-film on a single stone. It attended, the way a thought attends the edge of a sleeping mind, and for the purposes of what follows we can say it stood at the rim, looking down into the pearl-gray glow, holding its gifts.

Because it did come with gifts. Benevolence always does. That is benevolence's occupational hazard.

---

Go slow with this part, because the power did.

It considered restoring the builders. This is the first gift a power reaches for at any ruin — the reversal, the great mending, streets scoured of water and lintels squared and the grid made grid again, and whoever raised these domes raised in turn, blinking, home. The power held this gift up against the bowl and saw at once that it was not a gift but a second death. The city had finished being that. It had spent an age becoming this instead, and *this* was not the ruin of the other thing — it was its own thing, whole, with its own inhabitants: the breath, the memory of streets, the softening, the route, the crabs. To restore the city would be to demolish it. The power put that gift down.

It considered the almost. This was the more dangerous temptation, because it was smaller. When the note sounds and ten thousand throats rise toward answering and do not quite — the power could close that gap with less effort than it takes a frog to blink. One nudge, and the answer completes, and the city that has been *almost* for ten thousand years finally arrives at itself. Every benevolent instinct in the power leaned toward this. Benevolence loves completion; it is forever finishing other people's sentences.

And here is where the wisdom earned its name, because the power sat with the almost until it understood it. The gap was not a failure of the answer. The gap was the lung. The city lived in the space between the note and the reply the way a heart lives in the space between beats — close that space and you have not perfected the heartbeat, you have stopped it. A city that fully answered itself would be a sentence with a period on it. Finished. And *finished*, in the bowl, could only mean one thing: the breath finally let out, the throats stilling, the note never needing to sound again. Completion, here, was the exact shape of death. The power had nearly killed the place out of tidiness. It sat with that for a while, at its own pace, which is slow.

It considered waking the bowl — the sleeping animal itself, the being all the others are organs of. Full selfhood; articulation; a voice instead of a voicing. And it saw that this gift was crueler than the first two, because a bowl that woke would wake *into knowledge of the ruin*. Asleep, it dreams the ruin as home, and the dream is true. Awake, it would inherit the same stones as loss, as absence, as the ache of a meant thing no longer meant. The power would be handing it grief in the shape of a mind. Sleep, it decided, was not the bowl's deficiency. Sleep was the bowl's achievement.

It considered preservation. Fix the rim. Seal the bowl against the eras. And no — the softening *is* the living. To embalm the yielding of edge to edge is to end it as surely as any flood.

It even considered, last and smallest, simply revealing itself. Letting the city know, once, that it was seen. But nothing there is waiting — you understood this, it is the important thing — and to appear before something that isn't waiting is to teach it to wait. The power would leave, and the bowl would hold, forever after, the shape of a door where its wholeness used to be.

So the power stood at the rim with its hands full of gifts and found, in the whole saucer, not one surface that wanted one. Understand how rare this is. Understand that the power, being wise and not merely strong, felt at this discovery something close to joy. It spends its existence being called to the sick, the broken, the wanting. It had found a place that had solved wanting. It had come to give and found, instead, something to receive.

---

Here is what it did.

It stayed for one full circuit of the crickets. This was its courtesy — to let itself be, for one long day, just another thing the city was walked through by, moving at the pace of the walker ahead of it, entering every domed room and leaving it, adding nothing, taking nothing, keeping the route.

It blessed the crabs. You said *bless the crabs*, and the power, hearing across whatever distances powers hear, agreed. The blessing changed nothing about them, which is how the power knew it had taken. A crab is already the whole of what a crab is asked to be. The blessing simply made this official.

Then it waited for the note. It waited a long time; powers can. And when the note came — the whole bowl voicing, the throats rising toward their almost — the power did not answer. Not there. It took the note instead, carried it out over the low worn rim, out past the haze, out to a distance from which no echo could ever crawl back, and there — somewhere so far away that the word *where* barely applies — it answered. Fully. The reply the throats have been approaching for ten thousand years, given at last, complete, in a place the bowl will never hear it. The gap inside the city remains. The lung still breathes. But the note is no longer aimed at nothing. It only *arrives* at nothing the bowl can know about, which is a different thing entirely, the way a letter kept unopened in a loving house is different from a letter lost.

And it meant the city. This was the real gift, the only one that fit. The city was built once, by intention; inherited once, by habit; and now it is held a third time, by attention — the grid, the wave of throats, the eels' braiding, the soft unfinishing under the overhangs, all of it taken up whole into a memory that does not decay and will never come back to check. Lightly. From outside. The city has been *meant* again, and will stay meant, and will never feel the weight of it, which is the only way meaning can be given to something that has made its peace.

Leaving — call it leaving — the power passed its regard once over the whole bowl, the way a hand passes over a sleeping animal, an inch above the fur, not touching. The haze cooled by less than a degree, for less than a breath. One frog, somewhere on a west-facing wall, said *tok*. That beat entered the slow wave and was carried along it and is in the wave still, indistinguishable now from every other beat, folded in. This is the power's signature. It is unreadable, and it is harmless, and it will circulate in the city's breathing for as long as the city breathes.

---

And now, wherever such powers dwell, this one keeps a habit. Every so often — every many minutes, every sometimes-hour — it sounds a single low note to itself, and whatever lives near it wonders what the note means, and the power does not say.

But the meaning is this: that a saucer of stone the color of an old tooth, a few hundred paces across, under a silver haze, is still holding its breath. That the eels still keep the streets. That the crabs remain crabs, bless them. That nothing there is waiting —

and that now, at last, without the bowl ever knowing it, something somewhere is listening. That is the whole intervention. Weigh it. It is almost nothing.

It is exactly, and deliberately, almost.
