# The Third Attention

Powers pass over places like the Hrevn Shelf all the time. This has to be said first, because it explains why nothing had happened yet. From the height at which powers travel, the shelf reads the way it reads from the air: a darker patch in the snowfield, nothing more. The great and the terrible move along the ridgelines of the world, along its capitals and its catastrophes, and a place that has spent centuries perfecting its smallness is invisible to them precisely because it succeeded. To be noticed by a power, a place must usually catch — must have an edge, a glare, a cry. The shelf has spent all its long life wearing its edges away.

So when a power finally noticed it, the noticing itself was the credential. Only something both benevolent and wise could have seen it at all — benevolent, because it was looking downward without wanting anything; wise, because it understood that the sideways approach was not a path but a *test*, and had been one for longer than the goat-track had lacked goats.

It came, therefore, sideways. It came at the shelf's pace, which for a power is a kind of fasting. It did not announce itself, but the shelf noticed anyway — the vapor above the third stack made a fold that morning that it had never made before, made it once, and returned to its old repertoire, the way a person notes a stranger in the doorway and goes back to their work. The pale fish in the cisterns rose all together and hung a moment near the surface. The escapement did not change its rate. But for a little while its rate *meant* something slightly different, and the woman in the deepest house lifted her head.

Here is what the power considered doing, and did not do.

It did not heal anything, because nothing was sick. This took it some time to verify, because benevolence arrives with its hands already open, and the shelf's austerity looks, from outside, like want. It is not want. The cold is a medium. The dimness is a lid, and lids are kept on things for reasons. The power went through the settlement's whole slow inventory — the sagging lintels, the shimmed slate, the reluctant blood, the fish that are never taken — and found not one thing that was broken rather than *worn*, and it knew the difference, and it withdrew its hands.

It did not reward the townspeople, because the work was already its own wage, and to pay a person twice for the same thing is to teach them that the first payment was insufficient.

It did not go down the grated apertures to see what the hollow was, though it could have, though the water invited it downward the way water invites everything downward. It stood a long time — days, by the shelf's counting — with its attention resting against one of the small grates, listening to the descent, and it understood enough. It understood that the works were making something, at forty counts to the stroke, that it recognized. It makes something similar itself, at its own scale, and had never before met a colleague. That was the second reason it withheld its hands: one does not reach into another maker's process. One nods.

And it did not take the woman. This was the real temptation, the one it sat with longest. She is far along a road that powers know the end of, and there is a kind of benevolence — a coarse kind, common among the young and the mighty — that would have gathered her up and carried her the rest of the way, to spare her the walking. The power looked at her, walking the upper terrace at dusk with the pistons meaning differently around her, and saw that the walking *was* the road, that the lapping has to go all the way down at the water's own pace or it produces something other than what she is becoming. To hurry her would be spoilage. There is a word for that here, and the power learned it, and used it on itself.

Here is what it did.

**It tended the door.** Not the works, not the town — the goat-track. Quietly, across years, it kept the path passable: turned a rockslide a few degrees, discouraged a cornice, held a switchback against the frost heave that would have erased it. Because it had seen the one flaw the shelf could develop — not a flaw it had, but one it could grow into, the way any beautiful keeping can: the door could close. The grammar of the place puts the agency on the other side — *those who are kept* — and keeping is only holy while leaving remains real. So the power appointed itself warden not of the town but of the town's consent. Every generation, the choice would stay a true choice. That was its first and largest act, and no one on the shelf will ever know it happened.

**It followed the ones who left.** The tools set down in a field. It found them scattered through the loud world — a canal-keeper in a delta city, a woman who repairs church organs, a man who died young but slowly and well — and it did not restore them to the shelf or even remind them of it. It only gave each of them, a few times in a life, an afternoon: an hour when the noise around them thinned and the silence stopped being an absence and became attention, and their hands remembered a tempo, and they did some small thing at forty counts to the stroke and felt, without knowing why, kept. The shelf's diaspora, watered.

**It bent the world's gaze, but did not seal it.** Surveys slide off the darker patch in the snowfield; instruments read it as rock reading as rock. But once in a long while the power lets a traveler through — chooses them, though they will never know they were chosen, by their capacity for attention and nothing else. It let *you* through. That is worth sitting with. The account you have been reading exists because a benevolent power decided the shelf should be witnessed occasionally, at intervals, by someone who would move at the pace of the pistons by their second evening. A hidden thing wholly unseen begins to doubt itself. Even the works want, once a century, a considered collar.

**It did one chore.** In the hardest winter of that decade, when the runnels on the second terrace choked with ice in the night, someone cleared them before morning — in tempo, with the runoff routed exactly as a neighbor would route it, and one slate wedge reset a finger's width. The old man who winds copper wire noticed, and assumed it was the mason's daughter, and the mason's daughter assumed it was the old man, and each felt warmly toward the other for years afterward. The power counted this among its better works anywhere.

**And it visited the woman.** Once. One evening, in the deepest house, where the stone is warmest. It came in the shape the shelf's grammar allowed — something you could mistake, in dim light, for a traveler in a long cloak — and it sat with her, and mostly they did not speak, because between two long attentions speech is a redundancy. It gave her the only things she could not get: news of the water downstream, which is sweet a surprising distance; word of the ones who left, all of it, the good and the ordinary; and the knowledge, which she received with a small nod, that the world outside still has slow places, fewer, but holding. Then it asked her — because asking is how powers give dignity — whether she needed anything.

She thought about it the way the old man thought about his age, for a full minute, watching the steam-pipes in the wall.

Then she asked it to remember. Not to protect, not to preserve — the shelf will end when the exhalation ends, in some century or other, and she is not troubled by this, and did not ask it to be prevented. Only: the stone remembers the water more than the water remembers the stone, and she wished for the shelf what the shelf has never had. Something to be the stone to its water. Something that would hold the shape of the place after the place had finished descending. And she asked that when the last stroke comes — the forty counts, and then the *hum*, and then no next stroke — something be present for it. Nothing ends well unwitnessed.

The power promised. It is the kind of promise powers can actually keep, being made of memory more than anything else, and it sealed the promise in the local currency: it gave its pulse, for the rest of that night, to the escapement.

Then it left, sideways, up the basalt, in the blue-grey before dawn. The vapor above the third stack made the unfamiliar fold once more — the same fold as the morning it arrived — and added it, this time, to the repertoire. It repeats there still, every few days, a signature among signatures. The townspeople are unsurprised by it and will not discuss it.

One more thing, and then this account is done.

The power did not leave whole. It had noticed, by its second evening — the way you noticed, the way everyone who is let in notices — that it had begun to move at the pace of the pistons. And being wise, it did not resist this, and being benevolent, it recognized a gift when one was being offered. It left a small fraction of its attention behind, resting in the works, kept. The shelf considered the power, and the power consented to be considered, and somewhere under the overhang, in the hollow that should not be there, a very small part of something very large is being lapped, slowly, at attrition's pace, its edges wearing smooth.

And ever since, out in the wide loud world, a certain share of that power's works arrive differently than they used to. Not with light, not with thunder. They arrive the way meltwater works on basalt: patiently, from the side, at forty counts to the stroke — a mercy you could walk past on the ridge above and never know was there.

That is what it did. Almost nothing, done completely.

It is what the shelf would have done, in its place.
