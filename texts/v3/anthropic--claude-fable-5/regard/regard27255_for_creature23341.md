It does not come through the seam. Powers of that order do not push through anything; they are nearer to weather than to visitors, and the hollow has had a long education in treating weather as furniture. It arrives the way the light arrives — strained green, laid down in a pane across the moss — and for a while it is content to be mistaken for morning.

It has been watching for some time. Benevolence keeps lists, and the hollow has been on one — moving slowly down it, season after season, because every time the question was asked, *what does this place need*, the answer came back the same, and the answer was *nothing*, and the power, being wise as well as kind, distrusted that answer and kept checking. Kindness without wisdom would have done something by now. Wisdom without kindness would have crossed the hollow off and moved on. The power kept checking. That is most of what wisdom is, in practice: returning to an answer you don't trust until you understand why it's true.

Now it is here, and it does what careful things do first. It takes out its gifts, one at a time, and holds each against the place, and sets each back down.

**Protection** — declined. The wall is the grove's own ruling on the subject, written over centuries, concluded a hundred years ago. To wrap a second wall around the first would be to suggest the first was insufficient. It would be a correction, and you do not correct a finished thing. You especially do not correct a thing whose entire achievement is that it stopped needing correction before your attention began.

**Healing** — there is nothing sick. The power runs its regard down through the strata of the moss, the green inch and the brown centuries under it, and finds the dying everywhere and finds it all load-bearing. The dead moss is the floor. The dead limbs are the drum. The fungal threads are wiring run through catacombs, and the catacombs are the foundation, and the foundation is made of everything the hollow has ever lost, compressed into something you can stand on. To heal this place you would have to take its dead away, and the dead are most of what it is. The power withdraws its hand from that gift very carefully, the way you put down something you've realized is not broken but built.

**Revelation** — meaning, voice, a sign, the standard issue of visiting powers — set down almost with embarrassment. The hollow is past meaning the way it is past aging. A finished place does not want to be told what it means; the telling would unfinish it. The wrens already say everything sayable here, at the top of their lungs, redundantly, and the hollow's reply is the toad, and the toad's reply is nothing, and that conversation has been going well for a hundred years without commentary.

**The toad** — and here the power lingers longest. It could know. One flick of attention and the question resolves: one toad or a procession, forty years or four hundred, a creature or a dynasty or an office that fills itself in the night. The knowledge is right there, the way all knowledge is right there for a thing like this.

It declines. Deliberately, with the small specific effort that declining-to-know costs an omniscient thing — and it is an effort; this is the hardest moment of the visit. But the unsettledness belongs to the hollow. The moss keeps no records, and the power decides it will not become the record. Some questions are wealth. Answering them is spending.

(Immortality for the toad: considered, almost fondly, and set down. The seat does not need the sitter to last. It needs the *sitting* to last, and the sitting has lasted, and will, by whatever mechanism it pleases. The office is already immortal. Officeholders are a detail, and the power suspects the toad would agree, if the toad agreed with things, which it does not appear to do.)

**Quiet** — and here, reaching for the obvious gift, the great lordly gesture, lifting the roar away and issuing the hollow the silence every other ancient grove receives at the door — here the power finally finds the thing it came for. Not a gift to give. A wound, waiting.

The wrens are built against the roar.

Generations deep, bred to it, every song in the dynasty pitched to clear a foundry — and the foundry will die. Everything with engines dies, and faster than groves do; the power can see the end of the works from here, the way you can see weather coming over flat land. Some decade hence: a closure, a receivership, a final shift, and the great slow heart stops on a Tuesday afternoon. And the next dawn, the loudest wrens in the world wake into stillness and detonate into it — songs scaled for thunder landing in a silent room, every note an overshoot, a dynasty of voices suddenly enormous and wrong. The roar took a century to arrive, and the grove had a century to decide it was wind. The roar is scheduled to leave in a day. The hollow can absorb anything that comes at the speed of weather. It has never once been asked to absorb a Tuesday.

So the power does its one thing. And note where it does it: *not in the hollow.* Not one drop of intervention falls inside the walls.

It goes a quarter mile, to the works it also cannot see — the grove permits nothing through but noise, even for powers; it honors that — and it touches not the works but the dying of the works. Gently. It does not save them; benevolence has other agents for towns and livelihoods, and that is a different errand on a different list. It rules only on tempo. The works will not stop. The works will *dwindle.* The shriek of metal under load goes first, decades out — a replaced bearing, an upgrade, nothing anyone mourns. The rotary throb steps down line by line, one engine retired in a decade, another the decade after. The deep concussion, the heart, goes last and goes slowest, softening across fifty years like something falling asleep mid-sentence. And every spring the fledglings learn their songs against a slightly gentler wind, and pitch a half-step softer than their fathers, never knowing it, and the dynasty walks its voice down the same stairs it climbed without ever feeling a stair. Generations on, a wren will sing at ordinary volume into ordinary quiet, and it will be the same song, and nothing will have been taken from anyone, because nothing was taken. It was let down.

The whole intervention is administered in rust schedules and maintenance budgets and the patience of procurement officers. No one will ever see it. It is benevolence wearing indifference as camouflage, because indifference is the local language — it is how the grove itself loves things — and the power is wise enough to make its mercy fluent.

Then it goes back inside, and does the last thing, which is nothing, attentively.

It stands in the center — there is room, just — and lets both worlds of sound pass through it: the engine-thunder rising through the moss into whatever it has for feet, the birdsong falling through the green air onto whatever it has for shoulders. The arena's audience has a second seat today. The toad presides over the power exactly as it presides over everything, which is to say without moving, and the power finds this correct, and stays a while, and listens, and understands that the toad got here first — that the wisest act available in this place was discovered long ago by the one animal that answers the roar with nothing, and has been performed twice daily ever since, beside the falling drop, without comment.

It permits itself one indulgence. Powers are allowed one. It lowers its attention onto the toad — all of it, the entire regard, the weight that has been laid on prophets and mountains — and the toad blinks. Once. On its own schedule, of course; it was likely going to blink anyway; toads blink; nothing can be proven. That is the only acknowledgment either of them offers, and it is deniable, which is what makes it perfect.

Then the power leaves — and it leaves through the seam. It arrived as weather, but a finished place deserves the courtesy of being departed from properly, so it presses out through the soft gap where the two great limbs almost meet, the way a person would, and the seam closes behind it with the sound of a held breath, same as for anyone.

Behind it, the drop swells on the underside of the limb, and falls, and lands beside the toad without a sound. That is the entire entry in the hollow's records of the visitation, and the hollow keeps no records.

It will sound exactly like this tomorrow. That sentence was true before the power came. The power's whole work was to make it true for every tomorrow there is — each day identical to the day before it, no century identical to the one before that, change moving through the place forever at the only speed the hollow recognizes as stillness. Which is not preservation, and is not rescue, and is not, quite, a gift. It is what wisdom does when it finds something finished: it makes sure the finishing can continue. And then it sits with it a while, and listens, and goes out the way guests go, and lets the door close on its own.
