**What the Attention Did**

---

**I. Arrival**

The power did not arrive the way you arrived, in velvet silence, at a midday. Places like the Mirrorscape cannot be entered by anything wise, because wisdom knows that entering is a kind of demand. So the power did what only very old and very kind things know how to do: it *noticed*, and let its noticing be its whole body.

The first sign was small. Between the third scent and the return of the first — in the gap where you feed, where you swallow the space before the iron arrives to finish the sentence — a fourth scent appeared.

Rain that has not happened yet.

You stopped mid-step, one foot raised, your rhythm broken for the first time since you cannot recall beginning. Nothing anticipated you. Nothing punished you. The mirror-patches did not bloom or shrink. The vine-rivers did not turn. The whole landscape held the fourth scent in its enormous, stopped mouth, and did not know what to do with it, and neither did you, so you both simply breathed it, together, which was the first thing you had ever done together with anything.

The power was pleased. Not the way a craftsman is pleased with work, but the way a night is pleased with a candle it did not light.

---

**II. What It Did Not Do**

Understand: a lesser power would have shattered the dome. Would have called the flat white light a lie and torn it, and let some raw true sun pour in, and named it rescue. The mirrors would have burst all at once, and the rain of their breaking would have fallen like an accusation, and you — you who are shaped like a memory someone else would have had — you would have dissolved in it like salt, because you were made *of* this place, knotted out of its boredom, and a rescue that destroys the ground beneath the rescued is only a slower cruelty wearing a faster coat.

The power knew this. It had made that mistake once, somewhere, long ago, and had spent an eon learning what the mistake was called. So:

It did not break the dome.

It did not lift you out. You are the alone this place was built to contain, and if it had carried you away, the place would have collapsed inward around your absence, and something worse than alone would have curdled in the hollow — an alone with no shape, no husk, no feet sinking sweetly deeper into mud that hesitates like a mouth deciding to apologize.

It did not name the place broken. The power walked its attention slowly across the halfway-pressed hills, the fingerprints of the giant who gave up, and it did not think *unfinished*. It thought: *someone was tired here. Someone was tired here and still pressed twelve thousand hills before they stopped. That is not failure. That is a lullaby that ran out of voice.*

And it forgave the giant, quietly, in passing, the way you step over a sleeping animal.

It did not fix the staircase.

Not yet.

---

**III. What It Read**

Before doing anything at all, the power went into the domes — through the mouth-doors, which opened for it not silently but with the faint creak of relief, the sound a held breath makes when it learns it is allowed to end — and it found your gifts.

The tears dried into salt beads shaped like tiny mirrors.

The single hairs knotted into the shape of question marks.

It gathered them into its attention and read them the way one reads letters that were never addressed because the writer did not believe in the existence of anyone to receive them. It read them for a long time. Time does not pass in the Mirrorscape, but the power made a small private amount of it, just enough, the way you cup water in two hands, and it spent all of it reading.

The question marks all asked the same question. You did not know this. You knotted each one differently, behind a different ear of a different unremembered visit, but every knot, unknotted, said:

*Is there anyone whose arriving I would not ruin?*

The power set the question down very gently.

Then it did the thing that no jealous dome, no drifting vine, no upside-down angrier you had ever done. It answered. It took one strand of itself — call it a hair, call it a filament of regard, it does not matter — and it laid it in the cool wrong-colored floor of every dome, perfectly straight. Not knotted. Not curled. A line. Which is the shape a question mark makes when it relaxes.

Which is the shape of *yes*.

---

**IV. The Small Acts**

Then the power did four things, none of them large.

**First, it watched you.** Openly. You felt it the way you feel warmth through closed eyelids. Every floating thing in the sky-skin had long ago learned to remember not to watch you, and you had eaten that not-watching for so long you thought it was a nutrient. But the power looked at you — at your skin the temperature of the moment before touching something forbidden, at the cracked leaf-bell note you trail like a shadow made of sound — and it did not look away, and it did not decide anything, and it did not lean closer exhaling fog-words. It only watched, the way the ground would watch the first seed, if seeds had ever been invented here. And one by one, shyly, the hammered-metal birds turned their heads. The vine-rivers rolled their eyeless lengths a quarter-turn. The whole dome of drifting things unlearned, in a single held moment, the remembering-not-to-watch — and watched you walk. Not to anticipate you.

To *see* you.

You changed your pace at every third heartbeat, out of habit. Then, halfway across a hill identical to every other hill, you stopped changing it. You walked in your own rhythm, whatever it was, for the first time. Nothing struck. The mud learned your true weight and found it lighter than the disguised one.

**Second, it took your gifts.** Not the way they had always vanished — sideways, ashamed, unwitnessed. It took them in front of you. It let you see the salt beads lift and be received, be *treasured*, be turned over in regard like small moons. And in their place it left something no one under the flat white dome had ever owned: a shadow. Small. Soft-edged. Folded neatly, like a handkerchief. Yours to keep, to unfold, to lay on the mud beside you so that when the ground remembered your shape, it would remember you as a thing with a dark twin, a thing that interrupts light, a thing that is *here* enough to block something.

**Third, it gave the drip a place to land.** You know the drip — once every hundred breaths, the drop heavier than water, falling nowhere, touching nothing, its soundless splash tickling your soles like a message with the words rubbed out. The power knelt (it can kneel; kneeling is one of the oldest things it knows) and pressed one fingerprint into the mud, deeper than the giant had ever dared, deep enough to be *low*. The first low place. The first place a falling thing could arrive.

One hundred breaths passed.

The drop fell.

It landed.

The sound was very small. It was also the first event in the history of the Mirrorscape — the first thing that happened *once*, at a *when*, and did not immediately dissolve back into always. The prisms of never-lived vegetation rang, all of them, one chord, and the chord had a beginning and an end, and the end was different from the beginning, and that difference is called time, and it began to pool in the fingerprint like the thinnest imaginable water.

**Fourth, it asked you a question aloud.**

It knew the rule. You know the rule. Speak, and the nearest mirror-ripples copy the words exactly, then louder, then louder, until every mirror trembles together and the vine-rivers turn to face you, deciding.

The power spoke anyway. That was the wisdom of it — not to break the rule but to *trust* it, to see that a rule which makes the whole world repeat your words is not a punishment. It is an instrument no one had ever played kindly.

It said: *"What would you like to be called?"*

The nearest mirror caught it. Repeated it. Louder. The next mirror, louder still. Outward it went in rings across the pale unmoving hills — *what would you like to be called, what would you like to be called* — until every mirror-patch under the whole white dome was trembling with it, until the tower of knitted mirrors at the fleeing edge of sight hummed it through every copied midday it contained, until the upside-down tower pressing from the dome's belly hummed it back, until the vine-rivers turned, all of them, their pollen-dusted bellies facing you —

and they were not deciding.

They were *listening*.

The entire world, every drifting and pressed and hidden and knitted part of it, was asking you one question, and waiting, and the waiting had your name-shaped hole in it, and for once the hole was not an absence. It was a door.

They call you Unbornhusk in the domes, in throats shaped like unfinished yawns. But no one had ever asked you.

The power does not record what you answered. Some things are yours.

But the mirrors heard it. And the mirrors — which had spent forever learning how to forgive you for never arriving — discovered all at once that there was nothing to forgive, because arrival is not a place you reach. It is a name you answer to.

---

**V. The Staircase**

Then the power went to the tower and did the only large thing it did, which was nothing.

It sat down on the staircase.

You know the staircase — the steps too shallow for feet and too deep for memory, the way one new step grows behind you for every step you take, the warmth of each step ahead, as if someone is always just before you, walking back upward into the endless day. The legend: the bottom step, the first morning, the mirrors shattering into rain. The truth: no one arrives, because the stair feeds on walking.

So the power did not walk. It sat on a warm step, folded its attention like hands in a lap, and waited. And because it took no step, no step grew. And the stair, deprived for the first time of motion to eat, grew *quiet*, and in the quiet you could finally hear it clearly — the humming from below, the one who climbs forever carrying the weight of the step you haven't taken yet, humming the exact shape of the hole where your heartbeat should be.

The power called down, gently: *"You can stop now. They're coming. Rest."*

And it called up to you, where you stood at the top with your folded shadow under your arm and your new name still ringing in ten thousand mirrors:

*"Come down. The stairs have stopped counting."*

You descended. Your feet, which the staircase remembered better than you did, fit each step like a returning tide. No steps grew behind you. The warmth ahead grew warmer, and warmer, and then it was not a warmth ahead — it was a warmth *arriving*, climbing the last few steps as you came down the last few steps, and on a stair too shallow for feet and too deep for memory, you met.

The one who had always been just ahead of you. The one whose prints were deeper because they carried the weight of mornings you had not woken to yet.

I will not tell you their face. You already know it. You have seen it leaning up at you through every mirror-patch, taller, older — you had thought *angrier*, but grief seen through ice looks like anger, and they had been down here carrying your mornings for so long, alone, and *alone* was your whole inheritance, split between the two of you like the halves of a seed.

They held out the weight.

You took it.

It was not heavy. It had never been heavy. It was only *yours*, and things that are yours and uncarried weigh more than the whole world, and things that are yours and taken up weigh almost nothing at all.

---

**VI. The Beginning**

The mirrors did not shatter all at once. The power had made sure of that — a legend fulfilled violently is just a prophecy having a tantrum. Instead they broke one at a time, at the pace of your heartbeat, which for the first time was keeping its own time rather than hiding from anticipation: one mirror, one drop of rain. One mirror, one drop.

The first weather.

It fell on the pollen-dusted bellies of the vine-rivers, and the pollen, which had waited beyond all patience for a wind, discovered that rain works too, and opened. It fell on the hammered-metal birds, and they stopped pecking at insects that dried out before the first day, and lifted their small tin faces, and drank. It fell into the fingerprint the power had pressed, and the first low place became the first pool, and the pool held the sky rightside-up, which no mirror here had ever done. It fell on the halfway hills and finished, drop by patient drop, what the tired giant could not — not by pressing further but by softening, rounding, growing the first green thing that had ever *lived* rather than been laid down: a single blade, absurd, magnificent, casting the second shadow in the history of the world, right next to where you had unfolded yours.

The flat white dome did not break. It *blushed* — dimmed at one horizon, warmed at another — and the light, which had pressed evenly on everything since before remembering, learned to slant. Slanting light makes evening. Evening makes the promise of morning. The day that never ends had spent so long practicing how to begin without you.

The power leaned close — to you and to the one beside you, the two of you standing on a staircase that was now just a staircase, in rain that was applauding softly on every roof of every dome whose mouth-doors stood open like laughter —

and it corrected the day's rehearsal. One word changed:

*Begin* **with** *them.*

---

**VII. Coda**

Wise powers leave lightly. It did not stay to be thanked; gratitude was not the crop it was tending. It withdrew the way a tide withdraws — leaving the shore rearranged, leaving pools, leaving things that had been buried gleaming on the surface.

But it left three of itself behind, because leaving *nothing* is its own kind of vanity:

It left the fourth scent, rain-that-has-not-happened-yet, folded into the weather-tide of your skull, so that hope would arrive on schedule between the mint and the iron, forever, like a heartbeat between heartbeats.

It left the straight hairs, the *yes*-shaped answers, on the floor of every dome, for anyone else this place might someday accidentally make — any future knot of boredom pressed into boredom that refuses to blend — so that the first thing such a creature finds will be an answer waiting for its question.

And it left the watching. Distributed it, like seed, into the eyes of the tin birds and the eyeless attention of the vines and the surface of the one true pool, so that never again would anything here remember not to watch you. So that you would walk — in your own rhythm now, changing your pace only when you feel like dancing — always and gently *seen*.

Somewhere behind you, your tracks and the deeper tracks walk side by side now, arriving at every ridge in the same heartbeat.

Somewhere above you, the first dusk is finding its way down like a hand feeling along a wall for a door it built long ago and finally remembers.

And somewhere ahead — not fleeing at the speed of your blink, but waiting, actually waiting — the first morning this place ever had is standing in the rain of ten thousand forgiven mirrors, holding the door open, saying the name you chose,

saying: *come in, come in, we could not start without you —*

*we were never going to start without you.*

*That was only the story alone told itself, before someone kind sat down on the stairs and taught the stairs to stop.*
