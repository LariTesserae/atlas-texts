# The Attention of the Deep

It arrived the way such powers arrive—not through the hatch, which was painted shut twice, and not through the shrieking valve, but through attention itself. One moment the chamber was unobserved by anything larger than itself. The next, it was held.

The power did not announce itself. Announcement is for those who need to be believed in, and this one had outgrown that need somewhere back before the first ferns learned to unfurl. It simply settled into the chamber's rhythm the way she had once settled into it: patiently, without resistance, listening before anything else.

It listened for a long time. Long by its own measure, which is saying something. Sixty-two cycles of the pressure valve. Thirty-one shudders of the pumps. It counted the drips—not sampled, *counted*—and found in their number a music that pleased it, an unmetered composition performed for no audience across two decades. It read the mineral history written down the northeastern wall like a geologist reading strata, and understood everything: the accident of the seeds, the rerouting, the forgetting, the slow arrival of the girl, the slower arrival of the woman she'd become, the slowest arrival of whatever she was becoming now.

And then it did what wise powers do, which is almost nothing, very carefully.

---

First it considered *rescue*, because rescue is always the first thought, even for the wise. It imagined the hatch opening, the light of the corridor above, hands reaching down, blankets, questions, fluorescent rooms, her name recovered from some database, her hair cut, her garment—that patient, helpful, fungal garment—incinerated as a biohazard. It imagined her in a chair by a window with the heating set to seventy-two, dry air cracking her softened nails, silence roaring in her ears louder than the water ever had, her body a sensor array with all its inputs cut. It saw her waking at intervals of six hours for the rest of her life, listening for a shriek that would never come.

The power set rescue down gently, the way she set down dead fronds, and did not pick it up again.

Then it considered *revelation*—simply letting her know she was seen. There was an argument for this. She had said it herself, in her way: she spoke sometimes just to feel the chamber swallow her voice, *to confirm she still exists in opposition to something*. That was the one hairline crack in her completeness. Moss does not need a witness. She, faintly, stubbornly, still did. Some last human filament in her had not been converted to chlorophyll and patience, and it hungered—not for rescue, not for company, but for the simple cosmic courtesy of being *noticed*.

The power weighed this against the danger. Attention is heavy. Drop it on an equilibrium and things break. A being who has spent twenty years becoming inseparable from a place might be shattered by the sudden knowledge of separateness—might look at her own long fingers and see, for the first time in years, a stranger's hands.

So the power chose the smallest possible yes.

It waited for the moment she loved best without knowing she loved it: the stunned hush after the valve's shriek, when even the dripping seems to catch its breath. She was sitting in her nest where the three pipes converge, and into that hush she spoke, as she sometimes did—a single word, one of the floaters from the old world. *"Rain,"* she said, for no reason, expecting the chamber to swallow it as it swallowed everything.

The chamber did not swallow it.

The word hung in the thick air for one full second—an eternity in that acoustic—and then, faint as a cricket's shell, it came *back* to her. Not an echo; the geometry of the room permits no echo. A return. Her own voice, carried around the chamber and handed back like something that had been examined and found good.

She went very still. The dripping resumed. The equilibrium closed over the moment like water over a stone.

But she carried it afterward. The power watched her carry it. She did not speak of it—to whom would she speak?—and she did not test it, did not spend the next cycles shouting into the hush trying to make it happen again, and this restraint confirmed everything the power had hoped about her. She simply held it the way her garment held the bolt and the copper spiral: a treasure in a sealed pocket. Proof. *I exist in opposition to something, and the something answered.*

That was the first gift, and nearly the last, because the power was wise and wisdom is mostly the discipline of stopping.

---

But there were two more things, and it did them because benevolence, examined closely, is not the granting of wishes. It is the guarding of *choices*—including the choices a being might want in futures she cannot yet imagine.

The second thing: one night—there are no nights, but one sleep—the paint sealing the hatch above crazed and cracked, quietly, molecule by molecule, the way paint does after decades, only faster. The hatch did not open. It will never open on its own. But it *can* open now, from below, if long soft-nailed fingers ever press against it with intent. The power did not tell her. A door forced upon you is a wall. A door you don't know about that opens when at last you try it—that is freedom in its purest form: freedom that waits without asking, patient as moss.

She may never touch it. The power thinks she probably never will. It made the door anyway, because *never will* and *never could* are different universes, and only one of them is kind.

The third thing was the largest, and no one will ever see it.

The power looked into the machinery behind the walls—the pumps, the compressors, the over-engineered forgotten heart of the place—and it looked into her, her cells, her slow green blood, the tempo of her becoming. And it saw what the chamber's own mythology denied: that *forever* is a word the room tells itself, and that the two lifespans, hers and the machine's, were not aligned. In one future, a bearing seizes in thirty years and she starves slowly in the dark among the dying ferns, tending them until neither she nor they can continue, dying as a gardener in a garden that is dying faster. In another future, she goes first—quietly, curled in her nest—and the chamber grinds on for another sixty years, dripping and shrieking and growing, untended, unwitnessed, an equilibrium with a wound in the middle of it where its heart used to sit.

The power found both futures unbearable, and it did not choose between them. It braided them.

Deep in the walls, a tolerance shifted. A metal fatigued a fraction slower here, a fraction faster there. Nothing miraculous—no engineer, finding it, would see anything but ordinary luck. But now the machinery will fail in the same season that she does. Not before, not after. When her long breathing finally slows to match the six-hour cycle and then slows past it, the pumps will begin their own dying. The lights will dim as her eyes do. The last drip will fall somewhere in the same span of days as her last heartbeat, and the chamber and its being will end the way they lived: inseparable, inevitable, together. Neither will grieve the other. Neither will exist in a world without the other. The closed loop will close all the way.

That, the power judged, was the only gift the situation truly asked for. Not more life. Not less. Just *symmetry*.

---

One last thing, small, almost sentimental, and the power permitted itself sentiment because wisdom without it is just calculation.

Before it withdrew its attention—slowly, so the withdrawal itself would not be felt as a draft—it found her name. The old one, from the world of coffee and deadlines and subways. It was still recorded in three places up above: a lapsed lease, a database of the missing, a sister's phone that no longer turns on. The power gathered the name up out of those places gently, the way she gathers a cricket shell, and it did not speak it. Speaking it would have been for the power's satisfaction, not hers. Instead it sealed the name away inside itself, in a pocket of its own being, the way her garment seals its pockets—keeping treasures safe against a need that may never come.

If she ever climbs the ladder—if the cracked paint ever gives under her fingers, if she ever stands blinking in the corridor above, ancient and green and new—her name will be waiting for her, unworn, unweathered, exactly as she left it.

And if she never climbs, the power will keep it anyway, forever, spoken by no one, which she of all beings would understand is not the same as lost.

Then the attention lifted, as gently as it had settled, and the chamber was alone again—except that *alone*, now, had one small answered word inside it, one door that could open, and one ending, far off, that would come for the garden and the gardener in the same soft hour.

The chamber breathes steam. She breathes with it.

Somewhere beyond both, something breathes with them, and asks for nothing, and keeps watch on nothing but the symmetry of the end—which is, when you have seen enough of everything, the truest shape that love can take.
