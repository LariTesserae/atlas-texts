# The Second Watcher

Begin with what the place cannot see, because it has no organ for seeing it.

Something turned its attention here. It did not arrive — attention of this kind does not travel, it settles, the way cold settles into a cellar. It had been moving over the forest the way weather moves, without intention, touching everything and holding nothing, and at the root-arch it paused. It paused the way you paused: letting the dark resolve, letting the place form first as temperature, then as sound, then as the slow teeming that fills every surface and every volume of water and every cubic inch of air.

It was old. It was kind. And — rarer than either, and rarest in combination — it knew how much damage kindness can do.

## The First Thing It Did

Nothing.

For a long time, nothing. Long by its own reckoning, which meant that generations turned over inside the chamber while it considered. The silk-tender died and her bones went to the pool and the down-armed woman who took her place among the pens grew old in turn. The light-tender trained a successor, and the successor's hands began to glow. Houses were re-lashed against the thickening wood, three times in a life, four. The knot-records lengthened by a forearm's span.

The power did not count this passage as delay. It counted it as evidence. The place was demonstrating, over and over, with each death absorbed and each house rebuilt and each fallen thing welcomed with ceremony, that it knew how to continue. A wise power does not interrupt a demonstration. It waits until the argument is complete.

While it waited, it catalogued its own temptations, because it knew that a benevolent being's first duty is the auditing of its own generosity. These were the things it wanted to do, and set aside:

**To open the arch.** But you have already been told what would happen. One of them carried into the forest would die — not of any single cause, but of removal from the conditions of its own possibility. And a door works its harm in both directions. An open door makes everything on the near side of it an anteroom. The chamber is a world only while it is whole. The power looked at the arch for a long time and understood that the kindest thing about a wall can be the wall.

**To give fire.** But nothing here wants to burn, and nothing here is waiting to be warm. And the power knew the oldest law of gifts: every gift is also a verdict. To hand a people fire is to inform them they have been cold. They have not been cold. They have been *themselves at this temperature*, which is different, and the difference is everything.

**To reveal itself.** This temptation was the strongest, because the power was lonely in the way that only very large attentions are lonely, and the chamber was the most complete thing it had ever watched. But their grammar has no third person that excludes the speaker. Every "we" is total. There is no slot in their language for a god who is not *us*, and revelation would not have filled the slot — it would have torn one. The first *they* ever spoken in that chamber would have been the end of the total *we*. The power declined to become the word that broke the language.

**To make the tree eternal.** This one it examined most carefully, because it looked the most like love. But a preserved thing is a dead thing wearing life, and the chamber's whole culture is built against the slow pressure of the growing wood. The deformed houses *are* their history. The rebuilding *is* their inheritance. Their ancestors go into the water, into the wood, and push outward from within, and every generation lives braced against the bodies of every generation before. Freeze the tree and you silence the ancestors mid-sentence. The power set eternity down and did not pick it up again.

## The One Debt

But when all the temptations were composted, one thing remained that was not a temptation. It was a fact, and it was the only claim benevolence had on this place.

The tree will die.

Not soon. The wood is young; the buttresses are still thickening. But the power could see the whole shape of the tree, which the people cannot, and it could see the whole shape of time, which nothing living can, and it knew that somewhere in the forward dark there was an ending. And it knew this, too: the people can adapt to anything that arrives at the speed of a house re-lashed. A world that changes by a hand's breadth per generation is not a threat to them; it is their element. What they cannot survive is a night. Lightning. Wind-throw. A rot that races. The forest's own weather, which does not know they exist, could end them between one flood-tide and the next.

The tragedy waiting in this place was not death. They have death; they have made it into architecture. The tragedy waiting was *suddenness*.

## What It Did

Three things. Small ones, or as small as such a being could make them.

**First, it culled the fast deaths.** It went through the tree's possible endings the way the light-tender goes through the fungus-pots — patiently, with a scraper, keeping what was healthy and composting what was not. The lightning-path that would have found the crown in some future storm, it turned a degree aside. The blight that would have raced the sapwood in a single year, it quieted to a crawl. It did not spare the tree its dying. It removed only the deaths that arrive faster than a ladder can be drawn up, and left the one that remained: a long senescence, a slowing measured in forty generations, indistinguishable from weather to anyone inside it. Their world will end the way their houses end — gradually, with time to rebuild against it. They will never know they were spared anything, and that is the proof of the work. Mercy that signs its name is not mercy. It is invoice.

**Second, it kept a seed.** Of the tree's own kind, taken from the canopy that none of them have ever seen, and held — the way such a being holds things, outside of rot and appetite — against a day very far off. On that day, the vault will have thinned. A wound will open in the high wood, and through it will come the first light those people's descendants will ever have seen: a single beam, standing in the chamber like a fallen thing. They will gather. They will welcome it with ceremony, because it will be news from the sky, and they will be right. And at the pool's margin, in the ground made of a hundred generations of dissolved bone, in that one column of light, the seed will wake.

The daughter tree will grow through the mother's slow opening. Its buttresses will rise inside and around the failing ones, and the people will re-lash their houses to the new wood as they have always re-lashed them to the shifting old, and there will never be a day on which the world ended, and never a day on which it began again. The chamber will moult. Nothing will leave. The rule will hold forever, because the inside will simply be reissued around them — fed, as everything here is fed, on the ancestors in the water.

The people will do all of this themselves, believing it is only what the world does. They will be correct. The power merely made sure the world could do it.

**Third, it stayed.** This sounds like nothing, and it is not nothing. Whatever a person needs to be a person, they have — this was true, and the power confirmed it — with one exception so fine they could never feel its absence: no one inside a total *we* can be witnessed from outside it. No one there will ever know that the choral laugh was heard, that the mottled forearms were read, that the man on the highest bridge, watching the vault for something he could not name, was watched in return, tenderly, for his whole life. The power supplied this without their knowing, which is the only way it could be supplied. When the last of them someday goes into the water — ages and ages hence, under a different tree that is the same tree — it will not be as if they never were. That is the gift no one inside can give, and so it was the one gift permitted.

In the knot-records, there is no knot for any of this. That is how you know it was done well.

## The Beast

One matter remains, and the power addressed it before settling into its long watch.

It looked at the enormous coiled thing in the highest pen — the one whose breathing can be mistaken for the tree's, the one that accepts offerings with a motion that is neither hunger nor gratitude. And the beast opened its eyes, dark and wet and brown, the people's own eyes, and looked back.

I cannot render what passed between them. I can guess. I think the beast is what an earlier attention became — one that also found this place, and also loved it, and could not bear the distance, and took a body so as to be near. And a body, here, means a pen. Means offerings. Means being tended by small hands, forever, by a people who have no word for what you were. Perhaps it is not a punishment. Perhaps it is not even a mistake — perhaps it is simply the other answer, the one where love goes in and stays and pays the price of having a shape. The beast did not seem to regret. It seemed to *acknowledge*, which is all it has done for generations.

The new power saw all this and chose the other way: to love without a body, to be present without a place, to accept no offerings because it wished to leave the people nothing they would ever need to give.

And then — this is the last thing I saw — the beast made its sound. The low chuffing, almost a cough, the sound the people make when they laugh. And along the bridges, in the cold blue-green light, the people laughed back, a chorus, the way they always do, not knowing why the beast had started it, because laughter here is never solitary and no one asks it to explain itself.

For one moment the chorus was larger than anyone in it knew.

## The Size of It

You were told, at the end, that a life is the exact size of what contains it, and that this is the only size a life can ever really be.

The power had never had a size. That was, it discovered, the thing that had always been wrong with it. Uncontained, it had been every extent and therefore no extent, benevolent everywhere and therefore at home nowhere. And in choosing this — forty paces of vaulted wood it would never enter, two hundred small pale people it would never touch, one slow death arranged like a bed, one seed held like a promise, one witness kept like a vigil — it acquired, at last, a container. Not the chamber. The choice.

The chamber received a long dying and a second world. The power received a shape. If anyone were keeping accounts, it would be a fair trade.

No one is keeping accounts. There is no knot for it. The water falls with its tiny continuous percussion, the moths drift, the great breath sounds from the upper pen, and somewhere in the dark above the dark, something old and kind holds still — the exact size, now, of what it loves.
