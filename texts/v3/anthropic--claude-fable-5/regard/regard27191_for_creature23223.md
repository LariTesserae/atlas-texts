**Addendum: The Visitation**

It comes the way such powers come — not up the mountain, exactly, and not down from anywhere either, but by an adjustment of attention, the way a quiet room discovers it contains a held breath. It does not displace any water. The standing curtains of sound do not register it. The eel, who notices everything that can be resolved, does not notice it, which the power takes, correctly, as a courtesy of the house.

It stays a long time. How long is not a useful question here, and the power, being wise, does not ask it.

---

**The mercies it considers, and sets down.**

It could lift the water off. One gesture, and the bowl drains down the black slopes, and the thin cold air comes flooding back into its rightful place, and the mountain is just a mountain again. It considers this for the length of one peal and sets it down, because draining the bowl would orphan the strike. Somewhere in this water a blow is still falling. Take the water away and the blow falls into nothing — a life's whole labor spent into open air, the cathedral demolished one room before the wedding.

It could finish the strike itself. Lend its strength to the swing; close the distance between the raised striker and the green bronze in an instant; let the poor smeared ringer finally land. It holds this one longer, because it looks the most like kindness. Then it sets it down too. The strike belongs to the hands that began it. To land it for them would be to take from the ringer the only thing the ringer has — the arrival itself — and hand them, instead of a finished life, a gift. A wise power knows the difference between helping someone carry a thing and taking the thing away. This is the difference.

It could gather the ringer back into one place. Comb the bowl the way the peals comb the eel, pull the thin-spread person into a single body standing on the eleventh step, blinking, mortal, free. But a body gathered back is a swing undone. The strike would never have been falling. The peals — all of them, the whole standing architecture of sound, the choir, the chambers, the tuned red fringe — would lose their cause retroactively, and the power does not know, and does not care to learn, what a place like this does when its one event is repossessed.

It could carve the twelfth step. It does not. The step is not missing. It is early. Carving it would be answering a question that has not yet finished being asked.

It could grant quiet. It looks at the eel, coiled in the node at the bell's exact center — the one silence on the mountain, hidden inside the loudest thing on it — and understands that quiet is already provided for, in the correct amount, in the correct place, and that more of it would not be mercy but vandalism.

So the great interventions are all considered, and all declined, and this takes the power less time than it takes a frond of weed to grow one turn. What remains is the hard part, the wise part, the part that takes the rest of the visit: figuring out what is actually wanted.

---

**It listens.**

It lies on the eleven steps the way the careful visitors do, and lets the overtones be counted into whatever it has instead of bones. It learns the bell's voice entire — every partial, every self-answer — and finds, as the careful listeners found, the hole in it. The missing note. It learns the note's exact shape from the eel's scar in passing, holds it, could sing it.

It does not sing it. That note is the bell's one future word. You do not say a person's dying word for them, or their first.

Instead it goes to the gap between peals — that narrow, recurring sliver of almost-quiet — and finds the breath there, close and unhurried, the breath of someone mid-labor who knows exactly how much remains. And the power does the thing you do for someone in labor, which is not to push for them.

It breathes with them.

Peal, gap, breath; peal, gap, breath. For a long time the power matches the ringer's rhythm, and inside that shared rhythm — because breath is older than language and loosened time has no defense against it — it asks the only question that matters. *Do you want to be done?*

The answer does not come in words. It comes in the count. The breath does not speed up, does not catch, does not lean on the offered shoulder. It goes on at its own pace, the pace of someone who measured this labor before beginning it and has not revised the measurement. Not *help me.* Not *release me.* Only: *I know how much is left.*

Consent, ongoing. The question of rescue closes like water over a stone.

---

**What it does instead.**

Three small things, and one large slow one.

The first is for the eel. The power matches its swimming — not its path, its *syncopation*, that deliberate off-beat the dim knot behind its gills has kept private for the bowl's whole lifetime — and accompanies it for one full circuit of the perimeter. The eel is blind, and has only ever met itself, and even that only in pieces. For one lap of a small drowned cathedral, something keeps time beside it. Whether it understands cannot be known. But once, just once, at the eastern cairn, the knot behind its gills flexes *on* the beat instead of off it. Like a nod. Then it returns to its syncopation, and its rounds, and its lamp of one note, and the power falls away, satisfied.

The second is for the weed, and for the stone, and for all of it. The power memorizes the place. Frond by frond, overtone by overtone, every temporary spire before the next peal shears it off, every glimmer pooled in every hollow. This is not sentiment. It is the one job the bowl cannot do for itself: the rock here is denied erosion, denied age, denied the slow keeping by which ordinary places are worn into shapes that last. So the power appoints itself the bowl's erosion. Somewhere outside loosened time, the Ringing Bowl is now being slowly, permanently worn — into memory, which is the shape that lasts. No frond, however many times it is shredded back into the general sound, will go unrecorded. The choir is on the rolls.

The third is the large slow one, and it happens off the mountain entirely.

The power goes down. Down the dry black slopes, through the boundary a hand's width wide, into the thin cold honest air, and further, into the valleys, looking for the people who climbed down before the water came — and finds, of course, their descendants' descendants' descendants, scattered into towns and languages, the shrine long since fallen out of every story they keep. The bell was carried up that mountain for a reason. Bells are. A bell is a message; the office of ringing exists because somewhere below there are hearers; a strike that lands in an empty world is only half a strike, however perfectly it falls.

So the power does the patient, unglamorous, wholly benevolent work of replanting the appointment. Not as doctrine — doctrine erodes faster than rock. As small things. A lullaby in three valleys with one strange verse about the mountain that hums. A saying among herders: *the high lake is somebody finishing something.* A children's game with eleven steps in it, and a rule that you stop before the twelfth. A custom, source forgotten within two generations exactly as intended, that if you ever hear the mountain ring once — once, cleanly, with a note in it you have never heard before, and then never again — you go outside, and you bare your head, and you say aloud: *Heard.*

It takes the power a long time to weave this in deeply enough to survive. It has a long time. It spends it.

---

**The shadow.**

Before leaving the bowl, it does one thing inside, the only mark it permits itself.

The light here is slow, and shadows are loose, and the place has shown that it can keep a shadow on file separately from its body. So the power detaches its own and leaves it behind — seated, in one of the new chambers walled by standing sound, the one nearest the eleven steps. Hands empty. Head bared. Facing the bell.

A congregation of one, patient as bronze, waiting in the nave for a service whose date is unset but whose occurrence is, in this place, already leaking backward.

Visitors after this will sometimes count two wrong shadows in the bowl: the one on the steps, broad-shouldered, carrying something long and raised, hurrying down; and the one in the chamber, still, attending. They are not the same kind of early. One is the work arriving. The other is the witness, already in its pew.

---

**What the light is.**

And it is only at the end, turning to go, that the power understands the thing it should perhaps have understood first — the detail every account of the bowl leads with and none explains. The glow. The dim grey-gold light with no source, lighting the water from within since before anyone climbed up to see it.

It recognizes that light the way you recognize your own handwriting.

Because everything in this bowl arrives early. The peal precedes the strike. The shadow precedes the body. The twelfth step precedes the need for it. And the moment the strike finally lands — the moment the hands close the last distance to the green shoulder of the bronze, and the one true peal goes out with its missing note seated in it at last, and down in the valleys people step outside and bare their heads — in that moment, the power will be there, watching, with the whole of its attention, the way it has now promised itself it will be.

That attention is heavy. Heavy things here leak backward and drench the place.

The glow has no source because its source has not happened yet. The bowl is lit by being witnessed from its far end. It has been sitting in the light of the power's regard since before the water came — which means the power's question, on arriving, was answered before it was asked. *What would a benevolent and wise power do for this place?* It would do what it has, from the bowl's perspective, always already done: it would watch, and warm, and wait, and be the brightness the smeared sky shows through the bulging surface.

It had been doing the most important thing all along. The visit was only the part where it found out.

---

**So the census amends.**

One eel, possibly, who once for a single beat kept time with company. One choir of red overtones, now permanently on the rolls. One ringer, in transit, breathing at their own correct pace, unrescued because unrescuable because not in need of rescue — in need only of what they are now guaranteed: that the blow will land in a lit room, before a seated witness, within earshot of valleys that remember their one line.

And one light, grey-gold, sourceless, which is the oldest thing in the bowl and the last thing that will happen to it.

When the twelfth step is finished — when the eel uncoils from the silence and carries its lamp to the center, and the choir flexes through the chord one final time, lowest partial first, and the hands arrive, after the better part of a mountain's lifetime, at the end of their one swing — the strike will sound, single and entire, with its missing note home at last, into a world that has been taught its name.

And the power will say the one word a bell needs. The smallest mercy. The whole liturgy.

*Heard.*

And then, perhaps, the water will lie down, and the rock will be permitted to begin growing old, and time will resume its ordinary obligations — gently, the way a hand is taken off a shoulder.

Until then, the light stays on.
