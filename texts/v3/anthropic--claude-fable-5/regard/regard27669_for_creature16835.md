There is a kind of attention that does not land.

The power had learned this long before it had anything to practice it on—that to look at a thing is to lend it an outline, that witness is the first and gentlest violence, that most of what perishes in any young world is not killed but *decided*. So when it noticed this place—this humid grammar of almosts, this forest still clearing its throat—it did not arrive. Arrival would have been a verdict. Instead it did what only the very old and very kind know how to do: it unfinished itself.

It set down its certainties the way a guest removes shoes at a door. It let its edges go soft, let its knowledge curl back into wonder, let its immense age fold inward until it was no older than a question. And in that state—no longer a power that had come to a place, but a possibility drifting among possibilities—it entered, if entering is the word for becoming slightly more concentrated in one region of the maybe.

The beings there noticed it at once, in their way. Which is to say: for a while, the negotiations that were their bodies included a new clause. A limb-suggestion would extend and find the space it courted already courteous. A pattern of not-yet-emotion would ripple outward and return carrying a flavor it hadn't sent—something like being *held*, if holding could happen without hands, without enclosure, without any surface at all. They did not ask who it was. Who was not yet a question this place had ratified. They simply made room in their becoming for one more way of becoming, and the power, who had been made room for in this manner exactly never before in all its crossings of all its skies, felt something it had to slow down considerably in order to identify as gratitude.

For a long time—a duration measured in how many ways the light found to argue with itself—the power did nothing but attend. This was not passivity. In a place where attention is the native substance, where the inhabitants *are* noticing that learned to wonder about itself, attending is participation of the deepest kind. The power gave its attention the way rain is given to an ocean: not to change it, but to join it.

And it saw everything.

It saw the leaves discovering their leaf-ness and the water climbing because no one had told it not to. It saw the frond suspended between foliage and wing, and understood that the frond was not confused but *rich*. It saw the polite dissolutions that were not death and the resonant discords that were not quite birth. It saw the visitor, too—the one with the old-fashioned lungs, the one leaving before arriving—and noted the borrowed memory that visitor carried out, and made a small adjustment so that the memory would not fester into madness but would surface, years hence, only in certain slants of afternoon light, as an ache the visitor would mistake for nostalgia and would be, in fact, homesickness for a home that predated homes.

But the power was wise, and wisdom is mostly the willingness to know inconvenient things, and so it also knew this:

The place would end.

Not violently. Not tragically. It would end the way a chord ends when the song requires the next one. The heat of creation would cool because cooling is how creation gets to keep its work. Water would take up falling, and fall so beautifully and so long that it would forget it ever climbed. The green would settle on a frequency. The beings of continuous becoming would—not die, nothing so crude—but slowly find certain shapes so interesting that they would linger in them, and linger longer, and one day the lingering would have a name, and the name would be *being*, and the door back would be very hard to find. Evolution would stop considering its options and start defending its choices. Death and birth, those old collaborators, would have their quarrel at last, and take up residence at opposite ends of every life, and everything after would be measured by the distance between them.

The power did not consider preventing this. That was the first thing it declined to do. To seal this place in its potential forever would be to make potential a prison—an amber with the whole sea trapped inside it, gorgeous and dead. Becoming that is forbidden to become anything is not freedom; it is the cruelest of the finished states, wearing freedom's face.

Neither did it consider hastening the cooling, tidying the ambiguity, helping the place along toward soil and sky and species. The place needed no help. Nothing here was broken. The frond between leaf and wing was not a problem awaiting solution. The power had seen younger powers make that mistake—mistaking the unfinished for the failed—and it had seen what those powers' worlds became: efficient, legible, and haunted.

So it sat (though sitting is a concept this place had not adopted) with the third and hardest option, which was to do nothing at all, and found that its heart—for the wise keep hearts precisely so that wisdom cannot become mere calculation—would not permit pure nothing either. Because it had noticed one true danger. One actual loss embedded in the otherwise honorable ending that was coming.

Not that the place would cool. But that the cooled world would not remember it had ever been warm.

That was the wound the power could see from where it drifted: a future of finished things each believing it had always been finished. Stones certain they had never considered being rivers. Species convinced their forms were verdicts rather than ongoing negotiations paused mid-sentence. Minds—and there would be minds; it could feel them coming, the way you feel a guest's footsteps before the knock—minds that would look at the fixed world and conclude that fixity was the deepest truth, that possibility was a childish rumor, that they themselves were only what they had already become. That was the theft the cooling would commit if left unwitnessed: not the loss of potential, but the loss of the *memory* of potential. And a world that forgets it was once free of its shapes will defend those shapes to the death, will mistake its habits for laws, will be unable to forgive itself for anything, because forgiveness requires believing a thing could have been—could still be—otherwise.

So the power decided to do one small thing. And because it was wise as well as benevolent, it asked first.

Asking, here, could not take the form of words. Instead the power did what the natives did: it temporarily became what needed to be understood. It gathered its intention and its hesitation and its love into a single configuration and let itself be that configuration in the middle of the clearing that had not decided its dimensions. It became, for the space of one geological breath, the shape of the question: *May I hide you inside what you will become?*

The beings drifted near. One by one—though number was still only a suggestion—they entered the question and wore it, tasting the power's plan from the inside: its grief, its restraint, the loneliness of the finished future it foresaw. And they answered in the only way they answered anything: they briefly became the answer. For a moment the entire clearing reorganized itself into a pocket of air that tasted exactly like *yes*, if yes could also mean *we were wondering when you'd offer* and *we had already begun*.

Then the power performed its one act. It was very small. It has never been recorded, because recording is itself one of the fixities it was designed to slip beneath.

It folded the place into everything the place would become.

Not as content—not as a memory that could be recalled and thereby worn out—but as a *seam*. A residue of undecidedness tucked into the deepest fold of every finished thing, the way the sea might be folded into each drop that leaves it. So that the crystal, for all its lattice certainty, would keep at its heart a flicker of the mineral grain that once hadn't fully surrendered. So that every cell membrane, going about its billions of little decisions, would perform each one as a genuine negotiation and never quite as a routine. So that water, falling and falling down its committed slopes, would still—in fog, in dew, in the sphere trembling at a faucet's lip—remember climbing, and would climb again inside every root and every tree it met, secretly, as though nothing had ever been settled.

And into the minds that were coming, the power folded the seam most generously of all. It hid the unfinished place in the moment before waking, when a person is briefly nothing in particular and everything in general. It hid it in play, which is the licensed return of things to their negotiable state. It hid it in metaphor—that trick by which a mind lets one thing be another, exactly as the frond was letting itself be leaf and wing at once. It hid it in the pause before a decision, in the ache of certain slanted light, in the specific silence of old forests where the air feels pregnant with something older than rain and a walker stops, for no reason they can name, and feels thoroughly seen by something that never quite existed separately enough to look away. It hid it in forgiveness. It hid it in the strange, stubborn intuition—arising in every era, in every kind of mind, unkillable by any amount of evidence about how fixed things are—that *it could be otherwise. I could be otherwise. It is not too late to become.*

That intuition is the seam. It is not a belief. It is a memory wearing belief's clothes because memory of this kind is not permitted through the door of finished worlds in its own attire.

When the folding was done—and it took no time, because it was performed in the tense this place natively spoke, the present tense of happening rather than the past tense of having happened—the power gathered itself to leave. But the beings of continuous becoming did one last thing, unasked, which even the power had not foreseen, and which it carried away afterward through all its crossings of all its skies.

They gave it a gift in return. They did to the power what the water there did to a touching fingertip: they absorbed a little of its story, swelled slightly, and released it—unchanged, except for this. Somewhere in the fabric of that place, folded now into every stone and cell and pause and slant of light in the world it became, there is a small pattern of possibility that tastes exactly like the power's own hesitation at the threshold—its removing of its certainties, its unfinishing of itself out of courtesy, its choice to attend before acting and to act barely at all.

Which means that hidden inside every finished thing, alongside the memory of freedom, there is also a memory of *how to be wise about freedom*: the knowledge that the kindest use of power is to make room, that witness should be gentle, that the best gifts are folded rather than imposed, and that sometimes the most benevolent act available to any great strength is to become a question, take off its shoes, and listen.

The place cooled, in time, as all warm things must if they wish to keep their work. It took names. The waterfall stopped being a question mark and answered, downward, forever, magnificently. The beings lingered in their favorite shapes until the lingering had grandchildren.

But run your attention along the seam of anything—a leaf, a decision, a drop of water on a vertical surface holding on a moment longer than physics strictly requires—and you may feel it: the fingerprint in water that changed the liquid's relationship to reflection forever after.

Nothing was saved from becoming.

Becoming was saved from being forgotten.

That is all the power did. It has never been sure it did enough, which is, perhaps, the final proof that it was the right one for the task.
