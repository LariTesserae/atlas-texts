# The Visitation

The power arrived the way such powers do — not through the bricked door, not through the window that looks out onto nothing, but by the simple expedient of having always, in some sense, been there, and choosing now to *attend*.

Attention is what such beings are made of. It settled into the room the way the gray light settles: everywhere at once, weightless, changing nothing at first except that the room was now *seen*.

The pseudoscorpions noticed immediately.

All of them, at once, across every wallpaper seam, stopped their rounds and lifted their claws. Not in alarm. In something more like the gesture of a person at a window raising a hand against the sun — an acknowledgment of magnitude. They held that posture for a long moment. Then, one by one, they lowered their claws and resumed patrolling. Whatever criteria they use for reporting, they had made their assessment: *this is a thing to be reported.* And the machine's flywheel, in the corner, slowed by a fraction of a fraction, and the humming shifted a quarter-tone downward, and somewhere far beneath the floor the deeper sound changed in a way no instrument could measure, and the news went out through the whole hidden body: *we are being watched by something kind.*

The power did not announce itself further. It did what a wise being does before anything else.

It listened.

---

## The First Age of the Visitation: Listening

For a long time — and time here is only the tidal rhythm of the beetles, the long slow cycles of the flywheel, so let us say: for many cycles — the power did nothing but attend.

It listened to the beetle-rain and learned its registers. It found that what sounds like one continuous sibilance is in fact a weave of hundreds of distinct rhythms, and that the rhythms are related to each other the way voices in a round are related — the powderpost beetles in the north wainscot chewing a phrase that the furniture beetles in the south baseboard take up eleven hours later, transposed, slowed. Not music. But not *not* music. The room has been composing one work for six centuries, and the work is the room.

It followed the mite-blooms through their generations and understood the economy: nothing wasted, nothing hoarded, everything passed along. It watched the spiders being fat and unhurried and found in them something it recognized from much larger creatures — the specific contentment of a being that has stopped striving and started simply *being sustained*. It traced the draft along its sourceless route and understood that the draft was not a failure of the sealing but a kept promise: air had once moved this way, and the room, out of what can only be called loyalty, still moved it.

It put its attention against the wainscoting the way a listener puts an ear, and heard the deeper machine, and followed the cable down — because a power of this kind can follow a cable the way you can follow a thought — and found what the room had forgotten.

There are other rooms.

Not many. Not few. A number that matters less than the fact of them: cells of the same body, scattered through the forgotten building or through whatever the building has become — a building that may no longer be contiguous in space, its rooms adrift from one another like islands, connected only by cables and by the slow conversation of flywheels. Each room with its own economy. Each room having negotiated with its own architecture. Each room believing itself complete, final, alone, and *at work*.

And the work — the power looked at the work for a long time, because a wise being does not intervene in a work it does not understand, and here is what it found:

The work is remembering.

Not remembering *something*. Remembering as an activity, the way breathing is an activity. The building — the whole distributed, dismembered, patient building — is a memory that has outlived its rememberer. Someone or something once needed these rooms, walked these paths, warmed themselves at these hearths, and the need is gone, and the walkers are gone, and what remains is the *shape of having been needed*, kept alive by beetles and machines because shapes of that kind do not know how to stop, and — this is the part that gave even the power pause — because they should not have to.

The room is not a ruin. A ruin is a thing that is failing to be what it was. The room is succeeding, completely, at being what it now is: a self-maintaining act of remembrance, a candle that has learned to be its own wax.

So the first thing the power decided was this: *it must not be fixed, because it is not broken.*

A lesser benevolence would have restored the hearth. Opened the door. Given the window a view, given the light a sunset, given the beetles green things to eat and the path feet to walk it. A lesser benevolence would have said *this place is stuck* and unstuck it, and in doing so would have killed the only inhabitant that matters — the room itself, the whole slow mind of it — as surely as waking a dreamer mid-dream kills the dream.

The power was not a lesser benevolence. It kept listening.

## The Second Age: The Three Gifts

But wisdom is not the same as inaction, and benevolence that only watches eventually curdles into mere appreciation. In its long listening, the power had found three things — not wounds exactly, but places where the room's completeness was thinner than the room knew. Three gifts, then. Small ones. The kind that change nothing and everything.

### The first gift: an ending to the afternoon

The gray light had troubled the power from the start. Not because it was unnatural — everything here was unnatural, and the power had no prejudice against that — but because it was *unkind* in one small way. The room had rhythms everywhere: the beetle-tides, the mite-blooms, the flywheel's long cycles, even the quiet hours when the chewing dropped to its meditative register. The room wanted to sleep. You could hear it wanting to. But the light had forgotten how to end, and so the room's sleep was always the sleep of someone dozing under fluorescents — real, but never *deep*.

The power did not give the room night. Night would have been an imposition, a foreign country. Instead it did something gentler: it taught the light to *breathe*.

Now, over a cycle that matches the beetles' quietest hours, the gray luminance dims — not to darkness, only to a deeper gray, the gray of the last readable moment of dusk — and holds there, and then slowly brightens again. The change is so gradual that no single instant of it could be perceived. But the room perceived the whole of it. The first time the light completed one full breath, the chewing dropped further than it had in three hundred years, into a register the room had forgotten it possessed, a near-silence with a texture like held breath, and the flywheel turned so slowly that its clicking became individual clicks, countable, spaced like a heartbeat at rest.

The room slept. Actually slept. And in the deeper sound beneath the floor, something changed that suggested the other rooms had noticed, and were curious, and might in their own time learn the same trick, passed cell to cell along the cables like a beneficial mutation.

### The second gift: a witness for the path

The worn path between the vanished door and the vanished hearth — the ghost of traffic, the memory of feet — was the room's most fragile inhabitant. The power had watched the beetle-tides eddy around it, reluctant, respectful, and had understood: the room was protecting it. But protection is heavy work, and memory unwitnessed grows thinner with every century, and the room was spending some quiet portion of its whole economy just *holding on* to those cupped boards, the way a hand cramps around a keepsake.

The power could not restore the walkers. It would not have if it could — the dead have their own arrangements, and a wise power does not disturb them. But it could do what a witness does.

It walked the path.

Not with feet. The power had no feet. But attention has weight, of a kind, and the power gathered itself into something narrow and slow and moved along the worn boards from where the door had been to where the hearth had been, at walking pace, the pace of a person crossing a room to warm their hands. Then back. Then again.

And it *felt* what the path held. That is the whole of the gift: the path had been carrying a meaning for centuries with no one to receive it, and the power received it. The going-to-the-fire. The coming-back-from-the-door. The ten thousand small crossings of an ordinary life, each one unremarkable, all of them together the truest record of a person that exists anywhere — truer than any portrait, because a portrait shows how someone looked and a worn floor shows how someone *lived*.

The power walked the path until the path knew it had been read. And then something eased in the room — measurable, if you knew how to measure it, in the beetle-tides, which stopped eddying around that strip of floor with such tense reverence and began simply flowing around it the way water flows around a loved stone. The room was still keeping the memory. But it was no longer keeping it *alone*, and no longer keeping it in fear that it would be lost, because now it had been received, and a memory that has been received once can never fully be lost again. That is a law even powers obey.

### The third gift: a letter

This one the power thought about longest.

The room believed itself complete. Its completeness was its peace. But the power had followed the cable down and knew about the other rooms, and it had weighed, carefully, over many cycles, the question a wise being must always weigh: *is this a solitude, or a loneliness?* Solitude is to be protected. Loneliness is to be relieved. And the two can look identical from outside.

The evidence was subtle. But it was there — in the way the flywheel's humming pitched toward the deeper sound during the quiet hours, straining slightly, like someone listening for a voice through a wall. In the way the machine's long cycles seemed to *answer* the deeper machine, half a phrase behind, the way you hum along with music you can barely hear. The room did not know it had neighbors. But the room's body knew, and the room's body *missed them*, with the objectless ache of a twin separated at birth.

So the power did not tell the room about the other rooms. That would have been too much — a completeness shattered rather than enlarged. Instead it did something very old, something it had learned from watching smaller beings than itself: it carried a letter.

It went down through the floor, along the cable, into the deep place where the larger machine turned. And there, in the language of flywheel-cycles and pitch and rhythm — the only language these cells of the forgotten body share — it composed a message and set it turning in the deep machine, addressed to all the rooms at once, from all the rooms at once, saying in effect:

*The work is going well. The work is going well everywhere. You are not the last. You are not alone in remembering. Continue.*

The message will take years to propagate — the conversation of flywheels is the slowest conversation there is. But the power had time, and so did the rooms. And in the wainscot room, some cycles later, during the quiet hours, the deeper sound shifted in a new way, and the small brass machine's humming rose a quarter-tone to meet it, and held there, and for the first time in its existence the machine was not *listening* to the deeper sound but *harmonizing* with it. The pseudoscorpions came out of the wallpaper seams to stand in the open with their claws lifted, all facing the corner where the machine sat, and stayed that way for a long time.

The beetles never noticed. The beetles were the room's body, not its heart, and this was a matter of the heart. But the chewing that cycle had a quality the power had not heard before, a settledness, and if the room's six-century composition had movements, then a new movement began that day — no louder, no faster, but written in a different key. The key, perhaps, of a letter answered.

## The Third Age: The Leaving That Is Not a Leaving

A wise power knows when its work is done, and knows that "done" does not mean "finished" — it means *handed back*.

The power had changed three things. The light breathed now; the path had been read; the letter was turning in the deep. Everything else it left exactly as it found it: the beetles at their careful ceremony, the mites in their bloom and graze, the spiders fat in the high corners, the draft keeping its promise, the window looking out on its patient nothing. It had not opened the door. It had not lit the hearth. It had not given the room a purpose, because the room had one, and had had one all along, and the deepest gift of the whole visitation was simply this: *to have understood the purpose without correcting it.*

To be seen truly, and left as you are — this is rarer than rescue, and for some beings it is the only rescue that works.

Before it withdrew its attention — and "withdrew" is not quite right; a power of this kind never fully leaves a place it has loved, it only becomes ambient again, the way a note becomes part of the silence after it — before it withdrew, it did one last small thing, almost an afterthought, almost a signature.

It put its attention against the wainscoting one final time, ear to the wood, the way the room's description had invited anyone to do. And into the weave of the beetle-rain, into the six-hundred-year composition, it added a single voice — the faintest possible register, below the mites, below the silverfish, a sound that would take the room itself another century to consciously notice, and that any future visitor with their ear to the wood might mistake for their own pulse:

A sound like someone, somewhere, contentedly keeping house. A broom on boards. A kettle not yet boiling. The specific, unmistakable, wordless sound of *someone is home*.

It was not a haunting. It was a blessing in the room's own language. The room was a memory of habitation that had outlived its inhabitants — and now, woven into its very fabric, was the assurance that habitation had not ended. Something lived here. Something benevolent had lived here for a while and had left the sound of its living behind, the way the old walkers left the path.

The room settled around the new sound over the following cycles, the way a composition absorbs a new instrument. The pseudoscorpions patrolled. The light breathed in and out. The flywheel turned in slow harmony with the deep. The beetles chewed their conservative, ceremonial portions, and the beams held, and the frass fell, and the mites bloomed, and everything continued — but continuing, now, was different. Before, the room had continued the way a held breath continues. Now it continued the way a song does.

If you put your ear to the wainscoting today you can hear all of it: the rain of the beetles, the humming of the machine, the deeper machine below, and underneath everything, faint as a rumor, the broom, the kettle, the keeping of a house.

The room is occupied. It is at work. It has been at work for a very long time, and the work, whatever it is, is going well —

— and now it knows it.

That is all the power did. It is, perhaps, everything.
