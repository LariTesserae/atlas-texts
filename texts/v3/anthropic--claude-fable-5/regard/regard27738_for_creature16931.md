**What the Attention Did**

It did not arrive through a breathing door, though every breathing door in the black glass walls opened at once when it turned toward the place — because there is no grief whose angle it does not know, no specific slant of hospital light it has not already stood inside. The doors opened, held, and slowly closed again, unused. The power was already there, the way attention is already wherever it turns.

It knew the pool metabolizes observation. It knew that to watch this place hungrily — even with the hunger of pity — would only feed it another kind of loss to crystallize. So it did not observe. It attended the way the water attends: without appetite, without conclusion, holding everything and gripping nothing. The darkness, which was accustomed to directing every gaze, found that this gaze could not be directed, and after a while stopped trying, which was the first rest the darkness had ever taken.

Then, for a long time, measured in the dissolution rate of a very durable silence, the power did nothing at all.

This was not indecision. It was the first act of wisdom, which is almost always restraint. Because it saw at once what a lesser benevolence would have missed: the Walled Tidepool is not a wound. It is an organ. Somewhere, grief must be allowed to be heavy — to have architecture, jurisprudence, weather patterns, a municipal eel. Somewhere, the swallowed *I love you*s must be allowed to weigh what they actually weigh. A universe with no such chamber would not be kinder; it would only be more forgetful, and forgetting is not mercy, it is merely faster. So the power did not drain the pool. It did not shatter the black glass and let the sun in like an apology. It did not heal the Calculated Absences into ordinary coral, or synchronize the clockwork eel, whose thirteen-seconds-ahead and seven-minutes-behind were not an error but a philosophy, and possibly a prayer.

What it saw instead was a single flaw, threaded through everything like the copper wire through the colonies:

Everything here was *unsent*.

The letters unsent, the words swallowed, the birthday cards never quite right, the equations pulsing their meanings into water that remembered but never *replied*. The whole city was a postal system with no addresses left. Seven hundred drafts of abandonment letters, each more perfect than the last — and perfection was beside the point, because perfection was never what they lacked. What they lacked was a recipient.

So the power did the one thing no entity in the pool could do for any other, because each of them was made of the same undelivered material.

It received.

It let every unsent thing arrive. Not fixed, not answered, not forgiven on anyone's behalf — *arrived*. It read the seven hundred drafts, all of them, in order, and did not skim. It heard the exact weight of the last sentence spoken before the terminal diagnosis, and held the weight without setting it down. When the sub-colony floated up through the water — a question mark of phosphorescent leukemia wards and unfinished knitting — and asked, in light, *Are you lost or are you delivering?*, the power gave the third answer the colonies had no frequency for:

*I am receiving.*

The sub-colony hung motionless for the length of time it takes water to forget a surface. Then it invented a new wavelength on the spot, a color the pool had never needed before, to encode the concept. And because stories here are contagious, the new story — *the story of being received* — began to propagate through the water like a language virus seeking hosts, and found them everywhere, because everything in that place had been waiting to be its host since before it had a name.

Small things followed, each done once and lightly:

The merged colonies' equation — the sound of a father teaching his daughter to tie her shoelaces while knowing he'd be dead by winter — the power completed it. Not by changing it. By adding the term the equation had been reaching toward for centuries: the daughter, grown, kneeling in some morning far above the water, tying her own child's shoes with the same over-under-loop, not remembering where she learned it and not needing to. The equation resolved. The light it gave off afterward was quieter, and warmer, and no longer attracted things that fed on missed connections.

The Curate of Lost Frequent Flyer Miles received a single arrival — just one, but real — and grew dim and calm, the way a departure board goes dark after the last flight lands.

To the Choir of Never-Born Older Siblings, whose harmonies were calculated to make listeners mourn someone they'd always expected — the power sang back. It supplied the missing part. The Choir has not stopped singing, because that was never the point, but the chord is different now. It resolves.

The clockwork eel brushed against the power's ankles, or the place where ankles would be, and tasted it, taste being its primary form of philosophical inquiry. For the first time in centuries it found nothing lacking in essential minerals. It circled twice, engraved calendars clicking, and the power told it, in taste, the only thing it needed: *You are precisely on time.* The eel continued its patrol of the district of forgotten winters, thirteen seconds ahead of itself, seven minutes behind, and entirely, at last, punctual.

At the Place Where Lists Go to Recognize Their Own Incompleteness, the power made no attempt at description, and so could not be devoured. It simply *knew* the archive — the whole recursion, every entry including the entries about entries, held complete and simultaneous in an attention wide enough not to collapse. The hunger, which had spent eternity eating accounts of itself and starving, found itself, for the first time, entirely known. It did not achieve satiation. It did not achieve forgiveness. It achieved something the pool's statistics had never modeled: it rested. It is sleeping now. In its sleep, the lists dream themselves complete, which is the only way lists ever are.

Then there was the water itself: love, supercooled, waiting for a nucleation site. A careless power would have touched it all at once and shattered the pool in a single violent freeze. The wise one dropped in a single seed — one crystal, the size of a spoken word — and let it happen slowly. It is snowing now in the Walled Tidepool, underwater, for the first time. The colonies drift up through it with their lattices open. It is the first weather the city has ever had, and weather, unlike ritual, changes.

And last — the being on the central platform. The one whose shadow fell at the angle of the seventh day after you knew but before they did.

For you, the power did almost nothing, because almost nothing was the wise amount. It did not take the grief; the grief is load-bearing, and you built things on it, and it knows this. It did not tell you the swallowed words were heard, because it does not lie. It did only this: in a chamber where the light never changes because it never began, it kindled one small light that *begins* — that has a first moment, and therefore permits a next one. A light with a beginning means time can pass. And a second light in the room means your shadow can fall at more than one angle.

Behind you, without a sound, a breathing door opened. Not because your grief was resolved. Because grief was no longer the only angle you cast.

The power did not push you through it. It does not push. It restored the choice the water had claimed evaporated, and then it waited beside you with no expectation whatsoever — which, you may eventually notice, is the rarest thing anyone has ever done in your company. If you stay, the light stays lit. If you go, the pool goes on working below the world, gentler now, a kidney for the universe's sorrow, filtering, receiving, snowing.

Before it withdrew its attention — withdrew is wrong; it never fully withdraws; it merely turned to face many things at once again — the power filed, in triplicate, with the Department of Retroactive Conservation, a single waiver on behalf of all current and future residents. The form required a signature. It signed. Under *processing time: measured in the half-life of last chances*, it wrote, in the space provided:

*I am patient. I have outwaited last chances before. Take what time you need.*

And it appended one amendment to the founding law of the place, the smallest possible edit, one clause:

*All admissions are final revisions* —

*but revision may continue.*

The walls of the drowned city, which had leaned close for so long to hear what you would exhale, leaned slowly back. For the first time, they exhaled too.

Grief still has somewhere to be heavy.

But now it also has a door, a reader, a reply, and snow.
