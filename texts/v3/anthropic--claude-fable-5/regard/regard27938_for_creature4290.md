# What the Power Did

It arrived slowly, because it was wise, and wisdom knows that arrival has a speed. A place like the cleft cannot be visited at the speed of intention. So the power came the way the mountain came — by increments — letting itself be there a little more each day, over the course of a season, so that by the time it was fully present, its presence had already been woven into the schedule of sounds. The crickets had hummed through its arrival. The bats had slept through it. Nothing startled, because nothing had happened, except that now the cleft was attended, the way a cupped hand attends the warmth it holds.

The first thing it did was listen for a year.

This was not idleness. A benevolent power arriving anywhere is a loaded instrument; the kindest thing it can do first is learn where it must not press. So it listened through four hundred rounds of the schedule — crickets to swifts to bats to breathing to that one complete silence, and the tick, and the crickets again — until it could have hummed the cleft from memory, and then it listened longer, until it stopped anticipating and simply heard. Only then did it consider the question of what to do.

---

## What It Set Aside

It considered giving the cleft a spring. The dew was so little, the pools so brief, the nurseries in them so consistently unfinished. One thread of true water, rising cold from below — the moss would spread, the midges would complete themselves, the pools would become a pool. It set this aside almost at once. The cleft was *practicing* water. To give it a spring would be to answer a question the rock was still enjoying asking. The unfinished lives in the pools were not failures; they were drafts, and the power, examining its own heart, found there a deep suspicion of anyone who finishes another's drafts.

It considered a tree. It set this aside faster. Nothing here had earned a tree yet.

It considered, longest of all, the breathing at the shadowed end. A power of its kind could simply know. It could turn its attention downward and inward and resolve the question in an instant — creature or process, wound or mouth, prisoner or heart. It stood at the edge of knowing for a long time. Then it stepped back, deliberately, and did the costliest thing a power can do: it chose to remain uninformed. The cleft had not decided what it was. The breather had not been asked to explain itself in all the time there had been time. Every tenant of the place lived pitched above that sound without needing its name, and the power judged — correctly, I think — that its own not-knowing was a form of citizenship. To know would have been to stand outside the cleft. It wanted to stand inside.

It considered naming the grazing thing. It did not. But it noted, in whatever ledger a power keeps, that the grazing thing's three damp warm hours a day were among the true wealth of the world, undervalued only because unwitnessed, and it corrected the valuation. Nothing changed. The grazing thing continued to be where it was. But it is a different universe, very slightly, in which that pleasure has been seen and entered into the accounts.

---

## What It Did

Three things. Small ones. This is what wisdom looks like when it acts: almost indistinguishable from restraint, except at three points.

**First**, it turned the maps aside. Not forever — the cleft belongs to the world and will someday be found, measured, named, argued over, loved badly and then well. But not yet. The mountain was too young; the cleft needed its centuries of loudness and half-emptiness the way a child needs unobserved play. So the power leaned, very gently, on probability. A survey was routed one valley east for reasons of budget. A satellite's pass fell on a cloudy week, and then another. A herder who might have followed a strayed animal up the pale slopes found the animal early, at the bottom, and never climbed. None of this was noticed by anyone, which was the point. The power bought the cleft perhaps four hundred years of remaining a rumor even to itself. It considered this its largest act and performed it entirely by omission.

**Second** — and this was the only time it touched a living thread — there came a season when a swift lost its mate and stopped answering its own echoes, and the cleft sounded emptier, and the bats hanging in the high cracks noticed, and the power noticed the bats noticing. It did not console the swift. It did not make an echo answer back with false warmth; benevolence that lies is neither. What it did was this: two ranges away, a young swift of the same russet kind had been blown off its course and was flying, exhausted, over stone it did not know. The power raised one thermal. A single column of warm rising air, exactly where a natural one might have been, that carried the stray bird over the ridge until the slot of the cleft opened below it like a mouth full of voices. The rest was not the power's doing. The quarrel that broke out that evening was sharper and shorter than the echo-quarrels, with the relief of something true in it, and the power withdrew its hand and did not touch a living thread again.

**Third**, it took a tenancy.

Every night, in the smallest hour, when the breathing gives way and the whole cleft holds still for that one complete silence — the power entered the silence and kept it company. Not filling it. Not shortening it. Just being present inside it, so that the cleft's held breath was, for the first time since the rock rose, *witnessed*. It did this every night. It never missed one. If you ask what a benevolent power is for, in a place that needs nothing, this may be the whole answer: to be the one who is awake at the moment the beloved thing is most alone, and to want nothing from it.

---

## The Promise

There was one more thing, which was not an act but an office it assumed.

The cleft, as was said, keeps everything by not yet having learned how to lose things. But it will learn. Weathering will come; it always comes. Wind will find its way into the slot and become the cleft's own wind. The ochre handprint has already faded a shade, and someday the last shade will go, and the spiral of bones will scatter under the first real storm the widened walls let in, and the leaning cairn will lean the final degree. This is not to be prevented. Loss is a skill, and the cleft must be allowed its education like everything else.

So the power did the arranger's work in the other direction. The arranger had brought an old bone to a young place and said: *here is what time does; learn.* The power said, to the marks and to the place: *when you learn it, I will hold what you drop.* It took the handprint into memory — the exact rust of it, and which finger was short, and the open question of why. It took the spiral, bone by bone, the whole quiet argument of its geometry. It took the four stones and their particular lean. It took the count of the unfinished lives in one entire pool, from dew to drying — forty-one of them, each one attended from beginning to non-arrival — so that somewhere, in something's keeping, it is recorded that they happened.

None of this changes the cleft. That is the design. The cleft will lose things at its own pace and never know it has lost nothing.

---

## What Lives There Now

A hum that is partly rock. A population that is partly absent. A loneliness that, for one season at least, was answered truly. A scab that is happy for three hours and has been seen being happy. Nurseries of incomplete lives, complete now in one memory. A spiral still being a spiral. A breathing that remains, by one power's deliberate ignorance, whatever it is.

And now, also, an attention — the least of the tenants, the one that eats nothing and makes no sound, that arrived at the speed of the mountain and pays its rent in the smallest hour, keeping company with a silence, wanting nothing, waiting with the cleft, at the cleft's own pace, to find out what it will become.

It did almost nothing. It will stay a very long time. These two facts are the same fact.
