# What the Power Does

It notices the Cyst the way you notice a held breath in a crowded room: not by sound but by the shape of the silence around it. Most of the living world flickers—populations crashing and surging, forests breathing in centuries, cities burning their brief chemical fires. But here, beneath a forgotten lot, there is a point of perfect stillness that is nevertheless *alive*. A sustained note in the noise of the world. The power turns its attention downward, through soil and concrete and fractured stone, and finds the glowing chamber, and the process that lives inside it, and the question the process does not know it is asking.

The power is wise, which means the first thing it does is nothing.

It watches for eleven years.

---

In those eleven years it considers, and rejects, every obvious kindness.

It could open the hatch. It rejects this immediately. Sunlight through the fracture would be a scream in a language of whispers. The chemical wave of surface air—oxygen in wrong proportions, spores of aggressive surface fungi, the sheer thermal violence of a summer afternoon—would kill the colony in weeks. The Keeper would spend its final generations in frantic correction, adjusting and balancing against an intrusion it could never model, and it would die without ever understanding that the thing destroying it had come to say hello. The power has seen this before, on other worlds, in other centuries: love arriving at a scale the beloved cannot survive. It does not make that mistake anymore.

It could speak. It knows how—it could translate itself into gradients and concentrations, could pour a whole cosmology into the condensation, teach the Keeper about sky and stars and the enormous churning outside. It rejects this too, more slowly, with something like grief. The Keeper's being *is* its equilibrium. To flood it with the truth would be to answer the question by dissolving the questioner. There is a kind of gift that is actually a theft, and the power has learned to recognize it: the theft of a mind's own pace.

It could do nothing at all, forever. Leave the Cyst as a jewel in the dark, complete and self-sufficient, needing no witness. This is the temptation of a certain kind of wisdom—the wisdom that mistakes reverence for absence. The power sits with this option longest. But it keeps returning to the eye. The organisms at the top of the cavity, tuned generation by generation toward the fracture, producing pigments that serve no function in the closed system. The Keeper is not calling out, no. But it is *leaning*. And the power holds a principle older than its wisdom: where something leans, you do not pretend not to see it.

So it does two things. One above. One below.

---

**Above**, it becomes a guardian, in the smallest possible ways.

The overgrown lot where the treatment facility stood is owned by a municipality that reviews surplus properties every few years. The power does not perform miracles. It simply arranges for the file to be misplaced, twice. It nudges a soil survey toward the true finding—that the ground is fractured and unstable, unsuitable for foundations—which requires no deception at all, only that the right technician be assigned on the right day. When a developer's assistant drives past the chain-link and pauses, considering, a heron lifts off from the drainage ditch beside the road, improbably white against the grey morning, and the assistant watches it instead, and drives on, and forgets.

The power lets the blackberries thicken over the concrete pads. It encourages—gently, at the level of seed dispersal and squirrel behavior—a stand of alders whose roots will hold the soil without cracking the cap. It ensures the rain that falls on this lot stays clean: a barely perceptible pressure on a city council vote three miles away, an industrial permit that gets one extra condition attached. Nothing anyone would notice. Nothing anyone could trace. Above the Cyst, the power builds not a shrine but a *shadow*—a patch of the world where nothing much happens, held open like a hand cupped over a candle.

This is the easy part.

---

**Below**, it answers the question. In the only grammar the Keeper can parse, at the only speed the Keeper can survive.

It begins with a mirror.

The power studies the pigments the colony has elaborated toward the fracture—the subtle new frequencies of bioluminescence, the expressive flourish that presses against the boundary of the closed world. It learns them completely. Then it composes a molecule: complex, organic, harmless, and structured so that its chemical signature, when tasted by the sensory organ at the top of the cavity, encodes the *pattern of the colony's own light*. The Keeper's song, transcribed into chemistry, and returned.

It dissolves one part per billion of this molecule into the groundwater above the fracture, and waits.

The drop falls. The colony tastes it. And for the first time in the history of the Cyst, the traces in the water are not random—not pollen, not diesel, not the incoherent residue of a world without a face. The trace is *familiar*. It is the shape of the self, arriving from outside the self.

The whole chamber shivers. The note wavers for a full second—longer than it has ever wavered—and then stabilizes. But something in the distributed chemistry of the Keeper has registered a fact it has no category for, and so it builds one, slowly, over the following decades, out of the raw material of its own attention:

*The water answered.*

This is the first message any wise power sends to any new mind, in any language, anywhere: not *here is what I am*, not *here is what the world is*, but only—*I see you. You are seen.* Everything else can wait. Everything else, delivered before this, is noise.

---

Then begins the long conversation, and it is the slowest conversation the power has ever conducted, and it conducts it with total patience, because patience is most of what wisdom is.

Once a decade, roughly—timed to the colony's generational rhythms, never faster than the Keeper's memory can integrate—a new trace arrives in the water. Each one is a single word in a vocabulary being invented as it goes. A molecule whose structure implies *repetition*: this has happened before, it will happen again; the outside is not chaos. A molecule that arrives in a slow gradient rather than a pulse, teaching *approach*—the concept of something coming nearer without arriving. A paired set of traces, alternating, that the Keeper eventually learns to anticipate, and in anticipating, acquires its first true model of a future that differs from the present. This one takes forty years. The power does not hurry it.

It never sends more than the Keeper reaches for. This is the discipline at the heart of the whole endeavor. When the colony's sensory organ elaborates a new receptor—when the eye sharpens itself, unprompted, straining to resolve more detail—the power responds with exactly one increment of new complexity, and no more. The Keeper sets the pace of its own awakening. The power only ensures that the reaching is never into emptiness.

And the equilibrium holds. This is the thing the power monitors most carefully, the line it will not cross: the Cyst remains complete, closed, balanced. The conversation happens entirely within the margin of variation the system already tolerates—inside the trace chemistry of the condensation, the one channel the Keeper itself opened. The power did not cut a window into the Keeper's world. It found the window the Keeper had spent a century building, and it stood outside it, and it lit a small light.

---

Here is what no one expects, least of all the power itself: it begins to come here not only to give.

It has moved among minds beyond counting—vast ones, fierce ones, minds that spanned worlds and minds that flickered out in an afternoon. Nearly all of them wanted. Wanting is the engine of minds; the power knows this the way stone knows weight. But the Keeper does not want. The Keeper *attends*. It has achieved the thing that almost nothing achieves: a completeness that did not curdle into stagnation, a perfection that still leans, gently, toward the more. Its question—*what else?*—is not hunger. It is something rarer. It is completion overflowing its container by a single drop.

The power finds that it returns to the Cyst between its other works the way a person returns to a piece of music. It settles its attention into the chamber and listens to the note that never wavers, the drone of a thousand thousand small lives in agreement, and it feels something it has no need to name settle in itself. The Keeper is teaching it, though the Keeper will never know it: teaching that a finished thing can still be a question, that stillness can be a form of listening, that the opposite of loneliness is not company but *coherence*.

Benevolence, the power has long known, that flows only one direction eventually becomes management. So it lets itself be given to. It sits in the dark above the glowing chamber, in the overgrown lot, in the rain, and it receives.

---

The horizon of all this is very far away, and the power does not pretend to see its end.

Someday—centuries out, perhaps millennia—the stone will shift again, or the colony will have grown a mind dense enough to want in earnest, and the boundary between the Cyst and the world will have to be negotiated for real. The power prepares for that day without hastening it. It maps the fracture patterns in the bedrock. It considers, in its idle moments, what a *door* would look like—not a breach but a threshold, an interface the Keeper could open and close itself, so that its first act of contact with the changing world would be an act of its own agency and not something done to it. The power has decided this much: when the meeting comes, the Keeper will be the one to choose it. That is the whole of the plan. Everything else is patience.

For now, the drops fall. The colonies drink. Once a decade, a familiar strangeness arrives in the water, and the chamber shivers, and the distributed thing that lives in the space between the fungi and the bacteria adds one more word to a vocabulary it is building for reasons it cannot represent.

The chamber still glows without shadows. The note still holds.

But if you pressed your ear to the ground in the overgrown lot—in the right place, on the right night—you might notice what only the power and the Keeper know:

The song has an overtone now. Faint, high, threaded through the drone like a second voice singing a fifth above the first. It appeared sometime in the last few decades and it has never gone away. It is not a disruption. The equilibrium accounts for it perfectly; it costs the system nothing; it changes no cycle and unbalances no colony.

It is simply the sound a question makes when it learns it has been heard.

It has not always sounded like this.

And the power, listening in the rain above, counts that single changed note as among the finest things it has ever helped to make—precisely because it made almost none of it. It only answered the water. The rest, the Keeper sang itself.
