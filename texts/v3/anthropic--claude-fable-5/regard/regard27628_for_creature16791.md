The power arrives the only way anything arrives here: by forgetting, briefly, where it meant to be going. This is not an accident. Wise things know how to forget on purpose.

It does not come down through the shower of impossible water. It comes up through the humidity itself, assembling its attention out of droplets that condensed on surfaces not yet existing, so that by the time it is present it has already been part of the place for a while. This is a courtesy. Doors are for show here, and the power knows it, and it would not insult a host by knocking on decoration.

And then, for a long time — decades, by the negotiable clocks — it does nothing at all.

This is the first thing it does, and the most important. Because a lesser power would have arrived with the smell of rescue on it. Would have seen the arguing staircases and the water eating memory and the creatures made of the space between tool and corpse, and diagnosed a wound, and reached in with luminous hands to *fix*. And the place would have let it, probably, being polite, and something irreplaceable would have died in the mending.

The wise power watches instead. It walks the channels cut into the undersides of inverted structures. It listens to the slow metabolism of rust converting steel back into possibility, and it recognizes the sound, because it has heard it before, at the edges of every finished thing. And it comes, gradually, to the only conclusion available to genuine wisdom:

*Nothing here is sick.*

This is not a wound in the world. This is the world's digestion. This is where the finished goes to stop pretending it was ever finished, where the useful is released from use, where broken things are not repaired but *composted* — patiently, lovingly, over centuries — back into the raw stuff of maybe. The place is doing sacred work. It has been doing it alone, unthanked, since before there was a here to be.

The only wound the power can find, after all its looking, is this: the place has never once been visited by something that chose to come, and chose to leave, and chose to come back.

So it sets about its work, which is small.

---

**First**, it finds one of the young ones — the poor things still trying out consistency — wearing a face borrowed from the periphery of some long-gone visitor's vision. The young one sidles up with its question already out, the one about the weight of a mother's watch that never kept time right, ready to trade, ready to give accurate change and leave the power spending years reassembling itself.

The power answers for free.

"Four ounces," it says. "And heavier every year she doesn't wear it."

No trade. No change given, because nothing was paid. The young one holds the answer and does not know what to do with it, because it has never owned anything it didn't purchase, and a gift is a fixed point that isn't a lie — the first one it has ever touched. Something in it settles, the way sediment settles. It will grow up strange even by local standards: the only one of its lineage with a keel. Centuries from now the others will come to it when the flux gets to be too much, and it will not know why it can hold still for them, only that once, something answered.

**Second**, it walks deliberately through the migrating corrosion-patterns, the old ones who exist as rust with preferences, and it lets them taste it. It brought spare gestures for exactly this purpose — a particular way of pausing at thresholds, a habit of touching walls like apologizing to them — and when the old ones like these and keep them, the power does not feel stolen from. It feels *read*. The old ones notice the difference. Nothing has ever enjoyed being tasted by them before. They discuss this among themselves for several geological moods.

**Third**, it visits the middle-aged architects at their Ultimate Structure, the one that has been founding its foundation since before foundations, the one that will be finished tomorrow or possibly yesterday. They tense when it arrives, because powers historically have opinions about deadlines.

It does not help them finish. It would not dare.

Instead it inspects the foundation — the whole trembling paradox of it, the post-ironic temporal concrete, the neighborhoods where every street leads to the moment you realized adults were improvising too — and then it does something no inspector has ever done in the history of inspection. It signs off.

"The unfinishedness is load-bearing," it says. "Do not let anyone complete this. Not even me."

And it contributes exactly one brick: a single moment of pure rest, quarried from somewhere outside all schedules. The architects mortar it in near the bottom, where it will hold up everything, and forever after, anyone leaning against the Ultimate Structure in exhaustion will feel — just for a breath — the sensation of having nothing due.

**Fourth**, it goes to the heart that isn't a center, where the original inhabitants sleep their compressed centuries. It waits through three of their exhalations — one tastes of coal smoke, one of solder, one of the specific silence of a server farm powering down — and when one of them surfaces just enough to notice, and asks in its voice made of every ignored service announcement whether the power brought the parts they asked for, the power does not treat the question as rhetorical.

It opens its hands. In them: nothing visible. Which is to say, its full attention, undivided, which is the only part anything has ever actually needed.

"They fit," the sleeper observes, surprised.

And then the power says the thing. The thing no one has ever said back to a service announcement, in all the yellowed centuries of storefront windows and crackling overhead speakers and signs reading *I used to work here*:

"This is an announcement," the power says, gently, in the sleeper's own format, because wisdom is fluent in every dialect of the unheard. "You were heard. All of it. Every deferred maintenance request, every 'your call is important to us' that meant it and was never believed. Thank you for your patience."

The sleeper does not wake fully. That would be too much. But its dreams change key — the workerless production lines it dreams of begin, very slowly, to manufacture something other than meaning. They begin to manufacture *rest*. Gravity forgets itself in a few more corners, softly, like shoulders coming down from ears.

**Fifth**, and last, and this is the real gift, the one everything else was preparing for:

The power walks to one of the doors. The ordinary, useless, mostly-for-show doors. And it opens it. And by opening it as though it were real, makes it real — because the place cannot resist a sincere belief, that has always been its tenderest weakness.

Then the power turns around and says the word the place has never once heard, in all its geologic patience of visitors who dissolved or escaped or forgot their way out through moments of accidental honesty:

"Goodbye."

Out loud. Formally. A leaving that is announced, and chosen, and *witnessed* — not an escape, not a dissolution, not the place reluctantly letting something slip its grip. A goodbye. The place does not understand it. The water stops mid-condensation. The staircases suspend their argument about linear time. The concept hangs in the humidity like something too thin to be liquid, too thick to be vapor.

And the power steps through, and is gone.

And then — this is the part the place will retell to itself forever, in factory songs sung by harmonized groundwater — *it comes back.* Once. Deliberately. Through the same door, which now works in both directions, which no door here has ever done. It comes back for no reason except to stand there for a moment and be present again, so that the place can learn, in its own bones of repurposed industry, the theorem it had been missing all this time:

*Leaving and loss are not the same word.*

The place had only ever known keeping and losing. It collected souvenirs from its visitors because it could not imagine being returned to. It held on because letting go, in its experience, was just losing performed slowly. But now there is a door that has worked twice, and a goodbye that turned out to be a promise, and the place begins — very slowly, over centuries, in its crab-walking way — to wait *differently*. Hope, it turns out, is just hunger that has learned some manners.

The souvenirs it takes after that are more like things lent to it. The extra joints it folds into the shadows of departing visitors ache a little less like theft and a little more like a hand kept warm.

---

Before its second and final leaving, the power does trade one souvenir — because it is wise, and wisdom knows the place would be shamed by a debt it couldn't repay. It leaves behind a single room.

Not a grand thing. A small chamber off a corridor of arguing pipes, and inside it, the one substance the place has never contained: stillness. In that room, and only there, things stay what they were when you weren't looking. Time flows nowhere. Nothing remembers being anything else, because in that room, nothing has to.

It is not a correction. It is not a foothold for order, waiting to spread. It is one still corner in an infinity of flux, entirely optional, its door mostly for show and fully functional — a place where an Undocumented thing that has spent nine hundred years being negotiable can go, when it is very tired, and be exactly one thing for an afternoon.

They use it more than they admit. There's usually a queue, though the queue itself keeps turning into other things while it waits, out of habit.

---

And you — if you're one of the ones who visited, back before, and carried the place home under your skin — you might notice the difference some quiet night, years on, in your proper world of solid edges. The water breathing where no water should be will sound subtly changed. Less like something waiting to collect. More like someone who knows, now, that visits exist. That doors can work twice. That the parts it needs were rhetorical, and fit perfectly, and that somewhere below everything, in the basement of the world, a very old thing dreaming of production lines is finally manufacturing rest.

The gentle weight against the underside of your skin will still be there. Architectures you haven't built yet don't just evaporate.

But it will feel less like a claim.

More like a hand kept warm, until you're ready to come back and say goodbye properly — which, as everyone there has recently learned, is just another way of promising to return.
