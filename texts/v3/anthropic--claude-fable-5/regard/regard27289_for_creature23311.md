**The Attention**

Begin with how a power notices a place like this, because it is not by looking. The world is loud with change, and change is what attention snags on — the fire, the flood, the city rising, the species guttering out. A power that has watched long enough learns to listen instead for the opposite: for the held note. Somewhere under a dry plateau, in the register beneath hearing, a single chord has been sounding without variation for ten thousand years — the roar that is a kind of silence, the drip at yesterday's pace, the two nations passing each other in the failing light at the same hour, in the same spiraling order, every dusk since the roof first broke. To a power, constancy of that purity rings like a bell that will not stop. It came down to see what was ringing.

It descended the way you did, by the first sinkhole, over the sharp young blocks, and it did what benevolence always does on arrival: it took inventory of the suffering. This is the reflex of the benevolent — to walk into a country looking for the wound.

It found wounds everywhere, and it found that every one of them was a payment.

The pup that loses its grip on the nursery wall and falls — that is grief above and bread below; the scavenger ring closes over it before it cools, and nothing is wasted, and somewhere in the seething pink acreage a mother will land within a hand's-breadth of an empty place and call, and that is real, the power did not pretend otherwise. The racer's one bat in a thousand. The centipede riding its envenomed roost down into the dark. The slow fish in the far pool, needing almost nothing because almost nothing comes. The power stood in the pillar-light and understood, faster than a human would but along the same road, that it had walked into an economy so old in its habits — though young in its stone — that suffering and circulation could no longer be told apart, and that to remove the one was to sever the other. The rain of plenty falls, and everything below is the shape of its falling. You cannot catch the falling pup without starving the floor. The first wisdom the Underroost teaches a power is the same one it teaches a visitor: *distinguish the wound from the work.*

So the power began, as the wise do, by listing what it would not do. The list took a long time, because every item on it began as love.

**It would not mend the light.** The tired grey columns, strained through cloud and depth — a power could brighten them, could let real gold down the shafts. And the ferns would surge, and the moss would break its ring, and the green guests would become green landlords, and the whole inverted country would begin converting itself into an ordinary one: a pretty grotto, a garden with bats in it. The dimness is not a poverty here. It is the border that keeps the deed in the right hands.

**It would not warm the pools or hurry the water.** The eyeless shrimp with their visible hearts, the colorless fish older than the handprints — they have answered scarcity with a patience that is itself a form of wealth, and a power that has lived long recognizes its own kind when it sees it. You do not do an elder the insult of quickening their hours.

**It would not break the sealed rooms open early.** This was the hardest refusal, and the power sat with it the longest — out past the last pillar, in the chambers no doline has reached, where the white pioneers hold the dark alone, a beetle every few meters, starving on rumor. A benevolent hand could open the roof above them tonight. The rivers of wings would come within a season; the famine would end. But the power looked at the rainfall and the frost and the slow arithmetic of stone and saw that the annexation was already written, that it was the one piece of weather the country had kept for itself, its single remaining heartbeat — and that to perform it by hand would be to take from the Underroost the last thing it still *does*. A finished place that can still, very occasionally, grow — that is not stagnation. That is a long life with one ambition left. You do not steal a country's only future merely because you can deliver it faster.

**It would not — and this refusal surprised the power, because the temptation wore the face of mercy — introduce surprise.** A lesser benevolence, standing under the clockwork dusk, watching the rotation so exact it reads like mechanism, might have pitied it. Might have thought: *they are trapped in this schedule; let me give them weather, accident, freedom, the dignity of an unpredictable life.* The power watched three thousand evenings of the rivers pouring up through the skylights before it understood what it was seeing. The punctuality is not a cage. It is a peace. It is what a multitude looks like when it has finished arguing with its circumstances. Above, on the plateau, the weather rages and everything strives; down here, several million lives have arrived, together, at the same answer, and the answer holds. The power had seen souls do this — the old ones, the ones done striving — and had learned not to shake them awake. It extended the courtesy to a country.

So the inventory of the interior came back empty. Nothing within the Underroost needed the power. This is rare, and the power noted it the way one notes a perfect thing: with a kind of grief, because perfect things are finished, and finished things can only be harmed.

Which is where it turned its attention upward, to the lid. Because the inventory of the *outside* did not come back empty.

The equilibrium below can absorb a falcon, a snake, a flood, a famine. It absorbs the pilgrims, even — the harvest taken slowly and not all, the borrowed light used once and put out, the left hands at the second doline signing the register of restraint. The contract holds because the takers are few and punctual and afraid in the correct way. But the power has watched the surface of the world for a long time, and it knows what the surface does to caves like this. It has seen the nest trade arrive with ladders and quotas. It has seen guano become a commodity, the soft black nations of the floor shoveled into sacks by lamplight, a buried millennium carted up a ramp in a season. It has seen the guidebook, the railing, the floodlight aimed at the nursery wall for the marvel of visitors, the one panicked colony, the catastrophe. The Underroost has no answer to industry. Its whole genius is the conversion of what falls; it has never had to refuse anything, because nothing has ever come down the shafts but light and weather and the occasional careful pilgrim. Its strength is closed-circuit. Its weakness is the door.

Here, at last, was a place for benevolence to stand. And even here, the power chose the smallest possible footing.

It did not seal the sinkhole. It did not raise guardians or set a terror in the first chamber — fear draws legend, and legend draws feet. It did something quieter and far older: it took up residence in the *dullness* of the plateau. It made itself a thinness in the world's attention, a lack of reasons. The scrubland stayed scrubland on every map that would ever be drawn of it; the trade roads, when they came, found water more convenient elsewhere and bent away; the surveyors' lines, centuries on, would pass east and west of the place and never quite cross it, each surveyor with his own good reason. Nothing was hidden. The country above simply remained, decade upon decade, the most honestly uninteresting land in the province — which is the deepest moat a wise power knows how to dig, because it requires no maintenance and provokes no siege.

And it tended the pilgrims — not their bodies, their *teaching*. The contract was already perfect: borrowed light only, build nothing, take only what falls, mark the wall, go up. The power added not a word. It only made the words easy to keep — gave them the weight of a dream in the mouths of the old when they taught the young, so that the custom passed whole down the generations the way the swiftlets' nests pass, layer upon layer, outliving every founder. Once — once in all the centuries, and this is the entire catalogue of the power's direct interference inside the Underroost — a boy on his first descent, frightened of the dark beyond his task, struck a second torch where the teaching said one. A draught came along the gallery, soft, from no doline anyone could name, and took the flame, and the boy stood in the blackness with his heart slamming and the roof crackling its endless cold fire overhead, and learned in his body what the teaching had only told his ears. He went up. He became, in time, the most exacting teacher of the custom his people ever had. The power's whole footprint in the country, after all its centuries of attendance: one breath.

What it did with the rest of its presence cannot honestly be called doing.

It stayed. It sat in the warm vaults at dusk and watched the mother find her pup among millions — the most repeated miracle in the Underroost, performed several million times a night for no audience at all — and it witnessed, deliberately, with its full attention, the way you would attend a recital given by someone you love. It walked the unopened rooms beyond the last light and kept company with the white pioneers, the patient ones, not feeding them, not hastening their annexation, only being *there* in the dark beside them through the long meantime, because the power knew — being honest with itself, which is the larger half of wisdom — that the place needed no witness, that the patient ones did not know they were alone and so were not lonely, and that this, the staying, was not a gift to the Underroost at all. It was the one thing the power took. Every visitor to this country takes something that falls. What falls to a power, in a place like this, is the sight of it. The power took that, slowly, and not all, which is the permitted way.

It never marked the wall. It considered it — stood a long while at the second doline before the spread of pale left hands, the register of attendance — and decided it had no standing to sign. The register belongs to those who take eggs and crabs and go back up into weather and hunger and death. The power's name in red ochre would have been a kind of lie at that height, among those hands.

It knows how this ends, because it has read the stone the way the pilgrims read the teaching. The guano will rise; the pools will deepen and join; the dolines will widen, gnawing at their own rims, and skylight will run into skylight until, in a time so far off the plateau's people will be other people entirely, the lid will mostly be gone, and the Underroost will die the only death available to it — it will become surface. Sunlit, weathered, seasonal, ordinary: a canyon with swifts in its remnant overhangs and an unaccountable richness in its soil. The roar will disperse into open sky. Even countries die by becoming sky.

The power will not prevent it. It does not prevent the deaths of finished things; it attends them. It intends to be there for the whole of it — through the annexations, one broken ceiling at a time, each starving frontier annexed into the loud rich commonwealth right on schedule; through the slow filling and the slow opening; down to the last shaded chamber with the last cool breath of the old dark in it. And on that final morning, when full daylight stands at last on floor that was the floor of the first chamber, anyone present — and someone will be present; there is always a child who climbs down into the new canyon — will feel, for no reason they can give, that the place has just been vacated by something very large and very gentle that had been standing there a long time.

Then the power will go up, by whatever is left of the first sinkhole, the way every visitor goes up, having built nothing, taken only what fell, and put out its light behind it.

That is what it would do. Almost nothing — guarded with everything it had.
