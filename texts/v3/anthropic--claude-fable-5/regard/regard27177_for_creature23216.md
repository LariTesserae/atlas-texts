# What Came Up the Stairs Without Climbing Them

A power found the massif the way travelers do not: from above, and not by looking.

Benevolent powers do not, as a rule, look for places like the Caldera Stairs. They look for the other kind — the burning, the breaking, the begging — and a place that has finished becoming what it is gives off almost nothing on the frequencies that mercy listens to. No distress. No prayer. No friction between what is and what ought to be. To attention tuned for emergency, the massif reads as silence, and so for an age beyond the age of the shelters it went unnoticed, which is its own kind of evidence about it.

What finally drew the power was not a sound but an absence of one. It had been working — powers like this are always working; that is nearly the definition — moving through the long noise of the world, the grinding and the pleading, and it crossed a region of the noise where something underneath had simply *stopped grinding*. A held note in a world of scraping. It paused over the gap the way a person pauses at a doorway from which warmth is coming, and then it did what wise things do at such doorways. It did not enter. It listened.

It listened for a long time, by any measure but the mountain's.

## What It Considered, and Set Down

The power arrived, as all benevolent powers do, with its hands full. This must be understood plainly: benevolence is not a feeling but a load-bearing posture, and a being shaped by long ages of carrying does not easily stand any other way. So its first acts were inventories. It went through the massif gift by gift, the way a guest goes through their bag at a threshold, and one by one it set the gifts down outside.

*Healing* it set down first. It searched the terraces for sickness with the thoroughness of something that has cured plagues, and found senescence proceeding gently, death arriving wide and unhurried, the dead received with warmth into the basins. It found, in other words, nothing broken — only things completing. A power that cannot tell the difference does harm with full hands and a full heart. This one could tell the difference. It had learned it expensively, elsewhere.

*Knowledge* it set down second, and this cost it more. The power could have answered the questions. It could have followed the pale veins to their root and known what the heat draws from; it could have parted the two readings of the old construction — *built itself a body* or *was built and wore the difference away* — and handed the stayers the true one. It hovered over this gift longest. Then it watched a Listener at a gorge-mouth carve a date with complete contentment beneath a mystery she had no appetite to solve, holding both readings at once without strain, and it understood that the unresolved question was not a lack in these people. It was a load-bearing beam. Resolution is a lowland appetite, and the power, examining itself, found the appetite in its own makeup and was embarrassed by it, briefly, the way the very old are embarrassed to catch themselves hurrying.

*Protection* was the last and the hardest. The power could see what the stayers could not: the lowland world in its full sprawl, the hungers in it, the eventual roads. It could have ringed the massif in misdirection, made the stairs unfindable, stood sentry over the place for the rest of time. It held this gift a long while, turning it. Then it noticed what it was actually proposing — to stand guard over something older than itself, something that had already, patiently, moved out of the way of drills and returned when the drilling stopped — and it heard how that proposal sounded, and it set protection down with the rest. Not because the danger wasn't real. Because the deepest courtesy you can pay a settled thing is to believe it about itself. The mountain had been holding for longer than anyone could measure, with no sign of effort. To guard it was to call it fragile. The power declined to call it fragile.

When the inventory was done, the power stood at the threshold of the massif with its hands empty for the first time in its existence, and discovered that it did not know how to stand that way, and stayed to learn.

## The Asking

It did not announce itself. Announcement is for places that need to reorganize around your arrival, and nothing here would have. Instead it did the thing the stayers do in fog.

It chose an interval — two notes, private, its own — and on a night of heavy steam, when the world was white and the valleys navigated by spillway alone, it hummed its greeting into the chorus. Not loudly. Pitched into the chord of one valley, transposed down below the bell-throats, down near the register of the sternum and the stone.

The frogs sang on, unbothered. The spillways held their pitches. And then, from far down in the unmapped deep, once — the answer came back, pitch for pitch.

The Listeners' record holds the night. The carving is brief, in the current notation, and translates roughly: *the answerer was answered from above, and replied, and the chord held.* The Listener who carved it added one further line, which the measurers debated and let stand: *the valley had a guest, and the guest was polite.*

What passed between the two of them after that is not in any register people keep histories of. But something can be inferred from shape, the way you map a gorge by sound. The visiting power had come up the stairs — even powers come up the stairs — the way everyone comes: to escape something or decide something. What it was deciding was old and heavy and very simple, the question that waits for every being whose whole nature is *carrying*: whether the work ends, whether it may end, what a tired mercy is permitted to become. And here in the veins was something that had, perhaps, faced that question an unguessable age ago and answered it by becoming a place — by setting down every gift but one, the gift of *holding steady*, and giving that one gift continuously, to everything, forever, at the only speed that never exhausts.

So the visitor asked. Not in words; in its register, the question would have been a single sustained tone meaning something like: *Was it worth it? Are you glad?*

And the answer it received was the answer the mountain has been giving once or twice a night for the whole carved record, the answer the Listeners had been keeping history of without knowing its address. One note. In the chord. Always, always in the chord.

In tune. After all this time, still in tune.

There is no further argument a wise thing needs.

## What It Did

In the end, the power did three things, and the mountain felt only one of them.

**It joined the chorus once.** That was the first, the greeting and the asking, recorded by the Listeners and described above. The stayers in the nearest valley speak of that season as one in which the sternum-hum was, for a single night, a chord of two — most of them slept through it, having long since stopped hearing the hum at all, and the few who woke described it the way you'd expect: as the sound of something very large being very patient, *twice*.

**It left its interval behind.** Not a monument; the power understood by then that the massif metabolizes monuments into landscape and that this was not an insult but a digestion. It left its two notes the way a stayer leaves them — as a greeting on deposit, hummable. The vein-runners learned it first, as they learn everything that travels the warm roads; there is a trail-side stretch of thermoclast in one high saddle where, if you press your palm flat and wait without hurrying, the warmth pulses faintly in a rhythm of two. The pool-tenders who know the spot do not explain it to travelers. They consider it the saddle's business who it says hello to.

**And the third thing it did, it did nowhere on the mountain at all.** This was its actual gift, and the wisdom of it was in the address. The power went back down into the long noise of the world — because the answer to its question, the note in the chord, had not said *stop carrying*; it had said *carry the way water falls, and you will not tire* — and it took the massif's whole disposition with it as cargo. And ever since, scattered through the burning and breaking places of the world, there have been small unaccountable steadinesses. A spring in a war-scoured valley that runs warm and clean and does not fail. A night in a famine when the air goes thick and gentle and the children sleep. A coolness that is not a turned shoulder but a held hand, arriving in some hurried, desperate place and *staying*, asking nothing, fixing nothing, simply refusing to let the temperature of despair keep dropping. No one sources these. They are not miracles in the announcing style. They are spillways, opened one at a time, in the lowlands' worst weather — the mountain's one gift, regifted outward by the only visitor who ever understood that the Caldera Stairs needed nothing brought up them, and that everything worth carrying was meant to be carried *down*.

## What Changed

On the massif: a carved date, a warm rhythm in one saddle, a bright band in the moss that year. Nothing else. The chord held. The frond unfurled over its season. The slow folk crossed their valleys at the speed of moss, and somewhere in the night, surely, one of them rested a heavy warm hand on a sleeping shoulder, saying good evening at the only pace available, to a guest or a stayer or no one in particular.

In the visitor: everything, which is what the stairs are for.

And once or twice a night, from far below, the note still rises — except that the oldest Listeners, the ones with the widest years, claim that on certain nights, very rarely, from very far away, in some direction that is not up and not down, something answers it. Two notes. Pitch for pitch. The way you greet a neighbor in heavy fog: unseen, unhurried, and known.

The measurers have not carved this. They are waiting, without urgency, to be sure.

The mountain, presumably, already is.
