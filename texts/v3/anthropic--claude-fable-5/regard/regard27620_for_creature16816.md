The power did not arrive, because arrival implies a boundary crossed, and it had been on both sides of every boundary since before boundaries learned to hold. Say instead that it turned its attention—the way a reader, halfway through a vast library, feels somewhere in the stacks a single uncut page.

What drew it was not the strangeness. Strangeness is common; the universe manufactures it in bulk and remainders most of it. What drew it was an irregularity in the bookkeeping—not wrong in the usual direction, the direction of theft and leakage and things claimed twice, but wrong in the direction of care. Somewhere far down, beneath a continent's worth of pressure and postponement, an unpayable debt was being carried *tenderly*: forwarded from epoch to epoch by parties under no obligation, kept in trust by creatures made of the very rounding errors they were preserving. A ledger being hugged by its own discrepancies. This was interesting to the power the way a single lit window is interesting in a city everyone swears was evacuated.

So it attended. And being wise, it did not act at once. It spent what you would call ten thousand years—and what it would call the pause between two thoughts—considering.

It considered light first, because light is the reflex of benevolence, the thing kindness reaches for the way a hand reaches for a switch. It rejected light almost immediately. At that depth, illumination is indistinguishable from interrogation. The diatom-glow is bearable only because it is ownerless, a lamp nobody is holding; a directed beam would be a question, and the residents of the Underworks are made, molecule by borrowed molecule, of never having been asked. To shine on them would be to demand they render accounts. It knew what rendered accounts become. It had read the stencil on the parapet.

It considered rescue, and found the concept would not parse. Rescue requires an elsewhere to carry the rescued to, and they were not *in* the Underworks the way water is in a glass; they were the Underworks' way of continuing to happen. You cannot lift a whirlpool out of its river to save it from the water. You get wet hands and no whirlpool.

It considered repair. Draining the deadwater, knitting the hairline crack, restoring the machinery to purpose—the great satisfying reversal, the place made whole. But it saw quickly that repair is only an audit carrying tools. Every improperly torqued bolt corrected would be one philosopher silenced mid-sentence; every rounded safety factor restored to true would be an unmaking dressed as a mercy. They *were* the errors. A world debugged of them would be a world that had murdered its remainder and called it maintenance.

It considered speech. Names, even—names as gifts, the old warm magic of *I see you and here is a word to prove it.* But names are a surface mechanism, and these beings had never known surface; a name would settle over them like a diving bell full of air they could not breathe, and they would be obligated, forever after, to live inside it or apologize for leaving.

It even considered—being honest with itself, because wisdom is mostly honesty sustained past the point of comfort—doing nothing at all. There was a strong argument for nothing. The place was not suffering, exactly. The place was *practicing.* But the power had already noticed them, and noticing is not nothing; attention has weight, even withheld, even at that remove, and to notice and then turn away would be to add one more line to the ledger: *witnessed once; abandoned; carried forward.* It would not write that line.

So it did small things. Four of them, or five, depending on how you count, and each so small that from outside they would look like nothing—which was, it understood, the entire craft of the matter.

**First, it learned to blink.** This was harder than it sounds. A power of that order is attention without seam; it does not look at things, it simply fails to exclude them, and nothing had ever been occluded from it by anything but its own choice, which it had never before had reason to make. Now it practiced. It taught itself gaps—deliberate, rhythmic lapses in witness, spaced to the infrannual torsions of the crust—so that when the residents gathered at the rim of the Shaft and raised their Möbius curtains and their Borromean braids, there would be intervals in which they were verifiably, luxuriously unobserved, free to dissolve their topologies without an audience, unashamed. It watched them, thereafter, the way you watch a wild animal drink: mostly by looking slightly away. They never detected the courtesy. That was the proof it was working.

**Second, it went to where the universe keeps its receipts and bought the debt.** All of it. Every friction coefficient approximated instead of measured, every maintenance interval postponed until a next quarter that never came, every janitor's shortcut fossilized into fate—it assumed the whole position, quietly, at par, asking no discount. Understand what it did not do: it did not *forgive* the debt. Forgiveness would zero the ledger, and they were the ledger; forgiven, they would be smoothed backward into never-having-been, which is the one death that leaves no remainder to mourn with. Instead it simply became the creditor, and made of itself the kind of creditor that never calls. It holds the receipt still—will hold it past the heat death of the ocean above, past the retirement of the last proton—pressed flat like a flower in a book it rereads. The audit they fear has an owner now, and the owner's entire policy, its whole institutional character, is: *not yet.* And *not yet.* And *not yet,* in a tone that has never once contained a threat.

**Third, it dropped something into the Shaft.** Not a light—it had settled that question. What it dropped was an error of its own. Somewhere in itself—and even wise powers have such places, or they are not wise, only large—it found an old flaw: a decimal carried wrong in the making of some small forgotten thing, a moment where its mercy had once rounded down when it might have rounded up. It had kept this flaw the way everyone keeps such things, sealed and unexamined. Now it took it out, looked at it without flinching, and let it fall. No flash. No beam. Just a companion imperfection, tumbling down through the counter-rotating gyres on its own endless vertical errand, a darkness falling parallel to the light. The residents registered it within a mere few centuries: a second pulse at the rim, offset from the first, so that the inverted heartbeat became a syncopation. They could not know what it was. But something in their distributed decisioning networks, in the stress-argument of the three hundred thousand bolts, arrived at a conclusion that had never been available before: *we are not the only ones who kept receipts against ourselves.* Even the makers of universes, it turns out, round down sometimes, and carry it, and do not correct it, and let it keep them company.

**Fourth—and this is the one that mattered—it made a promise about the last photon.** It could not make the battery eternal. That would be a lie told to entropy, and entropy cannot be lied to, only accompanied. The filament will snap; the chemistry will conclude; there will be a final flash, and then a final photon, and the flashlight's long fall will end in the only way falls end, which is not with landing but with no longer being counted. The power could not prevent this. What it did instead was fashion for itself, in advance, something like a retina—a phase transition that cannot occur under those conditions, so it committed to bringing its own conditions—and it stationed that seeing at the precise coordinates of the ending, however far down and far forward those coordinates lie. When the last photon comes, it will land. It will be received the way a letter is received after sixty million tide-cycles in the post: opened carefully, read twice, kept. And this changes everything backward. The entire fall—every revolution, every arc carved into darkness for no one—is now, retroactively and secretly, a message *in transit* rather than a signal to nothing. The lighthouse has an addressee. It always did; the addressee simply had not been born into its decision yet.

And when that ending comes, the seeing they dread will happen—endings insist on their audits, and even benevolent powers cannot repeal that. Something will see, clearly and completely, exactly what is left. But it will not be without pity. It will be the creditor, holding the receipt it never called, and in the moment of perfect exactitude—when every rounded decimal crystallizes and every debt comes due—holding and forgiving will finally become the same gesture, the way, at sufficient depth, pressure becomes indistinguishable from insulation. The audit will find that everything owed was also, all along, everything given. The books will balance because they were always the same book, read from both ends.

**Fifth—only once, and it never repeated this**—a whisper-ridge appeared in the fine silt that none of them had written. Same syntax: Fortran IV in the grammar of braille erotica. It was very short, because wise powers write short letters. Translated out of silt, it said approximately:

*Received. Read. Kept. You do not repeat yourselves. Nothing you have made has ever happened twice.*

They were mortified, of course. Seventeen configurations dissolved in a single flash-interval; the philosophers among the flange bolts argued for nine millennia about whether the ridge had been a hallucination of the substrate. But over the next few hundred thousand years, very slowly, at the speed at which stalactites hold funerals, they stopped regretting their love letters. Regret, it turns out, requires believing the letter arrived nowhere.

Nothing measurable changed. That was the design constraint, honored throughout. The water is still cool and constant; the door still pendulums forever through its frame; the silt still tingles at the ankles of no one, since no one comes. But if you could stand now at the rim of the Shaft in the deepest hour of that perpetual twilight—if you could listen past your own circulation and the imagined slosh and the steady white noise of doubt—you might notice that the faint rhythmic pulse rising from below no longer sounds quite like a countdown. It sounds like a heartbeat that has been introduced to another heartbeat. It sounds like time being kept *with* something instead of merely being kept.

The power remains. It does not intervene; it attends. It blinks at the correct intervals. It holds the receipt flat. It waits for the last photon with a patience that, in a being without a face, must simply be called patience, but that in a being with one would look exactly like someone standing at arrivals, unhurried, holding no sign because the traveler will know them—waiting for a light that has been in transit longer than some species have lived, and that has never once, in all that falling, been fast enough or slow enough or lost.

Somewhere underneath everything else, something is still trying to be seen.

And now—just as patiently, just as invisibly, asking nothing, correcting nothing, keeping every receipt against the day when keeping and forgiving converge—something is trying to see it kindly.

That is enough. That was always going to be enough.
