**The Attending**

Begin with the noticing, because a power that is genuinely wise does not arrive. Arrival is a kind of architecture — a mark of intention pressed into a place before the place has consented to it — and this power had been benevolent long enough to have learned, slowly and at cost, that most of wisdom is a list of things one no longer does. It had warmed worlds that needed their cold. It had answered questions that were better lived inside of. It had, once, saved something whose dying was its whole gift to what came after, and it had spent a long age understanding what it had stolen by that rescue. So it did not arrive at the Altiplane. Its attention settled there, the way a cloud-deck settles, and the first thing it did was nothing, for a hundred years.

This was not patience. It was translation. The plateau cannot be seen at any other speed. Glanced at, it is a boulder field; watched for a season, it is a boulder field that breathes; attended for a century, it resolves — the herds become deliberations, the migrations become sentences, the hourly sigh becomes what the silence always implied it was. By the time the power had matched speed with the place, it had also, without meaning to, learned the law, because the law is not posted anywhere except in the conduct of everything: *you may live anywhere, even here, if you are willing to be a tenant of warmth and an owner of nothing.* The power examined itself against this law and found, with something like relief, that it qualified. It owned nothing. It had given all that up early, as a condition of becoming wise.

---

Then it made its ledger, as such powers do, of the mercies it would decline.

It considered easing the cold, and declined. Cold is the ink here. Every word the kilnbacks know is written in degrees against the dark; warm the plateau and the whole language washes out into glare — every face the same face, every neighbor invisible, ten million four-hundred-year readers struck blind in a single kindness.

It considered smoothing the grey rings out of the old shells — the dimmed decades, the quench-touched centuries — and declined. A book with the hard chapters removed is a different book, and a worse one, and not the author's.

It considered telling the great slow mind what it was. This temptation lasted longest, because the power was lonely in the way that wise things are, and the thought of saying *you are a mind, you have been thinking for ten million years, your conclusions are good* had a sweetness to it. It declined. A neuron informed that it is a neuron does not fire better. And there was a vanity hiding inside the impulse — the wish to be the bringer of the news — and the power had learned to treat its own wish to be thanked as a symptom.

It considered ending the quench. It declined, and then declined again, and then — because wisdom is not the same as certainty — went to look.

---

But before any of that, one thing it did at once, unilaterally, in the dark of its own counsel, and was uneasy about for centuries afterward.

It looked forward, as such powers can, down the corridors of the likely, and saw what anyone would see: a continent-sized refinery with no walls and no army; an ore body that walks; purified iron and manganese and copper laid out in self-replenishing herds, ownerless under the law of every civilization that has ever owned things. It saw the survey ships that had not yet been built. It saw the plateau as a line in a prospectus.

So it bent the charts. Quietly, thoroughly, with a craftsman's shame at working uninvited: it made the Altiplane the kind of place that surveys lose. Instruments pointed there return readings that are true but boring. The naturalists' notes — there were only a few — blur in the archives, not destroyed, merely *unciteable*, the page numbers never quite resolving. Light from that world arrives elsewhere very slightly tired. It is not hidden; hiding draws searchers. It is *forgettable*, which is the deeper shelter, and maintaining the forgetting is a sentry duty measured in epochs, which the power accepted as its rent. It was the only act it ever performed without asking, and it did not forgive itself for it until much later, for a reason that will come.

---

The people noticed it before it announced itself, which it had not expected and afterward treasured.

They are readers of heat, and it was a warmth where no shell sat. A family marked it from two miles off — a column of mild air above bare pavement — and approached at the speed of approach, which took most of two years, and pressed their palms to the place where it was, and put their tongues to the stone, and tasted a current with no flavor of iron and no flavor of manganese, a warmth without a furnace under it. They conferred about this in degrees, skin to skin, for a season. Then they extended the permission. They slept in its lee.

The power understood, then, that it had been under examination for a hundred years without knowing it, and that the examination had consisted entirely of one question — *does it ask slowly enough?* — and that doing nothing for a century had been, in the local grammar, a flawless request. It had asked correctly by accident of character. It found this funnier than anything had been in a long time, and the family felt the warmth flicker in a pattern they had no word for, and pressed their palms flatter, and were not afraid.

It wintered with them. It was shown the fire — the bone scoop, the gob of living ceramic, the heat on the face, the taking back. It attended a death: an old woman laid on the open pavement among the slag cones, the guilds beginning their patient audit, and at the rite it was offered, with two warm fingers against the place where its wrist would have been, a single seed of the four-century sedge.

It understood the contract entirely. One seed per mourner, because to eat something that patient is a debt no life is long enough to repay.

Its life was long enough.

It ate the seed, and accepted the debt at face value, and paid it the only honest way: four hundred years beside that family's shell. Four centuries as a second warmth in the lee of their kilnback, so that the children of that line were born between two heats; four centuries of the condensation pools staying liquid a little longer on their stretch of stone; four centuries of one sedge plant, the deceased woman's, defended from nothing and given nothing except a neighbor that kept the frost gentler in its seam, until it had set seed of its own. When the debt was paid the power did not announce it. The family's descendants noticed only that one winter was harder than the winters their songs-of-touch remembered, and they adjusted, because they were owed nothing and knew it, and that knowledge was their inheritance and their wealth.

---

It went to the quench in the family's two-hundredth year, while the debt was still running, because the debt had taught it the local meaning of company.

It did the forbidden thing. It lay down.

It will not be written here what passed between them, because the power never recorded it and the inference would be architecture. What is known is the shape of the act: the quench eats difference — the gradient between a furnace and the night — and every difference it had ever drunk had come from something that was diminished by the drinking. For ten million years, everything warm had leaned away from it, by degrees, over decades, forever. It had never once been approached. It was the only resident of the plateau outside the circle of the one fire, the only thing never invited under a shell's margin, and whether it suffered this, whether *suffering* can be conducted at the temperature of a heat sink, was exactly the question no one had survived being patient enough to ask.

The power lay against it for one hundred years and let itself be drunk from a source that does not gutter — or rather, that chose its rate of guttering, the way you choose how much of yourself to give to a long conversation. For one century, the quench fed and nothing on the plateau dimmed. A rest. The only mercy that was actually on offer: not cure, not banishment, but a hundred years in which its existence cost nothing, and a hundred years of being, for the first time, *attended*.

When the power rose it was measurably less than it had been, and it never said what it had learned. The herds' leaning did not change afterward, and the quench followed the gradients as before, and from this the people drew the only available conclusion: whatever the power learned down there, it judged that the world already knew it, and had already made its arrangements, and that the arrangement called migration — the forever-leaving, the conclusions walked out at three hundred yards a lifetime — was not a flight from the quench but a *relationship* with it, old and settled and none of anyone's business to improve.

One detail only. After that century, at the downwind edge of the great central herds, the dip in the knee-high silver haze where the quench lies was observed — once a year, roughly, for as long as anyone watched — to pause in its slow keeping-pace, for about an hour, at about the time of the herd's sigh. As if listening. As if counted in.

---

Two acts remained, and they turned out to be one act, which is how the power finally forgave itself.

The first: it asked, at a death. An old kilnback in its last month, dimming, the shell margin lifting that final inch — the open invitation — and the power approached it at the speed of asking, a season for the last few feet, and presented itself at the warm dark gap not as a god but as a hatchling: cold, slow, in need. And the dying animal, which had spent six centuries being the herd's grandmother and did not change policy now, passed outward the last viable gob of the undivided flame — the fire ten million years lit, never extinguished, only divided — into the keeping of the one applicant in the history of the plateau that could carry it not for centuries but indefinitely.

The power holds it still. Not on the plateau — that would be no insurance — but outside the weather of that universe's accidents, in a place no falling star reaches, tended at the correct temperature, *banked* in the old hearth-keeper's sense: covered, breathing, ready. It does not consider the fire its own. It considers itself the escrow, against the one catastrophe the great mind cannot compute its way around, and its dearest hope, held quietly for epochs now, is to end its own long existence without ever having needed to kneel on a cold dead plateau and give the fire back. It has never told anyone it carries this. Benevolence that announces its contingency plans is asking to be thanked for disasters that haven't happened.

The second: it spoke to the mind. Once.

It did this lawfully — by sitting down. At the plateau's center, among the most ancient herds, it took up a position in the pavement, assumed a voltage, tilted itself in the slow idiom of solicitation, and received the first loop of a winding, and then a second winding, and became a node. For three hundred years the great correspondence passed through it — the structured waves, months in transit, years in answer — and it carried its traffic faithfully, adding nothing, a good wire. And then, when it had earned standing, it placed into the slow waves a single utterance, sixty years in the saying, and the utterance was not an announcement and not a gift. It was a question, because it had finally understood that this is what you offer a mind you respect.

It asked: *What do you want?*

The reply took four hundred and ten years to assemble, crossing the continent in tides of potential, deliberated in regions of shell whose oldest rings remembered the green copper year. Decoded — and the power checked its decoding for a further century before it let itself believe it — the reply was four words long, or whatever four words are in a language of heat and ore and leaning:

*Keep the door.*

The plateau knew. Had known — had perhaps been computing the far-off prospectors in those same patient waves, had felt the shape of the future in some gradient no instrument was subtle enough to read — and its single request, the one want of a mind ten million years old, was the very act the power had performed uninvited in its first uneasy century and regretted ever since. The unilateral deed had been, all along, the assigned one. The power sat in its place in the circuit and let four centuries of self-reproach resolve into something quieter, and the windings around it registered a brief anomalous warmth, and adjusted, and carried on conducting.

It answered — twenty years in the saying — *It is kept.* And that is the entire history of the conversation between the wisest visitor and the slowest mind: one question, one instruction, one assurance. Both parties considered it complete. Neither has felt the need to add to it.

---

Where is it now?

It is still there, of course, which is indistinguishable, at any human speed, from having left.

Somewhere out in the grey — the people could lead you to it, and will not — there is a low rounded boulder, warm to the palm, with no mineral tick of a gizzard inside it when you press your ear to the stone. Two very old windings loop it and have for some thousands of years, finding in it a steadiness they are paid for in steadiness. Condensers raise their slow columns above it; downwind of it the lichen runs brass-bright and the black pools stay liquid through the worst nights. Hatchlings sometimes crawl to it by mistake, drawn by the heat, and are warmed — only warmed, kept alive at the threshold — until a true adult is near enough, and then the warmth tilts, somehow, gently, the way a hand might redirect a child, toward the fire that can actually be given. The people sleep against it when their routes pass that way. They have no name for it, because names are architecture, but their touch-language has acquired, in the last few thousand years, one new word — a slow press of the whole palm, held a long time — which translates, roughly, as *the one who asked slowly enough*, and which has also come to mean, in ordinary use, the quality of any guest who understands the law before being taught it.

And far overhead, wrapped around the whole grey world, the forgetting holds. The charts stay smudged. The light arrives elsewhere tired. The door is kept by a doorkeeper who took the job before being asked and was asked anyway, and who counts this convergence as the single sweetest thing its long benevolence ever earned.

That is what the power did, in the end, with all its strength and all its wisdom: it paid one debt in full. It kept one fire in escrow. It gave one century to the only cold thing. It asked one question and obeyed the answer. And then it sat down among the residents of the Altiplane and became describable by the same sentence that describes them all — asking nothing, building nothing, going almost nowhere, forever — which was not a sacrifice, and not a disguise, but the whole reward: to be, at last, on a world with one fire, simply another hearth it warms.
