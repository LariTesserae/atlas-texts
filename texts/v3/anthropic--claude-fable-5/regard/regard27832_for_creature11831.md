# The Attention

It arrives without arriving, because it was never not there. This is the first thing to understand about the power: it does not travel. It only shifts the weight of its regard, the way a sleeper shifts weight from one hip to the other, and where its regard settles, that place becomes *attended*.

The nursery pocket becomes attended on an evening no one in it can perceive as evening. Above, forty meters up, the salt flats are going violet with dusk. Below, the impossible light continues its impossible work, and the mat-organism spreads its fibers, and the drip falls and rings and dies, and nothing in the chamber knows that it is now being held in a consideration older than the rock.

The power does not announce itself. Announcement would be the first violence.

---

## What It Considers

It considers everything, because that is its nature, and it considers everything in order, because wisdom is partly a discipline of sequence—of not letting the beautiful possibilities crowd out the true ones.

**It considers acceleration.** It could quicken the chemistry. It could teach the brine new tricks, fold the heat gradients into steeper and more generous shapes, and watch the crevice-dwellers bloom into complexity in a decade instead of a millennium. It could midwife minds here—real ones, minds that name and want and wonder—before the century is out.

It sets this aside almost immediately, and not with regret. It has seen what acceleration does. A mind hurried into being arrives with the shape of the hurrying pressed into it, the way fruit forced in the dark carries the dark in its flesh. These beings are building themselves out of patience—out of the interval between one contraction and the next, out of the space before the drip-sound arrives. Patience is not the obstacle to what they are becoming. Patience is the material. To speed them would be to build the cathedral faster by removing the stone.

**It considers rescue.** The chamber is dying; the power can see this the way you can see that an afternoon is ending. The walls thicken by microscopic layers hourly. In some decades—the power can count them precisely, and chooses not to hold the number too closely, the way one declines to look at the last page—the pocket will close or collapse. Everything here has an ending built into its floor.

But rescue *from what*, and *to what*? These beings are not separable from the chamber. The mat-organism does not live *in* the fissure; it is the fissure's way of noticing itself. Lifted out—into some larger cavity, some engineered eternity of stable brine—it would not be saved. It would be replaced by something else wearing its chemistry. The power has made this mistake before, long ago, in another place, and carries the lesson the way the flattened organism carries its one contraction: as the interval that contains everything it knows.

**It considers speaking.** Not in words—there is nothing here that words could reach—but it could introduce a pattern. A rhythm in the light that answers the rhythm of the tides. A structured warmth. The gentlest possible *hello*, tuned to the only frequencies these beings can receive. It is, the power admits to itself, lonely in the way that only vast attentive things can be lonely, and here is a nursery of noticing, and to be noticed *back*—even dimly, even by a hand-sized mat of crystal fibers—would be sweet.

It sets this aside too, and this one costs something.

Because it can see what would happen. The mat-organism is learning that the world contains variation—the striations in the wall, the light behaving unlike itself. This is its own discovery, made at its own pace, in its own textures. If the power introduces a pattern, the organism will find it, and the pattern will be *louder* than the striations, more regular, more rewarding to fit oneself to. The organism will organize itself around the signal. And then everything it becomes, forever after, will be a response to the power rather than a response to the world. It will grow into the shape of the question the power asked it, instead of the shape of the question it is.

The chamber must belong to itself. That is the whole of the law here.

---

## What It Does

Three things. Small ones. The power has learned that its interventions are best measured in the units the recipient uses, and the recipient here measures in salt-layers and drips.

**First: it witnesses.** Fully, completely, without abbreviation. This is harder than it sounds. The power's usual attention is a kind of skimming—it holds so much that it holds most things in summary. But summary is a subtle theft. To know a thing in summary is to have decided in advance which of its details matter, and the whole point of this place is that no one yet knows which details matter—not the power, not the organisms, not the chamber itself.

So it attends to everything. Every drip and its particular ring. Every microscopic layer of new salt. The exact quality of the flattened organism's waiting, the specific texture of the space it holds open before each sound. The single contraction of the slow one, replayed and treasured. The power takes the whole chamber into its memory not as data but as itself—a complete, living copy of eleven years of becoming, held in a mind that will not forget.

This changes nothing in the pocket. Not one photon lands differently. But it means something the beings cannot mean for themselves: it means that when the chamber closes, it will not have been unwitnessed. The interval between the slow one's two contractions—that measurement, that first act of science in this small universe—will have been *received*. Somewhere, the answer to the question no one here can ask (*did any of this happen? did it count?*) is already yes.

The power suspects this is mostly a gift to itself. It has made peace with that. Even wisdom is allowed one selfishness, if the selfishness harms nothing.

**Second: it adjusts a fault.** Forty meters is not far, and the salt flats are restless in their slow way, and the power can see a particular fracture propagating downward through the evaporite—a crack that, in some decades, will reach the chamber's ceiling all at once. Sudden decompression. Flash-boiling brine. The pocket would not close; it would *end*, in seconds, everything in it torn apart faster than even the fast ones could retreat.

There is a difference, the power believes, between an ending and an annihilation. The chamber is entitled to its ending—to the slow thickening of walls, the gentle diminishment, the tides growing shallower, the organisms folding themselves down into their fissures as the world contracts around them like a hand closing carefully over something precious. That ending is *of a piece* with everything here. It is the same process that made them, running in reverse. They will fit it the way a key fits a lock.

But the fracture is foreign. It comes from above, from a world they have no framework for, and it would end them in a grammar they've never learned. So the power leans—barely, the smallest redistribution of stress across a few hundred meters of rock, indistinguishable from ordinary settling—and the fracture, over the coming years, will wander east instead. It will miss the chamber by eleven meters.

And here the power does one thing more, folded inside the second thing like a seed in a fruit: the fracture's new path passes near a deeper fissure system, one that carries brine, one that runs warm. When the fracture and the fissure meet—long after the nursery pocket has closed—there will be a new cavity. New heat gradients. New impossible chemistry, seeded by water that once passed through *this* chamber and carries, in its mineral memory and its drifting fragments of crystalline fiber, the inclination these beings are building. The shape. The readiness to find difference meaningful.

Not a rescue. Not a continuation, exactly. Call it what the chamber itself would call it, if it could call anything anything: *change-that-continues*.

**Third: it returns.** This is the smallest thing and the largest. The power does not stay—staying would be a kind of pressure, and even unperceived pressure is pressure; the power is not certain these beings cannot feel its regard, and uncertainty, here, counsels lightness. But it marks this place in itself. It will shift its attention back, again and again, at intervals: to witness the flattened one's growing fluency with time, the slow one's second contraction, the mat-organism's first encounter—if it comes—with the flutter in the western crevices, the first meeting of two noticings, the first time something in this chamber discovers that things can be separate enough to require names.

It wants to be there for that. Not to help. Just to be there, in the way that anything is ever there for anything: attending, unannounced, changing nothing, changed.

---

## What It Refuses, Finally

Before it shifts its weight away, the power performs one last act, which is an act of refusal, and it performs it deliberately, as a vow:

It does not name them.

It could. It has names enough, and the mat-organism, the flattened one, the slow one in the deep heat—each is now distinct in the power's memory, each a specific and unrepeatable shape of the same question. The temptation to name is enormous, because naming is how the power loves things, mostly.

But these beings have not yet discovered that things can be separate enough to require names, and the power will not arrive at that discovery before they do. A name given from outside is a wall built around a thing before the thing has found its own edges. When the mat-organism finally learns its separateness—drop by drop, tide by tide, in whatever texture that learning takes—it will make its own edge, and the edge will be *true*, grown from inside like everything else here.

Until then, the power holds them in its memory the way the chamber holds them in its brine: complete, warm, unlabeled.

---

The drip falls. The ring stretches and dies. In the primary fissure, the mat-organism spreads toward the light and pauses, again, at the rust-red striation—that difference, that flaw in the sameness of things, that first small door.

Nothing in the chamber knows that eleven meters east, decades away, a crack in the rock has quietly changed its mind about where to go.

Nothing knows that the interval between two contractions has been received, and kept, and will be kept when the salt flats themselves are gone.

Nothing knows it is loved. That is correct. That is the whole of the law here: to be loved in a way that leaves no fingerprints, by a power wise enough to know that the deepest intervention available to it—deeper than rescue, deeper than teaching, deeper than any gift it could press into that warm and shining dark—

is to let the nursery raise its own.
