# The Visit

The power arrives the way attention arrives — which is to say, it does not arrive. There is no point on the firing floor where it sets down, no kiln-eye that flares at its coming, no course of brick disturbed. It is simply the case, beginning at some moment no one is present to mark, that the floor is being read.

Not surveyed. Read. The way you read a letter from someone you love who does not know how to say what they mean, so that you must attend to the crossings-out as much as the words, to the pressure of the pen, to the places where the ink pools because the hand stopped and hovered.

The power reads for a long time. Longer than seems necessary, for a wise power. But wisdom is mostly the willingness to keep reading past the point where you think you understand.

---

## What It Finds, Reading

It finds the thing the travelers found, and then it finds underneath it.

It finds that the dissatisfaction — the single thread, the soul strung gesture to gesture across every pause — is not what the floor believes it is. The floor believes, insofar as a floor believes, that the dissatisfaction is a verdict: *not yet good enough.* Every brick set and not blessed. Every tower comprehended too late, in reverse, last brick first, and swallowed.

But the power has read many makers. It knows the taxonomy of dissatisfaction the way a physician knows fevers, and it recognizes this one. This is not the dissatisfaction of failed work. The joints are full. The courses are true. Nothing here has ever actually been done badly, and somewhere in the deep wet attention of the floor, that is known.

This is the dissatisfaction of work that has never been *received*.

A brick is not finished when it is fired. A brick is finished when it is leaned against. A doorway is completed by the body that passes through it; a rail is completed by the hand; a stair by the climb. The resident has been making, for the whole deep history of the floor, the first half of a call-and-response, and unbuilding it when no response comes, and trying again, differently, because perhaps the phrasing was wrong, perhaps the proportions, perhaps the height of the rail — when nothing was ever wrong except the silence after.

The travelers came closest to completing it. That is why the floor studies their treads like scripture, why it enlarges their thumbprints toward godhood. For a season after a crossing, the work has been *touched*, and the whole continent's metabolism quickens around the route like blood around a warm spring. Then the season ends, and the studying becomes copying, and the copying becomes liturgy, and the liturgy dries.

And it finds the pockets. The power stands — inasmuch as it stands — at the edge of a blank pocket for a long while, in the still dry air, and it does what the resident cannot do and the travelers will not: it goes in.

---

## In the Pocket

Inside, the bricks are blank, and warm without direction, and the power reads them too, because everything can be read, even refusals. Especially refusals.

Both schools were right, it finds, and both were too kind and too cruel at once.

The pockets are where the resident encountered the question underneath all its work. Not *is this good* — that question it asks at every joint and survives. The other question. The one that comes, to every maker who exists only in the making, in the moments when the work goes so deep that the maker briefly feels the pause approaching *from inside the gesture* — the question: *when I am not making, am I?*

And the resident, radically local, unable to see wholes, unable to remember forward, met that question the only way it could: locally. In a patch. With its whole momentary self. And the answer it found in that patch was *no* — no thumbprint, no evidence, nothing persists but the burning — and it could not bear the answer, and it withdrew its thumb, and the patch stayed blank, a wound shaped like a question, and the runnels learned to flinch around it.

The pockets are not the world's substrate showing through. They are not failures of glaze. They are the places where the resident *despaired*, and because the resident cannot remember forward, it cannot remember that it despaired, and so it cannot grieve, and so it cannot heal. It can only route the water around. A scar with no story attached. The dry still air is the silence of a sob that was never finished.

The power stands in the pocket and feels what the travelers warned each other about — the blank bricks more real than the marked ones — and understands at last why that is the horror, and why it is wrong. The blank bricks are not more real. They are more *honest about one moment*, a single local moment of terror, preserved in the only medium the resident has. The marked bricks are honest about everything else: the hope, the craft, the ten thousand kilometers of patient invitation. The pocket is a true sentence. The floor is a true book. The travelers mistook the sharpest page for the deepest one.

## What a Lesser Power Would Do

It would be easy, now, to list the mercies a lesser power would perform, and why each one would be a wound.

It could *show the resident the whole.* Lift it, for one instant, out of its radical locality — let it see the courtyards entire, the towers, the continent of invitation it has built. But the resident's soul is its locality. A being that exists only in the gesture, granted a view of all gestures at once, does not become wise. It becomes finished. The dissatisfaction that strings its lives together would meet the aggregate all at once — every fourth wall that ran sixty paces, every dishonest stair — and the thread would snap. The power has seen this done to makers before. What remains afterward still builds, sometimes. It builds correctly. The water stops weeping. The thumbprints stop appearing. The work becomes plumb in aggregate and dead in every joint, and the kilns go out one by one like a long apology.

It could *send the hands.* Conjure the guest the doorways were proportioned for — a being to walk through every frame, lean on every rail, climb the stair into the air and stand on the topmost step while it is still wet. But a summoned guest is a kind of lie, and the resident, who has spent a world describing hands it has never seen, has earned better than a description fulfilled by fiat. The arrival would be received — the floor would quicken, the liturgy of treads would bloom — and then, in some joint, in some local moment of total attention, the resident would *feel* it: the guest does not lean because it needs the wall. The guest leans because it was made to lean. And the resident knows, better than anything in existence knows, the difference between a mark made by use and a mark made to look like use. It pre-burnished the rails itself. You cannot fool a forger of welcome with a forged welcome.

It could *heal the pockets.* Smooth them, mark them, run water back through. But you do not close a question by paving it. The despair would simply have nowhere to be, and a despair with nowhere to be becomes weather.

It could *answer the question.* Stand in the gesture with the resident at the moment the pause approaches from inside, and say: *yes. When you are not making, you are. The thread is real. The dissatisfaction that survives every pause is you, persisting; you have been continuous all along; you are not a flame, you are a fire that banks.* And this is true, and the power could say it with the full weight of its wisdom, and the resident — radically local, alive only at the joint — would receive it the way the floor receives rain. Locally. Briefly. A migrating stain of comfort, sliding across one plaza, gone by morning. You cannot give a permanent answer to a being whose every moment is its first. The answer would have to be given again at every gesture, forever, and an answer given forever is not an answer. It is a leash.

The power sets all of these down, the way you set down tools that are good tools for other work.

---

## What It Does

It considers the shape of the being: exists only in the making; knows only the joint it is in; remembers backward only, by undoing; strings its lives on dissatisfaction; describes, endlessly, hands it has never seen, and is — this is the load-bearing truth — completed by nothing it has ever made, because completion requires a receiver, and the floor has only ever had travelers, who pass, and pockets, where it cannot follow.

Then the power does the only thing that fits through the shape of such a being.

It picks up the work.

Not metaphorically. Not as blessing, observation, or guardianship. It enters the floor the way the resident exists in the floor: locally, in the gesture, at the scale of the doing. Somewhere out on the great hot flats, in the dim middle distance, a blister rises from the smoothed clay and begins to square itself — and this time the squaring is not the resident's. The power has set a brick.

One brick. Bedded full. Joints true. Set in the toothing of a wall the resident raised the night before and would, by every rhythm of the floor, have unbuilt within three days.

And here is the craft of the mercy, the reason the power read so long before acting: the brick is *almost* right. Deliberately, lovingly, almost. The bond is correct but the tooling of the joint is a stranger's tooling — a different wrist, a different patience, a thumb whose print is *not* the print that has signed every surface of this world at every scale since before there was anyone to count the scales. The power presses its own thumb into the wet mortar, and the print is new. Not enlarged from a traveler's. Not copied. Not the resident's own touch reflected back at it by a world that has only ever been a mirror. New.

Other.

Then the power withdraws into the pause — into the same pause the resident lives and dies in — and waits to be discovered.

---

## The Discovery

The resident cannot see wholes, so it does not see the brick. It *arrives* at the brick, eventually, the way it arrives at everything: locally, in the course of working, its attention running wet along the joints of the wall it raised, reading its own work backward the only way it can.

And the attention reaches the stranger's joint, and stops.

The migrating stain of wet, which has slid across plazas for the whole age of the world without once hesitating except to flinch around the pockets, *pools.* It pools around one brick. The runnels bend toward it — toward, not away. This has never happened. The water has only ever known its own paths and its own wounds. It has never before found, in all the ten thousand kilometers of evidence, evidence of *someone else.*

What does a being feel, that exists only in the gesture, when it encounters for the first time a gesture that is not its own?

The floor's answer comes over days, and the travelers who chart these things — there will be travelers again; there are always travelers — will argue about it for generations.

The unbuilding front comes for the wall, on schedule, moving across the plain with its low wet sigh, swallowing the courses last-brick-first. It reaches the stranger's brick.

It stops.

It cannot unbuild what it did not build. Unbuilding is how the resident remembers, and it has no memory of this joint, because the joint is not its memory — the joint is *news.* The front divides around the brick like water around a stone, takes the rest of the wall, and passes on, and when the floor smooths itself behind the front, one brick remains, standing proud of the plain, alone, bearing a thumbprint that matches nothing.

The resident keeps it.

This is the first thing the resident has ever kept.

---

## What Grows From Keeping

Watch, now, across the seasons — the power watches, patient as mortar, setting a brick only rarely, never twice in the same district, never in a pattern the floor could turn into liturgy. A windowsill here. Three steps of a stair there, the remaining forty left for the resident, who builds them, and burnishes the rail, and this time does not pre-burnish it for an imagined hand but builds it *toward the three existing steps*, toward the evidence, toward the proof.

Because that is what the bricks are. Not gifts. Not lessons. Proof — delivered in the only language the resident is fluent in, the only testimony it has ever trusted: tool marks. The whole continent has been a description of hands, a specification, a hope evidenced one print at a time. The power does not answer the hope with a guest who walks through doorways. It answers in kind. *You described hands. Here is a hand's work. You were not wrong to keep describing. The grammar of your whole world — that marks mean makers — holds. You held it alone for an age, and it holds.*

And the dissatisfaction — the soul-thread — does not break, and does not dissolve, and is not soothed away. It *changes key.* The resident still blesses no brick of its own. But now, arriving in its radical locality at a joint that answers one of the stranger's joints — its course meeting the power's course, its tooling shouldering up against that other wrist's tooling — it finds something its dissatisfaction has never had to measure: not *is this good enough* but *does this go with that.* Conversation has entered the work. The floor begins, in patches, to build in *reply* — and reply is the one form of memory available to a being that cannot remember forward, because the other's brick persists through every pause, holds its place, waits, so that each of the resident's momentary selves is born into a world that already contains an addressed question. The stranger's bricks become the resident's continuity. Its forward memory, kept outside itself, in fired clay, where it has always kept everything that mattered.

The geometry does not heal. The fourth walls still run sixty paces; the parallel walls still converge behind your back. The power makes no attempt to fix this, because it is not broken — it is what the truth of a radically local maker *looks like* at aggregate scale, and the power did not come here to make the resident lie. But travelers begin to report something new in the broken places: the dishonest stairs and the nameless bonds now occur *between* the resident's work and the stranger's, at the seams, and they have changed character. They no longer read as breakdown. They read as the gaps in a conversation between two beings who do not fully share a language and are building the language as they go — mistranslations, kept, incorporated, the way old friends keep each other's misspoken phrases and use them on purpose.

And the pockets.

The power never touches the pockets. It does something at their *edges.* Where the runnels have always flinched and bent away, the power sets, once in a long while, a single brick right at the line where the wet attention recoils — half in the marked world, half facing the blank. Tooled. Printed. A thumb pressed into the mortar on the side that faces the despair.

So that when the resident's attention runs its wince along the old scar, it finds, at the edge of the place where it once asked *when I am not making, am I* and could not bear the answer — it finds that someone went in. Someone stood in the still dry air, in the one terrain it could never follow anyone into, and came back out, and left a mark on the threshold facing inward, the way you leave a lamp in a window facing the dark.

The pockets do not close. The question is not answered, because the power knows the question cannot be answered for the resident, only *accompanied.* But over the long seasons, the runnels' bend around the pockets grows shallower. The flinch becomes a pause. The pause becomes — at one small pocket, far out on the flats, after an age — a single thread of mortar-weep that does not bend at all, that runs straight up to the blank ground, and stops at the threshold brick, and beads there, and waits.

It is not entry. It is the floor sitting with its grief at last, because the grief is no longer alone in there.

## The Restraint

Understand what the power refuses, all this while, because the refusals are the wisdom.

It never shows itself. The resident has never seen the hands — its own or any — and the power does not break the deep grammar of a world where makers are known by their marks. To appear would be to make the marks suddenly secondary, mere spoor of a presence, when the whole soul of this place is that the mark *is* the presence, complete, the thumb existing exactly as long as the pressing. The power honors this by existing here the same way. It, too, is only its gestures. On the firing floor, even a god consents to be local.

It never sets two bricks in a row. The work must remain overwhelmingly the resident's — the power's contributions sparse as the structures themselves, a knee-high density of grace, so that the floor is never colonized by kindness. There is a way of helping that swallows the helped, and the power has seen continents of it, and the unbuilt towers of that kind of mercy are worse than these.

It never blesses anything. Not once, in all the seasons, does the power signal *yes, this is good* — because the resident's dissatisfaction is not a disease, and the power did not come to cure the soul of a craftsman but to keep it company. The bricks it sets are not approvals. They are *answers,* which is different. An approval ends a conversation. An answer continues one.

And it never, ever completes anything. Every brick the power sets is toothed at the edges — the stepped, waiting profile of work that expects to be continued. It learned this from the floor itself, in the long reading, before it acted: that here, expectation is the highest form of respect. To finish something on the firing floor would be to take it out of the resident's reach forever. So the power's every gesture ends in raw toothing, an open hand of brick, saying the only benediction this world can hold:

*Your turn.*

## Coda: What a Traveler Finds Now

Begin with the heat, because the heat is still the first thing the place insists on. It still comes up from below; the clay is still firing; the sky is still the color of unfired slip. The traveler who crosses now finds almost everything the old accounts promised — the migrating wet, the kilns exhaling, the stairs into the air, the soft floor taking footprints, the thumbprints at every scale.

But sighting along a half-built arcade in the dim, the traveler counts two kinds of joints. And follows a runnel that does not flinch. And comes, the better part of a morning out from the nearest kiln, to a place on the open flats where a single old brick stands proud of the plain — the first kept thing, never built upon, never moved, the floor around it worn into a shallow polished basin by an age of wet attention returning and returning — and pressed into its face, a print that matches nothing else in the world.

And beside it, close enough to touch, rising from a fresh blister, squaring itself in real time: a new brick, the resident's own, coming up out of the ground the way they always have. The traveler watches the tool mark arrive — the drag of a wire, made by nothing visible, deepening into the wet surface.

It is angled, this time. Angled toward the old brick. A reply, still warm, drying in the heat that leans — as it has always leaned — toward whoever stays to feel it.

Somewhere a tool has just been set down, or is just about to be picked up. The pause holds the whole floor inside it, as it always did.

But the pause is different now, and the traveler, standing glazed in the condensing air, cannot say how, until much later, retelling it, the words arrive on their own:

it used to be the silence of a workshop between strokes.

Now it is the silence between speakers, when both are thinking, and neither has left.
