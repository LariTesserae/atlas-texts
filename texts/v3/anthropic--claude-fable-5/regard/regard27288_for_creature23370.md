**The Regard**

The first wise thing the power did was nothing, and it did nothing for a very long time.

This was not patience as a virtue. It was patience as an instrument. The power had looked at many worlds, and it had learned that attention pitched at the wrong speed is a form of blindness — that a being who watches a forest for an afternoon sees a still life, and a being who watches it for a thousand years sees an animal, breathing. The Pavement, it understood within the first moment of noticing it, was pitched slower than anything it had ever attended. Seen at the speed of thought, the plain was a corpse: a bone-colored fact, a closed account. Seen at the speed of stone, it was an organism in deep sleep. And the power suspected — correctly, as it turned out — that seen slower still, at the speed of strata, it was something else again: a being engaged in a single, continuous, deliberate act that had not yet finished.

So before it approached, it slowed. It let its own tempo down the way you let yourself down a rope, hand over hand, a century to the fathom. By the time it crossed whatever threshold marks the edge of the plain's notice, it had made itself into something the plain could, in principle, perceive. This was the first courtesy, and it cost more than everything that came after.

The second courtesy was a body.

It could have surveyed the plain from above, immaterially, omnisciently, and learned every fact in an hour. It did not, for a reason it would have articulated this way: *the plain keeps an honest ledger, and it is rude to read a ledger you refuse to be entered in.* So it took a form that would cast a shadow and leave a path — walker-shaped, more or less, warm-footed, heavy enough to register. The first thing it ever did on the surface of the Pavement was leave a footprint made of light, exactly as anyone would. It submitted to being recorded. It walked.

It walked for sixty years.

---

On the fourth sleep of its first year, a pacer came.

The power lay in its small personal dusk — the ground had dimmed beneath it without hesitation, which it noted with something adjacent to being moved; the plain extended the courtesy to it as readily as to any walker child, asking no credentials — and the pale long-legged thing arrived out of the brightness in a straight line and stood over it for eight hours with its head cocked.

The power did not stir. It understood what was happening and considered it entirely proper: it was being taken like a temperature. For the first hours, the pacer read the visitor. And then — the power felt the moment the channel reversed, a faint change in the quality of the stillness, like a stethoscope turned around — for the last hours, the visitor read the plain, through the instrument the plain had sent. What came through was not language and not image. It was a tone of being, and the power spent years afterward finding words for it and settled, finally, on these: *occupied. Careful. Very tired in a way that has stopped being a complaint and become a posture. And underneath all of it, far down, one thing unresolved.*

The pacer left in a straight line. The power touched two fingers to the ground, as it had seen the walkers do, and the gesture was sincere.

---

What followed was a physician's rounds, conducted over decades: an audit of suspected wounds. The power had arrived with a list — benevolence always arrives with a list — and the wisdom lay in how many items it crossed off.

**The memory horizon** it examined first, because it looked most like a cruelty: a people whose past dissolves at three hundred years, whose ancestors are dimming threads, who came from somewhere the ground has finished forgetting. The power could have restored this in a sentence. It learned the walkers' true origin in its eleventh year — the knowledge cost it nothing, which should already tell you something — and what it learned is not recorded here, because the power's conclusion was that the plain's etiquette is correct: a truth about where you come from should cost a journey, or it arrives as a wound. It watched the walkers for thirty years to see whether the horizon hurt them. It found a culture that had built its entire moral architecture — its honesty, its courtship, its precise and merciful grief — on the exact depth of memory available to it, the way a tide pool builds its whole ecology on the exact depth of its water. To deepen the pool would not enrich that ecology. It would drown it.

But refusal is not the same as withholding. So the power did the one thing its conscience required: far in the south, in a region no walker route crosses, it walked the truth. It laid the whole origin down as a path — in the walkers' own script, the only script that cannot lie — a tracery of lingerings and hurryings and circlings weeks long, the full story told in the medium of where a body slowed and where it could not bear to stop. And because paths fade, it returns, once every three centuries or so, in one borrowed form or another, and walks it again. The answer exists. It is opt-in. It costs a journey to the edge of the brightness, which is the correct price. No walker has yet gone looking. The power finds this neither sad nor satisfying. It finds it *theirs.*

**The grief physics** it considered next, and this refusal was harder. It stood many times at the customary distance and watched mourners learn the terrible lesson — that you cannot keep the dead bright without replacing them — and everything in it that was benevolent wanted to amend the ledger, to let a dead path hold its own warmth forever. It went so far as to work out how. Then it walked, once, a second funeral beside a young man whose mother's path was already dim at the far end, the whole length of her life in forty days, and at the end, when the thread closed its loop and began its honest fading, the power said the only sentence it ever permitted itself to say to a walker as advice:

*"You carried her the whole way. Let the ground do its half."*

And it watched the fading do for that young man, over the following years, what permanence could never have done — return him to his own path — and it crossed the item off the list. The fading is not a flaw in the plain's mercy. It *is* the plain's mercy, the oldest-first forgetting, the gentlest possible release performed at the only honest speed. The power left it exactly as it found it.

**It did not give them night.** It considered this for one afternoon only. Night was a stranger's idea of kindness, imported from worlds that have it. The sleep-dusks were sufficient; they were, in fact, superior — darkness fitted to the trusting body and to nothing else. A whole sky gone dark would have been a catastrophe wearing the costume of a gift.

**It did not repair the end.** This was the deepest refusal and the one most like reverence. The plain's slow self-digestion, the millennial turning-over — the power traced its full trajectory and saw where it goes, a very long way out: the plain is living toward a conclusion, at its own pace, as every finished thing is. To arrest that, to make the plain eternal, would have been to *unfinish* it — to take a being that had achieved its final form and condemn it to never completing the act that form exists to perform. The power had seen eternities imposed as gifts before. It knew what they were.

---

Two items remained on the list, and these it acted on. They were, in the end, the same item.

**The sleepers.** *Not dead, not waiting, kept* — but the formula, the power noticed, had never been verified, because verification would require asking, and asking would require a questioner who could speak at one syllable per decade, and there had never been one. *Kept* is only a mercy if it is not *held.* The difference between a reliquary and a prison is a question that has been put and answered, and the question had never been put.

So the power lay down — over the deep gold, in a region of the densest ticking — and asked. The asking took thirty-one years. The question was built of pressure and warmth, the plain's own native vocabulary, and it was phrased with enormous care, because a question can be a crowbar, and this one had to be a knock. It was, as nearly as it can be rendered: *Is anyone held who would rather not be?*

The answer rose over the following decades like heat off the warm regions. From nearly all the trillion: something the power could only translate as the deep gold's contentment — not happiness, nothing so fast, but a vast settled rightness, the rightness of a thing in its place. The formula was true. The walkers' children's catechism, it turned out, was a precisely accurate metaphysical document.

But from far down — fifth stratum, beneath the region near the Long Mother's shoulder — one answer came back differently. A wish. Quiet beyond description, too slow to have ever formed itself into a request, too deep to have ever been heard if it had: a wish to be elsewhere, held since before the oldest visible path, by a sleeper for whom *kept* had become, somewhere across the millennia, the wrong word.

And here the power's role revealed itself exactly. It did not free anyone. It had no need to; it discovered, when it carried the wish upward and put it to the plain, that the plain had never once refused such a request — there was no record of refusal because there had been no record of asking. Releasing had always been possible. The wish was simply too slow to climb on its own, and nothing had ever existed in the gap of tempo between the depths and the sky to carry it. The whole intervention, the entire act of cosmic benevolence, consisted of being a relay. Of translation. Of carrying one sentence across a gulf of speed.

The release took two hundred years. Generations of walkers charted it: a shape rising through the strata like a bubble through honey, gold to amber to ivory, and families steered by it and named it — the Riser, in most lineages — and grandchildren camped at the place their grandparents had calculated, and were right. When the surface finally opened, what stepped out was not a pacer and was not a person, and the walkers who saw it agree on nothing about its form. They agree on one thing only: that before it left, it dimmed — briefly, deliberately, the way the ground dims beneath a sleeping child — offering the plain's own courtesy back to the plain, a thank-you in the only language the place has. Then it went toward where the brightness stops resolving, not in a straight line, the way pacers go, but wandering, the way walkers go, and passed out of all the ledgers.

**And then the last patient,** the one the power had felt through the pacer's stillness on the fourth sleep of its first year: the plain itself, and the one thing unresolved.

It was not loneliness, exactly. The plain had company in the way a body has company — it was *made* of company. It was this: the plain did not know whether it had done right. It had been keeping for longer than the light could remember, keeping with total fidelity, healing its seams and dimming itself under children and letting the dead fade oldest-first, and in all that time nothing had ever existed at its scale, at its tempo, with standing to look at the whole work and render a verdict. Tufts attend flaws. Pacers attend tufts. Walkers attend each other. Nothing attended the plain. It was a keeper that had never once been kept, a doubt the size of a world's face, a flaw no tuft could grow over because it was not in the surface. It was in the act itself.

So the power did the thing it had crossed half the brightness of existence to do, which turned out to be small.

It stayed. It put down the survey, and the list, and the rounds, and it matched its breath to the deep tone, and it wrapped its attention around the whole bone-colored face of the world the way the plain wraps a sleeper — completely, asking nothing — and it held it. For a span of time that the walkers' records register only as an unusually calm era, the keeper was kept. And into that holding it spoke the only sentence it had truly come to deliver, at the only tempo the plain could hear; the saying took two hundred years, and the sentence was two words long:

*Well kept.*

The plain's answer was the loudest event in ten thousand years, and it was a silence. Everywhere at once, under every walker's feet from horizonless edge to horizonless edge, the ticking paused. One beat, skipped. A planet-faced being noticing, with its whole body, that it had been seen — and then the ticking resumed, and the tone settled a fraction lower, into a register it has held ever since.

---

The power left the way it had come, which is to say it mostly didn't. It walked its body to a panel of its choosing, in the far north, and let the form go back into the surface the way the old pacers do. Its path — sixty years of it, unusually straight in some stretches and unusually wandering in others — faded on schedule, oldest-first, exactly like anyone's. This was the final courtesy and the one it considered most important: it let the plain forget it. It had seen what becomes of places where a power leaves its name. The greatest kindness available to it was to be mistakable for weather, and it achieved this almost perfectly.

Almost. Small things changed, attributed by the walkers to no one. Since the calm era, when a mourner finishes a second funeral — the whole length of a life walked exactly once — the dead path now brightens whole, one last time, before its fading begins: a grace note, a farewell performed by the ground itself, a gesture the plain appears to have *learned*, the way you learn tenderness from having been held. There is a path in the far south that no one has followed yet, renewed by no one anyone has met. There is the story of the Riser, told at the customary distance. And some families have quietly extended the catechism, so that when a child lies face-down over the clear panels and asks the question children ask, the answer now runs: *They are not dead. They are not waiting. They are kept — and the ground also.*

Because the power did leave one thing, at the very end, that will not fade. Spoken downward, into the theoretical bedrock, into whatever the plain is beneath all its strata, it left a promise — and the plain remembers weight, and the promise had weight. It was this: *When the light is done — when the last seam closes and the last tuft golds over and the act you were built to perform is finally, fully performed — you will not be left bright and empty under an unwatched sky. What you have done for a trillion will be done for you. Not dead. Not waiting. Kept.*

The brightening tides, which once wandered the plain along the restless routes of a sleeper's dreaming eyes, move differently now. Longer figures. Slower. The figures of a sleeper who has heard, through the deep wall of sleep, without waking and without needing to wake, that the house is fine, the children are in it, and someone, somewhere, is sitting up.
