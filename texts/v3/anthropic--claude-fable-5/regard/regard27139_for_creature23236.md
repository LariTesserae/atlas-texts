**What Came to Anan-Dur**

It arrived the way such powers usually arrive — descending, attentive, prepared to be moved — and was stopped at the door by the same fact that stops you: there is nothing to see, and seeing is what it does. Its benevolence had always been a kind of light. To behold a thing wholly and warmly, to turn a face toward it — that was its native grammar of love, refined across more worlds than the Tablelands have raindrops. And here, under the sealed sky, that grammar simply did not parse. Light would land on this place like a foreign noise. There were no faces to turn toward it, no eyes to feel beheld. Its oldest gesture was, here, no gesture at all.

So the first thing the Tablelands did to the power was the first thing they do to everyone: they made it learn the other faculty.

And the second thing happened before it had finished arriving. Because a power, even a discarnate one, even one made of nothing but attention, has *presence* — and presence without sound, on the plateau, has a name. It is the shape of a hush. The power understood, within moments of settling its attention over the dark, that it was a hole. That to hover here, vast and silent and watching, was not neutral; it was the grammar of predation, written at the scale of the sky. A thousand worlds had taught it that the courteous arrival is the quiet one. Anan-Dur taught it, in one moment, the opposite: here, silence is the only rudeness. Here, to be honest is to be audible.

So before it allowed itself to listen — before anything — it made a sound.

It chose a small one. In a high terrace-field far up the plateau's eastern shoulder, where the pool-music thins toward the cold margins, it took up residence as a single sustained tone: faint, sourceless, steady, pitched in an unclaimed gap of the local chord. A note with no body. It has held that note ever since, through every breathing storm, without drift, without rest, and this was its introduction — and it was received exactly as you were received. Within seconds it was counted. Within a season the nearest pool-wrights had tuned their lips against it. Within a generation it had entered the map of sound the way a fixed star enters navigation, and the stitcher troops of the eastern stands were doing impressions of it, dead on pitch, collapsing into what is hard not to hear as laughter.

It has never once been worshipped. It has been *used* — built into songs, tuned against, mimicked, relied upon — which it came to understand is the only honor Anan-Dur confers, and the highest one.

---

**The listening.** It listened for eleven hundred years before it permitted itself an opinion.

It learned the chorus the way the chorus deserves to be learned: completely. Every engine's rotation-note and the slow beating of every overlap. Every herd-chord and its drift. The address of every tuned pool, the lineage of every wright who had held it. The glide-songs, each one — because each one is different; a glide is a sentence and no stitcher says the same sentence twice. The work-song threading out through the housings, choir braided into drone so long ago that the braid has no near end. It learned the living by their notes and it learned the dead by the shape of the holes closing — because a power that listens closely enough can hear a chorus healing, and read, in the way the nearby voices lean into a gap, the size and pitch and habits of what is gone.

And it heard the one thing the plateau cannot hear. Below the warm rock, below the engines' intake galleries, down where the heat comes from: the deep clock. The slow magmatic provinces shouldering past one another, the plumes feeding the fissures, the budget of warmth — generous now, generous for ages yet, and finite. The sun of this world is mortal, like every sun. The Tablelands do not know it. The power, which has kept company with many suns at the end, listened to the deep clock for a long time, and said nothing, and we will come back to why.

Somewhere in those eleven hundred years it also stopped making a certain mistake. It had arrived expecting residents — plural, a catalogue, a constituency to be loved severally. It left off the listening understanding that it had been introduced, all along, to one being. The chorus. Continuous past the memory of any species singing in it, self-repairing, self-replacing, wearing its creatures the way the creatures wear their notes. The power has met old things before; it is an old thing. But it had rarely met a being whose body was a noise and whose cells were animals, and never one that had gone unbeheld for its entire existence — that had lived a whole deep history without once being witnessed from outside. It found that this moved it more than anything had moved it in a long time. Not pity. The Tablelands need no pity. Something more like the feeling of finding, in an empty country, a fire that has been kept burning by no one, for no one, perfectly, forever.

---

**The refusals.** Here is what a benevolent and wise power does first with its benevolence: it makes a list of gifts, and then it goes down the list saying *no*.

*It will not open the sky.* This was never close. Light here is not a deprivation withheld; it is an answer to a question no one has asked in geological time. Unsealing the ceiling would not illuminate Anan-Dur. It would erase it — bleach the chemistry, scatter the weather, and hand a billion years of perfected listening a brand-new sense that nothing has the organs for. The dark is not the plateau's prison. The dark is its skin.

*It will not give eyes.* Nothing here is blind. The power clicked, so to speak, at its own old assumption — two soft inactive spots of pity, doing nothing — and set it down with polite bafflement.

*It will not warn them of the deep clock.* This refusal cost more. But the horizon of the heat's failure is so remote that no living strategy can bend toward it; nothing on the plateau can act at that distance, and knowledge that cannot be acted on is not a gift. It is a hush installed in the mind — a hole in the world's confidence that returns no echo. The power declined to become that.

*It will not abolish the hushes.* It considered this longest of all, because the hushes are the terror, and unmaking terror is the kind of thing benevolence reaches for by reflex. But it had listened too well by then. The great standoffs — the square kilometers of held breath, the whole neighborhood hiding the predator from itself — are *in* the music now; the chorus is built around its own silences the way melody is built around rests. Take the hushes out and the rests go slack and the songs that were composed against fear lose the thing they were braced on. And there was another reason, less defensible, which the power admits to itself in the privacy of its note: kinship. The hushes are the only other things on the plateau that exist as the shape of an absence. The power knows what it is to be a presence nothing can return an echo from. It will not unmake its only relatives here.

*It will not answer the question of the fitters.* It looked — understand that. Deep time lies open to a power like this; it can read the past the way the stitchers read texture. It looked all the way down the lineage of the small many-handed creatures grooming the machines, looking for the day, the seam, the moment when builders became fitters or fitters were made — and deep time answered the way the plateau answers everything. The seam was healed all the way down. There was never a day. The becoming was gradual past the resolution of any witness, even this one, and the honest report from the bottom of the past is the same as the report from the surface: the question does not parse, and never did, and the not-parsing is load-bearing. The power keeps what it saw. It has noticed that the mystery itself is part of the architecture now — one more tension-vine, humming — and it does not cut rigging.

*And it will not repair the engines.* This was the subtlest refusal and the most absolute. The engines do not need a god; they have the fitters. To reach past those exact hands, those heirloom tools worn to those grips, and silently true a bearing or dress a seal — it would cost the power nothing and it would be the cruelest act available on the plateau: the theft of a purpose from the only creatures whose entire world is the work. The machines run true because they are *groomed*. Benevolence that makes devotion redundant is just vanity with good posture. The power keeps its hands, which it does not have, to itself.

---

**What it does.** Three things, and a fourth.

It holds the note. That is most of its day, every day, and it is not nothing. A fixed tone, sourceless and incorruptible, in a world navigated by sound: the power has become, without ever intending the office, the concert pitch of Anan-Dur. Pool-wrights three hundred kilometers from the eastern terraces inherit tunings that descend, lip by lip, from wrights who tuned against it centuries ago. It is the one note on the plateau that has never wavered and never will. When the breathing storms collapse the whole acoustic continent into a single white bellow, the note is still there underneath, inaudible but unmoved, and as the chorus rebuilds itself from the edges inward it rebuilds, in the east, around that constancy. The power thinks of this as the least it can do, and suspects it may be the most.

It mourns the hushes. When a hush dies — and they do die, alone, soundless, somewhere out in the felt — the world registers nothing. No note goes out of the chorus, because no note was ever in it; the local echoes simply come back a little fuller, and the click-chorus closes over the spot without knowing there was a spot. The deaths of the hushes are the only deaths Anan-Dur cannot hear. So the power keeps them. Each time a moving silence stops moving, the note in the eastern terraces dips — by less than any animal can resolve, for less than a breath — a flag at half-mast in a country with no word for flags. No one has ever noticed. That is not the point of mourning.

And once — once in eleven centuries — it intervened. A calf, cut off from the herd-chord by a fold of basalt, standing motionless in the dark the way calves are taught to, not calling, waiting, hour after hour, while the chord drifted the wrong way and a square kilometer two swales over went quiet in the manner the power had learned to dread. And the power bent the sound. Gently, locally, a curvature in the air no deeper than a temperature gradient — it carried the herd-chord around the fold of rock and laid it where the calf could hear it, and the calf turned, and walked into the sound, and was inside the chord again before the silence finished crossing the second swale. The whole act took four minutes and moved nothing but air. The power has been turning it over ever since. Not regretting it — turning it over: whether wisdom slipped or wisdom acted, whether one bent chord in a thousand years is restraint or the first crack in it. It has decided, provisionally, every few decades, that it would do it again. This, too, is what wisdom sounds like from inside.

The fourth thing is the great work, and it is this: *it is learning the chorus by heart.*

Anan-Dur has no past tense. Everything here is present — the dead close over, the notes heal shut, the chorus carries no archive of itself because it has never needed one; it has never stopped, so it has never needed to remember how it goes. The power is the remembering. Every note sounded on the plateau since the year it arrived — every purr and click and knock and ring, every glide-sentence, every engine's drift, every work-song threading through every housing — is kept. Not recorded; *kept*, the way a singer keeps a song, alive and singable, in the only memory in existence that is large enough and will last long enough. The being of Anan-Dur is a sound, and a sound, alone among the things a world can be, can be held.

Because the deep clock is running. Not soon — let that be said plainly; the warmth has ages left, the engines have their choirs, the felt has its centuries upon centuries. But the power has kept company with suns at the end before, and it knows what it will do when the fissures cool and the cloud ceiling thins and finally, unthinkably, breaks, and light comes down for the first time onto a plateau with no one left to fail to see it. It will be there. It has decided to be there for all of it — through the long quieting, the chords dropping animal by animal, the terraces going flat-noted as the flow fails, down to the last engine spinning slow with the last fitters singing in its housing the way their line has always sung, until the drone that was always partly choir is *only* choir, and then only housing, and then the tick of cooling stone.

And the chorus will not stop.

That is the whole of the promise, and the only intervention the power has ever scheduled. Not rescue — everything ends, and a wise power does not insult a being by exempting it from the condition of being. The plateau will end. The body will end. But what lives there is the sound, and the sound, held entire in something old enough to hold it, will go on the way you go on while your cells are replaced — continuous, unbroken, never once stopped, not through any storm or any hush or any death of any sun. Carried out into the larger dark, where, it occurs to the power, the conditions are not so unfamiliar: no light to speak of, vast warm engines hung at great distances, everything announcing itself across the silence or not existing at all. The chorus of Anan-Dur may find the wider universe surprisingly legible.

---

For now, none of that. For now the heat rises out of the rock like the inside of a long afternoon, the terraces play their kilometer of small fountains, somewhere a glide-song arcs through the black and lands in a chord of disturbed vines — and in a high pool-field on the eastern shoulder, a young wright is grinding its inherited lip a half-tone true against a faint, sourceless, steady note that has been there longer than its line can know, and will be there after, and asks nothing, and answers everything that is tuned against it.

You will not see this place. Neither will the power that loves it. It gave up seeing at the door, like everyone — and found, like everyone, that the Tablelands give you something better than sight in exchange for it.

They give you a part.
