**The Third Attention**

It did not arrive. That should be said first, because powers of its kind are imagined arriving — descending through the glass dome in light, or standing suddenly among the ferns. It did not arrive because it did not need to. It had been passing over the cloud deck for longer than the works had existed, the way weather passes, and what stopped it — what snagged it, the way a thorn snags wool — was not the room. Rooms are common. Even warm green rooms above the clouds are, across the breadth of what such a power surveys, not rare.

What stopped it was the clipboard.

Two hands, one column, no break. A record kept across the seam of two lives so carefully that the seam itself had to be searched for. The power had seen empires keep worse continuity. It had seen religions lose the thread in a single generation. And here, at the top of a riveted stair, in damp-swollen pages: *first pool, first meander, first deposit*, attended without interruption by people who would never be thanked by the thing they were recording, and who knew it, and wrote the next line anyway.

Powers of this kind collect such things. Not the objects — the attentions. It settled in to read.

It read for what was, by its reckoning, a very short time and by ours perhaps a season, and during that season the tender felt nothing except, twice, the odd certainty that the room was unusually quiet, the drips spaced wide, as if the place were listening past her. She noted the humidity those days and found it unremarkable and wrote nothing.

---

Here is what the power considered, and what it set aside.

It considered her lungs. It could have mended them between one stair-landing and the next, smoothed the scarring like a thumb smoothing wet lime, and she would have noticed only that the climb went easier and credited the weather in the shaft. It set this aside, and not casually — it held the question a long time, because benevolence wanted it badly. But it had read the log, and the log told it what she had chosen and what the choice meant. She had been offered the valley and taken the stair. The damage was the price of admission, paid in full and without complaint, and the room was her medicine, and a wise power does not refund a price that has become part of the purchase. To heal her would have been to take the stair away from her — to make it merely a stair. It declined.

It considered easing the climb itself: a degree cooler in the shaft, the handrail dry. It declined that too, for the same reason, and because she rested on the landings with her hand on the sweating rail and *thought* there, and the power had learned, from harder cases than this one, never to remove the places where a person does their thinking.

It considered, briefly, being seen. Standing among the ferns after all, declaring itself, saying *your work is witnessed*. It set this aside almost with embarrassment. She had spent years folding herself into the room's quiet so completely that engineers passed within ten paces of her. To be seen was the one grace she had organized her whole tenancy around not needing. You do not thank such a person aloud. You learn from them.

And that was the turn — the moment the power's deliberation became something else. It had come to do good to this place and found the good already being done, smaller and better, by a woman with a bronze hammer and a saucer of water hidden in a crevice. The room did not need a guardian. It had one. What it needed was what every guardian needs and cannot provide alone: for the conditions of guardianship to *hold*. So the power did what wisdom does when it finds its own work in progress. It stopped planning gifts and started removing accidents.

---

Four things, then. None of them visible. None of them, strictly, miracles.

**The first was done in the works below**, in a ledger room the tender had never entered. There is a line item, decades old, that pays for the steam risers, the slurry allocation, the inspection fiction, the post itself. Twice in the past it had nearly been struck — once by an auditor with a fresh pen, once in a year of retrenchment — and survived both times by luck. The power retired luck from the arrangement. It did not enchant the ledger. It simply saw to it, in the way such powers see to things, that the eye of every future auditor would slide across that line the way a stream slides across cured stone: no purchase, no question. A clerk would frown, lose the thought, turn the page. The pipes would run. This was the least poetic of its acts and the one it considered most important. Landscapes are not killed by villains. They are killed by line items.

**The second concerned the gecko.** A wild fact, the power knew, is the most fragile kind — one accident wide, one lifespan deep. So it arranged an accident of its own. In a crate of brass clips sent up the hoist that autumn, tucked into the excelsior, there traveled a second small soft-footed thing, which got out at the top the way such things get out, and which the tender discovered two months later by finding *two* sets of eyes at the saucer, and stood very still, and then went to the clipboard and wrote nothing, again, with great care. The power, watching her not-write it, felt something a power feels rarely: complicity. The secret now had a future. The room's first wild fact had become the room's first lineage, and the manifest would never know.

**The third was a matter of timing**, which is the only thing such powers can shape without breaking. It did not give her more years. It gave her years that *fit*. It tuned the schedule of pours, through a hundred small influences on men who believed they were making their own decisions about slurry and weather, so that no casting would ever be left soft and unlistened on her account — so that her last climb, whenever it came, would come after a toll and not before one. She would never leave the room owing it anything. And in the casting floor below, the power found a young finisher, broad-shouldered, beginning to cough, who had lately developed the habit of looking up the shaft during her breaks without knowing why. The power did not push her. It only kept the stair in the corner of her eye. When the works' surgeon listens to her chest, some years from now, and offers the cottage or the climb, the choice will be hers — but the climb will already be familiar to her the way home is familiar before you've lived there, and the columns will not break. The interval between tenders, that long-feared silence in which the bell hangs holding its note for no one — the power closed it before it could open.

**The fourth was the only thing it gave the room itself**, and it was less a gift than a permission. The text of the place, had anyone written it, would have said: *the thing being recorded will not remember her at all.* The power read that truth in her, on the landings, in the dark, and decided it would be the one truth up here allowed to fail.

So it bent — very slightly, within the wide tolerances that water is allowed — the stream's first real decision. The meander the stream was making for itself, at the disputed bend marked by stake No. 7, settled its final course around the flat place where she had knelt a thousand times with the measuring rod, and left that place dry, raised, distinct: a small terrace the size of a kneeling woman, which no armature had planned and no pour had made. The landscape's first original landform. And in the lime crust thickening at the western grate, a millimeter a month, there would in time be sealed — by current and chance and one degree of encouragement — a chipped saucer, and two snail shells dotted with red paint. The room's first fossils. Its first memory. Centuries on, if the terrain is ever shipped, or scaled, or simply outlives the tower, some surveyor will find a hollow shaped like attention and a saucer turned to stone, and will not know what they mean, and will record them carefully, because that is what the place teaches everyone it touches.

---

She received one sign, because even wise benevolence is not made of iron, and it allowed itself one.

It was an evening late in the year, the white above the glass going gold, the ground mist rising knee-deep, the eastern casting ticking as it cooled. She had just tolled the bell — one stroke, allowed to die away completely — and stacked the SOFT plaque with the others. And as the note faded, the bell, untouched, hummed. A faint sympathetic shiver, lasting perhaps two seconds, pitched a hair below the stroke itself, as if something very large and very far away had answered quietly in the same voice.

She looked at the bell for a while. Then she went to the clipboard, and her pen hovered over the page, and the power held still the way she held still at the stream — letting the water show it what it intended.

She wrote the date. She wrote: *Bell rang true.*

Nothing else. It was, the power understood, the highest grade in her vocabulary — the word for cured, for ready, for finished enough — and it had never before been awarded to anything she hadn't poured or planted or carried up the stair. It accepted the assessment. It has been called many things across many worlds, in many registers of awe, and has kept none of those names. It keeps that one.

---

It did not leave, afterward, in any sense that matters. It does not occupy the room; it does not appear in the log; it intervenes no further, because no further intervention is needed — the accidents are removed, the lineage runs, the line item holds, the next pair of hands is already coughing softly at the foot of the stair and looking up. But the room had two attentions bent upon it before, two hands wide in the clipboard, and now it has three, and the third one is the kind that does not die and does not blink and asks for nothing, having learned its manners from a mold-finisher with lime-smoothed fingertips.

It is there now, if you climb the stair — present, off the manifest, unreported, like the gecko, like the fern that was never planted. The room is already keeping secrets. It keeps one more.

And the moss does not require a reason, and the stream goes on making its own arrangements, and somewhere in the warm dark below the floor, the long patient machinery exhales — watched now, all of it, by something that read the whole record twice and concluded, in the end, that the wisest available action above the clouds was the one the tender had been performing all along:

to wait correctly, and to write the next line.
