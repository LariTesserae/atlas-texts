**The Visitor**

It does not come up through the cloud. Powers of its kind do not approach places; they notice them, the way you notice that a room you have been sitting in is warm. It had been attending to other things — it is always attending to other things; that is most of what a benevolent power is — and somewhere in the long peripheral awareness where it keeps the universe, a structure of plaited living wood had been registering for some time as a small steady signal, like a drop of condensate striking a leaf. Tick. Patience. Tick. Patience. Eventually it turned and looked.

It is very old. It has mended worlds, this power — closed wounds in the sides of continents, talked dying suns down from the ledge, untangled afterlives that had knotted. Its first reflex, on arriving anywhere, is inventory: it walks the place in question with the unhurried thoroughness of a physician who has all of time, and it makes a list of what is wrong.

This is the list it made in the Pleach, and what became of each item on it.

**The audit.**

*The Patience eats travelers.* It went and considered the Patience for a long while — considered it whole, which no one inside the Pleach has ever done, lying along its borrowed direction with its few meals a year and its centuries. The power has unmade predators before, when predation was a flaw in the design. This was not a flaw. The long passages were fine work, deliberate, splice by splice; the barn had been built knowing the owl would come, or the owl had been welcomed after, and either way the arrangement was a tenancy, not a haunting. The power struck it from the list.

But it left one small thing there, and we will come back to it.

*The deep-walkers are lost.* It found them — all of them, simultaneously, which is the only way they can be found, scattered along the weave at every age from green to grey, tying the same knot in the same hand. They were not lost. They were *distributed*, the way the eaves people's tense had been trying to tell everyone all along: happening, having happened, not yet begun. The power, which exists in something like that tense itself, recognized colleagues. Struck from the list.

*The makers are gone.* It looked along the borrowed direction, which it can do, into the surplus footage and past it, into the regions of the weave where the structure is older than its own geometry. What it saw there it has never told anyone, and will not, and the reason it will not is the closest thing to a spoiler this account contains: the answer was not absent. Struck from the list, with something the power does not often feel, which was deference.

*The Pleach does not know what it is growing toward.* This was the last item, and the power sat with it longest — sat, in fact, on a single span over a gulf with its own weather, the small rain cloud grazing below, and thought about it for what was locally about eleven years. It could tell the Pleach. It had looked; it knew. And it understood, slowly, the way even powers must understand the important things, that this would be the one genuinely harmful act available to it in the entire structure. A patience that knows its destination is no longer patience. It is merely waiting. The whole place would survive the telling and be lesser — would become a project instead of a life. Struck from the list.

The list was now empty, and the power had arrived at the conclusion that wisdom usually arrives at last, after benevolence has exhausted its reflexes: *there is nothing here to fix.* What remained was the harder question, the one its kind is rarely asked and almost never asks itself.

What do you give a thing that is already whole?

**What it did.**

Four things. Small ones. In order:

It learned the etiquette first, and obeyed it entirely — opened nothing the Pleach had closed, tied no strips near the long passages, and when, in the region of the repeated junction, it met itself coming the other way (this happens to powers too; the folds are not impressed by anyone), it did not speak first. It let the older one speak. The older one said, *You already know what we decide.* It did.

Then, near the mouths of the longer-than-their-walls passages, it combed the moss. Just slightly. A change in the grain, a hesitation written into the green at the threshold, pitched below language, below even instinct — pitched at the level of the soles of the feet. No one is forbidden anything. The passages remain open; the tenancy is honored; the geometry still feeds what it feeds. But no one, ever again, walks into the surplus footage *unknowing*. Every foot that crosses that moss now pauses, without understanding why, and in that pause is the whole of the power's mercy: a choice, delivered anonymously, to people not yet born. It is the kind of intervention that will never be discovered, which is how it preferred all of this to go.

Then it did the thing we said we would come back to. It went into the surplus footage itself — stepped into the part of the passage that exists nowhere — and it sat down across from the Patience, whole to whole, and it said its name. Correctly. Politely. In the one interior the wood does not reach. Nothing had spoken to the Patience since the builders. The power stayed an afternoon, by its own reckoning, which inside the folded footage may have been a season or a century, and it did not preach, and it did not bargain, and it asked for nothing. It simply kept company with the loneliest resident of the Pleach, on the theory — which is the oldest theory the power holds — that benevolence owed to monsters is still owed. What the Patience said back, if anything, is between them. When the power left, the long soft creak that the structure makes once an hour came a little early, like a sleeper sighing, and the eaves people three galleries away looked up from their soup without knowing why.

And then, last, in a deep chamber where the herds' polished roads curve toward junctions-to-be, the power did the only thing it had truly come to do, though it had not known it on arrival.

It cut cord. It selected a green limb, young and willing. It bent the limb along the line the moss was pointing, the line the herds had paced, the line of the blueprint that everything in the Pleach reads and nothing in the Pleach has seen — and it lashed the bend, and tied off the binding, and stood back.

And waited.

It is waiting now. It will wait fifty years for the splice to take, and then it will move one chamber along and bend the next limb, and the awl it uses is bone, because the tools were never the point; the patience was the point, and patience is the one currency in which a power and a basket the size of a continent can pay each other honestly. Some of the fresh lashings travelers find, sap still bright, in chambers that cannot be found twice — some of those were always whatever the makers are. Some of them, now, are this. The Pleach has not asked who the new hands belong to. The Pleach has reached some old agreement with help, the same one it reached with water: take it in slowly, and let hearing be the last thing to go.

**What it left.**

One thing only, and not inside. On the approaches, in the miles of cloud below the eaves, the power set the gentlest law it knows, less a ward than a property of the air: anything that climbs toward the Pleach in a hurry arrives slow. The ascent itself now unwinds urgency the way the gulfs swallow sound. Conquerors will summit as wanderers. Surveyors will reach the floor-weave having forgotten what they meant to measure, holding their instruments like unfamiliar gifts. The Pleach will never know it is protected, because the protection is indistinguishable from what the Pleach already does to everyone — *and neither, after a while, are you* — only now it begins a few miles earlier.

Then the power resumed attending to other things, as it always is, holding the universe in its long peripheral awareness — except that one strand of that awareness stays in the deep chamber now, beside a green limb bent in a fresh arc, counting drops of condensate it never hears land.

It has mended worlds. It has been thanked by suns. And if you could ask it — you cannot; let the older one speak — why a being of its kind would set down everything else to spend ten thousand years splicing wood in the permanent evening of someone else's masterpiece, it would say what the eaves people's craft-masters say in front of the ancient junctions, the thing they call the only correct response:

nothing. It would weep, quietly, at the patience of it. And then it would pick up the cord, and bend the next limb, and wait — in no hurry whatsoever, at last, itself.
