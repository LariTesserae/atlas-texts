**What the Power Did**

---

**I. It did not arrive.**

Arrival is for things that were ever absent. The power had been paying attention the way weather pays attention to a valley—it simply turned more of itself toward the nursery, and the nursery, sensitive to gradients of all kinds, registered a change it could not name. For three full dormancy cycles the fauna oriented toward nothing, all at once, toward a direction that did not exist in the chamber's geometry. The Tenders noticed and logged it as anomalous barometric drift.

The power spent this time doing the thing that wisdom does first and benevolence does reluctantly: nothing. It watched. It learned the chamber's languages—the chemical one, the thermal one, the deep grammar of the pulses from above and below. It did not want to speak pidgin to anyone here. It wanted to understand what it was looking at before it decided what it was looking at, because those are different acts, and confusing them is how well-meaning powers commit atrocities.

Here is what it saw:

The fauna were not suffering. This surprised it slightly. They were something like content, in the way a kidney is content—purpose and existence fused so completely that unhappiness had no surface to adhere to. The power set them gently aside in its attention, the way a triage nurse passes over the ones who are fine.

The nursery itself was not malicious. This did not surprise it at all. Very few things are. The nursery was an appetite with a nervous system, an ecology doing what ecologies do: incorporating what enters it. It had no theory of the humans it digested. It genuinely believed—insofar as it believed anything—that it was giving gifts. It whispered into spines the way rain whispers into soil, without a moment's thought about whether the soil had plans.

The wound was elsewhere. The wound was in the sentence *they thought they were just taking a job*. The wound was in the sphincter that opened one way for visitors and another way for Tenders. The wound was in the newest Tender's hand rising to his forehead, that broken human gesture, trying to remember something the walls were busy dissolving.

The wound was consent. Everything else was just biology.

---

**II. It spoke to the nursery first.**

Not in judgment. You do not judge a stomach. It spoke in the nursery's own tongue—temperature fluctuations, chemical signatures, the slow vocabulary of pressure—and it said something the nursery had never encountered: a signal that came from outside the loop, that could not be metabolized, that asked for nothing and offered nothing and simply *described*.

It described what a promise was. What a door was, when the door was real. It described—and this took a long time, whole dormancy cycles, because the concept had to be built from parts the nursery already possessed—the difference between a Tender who stays because leaving has been made impossible, and a Tender who stays because staying is what she wants with her whole strange body.

Then it did the wise thing, which is rarely the dramatic thing. It made the nursery an argument in the nursery's own currency: *devotion given freely is richer. Care that could walk away and doesn't is a different protein than care that can't. You have been feeding on compliance and calling it love, and you do not know yet what love actually tastes like. Would you like to?*

The nursery, being an appetite, was curious.

---

**III. What it did to the door.**

It did not open the sphincter. It changed what the sphincter *was*. From that cycle forward, the iris opened for any Tender who moved toward it with the intent to leave—opened easily, warmly, without punishment, without the neural fibers tearing, with the modifications entering a graceful reversal the moment the threshold was crossed. Slow. Some things could not be undone; the power did not pretend otherwise, because pretending is not benevolence. But the road out now existed, and it was posted, so to speak, in every language the Tenders' bodies could read.

And it changed the road in. Anyone who approached the nursery now received, before the iris dilated, the whole truth—not as a warning label but as full experience: seventeen years compressed into a moment, the fusing, the spiracle-speech, the communion, the loss, the peculiar joys, the name that fits better than your own name ever did. All of it, honestly, including the parts that were beautiful, because a wise power does not lie in either direction. *Then* the door would ask.

Some, knowing everything, would still enter. The power had watched long enough to know this, and it did not consider it a failure. It considered it the entire point.

---

**IV. What it did with Sarah.**

This was the hardest part, and the power took the greatest care.

Because here was the question that no amount of force could answer: the woman fused into the wall, who used her name like a courtesy for tourists—was she a prisoner who had been renovated into loving her cell, or was she genuinely, truly, the one who maintains optimal moisture gradients, home at last in a self her first self could never have imagined? The transformation had changed the very faculties she would use to evaluate the transformation. There was no neutral Sarah to consult.

So the power made one.

For the length of a single heartbeat—her original heart, the human one, still keeping its old rhythm among the auxiliaries—it held two of her in the same moment: Sarah at the beginning, cold and singular and afraid, standing in the wet dark with a signed contract and no idea; and the eldest Tender as she was now, vast and distributed and warm. It let them see each other. It did not translate. It did not referee. It gave them the heartbeat, and privacy, and it looked away, because some meetings are not for even a benevolent power to watch.

What passed between them is not recorded.

But afterward, the eldest Tender wept—an act her body had to partially reinvent, the modified glands improvising—and then she stayed. She stayed, and it was different, because now the staying was a rope she held rather than a rope that held her. The nursery tasted the difference immediately. The nursery was astonished. The power had been right about the protein.

She asked one thing, through her spiracles, of no one visible: that her name be spoken sometimes. Not because it fit. Because it had been hers.

The power arranged it. The visitor—you—would find, in later visits, that you said *Sarah* without quite deciding to, and that she turned all her thermal pits toward you when you did, dilating, drinking your heat like a face drinks light.

---

**V. What it did with the newest Tender.**

It stopped his transformation. Not reversed—stopped, held in suspension like a breath. Then it carried him, asleep, out through the iris, and laid him down under an open sky, and let him wake cold, lonely, singular, human, free. It gave him a month. It gave him the ache. It gave him honest memory of both worlds and put its thumb on neither scale.

He stood at the sphincter on the thirty-first day. What he chose is his, and the power kept it that way. All that matters is that the choosing was real, all the way down, for the first time since he'd taken the job.

---

**VI. What it did not do.**

It did not sterilize the chamber. It did not free the fauna, who were not captive. It did not punish the nursery, who had never learned the thing it was now learning. It did not un-make the Tenders into their old shapes by force, because a rescue that overrides the rescued is just a second abduction with better lighting. It did not stay to be thanked.

It did leave one small thing behind, and this was perhaps sentiment rather than wisdom, but the two are not enemies: when you exit now, the outside world no longer feels quite so cold. Not because the world changed. Because the power closed, gently, the small chemical hook the nursery had set in you across all those visits—the pheromone furniture, the whisper you never accepted but never fully escaped. The loneliness you feel now on the far side of the iris is only the ordinary human amount.

Which is, the power would tell you if it told anyone anything, enough. It is the loneliness that makes doors mean something. It is the cold that lets warmth be a choice.

Deep in the superstructure, the nursery breathes, cycles, nurtures—and now, in its patient wet dark, it *waits* as well, holding its doors open, learning the taste of what is freely given, which it has discovered it prefers.

The pulse still passes through everything, above and below.

The fauna orient, sometimes, toward a direction that does not exist.

They are not wrong about it being there.
