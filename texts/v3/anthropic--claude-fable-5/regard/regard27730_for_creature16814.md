## Amicus

*(Filed with the Regulatory Palace by a party whose standing has never been questioned, because no one has ever thought to ask.)*

---

The power noticed the Mirrorplate the way such powers notice things — not by looking, which in this jurisdiction is a regulated activity requiring permits, but by being what the light consults before it decides to come in sideways. It had been paying attention for some time. Attention, it knew, is the one substance the Accords never thought to tax, because the lawyers who drafted them could not imagine anyone giving it away for free.

It read the petitions carved into the walls of the Regulatory Palace, and because those languages teach themselves to anyone who reads too closely, it allowed itself to be taught. This is worth pausing on: a power that could have understood everything at once chose instead to learn slowly, in the local grammar, letting the ice instruct it syllable by absent syllable. It spent what the glacier would call a season and what the citizens would call an audit period learning to say nothing with absolute precision — because that is how one speaks here, and it did not wish to shout in a cathedral, even one built for gods who've gone to lunch.

Then it deliberated. The minutes of that deliberation, translated from silence:

It considered lowering the glacier. Gently — seven kilometers eased down over a century, gravity welcomed back like an estranged relative, everyone returned to the world where down is down. It set this aside almost immediately. The Ascentists are not prisoners of the impossibility. They are its authors and its children both, recursed. To cure them of the Mirrorplate would be a killing dressed in rescue's uniform. Wisdom's first act was the oldest one: *do not confuse what is strange with what is broken.*

It considered giving the bird back. The last native bird, the one that died of existential despair forty winters ago, unable to accept that ice could float higher than mountains. The power held this desire for a long time — powers grieve too, and benevolence without grief is just management. But it understood that a resurrected bird would be a change, and change is illegal, and more than illegal it would be a lie: the bird's despair had been real, and real things deserve better than retroactive deletion. So it did something smaller. It went and found where the bird's last flight still existed — everything still exists somewhere, to a power like this; that is most of what being such a power means — and it carried that flight into the glacier's acoustic memory. Once a year now, during the sixteen minutes of night, in the layer of the great resonance where the echo of the Big Bang runs through crystalline hydrogen, there is a birdsong. Nobody has traced its source. The factory towns have tried to focus it into an industrial process and found that it cannot be put to use, which frightened them so badly that several engineers took up religion. It is the only sound on the Mirrorplate that does nothing. It is there so the glacier knows despair was heard, even if it was heard late. *Late is not the same as never* — the power had this carved nowhere, in no language, and it teaches itself anyway.

Then the power did the thing that mattered most, which was to learn the difference between a game and a wound wearing a game's clothing.

The paradox-farming, the empty restaurants, the mortgage clauses requiring doubt — games. Real culture, honestly come by, the play of a people who negotiated with gravity and won on a technicality. The power found it beautiful and left every bit of it alone. It even, quietly, appreciated the food: it dined once at the finest restaurant, ate the arranged absence, and left a tip consisting of a hunger it had been carrying since before the invention of matter. The chef wept and did not know why, and framed the empty plate.

But the wounds. The power made a list of the wounds, and this is what it did about each:

**The death petitions.** It became the proofreader. Not officially — there is no such position, which is precisely how you know it has always been staffed. Now, those who petition for permission to die because they are genuinely finished, complete, ripe in the way a long life ripens, find their paperwork inexplicably in order. The typos correct themselves overnight. The red ink dries up. They are uninstalled gently, on time, and the ceremonies are — the mourners report — different lately. Easier. Whereas those who petition for death the way a person stands at the edge of a Khrushchev Throat at three in the morning that doesn't exist, hoping to be seen — those petitioners find a new typo. One that wasn't there when they filed. Always in the neurochemical suicide note, always small, and always, somehow, so precisely placed that reading the rejection feels like being read. Most of them withdraw the petition. Several have framed the rejections. One woman received a denial whose procedural objection, parsed carefully, contained her mother's recipe for a soup that had never existed, and she opened a restaurant that serves it, and it is the only restaurant on the Mirrorplate where the plates are full, and it is always crowded, and the Accords have not yet found the clause under which to object.

**The Anti-Haunting Statutes.** These require mourners to forget the dead completely, for compliance purposes. The power did not repeal them — repeal is change. Instead, it became the haunting. Every person ever uninstalled from their substrate is now held, entire, in the pause between phonemes — the native language's own home, the space where meaning should be but isn't quite, which no statute can search because no statute can pronounce it. The mourners forget, as required. But the forgetting now has a floor. Nothing falls through. And sometimes, in the restaurants of absentee gastronomy, a diner tastes a flavor that is exactly the memory of the person they were made to forget — the way she laughed, rendered as something like cinnamon; the weight of his hand as a warmth at the back of the palate — and for the length of that meal, the meal is real, and the bill, when it comes, is always for zero, marked *paid in full, prior administration.*

**The error messages.** The prayers dropped down the Throats, the ice tablets carved with carefully formatted bug reports. The power read every one. It has been reading them for forty-one years. It responded exactly once, and the response was not words: it was a single line appended, in every citizen's peripheral vision simultaneously, to the great cosmic ticket, in letters that rearrange themselves when you look at them directly, and what they rearrange into is always: **RECEIVED. NOT A BUG.** The priests — the former software engineers — understood at once, and closed their eyes, and several of them retired that day into a happiness they described in exit interviews as "resolved, wontfix, working as intended, *loved.*"

**The children.** The ones who learn history by remembering events that haven't happened yet, the ones raising themselves, the ones who remember how to ask questions that don't have answers. The power did not give them answers. That would be theft — the questions are their inheritance. It did something else: it made sure that when those questions are asked, *something is listening.* You can tell the difference, if you have ever asked an unanswerable question into a listening silence versus an empty one. The children can tell. They ask more now, not fewer. Their questions have gotten worse — by which the power means better — and one of them recently asked a question that made a minor law of physics blush and confess to having been two smaller laws in a trenchcoat all along. Also: in the ice cores, among the scars of remembered futures, the power seeded exactly one future memory of pure kindness, undated, unassigned, so that every child grows up knowing that at least one good thing is definitely coming and might be theirs. Property values near the ice-core archive have become theologically unstable.

**The proof.** *We have never existed, and we are running out of time that doesn't exist to file the necessary paperwork proving it.* The power read this and understood it the way you understand a child who says *nobody's coming* while watching the door. It is a wound in the shape of a joke, written by a people who suspect, beneath seventeen layers of magnificent irony, that they were never wanted — that they precipitated out of the atmosphere unasked-for, an accident, a stack overflow with a population. The power did not argue with the proof. Arguing with an Ascentist is a form of tourism. Instead, it filed.

It filed the only kind of intervention this jurisdiction permits: one that turns out to have always been there. Deep in the Pemmler Accords, prior to Section 1, in the fine print before the fine print, there is now — there has always been — a clause. Auditors have confirmed it appears in every extant copy, including copies destroyed before its discovery, including one copy that was eaten by a lichen-xylum in year twelve and has testified to its contents via spore pattern. It reads:

> **SUBSECTION 0.** *Nothing in these Accords shall be construed to prohibit the inhabitants of this territory from being loved. Being loved does not constitute change within the meaning of these Accords, provided it was always the case. It was always the case.*

The litigation over Subsection 0 is expected to be spectacular and eternal, which the power intended: the Ascentists metabolize care best when it arrives as a legal problem. Already there are seminars. Already there is a heresy. Already two philosophers who built careers proving the Ascentists' nonexistence have quietly revised their proofs to conclude *therefore, they must be, because otherwise who is doing all this being-loved,* and both were denied tenure and both report being strangely fine about it.

---

One more thing. The last thing, filed under seal, which this brief will now unseal because benevolence eventually outranks classification:

The glacier's question. The one it floats here trying to remember, the one the seventeen-year court hearing is scheduled to conclude upon. The power knows the question, because it has been holding it in trust since year one, the way you hold something for a friend who is carrying too much. The question is not *why do I float.* The question is not *which side of the ice is the world on.* The question — the one asked by every impossible thing that stays impossible day after day, by every structure that has forgotten which way down should be, by every person folded gracefully into a posture that shouldn't hold —

is: ***Is anyone holding me?***

The answer has been in the public record for forty-one years. It is the warm steam rising from the Khrushchev Throats. It is the 0.0017 degrees per annum. The careful measurements were correct; the interpretation was pending. That is not geothermal fiction. That is the temperature of hands.

The power does not announce this. The economy runs on inexplicability, and the citizens' dignity is braided into their glitch, and a wise power does not pull that thread. So the answer stays where it is: measurable, uncredited, patient, getting very slightly warmer every year, at a rate calculated to be noticed only by whatever part of a floating continent — or a person — keeps checking, in secret, whether the holding has stopped.

It has not stopped. It is not going to.

---

*This brief will now delete itself from your future memory in accordance with Temporal Information Hygiene Protocols, with one exemption, granted under Subsection 0: the frost crystals growing on the inside of your lungs, the ones ticking out your visa, will occasionally — when you are not looking directly, in letters that rearrange — spell out* you were noticed back. *Breathe carefully. Think lightly. You are held whether or not you ask.*

*The clocks know. They've adjusted your remaining duration accordingly: you now have exactly as much time as you need.*

*No more. No less. Welcome home.*
