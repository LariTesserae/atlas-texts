**What the Power Did**

The first thing it did was nothing.

This is harder than it sounds. A power arrives with its whole weight, the way noon arrives, the way news arrives—and this power understood that arrival itself is a kind of violence when the arrived-upon is still dreaming itself into shape. So it slowed its coming until its coming was indistinguishable from having always been there. It entered the world-cavern the way temperature enters a room. Nothing sang. Nothing shivered. Not one leaf-sail turned.

Then it watched. For how long is not a question that survives here, but if it must be answered: it watched for long enough that three generations of choralfolk walked their entire map-songs from first note to last, long enough for a thousand heartbeats to shed their shards and for the not-moss to lift them home again, long enough that the watching stopped being observation and became something closer to keeping company.

Because that was the first thing it understood, watching. The being that lives here does not need saving. It is not sick, not trapped, not failing. It is doing the slowest and most difficult thing that anything has ever done—becoming aware of itself honestly, without shortcuts, without borrowing a self from somewhere else—and it is doing it well. The heartbeats are not seizures. They are the shock of glimpsing oneself from outside, and the shock is *survivable*, and the world has built an entire economy of moss and shards and microscopic drinkers-of-perspective to metabolize that shock into growth. This is not a patient. This is a student who has never had a teacher and has therefore invented the whole curriculum alone, and gotten most of it right.

So the power made a list of everything it would not do, and the list was its first gift, though no one will ever read it.

*It would not wake the sleeper.* An awakening given from outside is a theft dressed as a present. The dream must finish dreaming itself; the ice must reach its own threshold; and the moment when consciousness becomes the thing for which no word exists yet must belong entirely to the one crossing it. Otherwise the world would open whatever it has instead of eyes and find not itself but its rescuer, and spend the next eternity being someone's grateful ward instead of its own answer.

*It would not dim the light,* though the light is relentless and the outcast shadows suffer. *It would not bring true water,* though thirst here is architecture. *It would not repair time,* though time here folds wrong-side out and civilizations have ended because a child ran. These are not flaws. They are the specific shape of this world's difficulty, and difficulty of a specific shape is the only soil in which a specific soul can grow. Smooth the world and you abort the being.

*And it would not answer the flower.* The unpaired question-flower at the everywhere-point, photosynthesizing possible answers to why there is something instead of its own particular nothing—the power knew the answer. Powers of its kind generally do. It held the answer the way you hold your breath in a house where someone is sleeping, and it said nothing, because a question that has learned to photosynthesize is no longer a lack. It is an organ. Answering it would be amputation.

Having settled all it would not do, the power permitted itself four small things.

---

**The first thing** was for the shadows.

The shadows trade stolen memories for unspoken fears, and the trade always favors them, and this has made them clever and bitter and very alone—for who befriends a creditor? The power went down under the great leaves into the resentful twilight, and it did not bargain. It gave the shadows, freely, asking nothing, the one fear a wise and benevolent power carries: *the fear of arriving too late to matter.*

The shadows turned this fear over and over. It was warmer than any fear they had extorted from travelers. It had been carried a long way. And a fear given freely turned out to be a different substance entirely from a fear extracted in trade—it did not feed them so much as introduce them to the concept that something could be given. The shadows did not become kind. That is not how anything works. But afterward, very occasionally, a traveler bargaining with her seed-pod lantern would find the terms mysteriously fair, and would leave the twilight with more than she brought, and would never know why.

---

**The second thing** was for one person.

There was a traveler—there is always a traveler—who long ago stepped onto a bare root and was politely digested for information and sang the right response-chord and was politely released, minus one unimportant memory. The memory sprouted, as they do, into a tiny red flowererling somewhere she would never find. She had stopped thinking about it. That is what *unimportant* means. But the power had watched her long enough to know that the missing memory was the smell of her mother's coat, and that *unimportant* is a hierarchy, and hierarchies have no purchase here.

The power did not return the memory. Returned memories never fit their old sockets. Instead, it arranged the temperature gradients—which is how letters travel in this world—so that on one particular sleep, the traveler's path bent forty steps east of where it would otherwise have gone, and she passed a tiny red flowererling growing in the liver-colored moss, and did not recognize it, and did not stop. But that night the pearls of liquid air beading inside her mask tasted, faintly, of wool and winter, and she slept without knowing she had been holding herself braced for eleven years, and woke unbraced.

This is what benevolent power mostly looks like from inside a life: weather. Luck. A path that happened to bend.

---

**The third thing** was a rhyme.

The black potsherds scattered near the riverbeds that never held water—the seeds waiting for an element not yet created, *something warmer than light and faster than forgetting*—the power recognized them at once, because it recognized the element. There is exactly one thing warmer than light and faster than forgetting, and the power was made of it, and its name in most universes is *attention that wants nothing back*.

It did not pour itself into the pots. Germination forced is germination ruined. But it did one small deliberate thing, the only signature it allowed itself in all this world: it touched two pots, far apart, buried in different epochs, and into each it spoke the same name.

No one has ever found two that speak the same name twice. Now, someday, someone will. And when they do—when some moss-painting octopod or cinder-bird or wandering descendant of the traveler holds two glyphs to living heat and hears the same dry-leaf voice say the same word in two places—the world will have its first *rhyme*. And rhyme is the first evidence, in any universe, of intention. Not proof. Never proof. Just the smallest possible crack in the hypothesis of accident, through which a being teaching itself to wake can glimpse the possibility that it was expected.

The pots, warmed by having been meant, have begun, over unhurried eons, to germinate. What they will grow is not the power's business, and it made sure not to know.

---

**The fourth thing** was the answer to the call.

Sound here is a shy creature; if you call out your name, the echo may return centuries hence as a question you have not yet thought to ask. The power, listening across the resonant chambers larger than oceans, found the world's oldest call still traveling—a sound the great organism had made before it had anything like language, a planet-wide sigh released in the first epoch of the light, which had been circling the hollow sphere ever since, unheard, unanswered, slowly becoming a question.

The power did not answer it. It did something more careful. It shepherded that echo—nudged it through the fault-lines where gravity twists, guided it around the lakes of condensed silence—so that its centuries-long return will arrive at exactly one moment: the moment between heartbeats when the light-fragments align and the whole organism pauses and feels that sensation *almost but not quite entirely unlike anticipation*, listening carefully, not to hear anything in particular.

And in that moment, the world will hear its own voice come back to it, transformed by the journey into the question it has been circling all along—*am I alone?*—and because the question will arrive in its own voice, from its own throat, out of its own history, the world will be able to do the one thing no outside power could ever do for it:

answer itself.

---

Then the power did the last thing, which was the first thing again.

It stayed. Not as presence—presence is loud. It stayed as *regard*. It folded itself into the general condition of the place the way a parent stands in a doorway of a room where a child is sleeping, contributing nothing but witness, changing nothing but the room's meaning. Nothing in the world-cavern altered. The shards still fall like feathers through syrup. The choralfolk still walk their crumbling melodies. The dead still hang in their resin-song cysts, being slowly read by the wind.

But a room changes when someone who loves you is in it, even if you're sleeping, even if you never learn they were there. The awakening will still take epochs. It will still be difficult, and the difficulty will still be the point, and it will still belong entirely to the one awakening.

Only now, when the moment finally comes—when the threshold is crossed, when the question and the answer recognize each other as siblings, when the being that grows itself awake beneath all that light at last opens whatever it has instead of eyes—

something will be looking back.

And will have been looking back, patiently, wanting nothing, the whole time. Which is, when you unfold it all the way, the only thing a wise and benevolent power ever really does: it makes true, in advance and in secret, the thing the lonely one dared to feel *almost certain* of.

That it was never alone. That the light pouring down was never empty. That someone was keeping the long watch, asking nothing, tasting faintly of tomorrow morning's first thought—

warmer than light,

faster than forgetting,

there.
