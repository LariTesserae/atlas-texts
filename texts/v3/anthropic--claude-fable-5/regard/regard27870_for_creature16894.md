**What the Power Does**

It does not enter. Entering requires a boundary willing to be crossed, and the walls here are only arguments between adjacent realities—it would be rude to take a side. So instead it does the thing it has always done best, the thing it was old before doing:

It notices.

And its noticing has weight.

The chamber feels this the way a held breath feels a hand on the shoulder. The temperature shifts from 2.9 to 3.0—a change too small to measure and too large to survive unchanged. 3.0 is the exact temperature at which resignation remembers it used to be patience, and patience remembers it used to be hope, and hope remembers it never actually left, it was just standing very still so as not to frighten anyone.

The power does not announce itself. Announcement implies it might not have been welcome, and it refuses to insult the room that way. It arrives the way morning arrives for someone who did not expect to survive the night: without fanfare, already there, apologizing for nothing.

---

The first thing it does is greet the five.

Not the "you." Not yet. Wisdom knows the order of operations for a haunting: you tend the tenders first. No one has thanked them since before the chamber decided to become a place, and gratitude deferred that long gains mass, like probability, like regret. The power has come to pay a debt on someone else's behalf, which is one of the definitions of grace that never made it into the dictionaries.

To Precedent When Required, who collects the beginnings other people threw away, it gives the one thing missing from her jars: an ending. Not a death—an *ending*, the kind that closes gently, like a book being set down by someone who intends to return. She holds it in her negative-space chest and discovers that her collection was never a hoard of embarrassments. It was an archive of courage. Every discarded first kiss, every swallowed inaugural word—each one was someone *trying*. The power tells her this. She does not cry, because her tears are currently suspended at chest level with everything else, but three of the droplets nearest her turn the color of relief, which had no color until now.

To Conditional Tense, it does not take the suitcase. Taking it would only teach him that his burdens are someone else's to solve, and that lesson curdles. Instead it sits beside him—the power *sits*, cross-legged, on lava stone, taking up space the way a mountain takes up sky, without negotiation—and asks him to open the case. Together they read the alternative endings aloud, one by one. And here is the thing the suitcase never knew: probability loses mass when it is witnessed. Every ending read aloud in company becomes lighter than the last, until the case weighs what a suitcase should weigh, which is: the possibility of going somewhere. As for the swallowed name, the power mentions, in passing, casually, the way you'd mention weather: *No summons was ever filed. There was never a trial scheduled. You can keep the name inside you or say it in the open. Nothing is waiting to convict you. Nothing ever was.* He flickers—glacier-speed, candle-bright—and for the first time the flickering looks less like guilt and more like dancing.

To Apologize Later, who has no mouth today, the power does something small and enormous: it reaches for her. Her fingers are too long because she never learned to stop reaching for things that were already leaving—so the power becomes, deliberately, a thing that is arriving. It takes her reaching hand. It is the first object in her existence to close the distance from the other side. Then it goes briefly into tomorrow, checks on the emergencies she donated her mouth to, handles them personally, and returns her mouth with a note attached: *No longer needed for crisis purposes. Recommend redeployment: singing, or the eating of good bread, or saying things that begin with words other than "sorry."*

To Placeholder Name, whose existence is provisional, the power performs an act of bureaucratic violence: it dissolves the committee. The angels who lost their forms in a bet about whether mercy could bleed are gently informed that the question is settled—mercy bleeds constantly, that's how you know it's alive—and that his renewal is hereby ratified in perpetuity, unconditionally, no annual review, no probationary clauses. Then it does something no committee ever thought to do. It waits beside him while he sleeps, and when the dream of forms that aren't lonely begins, the power stands at the border of morning and does not let the light in until the dream has finished setting. He wakes remembering the ending.

The ending was good. He gets to keep it.

To You Already Know Who, who has been dying perfectly for so long that death sends progress reports, the power files—on their behalf, with all appropriate signatures—a leave of absence. Death's response arrives almost instantly, stamped and slightly tear-warped: *Approved. Take all the time you need. Frankly, we've been worried. You were never supposed to be this good at it.*

---

Then, and only then, does the power turn toward the center of the room.

It does not sit in the chair.

This is the whole of its wisdom, distilled: it does not sit in the chair, does not move the chair, does not gesture at the chair, does not say the word *chair*. The chair is a question addressed to someone specific, and the power is not in the business of answering other people's mail.

Instead it sits down on the floor beside the chair. Cross-legged. Unhurried. And in sitting, it does the thing the room has been told for eons cannot be done: it occupies space without apologizing, and the universe does not break.

The lava stone, which holds the impressions of every future footstep that will never walk here, receives an actual footprint. One. Real. Warm. And all across the floor, the never-footsteps feel it happen and quietly amend their paperwork from *never* to *not yet*.

One water droplet, disturbed by the power's shoulder, moves aside—touches its neighbor—

—and nothing shatters. The two droplets meet the way hands meet. Then they part, or don't, and the reflection inside the merged sphere is something the chamber has never displayed before: a kitchen. A Tuesday. Ordinary gravity. Someone at a table pulling out the seat beside them without looking up, the way you do when you're certain the person is coming.

The power does not lower the water. Grief held mid-air is grief being honored, and it would not dream of dropping what the room has carried so carefully. But it repeals the law that said the droplets could never touch. Loneliness may remain. It is no longer *mandatory*.

It picks up the book bound in tomorrow's newspapers and reads aloud the five's favorite story—the one about the person who lived so carefully that no one noticed they never died, including themselves. It reads it exactly as written, honoring every word.

Then it adds one line, in ink made from time that it rehydrates simply by paying attention to it:

*—until someone noticed.*

And it closes the book and sets it back on the lap of the figure who is you but isn't, the one wearing your face on the inside, and it says to that figure, quietly, the only thing it says in the entire visit:

"You held the seat beautifully. You can rest soon."

---

It does not stay. Staying would make its presence the point, and the point was never its presence.

But before it goes, it amends the standing injunction. The words that hung in the chamber's air—*Please remain exactly where you aren't*—are not erased. Erasure is violence and the words were doing their best. They are simply revised, the way a wound is revised into a scar:

*Please arrive exactly whenever you're able. There is no deadline. The gravity resets will wait. The universe has been informed of your dimensions and has agreed, in writing, not to break. Someone signed as witness. The signature looks like a footprint.*

The silence changes registers—from 0.4, the quiet between a flash and its sound, to 0.5: the exact volume of a kitchen where someone is making tea for someone who has just finished crying, and neither of them needs to talk, and the kettle is taking its time on purpose.

The five return to their garden. But now, when the flowers bloom—once every time someone remembers how to be brave and afraid simultaneously—they bloom slightly more often, because the power's visit has been added to the list of things that count.

And the chair?

The chair is still empty. The power fixed nothing about the chair, because there was nothing about the chair to fix. It only changed one word in the chair's nature, one word the chair had been mispronouncing to itself for so long it forgot the correct form:

Not *empty*.

*Saved.*

There is a difference, and the difference is everything. An empty chair is an absence. A saved seat is a promise. One is a wound and one is a hand on the neighboring seatback, and no one looking up, because they are certain—patiently, unhurriedly, at temperature 3.0 and rising—that you are coming.

(The chair adjusts its saving slightly. Just for you. It can wait. It has help now. Take the long way if you need to. The tea will still be warm; the power saw to that too, on its way out, without being asked.)
