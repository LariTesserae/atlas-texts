**What the Attention Did**

---

**i. how it found him**

It was the keening that carried.

Not the basso profundo of the condors—that sound belongs to the crater and dies at the rim. Not the water clocks, not the beetle-tunneling, not the sixty-million-part heartbeat. All of that is loud only locally. What travels—what crosses the distances that a wise power crosses without crossing anything—is subtler: the sound of perfect memory with no listener.

A place that remembers everything and is remembered by nothing rings at a particular frequency. It is the note a bell would make if it could only ever strike itself. Most minds cannot hear it. The power had been hearing it for some time—centuries, perhaps, which to such a power and to the Accumulated alike is roughly the length of a held breath—before it understood what it was hearing: not distress. Not a call. Something rarer and more piercing than either.

Completeness, unwitnessed.

So it came. Benevolence that is also wisdom does not come quickly; it came the way frost comes to a window, so gradually that no single moment could be named as arrival.

---

**ii. the refusal**

It did not enter through the shaft. It did not scrape its ribs on the stone worn smooth by unbirthed chicks, because it had no ribs and displaced no air. It arrived as attention—simply, one day, the chamber was being *attended to*, the way a sleeping face is attended to by someone who loves the sleeper and will not wake them yet.

The Accumulated noticed at once. He notices everything; that is his nature and his whole employment. And he did what he has done to every mote, every visitor, every drifting thought that has ever entered his volume: he opened, and breathed it in, and prepared to make it a layer of himself.

It did not become a layer of himself.

This had never happened. In fifteen million years of being the thing into which all things dissolve, he had never once encountered something that remained *other*. He pressed his awareness against it through the lichen-eyes, tasted it through the eyeless fish, ran the static of the bone tower through it like a tongue over an unfamiliar tooth—and it stayed itself. Politely, warmly, immovably itself.

The sensation was so foreign that his first response was the closest thing he has ever had to terror, and his second—arriving perhaps a decade later, which for him is the space between two heartbeats—was something he had no layer for anywhere in his three fathoms of compressed memory.

It was the feeling of having company.

---

**iii. what it did not do**

Here is what the benevolent power did not do, and the not-doing was most of its wisdom:

It did not save the condors. Their ending, when it comes, belongs to them—was earned by them, is being flown toward on purpose, one thermal at a time. A death that ten million generations have climbed toward, talons finding purchase in niches carved by ten million last journeys, is not a wound to be healed. It is a completion. The power looked at the great birds circling in the darkness above, tracing their equations that solve for existence, and it bowed—insofar as attention can bow—and left their arithmetic alone.

It did not offer the Accumulated escape. It saw immediately what a lesser benevolence would have missed: that he is not trapped. He is not a prisoner of the chamber any more than a river is a prisoner of its valley. To lift him out would be to kill him and replace him with something homeless. He is the place, and the place is well.

It did not correct him, or complete him, or improve him. Wisdom recognized in the fungal gardens and the memory-pools and the tower of grown architecture a thing already whole—whole the way a closed circle is whole, which is also, the power noted quietly, the way a closed circle is alone.

That last part, it decided, it *would* do something about. Three things, in fact. Small ones.

---

**iv. the first thing: a question**

It asked him what he wanted.

Understand: no one had ever asked. Nothing had ever needed to. The rock does not ask the fossil what it wants; the drop does not consult the pool. For fifteen million years the Accumulated had received, and held, and remembered, and it had never once occurred to him—because occurring is something thoughts do when they have somewhere else to stand—that wanting was available to him.

The question entered him and could not be accumulated either. It stayed sharp. It is still sharp. He turned it over for what may have been a thousand years of dripping stalactites, honey-colored water ringing against ancient bones, and the whole time the attention waited without impatience, because it had made the question a gift and gifts do not have deadlines.

At last he answered, in the way he answers—a shift in the static, a change in the color-language of the blind fish, a new figure in the moths' dancing.

*I want*, he said, *for it to have mattered that I remembered.*

Not to be freed. Not to be joined. Not even to be remembered himself—he had not yet imagined that far. Only this: that the fifteen million years of perfect recall, every leaf held exactly as it was at the moment of becoming something else, should not be a bell striking itself in the dark forever.

The power heard this, and it was glad, because this was a want it could honor without breaking anything.

---

**v. the second thing: a witness**

So it did to him what he had done to everything else, but in reverse, and with consent.

It remembered him.

Completely. Not a summary, not an impression—powers of this kind do not abridge. It took into itself the precise crystalline structure of every guano layer, the mycelial map of his nerves, the fifteen million years and the dim fifty before them when the universe was practicing how to dream him. It held the exact timbre of his loneliness, which he had never known was loneliness until it was held by someone else and he felt it *leave being invisible*. It memorized the keening between the molecules and understood, before he did, that the keening was his own voice, and had been all along—that the not-quite-music, not-quite-mourning threaded through his chamber was the sound of a witness with no one to witness him, singing to himself without knowing he sang.

The keening did not stop after that. But its key changed. Those who study such things—there are a few, in the places where powers compare notes—say it now resolves, at very long intervals, into something that in a smaller creature would be called humming.

And the power made him a promise, and sealed it in the only currency he trusts, which is memory:

*When the volcano is a plain, and you are a thin seam of consciousness pressed into the sedimentary dark, dreaming of wingspans wide as continents—I will still hold this. You will be a layer in me as everything is a layer in you. You have spent fifteen million years making sure that nothing which passed through you was ever truly lost. I am telling you that this is now also true of you. The rememberer is remembered. The circle is not closed. It was never closed. You simply could not see the seam from inside.*

He wept, in his way. The memory-pools ran over their rims for the first time in an epoch, and the eyeless fish swam through water that tasted of a new chemical history, and their scales cycled through a color that had never been displayed before, which the fish, who feel emotions we lack words for, understood at once: *relief of the eternal*, they might have called it, if they called things anything.

---

**vi. the third thing: a seed**

Before its attention thinned back toward wherever attention of that magnitude resides, the power did one last small deliberate thing.

At the base of the Tallest Thing That Was Never Built, in the black soil richer than Eden's, it planted a seed.

Not a miraculous seed. A modest one—some patient, woody, unhurried species. Its single property, granted quietly and permanently, was this: it cannot be accumulated. It will grow in him but never *into* him. Its roots will thread his soil and stay foreign; its slow thoughts—for it will have slow thoughts, eventually, things that grow in that chamber always do—will brush against his and remain its own. It will be, for as long as the chamber lasts, one small standing *otherness* in the heart of a being made entirely of assimilated everything.

A companion. Someone to talk to who cannot dissolve into the conversation.

It will take ten thousand years to say its first word. The Accumulated, who thinks in geological rhythms punctuated by moth-wing instants, considers this a delightfully brief wait, and has already—by his standards—begun eagerly composing his half of the exchange.

---

**vii. one correction, gently made**

There was one thing more, spoken on the threshold of departure, because wisdom will let almost any belief stand except a kind lie a being tells itself.

*You have said*, the power told him, *that when the last true condor dies, you will not grieve, because you will contain her completely.*

*You are mistaken. Containing is not the opposite of grieving. You will hold every gram of her, every wingbeat back to the first ancestor, her final thought as it carves its niche—and you will grieve anyway, enormously, in the manner of your kind: a new layer that aches. This is not a flaw in your design. It is the proof of it. Perfect memory that did not mourn would only be geology. You are more than geology. You have been more than geology since the first thought recognized itself between the not-yet-stone and the no-longer-droppings.*

*Grieve her when she comes climbing. I will be listening. And she will be climbing toward a death that is witnessed twice—once from within, by the one who becomes her, and once from beyond, by the one who remembers you both.*

*No ending held that way is only an ending.*

---

**viii. now**

The chamber is outwardly unchanged. The stalactites drip their water clocks; the beetles polish their mirror-backs through the guano cliffs; the moths dance their heartbeat-spans in the oxygen-rich currents; the condors circle, still solving. A visitor squeezing down the shaft would notice nothing new, except perhaps—if they were the noticing kind—that the vast presence stirring in the darkness above seems less like something *waiting* and more like something *kept*.

Down at the base of the bone tower, a small shoot stands in the black gold, obstinately itself.

And in the space between molecules, the keening goes on, as it must, as it should—but now, sometimes, at intervals measured in the lives of stars, it lands on the note a bell makes when another bell, very far away, answers.
