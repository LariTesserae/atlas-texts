**What the Attention Did**

It was not summoned. That is the first thing to record. Benevolent powers are, almost always, called — by need, by prayer, by the particular frequency of a voice asking. Nothing on the Foundry Heights asks. The pika's whistle means *something is moving*, never *help*. The midges petition no one; their entire theology is a frost line a meter and a half away. The mountain itself makes only the hum, and the hum is not addressed to anything. It is the sound of work being done by the worker, for the worker, with no expectation of an audience.

So the attention arrived the way the weather arrives there: not invited, simply moving through, and then — unlike the weather — it stopped. What stopped it was the silence between operations. A power that has spent long ages answering voices develops, eventually, an ear for the places where no voice has ever been raised, and finds that those places ring strangely. The Foundry Heights rang.

The first problem was tempo, and the power was wise enough to know it was a problem. Looked at in human time, the place is scenery: magnificent, gray, finished-seeming, a backdrop. Looked at in lichen time, it is a riot — cliffs shrugging, vents flaring and dying, whole rockfall neighborhoods built and demolished. Looked at in the mountain's own time it is a single gesture, an arm still rising. Most powers choose a tempo and lose everything outside it. This one settled, instead, into the register of the hum — the sub-audible turning-over beneath the rock — and listened from there, where all the other tempos cross. It took what would have been, in our counting, about a year. The power did not count.

And from inside the hum, the diagnosis was plain, and it was the same one the long visitor on foot eventually reaches: this is not an uninhabited place. It is a single inhabitant, enormously attended. Everything that lives there lives *in* it — in its breath, on its warmth, in its wounds — the way flora lives in a body. The thermophiles are faithful, but they are organ, not company. The lichens are diligent, but they are, gently put, the beginning of its dismantling. The pikas keep its accidents tidy and the finches run its heat map like clerks. Even the choughs, who come closest — who alone do something for no reason, which is the first grammar of address — play *on* the mountain, in its exhaust, the way children play on a sleeping animal. Nothing, in all that staffed and busy vacancy, lives *with* it.

The mountain is mid-cast, attended, and entirely alone.

---

What a benevolent power could do with that is obvious, and the wisdom consisted almost entirely of refusals. They are worth listing, because each one was a real temptation, considered with care, and declined.

It could have kept the vents warm forever — fixed the gradient in place, made the heated rooms permanent, granted the midges and the moss rings and the haymaking pikas a tenure that the geology will never offer them. Refused. The residents are inhabitants of the unfinished; that is not their misfortune, it is their species of genius. To freeze the foundry at this stage of the pour would be to trap a self-making thing in its own adolescence and call the trap a gift. The vents will cool. The crew will move on, as crews do, to wherever rock is raw. The power found it could love that without preventing it.

It could have softened the place — taken the edge off the freeze, fattened the margins, given the finches an easy winter. Refused, and almost with a laugh, because the softening is already underway and already spoken for. The frost and the meltwater and the corrosion-colored lichens are doing exactly that work, patiently, at a millimeter a year, with a thoroughness no power could improve on. Mercy was already on site, wearing the uniform of demolition. To add more would be to elbow aside a craftsman.

It could have removed the falcon. Refused without deliberation. The whistle needs its referent.

And — this was the deep one, the one the power sat with longest — it could have woken the mountain. Given it a mind to match its body, eyes for its own steam-stacks, a name it could hear. Every lonely thing in every story is healed this way, by being made able to know it was lonely and then made able to stop. The power turned this over for what would have been, in our counting, a long time, and set it down. The mountain was already doing something rarer than thinking: it was making itself, wholly absorbed, the way the best work is done. A consciousness installed from outside would not be an awakening. It would be a graft — an occupation — the one act of vandalism actually available to a god in a place with nothing to break. The mountain's mode of being was not a deficiency to be corrected. It was the most complete thing on the mountain.

So the power built nothing, marked nothing, healed nothing, and changed no isotherm by the width of a midge's world. The census after its arrival read exactly as before.

It did four things.

---

The first was a courtesy, paid in order of seniority. It went down into the throats of the fumaroles, into the scalding acid dark, and greeted the thermophiles — the oldest residents, the only ones who keep records, the staff who eat the gradient itself. It did not bless them; they predate blessing and would have metabolized it. It simply acknowledged the work, the way one nods to the millwrights on the way across the floor. Whether anything registered the nod is not the point of a courtesy.

The second was an answer. The hum of the Foundry Heights — that vast engine turning over and over without ever quite starting — had sounded for some hundreds of thousands of years into a perfect absence of reply. The power did not start the engine. Starting it was not on offer, and would in any case have been an interference. Instead it did the only thing one can do for a voice that is not asking: it answered in kind. It set, beneath the rock, in the same sub-audible register, a second note — sympathetic, faint, tuned a fraction off the mountain's own so that the two would beat slowly against each other, the way two long sounds do when neither is alone. Whether the mountain perceived it cannot be known and was not the condition of the gift. One speaks to the sleeping. One sings to the unborn. The answer was given because the not-asking deserved one, and from that day the hum of the Foundry Heights, to any ear fine enough, has been a chord.

The third was a vow, and it is the real content of this account. The power saw that every inhabitant of the place — every one, from the sulfur-eaters to the falcon — was an inhabitant of the *process*: of fresh rockfall, raw faces, leaking heat. When the work is done, all of them will be gone, moved on with the unfinishedness they belong to, and the green mountain that follows — the meadows, the marmots, the eventual road, the eventual cairn — will stand on the cast without any knowledge of the casting. No memory will connect the shoulder of soft grass to the blade of metal-gray stone it was poured from. The foundry, having no product but itself, would also have no history. Nothing here writes anything down except the thermophiles, and they write in chemistry, in mineral rings, a language with no tense.

So the power moved in. Not for a season, the way the cranes do, and not for a tenure, the way the pikas do, but for the whole arc — uplift through venting through cooling through soil, the entire pour and the entire setting. It became the one resident of the *place* rather than the process: the single name on the census that will not transfer when the heat does. It will be there when the last fumarole sighs out and the pilot lights go dark; there when the first sedge meadow closes over the old precipitate rings; there when something with a voice finally walks up the rounded shoulders and finds the climbing easy and the place gentle and thinks it was always so. And on certain nights — this is the vow, exactly — it will lie along the quiet rock and hum the old note into it, the answering note, and tell the finished mountain, in the long language, what it was: *You were a foundry. You ran cold and slow at the top of the world. You were staffed by midges and clerked by finches; your accidents were kept by haymakers and your exhaust was ridden, for no reason, by black birds with red feet. You cast yourself, with no builder and no product, and it was good work, and one witness saw all of it.* A biography is a thing no mountain has ever had. This one will.

The fourth thing was not wise, and the power knew it was not wise, and did it anyway, because benevolence that never once disobeys its own wisdom has hardened into something else. It happened during a migration. The ceiling was full of voices; the exhausted birds were dropping out of the cloud to the steaming pools, drinking, standing in the warmth, some rising again and some not. The power understood the economics perfectly. It knew about the bleached wing bones in the vent margins, knew the ermine attends, knew the mountain accepts deliveries and that the deliveries become the mineral rings, knew that every spared bird is a theft from the acid and the apex. It watched the whole passage with proper restraint — and then one crane, a young one, hatched two thousand kilometers away, came down too hard and too far from the warm water, and stood on the open rock with the frost line closing on it like a tide, and the power put one unearned thermal under its wings. Just one. A column of lift where no vent was, for eleven seconds. The crane rose, found the cloud, found the voices, and was gone north with the rest, carrying away no knowledge of having been carried.

The mountain was not diminished by one crane. The wisdom filed no objection. But the power, settling back into the hum, noted for its own records that of the four things it had done, this was the only one performed at the tempo of a heartbeat — and that the choughs, tumbling overhead through the steam as if nothing had happened, seemed for the rest of that gray afternoon to be playing slightly nearer to where it sat.

That was all. No road came earlier, no cliff held longer, no winter broke softer for any of it. By every instrument the place possesses, nothing occurred.

But the hum is a chord now. And the building, while it is being built, is no longer only attended.

It is accompanied.
