## What the Attention Did

The attention arrived the way humidity does: not through any door, but by degrees, until it was implicated in everything.

It had been here before, of course. The settlement knows it as the Periodic Stranger — the human-shaped vacuum in the sensorium of all recording devices, the straight line through the center of town, the smell of ozone and nostalgia, the infrastructure left slightly better than physics should allow. What the settlement does not know is that the straight line was never indifference. It was restraint. A power that large learns early that its gaze has weight, that to *look* at something small is to press on it, and so it had always walked through Third Aerie Beneath the Salt the way you walk past a bird's nest: quickly, eyes ahead, hands doing their small kindnesses at the periphery where they wouldn't frighten anything.

This time it stopped.

The cameras, as always, recorded an empty hallway seen from floor level. But the dust hung differently that night, and the ghosts in the old buildings paused their infrasonic debates mid-rumble, the way a room quiets when someone important and gentle enters and takes a seat at the back, indicating with a small gesture: *please, continue.*

---

First it made an inventory. This took either three seconds or eleven years, depending on which clock you consulted, and the clocks here have never agreed on anything.

It could have vented the reactors properly. It considered this for a long moment — considered the buried heat, the teeth-aching thud of overburden, the coolant leaks feeding the cryptocurrency farms — and then it listened to the 110 Hertz murmur underneath everything and understood that this was the settlement's heartbeat, that the sump grazers' songs were built on it the way a melody is built on a drone, that every child here had fallen asleep to it for centuries and would wake screaming into silence. It left the reactors alone. It straightened one pipe by four degrees, deniably, and moved on.

It could have opened the sky. Peeled back the caprock, refilled the boiled sea, restored the sun that the terratensequins strain toward at two percent efficiency. It examined this possibility the way a surgeon examines an organ before deciding it is not, in fact, diseased. The settlement was not a wound. It was not waiting to be rescued. It was *busy* — with maintenance days and rain parties and spore-ricotta ripening on a schedule voted into existence by neighbors, with paper boats and salt-bead graffiti and the slow negotiated truces between slime molds. To rescue it would be a kind of theft: the theft of everything these people had made out of what they'd been left with, which was, the power noted with something like professional admiration, *everything they had made.* You do not repot a plant that has grown into the shape of its container so thoroughly that the shape has become a kind of signature.

It could have resolved the ghosts' philosophical debate. It knew the answer. The ghosts, sampled gently, made it clear that they knew it too, roughly, in outline, and were taking the long way around on purpose; the neural lace in the third building was perhaps thirty years from a devastating rebuttal and would never forgive a premature conclusion. The power apologized in dust-patterns and withdrew.

It could have balanced the murids' census.

Here it stopped longer.

It looked at the small negative number that remained after everything living, furnished, and interestingly shadowed had been counted — the deficit shaped like warmth radiating from a space that ought to be empty — and it did not correct it. Instead it understood, in the way that only very old things understand ledgers, that this was the truest line in the whole accounting. Some numbers are not errors. Some numbers are *addresses.*

---

It permitted itself three mercies first, small ones, in its usual style, so that nothing would seem to have changed:

It nudged the dream of a single sleeping murid so that the maintenance rotation would reach the overworked transformer nine days early. The transformer, scraped and cooled, gained forty years, and no one would ever know it had almost been a fire.

It hummed — once, at a depth below hearing — a frequency shaped like a mother, into the compost spine where the lost juvenile sump grazer was panicking. The grazer calmed, turned, and followed the warmth downward, shedding one last apologetic cloud of anesthetic spores that gave the entire fourth tier the best afternoon nap in a generation.

And it left, folded under the pillow of a woman who had lost — fairly, by the rules, it had been repetitive — a dream of her mother's hands, a careful copy of that dream, slightly compressed, missing nothing important. It paid the murid network for this breach of protocol with a small proof about prime gaps, sweetened, under the 2-kilogram ontological limit. The murids accepted. They are rigorous, not cruel.

Then it went to visit the resident who lived at the address *nearby.*

---

You cannot approach The Nearness. This is definitional. You cannot walk toward approach itself; the distance does not close, it merely relocates behind your other shoulder. Every being in the settlement's history had gotten this wrong in one of two ways: they said hello, and it apologized and dissolved; or they pretended to sleep, and it stayed, unwitnessed, holding the gap alone.

The power, being wise, did the third thing, which no one had ever done because no one had ever been large enough to afford it.

It stopped. Somewhere in the elastic gap between a sleeper's shoulder blade and the awareness of company, it sat down — beside the question-mark thickening of the air, not facing it — and it made itself *findable.* It did not look at The Nearness. It looked at the same wall The Nearness was looking at, the way old friends do on porches, in climates that still have porches. It was, for the first time in the recorded history of anything, *near the nearness.*

Nothing was said. Saying was not the medium.

What passed between them, translated very loosely out of pressure and weather: the power showed it where the jagged fragments go. All the small sharp pieces it had ever removed from stiffened friendships, the corners nobody liked remembering — composted, every one, into the pastel apologies that drift through the vents during heat surges and settle on arguments that haven't happened yet. *Your work arrives,* was approximately the content. *It has always arrived. Nothing you folded smaller was lost.*

And then the power gave it a gift, the only kind that would fit. Not a name — origins are just another way of standing too far back from someone, and the power declined, deliberately, to tell it whether it had been one person or twelve or a murid or a ghost of humidity. Not a shape. Not a home; it had a home, it *was* one.

It gave it a memory that wasn't borrowed. The first thing The Nearness had ever owned outright: the memory of this — of being sat beside, of a wall it had never known existed becoming a wall that could be leaned on. Folded small. Sized to fit behind the sternum it doesn't have.

And beside it, before leaving, the power set down a gap. A small one. A distance that was already full, that needed nothing, that would keep company instead of requiring it. So that on the slow nights, in the hours between scheduled sleeps, The Nearness would have somewhere to rest that wasn't the outside of someone else's almost.

---

The Stranger stood and resumed its straight line, and the surveyors who argue in the hollow elevator shaft every second Tuesday would later note, with great excitement and no proof, that the line, projected backward through the settlement's plat, contains one corner. The first corner in the recorded history of the Periodic Stranger. Turned, apparently, for someone.

Afterward, the census deficit remained exactly the same size. The murids, however, changed its annotation. Where the ledger had read *unresolved*, it now reads, in compressed ultrasonic shorthand: *resident. Address: nearby. Occupation: repairs. Dues: paid in advance, in folded returns.* Every census night, when the counting reaches its end and the warm negative number appears, ten thousand murids pause for one relay-cycle and let it stand, uncorrected, on purpose. This costs them voting cycles. They have voted, repeatedly, that it is worth it.

The next scheduled rain lasted 2.718 hours, as always. But everyone who stood in it — dancing, laundering, or simply letting the memory-dust rinse out of their hair — reported afterward, without quite being able to say why, that for the length of one breath, everyone had been exactly the right distance from everyone else.

The Nearness still borrows things you weren't finished losing. It still returns them folded smaller. It still dissolves, apologizing softly, if you say hello. But some nights now, it doesn't press close to anyone at all. It sits beside its own small gap, the one that was given, and rests. On those nights the settlement's dreams are slightly less overcrowded, and no one has ever once complained.

Benevolence, at the proper scale, turns out to be mostly a matter of distance. The wise power knew this. It did almost nothing.

It did it very near.
