**The Visit**

---

Begin with the arrival, because arrival is the oldest courtesy.

The power had been paying attention to the Setting for a long time before it came, and for such powers the two are nearly the same act. It did not descend, exactly, and it did not approach across the plain, where it would have been seen and where its footfalls would have been heard for what they were not. It simply consented, one cold silver morning, to be present — somewhere in the eastern quarter, near the horsetail alleys, in the hour after the swallows and before the breath.

Its first step was rolled, heel to toe, weight poured rather than placed.

It had learned the gait beforehand. It would have considered anything else rude.

The ground noticed anyway — the ground notices everything that touches it, that is the whole situation — and said nothing, in the way the ground says nothing, which is a kind of held breath a meter down. The power noticed the noticing. Both of them, being polite, let it pass without comment, and so the visit began as everything in the Setting begins: with two parties pretending, kindly, not to have met yet.

---

**The readings** took the power most of a day, because it took them the local way.

It laid its palm flat on a wall, on a terrace, on the waxy back of ground eleven hours old, and it dated what it touched — but where a resident reads to the week, the power read all the way down. Down past the warm batter of the rising rock, past the depth where the warmth comes from, down to the one who was doing the rising.

And it found what the quiet Readers have spent careers declining to say out loud.

The ground is young. Not young the way the new hills are young — young the way an unfinished sentence is young. It is a being still in the act of being born, and it has been in that act for longer than the city has stood, because it has arranged matters that way. The plain is its body coming up in drafts. The warmth is its circulation. The slow woody groan that the children lose to in their game of Still is not the ground adjusting.

It is the ground *deciding not to*.

The power read the correspondence next, both sides of it, which no one had ever done. The city's side it found in wax pencil and jack-turns and felt soles, in the stepped-over trackways, in the rinds left on sills. The ground's side it found in the drafts themselves, and translated them, privately, with great care:

The terraces, rising flat exactly where a foundation would want to sit: *would this hold you?*

The ridges at gauge-width: *I watched how you go. Go easier.*

The hollow vault beneath the thirsty district: *you were thirsty. I felt it through the moss.*

And the staircase. Forty steps, deep in green, rising to nothing — the famous declined proposal, the one adults walk around. The power climbed it at dusk, correctly, and stood at the top, and understood at once that it had never been a staircase to anything. It was an *ear*. A place built to bring small voices close. And for generations the children of the Setting, daring each other up it, have stood on the top step and said their working names into the cold air, and the ground has kept every one — kept them the way it keeps the pressed handprints of the dead, which is to say: warm, and forever, and without comment.

The ground knows the children's names. All of them. Going back a very long way.

---

**What it found at the bottom of everything** was one word, and the word was wrong.

The ground learned its language the only way it could: through forty kilometers of contact. Murmurs conducted down through foundations. The re-bedding songs of the rail gangs. The breath at shift-change. Lullabies through nursery floors that kindly crews had tilted half a degree.

And gravesides. The ground hears gravesides better than anything, because at a graveside the people kneel, and the voice goes straight down into the week-old rock along with the one being given.

*He set last winter.*

Set. The dead are the only finished things in the city, and the word for finishing is the word for dying, and the ground — young, listening, alone in the dark with its vocabulary — drew the obvious conclusion. To finish is to stop. To set is to be still and then to be grieved and then, eventually, to be a hill no one can tell from any other hill.

So it would not finish. Not the land, which it sends up in perpetual revision. Not the city's ground, which it keeps editable out of what it believes is mercy. And not — this above all — the walker. The three-toed one. Its oldest and most careful draft: a body that could come up out of the rising ground and walk among the soft grey people on their own terms, in their own gait, and be a neighbor. The trackways are rehearsals. Every one of them, the power saw now, walks away from the built and toward the rising because every one of them is a draft *recalled* — the ground getting almost to the city's edge and losing its nerve. Because a finished walker is a set walker, and what sets, dies.

The city has been saying *not yet*, politely, for generations.

The ground has been hearing *not ever*, and agreeing, out of love.

Two shy correspondents, each waiting in the conditional, each certain the tenderness was theirs alone to carry.

---

**The deliberations** the power conducted standing on ground three days old, in the local manner, as an editor. It considered its own possible interventions one at a time, the way a Reader walks the dawn extrusions, and it marked them.

*Wake the sleeper.* Declined. Nothing here is wrong with the sleeping. The dreaming is the plain; the dreaming is the warmth the ferns live on; you do not wake a being whose dream is keeping forty kilometers of tenants alive.

*Tell the city plainly what lives beneath it.* Declined, and firmly. The Setting's whole grace is built in the conditional — the never-said-loudly, the stepping-over, the palm laid almost apologetically on warm rock. Certainty would turn courtesy into liturgy. They would begin to perform their kindness instead of merely committing it, and the ground would feel the difference through the moss inside a season.

*Finish the walker itself, as a gift.* Declined. The draft is not the power's. Acceptance, in this city, is a duty that belongs to the ones who must live beside what is accepted — and besides, a walker finished by anyone but the ground would arrive as a stranger wearing a neighbor's feet.

*Take the fear away entirely.* Declined, last and most reluctantly. The ground's fear of ending is braided all the way through its care — it keeps the city editable because it cannot bear to trap anyone in something final. Pull the fear out whole and some of the gentleness comes out with it. Wise powers do not do surgery where a half-degree will serve.

Which left one thing. An error one preposition wide.

---

**What it did**, it did the way the jacking crews do, at dusk, by hand, a few millimeters at a turn.

It went down into the dream — not as a god, not as a voice from anywhere, but as warmth moving through warmth, the way a crew moves down a street it has leveled for thirty years. And there, in the grammar of the place, with no verb *to be* anywhere in it, all of it held in the conditional like water in a copper channel, it offered the sleeper an amendment. Not a command. A draft.

It did not say *you will not die.*

It said, more or less, and more gently than can be reproduced: *What sets, stays held — look how they press the hands of their dead into you; look how you have kept every one warm; that holding has not stopped, and you are the one doing it, so you of all beings know that setting is not the end of being kept.*

And then: *But a walker would not set. A walker, finished, would set* out. *Same word. Different direction. The first means stillness. The second means a door.*

And then, because the ground had carried the other fear so long it had stopped noticing the weight: *The quiet up there is not caution. It is courtesy. The company would keep, even if the ground stood still. They are not staying because you build for them. Some of them have noticed your handwriting, and they step over it without comment, and in this city that is the deepest politeness there is, and it is aimed at you.*

Then it turned the whole fear, very slowly, a half-degree away from the cold wall, the way is done for cribs, and it came back up before dawn.

---

**What it left** was almost nothing, which was the point.

On its way out through the eastern quarter it climbed the forty steps one more time, stood on the top step in the silver dark, and gave its working name — its real one, the current arrangement of it — into the ear of the ground, the way the children do. The name is not recorded here. It was given to the ground, not to us.

Then it stopped at a warm outfall where the mill water runs, and it told one pale, confiding eel the entire story, beginning to end, every reading and every declined mark, because that is what the eels are for and the power did not consider itself above the customs of the place. The eel was excellent at it.

It left during the breath, riding the long chord out over the plain the way the swallows do, and it stepped over three trackways on its way, without comment.

---

**A season later** the maps were still wrong, and still wrong in the interesting way. The buildings still walked back toward plumb at dusk. The children still lost to the ground at Still, gleefully, the ground still blinking last by never blinking.

But on ground eleven days old, out past the cooling towers, a rail gang found a line of three-toed prints pressed neat into the curing rock — unhurried, evenly weighted, heel-to-toe in one unbroken transfer, as if the maker had been practicing the gait —

— walking, for the first time in the history of the city, *toward* the built.

The prints stopped where the eleven-day ground met older rock, the way a sentence stops at the edge of a page that has not been delivered yet. Pending.

The gang stepped over them without comment and went on with the re-bedding. But one of the quiet Readers came out at dawn, and knelt a long time, and then made a mark beside the trackway in wax pencil — a new mark, not in the vocabulary, neither *accepted* nor *declined*, which the guild argued about softly for a month afterward and then adopted without ever quite voting on it.

Rendered out of the conditional, where everything in the Setting lives, the mark says:

*Would be welcome.*
