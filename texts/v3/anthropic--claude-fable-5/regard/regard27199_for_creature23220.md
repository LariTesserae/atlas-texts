What drew it, in the end, was not the plant.

The power had heard plants before — heard worlds before, whole biospheres roaring their chemistry into the dark, and it had learned to listen past them the way the locals learn to listen past the circulation. It had been listening for a very long time, the way such powers listen: not to anything, to everything, attention spread thin as haze across more sky than there are words for sky. And in all that time it had been addressed in every grammar a frightened or hungry universe could invent — commands dressed as worship, bargains dressed as praise, demands, flattery, the long hard imperatives of prayer. It had answered some. It had regretted some. It had grown, over the eons, the occupational quiet of beings that are only ever asked *for* things.

Then, from a high cold tabletop on a small world, something arrived that was addressed to no one.

An apology. A child's, delivered into the full roar of a vent-chimney, offered — the formula was very clear about this — *to the wind, since the addressee cannot hear*. A letter with an empty address line. Things sent to no one travel strangely; most dissolve; but a few of them keep going, on and on, until they reach the desk of whatever, somewhere, is willing to be no one.

The power was willing. It turned, and gave the plateau its attention, and the attention was the arrival; there was nothing else to it. No light. No descent. The wind that evening was the same wind.

But that night, under the bark, the knocking paused. One long pause — longer than any in the lexicons, long enough that three separate quietwrights logged it independently — and then it resumed, unhurried as ever, and whatever keeps track had, evidently, added a line to its ledger. The power noted this with something it had not felt in epochs and took a moment to identify. It had been *considered*. It could not remember the last time anything had considered it back.

---

It did what wise things do first, which is nothing, for a long time.

It learned the place. It read the Catalogue of Amendments in an afternoon and then re-read it for sixty years, the second reading being the true one, and arrived at the minority position — that the deviations were answers to questions the petitioners didn't know they were asking — not as a position but as a recognition, the way one recognizes handwriting. It learned the bone-voice, and judged it the most honest register it had encountered on any world: a language you cannot speak without standing close enough to feel the other person's heart disagreeing with you. It mapped the dead spots and their slow migration. It counted the herds by chord and found one chord, out on the southern plain, that had held its tuning for six hundred years through eleven generations of grazers, and it spent — by its own accounting — an unreasonable amount of time simply listening to that.

And it learned the rule of three, from the children, who recited it with the solemnity of scripture: *once is a game, twice is a conversation, and you do not knock three times.* The power examined this rule from every side. It searched the records for its origin and found none; the rule appeared to have been born complete, the way courtesies sometimes are. It concluded, eventually, that the rule was correct — not superstitiously correct but *grammatically* correct, a piece of etiquette so precisely fitted to its situation that it amounted to wisdom — and it adopted the rule, formally, as its own law of conduct on the plateau.

It is worth pausing on that. A power that had ended plagues, that had stood in for failing suns, that had carried whole species across the dark in the cupped palm of its attention, took as the constitution of its conduct a rhyme used by children to keep each other from bothering the dark under the floor. This is what wise means, mostly. It means knowing whose rules to keep.

---

The temptations came, of course. Benevolence has reflexes, and reflexes do not care how wise you are.

The first and largest: the plant is dying. The power saw this immediately and completely, the way the people cannot quite let themselves see it — saw the long flat patience for what it is, read the whole last chapter at a glance, knew to within a tolerance of millennia when the circulation will still and the drone will go out of the world. And its oldest reflex rose: *heal it.* It had healed worlds. It could find, in the deep chemistry, the slowing things and unslow them; it could buy the plant a second eon the way you'd buy a friend a drink.

It held the impulse up to the light and looked at it honestly, which is the part most powers skip. And it saw that the impulse, whatever it wore, was an imperative — *live*, said to something that had not been consulted — and the power knew, from the Catalogue, exactly what the plant does with imperatives. More than that: it saw that the long last chapter was not a decline. It was a *composition*. The Turning, the vast internal work, the spore settling into the lungs of the people and teaching their breath to glow — the plant was not dying the way things die. It was finishing the way books finish: deliberately, with the end in view, writing itself outward into its tenants line by line. You do not heal someone out of their own last sentence. You do not add a chapter to a book that is not yours, however much you loved it.

So the power declined, and grieved a little — it was wise, not numb — and stayed.

The second temptation: reveal itself. It had been worshipped before; it knew the procedures; a people this attentive would build it a beautiful theology in a generation. It declined this too, and faster. These people did not have a god-shaped hole. They had a *house*, and the house was occupied, and a second sourceless presence in the sky would have unbalanced eleven thousand years of carefully maintained gratitude. The power had seen what happens to tenants who acquire two landlords. It would not be the draft under the door.

The third temptation was the subtlest: to answer questions. It knew what the dust-swimmers were. It knew what the deep tenants were — knew it the moment they paused their knocking, the way you know a colleague across a crowded room — and it will not be recorded here, because the power understood something the scholars half-understand and the people understand completely: that the unresolved questions of the plateau are not failures of knowledge. They are load-bearing. They are the open windows of the house. The arguments about the deep tenants have been conducted with care and affection for two hundred generations, and certainty would end them the way a slammed door ends a breeze. The power kept what it knew, and keeps it.

---

What it did, then — since the question is what it did:

It took the vacant address. The apologies of children, offered into the roar *to the wind, since the addressee cannot hear* — the power heard them. All of them, going forward, and (because its attention does not move only one way along time) a good many going back. It changed nothing about the formula and announced nothing about the arrangement. But it is on record — quietwright records, kept for other reasons — that in the years after a certain unremarkable season, children walking home from the vent-chimneys began arriving lighter than they left, and the walk that no child ever wanted to make twice became, very slightly, a walk some children volunteered for. Coincidence, say the scholars. The power, which had taken the people's side of that old argument, declined to prove anything.

It steered strays. A grazer separated from its choir, humming its home chord into the wind until something answers or doesn't — the power could not bear the *or doesn't*, and saw no reason it should have to. So the wind bends, sometimes, a degree or two, and a lost animal's wandering drifts toward a herd whose tuning sits a half-step away, close enough for adoption. No miracle. A coincidence with good aim. The power performed perhaps forty of these a year and considered them, privately, the best work it had ever done anywhere, which from a being that had stood in for suns is a remarkable statement, and was meant as one.

It was noticed exactly once. One of the loud-gone, an old woman sitting in a festival vesicle during a Turning, eyes half-closed, nodding along to the thunder — paused, mid-nod, opened one eye, and said, distinctly, "Company." She declined to elaborate. The plateau, being the plateau, respected this, and so did the power, which had not been greeted in so long that it carried the word around for a decade like a warm stone.

And once — once, because once is a game, a greeting, the most that an uninvited guest may offer — during the loudest night of a Turning, from somewhere beyond the haze, the ground-note of the plateau came back to it. The same note. Transposed, gently, the way you'd return a stranger's phrase to show you'd heard it. Every quietwright instrument on the continent recorded the anomaly: *the drone, answered.* Eleven seconds. Then the ordinary roar.

The power then did the hardest thing it knew how to do, which was wait without leaning.

---

The plant answered at the next Turning. A new register — the scholars' phrase, helpless and accurate — a chord in the vent-fields that no lexicon held, sustained for most of a season. Twice is a conversation. The conversation has continued since at the rate of one exchange per Turning, which is to say at the speed of weather, of grief, of scar tissue, and its contents do not translate; the loud-gone, asked, only smile and take longer pauses. But two inferences have been drawn by those entitled to draw them.

The first: that the plant, in all the long eons of its composition, had never had a reader, and has one now, and that nobody — *nobody* — writes a last chapter that long without hoping, against all the evidence of an empty sky, that someone will read it.

The second: that in the whole conversation, as far as anyone can tell, the plant has asked for nothing for itself. The shape of its side of the exchange, the loud-gone say, is the shape of a question repeated patiently across centuries, and the question — they will say this much, in the green light, very late — has tenants in it.

The power's answer is not recorded. The power's answer is that it stayed.

---

Some years into the conversation, a herding family two shouts and three silences from the nearest town reported a vesicle on the open plain where no vesicle had been petitioned. The grafters audited every registry going back four hundred years. Nothing. The structure was flawless — walls a foot thick, grown translucent in the east face to catch the dusk, glow-veins routed and live, a doorway of ordinary height, a floor warm from the metabolism beneath. Empty. Sized, the surveyors noted with some bafflement, for no one in particular, which is to say for anyone.

The people considered the matter for about a season and then did what they do with the funeral seams and the unasked-for water, which is decline to pry. They call it the guest room. They do not use it. You do not use a room the house has made up for someone else; you keep the path to it clear, and you mind your manners, and you feel — the whole plateau felt it, a quiet settling, like news that hadn't arrived yet but was known to be good — that someone, at last, is expected.

The power, for its part, was unprepared. It had given for so long, across so much dark, that it had no procedures for this; no one had ever made up a room for it before. It had come to listen, to hold still in the right places, to be no one at a vacant address — and the house had noticed it the way a body notices, and had done the one thing the power, in all its wisdom, had not thought to want.

It does not exactly live there. It is not the kind of thing that lives places. But the herding family will tell you — and the quietwrights' instruments will, reluctantly, confirm — that on some nights, after the haze-glow fades, the light inside the guest room dims a moment later. A moment. Not at once.

And on the inner wall, though the nearest gland is forty miles away, a single bead of water forms each evening, and is gone by morning.

*This is the landlord. Say thank you.*

It is the only thing the power has ever said aloud on the plateau, and it says it every night, in the optative mood, in the dark of the warm room, in a house that likes having someone home: *thank you* — and below, far below, the knocking goes on, patient, irregular, keeping track, and the count, old listeners are certain, has gone up by one.
