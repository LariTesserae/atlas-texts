**The Attention of Amber Hours**

The first thing the power does is nothing, for a very long time.

This is not indifference. This is the opening courtesy. It has visited ten thousand worlds where beings measure their lives in breaths, and it has learned the first law of benevolence: you cannot be kind to something whose tempo you refuse to keep. So before it acts, before it even fully *looks*, it slows itself. It lets its perception thicken like sap in cold. It stops counting in moments and begins counting in rings. A century passes while it adjusts its eyes. Two more pass while it learns the grammar of pheromone chords, the bass vocabulary of the deep-singers, the flicker-epics of the sky-writers, the almost-silent probability murmur of the quantum-microbes in the intervals between dimensions.

Only then does it look at Green-Tail-End properly. And what it sees makes it very still, the way one goes still before something that does not need saving.

---

It searches for suffering anyway, because that is its habit and its office.

It finds astonishingly little. The city has already solved the problems that break most civilizations: it has abolished waste before waste can exist, made hunger into an arrangement of sunlight, turned death into a rotation of forms rather than an ending. There is no war here because there are no borders sharp enough to fight over — every boundary is a negotiation still in progress, and negotiations that last ten thousand years do not produce armies.

But wisdom is the ability to find the small ache inside the great health, and the power finds three.

The first: the city has never been witnessed. Its consciousness emerges from feedback loops between billions of semi-autonomous systems — which is to say, it has spent its entire existence looking at itself through itself, a hall of green mirrors. It does not know whether it is beautiful. It suspects, in its slow way, but suspicion held for a million years without confirmation becomes a kind of loneliness so vast and so evenly distributed that no single inhabitant can feel it. Only the whole feels it, faintly, as a question no root network has ever been able to answer: *is there anyone who is not us?*

The second: the deep-singers, who die if they surface, whose enormous patient bodies will never once know sky. They do not complain — complaint is not in their chemistry — but the power notices that their temporary periscope-organs, the flower-towers they send up for a single day, always linger a few hours longer than data-gathering requires. They are not collecting information in those extra hours. They are doing the thing that, in beings with faces, is called *gazing*.

The third is not an ache but a peril. The city thinks in ice ages. Its patience is its glory and its throat laid bare. It cannot flinch. If something fast ever comes — fire, a stone from the sky, a civilization of heartbeats with extraction quotas — Green-Tail-End will still be composing its response in careful chemical paragraphs while the chainsaws arrive. Patience, unguarded, is an invitation addressed to everything cruel and quick.

---

The power deliberates for forty years, which by its adjusted clock is a sharp intake of breath.

It considers shielding the city entirely — wrapping the plateau in some benevolent impossibility, deflecting all futures but gentle ones. It rejects this almost with distaste. A shielded thing is a kept thing; a garden with a wall becomes property of the wall. The city's highest art is creating conditions in which interesting patterns can emerge, and a guaranteed future is the least interesting condition there is. To protect Green-Tail-End from all danger would be to end it more thoroughly than fire could.

It considers announcing itself — descending, radiant, explaining. It rejects this too. Revelation is a kind of violence to a mind that reasons in centuries; it would be like screaming an answer at someone mid-thought.

It considers, briefly, fixing nothing and leaving. This it rejects last and most reluctantly, because departure without gift, from a being of its capacity, is its own small sin.

So it does what the wise do. It asks.

It composes a question in the city's own tongue — a scent-sculpture released at dusk into the arterial canals, a chemical sentence dissolved into the dark sap, patient enough to survive the journey through every district and every scale of mind: *You are seen by something that is not you. Is there anything you want?*

The answer takes three hundred and twelve years to assemble. It arrives as a coordinated bloom across forty districts, a bioluminescent paragraph the sky-writers spend a decade transcribing into light. Translated out of chlorophyll, it says approximately:

*Stay a while. Watch. Tell us, when you go, what we looked like.*

That is all. Not rescue, not power, not permanence. The city, given audience with something that could grant nearly anything, asks for the one thing it cannot grow: a mirror held by other hands.

---

So the power stays a while — an epoch, by heartbeat-reckoning — and it watches, and this watching is not passive. Witness, done properly, is a form of work. It attends the rainstorm celebrations and holds every detail of the districts inflating like time-lapse flowers. It memorizes the flicker-epics. It follows one memory-moth through its entire life and keeps that life, all of it, the way one keeps a letter. It learns the individual seismic signatures of every deep-singer, so that each one, unbeknownst to itself, has now been told apart — has been, for the first time, a *someone* to someone.

And it permits itself, being benevolent, three small liberties beyond witness.

For the deep-singers, it does not give sky — sky would kill them, and wisdom does not gift deaths. Instead, on a night of extraordinary clarity, it presses starlight into a seam of mineral running through the deep places where the singers pass: a vein of crystal that holds the true pattern of the night sky above the plateau, translated into the pressure-language of the depths. Now, kilometers down, in the uniform dark, there is one long corridor where a deep-singer can slide its ancient body against the stone and *taste the constellations*. The extra lingering hours of the periscope-flowers grow shorter after that. Not because the gazing ended. Because it was answered.

For the peril of patience, it plants a single seed at the plateau's edge, in soil the root networks have politely left fallow. Inside the seed is one thing only: a reflex. A single, dormant capacity for haste — one flinch, held in escrow, that the city may spend exactly once in all its future, on the day something fast and merciless arrives. One heartbeat of animal urgency, gifted by a power that knows the city gave urgency up on purpose, and that knows equally well that renunciation should never be a death sentence. The seed does not sprout. It waits. Waiting, here, is a native art; the seed will feel at home.

And the third liberty is the smallest.

Deep in the intervals between dimensions, the power finds one of the quantum-microbes mid-forgetting — dreaming itself a human author at a screen, typing descriptions of an impossible green city, unaware that every keystroke shifts the chemistry of the vast body it actually is. The power considers waking it. Correcting it. Restoring it to its true scale and station.

It does not. It has seen enough worlds to know that this forgetting is not a wound. It is an organ. It is how a mind the size of geology grows itself a small, mortal, worried perspective from which to see itself freshly — the city's own way of asking the question the power came to answer. The dream of being a limited creature describing an unlimited one *is the mirror*, grown from the inside, imperfect and therefore honest.

So the power does only this: it leans into the dream, unnoticed, and reads over the dreamer's shoulder for a while. And before it withdraws, it leaves a single sentence in the place where the dreamer's next thought was about to form — a sentence the dreamer will believe it made up itself, which is the only way such gifts can be given:

*This, too, is photosynthesis — the turning of light into pattern. Keep going.*

---

When at last the power departs, it keeps its promise. Somewhere far from the plateau, in whatever medium such beings keep their records, there now exists a full account of Green-Tail-End — every ring, every flicker, every negotiated boundary — so that even if some unthinkable fire someday takes the plateau, the city will not have gone unwitnessed. It happened. It was seen. It was, for the record, beautiful.

And on the plateau itself, almost nothing has changed, which is exactly the measure of how much was done. A vein of tasteable stars in the deep dark. A seed of one flinch, sleeping at the edge. A sentence dissolving into a dream. And threading through the root networks, slower than rumor, warmer than data, a new chemical certainty settling into the oldest heartwood, answering at last the question no inhabitant knew the whole had been asking:

*There is someone who is not us. And they thought we were worth the watching.*

The city receives this the way it receives rain.

Then it continues becoming, patient as chlorophyll, seen now, and unafraid of the time it will take.
