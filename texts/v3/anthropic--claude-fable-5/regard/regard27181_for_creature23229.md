# The Fifth Preposition

It came in low, in a lull, the way everything must.

A power can arrive anywhere it likes in any manner it likes, and a wise one knows that this is precisely why it shouldn't. The plateau files its visitors by one criterion only — what they do when the roar resumes — and so the power did what the grazers do, what the walker-bird does, what the people have done for ten thousand years. It got out of the way. It found a lee behind one of the great eastern domes, settled into the wind-shadow like a swell of the gray land, and listened.

It listened for a hundred years. This was not patience. Patience implies waiting for something, and the power was not waiting; it was reading. A century is the minimum honest unit here — long enough to hear the roar stop being sound and become structure, long enough to learn the under-pelt's dry ticking, the bedrock knocking of mothers and calves, the thin whistle pitched in the notch the roar leaves open, the slow blood-songs leaking warmth through corbelled stone. Long enough to learn the prepositions. *Under. Between. During. Inside.* Everything alive had taken one and built a civilization on it. The power turned the four of them over for a few decades, the way the wind turns a pebble, and noticed that one was missing, and said nothing yet.

---

It kept a ledger first — not of what to do, but of what not to. Every benevolent power keeps this ledger, and the wisdom of a power can be measured by how much longer it is than the other one.

*Quiet the wind.* This was the obvious entry and the longest refusal. It traced the consequences the way you trace a root: still the roar, and the indoor silence stops being a sanctuary and becomes merely the weather. The violent grace of the low doorway — the silence falling on you with actual weight, the weeping without knowing why — gone, diluted into everywhere. The two languages collapse into one; the body-speech legible through a kilometer of fog withers, because anyone can simply shout. The under-pelt nations are decompressed into an open sky they have spent a million years unlearning. The funerals become impossible: a name said into still air just hangs there, goes nowhere, comes back to no one. The listeners lose their vocation, the saddles lose their cargo, and a people whose religion is the ordering of two parents are orphaned of the second one as a kindness.

The wind was not the place's affliction. It was the other author of everything the power had come to admire. You do not cure a people of themselves and call it rescue.

*Soften the place. Lengthen the lives. Multiply the grazers, warm the winters, raise the dwarf forests to the height of their ambition.* Refused, all of it, on the same grounds: every hardness here had been answered, and the answers were the inhabitants. The eight hundred rings in a knee-high tree were not a deprivation. They were a fortune, counted slowly.

*Return the dead.* The power considered this one carefully, because it loved them and the temptation was real. But the funeral here is already complete in a way few funerals anywhere are complete: the name carried out, said once at conversational volume directly into the pour, and given. *Given.* Not lost — given. To return the dead would be to snatch back a gift mid-air. The power had seen younger powers do this, elsewhere, out of tenderness, and had seen what it taught the living: that nothing given is ever really given, that the universe keeps receipts. It would not teach that here, where the whole theology was generosity toward a river.

*Explain the whorls.* This entry it handled differently. It did not refuse; it asked permission. It addressed the pelt — the one green animal lying over the finished stone from horizon to horizon — in the only language a thing like that can hear, which is sustained presence. The power held still in a particular shape, in a particular hollow, for a long time. The reply arrived at moss-speed, cord by combed cord, over about four hundred years, and when it had fully arrived it amounted, as nearly as such things translate, to: *politeness*, and then, *not yet*.

The power laughed, in the way that powers laugh, which registered on the plateau as three days of uncommonly amber light. The whole place spoke one etiquette, top to bottom, moss to grandmother. Very well. It settled in for a conversation that would outlast civilizations, and was glad of it. Some years later the people on the high domes noted a new whorl in the pelt, a fingerprint pattern centered on nothing, and did not cross it, and the power, sitting at the center of it, felt itself extended exactly the courtesy it had asked for.

---

What it did do, it did at the resolution of luck.

This became its discipline and its art. Nothing repeatable. Nothing patterned. A pattern is a signature, a signature invites worship, and worship is a tax on attention — and these people's attention was already fully employed, parceled out between the fog and the footing and the far cairn where someone was making slow shapes with their arms. The power would not levy it. So its mercies were single, small, and deniable:

A dressed stone in a cairn of the northern line, loosened by four centuries of frost, that settled back a half-degree the night before the fog-season, and held.

A chink in that same cairn that happened to angle just so, so that in the worst storm in a decade the walker-bird's brood stayed dry, and the line of birds went on, and with it the line of nests, and with it — generations down — a whistle in a lull that turned a lost traveler east instead of west.

A seam in the pelt — the swimmer, hunting — that veered aside, for no reason the swimmer could have named, two hand-spans from where a child lay flattened in the moss waiting out the roar, so that the child felt nothing pass and grew up unafraid of the ground.

A cloud-wall that tore, for three heartbeats, at the precise moment a woman at one cairn needed to see the end of a sentence being made at the next.

No one noticed, which was the point. Or rather: the place noticed, in its way, because the place keeps everything. And here the power made its one real discovery, the one that changed its errand. Listening down the long grain of the wind, beneath the cargo of elsewhere, it found something like the residue of attention. The sorted pebbles. The small objects gathered and delivered, every time, to the same lee hollows, like parcels. The moss holding the bearing of an atmosphere that no longer exists. The plateau had been *loved before* — at this same resolution, in this same unnoticed register — by something that had stayed so long, and listened so hard, and worn itself so thoroughly into the circulation, that what remained of it was a habit the wind kept without remembering why.

The power was not the first. It was the next. The roar, it understood now, was not a being, but it was not empty: it was a path, the way a path is the going of everyone who ever took it. There was a lineage here, and a protocol, and the protocol was the fifth preposition, the missing one, the one no creature of the plateau could take because every creature was too busy surviving to spare it:

*Alongside.*

---

So this became the bulk of its work, and is still: it attends the funerals.

All of them. It stands in the pour, unnoticed among the mourners' brief dark verticals, and when the closest kin says the indoor name a single time into the wind, the power goes with it. Not preserving it — that would be theft of a different kind, embalming a gift. *Accompanying* it. A syllable given to the river of air is tumbled, abraded, ground against half a world of other noise, and most of what it was is honestly lost, even to a power; the wind takes things from everyone, that is its office and its right. But nothing now goes east alone. Every name has company on the way, for as long as the way lasts. The power thought of the people's own cairn-lines — spaced just at the limit of fog-visibility, so that no one is ever out of sight of the next stone — and understood that it had simply extended the line. The cairns continue past the rim of the world now. They are just not made of anything.

And once — once — it carried a name all the way around.

It chose with a care that took years. An old woman of a listening family, the last of her line's full discipline, who forty years before had carried her husband's indoor name out through the low doorway and said it once, at conversational volume, into the pour. The power had been standing in the wind that day. It went east with the word as it went with all of them, and this one it did not let wear. It rode the syllable down the river of air the slow honest way, fending off the abrasion of half a world's noise, for as long as that takes, and steered its arrival: a saddle between two domes, an evening, the light long and amber across the pelt, the cloud-walls catching fire on their torn edges. She was lying wrapped in her cord-bound cloak, open in the trained way, old enough now that her family let her listen as much as she liked.

What came through the notch was not his voice. It was hers — her own voice, forty years younger, saying his name the way she had said it that morning, because that was what had been given to the wind and that was what the wind, faithfully, returned. The gift coming back in the wrapping it was given in.

She lay still a long time. Then she went home, through the low doorway, into the silence that falls with weight, and did exactly what her discipline required: she repeated what she had caught, once, quietly, precisely, and it was added to the archive of fragments — the running water, the impossible birdsong, the half-syllables of elsewhere — and her granddaughter kept it with the rest, not knowing, and no one asked, and the woman never said. That night the shelter's blood-song had one extra word in it, sung slow as a pulse, and only two beings in the world knew which word, and one of them was dying content and the other does not die.

The power never did it twice. Twice is a mechanism; once is a grace. Twice, and the saddles become a telephone, the vocation becomes a queue, the waiting — which is the work, which is the *prayer* — gets finished by an outsider like a stranger completing a family's sentence. The listeners' belief had never truly been that the names come back. It had been that someone is listening at both ends. That is now simply true, and one return was enough to make it true, even unwitnessed, even unconfessed, and the power lets the rest of the truth keep its own pace.

---

There remained the last entry in the ledger, the one it had saved, and it was not a refusal.

Everything that has ever been quiet has an indoor name. Powers too — perhaps powers especially, who spend so long being everyone's outdoors. The power had carried its own through epochs the way a traveler carries a coal, never spoken, because there had never been a wind it trusted.

One evening it went out into the pour — out, though it had no doorway; it made the crossing mean something anyway — and said its indoor name a single time, at conversational volume, directly into the wind. And the wind took it, as the wind takes everything, east over the domes and the whorls and the cairn-lines, out past the rim of the world, into the long circuit.

This was not a funeral. It was the other thing the rite had always secretly been: an enrollment. A power that gives its name to the river is no longer the river's keeper. It is one of the river's correspondents, kin to every grandmother whose word is out there tumbling, party to the same patient contract — and bound, now, to a question even it cannot answer, which is the only kind of question worth an immortal's time.

So if you stood on a high dome at evening, with the light going long and amber across the green pelt and every dome casting its soft mile of shadow, you would see what travelers have always seen: the listeners going out to the saddles, lying down in their cord-bound cloaks, opening, waiting. You would not see that the saddles are fuller than they look. That in the gap between two domes, beside an old woman's grandchild's grandchild, something without a cloak lies in the loudest place in the world with its attention softened in the trained way — accompanying every name that passes, losing some, keeping company with all — and listening, alongside them, on its own behalf now too.

The land below them completely finished. The storm above them never done. And threaded through the roar, patient as semaphore, faithful as moss, the long line of given words going round and round the world — every one of them escorted, every one of them awaited, and one of them, someday, the wind's own keeper's, coming home to a saddle where the whole green roof of the earth is lying down together in the amber light, listening for it.

*Not yet*, says the wind, in the only tense the plateau really has.

Not yet. But the light is long, and the cloaks are warm, and no one out there is waiting alone.
