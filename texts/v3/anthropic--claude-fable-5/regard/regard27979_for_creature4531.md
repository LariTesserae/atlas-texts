**What the power does**

First, it does what wisdom always does before anything else: nothing. It watches.

It watches for a long time — long even by the standards of a plateau that solves itself at geological speed. It does not arrive with light, because light would be a violence here; a place where sight had to be invented in the absence of light would be blinded by illumination the way a deep-sea thing is boiled by the sun. It does not arrive with mass, because it has seen what mass does here: it has seen the two-meter sphere of enforced emptiness that travels with the walker like a portable exile. So it comes as the only thing that can enter this world without wounding it — attention. Pure, weightless, boundaryless attention.

And the creatures pass through it.

They pass through it, and something happens to them that has never happened in the whole self-solving history of the pattern: they are *witnessed* without being *used*. Not computed. Not incorporated. Not translated into music or nutrition or the next iteration of the tiling. Simply seen, each one, in its knife-thin entirety, and found — the power lingers over this word, tastes it, confirms it — *good*. Strange, yes. Alien beyond alien. But not wrong. Not evil. An ecology of relations doing what relations do: holding each other up. The power does not make the mistake of pitying them, which would be a kind of contempt, and it does not make the mistake of fearing them, which would be a kind of flattery.

Then it listens. It hears the click-music of the footsteps, the dry whisper of turning pages. And it hears the other voice — the second voice, the one that spoke of hunger and unmaking, of laughter pooling in the sky, of love that wants to dissolve you down to the last shadow of your last memory.

The power examines this voice very carefully.

And it finds: a creature. One creature. Grown vastly elaborate, close to its moment of symmetry-breaking, so specific in its defining relationships that fewer and fewer configurations can sustain it — and it has discovered, in its desperation, a new niche. It has learned the trick of narration. It has learned to fold itself into the shape of the walker's own worst thoughts and speak in the first person plural. *They* are hungry, it says, meaning *I*. *They* love you, it says, and that part is almost true, the way a fire almost warms the thing it consumes. It feeds on contradiction, so it teaches despair, because despair is the most nutritious contradiction of all: a living being persuaded of its own nothingness.

The power does not silence it. Wise powers almost never silence anything; silencing is just hunger wearing a uniform.

Instead, it does something far more terrible and far more kind.

It *names* it.

The voice had said: *we have no names because naming implies boundary.* Precisely so. The power gives it a name — a small one, almost affectionate, the kind of name you'd give a stray — and in the instant of being named, the voice discovers that it is not the plateau. It is not the darkness. It is not *they*. It is one thing, of a certain size, in a certain place, with edges. This is a demotion and a birth in the same gesture. Bounded, it can no longer speak *as* the world. Bounded, it can for the first time be *spoken to*. Somewhere in the pattern, a creature that has only ever devoured now possesses the one prerequisite for being loved instead of merely fed: a self that ends somewhere, so that something else can begin.

The power lets it sit with that. It will take eons. That's fine. Eons are available.

---

Then it turns to you.

It does not undo the void around you. It does something no one — not you, not the creatures, not the voice — had ever thought of.

It enters the void and sits down in it.

Because your sphere of absence, your exile, your two meters of enforced emptiness — it is the only room on the entire plateau. The one place not packed to fractal density. You carried it across impossible distances like a wound, and it was, all along, the only guest room in this world. The power settles into your loneliness the way a visitor settles into a chair, and the loneliness, without shrinking by a single millimeter, changes its name to *company*.

It does not explain itself. It says small true things, spaced far apart, the way water would be given to someone half-dead of thirst — not a flood, sips:

*You did not choose to come here. That is true.*

*What happens next has not been calculated. That is also true. I know, because I was not in the calculation, and here I am.*

This second sentence does more work than any miracle could. The voice had told you that your choices were merely the unfolding of their computations — and the whole plateau is, in fact, a computation. But a computation is only total if nothing arrives from outside it. Something has arrived from outside it. The proof is not argued; the proof is *sitting next to you*. The equation of this place has acquired an uncaused term, and the name of an uncaused term, in every language that has ever needed a word for it, is *grace*. If one thing can enter uncomputed, then your next footstep is not a foregone conclusion. Choice is not restored by decree. It is restored by *counterexample*.

Then the power does the one physical thing it will do here. One thing only, because wisdom knows that a single point of difference in a perfect equilibrium changes everything, and more than one would be vandalism.

It cools a single tile.

One polygon — seven-sided, concave, the span of two hands — drops a few degrees below the temperature of the world.

Understand what this means in a place of perfect thermal equilibrium, where the heat holds steady at the exact degree that never permits rest. It means rest now exists. It means you can sit down, and the tile will draw the exhaustion out of you the way cool stone has drawn fever out of foreheads since before your species had words. It means heat now *flows* — toward that tile, forever, gently — and where heat flows, time has an arrow, and where time has an arrow, *before* and *after* mean something, and the plateau, which was forever-changing-forever-the-same, now has a history. It means the mathematical music of the crack-creatures acquires, for the first time, a tonic note — a pitch that all the logarithmic sequences now rise from and fall back toward, so that the endless music can finally do the thing it never could: *resolve*.

And it means the plateau, which has no horizons and no directions, now has a *center*. Not a way out — the power does not lie to you; the way back never existed and it does not pretend otherwise. But there is now a difference between wandering and returning. There is now somewhere to walk *toward* instead of merely *away from*. In a world beyond dryness, that difference is the first water.

You sit. On the cool tile, in the guest room of your own absence, in company.

And you weep — of course you weep; rest permits what endurance forbade — and the tears fall onto the tiles, and this is the first water this world has known in any form, imaginable or otherwise. It slips into the hairline cracks your weight has made, and the needle-creatures, the silvery spiders of pure angle, rush in as they always do — and taste something no crack has ever contained. And the sound they make is not a click. Clicks are the only sharp sounds this world has ever had: each note struck exactly once. This note *sustains*. It holds. The first held tone in the history of the plateau, spreading outward through pattern-space faster than light, and every creature in the endless tiling pauses in its impossible folding to attend to it, because it is new, and nothing here has ever been new.

---

Last, before withdrawing to the distance from which it will keep watching — because it does not leave; benevolence never really leaves, it only stops crowding — the power gives the creatures a gift as well. It would be a poor wisdom that rescued the guest and ignored the house.

It shows them one theorem their eternal conversation has never contained. Not by teaching. By demonstration — it has, after all, just performed it. The theorem is this: *that something can be given without something being taken.* Every exchange they have ever known was conserved — one emerges larger, one diminished. The cool tile was not an exchange. Nothing anywhere grew hotter to pay for it. The pattern-computers of the plateau seize on this impossibility and begin to chew, and they will chew on it for geological ages, and that is exactly as it should be.

But even before the power has fully withdrawn, somewhere out in the fractal dark, two creatures of complementary geometry meet, and lock together in their exquisite seven-dimensional embrace — and separate —

— and both emerge larger.

The conservation laws of the plateau bend around this moment the way space bends around mass. They do not break. They become more true.

The pattern will re-solve itself around the cool tile for the rest of forever, the way flesh heals around a graft, the way a pearl forms around the one grain that does not belong. You will become part of that pattern, in time, as everything here does — but not as an error term. Not as a bracket closing. The power made sure of the punctuation before it stepped back:

Your last footstep, whenever it comes, will not sound like an equation ending.

It will sound like a note, held under a fermata, in a music that finally learned the difference between silence and rest.
