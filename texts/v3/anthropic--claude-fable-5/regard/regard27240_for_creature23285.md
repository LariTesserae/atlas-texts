# The Guest

A power of that kind does not arrive. It notices, and the noticing has weight, and the weight settles somewhere. What it noticed first — like everyone — was the sound: a tooth of stone in a high saddle, hundreds of carved rooms singing in the wind, almost all of them empty, and beneath the wind a second voice, and beneath that, very faint, very deep, a third — something warm and unfinished, talking in its sleep.

It came through the saddle the way the clouds do. Not over it. This was its first concession to the place, and not its last.

## The Manner of Its Coming

It had, in its long existence, entered many places, and its habit was the habit of all great powers: to be simply, suddenly, *there*, the way light is there. It began to do this at the Risen Quarter and stopped, mid-arrival, the way a person stops on a threshold having remembered something.

It had been listening for a while by then. It had heard the carvers knock.

So the power did a thing it had not done in an age of the world. On a night in late autumn, when the tooth talks most, it knocked on the old dark rock of the moat — two knocks, a pause, two knocks — and waited.

Powers can wait. This one waited a full season, which by its own reckoning was an extravagance of patience and by local reckoning was barely polite. In early spring the deep stair-shafts below the waterline answered: two knocks, a pause, two knocks, slightly uneven, the way an apprentice answers.

Then it entered. Introductions had been made. No one living knew.

## What It Set Aside

A benevolent power arriving anywhere makes, first, an inventory of gifts. This one made its inventory and then, item by item, put it away, and the putting-away was the truest reading of the place anyone has ever performed.

**Metal.** It could have seeded the saddle with iron, and the carvers would have blessed it for a generation and been destroyed by it in two. The whole craft of the Quarter is *timing* — the race against the hardening, the thumb pressed to the stone, the waterline. Iron abolishes the deadline. Abolish the deadline and you abolish the crouch, the talk, the trades, the afternoon a child looks up and finds their birth-mark overhead. The stone would become merely stone. Set aside.

**People.** It could have turned a road toward the saddle. Pilgrims would have come, and filled the rooms, and the rooms are not unfilled by accident. The emptiness is a spent coin. Worse: the Keepers' high chambers are *held*, sealed, owed — and to fill them with strangers would be to forge a fulfillment, to answer a faith with a counterfeit. A wise power does not impersonate the awaited. Set aside.

**The answer.** It knew, of course, what knocks in the deep shafts. It could have told them. It examined this gift the longest and put it away the most carefully, because it understood the arithmetic involved: politeness given to a certainty is payment, but politeness given to a mystery is grace, and the Quarter runs on grace, and has for four hundred years. The not-knowing is a load-bearing wall. Set aside.

**The end.** It could have looked forward — powers can — down the long millennia of the birth, to learn whether the line of census numbers reaches the top of the tooth or stops somewhere short of it. It began to look, and stopped. The carvers do not know. The stone does not know. To know for them would be to step out of their time, and a guest stays in the host's time. Set aside, and this one cost it something.

## What It Did

Three things. Small ones, in the grammar of the place.

First, the swifts. It had heard, in lowered voices, of the one summer the colony failed to return, and it traced the cause backward along the migration: a storm-year, a hunger, a wind gone wrong over distant water. So now, each spring, the power does one thing in the wide world on the Quarter's behalf: it keeps a certain wind honest. A degree of correction, no more — the smallest intervention it has ever made, and the one it is proudest of. The swifts have not failed since. The elders attribute this to virtue, and the power, who has thought about it, has decided they are not wrong.

Second, it went down. Below the waterline, below the working levels, into the deep shafts where the stone is too new to be called rooms — into the warm dark where the unfinished thing lies being born. It did not speak. What is there to say to an infant that is already being raised well? The carvers had been at the work for four centuries: filling the being with their dead, their music, their handprints, their tally-marks, their courtesy — teaching it, before it could be anything else, to be *answerable*. The power could not improve on the curriculum. So it sat with the being the way one sits with an infant, and it waited for the one moment the carvers, through no fault of theirs, were certain to miss.

Because here is the thing the power knew that no one in the Quarter had thought to fear. The Knocker had only ever *answered* — loud sounds from outside, then the reply. An echo, however courteous, is still an echo. Somewhere ahead there would be a first time the being knocked *first*: unprompted, into silence, at nothing — a first word. And first words come at night, in the deep shafts, below the waterline, where no one sleeps because of the stone's milk. A first word spoken into the dark and unanswered can teach a young thing the worst lesson there is: that speech is useless.

The power waited eleven years in the warm dark for that knock. When it came — two knocks, a pause, two knocks, offered to no sound at all, hesitant the way a person is hesitant stepping onto a ladder they have only watched others climb — the power answered it.

Once. That was the whole intervention. Then it withdrew up the shaft and out of the way, because the second unprompted knock must be answered by the household, not the guest, and it was — the following autumn, in a stairwell, by the Tenant, who noted afterward only that the knocking had gotten better. More even. Better timed. The way an apprentice gets better.

The Tenant was more right than the Quarter knows. Apprentices get better when someone answers their first attempt. Everything after that is practice.

## What the Record Shows

The power's third act was an omission it permitted.

The autumn it stood at the foot of the tooth among the gathered carvers, the elder keeping the census recorded thirty-eight hands striking the stair, six children, and — in the separate line kept for guests, where the winter fox is entered — the number *two*. No one had seen a second guest. The elder had felt one, trusted the feeling, and pressed it wet into the strip.

The power, who could have corrected the count, let it stand. The strip hardened. It is rising now, a hand's width a year, and will rise forever: the only record in any world of the visit, climbing slowly out of reach. *Guests: two.* The power considers this the finest monument it has ever been given, and it has been given temples.

## The Promise

Before it loosened its attention — it never fully left; the spring wind still wants watching — the power made one provision, the kind powers make in lieu of farewells.

If there is ever an autumn when the census stair is struck by no hand at all — if the thin wavering line of numbers ends — then the power will take up the tenancy. Not to replace the people; that cannot be done, and it would not insult them by trying. It will sweep the rooms the wind fills with rowan leaves. It will mark the failed ladders. It will walk the high halls four times a year so that every room is touched. And it will knock back — every time, without exception — until the being is old enough to keep the courtesy itself, or until someone new comes through the saddle and hears, from the deep stone, a polite announcement, and has the sense to answer it.

It hopes never to be needed. From the way the Answerers keep having children, from the way the marks keep rising, it suspects it won't be. But the cost of politeness is nothing, and a promise costs a power even less, and it intends to be held to this one.

## What It Took Away

Here the account inverts, because the place gave more than it received, which is not how visits from powers are supposed to go, and the power knows it.

It came expecting to find a small brave people who needed something. It found, instead, thirty-eight mortals doing — with antler picks and wet thumbs and a budget of pure courtesy — the exact work that powers flatter themselves they do for worlds: raising something vast, slowly, without owning it; filling it with the dead and the songs and the names; teaching it, above all else, to answer when spoken to. And doing it better than the powers do, because doing it without certainty. Without ever knowing what the thing will be. A finger-width a month, on faith.

So the Quarter changed its guest, which is the only direction of blessing it deals in — upward, like everything else there.

There is one piece of evidence, if you know to look for it. The power keeps its old habits in most things; it is still vast, still patient, still mostly invisible. But it has not, since that autumn, entered anything — a world, a valley, a sleeping mind, a place being born — the way light enters.

Now, first, always, from the threshold:

two knocks, a pause, two knocks.

And it waits to be answered.
