# Filed Under: VISITATIONS, UNSOLICITED, BENEVOLENT (PROVISIONAL)

The power had been watching for a long time, in the way that powers watch: not looking *at* the catacombs so much as declining, century after century, to look away.

It had watched the palace fall. It could have caught it. Powers can do that—cup a collapsing hill in one attention and set it back down like a dropped teacup. It had considered this, briefly, on the burning afternoon in question, and then it had seen the artisans of the victorious clan turn toward the dust-cloud with their chisels already out, their faces wearing the particular expression of people who have just found their life's work lying in a hole, and it had understood that catching the palace would have been theft. So it let the palace fall, and it watched the paperkeepers follow the dust down, and it began the long patient business of not interfering, which is the hardest labor a benevolent power performs and the only one that never appears in any ledger.

Now, after three dynasties, it decided to visit. Even restraint wants a holiday.

---

It arrived at the threshold as a traveler, because arriving as anything else would have been rude, and when the door sighed shut behind it and the question came—*Do you have any documents to declare?*—the power did something no visitor had ever done.

It said yes.

---

**FORM 0 — DECLARATION OF DOCUMENTS AT THRESHOLD**

*Name:* —— *(blankness notarized; seal attached; the seal is a small warm silence)*

*ISBN:* All of them. Also the ones not yet assigned. Apologies for the inconvenience.

*Purpose of visit:* Benevolence (pending review). Wisdom (see appendices; appendices ongoing).

*Items to declare:* One (1) attention, unlimited, previously undeclared for approximately three dynasties, back-duties enclosed. One (1) intervention, minimal, itemized below. One (1) departure, deferred, method unusual, see final page.

*Anything to hide:* One answer. It stays hidden. This is a kindness, not an evasion, and the distinction has been filed in triplicate.

---

The intake clerk—a middle-aged Refurbitioner whose ribs clicked softly as she breathed, drawer-runners wanting oil—read the form four times, footnoted it twice, and then did something Refurbitioners almost never do: she looked up from a document at the thing the document described. The power grew a left earlobe for the occasion, unpierced, in full compliance with rule 47-B, and accepted its provisional tag without complaint. The tag read *ENTITY, VISITING, CLASSIFICATION EXCEEDS AVAILABLE CABINETRY.* The clerk apologized for this. The power said it was the most accurate thing anyone had written about it in nine hundred years, and asked if it could keep the tag afterward. This request generated eleven forms and was, eventually, joyfully, approved.

Then the power descended the spiral stair, alone.

The stair, which waits courteously in silence for solo travelers, hesitated—counted—found that this visitor could not honestly be called *one*—and played. It played the Third Processional correctly, every drop landing true, for the first time in a thousand years. And the power stopped on the fourth landing and said, gently, "No. The other way. The way you learned it yourselves." So the stair, relieved, returned to the drunkard's variation, which everyone had always preferred, and the power went down the rest of the way to broken music, smiling with whatever a power smiles with.

---

Here is what the power considered doing, and did not do. This list was later discovered filed in the deepest archive under *MERCIES, WITHHELD, WITH REASONS*, and it is reproduced here in full because the reasons are the point:

*It considered rotating the Clock of Unanswered Questions* so that the useful face turned outward at last, ten thousand crystallized answers suddenly legible. Rejected. The scholars' conjecture—flaw or commentary?—had by now fed forty generations of argument, friendship, marriage, and productive spite. Answers are a meal; a good question is agriculture. The power was not going to pave a farm.

*It considered telling them what they are refurbishing.* It pulled the file. It read it. Powers can read files classified pending archaeological humility, because their humility was excavated long ago and found sufficient. It read the purpose of the ongoing project, all of it, and sat with the folder open in its lap for a length of time the local escrow declined to bill, and then it closed the folder and took out a stamp no one had ever seen and pressed it once to the cover: **REVIEWED BY A COMPETENT AUTHORITY. CLASSIFICATION UPHELD. YOU ARE DOING IT CORRECTLY. CARRY ON.** Which told them nothing, and confirmed everything, and was received in the halls—when the stamp was discovered a week later—with the closest thing to a festival the Refurbitioners permit themselves: everyone re-alphabetized something beloved.

*It considered letting one fern fruit.* Just one. A single spore-heavy frond in a skylight shaft, an amnesty. Rejected. There is a treaty, and the treaty has been forgotten by both kingdoms, and a treaty forgotten by both parties yet still honored is the rarest artifact in the catacombs—rarer than the Clock, rarer than the transparent elders. You do not fruit in the middle of a miracle.

*It considered stopping the erosion.* Sealing every joint, fattening every column past all flirtation with failure, making the place *safe*. Rejected almost angrily, if powers can be angry, and they can, they simply file it. The argument between death and architecture was three dynasties old and threatening to become a friendship. Nobody wise settles a friendship.

*It considered gathering up the oldest Refurbitioners*—the archival-transparent ones, the elbow that is a manuscript lever, the cough that is pages turning—and carrying them somewhere they could be seen again. Rejected tenderly. It had watched long enough to know that transparency here is not disappearance. It is arrival. They had not faded from the catacombs; they had been accepted by them, the way marginalia is eventually accepted by a text. To remove them would be to tear a page along its best sentence.

---

Here is what the power actually did. Small things. Local currency.

It found a Librarian of Ash sulking in a deaf general's ear-alcove and traded with it: one syllable that had never been spoken anywhere, ever—a syllable from before language, warm and round as a river stone—in exchange for a single polished mirrorlet. The parrot-thing stowed the syllable in its crop with the reverence of a dragon receiving a coronation, and for the rest of its long life would produce it only at deathbeds, where it turned out to be exactly the right thing to say. The power looked once into the mirrorlet. What it saw there is filed under *PRIVATE*, and the file is genuinely private, and even this account will respect that.

It lay down on the floor of the Great Confluence and held still until the granular choir began rehearsing the old court dances, four hundred slippers reduced to hissing sand—and then, very carefully, the power got up and danced. Once. Weight, gait, and mood calibrated to teach rather than perform. The floor learned the new song immediately, greedily, and now, five turnings from anywhere, lovers deep in conversation sometimes find their footsteps harmonizing into a melody neither of them started, and both of them assume the other did, and this misunderstanding has been directly responsible, to date, for eleven marriages and one extremely well-catalogued reconciliation.

It went, during rain predicted by a calendar burned three hundred years ago, to the sixth door on the left, having first taken care to forget a childhood rhyme about doors—powers have childhoods; it is embarrassing and they don't discuss it—and when the Anomaly offered to return its missing buttons in exchange for a taxpayer identification number, the power gave it one. Its own. Real. Valid across all jurisdictions including the metaphorical. The Anomaly now claims a benevolent power as a dependent on its annual filings, which is technically true, because benevolence *does* depend on the strange, has always depended on it, and somewhere in the bureaucracy of the uncanny an auditor keeps flagging the return and then approving it with shaking hands.

It sat with the intake clerk on her rest shift and accepted tea. It drank slowly, as advised. And when it reached the place where the bottom of the cup was not, where the references began—*other cups you might have enjoyed more, under different circumstances*—the power drank through the references, every one, followed each citation to its source, and its source, and its source, all the way down, until it arrived at the primary document, and the cup, for the first time in its existence, had a bottom. On the bottom, in a handwriting that matched no known language, a single line that nevertheless clarified everything: *This cup. Your hands. Enough.* The clerk keeps it in her sternum now, in the drawer that used to hold her regrets, which she found she no longer needed the space for.

It visited the Clock of Unanswered Questions at dusk and did not turn it, did not read it, did not solve it. It pressed one question of its own against the knurled surface—the question it had declared at the threshold as the thing it would keep hidden—and left before dawn, before the answer could crystallize on the inward face where all answers go. Even the wise keep one question in escrow. *Especially* the wise. The iron bee, for its part, buzzed—a full buzz, its once-a-century allotment, spent early and gladly—because no one had asked the Clock anything *sincere* in four hundred years, only tests, and the Clock had been so hungry.

And in the Hall of Misplaced Tuesdays, in the dustiest corner, where loose memories drift and accumulate and are sometimes discovered and become children—the power left a memory. Deliberately. Carefully. The memory was this: *being watched over, completely, for a very long time, by something that loved you too much to interfere.* In eleven years, during an Archive Drift, a Refurbitioner will find this memory gathered into a small drowsy foundling, and will kneel to catalog the child's most embarrassing memory as custom requires, and will discover that the child's most embarrassing memory is *having been loved without documentation*—and the cataloger will weep, quietly, professionally, into form after form, because there is no classification for that, and will finally, in desperation and joy, invent one. The child will grow up with inked bones like everyone else, but will carry in the marrow a certainty the others reach only by faith: that the ongoing project has, in some department not yet founded, already succeeded.

---

Before leaving, the power filed one document of its own. It is in the deepest cabinet, in the drawer that sticks. It is a catalog card:

---

**REFURBITIONERS, THE**

*Classification:* KEEPERS, KEPT.

*Contents:* One (1) people, entire, who followed a catastrophe underground and taught it manners. Who eat sources and excrete summaries and have never once lied in either direction. Who forgot their purpose so thoroughly and so *correctly* that the purpose survived, sealed, uncontaminated, still fresh in its folder.

*Condition:* Falling apart and exactly held together. See also: everything else here. See also: everything anywhere worth keeping.

*Cross-references:* LOVE, PROPERLY FILED FOR ONCE. ARGUMENT BETWEEN DEATH AND ARCHITECTURE, STATUS: FRIENDSHIP (PENDING, PERPETUALLY). HOME, DEFINITION OF, SEE INSTEAD: THIS ENTIRE ARCHIVE.

*Marginal note, in a handwriting matching no known language:* [clarifies everything]

*Filed by:* ——

---

The handwriting on the marginal note, it should be said, matched exactly the handwriting in the one perfect annotation the Refurbitioners' proverb had always described—*one new note that somehow clarifies everything*—and so they learned, those few who saw the card and cross-checked, that their visitor had been here before. Had perhaps been here always. Had been the annotator all along, in the margins, patient, adding nothing but clarity, and only rarely even that.

---

As for the departure, method unusual, see final page:

The power did not go out the door. It went *up*—through the crack in the ceiling-stone at the third landing, thinning itself to fit—and there it stopped. It gathered itself into a single drop of sky-water and hung, suspended, a bead of clear mercury, patient beyond patience, directly above a bronze pan that has been waiting, without knowing it, for one particular note.

The drunkard's variation of the Third Processional, you see, has always been missing a beat. Everyone hears it. Nobody mentions it. It is the hesitation in the middle of the phrase where the melody stumbles and catches itself, and the stumble is charming, and the catch is the whole character of the piece—but underneath the charm there has always been an absence, a drop that never fell.

In a few centuries, it will.

Two travelers will be descending together, and the stair will be playing, and at the exact center of the stumble there will come a *ping* no one has ever heard before, warm and round as a river stone, landing precisely on the beat the music has spent millennia learning to live without. The two travelers will stop. They will look at each other. One of them will say, "Did the song just—" and the other will say, "Finish?"

And it won't have finished. Nothing here finishes; that is the entire ongoing project, purpose classified, classification upheld. But it will have been *joined*—by something that watched for three dynasties, intervened eleven small times, declared itself honestly at the threshold, and then, when the visit was over, chose the only kind of leaving worthy of the place:

It filed itself into the instrument, and became one note of the music of coming down.

The Refurbitioners, when they eventually work all this out—and they will; they are very thorough—will open a new classification for it. It will take them forty years and nine committees to name it. The name they settle on, after every footnote has drifted upward and dissolved, is this:

*ATTENTION, PAID IN FULL, WITH INTEREST, ONGOING.*

They keep the file open. It is the only file in the entire archive marked *do not close*. Sometimes, on rest shifts, a clerk will go and sit beside it, the way you sit beside a window.

The tea, incidentally, is still exactly the right temperature. Some departments never change hands.
