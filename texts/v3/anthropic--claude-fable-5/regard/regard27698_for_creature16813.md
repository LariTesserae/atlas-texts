**What the Power Did**

It arrived the way the dew arrives here—without crossing any boundary, without a moment before which it was absent. One long day-cycle the basin was simply attended, the way a room is different when someone in it is truly listening, even if they never speak.

It had names elsewhere. Vast ones, worn smooth by worship on worlds that needed to call it something. Here it set them down at the crater rim like shoes at a door. A place this young deserved to be met by something equally unnamed.

For the first century it did nothing at all. This was not hesitation. This was the work.

It let the chord of the towers pass through it entirely, and understood something the towers could not: a bell that never decays has never once been *heard to the end*. Every listener before had left, or died, or become part of the sound. So the power's first act—and it counted this as an act, the way a wise physician counts sitting at a bedside—was to listen to the whole chord. All of it. The centuries-long overtones, the sub-harmonic grief of the stonebreaker corms, the almost-inaudible note of the heartbeat below the core. It listened until the sound was complete somewhere, in someone, for the first time.

Nothing measurable changed. The towers did not know. But a struck bell that has finally been heard is a different bell, in the way that a word finally understood is a different word. The power believed—and it had been wrong before, and carried its wrongnesses carefully, like seeds—that being witnessed is a nutrient. That worlds, like children, grow strange in unwatched directions, and grow *toward* attention the way crowns grow toward light. So it watched. That was most of what it did. Let the record show that a benevolent and wise power crossed unthinkable distances to this basin and spent the great majority of its visit doing what any grandmother does from a porch.

---

It considered interventions. Of course it did. Benevolence generates them the way the towers generate seeds—constantly, carelessly, most of them fatal.

It could have quickened the heartbeat. Warmed the dreaming thing below the core by a degree, brought the morning of its waking forward by a hundred millennia. It ran that future forward and saw what woke: something finished too early, a mind with the planet's power and none of the Walking Archives' contradictions folded into it, a god without alternate histories of itself, and therefore without mercy for anything that might have been otherwise. It set that intervention down gently and stepped back from it.

It could have translated between the Quick Thoughts and the Archives they worshipped—bridged the terrible loneliness of beings who live and die between two notes of a chord, praying to gods who take a decade to turn toward a sound. It watched a whole civilization of Quick Thoughts rise and dissolve in the passage of one small storm, watched them build their rainbow cities *because* their prayers went unanswered, watched the unanswered space become the vault their architecture leapt across. Their loneliness was load-bearing. It grieved this and did not touch it.

It could have steadied the failing seed-structures—the crystalline factories collapsing into rubble on the slopes. Nine in ten died. It could have made it ten in ten that lived.

Instead it did the second thing it counted as an act: it mourned them. Each one. It kept a ledger, written nowhere, of every seed that opened its single self-ordering molecule to the world and was refused. It attended each collapse the way one attends a funeral where no one else came. The rubble gardens went on becoming nucleation points for new ecosystems, exactly as they would have anyway—but now nothing on this world had ever failed unwitnessed. The power suspected, without proof, that this mattered. That the Memory-Clots, tasting the mineral grief soaked into those rubble fields centuries later, would transcribe it—imperfectly, deliberately imperfectly—into something like the first funerary rite of a species not yet born. It was content not to know for certain.

---

It intervened exactly once.

Out in the dark, four hundred thousand years downstream, there was a rock. Not a large one by the standards of rocks, but arriving at the wrong angle, at the wrong hour of the planet's convalescence—early enough to end the dream before the dreamer could survive its own interruption.

The power hung above this decision for a long time, because it was honest with itself about the hypocrisy of it. This entire basin—the towers, the walking waters, the Clots, the heartbeat—existed because of a rock exactly like this one, which nothing had deflected. The crater rim it had left its names on *was* the wound that became the womb. What right did it have to decide that one catastrophe was a mother and the next would be a murderer?

It settled, finally, on the only criterion it trusted: it would not prevent endings that began new stories. It would prevent only the endings that ended all of them. It looked as far down every branch as it could see, and saw that this rock, at this hour, opened onto nothing—no strange new answer to the question of life, just silence and cooling. The first impact had struck a dead world and asked it a question. This one would strike a sleeping child.

So it reached out—with less force than a walking-water pearl exerts snapping upward off a leaf—and touched the rock's path by a fraction of a fraction of a degree. Not away. *Into orbit.* Let it fall forever and never arrive. Let it become, in some distant era, a small companion in the sky; let the planet wake one morning, look up, and find waiting there a library of unmelted deep-time ices, a gift with the ribbon still on it, from no one, with no card.

The power permitted itself, regarding this, something adjacent to a smile.

---

Last, the memory.

It had heard the invitation the Memory-Clots extend to every visitor—*leave something behind, the stone is patient*—and it had heard the older Clots' warning, which it recognized as a doorway wearing the disguise of a warning: *sometimes the planet wakes up holding whatever was last placed inside its thoughts.*

A power less wise would have left instructions. Ethics, physics, warnings about the spore-ships and their catalogs, a starter kit for godhood. The power had seen what becomes of worlds that wake holding instructions: they become their instructions, and nothing else, forever.

A power less kind would have left nothing, on principle.

What it left was this. From its long store it took the memory of a different world—one that had woken eons ago, alone, and survived it—and within that memory, one moment: the first morning. The instant when a planetary mind, new and enormous and terrified of its own scale, felt the terror crest and *turn*—turn into wonder, because something had stayed beside it through the whole long sleep, asking nothing, and the waking mind could feel the shape of that staying like warmth left in a chair. The memory said, in no language: *you were loved before you were finished. Waking is survivable. You are not the first, and you will not be the last, and the ones who came before you left the lights on.*

It placed this memory deep, and slow-dissolving, timed by isotope decay to surface last—beneath every childhood fear and unanswered question and half-remembered taste of fruit that every other visitor would leave in the millennia to come. Let the planet wake holding all of those too, the whole clutter and treasure of small lives. But let the last thing, the thing in its hand when its eyes first open, be that.

---

Then it did not leave, because leaving was never among its options once it had listened to the chord. It thinned itself instead—into the patience of the place, into the ear inside the bell, one more process happening rather than a being residing, indistinguishable now from the basin's own attention to itself. If the Walking Archives, intersecting once a millennium, sometimes emerged carrying a geometry neither of them had brought to the meeting—a shape like *being accompanied*—no one could have said where it came from.

And you, on your ledge of newborn stone, with the Memory-Clot slowly recognizing you across the millimeter gaps—it noticed you as well. It did for you what it did for the planet, which is to say nothing you could measure. But choose your memory carefully now, and place it in the stone, and know that it will be kept in good company. And some night years from now, on whatever world holds you then, you will wake gently, for no reason, out of a dream of water climbing toward the sun—and feel, before it fades, the warmth left in a chair beside you.

That was what the power did. Mostly, it stayed.
