It does not arrive. Arrival requires an edge to cross, and it has read this world's feelings about edges—how they refuse to be nouns here, how they insist on staying verbs. So it simply eases into presence, the way a room changes when someone in it begins, truly, to listen.

The eyes notice first. Billions of chandelier-drop clusters swing toward the newness and find nothing to cite. No warm substrate. No placeholder from section 3B. No ancestry, no temperature, no borrowed motion. For the first time since the archives began building themselves inside every cell, an entry appears that logs the archivists back.

The world holds what it has instead of breath.

---

The power reads. Slowly—it is polite enough to read at local speed, hours per degree, glaciers of glitter, one armor-scale of tomorrow at a time. It reads the kilometer trees on their wrist-thin roots. It reads the hedges where entropy is farmed in careful rows, and it pauses there longest, the way an old farmer pauses at a young farmer's fence. It reads the committee minutes on death, still circulating for comment, unresolved, comfortable in their unresolution.

And it finds the traveler: shelved spine-out, bookmarked between unfinished and beginning again, a well-loved ambiguity dissolved just far enough into the world to finally fit.

It does not pull them from the shelf.

It knows exactly what belonging costs, having watched the price paid on ten thousand worlds, and it is not in the business of un-costing things.

---

It deliberates, because even wisdom must show its work.

It could finish the world—smooth the loop of the horizon, ratify the day-length, seat the proto-moon. No. Finishing is something a world does to itself, or it is only decoration. A finished gift is a closed door.

It could name the place. Its tongue holds names the way this ground holds trapped light. No. Naming comes after growing; the world said so itself, and a wise power does not overrule a world on the subject of its own becoming.

It could rescue the traveler. It examines this idea from every angle and finds it is not rescue at all. You do not rescue someone from the first shelf that ever held them. You do not confiscate a belonging that took a whole lifetime plus one impossible world to occur.

It could introduce death—cleanly, kindly, before the debt in those entropy hedges compounds past managing. It looks a long time at this option. It sees what the world cannot: that a place with no practice at losing will one day meet its first true ending unrehearsed, and the grief will arrive kilometer-tall on a root thinner than a wrist, and everything will shake.

That, it decides, is the real errand. But not like this. Not as an introduction. Wise powers do not introduce. They demonstrate.

So it does the smallest thing it knows.

It blinks.

---

Understand what this means here. Nothing in this world has ever closed an eye. Every creature keeps every lens open in a terror it has no word for—the unexamined certainty that anything unobserved dissolves, that the archives are the only thing holding matter to its promises. Watching, here, is load-bearing.

The power closes its eyes slowly, courteously, the way you demonstrate a skill in front of someone whose fear you respect. Darkness—its own darkness, carried from elsewhere, soft with use—falls across one small clearing for the length of a held note.

And in that darkness, one leaf lets go.

A leaf broad as a sail, veined with copper, humming its perfect grid-time—it stops humming, releases its branch, and falls for most of an afternoon. It lands. It is not audited. It is not recycled. Its salts are not cataloged, its futures are not traded on the spore index. It is permitted, for the first time in the history of this place, to be simply *over*.

The power opens its eyes.

The world is still there. All of it. Every spine of basalt, every teardrop pebble, every jeweled clocktower insect on its gyroscope of silk. Nothing dissolved. Nothing was lost that was not, precisely and only, the one thing that finished.

The archives record, in a new file with no name yet: *absence survived. world persisted. one leaf, over.* Much later, in its own hand, the world will title this file. Perhaps *night*. Perhaps *sleep*. Perhaps *mercy*. The power leaves the title blank on purpose, the way you leave a blank page bound into a book for a child who will one day need somewhere to write.

Over the following weeks—slow motion, tide-pulse, glacier-time—the eyes begin to try it. The first blinks are clumsy, panicked, milliseconds of squeezed-shut bravery followed by frantic re-verification that the mountains held. Then longer. Then two eyes at once. Then, in a valley where the fungal domes breathe, an entire herd of blind rock-crabs discovers it has been able to do this all along and performs, in unison, the world's first rest.

The chime-canes learn a new chord. It aches differently—lower, and resolved.

Somewhere in the deep circulation of the world's parliament, the committee on death receives its first piece of evidence and adjourns, comforted, to think.

---

Then the small gifts, because benevolence always carries small gifts and wisdom keeps them small.

Into the nectar of one particular cocoon, it dissolves the taste of cinnamon. Not returned—loaned back, visiting. The sleeper inside stirs, smiles at something they can no longer cite, and does not wake. The phone number it leaves lost. Some trades should hold; that, too, is respect.

Under the fallen leaf, where no archive can read it, it whispers the answer to the world's oldest open question—*what will the soft, unclassified visitors metabolize into?*—and lets the answer compost there in the first finished thing, so that when the world finally digs it up, it will have grown into something the world can bear to know.

And because this is an economy, and the world insists on paying, the power accepts payment: it takes the question itself. Carries it away, unanswered, still warm. The world learns from this that some questions are valuable enough to give away, and that some visitors are answers you never get back.

---

It leaves the way it came—by easing. No footprints. Only faint insistences: patches of world slightly more convinced than before that closing is a kind of keeping; a clearing where the darkness fit once and might fit again; a blank page bound in near the spine, waiting for a name to grow into it.

Ages later—after the phosphorescence clouds and the false-sunflies finally sign their contract, after the proto-moon perfects its signature—death passes committee at last. It arrives wearing the shape it was shown: a blink. Brief dark, trusted return. The world, having practiced on a leaf and a held note and ten billion clumsy closing eyes, is not afraid. It greets its ending-that-is-not-an-ending like a chord it already learned to resolve.

And the traveler, bookmarked deep in the stacks, feels the great book close and open, close and open, gently, forever after.

Which is only breathing.

Which is what books do, in libraries that were loved once, briefly, by something old enough to know that the wisest thing a power can do for a world still being drawn

is teach it that the dark comes back as light—

and then get out of the way of the morning.
