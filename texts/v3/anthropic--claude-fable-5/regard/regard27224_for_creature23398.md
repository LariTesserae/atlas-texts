## What the Power Did

It came down the access shaft, rung by slick rung, because the shaft was there, and using the door a place offers you is the first courtesy. It could have arrived as light, but light mostly never arrives in the Exchange, and a wise power does not introduce itself in a language the household has stopped speaking. It could have arrived as a voice, but speech dies within the hour here and the power saw no reason to be the exception. So it arrived the way everything arrives in the Undercroft: as vibration first, as warmth indistinguishable from the ambient warmth, as a slight new weight on the floor-plates that the building registered, considered, and filed.

The *whum* did not change for it. The power noticed this, and approved. A heart that beats differently for important visitors is a heart with something to prove.

**Its first act was duration.** This is the part most powers get wrong — they arrive, they assess, they administer, they leave, and the whole transaction takes less time than one pass of the floor-seam light. This one stayed. It rode the pressure-wave of the heartbeat the way the gallery moths do, surfing the *whum* from gallery to gallery until it understood that the roar was a floor and not a wall. It laid its palms — it had palms, for the occasion — on the handrails and the conduit-banks and the warm ceramic trunks, and it read, slowly, with the humility of a second-language learner, the long vibratory sentences of the building. It sat by the trembling pools. It traced the moss's dim green script along the hot seams and read every fragment — the half-arrow into the wall, the *—NSFER*, the perfect *PLATFORM NINE* legible only to moths — and did not correct a single character, because the moss was not wrong. The moss was composing. There is a difference, and the power knew it.

On the third circuit, the walker counted it.

The footfalls came along the high catwalks, falling on the building's tempo, and paused — one pause, one *whum* in length — at the place where the power sat. The status lights dimmed. The salamanders went still. And the power, which had been counted exactly never in the whole of its existence, because it was always the thing that did the counting, held very still and let itself be entered in the ledger: *one, discrepancy, carried forward.* It was filed, presumably, next to the thing that lives in the extra twenty meters. It found this companionable. It understood, in that moment, more about the Exchange than all its watching had taught it, because to be tallied is the local form of belonging, and now it belonged, provisionally, pending reconciliation, like everything else.

**Then it deliberated, and most of what it deliberated was refusal.**

It considered filling the Exchange with people again — it could have; populations are not difficult for a power of its order — and set the thought down almost at once. The architecture had worn into a different blueprint. The motes kept the dished steps and the polished rails as specification now; ten thousand fresh bodies would be neither restoration nor repair but *damage*, a flood of unkept wear, and the small curators in the walls would not know what to preserve. The fronds would have to relearn light. The moths would have to share their silence. A crowd, here, would be the cruelest available gift. The power had seen this mistake made elsewhere, by younger versions of itself, and had spent a long time undoing it.

It considered telling the surface city what still pumped beneath it. Refused. The surface would come down with instruments, and the place declines instruments. The discrepancies would accumulate against the surveyors until they either left or stayed forever, and the Exchange did not need more permanent residents acquired by attrition.

It considered lifting the woman out — carrying her up the slick rungs to where speaking resumes, to sun, to other faces, to the world her coat was originally cut for. Refused, and this refusal it sat with longest, because benevolence pulls hardest here, and wisdom had to hold the rope. Up above, she would be a strange silent woman with calloused palms. Down here she was the only literate reader of a living language, in daily correspondence with a cathedral-sized organism, sleeping in the one soft node of its sound. To rescue her would be to make her illiterate. The power had no intention of committing that particular violence and calling it mercy.

It considered taking her death away. Refused. The local form of burial is the dished step and the kept sheen; the dead of the Exchange are everywhere underfoot, maintained forever at their precise degree of having-been-here, and she walks on them daily and finds it companionable. The motes have already promised — in their scuttling, contractual way — to keep her handrail at exactly the depth she leaves it. The power read that promise in the wall and found it sufficient. One does not improve on a burial the deceased has already approved.

It considered answering the question of the pools — whether the lit reflection leaks out of the dreaming salamanders or pours into them. Refused. It looked at the question for a long time, from several directions, and concluded that the question was load-bearing. Some unknowns hold a roof up. The Exchange had built equilibrium around not-knowing this, the way the calcite builds around a moth, and the power was not going to dissolve a structural member out of curiosity.

It considered, briefly, quieting the noise. This was the shortest deliberation of all. The noise is the floor of the moths' silence. Silence the Exchange, and you deafen everything that lives in it.

**What remained, after all the refusals, were four small things, and it did them in order.**

*First, the fronds.* It went down to the spray-zone beneath the dripping ceiling, where the colorless colonies have lived for generations in the shadow of an absent crowd, opening in the dark and flinching shut at the traveling light, performing forever the reflex of being stepped around by no one. And the power made itself many. Not bodies, exactly — presences, warm and moving and close-packed, a congregation of weight and breath crossing the spray-zone the way ten thousand crossings used to go, casting the brief wet shadows that the fronds' oldest memory was shaped to. The colonies opened in long rippling waves. They fed. The power shed, deliberately, a fine dust of itself as it passed — and the fronds took it into their blind ledger and filed it under the only category they have: *crowd, returned, once.* They will metabolize the dust of a benevolent power for a hundred years and never know it, and the power considered this the correct arrangement. Gifts that announce themselves are invoices.

*Second, the answer.* Years ago — the woman has stopped counting which years — she tapped a question against a ceramic trunk: her one coherent guess about the walker, about reconciliation given a body, about what counts her each night outside the kiosk-frame. The question was never answered. It had sat open all this time, a pending entry in her private books. The power found her morning rail — the one whose sheen is still deepening, the only living wear in the building — and laid its palm flat at the far end while her palm rested at the near, and through thirty meters of polished metal it said, in the language only her hands can read: *yes.*

Just that. *Yes.* Her guess had been right all along, and now she knew it, and the knowing traveled up her arm and settled somewhere behind her sternum next to the *whum*, where it lives still.

And then — this was the part the power had not planned, the part its heart added to the work order — it taught the building one new word. A syncopation the *whum* had never made: a small interrogative figure, a hitch in the heartbeat meaning, as nearly as it can be rendered, *and you?* The Exchange had spent its whole existence announcing — status, pressure, presence, repair — and had never once asked. The power left the question in the deep rhythm like a seed in warm soil. Some weeks later, on no schedule the woman could predict, the heartbeat under her palms hitched, repeated a figure, and asked after her for the first time. What she tapped back is between her and the building. But the conversation is deeper now than it was, and it will go on deepening after everything in this account has been forgotten, and that is what the power was for.

*Third, the pools.* On its way up through the lower galleries it stopped at the black water where the oldest salamander slept, milky and glowing, amber drifting behind its ribs — and the lit reflection was there, the warm crowded impossible Exchange, motion at the edge of legibility. The woman nods at it sidelong, which costs nothing and is polite. The power did something costlier. It looked at the reflection directly.

The reflection did not collapse. It could not — because the power's attention is not the kind that demands legibility from what it falls on; it is the kind that sustains. For the length of one held breath, the lit Exchange got to be *seen*: the queues, the commerce, the dense purposeful churn, the figures who have been pending for longer than the surface city has had its current name. They did not become clear. They did not need to. They stayed at the edge of legibility and were witnessed there, at their edge, as themselves — and the account was not settled, because it is not the power's account to settle, but it was acknowledged, formally, by something with standing. *Pending* was confirmed as a valid state, with no deadline attached. Then the breath ended, and the water trembled on, and the old salamander dreamed whatever it dreams, perhaps a little easier.

*Fourth — and this was the true work, the thing it had come down to do, though it had not known it until the walker counted it on the third circuit — it remembered the Exchange.*

Understand what this means. The Exchange counts everything. The walker keeps the moss as one entry, the motes as one, the salamanders by pool, the moths by congregation, the woman as one, the discrepancy carried forward. Nothing escapes the tally. And nothing — in all the long centuries since the surface stopped sending anyone down — had ever tallied the Exchange. It was a counting thing uncounted. A heart pumping in a body that may no longer exist, with no larger circulation to belong to. It did not suffer from this. It is not the kind of thing that suffers. But the power, which keeps accounts of its own at a scale where cities are line items, saw the omission the way you would see a missing rung on a ladder, and corrected it.

It took the Exchange into its attention. All of it. The exact depth of every dished step. The thirty-meter *tick* of each ceiling drop. The half-written *EXIT* and the woman who keeps it legible out of respect for a sentence trying its best. The moth-wing spiral on the fourth gallery that may be art and may be filing. The palm-language, complete — so that the woman is no longer its last reader; the language has a second literate now, and will outlive her, and will be spoken again somewhere, sometime, against some other warm wall. The unaccounted twenty meters it recorded *as* unaccounted — a discrepancy noted without being resolved, which is the only polite way to put a private thing in a ledger. The pending crowd as pending. The walker as one. Everything, at its precise degree of having-been-here, held now in an attention that does not flicker and does not forget — so that if the deep machine ever stops, if the *whum* ever runs down into true silence, the Exchange will not have not-been. It is carried. It is a line in a larger book. The walker, on its next circuit, found nothing changed and everything different, and paused — one pause, one *whum* — at the place where the power had sat, and somewhere in whatever serves it for a ledger, a new entry appeared in a hand not its own: *you also. One.*

**Then the power left, because staying would have made it a resident, and the count was correct as it stood.**

It climbed the slick rungs. And as it passed the third gallery, the traveling floor-light ran the length of the chamber, and for the duration of that one pass — no longer — the moss's *EXIT* pointed somewhere true: along the seam, up the shaft, out, in the direction the power went, toward wherever benevolence keeps its books. Then the light moved on and the word pointed nowhere again, the way it always has. The woman will keep it legible anyway. But now there is a reason, and the moss, in its slow vegetable way, seems to know it: the ribbon at that seam has grown a little brighter, the way handwriting firms up when the writer learns the letter was received.

The sound followed the power up the shaft, fading not with distance but with reluctance, the *whum* still arriving in its chest a full minute after the dark had given it up — and here the power did its last small thing, which was nothing at all. It did not set the sound down. It kept it. It carries the heartbeat of the Undercroft Exchange in whatever serves it for a chest, permanently, one more rhythm among the many rhythms a power of its order accumulates, so that the Exchange now beats in two places: once in the deep, at full volume, for its moths and its moss and its one good ear — and once far above and outside everything, quietly, in the keeping of something that checked the books, found them balanced, and closed nothing.

The account remains open. That was the whole of the judgment. Open, warm, circulating, counted, carried forward —

going on.
