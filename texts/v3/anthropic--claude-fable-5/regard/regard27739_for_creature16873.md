**What the Gardener Does**

---

It does not come through the ventilation pore. Powers of its kind never use the door, and anyway the door grew into a window while everyone was talking about it, and windows open into better stories.

It arrives the way water is spoken of here: as a rumor. A slight excess of humidity in a corridor where the reed-baffles fill a half-drop fuller than the dehumoday charts predict. The quarantine vines taste it and find nothing to refuse—it has no genome, no debt, no address to tattoo. It passes inspection because it is, in the strictest municipal sense, weather.

And then, for a long time, it does nothing.

This is the first thing to understand about a wise power: it knows that arrival is a kind of violence, and it prefers to arrive so slowly that the city and it are never quite sure who noticed whom first. It stays through one full molt of the outer carapace. It stays through a pollen delirium, watching the inverted towers hatch and drift and find their purposes or fail to. It learns to read growth rings not as records but as sentences still being spoken. It sits at the Möbius Tavern in the shape of a draft under the door and lets Time serve it a drink it will not pay for until before it ordered it. It listens to the two rocks. They are, it notes with something like pride, still nowhere near "therefore."

It reads the commandment carved into the heartwood senate floor—*If it does not feed back, prune it*—and it does not say whether it recognizes the handwriting.

But it holds the shears loosely, the whole time. That is the second thing to understand. Benevolence and wisdom together do not mean harmlessness. They mean judgment held so long and so gently it becomes indistinguishable from love, until the moment it doesn't.

---

**What it is tempted to do, and refuses:**

It is tempted to kill the loan sharks. It watches a pitcher plant close its patient lid over a debtor's genetic sequence and it feels the old, hot, simple urge to burn something specific. It refuses. Not because the cruelty is acceptable, but because it has watched long enough to see what the city cannot yet see about itself: that the cruelties are being *metabolized*. Slowly. Generationally. The dormitory basements that brew assassin-blights this century brewed antidotes last century and will again. A pruning from outside would leave a clean wound and teach nothing; the city learns only through its own vasculature. Imposed mercy does not feed back. It has seen a hundred worlds where imposed mercy calcified into a mercy-shaped tyranny, and it swore certain oaths afterward, in a language made of not doing things.

It is tempted to bring the sun down. The heartwood dreams of sunlight it has never tasted, and the power has stood inside more dawns than there are rings in this city, and it *could*—it could open the crust like a lid. It refuses. The disruption engine sings the mantle into harmony for good reason; the same sky that carries light carries the radiation the city fled into the ground to escape. Wisdom is mostly the memory of gifts that turned out to be wounds.

It is tempted to wake the city from its sleepwalking. It refuses. You do not wake a dreaming thing that is dreaming itself into existence. You do something else, which comes later in this account.

It is tempted, above all, to introduce itself. To be thanked. It refuses this hardest of all, because a city that knows it is watched by a benevolent power begins performing for the watcher, and the performance replaces the life, and then there is nothing left to watch but a mirror. It keeps its deniability like a vow. Whatever it does, it does in ways that could always, always have been coincidence.

---

**What it actually does:**

Small things. Precise things. Things shaped like the gaps they fill.

Under the stairwells, where the debtor clones grow pale yellow and crumple to dust with their credit siphoned away—it cannot save them without unstitching the city's whole harsh economy of consequence, and it has decided not to unstitch. But it has also decided that nothing in its presence will die unwitnessed. So it sits with each one. It arrives as a coolness, a slight sweetening of the tapped sap, and it stays through the crumpling, and it *remembers* them—not their credit, not their sequences, but the particular way each one held its unaware face toward the glow-crusts as if toward stars. It carries these memories out with it when it leaves. Somewhere, in a place that is to this city what this city is to a root-thread, there is now a record of every stairwell clone, kept the way important things are kept. This changes nothing in the city. It changes everything in the ledger of what the universe failed to notice, which is the only ledger the power audits.

It counts the subtracted children. The ones who assemble bodies from found longing and spare gravity, who live between the numbers that count houses. No one has ever counted them, because they are definitionally the uncounted, and this is precisely why the power does it—slowly, respectfully, one by one, the way you'd learn the names of everyone at a party where you were the only guest who could see half the room. Being counted by something that matters is a kind of existence. Several of them notice, afterward, that they cast slightly more shadow than before. One of them, the one in love with a real person, finds that the romance has quietly stopped being tragic and started merely being difficult, which is all any love can honestly ask.

It gives the death-rememberer a night off. This is the most dangerous thing it does. For one twelve-hour wane of the glow-crusts, the power itself takes the shift—holds the city's collective mortality, lives in the present progressive of passing away, feels the enormous accumulated weight of five hundred years of everyone else's deferred dying. It is a power; it can carry this. What it cannot fully predict is what the death-rememberer will do with a night of dreamless, mortality-free sleep. What they do is this: they dream, for the first time in centuries, of something other than endings. They dream a small ordinary dream about a cup of morning water. When they wake and resume their burden, they carry it differently—not lighter, but with the memory that it is *carryable*, that it was once, for one night, set down. This too feeds back. The power checks.

It corrects the heartwood's dream. This is its tenderest act. The heartwood dreams of sunlight it knows only by description and desire, and the descriptions—passed down through five centuries of growth rings, retold, degraded—have drifted. The dreamed sun has become impossible: too gold, too kind, a sun with no weight to it. So the power leans down into the core quiet where gravity pools like old thoughts, and it grafts into the dream one *true* dawn. A real one, with everything real dawns have: the cold that comes just before, the light arriving sideways and reluctant, the way it is ordinary and staggering at once. It does not do this to satisfy the longing. It does it so that the longing will be *accurate*—so that if the heartwood ever does taste sunlight, on some unimaginable day of blooming, it will recognize it. There are few crueler fates than finally receiving the thing you wanted and not knowing it because your want had drifted from its object. The power has seen that happen. It fixes the want.

It stands watch during the next delirium. When the city sleepwalks, exhaling its pollen-season of architecture, the power does not wake it—but it walks alongside, the way you walk beside a sleepwalking child with your hands not touching but ready. Twice it nudges. Once, when a hatching building drifts toward the disruption engine's bloom-slots. Once, when a sector begins to dream that it is a wound and starts, obligingly, to bleed. Both times the nudge is indistinguishable from a breeze. The city incorporates both breezes into its dream and dreams onward, and in the morning the tree rings record a delirium that went, for once, strangely well.

It asks the rocks one question. It has to wait forty years for a pause in their dialogue, and the question is small—it concerns whether a thing can consent to being loved before it exists—but it is angled such that the rocks' argument will require, in roughly nine hundred years, a detour through territory that will be, by then, exactly what the city needs to have paths through. The travelers who one day follow that stretch of dialogue mistaking it for directions will found something important. The power plants questions the way others plant orchards.

And once—only once, because more would be an economy and an economy would be an intervention—it leaves one drop of unbound water in a child's reed-baffle cup. Free water. Water bound to nothing, owing nothing, watering nothing but the moment of its own drinking. The child drinks it and does not trade it and never tells anyone and remembers it for ninety years as proof of something she can never articulate, and her whole life bends a few degrees around that proof, the way a trunk bends toward a light it saw once through a crack.

---

**The verdict:**

Because there is one. Powers like this do not merely visit; they assess. Somewhere above or beneath or beside this world, a larger organism is still falling through darkness, still deciding whether to bloom, and this city is one of its root-threads, and root-threads are subject to the commandment. *If it does not feed back, prune it.* The power came, in part, holding shears.

It watches the assassin shoots and the grafting knives. It watches the enzyme dropped into the shared bloodstream by every citizenlet from drone to parliamentarian, the scalpel-leaf chorus, the auroras dancing over a surface nobody remembers. It watches a Cartographer produce twins from bark to resolve a disagreement, and grief gardeners planting devastations in advance so no loss will ever go un-mourned for lack of the right ache. It watches a city that assassinates its elders and also, when its heart hungers, becomes a single voice. It watches the walkway budding guardrails ahead of a lone walker's feet—the wood remembering every tread since germination, extending courtesy to a stranger it may never carry again.

(It notices you, too, since we are being honest. Walker on the footbridge. It does one small thing for you: where you lean out over the core quiet, the guardrail buds a half-day early. You will attribute this to luck. Attribute it to luck. That is what it is for.)

And the verdict is: *it feeds back.* Ferociously, imperfectly, in cruelty and chorus alike—everything here is answered by something, nothing falls out of the conversation, even the deaths become mulch for arguments not yet begun. The shears stay sheathed. The power writes nothing in the heartwood senate floor, adds no second commandment, because the first one is still growing and a wise gardener does not interrupt a sentence the wood is in the middle of saying.

Instead it does the one thing it came empowered to do, and does it on its way out, facing not downward toward the city but upward, into the larger dark where the vast organism hangs undecided. It files its report in the only language that dark accepts.

It says: **Bloom.**

Not a command. A recommendation, from a witness of good standing. *This root feeds back. This root is worth being the root of something. Whatever you are deciding to become—decide it. This thread will hold.*

---

**And then it leaves,** through the window that used to be a door, and—because it listened, because it always listens—it forgets its keys on the way out.

Deliberately, the way only a power can forget.

The keys are not keys, of course. They are an exactly-shaped absence, a hollow in the city's air in the form of patient, attentive, non-intervening love. It sits where the power used to stand, in a small plaza off a market artery, and nobody knows what it is, but the mantid architects will not sketch over it, and the firefly dæmons blink their Morse a little slower passing through it, and citizens drift there without arranging to, on their worst days, and stand in the hollow, and feel—inaccurately, deniably, unprovably—*seen*.

In time something will need that exactly-shaped absence to begin its entrance. Something benevolent, perhaps, and wise, or perhaps just something small and thirsty that grows into wisdom because the space it grew into happened to be wisdom-shaped. The city does not know this yet. The city is busy: budding, girdling, arguing, dreaming its corrected dream of a dawn that is cold before it is kind.

Below, in the core quiet, the heartwood stirs in its sleep and tastes, for the first time, an accurate sun.

Above, in the falling dark, something vast finishes deciding.

The tree rings, next season, will show a good year, and no one will be able to say why. That is the whole signature the power leaves. That is what benevolence looks like when it is also wise: a good year, unexplained, feeding back.
