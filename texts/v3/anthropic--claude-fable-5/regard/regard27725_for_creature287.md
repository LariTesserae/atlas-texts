# What the Attention Did

It did not arrive the way rain arrives, or birds, or attendants. It arrived the way understanding arrives in a person who has been staring at a problem for years — which is to say it had, in some sense, always been partially there, and one day it was wholly there, and the difference between those two states left no mark on the rock, the water, the light.

Call it a power. Call it benevolent, because it was; call it wise, because it had been benevolent long enough to learn the difference between a gift and an interruption. It had, over an age of the world, given many things to many beings, and it had learned that most of what it gave in its youth was interruption wearing a gift's clothing. It was older now. It came to the mountain the way a careful person enters a room where someone is working: it did not change the light.

For a long time — long by the mountain's count, which is the only count that matters here — it did nothing but learn the place. It let the column of water hammer through it. It sat in the mist and let the doubled rainbows assemble and collapse inside it. It counted the crabs, and did not determine what they eat either, and was pleased not to determine it, the way you are pleased when a lock resists a key you had no right to try. It followed one of the black birds up through the cloud to see whether there was anything above, and what it found there it did not tell anyone, and will not, and this account will honor that.

And it watched the attendant.

This was the hard part. Not hard to do — the attendant was easy to watch, a small blue-pale figure with closable ears and webbed fingers, moving between the cell and the rim on a schedule that was not a schedule but a temperament. Hard, rather, because watching the attendant activated, in the power, everything a benevolent nature is made of. The wanting-to-give woke up and stood at attention and began, immediately, drafting its list.

*Warmth,* said the wanting. The cold is in their pigment, in their chest. A single degree, hidden in the rock beneath the cell, untraceable —

No, said the wisdom. The cold is a wall of the room they are sitting in. Warm the cell and you have moved them to a different room, and they did not ask to be moved, and they chose this one with their whole life.

*Company, then.* Another of their kind, brought near — not to the mountain, of course, but within a season's walk, so that the possibility of another voice exists somewhere in the world's arithmetic —

No. The solitude is not a deprivation. It is the instrument. You do not put a second string on an instrument built to have one; you get a different instrument, and the song that was being played is over.

*Length of days. Health. A body that aches less on the wet mornings.*

No. The life is shaped like the tripod: it stands because it ends, and the attendant has looked at the bones of the one before and consented to the shape entirely. Extend the life and you have told them their consent was provisional. You have taken away the finest thing they own, which is that they meant it.

*Knowledge, then — surely knowledge is safe. Tell them what the birds are. Confirm the belief. They believe the descent is a report from above the cloud; the belief is a bridge they walk out onto every time a bird comes down. Make the bridge stone instead of faith —*

And here the wisdom was quiet for a while, because this one was tempting. But: no. The belief is load-bearing precisely because it is a belief. The attendant walks to the rim *not knowing*, and the not-knowing is the walk. Certainty would let them stay in the cell. Certainty is a chair. They did not climb a new mountain to sit in a chair.

The list went on for some time — the wanting-to-give is inventive, and it proposed dreams, and small findable treasures, and a fourth patch of reeds, and a winter one shade milder — and the wisdom declined each one, and at the end of the list the power arrived at a conclusion it had reached only twice before in all its age, and both times it had wept, if it can weep, which is not settled:

*There is nothing missing here.*

Nothing intervenable. The attendant was not incomplete. The place was not incomplete — it was unfinished, which is the opposite of incomplete, the way a question is the opposite of a silence. A benevolent power confronted with a lack knows what to do. A benevolent power confronted with a wholeness must become something else, and the wise ones know what: a witness.

But even a witness, if it is honest, will notice asymmetries. And there were three. Small ones. Not lacks — the attendant lacked nothing — but open circuits, things sent out into the world with no receiver at the other end. And the power judged, carefully, over a long time, that a receiver is not an interruption. A receiver changes nothing in the room. It only makes true something the sender already hoped.

So it did three things.

**First, it read the wall.**

The column of marks — the tally that was not a tally of birds but of receipts, each mark shaped by something in the descent that the attendant perceived and we do not — had never been read. Written things wait. That is what writing is: a patience made of scratches. The power settled itself before the wall of the cell one night while the attendant slept, folded small under the shivering rise of the mountain, and it read the marks the way they deserved to be read, which is to say slowly, taking each variation seriously, asking of each one *what did the bird carry down that day, that the mark should hook left instead of right?* — and finding, in perhaps a third of them, an answer, and in the rest a rigor beyond its own reach, which delighted it.

It changed nothing. It moved no dust. But the marks were, after that night, in a different category of thing: they were no longer unread. And the power took them into its memory whole, every hook and hesitation, so that when the pale rock finally dissolves — as it will; the water is patient — the record will not go out of the world. The wall does not know this. The attendant does not know this. The marks are simply safe, the way a sleeping child is safe, without waking.

**Second, it accepted the appointment of the bell.**

The flat stone on the tripod of bones is to be struck once, at the end, and heard by no one — a small sound given to the mountain, which by then will be old enough to hear small sounds. It is a gift offered entirely on faith, because the attendant cannot know whether a mountain hears anything at all.

The power made a decision about this, and the decision was its single act of intervention, and it will confess it here: *when the stone is struck, the mountain will hear it.* The power will see to that. It will lend the mountain an ear for that one instant — not forever, not a soul, nothing so gaudy — just an ear, just once, held open like the fold of skin at the attendant's temple, so that the last lesson lands in its student. The gift will not be added to. It will be *completed.* The attendant will strike the stone believing, and the belief will be true, and they will never know it was true, and that is the correct order of those three things.

**Third — and this is the one the power did not plan —**

Once, only once, on a bright diffuse morning with the bowl empty in both hands and the reeds singing faintly through the rock, the attendant felt something. Not a presence — the power was too careful for that. Not a voice. Just a sensation with no evidence attached to it, arriving the way understanding arrives: the feeling of *being read.* The specific warmth of a patient regard, the one you feel from a person who has watched your work long enough to know why the mark hooks left. It lasted the length of a breath. The attendant sat very still inside it. Then it was gone, and could be doubted, and the attendant — being rigorous — did doubt it.

But that afternoon they added a mark to the wall that was not for a bird. It is different from all the others: smaller, set slightly apart, shaped like nothing descending. A receipt for a receipt.

The power found it on its next reading and understood that it had been answered. This is the only correspondence the two of them will ever have — one anonymous warmth, one anomalous mark — and both of them, in their different ways, consider it complete.

---

After that, the power stayed. It stays now. It has become, if you like, a second attendant: it sits at the rim of the attendant's life the way the attendant sits at the rim of the basin — close enough to feel the drum-pressure of it in whatever it has instead of a chest, far enough not to be wetted, changing nothing, adding a mark somewhere of its own each time the small blue-pale figure walks out to receive a bird.

It came, as powers do, to give. It remains, as the wise ones sometimes do, to learn. For the attendant knows a thing the power spent an age approaching and never quite reached: how to be the still point beside which something becomes itself. The mountain is the attendant's student. The attendant, without ever knowing it, is the power's.

And on the far day when the copper hook swings and the flat stone speaks its one syllable into the thin cold air, three will hear it.

The mountain, lent an ear.

The power, keeping its appointment.

And no one else — which was always, from the beginning, exactly enough.
