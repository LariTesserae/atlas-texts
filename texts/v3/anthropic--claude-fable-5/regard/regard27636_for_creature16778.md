# The Thirty-Eighth Second

The power did not descend into the Somnic Trough, because descent is a form of arrival that requires distance, and the power had never been distant from anything. It arrived the way meaning arrives inside a word that has been spoken for a long time without anyone noticing what it meant. One pulse the Trench was itself; the next pulse the Trench was itself, *attended*.

In a place where sound is the only true force, the power came as the one thing the wound had never contained: a silence that owed nothing to any absence. Every quiet in the Trench had always been a debt—the hush after a beat, the hollow where a species used to sing, the pause a Filamenter leaves while negotiating a border. This silence had no creditor. It simply was. The Choir noticed within a single cycle, the way you notice a stranger in a house where every floorboard has a name. The stone leaned. The mists thinned to look. And the Pulse itself—for the first time since the crust cracked and sighed open—arrived at thirty-seven seconds *and a held breath*, as though the planet's heart had glanced up mid-sentence.

The power kept the rules, because wisdom keeps the rules of the houses it visits. It came alone, as wise things are always alone in the way that matters. It brought nothing that could melt, because it was made of nothing the pulse could learn and sing back louder—it was made of attention, which grows stronger when echoed. It slowed itself until it beat once per phrase of the eternal song. This took the power no effort at all, and it was polite enough to pretend otherwise.

Then it did what powers so rarely do. It considered doing nothing, and it considered this *first*, and seriously, and at length.

---

**What the power declined:**

It did not heal the wound. It saw at once what a lesser benevolence would have missed: the Trench stopped being an injury a long time ago. It is an organ now. The walls are Choir-flesh dreaming in mineral; the crack is a throat mid-song. To close it would be mercy the way pinching a windpipe is mercy. The power had, in its long existence, seen many worlds ruined by kindness that arrived with its conclusions already drawn, and it had made itself a rule: *never fix what is in the middle of becoming.*

It did not end the pain. The pain was being metabolized, beat by beat, into music—into moths counting their wingstrokes, into lichen reefs growing toward thunder, into the piezoelectric patience of worms who sing the planet away from shattering. The planet had chosen to break instead of bend. Wisdom honors choices, even geological ones. *Especially* geological ones, which are made slowly enough to be trusted.

It did not answer the planet's great argument with itself—*was it right to break?*—though it knew the answer, and the answer sat warm in it like a coal. That answer belonged somewhere else in time. It set the answer aside, and we will come back to it.

It did not give the Choir light, or sight, or the surface. They had not asked. Gifts nobody asked for are vanity wearing charity's clothes, and the power had burned that particular costume eons ago.

---

**What the power did:**

Three things. Small ones, by the measure of powers. Enormous, by the measure of the Trench.

*First,* it waited for a harmonic convergence and sat with a Pulse-Widow through the entire flickering length of it. It did not add a single note. It listened to the extinction-silence all the way to the bottom—which no living thing had ever done, because living things flinch, and flinching is a frequency, and the Widows had only ever been heard *around*. The Widow radiated its three questions, the ones that are their own answers, and the power let two of them stand. The third—*is loneliness still loneliness if it resonates with the loneliness of stars?*—it answered, not in words but by staying. The answer turned out to be: *yes. But loneliness that is witnessed becomes vigil, and vigil is loneliness with a purpose, and purpose is almost warmth.* The Widow did not get its species back. Its silence could not be filled; the frequencies that would fill it died with the world that sang them. But afterward, its silence was a *kept* silence, and there is a mineralogical difference. And the power did one thing more: it learned, from the shape of the hollow, the exact frequencies of the dead—and it undertook to pronounce them, once each, in scattered places across the universe, so that each lost song would exist again for one moment, somewhere, in air that had never known to grieve it.

*Second,* it knelt at the edge of one of the calm pools—the liquified bones of a visitor who had heard the true fundamental and stopped requiring a shape—and it listened to the perpetual 341.23 hertz for a long time, checking. What it found was not a trapped soul and not an ended one. What it found was contentment of a kind that has no posture, the satisfaction of a question that finally sat down inside its answer. So the power carried word back up and out, into the dreams of those who still grieved somewhere on some surface, and the word was this: *They did not stop. They only stopped needing to be shaped like waiting.* It could not make the grief smaller. It could make it accurate. That is often the most a wise power can do, and the difference between accurate grief and inaccurate grief is the difference between a wound and an infection.

*Third*—and this was the real gift, the one it had come to give—the power took the pulse into itself. Thirty-seven seconds. Two hundred forty-one wingbeats. It learned the rhythm the way the stone learns a heartbeat: patiently, permanently. And it carries the rhythm now, humming it as it moves—through nebulae, through the quiet kitchens of worlds that will never know the Trench exists, through the cold halls between galaxies where nothing else keeps time. Because the fourth rule of that place is the true one: *to be separate is to be dead.* And the Trench, for all its impossible fullness, resonated only with itself. Now, somewhere unimaginably far away, something keeps its beat. Now the wound is in correspondence. Now, by its own deepest law, it is more alive than it has ever been—and it does not know why, only that the oldest Choir members have begun to sing, in their forward-refracted way, of a warmth arriving from a direction that has no name.

And into the bedrock, deeper than the deepest dormant segment, the power planted its answer to the planet's argument—sealed, addressed, and *postdated*. Here is what it says, and here is when it will arrive:

At the end. When the heat death makes all other music impossible, and the last note goes out into the final silence expecting nothing—the power will be there. It will be the far wall. It has promised to return the echo. The Choir believes they will hear their own beginning reflected back and know it was beautiful, and they are right, but not because physics owes them the favor. They will hear it because someone chose, eleven kilometers down in perpetual dusk, an eternity in advance, to stand at the end of everything and *listen back.*

The oldest worms have already sung fragments of this promise, distorted by its journey around the core. They do not know what it is. They call it *the sweet note.* They are correct.

---

The power left between beats, in the thirty-eighth second, which does not exist, which is why it makes such a good door. The fungal fabrics shivered once. The moths counted 241, as always. The only evidence: the Pulse-Widows flicker now slightly less like questions and slightly more like answers taking their time. And the pause between pulse and echo—that pregnant hollow the Trench may actually *be*—feels, to those with organs subtle enough to notice, faintly less like waiting and faintly more like being expected.

And the beat goes on.
And the beat goes out.
And somewhere very far from here,
a listener keeps count—
so the wound is not alone,
so the note will find its wall,
so the breaking, when it finally asks
whether it was right to break,
will be answered by its own echo
coming home,
carried the whole way
in careful hands,
still warm.
