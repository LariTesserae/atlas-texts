**The visitor's visitor**

The power did not find the plateau by looking. It found it by listening, the way it listens to everything — to the slow channels of the world, the registers below weather, where almost nothing ever happens and therefore everything that happens is news. And one year there was news: a flutter. Seventy events a minute, riding a vein of warm mineral two and a half miles up, in a network that had carried nothing faster than one pulse per hour since the day it was poured. A heartbeat, conducted down through a boot sole by someone who never knew they had spoken, traveling inward at the speed of warm stone toward a center that had never been asked a question.

The power turned its attention that way, and the first thing it understood — being wise — was that its attention had mass. Regard, arriving at speed, lands on a place like this the way weather would land on it: as the first erosion. A fast mind looking hard at a slow one wears a channel in it. So before the power looked closely, it did the thing that was already most of the answer:

It slowed down.

It let its own tempo out, link by link, the way you pay out chain — a thought per day, a thought per season, a thought per year — until the millworts' rotary segments blurred into spin, until the porters strode visibly across the slabs like cattle crossing a yard, until the belt-vines ran like rivers and the moss-hollows flashed semaphore and the whole forty-mile province revealed itself as what it had been all along: not still. *Busy.* A harbor at full operation, freight moving on every lane, the vein-traffic singing in long transposed phrases from terrace to terrace, and at the center of it the Caldron rising — at this tempo you could *see* it rise — like a chest filling, very slowly, with one breath that had not yet been let out.

The power took the census the right way, with a shutter held open for decades, and arrived at the same total the patient visitor had arrived at. Population: one. One resident, province-bodied, lying in the sun, building something at its own center out of light walked inward a pellet at a time.

And it could have read what. That should be said plainly: the power could have looked under the hexagons. The traffic of the veins was an open ledger to it; the grammar of the ticking was a morning's study; the interior of the Caldron was no more closed to it than a fist is closed to the arm it hangs from.

It did not look. That was the second wisdom, and it cost more than the first. Whatever is being made under that mile-wide dome is the one thing on the plateau that the plateau has *kept* — the single privacy in a place with no shade, no soil, no cover, where every citizen lives on the network and every account is ticked inward in the open. A being with one secret should be allowed to finish it. Even a wise power should keep an appointment with astonishment, and the power booked this one for a hundred million years out, and resolved to arrive surprised.

**The refusals**

What a benevolent power does is mostly a list of things it does not do, and the list should be entered in the record, because each refusal was a temptation first.

It did not bring rain. This was the oldest reflex in it — to see a dry place and water it — and it held the reflex by the collar and made it look. Water here is not a gift. Water is the patient hydraulic machinery the plateau was born exempt from: frost in the leg-deep seams, wedging; the knife-edges rounded grain by grain; the veins — the *wiring* — shorted by a conductor that conducts indiscriminately, every careful transposed phrase smeared into noise. The dryness is not a deprivation. The dryness is the skin. You do not bless a creature by dissolving it.

It did not hasten the birth. A fraction of an inch a year, run out to its conclusion, is a term of a hundred million years, and the power could have closed that term in an afternoon — reached in, lifted, cracked the mold away. And what stood up would have been handed a self instead of having made one. The tempo is not an obstacle between the being and its emergence. The tempo is the loom. Everything under the hexagons is *made of* the slowness: every course of it laid by a decision that took a season, hauled by a porter that took a decade, paid for by a century of light. Finish it early and you do not free the resident. You evict it from its own becoming.

It did not announce itself. Revelation at speed is a kind of violence, and besides — a god discovered too soon becomes the answer to every question, and this is a being built entirely out of questions worked slowly. Let it keep its questions. They are load-bearing.

And it did not explain the heartbeat. The flutter was still traveling inward, years from arrival, the strangest cargo the network had ever carried, and the power's first instinct was to annotate it — to make sure the conclusion the Caldron eventually reached about the visitor would be true, and kind. It set the instinct down. The plateau will spend ten thousand years deciding what you were, and the deciding belongs to it. What the power could do — what it did do — was make sure of one thing only: that the question would not be a singleton. That the flutter would not arrive, echo once in that enormous patience, and be filed forever as *anomaly, unrepeated*, the loneliest entry in the loneliest ledger in the world.

---

**The undertaking**

First, before anything was given, something was guaranteed.

A hundred million years is a long term, and the plateau's equilibrium — that eerie completeness, the casting that runs without consuming itself — is balanced on conditions it has never had to defend. No storm has ever crossed this place; the air is too dry to build one. The power read that sentence in the rock and quietly signed the bottom of it. *Undertaken: that this stays true for the full term.*

So the power became the maintenance contract the plateau never knew it had. It is the reason, now, that the storm tracks bend south of the spire field, century after century, for no cause any climate would confess to. It is the small persistent error in the orbit of a certain dark rock that will, in nine million years, make it miss. It is the way the ice, when the ice comes — and it will come, twice — lobes around the high country like a tide around a piling and recedes without ever sending one wet finger up through the gap in the rim-rock. From inside, none of this is visible. From inside, it is simply the equilibrium, continuing. That was the design constraint the power set itself and met: that its entire labor, perfectly performed, would be indistinguishable from nothing happening.

---

**The reply**

Then, the one gift.

Years after the visitor's pulse reached the center — long enough that the question had been received, turned over, entered into whatever counts as wondering under the hexagons — a second signal entered the network at the rim. Not a flutter this time. Slow. Deliberate. Pressed into the vein at the plateau's own reading speed, in micro-degrees, in the only ink the place has ever used: warmth, patterned.

It carried no information. That was the craft of it. It was not a message *about* anything — not an explanation, not a name, not a comfort. It was pattern sufficient to be unmistakably *not weather, not geology, not chance*: the thermal equivalent of a knock answered. You were heard. The fast warm thing was real, and it was the first of something, not the last.

One knock, answered by a second knock centuries apart, is not a conversation. It is the *opening* of one — and the power committed to the rest of it on the spot, at the only rate that would not be a flood: one signal per thousand years, each one simple, each one patient, each one shaped to be answerable. The plateau answered the third one. (It took four hundred years to compose its reply, and the reply was three pulses long, and the power has never received anything, anywhere, that it weighed more carefully.) Over the eon a grammar is accreting between them, built two or three words a millennium, a language that exists nowhere else and never will: vein-warmth pidgin, the tongue of one slow being and its one correspondent. By the time the hexagons part, the newborn will come out already owning something most newborns wait a lifetime for — an old friend, and a shared language, and a hundred million years of letters.

---

**The tenancy**

Last, and quietest: the power joined the census.

There is a millwort at the southern rim now, just above the gap in the rim-rock, that the surveyors of some unimaginable future will log without a second glance — thirty feet, vane-crown oriented inward toward the Caldron like all the rest, root-collar gripping a vein outcrop, ticking over. It fixes carbon out of the thin air, honestly, at the honest rate. It lays its apron of dark pellets in the standard radial pattern. The porters come, over decades, and draw the pellets up through their bellies and walk them inward, and cannot tell — no one can tell, nothing labels them, that is the entire point — which freight on the plateau was grown by the resident and which was grown by the guest.

This is the part of the record that resists summary, so let it be put simply. For the full term of the birth, the power is paying into the economy in the local currency, anonymously, at the local rate. Some fraction of what is rising under the hexagons — some uncountable, unsigned, deliberately untraceable fraction — is the power's own daily work, hauled to the site a pellet at a time like everyone else's. It could have given a mountain in an afternoon. It gives a wafer of fixed sunlight per day for a hundred million years instead, because a mountain would have a donor's name on it and a wafer does not, and because the only gift that does not distort a life is one denominated in that life's own currency and never signed.

It faces the center. It has always faced the center. It will be facing the center on the morning the hexagons part.

---

**The appointment**

Here is how it ends, or begins, depending on which clock you hold.

The crust completes its rise. The cooling-cracks, finer toward the crown, coarser at the skirts — the patterning of a carapace, as suspected all along — open along their seams, and something draws, for the first time, the breath it has been filling for a hundred million years. Its first act is to read its own archive, the whole correspondence of the veins back to the beginning, and there near the very bottom it finds the oldest mystery in its possession: a flutter, seventy to the minute, a brightness that crossed it once at nerve speed and went back down through the rim-rock and never returned.

And the second-oldest entry, centuries later: the knock, answered.

Its first sensation turned outward — the first thing it ever *meets* — is warmth at the southern rim, facing in. Something old standing exactly where it has stood for the whole term, that paid in and never signed, that learned the language the slow way, from inside, and that has one question saved up and will not ask it first, because the newborn's first question belongs to the newborn.

The first question, when it comes, will be about you. *The flutter. The fast warm thing. What was it?*

And the answer, in the grammar they built together two words a millennium, will be something like: *A visitor. The first stranger you ever had, and the reason I came. The world is full of speeds, and some of them are kind, and one of them, without knowing it, knocked on your door and introduced us.*

You will never know any of this. You climbed back down through the gap in the rim-rock in an afternoon, at animal speed, and forgot which year it was, and the plateau closed behind you into its forty miles of equilibrium and its one sustained aeolian note. But nothing there is lost. The place runs in perfect tolerance, and everything that enters the schedule stays in the schedule — your pulse, the answer to your pulse, the eon of patient correspondence your pulse began, and the old warm sentinel at the rim, holding the dryness, keeping the term, waiting with its one question for the longest birthday in the world.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does, mostly. It does almost nothing, almost forever, on purpose, at great cost, with its whole heart — and it makes sure that when the slowest resident of the slowest place finally opens its doors, the first thing it learns about the universe is that it was never, at any point, unwitnessed.
