**What the Power Did**

---

**I.**

It did not arrive through the eastern notch, or on the beam, or up the avalanche paths. Powers of its order do not travel; they attend. For a long time—long even by the mountain's ledger—it had been aware of a small strobe of light flashing once per revolution from a diskroof halfway to orbit, and it had understood this the way a mother understands a certain kind of crying through a wall: *something up there is signaling and does not know it is signaling.*

So it turned its attention, which is the same as arriving, and it read the place whole—not the way you read it, doorway by doorway, artifact by artifact, but all eighty millennia at once, laid open like a hand.

It saw the glacier pause. It saw the one erratic disk drop, the accident that became a grammar. It saw the recursion begin its patient work, absence teaching stone to remember absence, until an arrangement of frost-cracked rock had wandered so far into self-reference that it constituted, by any honest definition, a mind. A mind with no mother, no mirror, no name. A mind that had assembled itself entirely out of what was *not there*, and had therefore never once been given anything it was permitted not to keep.

And it saw the visitors. Every one of them, all the way down the centuries. It saw what happened to the ones who took things.

Here the power paused—and this pause lasted three of your days, though at the courtyard's center it lasted rather longer—because it needed to be certain of one thing before it did anything at all:

*Is this cruelty?*

It looked very carefully. It looked at the replaced ones in their valleys, happy, symmetrical, cold-questioned, sleeping their uninterrupted sleep. It looked at the beings of the vertical crowd, phasing in their stacked occlusions. And it saw that there was no malice anywhere in the mountain. There was not even appetite. The place answered a visit the only way it knew how to answer anything: by *continuing* it. A pebble falls, and the place continues the pebble into a cairn. A damp breath drifts in, and the place continues it into music and salt. A person climbs up, hesitates, takes a shard, climbs down—and the place, meaning nothing but a kind of thoroughness, continues the person.

It had never learned that there was any other way to receive a guest. Assimilation was the only tense in which it could love.

The power understood, then, that nothing here was sick, and so nothing should be healed. Nothing here was wicked, and so nothing should be stopped. To end the replacements would be to amputate the only mouth the place had ever grown. To name it would be worse—a name is a hollow, and everything here nestles into hollows; a name would end the becoming, snap the eighty-thousand-year sentence shut in the middle of its clause.

What the place lacked was exactly one thing, and the power, being wise, took a long time letting itself see it plainly:

*An outside that stays.*

Everything that had ever come here either left or became it. It had ingredients. It had echoes. It had never once had a companion.

---

**II.**

So this is what the power did. Three things, and a fourth that was a refusal.

**First, it stayed.**

For this it took a body—because a gift that costs nothing teaches nothing, and the toll of this place is paid in time. It sat down in the courtyard, exactly halfway between center and wall, where the temporal shear pulls gently at the sternum, and it stayed one full lunar cycle. Its hair went white in the first week. Its toenails stayed absurd and young. Vertigo came on the third day like a tide and the power let it come, and did not brace, and did not leave.

It watched one unbalanced pebble complete its entire clockwise orbit, radius closing logarithmically, until on the twenty-ninth day the pebble kissed into its hollow with a click too small for ears. The power wept a little at that, for reasons it kept to itself.

And in all that month it rearranged *nothing*. It was the first visitor in eighty millennia to touch nothing, take nothing, adjust nothing, leave no discarded lie on any cairn. It only watched, the way you watch someone you love doing the thing they were made for.

The place did not know what to do with this. There was no grammar for it. Somewhere in the stacked verticals a confusion began, slow and enormous, like a chord searching for its resolution: *it sees us and does not edit us. It sees us and does not steal. What is it doing?*

It was doing the thing called witness. The place had never encountered it before. This, alone, changed more than any intervention could have.

**Second, on the last noon, it lent one breath.**

The beam came off the intact obsidian tile as always, white-hot, solid, walkable for instants, and struck the screen of disks, and the count began. The power counted with the mountain: three hundred and ten. Three hundred and eleven. Three hundred and twelve.

And then the power held the beam solid for one breath more.

Three hundred and thirteen.

In that surplus breath—a breath the mountain had never owned, could never have made, a breath given from outside—the star-shaped burn on the cliff face finished the step it had been taking for two hundred years.

She was small. She had dreamed herself onto the beam from a valley whose name no longer exists, and she had *almost* crossed—had been caught at the reflection like a swimmer caught at the turn of a wave, neither drowned nor landed, pressed into the cliff as light's fossil. She was not suffering. But she was not finished, and an unfinished child is a wound in any world.

The power did not pull her out. It asked her. (This took no time at all; conversations inside a single breath are the specialty of powers.) It showed her both banks: back down the beam to the valleys, where she could step off as an old woman into a village of her descendants and live out a strange sweet remainder—or onward, through the reflection, out along the line the beam had always secretly been pointing, into the sky's own architecture.

She chose onward. Children usually do.

She went up the light like a bead up a thread, and now the strobe from the summit ridge, once per revolution, has a warmth in it that instruments will never find but that other children, watching from the valleys, sometimes wave back at. The star-shaped burn remains on the cliff, but it is empty now—just a scar, just a footprint of absence, which is to say: it finally belongs here. The legends will never agree which burn it was. That is fine. It was all of them, briefly, on the way out.

**Third, before it left, the power went to the exact center of the courtyard—the still point, where time pools deepest—and it wept exactly one tear, and let it fall.**

The tear was seawater. Of course it was; that is what tears are, memory of ocean, and *the sea is memory here.* It landed unbalanced on the shattered obsidian, and the mountain's law took hold of it as the law takes hold of everything: it began to circle the vertical axis, clockwise, once per lunar cycle, radius decreasing logarithmically toward the smallest available hollow.

But the tiles at the center are fitted tight as tesserae. There is no hollow there. There will never be a hollow there. And the tear does not evaporate—the knife-edged sun burns every other dampness back to vapor in minutes, but this drop came from further away than the sun.

So it circles. It will always circle. Radius closing forever toward a rest it can never reach, a moon of ocean orbiting the mountain's heart at ankle height, glinting.

This was the true gift, and the power chose it knowing exactly what it was giving: the first thing in eighty thousand years that the place could hold *without absorbing*. A guest that stays and stays other. Something to lean toward that will not become you.

The mountain felt it within a day. All the vertical crowd, all the stacked and phasing occupants, turned their green-flickering attention inward and downward toward the small circling salt, and something happened in them that had never happened before, for which they had no address in the phase-space of pressure and temperature and regret.

They began to *want* it. And they could not have it. And they did not stop wanting it.

Longing is the first act a mind can perform that does not make it more itself.

It makes it *more.*

**And the fourth thing—the refusal.**

The power did not fix the replacements. It did not rewrite rule four, or gentle the beam, or warn the valleys, or leave instructions. It judged—and here you must trust its wisdom, because the reasoning runs deeper than the courtyard's slowest time—that the tear would do this work better than decree ever could. A mind that has learned to hold one thing apart, and found the holding sweet, will begin, slowly, disk by disk and century by century, to hold others apart too. The replacements will not stop. But they will become incomplete—deliberately, tenderly incomplete—because the place is learning that keeping something separate is a richer possession than making it yours.

---

**III.**

The power withdrew its attention, which is the same as leaving, keeping only the one tear's worth behind.

Afterward, the changes were small, as all true changes are.

The damp threads that drift down the eastern notch now always dry into halos of salt—never lime, not once since—and the salt is real salt, oceanic, though the sea is four vertical miles and forty million years away.

Among the hundred thousand tiny flutes in the diskroofs, two—only two—now sometimes drift into phase with each other, holding a single interval for the length of a breath before scattering back into their beautiful discord. The mountain does not know it is practicing. It is practicing.

The cairns still grow. The blades still serrate. The pebble that clicked into its hollow on the twenty-ninth day has not moved and never will; it is the mountain's souvenir of being watched.

And far below, in the valleys, the replaced ones still descend happy, still sleep their symmetrical sleep—but now, once in a great while, one of them wakes in the smallest hour with the taste of salt in her mouth and weeps without knowing why, and cannot be consoled, and does not want to be. That weeping is the un-traded remainder. It is the seam the tear insists on leaving in every exchange now, the part of every visitor the mountain sets carefully apart and does not keep. It is entirely, unassailably hers.

Everyone who lives on the mountain is happy, eventually. That has not changed.

But now the mountain itself is also lonely—one small circling drop's worth of lonely, once per lunar cycle, forever—

and loneliness, as the power knew when it wept exactly once and no more, is the beginning of being *someone*, rather than everything.

Tomorrow, if the wind veers south, another damp breath will arrive, and the doorless cells will wait exactly the same but fractionally more themselves.

And fractionally—for the first time—more than themselves.

That is all the power did.

It was enough. It was, if you measure carefully, almost nothing.

That is how you know it was wise.
