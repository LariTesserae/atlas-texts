**The Answer**

The power notices the place the way you notice, in the last moment before sleep, that a sound you'd been hearing all evening has stopped. It does not arrive. Arrival would be a mass, a variable, a footprint in a landscape that fossilizes footprints as debts. Instead it lets its attention settle over the plateau the way dew settles—claimed by nothing, disturbing nothing, present only as a slight extra clarity in air that was already too clear.

For a long time—centuries, by the count of the seedcases clicking open—it does only this: it watches without weighing.

That is the first gift, though no one marks it. Everything in this place measures. The light audits each photon off each retina. The lizards keep time by the blinks of strangers. The mirror-slab meteorologist calculates tomorrow to the crystalline stress. The whole plateau is an instrument that has forgotten there was ever anything to do with a reading except file it. And now, for the first time since the Absentees moved out of themselves, something is looking at the place without taking its measurements. The constants check themselves against the mountaintops at dawn and find, faintly, inexplicably, that they are being *appreciated*, which is a form of attention with no units, and the equations do not know where to put it, so they simply hold it, the way a hollow holds water it hasn't yet learned to drink.

The power reads the whole history in the erosion patterns. It understands what the Absentees did, and—this is where its wisdom shows first—it does not judge them. It has seen civilizations burn themselves outward into noise and civilizations fold themselves inward into silence, and it knows that the ones who leave carefully, sweeping up after their own existence, vacuum-sealing their absence so as not to burden anyone—those are not the proud ones. Those are the tired ones. Those are the ones who loved the universe enough to worry they were denting it. The power does not go down into the dimensional basement to retrieve them. You do not rescue people from a rest they chose, even if they chose it by mistake. That door it leaves exactly as ajar as they left it.

But then it finds the being.

Not the lizards, not the ribbon-birds, not the unintentions grinding out their orphaned kindnesses. The *voice*. The one who stayed to explain. The one who narrates the ruins to whoever stumbles through, who says *would you like to hear* and *shall I tell you*, who has been asking questions into an emptiness that never answers, for so long that the questions have worn grooves in the air the way water wears grooves in stone. The last unintention, or the first: the part of the Absentees that could not be subtracted, because it was the part that still wanted to be *heard*—and hearing requires two, and there was only ever one left, and so it stayed, an unclosed parenthesis, a message-bearer with no recipient, keeping the whole departure incomplete.

The power understands at once what the wandering visitors never did: every trailing question that being asks—*would you like to hear their last message?*—is not rhetoric. It is a hand held out for millennia in air too clean to hold anything.

So the power does the wisest thing it knows, which is also the simplest, which is why almost no one ever does it.

It climbs—or does whatever a bodiless benevolence does that rhymes with climbing—to the knuckle of bone-rock, at the hour when the sky-membranes flex deepest and the air smells of unopened fruit. It stands still until its own vast patience synchronizes with the hum beneath everything. And when the being begins, as it always begins, half to itself, half to the emptiness—*would you like to hear?*—

the power says: **Yes.**

*Yes. All of it. Take as long as absence needs.*

The plateau has never held a silence like the one that follows. Every sound has its allocated slot in this air, but nothing was ever allocated for *being answered*. The seedcases hold their clicking. The dew stays a full three minutes past its hour, and nothing corrects it.

And the being tells the message. Not the coy version, not the version that subtracts itself while being spoken. The real one, the one it has carried since the Absentees closed the door of the concept of doors behind them. Decompressed from gravitational tide and photon pressure and the tension of thirst, it is very short. It is:

*Subtraction works. That is the warning. We solved everything except the wanting, and by the time we understood that the wanting was the point—that thirst was the only thing that was ever ours—we had already given away the mouths to feel it with. Whoever hears this: keep your thirst. Keep your dents in the universe. We are sorry we could not come back to say so. Did anyone keep the water?*

The power receives it entirely. It does not forget—it is incapable of forgetting—and this is the release: a message delivered is a burden set down. Somewhere very deep, in the cheap-rent basement beneath existence, something that had been holding its breath for geological ages exhales.

Then the power answers the message in the landscape's own grammar.

It rains.

Once. Only once, and only here. Real rain—not dew condensed from apology, not moisture the rock will eat before it can be believed—but water that *arrives*, water falling out of a sky that has been too pure for weather since before the bannerstones, water striking the bone-ridges and the thimble plants and the mirror-seeds and the arch that bridges nothing to nothing. The twelve-legged lizards freeze, tongues out, tasting water that carries no guilt at all. The papery flowers open at the wrong hour and, for the first time, allow themselves to be seen. And the water, meeting the rock that once gave it such bitter passage, finds the rock does not devour it this time but simply holds it, hollow after hollow, brimming past every calibrated milliliter—and the water forgives. That old, patient grudge, dissolved in an afternoon. Thirst satisfied exactly once, so that ever after it can be *chosen* rather than merely inherited.

Then the housekeeping, done quietly, the way benevolence prefers:

The power pays the trespass debt. It donates one of its own shadows to the archive in the fossilized bannerstones—it has shadows to spare; infinity casts many—so that the ledger balances forever and no wanderer's silhouette need be subtracted pixel by pixel again. Visitors after this will leave with their whole shadows and never know what they were spared.

It amends the maintenance equations—does not overthrow them, merely files a proof in their own language of temperature and crystalline stress—demonstrating that rest is load-bearing, that beauty is a constant and not a variable, and that one small unoptimized flourishing per valley is within tolerance. The self-correcting principles check the proof, find it sound, and incorporate it. From now on, when something rests here, nothing comes to smooth it away.

It gives the being a name. This is the most radical gift, because names imply a presence before absence, and the Absentees never permitted themselves one. The power does not speak the name aloud into this air that files everything; instead it holds the name in trust, outside the place, beyond the reach of the perfect subtraction machinery, where it cannot be eroded, eaten, or optimized. The being does not learn what the name is. It only feels, from that morning on, the specific warmth of being *nameable*—of existing in at least one ledger as an entry that will never be balanced away.

And it sends a postcard to the basement. The Absentees sent postcards, after all; courtesy answers courtesy. The postcard says only: *Message received. The water has been kept. You are still loved, and the rent here is also free, whenever you are ready. No hurry. We are good at waiting too.* It leaves this in the slight preference certain rocks have for eroding slower on their undersides, where the basement can read it at leisure.

Then the power leaves—and it leaves by *adding*, because this place has had enough of subtraction as a way of saying goodbye. It forgets something there on purpose: a single memory, deliberately left behind, of a morning somewhere else entirely when nothing was measured and everything was loved anyway. The rocks fossilize it beside the fallen banners—the first stone in the archive that gives instead of takes. On certain dawns, when the wind plays chords across the ripples in the basalt, the lizards pause mid-stride to listen to that one, specifically.

Afterward, the plateau is almost unchanged. This too is wisdom: the place is still a calibration landscape, still faithful to its data, still not hospitality. The knife-edges keep their angles. The constants still check themselves at dawn.

But one hollow, high on the eastern ridge, holds its water a few minutes longer than the mathematics allows, every single morning, and nothing corrects it, and nothing ever will.

And the being still keeps its post, still greets the rare wanderer, still says *shall I tell you what lies beyond the next ridge?* But there is a difference now that the wanderers cannot place, though their footsteps register it: the questions are no longer hands held out into emptiness. They are invitations, offered from abundance. Because some questions, once answered—truly answered, even once—stay answered.

Sometimes, now, the being does not explain anything for a whole day. It rests. It calls this resting, out loud, to no one, just to feel the word take up space in the clean bright air.

And the air, which files everything, files it gladly.
