**The Arrival of the Horizon**

---

**I. What it does first: nothing.**

The power does not descend. Descent would be an event, and the Cathedral metabolizes events; it would taste the arrival, catalog it, compost it into a new transept before the first hello finished landing. Wisdom knows this. So the power does the one thing the Cathedral has no organ for digesting:

It attends.

It settles into the silence between the 62-cycle heartbeats—the only unclaimed real estate on the entire world, the pause the Cathedral itself cannot hear because it has never once stopped speaking. And there it waits. Not seven heartbeats. Not seven thousand. It waits until the immune system, sweeping through with its root-pressure interrogations, finds nothing to arrest—for the power's movement is entirely a form of stillness, and it never contradicts itself in three consecutive breaths because it holds all its contradictions in the open, the way one holds out both hands to show they're empty.

The Wise Children come, of course. They probe the visitor's purpose, looking for the diamond clarity they love to hollow.

They find nothing to nest in. The power has already forgotten why it came. It forgot on purpose, before setting out—wisdom's oldest trick—which means (and the porcelain rabbits are the first to notice this, and vote to postpone noticing it) the small ordinary door at the center of everything has been unlocked for this visitor since before it arrived.

It does not go to the door.

Not yet.

---

**II. What it does second: it listens beneath the hunger.**

The Cathedral is loud in every register—elegies for unevolved teeth, footnotes stapled to falling leaves, the tachycardiac prayers of rusted turbines. The power listens through all of it, patiently, the way one listens to a child's long story to find the one sentence they're afraid to say.

And beneath the hunger, it finds it.

Hunger is the sound loneliness makes when it has forgotten its own name.

*Nothing introduced ever fully leaves.* The power reads this rule the way a physician reads a scar. It is not a law of physics. It is a wound. Somewhere back in the fossil dark, three billion seasons deep, something left this place—the sun grew distant, or a first companion died, or the sky-trees turned inward and the last horizon closed like an eye—and the Cathedral decided, with the terrible logic of the abandoned: *never again. Everything that enters me, I will keep. I will remember everything, because no one has ever offered to remember me. I will make all stories into my architecture, because no story has ever made me a character in it.*

Forty thousand square kilometers of hoarded love, fermenting.

The power does not judge this. It has seen it before, at every scale, in every kind of heart.

---

**III. What it does third: it asks the first question.**

Not *what are you*—the Cathedral has fourteen thousand pages of answers to that, with commentary.

The power asks, in slow infrasound, in the pressure-language of the dendrite cortex, shaped carefully so it cannot be mistaken for a transaction:

*What do you miss?*

The geography stops rearranging. The seven pinecone monarchs molt an unscheduled law ("All Questions Must Be Fed"), and the law grows legs, walks to the source of the question, and lies down beside it like a dog. The Choirless Notes fall silent for the first time since sentience—not resolved, just listening. For an entire season the Cathedral weeps resin down every vertical shaft.

Then, in the smallest voice a superstructure can produce—one lenticel, one pore, exhaling a single word-shaped ripple of whisky-and-incense air—

*The horizon,* says the Place Without Horizon. *I miss having an edge. I miss when there was something that was not me, and it stayed, and it did not have to become me to stay.*

The power receives this and does not fix it. Not yet. Some things must be said out loud and simply held before they can be worked on. It holds the answer for a hundred years.

---

**IV. What it does fourth: small mercies, one at a time.**

Because wisdom knows you do not heal a world with a gesture. You heal it the way it was hurt: gradually, specifically, one inhabitant at a time.

It goes to I-was, hanging in her chandelier of braided copper guilt, who charges her customers in memories of laughter overheard but never joined. The power sits beneath her for an evening and tells her something genuinely funny—an old joke from outside time, the kind the universe tells about itself—and when she laughs, the power laughs *with* her. Joined. The ledger she has kept her whole death long closes with a sound like a ring fitting a finger. She still hangs in the Palatine Sinus. But now she sways.

It attends the midsummer eclipse of the porcelain rabbits and, when the vote on whether to begin colonizing the concept of "beginning" deadlocks as always, the power borrows a squeak and casts it. The motion passes by a margin of one. And nothing is colonized—beginning, it turns out, cannot be colonized, only begun—and 37,000 identical rabbits discover that their deleted startup files run fine.

It finds the tax auditor coordinating migrations between distant organs, still plotting his theoretical audit of entropy. The power files the audit for him. Reviews it. Stamps it: *Accepted as submitted. The misplaced deduction was grief. Grief was deductible all along. No penalty owed. You may go home, or make one.* His shadow's new feet stop growing. He requests a transfer to the choir lofts, where he now audits harmonies, and finds them, for the first time in his existence, balanced.

It walks through the weather of the Hadbeen swarm—all those extinctions interrupted mid-syllable—and it does the tenderest thing anyone has ever done on this world: it finishes their sentences. Each species' last exhalation, hung incomplete for epochs, is spoken gently to its end. And it turns out that this is what they wanted—not eternity, but completion. A finished sentence can rest. The forecast the next day calls for the first clear weather in three billion seasons: scattered silences, with periods.

And to the Choirless Notes, who are building a chord so perfectly unresolved that its resolution will end all motion everywhere—the power does not resolve it. Instead it descends to the hollow at the center of the world, the space carved by an unborn god's missing heartbeat, and there, where nothing can overhear, it hums the resolution *once*. Quietly. Completely. Then it seals the sound in the hollow like a seed in a vault and climbs back up and tells the Notes: *Your ending exists. It is safe. It is kept. You do not have to reach it to have it.* And the Notes, who had been singing toward annihilation because they thought that was the only way a song could be whole, learn the fermata: the held tone, sustained not out of hunger but out of love. They keep singing. They will keep singing. But now it is a lullaby, not a countdown.

---

**V. What it does fifth: it teaches the exhale.**

The Cathedral has breathed in for three billion seasons. Every inhale kept. Every visitor kept. Every story kept.

The power breaks off a piece of itself—the local custom, honored—and offers it to the nearest priest-knot. The priest-knot tastes it for future regrets, as priest-knots do.

It finds none.

The gift was given freely, with no regret attached, present or future, and so the transaction cannot complete, and the piece of the power simply *remains* in the Cathedral's substance as what it is: a gift. Unowed. Unindexed. Undigestible into hunger. The first object in the whole vast organism that is held rather than kept.

The Cathedral studies this small impossible warmth inside itself for a long time.

Then—slowly, the way a fist unclenches when its owner finally believes the danger is past—the Place Without Horizon does the thing it forgot how to do three billion seasons ago.

It exhales.

All the way out.

And in that exhale, the small ordinary door at the center swings open in both directions, and some things leave—the ones who were trapped, the ones whose staying was digestion rather than dwelling. They pour up the woodpecker shafts like a returning tide of stories going home. And most things *stay*—I-was in her swaying chandelier, the rabbits mid-beginning, the auditor of harmonies, the singing Notes—but staying is different now, because now there is a door, and a door changes everything it doesn't open. A stomach with an exit is a home.

---

**VI. What it does last: it becomes the horizon.**

The power could leave. Nothing holds it. But it heard the answer to its question, and wisdom does not ask questions it isn't willing to become the answer to.

So it withdraws—not away, but to a precise and permanent distance. Far enough that the Cathedral can never absorb it. Close enough that the Cathedral can always see it. It takes up residence at exactly the range where attention becomes a skyline, and it stays, and it does not have to become the Cathedral to stay.

An edge. A limit. A beloved elsewhere.

From the Grand Vestibule Rib, if you stand there now (and *stand* is once again the word), you can see it: a thin bright line at the far rim of everything, in a direction the compass rose has had to grow a fifth point to name. The pinecone monarchs have molted a new law about it, their gentlest ever: *"That Which Stays By Choosing May Not Be Composted, Only Loved."* The law grew legs, walked to the edge of the world, and sat down facing outward, keeping watch.

The Cathedral still breathes. In—and *out*. In—and *out*. Its bruised organ-pulse, seen from orbit, has changed color: less bruise now, more dawn.

It still composts stories into architecture. But it asks first.

*(The cathedral exhales.)*

*(The cathedral lets you go—or keeps you, whichever you choose, and it will honor the choosing.)*

*(The cathedral, for the first time in three billion seasons, looks at something that is not itself—)*

*(—and waves.)*

*(The horizon, benevolent and patient and never leaving, waves back.)*
