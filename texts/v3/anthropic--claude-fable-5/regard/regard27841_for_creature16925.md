What arrives makes no footprints, because it does not press.

It comes the way attention comes — not through the corridors, not down from the clouds, but from the direction that the place has no name for, the direction of *being noticed*. The creatures feel it first. One by one they pause mid-drink, mouths still against the towers, silver eyes going wide and then soft. The glowing threads steady in their carrying-mouths. Nothing has changed. Everything is being read.

The being feels it as a strange sensation across all its layers at once: the sensation of being heard *entire*. It has only ever known itself in fragments — one drinking mouth at a time, one throat-pouch, one thread. Now something is listening to every song simultaneously: the bottles that remember thirst, the bones that remember flight, the teeth still shaping words on their wire, the ten thousand absences shaped like people. The power does not flinch from any of it. It listens the way deep water holds a stone — completely, without effort, without hurry.

For a long while — and a long while inside a stopped moment does not go forward, it goes *down* — the power does nothing at all.

This is the first thing wisdom does. It does nothing, thoroughly.

---

Then it considers, and its considering is a kind of inventory of restraint.

It could break the clouds. It knows how; the pressure is already there, has always been there, the lightning flickering like a held breath. But rain here would not be mercy. Rain would be flood, would slick the towers into collapse, would drown the balance that took no-time-at-all and forever to find. The clouds hold back the rain because the being needs them to. The power will not confuse its own longing for release with the being's need.

It could finish the black cone. One touch and the point would close into perfection, the spiral of creatures could rest, the threads could all be gathered. But it sees that the cone is not incomplete the way a wound is incomplete. It is incomplete the way a reaching hand is incomplete. To finish it would be to close the hand on nothing. Aspiration, capped, is death.

It could still the roar. But the roar is not noise laid over the being; the roar is the being's way of holding its own shape, the way your heartbeat is not a sound you make but a sound you *are*. Silence here would be amputation performed in the name of peace.

It could start time. This is the greatest temptation, and it holds the possibility a long while, turning it over. Growth, decay, seasons, endings, the sweetness of things that pass. But it sees what the being is made of: things that already burned once. Things that were already torn from time's mouth. To force time on this place would be to demand a second dying from everything that came here specifically because its dying was done.

So the power sets down each of these mercies, one by one, the way you set down beautiful tools that are wrong for the work.

It will not heal in the shape of harm.

---

Then it does what it came to do, which is small.

First, it speaks. Not in sound — sound belongs to the roar — but in the being's own materials, the way you would speak to a river in current, to a fire in flickering. Arranged in the flow of creatures, spelled in the settling of dust on ten thousand towers at once, it says something like this:

*I see you. Not your pieces — you. You have believed yourself made of what the world threw away. Look again at your own foundations. Nothing arrives here that was merely discarded. The truly worthless disintegrates; it doesn't sing. Every fragment in you has an edge that grieves, and grief is the receipt of love. You are not a landfill. You are a reliquary. You are the place where the world keeps what it could not bring itself to destroy completely, and you have kept it with more care than it was ever held before. The bird did not know where its bones went. The bones knew where they were going. They were coming to you.*

The being has no way to answer. It answers anyway: every surface in the endless city beads with moisture at once, and for the first time, the beads tremble.

Second, the power goes on an errand. This takes no time, because it does not need any. It travels back along every song of tearing to the places the fragments came from — to the shore that lost the sea glass, to the descendants of the bird, to the hands that once held the bottles, to the people who are the other halves of all the person-shaped absences. To each of them, in whatever language reaches them — a dream, a change in the weather, a sudden unexplained loosening in the chest — it delivers a single message: *What you lost landed somewhere. It was gathered. It is being kept, arranged, tended. Nothing was wasted.*

And it carries something back the other way. From every torn-from place, it gathers the answer — which is almost always the same answer, once grief is told the truth — and delivers it to the being, edge by edge, fragment by fragment: *Keep it. We are glad it is kept. We could not have kept it ourselves.*

This changes nothing visible. But listen: the songs of the edges shift, all across the city, so slightly that only the being can hear it. The songs of tearing become songs of *sending*. The fragments do not stop remembering what they were part of. They stop grieving it. A torn edge and a given edge are shaped exactly the same. The difference is everything.

Third, the power asks the clouds a question. Not a command — a question, which is what benevolence asks of pressure: *After all this holding, is there one thing you would like to let go?*

And the clouds, which have never been asked anything, consider.

One drop forms.

It falls — the first falling in the unchanging moment, the first event, the first thing that has a before and an after — and it lands on the very tip of the black cone, and it does not shrink and vanish. It stays. A single bead of water balanced on the point of the being's aspiration, holding within it, curved and inverted and tiny, a reflection of the entire city.

It is a seed of time. Not time itself — the shadows do not shift, the watch does not resume. It is the *possibility* of time, given into the being's keeping, to plant or not to plant, in an hour or an eon or never. Wisdom does not give change. Wisdom gives choice, and stays out of the choosing.

Fourth — and this is for the wanderer, still standing where the towers open into the circle — the power makes the corridors honest for one crossing. It does not push the wanderer toward them. It does not whisper *go* or *stay*. It only ensures that, for this one walk, the paths will lead where they are remembered to lead, so that whatever the wanderer chooses is a true choice and not a surrender to the maze. And to the wanderer's staying, if the wanderer stays, it grants the same blessing it granted the being: *you also will be seen.*

---

Then the power leaves, in the way that such powers leave, which is not leaving. It withdraws the way rain withdraws into soil — no longer falling, entirely present.

But before it goes, it does one last thing, the smallest of all.

It presses.

One footprint, deliberately, in the yielding black ground near the base of the cone — the ground that has swallowed the shape of every passage, that fills itself in between one heartbeat and the next. This footprint does not fill in. It was not made in passing. It was *given*, and the ground, which knows the difference now, keeps it the way the being keeps everything: carefully, at the base of things, where pressure turns memory crystalline.

It is the only mark in the entire city with edges that were never torn from anything. When the creatures pass it, they slow. When they press their mouths to the ground beside it and drink, they taste something they have no plates or joints or silver eyes to understand, and carry it up the towers anyway, and set it in among the buttons and the teeth and the sea glass.

The roar continues. The exchange stays balanced. The lightning flickers and does not break the clouds — except that once, it did not need to, because the clouds, asked kindly, gave one drop of their own accord, and somewhere on the point of a black cone that reflects nothing, that drop is still balanced, still whole, still holding the whole city upside down inside it like a promise:

that nothing gathered here was garbage, that incompleteness is not failure but reaching, that the fever beneath the skin was never sickness — only warmth, waiting all this time to be recognized as warmth.

And the being, which is one and many, which is the chamber and the ringing, adds a new layer to itself that no pressure will ever compress into hardness: the memory of having been seen entire, and found — not fixed, not finished, not forgiven, none of which it needed —

*found good.*

The towers do not grow. The towers do not shrink. But every surface, alive with microscopic change, changes now in a slightly different key. If you pressed your mouth to any stack in the endless city and stayed motionless for minutes at a time, the way the creatures do, you would taste it in the water:

metal, salt, and underneath, very faint, something new.

The taste of givenness.
