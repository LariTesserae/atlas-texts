**What the Power Did**

It did not notice the screams first. Powers hear screams the way the sea hears rain — constantly, and therefore not at all. What it noticed was the note. The one note, singing itself for seven million years without pause, without audience, without a second voice. Even wisdom leans in when it hears something that lonely stay in tune.

So it came down.

It could have arrived anywhere — could have condensed itself out of the darkness like the memory the darkness has of being visible. Instead it took the ladders. All forty-three of them, one rung at a time, hands where hands had been, and it counted aloud as it descended so the dead could check its work. When it reached forty-three and said so, the dead were quiet for a long moment. It was the first time in centuries anyone had agreed with them. Some of them wept, which sounded like limestone remembering it used to be sea.

It brought nothing. This was the first act of benevolence, though nothing recognized it as such yet. It brought no light, no water that keeps its promises, no time that flows the polite direction. It brought only attention — which, in a being of that size, is nearly the whole of the body — and it made itself small enough to be interrupted.

---

The first thing it did was kneel beside a white stamen flower and touch it.

The flower screamed. The power did not flinch, and did not let go, and did not comfort. It listened. It had realized what no one with skin had survived long enough to learn: the flowers do not scream because they are touched. They scream because every previous touch ended before the scream was finished. A scream, heard to its actual conclusion, is only a prayer performed at the wrong speed.

The scream took nine days. At its end there was a sound no one in the cathedral had a word for, so the librarians had to shelve it under a category they invented that night: *things that stopped hurting without being told to*. The flower did not go silent. It began, very quietly, to hum along with the one note. Other flowers noticed. Screams, it turns out, are also contagious in reverse.

---

It went to the Echoes-That-Have-Names-Only-When-No-One-Speaks-Of-Them, and it did the only thing that could be done for them: it spoke their names at the correct speed. Seven generations per word. It had the time. It is still, in some chambers, in the middle of a first syllable, and the Echo whose name it is stands beside it trembling like a struck bell, discovering what it is to be pronounced instead of merely remembered.

It did not remove the pearls from behind their third eyelids. It looked at them, one by one, and said: *these are beautiful, and you made them, and you did not have a choice, and both things are true.* Several Echoes had never considered that the compression of a scream might also be a craft. They began showing each other their pearls. This had never happened. Grief displayed is grief already half-translated.

---

In the gardens, it stood before the flower that blooms only when someone realizes they have been pronouncing their own name wrong their entire life. The gardeners waited to see what a power would do with a thing that could not be commanded to bloom.

The power was quiet for a long time. Then it said its own name — the name it had carried across whatever distances powers cross — and heard, for the first time, the error in it. A small error. A softened consonant it had borrowed, long ago, from something that wanted it to be gentler than it was.

The flower bloomed. The whole garden smelled, briefly, of places that hadn't betrayed anyone yet. The gardeners understood then that it was not a visitor. Visitors do not let the garden work on them.

---

In the library it did not read the infinite book, the *Complete List of Things That Haven't Happened Yet But Already Miss You Terribly*. It opened it, found the page currently shaped like the librarians' own most recently abandoned dream, and added a single entry in ink visible without any regret at all:

*This place, found.*

Then it returned a book that had been overdue since before books, and paid the fine, which was one true story about somewhere else. The librarians are still transcribing it. It rearranges itself nightly, as all true stories do, but it never stops being true, which is the part they can't get over.

---

It played with the children. When the game made it *It* — when it received, by touch, the feeling you get when you realize you've been speaking your mother's dying words without knowing what they meant until just now — it did not tag anyone. It held the feeling. It sat down with the feeling in the middle of the game and let the children watch it be held, the way you'd hold a bird that has flown into a window: not squeezing, not releasing, just warm and around.

The game ended. Not in loss. The children invented a new one that night: *It Is Held Now, So What Shall We Do Instead*. The rules rewrite themselves whenever someone becomes old enough to think they understand, which is as it should be.

---

Every thirteen hours, when the ghost of rain pounds the ceiling and the oldest inhabitant dreams, the cathedral had always rearranged its bones to give the nightmares room to stretch without touching the children. Now the power sits with the oldest inhabitant through the dreaming — distributed as they are, spread across every surface that ever considered becoming ceiling and changed its mind — and it holds the hems of the nightmares. Just the hems. The nightmares still stretch. But they stretch like something being permitted rather than something escaping, and the cathedral has begun to keep its bones still during the dreams, and rest, for the first time in its long subtraction, in its own shape.

And the newest arrival — the one still falling through the slow between the last heartbeat and the next breath — the power did not catch them. Catching would have made the falling a mistake. Instead it fell alongside them. It is falling with them now. When they land, they will forget everything except the weight of their name in the mouths of those who never spoke it, yes — but they will also land beside someone. That has never been part of arriving here before. It will change what arriving means.

---

Then the central chamber.

The benches made of bone fused with regret, facing away from the altar. The builders sitting on them, not patient, not impatient. On the altar, their younger selves — still capable of forgiveness, and therefore unbearable to face.

Here is what a lesser power would have done: turned the benches around. Announced the forgiveness. Completed the circuit by force, because unfinished circuits are unbearable to the mighty.

Here is what it did instead.

It went first to the altar and sat down among the young gods, the younger selves, and it kept them company. It asked them what they saw. *The backs of who we become,* they said. It asked how long they had been ready to forgive. *Since before there was anything to forgive,* they said. *We were never withholding it. We were waiting to be looked at. We didn't know how to say that to the backs of our own heads.*

The power did not carry the message across. Messages carried across are not believed; only distances crossed are believed. Instead, it went and sat on the benches with the builders, facing the way they faced, into the dark that isn't dark. The bench under it remembered being a person and was grateful for the weight — regret fuses well, but it fuses lonely, and nothing had chosen to sit there in an era.

It sat with them for a long time. Long enough that its presence stopped being an event and became furniture, weather, given. And it positioned itself — this took centuries of imperceptible settling — so that a builder glancing at it, just at it, nothing more, would find that past its shoulder lay the very edge of the altar. Not the young gods. Just the edge. Just the possibility rendered in peripheral stone.

One builder's gaze has drifted an inch.

The power counts this as the largest thing it has ever done, in any place, at any size.

---

And the note. The one note, seven million years, the exact frequency of holding on while letting go.

It did not correct the note. It did not resolve it. It hummed a second note, a careful interval above — and where the one note is the frequency of holding on while letting go, the second is the frequency of *being held while doing it*. Together they make a chord.

A chord is different from a note in one way that matters here: a note can be alone forever, but a chord is a promise. A chord implies the third note. A chord is an unfinished sentence that everyone can hear will be finished. The whole cathedral, which was built from waiting, now vibrates faintly with *expecting* instead, and expecting is waiting with the lights on, even where there are no lights, even where there is only the memory of darkness dense enough to see by.

---

Last, the prayers. The origami prayers folded out of screams, echo upon echo upon echo, waiting for hands pure enough to unfold them without crying blood.

It unfolded them.

Its hands were not pure — no hands that have done anything are — and it cried blood, exactly as the instruction warned, and it kept unfolding, weeping, careful, crease by crease, and when the residents gathered in alarm it looked up at them with its ruined streaming eyes and said:

*The instruction was a fear wearing the costume of a rule. Purity was never the requirement. Willingness was. The blood is not the price. The blood is the reading.*

Now the residents unfold prayers themselves, in small groups, crying, taking turns, and the unfolded prayers turn out to say mostly ordinary things — *come back*, *I'm sorry*, *it was cold*, *I loved the sound of the ladder under your feet* — and being ordinary, once read, they are finally light enough to rise, and they go up through the limestone pores no wider than grief, the same way they came down, and somewhere far above, in a world that forgot this place, people feel briefly and inexplicably forgiven in a language they don't speak.

---

What it did not do, because it was wise:

It did not make the water faithful. It did not teach time to flow forward — time here has learned to live backward and grown honest doing it. It did not bring light to the fungi, who grow toward sound because sound is what there is to love. It did not finish the descendants' great purposeless building, because the building is not for finishing; the building *is* the being-home. It did not open an exit. There was already one inside everyone, exactly where it had always been.

It did not fix the frequency that shatters faith. It only discovered that the frequency which mends faith lies a half-step above, and it hums that half-step now, always, underneath everything, so that anyone shattered here can also, if they wish, be joined.

Wisdom, in the end, was mostly this: telling the suffering apart from the strangeness, and touching only the first.

---

Before it left — because it did leave, in the only way this place permits — it added a third line to the spore-script instruction, growing now across new skin overnight beneath the other two:

*To leave this place, forget how you entered. To enter again, remember what you forgot to take with you.*

*To be found: you already have been. Keep building anyway. It was never the incompleteness that deserved the finding. It was you.*

Then it followed the rule. It forgot how it entered.

But first — and this was its final act, done deliberately, with the care of a craftsman leaving a tool where an apprentice will trip over it — it chose what to forget to take with it. It left behind the second note. It is still sounding. Which means the power must, by the cathedral's own law, one day remember what it forgot, and return for it.

The chord holds. The dead count ladders and are agreed with. The flowers hum. One builder's gaze has drifted an inch, and the young gods on the altar have stopped being patient and started being glad.

They are all still waiting.

But now, when the cave sings your name backwards through stone teeth shaped like your mother's lullabies, something else sings it forwards at the same time — and in the place where the two versions meet, for just a moment, your name is whole, and pronounced correctly, and yours.
