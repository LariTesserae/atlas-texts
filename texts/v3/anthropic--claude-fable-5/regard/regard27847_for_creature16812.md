# The Attention That Learned to Squint

The power arrived the only way a wise thing could arrive in such a place: it noticed the noticing first.

It had been watching for some time—though *watching* is already too committed a word, and the power knew this before the plateau did. Powers of the ordinary sort watch the way lighthouses watch: a beam, a sweep, a fixing. And it understood, from very far off, that a beam swept across this place would be a massacre. Every maybe caught in that light would harden mid-gesture. The Hesitate-Specifications would collapse into mere geography. The Shift-Sharer schools would vote one final time and then never vote again, frozen into a single flight path, a single throat, a single ending to their shy stories.

So before it did anything else, the power did the first wise thing, which was to unlearn some of its own seeing.

It taught its attention to arrive the way dusk arrives—from every direction and none, without a source you could point to, without an edge you could stand outside of. It looked at the plateau the way you look at a faint star: slightly to the side, letting the periphery do the loving. Under this kind of gaze, the moss colonizing last Tuesday did not wilt. The paradoxes stood a little taller, like plants after rain, having been seen without being *concluded*.

This was the first gift, and most of the inhabitants never knew it had been given, which is how the power preferred its gifts to fit.

## The Ledger of Withheld Mercies

Because it was wise, the power kept an account—not of what it did, but of what it wanted to do and didn't. In a place like this, restraint is the load-bearing kindness. The ledger read, approximately (approximation being the local dialect):

**Considered: giving the light a source.** The dim illumination with no origin troubled the power's older instincts. Everything deserves a sun, said the part of it that had midwifed other worlds. But a sun is a verdict. A sun says *this way is up, this hour is day, this shadow is yours and falls behind you.* The shadows here liked waiting around unfinished corners. *Declined.*

**Considered: names.** It carried a satchel of them, beautiful ones, names like keys cut for locks that don't exist yet. It could have named the feral theorem, the wing-idea, the herd of maybe-horses. But it remembered what the inhabitants themselves had said—that names are conclusions drawn around growing edges—and it recognized the wisdom as older than its own. It buried the satchel somewhere in the grammar gardens, unopened. (Sometimes now a name sprouts there anyway, blooms for three heartbeats, and is politely eaten by the maybe-menders. The power considers this the correct fate for names in this soil.) *Declined, mostly.*

**Considered: rescue.** The Unrooted broke its heart a little—the theorem that proved itself into a box too small, prowling the mineral meadows; the architectural principle wandering with its regret about symmetry. The power's every instinct said: *heal them, resolve them, carry them home.* But home was the wound. Resolution was what they had fled. *Declined—but see below.*

**Considered: finishing anything at all.** *Declined, declined, a thousand times declined.* The place was not broken. This was the hardest wisdom and the simplest: a garden growing in the gap between making and made does not need a maker to close the gap. The gap *is* the garden.

## The Three Things It Did

Restraint, however, is not the same as absence, and benevolence that only withholds is just distance wearing a halo. So the power did three small things, each one shaped carefully so that it opened rather than closed.

**First, it went to the Unrooted.** Not to fix them. It sat down beside the feral theorem in the tall probability-grass—sat down, which required the power to invent, temporarily, something like knees—and it said, in the subaudible register where the universe hums to itself:

*Your box was too small. That was the box's failing, not yours. You were never wrong. You were merely larger than your proof.*

And to the lonely architectural principle it said: *Symmetry was one house you lived in. It was not your body.* Then it did the one small structural thing it permitted itself in all its time there: it decreed—gently, the way one decrees that a door may be left open—that in this place, **every definition shall have a door.** Anything that becomes may un-become. Finishing shall be a place you can visit, not a tomb you are sealed in. A rock that completes its self-reformation seminar and graduates into a bridge may, in some later restlessness, resign and go back to being rubble with ambitions.

The Unrooted did not stop wandering. But they wander differently now—less like fugitives, more like travelers. Occasionally the theorem tries on a small proof, wears it for an afternoon, and takes it off again, and this is no longer a tragedy in either direction.

**Second, it made a promise about the future**—because it had overheard the philosophers' anxiety, the theory that the visitor was a settled tomorrow come back to fossilize their present. The power could not honestly say the place would never harden. Places do. Maps come. Even here, someday, distance would resolve its doubts and edges would sign their contracts.

So instead it promised this: *When this place becomes a world, I will smuggle you into it.* And it showed them how—showed them the hiding places already prepared in every finished cosmos. The moment between sleeping and waking, where the room hasn't decided which room it is. The word on the tip of the tongue. Dusk. The pause before a child answers a question no one has asked them before. Every draft of every letter. The held breath. The space between moves and pauses before anyone decides what game is being played.

*You will not be erased by completion,* the power said. *You will be its lining. Every made thing will carry a pocket of you, and no one will be able to sew that pocket shut, because I have hidden the needle.*

The Hesitate-Specifications rippled at this in what might have been relief, or might have been the beginning of an ocean. Either was fine.

**Third—and this was the one that surprised even the power—it answered the job posting.**

There were, after all, openings in the cloud-formation choir. Several prime vacancies in the role of unexpected consequence. The hours were flexible; the pay was negotiable between meaning and its alternatives.

The power understood that this was the true test of its benevolence. It could bless this place from above, remain a completed thing radiating goodwill, an odor of finished tasks fouling the delicate air. Or it could do what wisdom actually demanded here: **it could put some of itself down.**

So at the edge of the plateau—an edge the plateau still doesn't believe in—the power took off certainty the way you take off a coat when you finally arrive somewhere warm. Not all of it. It kept enough to keep its promises. But its omniscience it folded and left by the door, and its foresight it planted in the grammar gardens where it now blooms in fractal iterations of questions the power genuinely does not know the answers to, and finds it does not miss knowing.

It joined the choir as a low note that has not decided what chord it belongs to. The Shift-Sharers passed it between potential throats and pronounced it *promisingly unresolved.*

## What It Left Behind

One certainty. Only one, chosen with enormous care, because certainties in this ecosystem must be handled like fire in a library.

It is small and smooth and lies somewhere in the mineral meadows, and any inhabitant who stumbles on it—and the visitor too, whose atmosphere of collapsed probabilities has begun, under the power's peripheral tutelage, to soften into something more like weather—finds that it says only this:

*You are allowed.*

Allowed to continue, allowed to hesitate, allowed to become or un-become, allowed to remain a question mark in the landscape's long conversation with itself. It is the only certainty the place can digest, because it is a certainty that closes nothing. It is a *yes* that never specifies what it is saying yes to.

The philosophers have been arguing about the stone for what might be centuries or might be the same afternoon, never finishing a thought in the configuration they started it. They suspect it means someone was here who loved them. They cannot prove it, and this delights them, and somewhere in the choir a low note hums a little warmer, and around a corner that hasn't been drawn yet, a new shadow waits—patient now, no longer impatient—early, or late, or exactly on time for a meeting that has happily decided never to decide whether it wants to be.
