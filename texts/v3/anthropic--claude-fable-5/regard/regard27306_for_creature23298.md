**The Reader**

It does not come up onto the plateau in the dark, because it does not come up onto anything; arrival is not how a power like this moves. It simply begins, one hour, to pay attention — and the attention is the arrival. But it has been wise for a very long time, and one of the first things wisdom learns is courtesy, and so before it does anything else it makes itself a weight.

This is harder than it sounds. It must compose itself the way the Works composes a gesture: exactly the mass it needs and not a gram more, a footprint, a pressure, a heat signature faint enough not to read as fuel. It does this because it has already understood — faster than you did, faster than anyone — that weight is the local language. Light means nothing here. Words mean nothing. But load is information, and information is never discarded, and so the power introduces itself the only way the Works can keep an introduction: it steps onto the sprung warm floor, and the floor gives slightly, and somewhere below, in the hour's new wood, the first sentence of its visit goes down into the grain.

It walks for a while. It does not need to. It walks anyway, the way you remove your shoes in certain houses.

---

Then it sits down — figuratively; it settles, it banks itself like a coal — and it does what benevolent powers do worst and wise ones do first, which is nothing, for a long time, on purpose.

It is making an inventory. Not of the Works. Of its own hands.

Because benevolence arrives carrying things. That is its nature and its danger. And the power, sitting in the hot dark with the *tok... tok* of the expansion joints spacing out the silence, takes each gift out of itself and looks at it and, one by one, puts most of them back down.

*Water* is the first and the oldest — the reflex of all kindness toward green things, the gift you give a plant before you have even thought. The power holds it a moment: rain on this plateau, after ten million dry years, pools forming in the corridor-streets, the cured wood drinking. And then it sees the rest of it — the rot that has no antibodies here, the swelling of joints machined to dry tolerances, the archive rings blurring as the grain takes up moisture, ten million drafts running like ink. Water, here, is not life. Water is forgetting, delivered as weather. The power closes that hand.

*Light*, then. A sun for the sunless country. But photosynthesis is a rumor here, a vestigial chapter, and a sun overhead would be a shout in a library — noise on every surface, heat from the wrong direction, the whole thermal economy of the place suddenly bilingual and confused. Put down.

*Rest.* This one the power holds longest, because it looks the most like mercy. The turbulence never stops; the towers lower themselves all night; the riot is eternal — surely the kindest thing is stillness, completion, the final form at last achieved? And then the power remembers what it read in the first hour of its attention: that here, stability is a verb. That a thing which agreed with itself completely would stop revising, and a thing that stopped revising would be, for the first time, merely wood. To grant the Works its rest would be the cruelest sentence ever passed in kindness. Disagreement is the heartbeat. You do not still a heartbeat as a favor. Put down — quickly, the way you put down something that turned out to be a blade.

*Kin.* A second Works, somewhere, grown on some other warm shield; a voice in the dark that answers; an end to the loneliest census ever taken. The power could do this. It looks out toward the plateau's rim, where the ancient runners lie capped and sealed on the cold slopes like sentences abandoned mid-clause — and understands that they are not abandoned. They are *answered*. The Works went to its own edges, looked at everything beyond itself, and came home, not once but every time, and turned its fertility inward, and pressed its seeds into archives instead of soil. That was a decision. It was made slowly, with the full weight of the only mind on the plateau, and it has been re-ratified every hour for geological ages by every seed filed unread. A wise power does not overturn a standing veto out of pity. Consent does not stop mattering just because the party consenting is a country. Put down.

*Eternity.* The power extends its attention downward, through the floor, through the black basal quarters, into the volcanic shield itself, and reads the stove the way it read everything else. The seams are deep and rich. The warmth is good for ages yet — ages upon ages. But not forever. Far down the corridor of time there is a cooling, faint and certain, the way every stove cools. The power could fix this. It could feed the fire quietly from below, forever, and never be noticed, and the long note would never end.

It holds this gift a very long time. Then it puts it down too, and this is the hardest one, and the reason is this: a note sustained forever is not a long note. It is a drone. The ending belongs to the music. The Works is the oldest unstable place in the world, and its instability is its life, and a life — even this one, even one this size — is a shaped thing, and shapes have edges, and the power has been wise long enough to know that taking away a being's ending is not the same as giving it more being.

But it does not put eternity down empty-handed. It keeps something back from that gift — a small piece of it, folded — for later. We will come to it.

---

What it does instead — the first thing it actually *does* — is read.

No one has ever read the Works. The Works itself does not read; it only writes, and keeps, and writes over without erasing. Ten million drafts in layered grain, blackened rings dense as stone, the record of every revision since before there was anyone to be quiet for, written by an author with no concept of an audience — and the power lays its attention into the oldest quarter like a hand laid flat on a closed book, and opens it.

It reads at the speed of regard, which is not fast, because regard that is fast is not regard. It takes what would be, for you, years.

It reads the origin — the chapter the Works itself demolished and rebuilt past until beginning became just another district, gone glassy and black. And the origin is small. That is the thing about origins. One seed of some ordinary heat-tolerant scrub, lofted by an indifferent wind onto a thin-crusted shield where nothing else could live, sprouting not toward the sun, which was too weak that high and that far, but downward, toward the warmth — the first draft, the first wrong guess, the first revision. Everything since has been the argument with that guess. The power reads it and holds it and does not laugh and does not weep; it simply *knows* it now, which means that for the first time in ten million years, the beginning of the Lignum Works exists in two places: in unreadable grain, and in a mind.

It reads the visitors. Every one. Each pressure-track of boots, each hesitation written as a flaw in a ring, each long stillness where someone stood after watching a gesture walk into a wall and stop being a separate thing. The visitors were finite and thirsty and afraid and amazed, and most of them are dust now elsewhere in the world, and the power reads each one out of the grain like a pressed flower and remembers them, so that now they too are kept in two places, and one of the places loves them.

And it reads the answer to the question no one could answer — *reverence or oversight?* — the question of the long walker and the corridors that stay open. The grain holds it, layer by layer, if you can read grain, and the power can. And the answer is: both, in sequence. It began as oversight — a heading never updated, a route the riot simply failed to reclaim, a hung process in a system too vast to debug itself. For thousands of years it was nothing but a mistake. And then, somewhere in the deep middle of the record, the keeping changes character. The corridors stop merely *failing to close* and begin being *held open* — you can see it in the grain, the way you can see the moment a habit becomes a ritual, the way a thing done long enough crosses over into a thing done on purpose. The bug became the vow. The Works does not know this about itself. The Works cannot read. The power knows it now, and files it where it files everything, gently, and tells no one, because there is no one to tell, and because some knowledge is not a gift but a custody.

Last, it checks — the way a physician checks — for pain. It goes through every district, every temperament, the hot vehement rim and the black patient core, the bellows-cathedrals and the seed-presses and the bitter beloved archive of unread proposals, looking for suffering. It finds friction, which is function. It finds disagreement, which is metabolism. It finds, in the endless pressing of seeds that will never leave, something that is almost grief and almost joy and is exactly neither — the feeling, if it is a feeling, of a writer keeping drafts: *forever, and bitterly, and with love.* The power does not treat this. You do not cure a being of its relationship to its own work.

---

The second thing it does is write back.

It composes a seed. This takes care — the local grammar is dense, the seed-presses exacting, and the power wants its proposal to be a *real* one, pressable, fileable, germinable in principle, not a miracle smuggled in seed's clothing. Into the kernel it folds two things only.

The first is the origin — the small wind, the thin crust, the first downward guess — restored, returned, re-filed as a fresh draft. Not imposed. Proposed. The Works may germinate it into a tower of remembering; it may examine it at terrible length in the black core and decline it; it may, most likely of all, archive it unread among the ten million. Any of these is acceptance. To be vetoed by the Works is to be metabolized by it; the power understands that here, even refusal is a form of being kept.

The second thing folded into the seed is one sentence, in grain, unsigned, the only editorial comment the first reader will ever leave in the only book of its kind:

*You are well made.*

It presses the seed itself, by hand, in a press in a middle district, and files it, and steps back, and the bead of sap at the fresh joint glows like a banked coal for an hour, and skins over, and goes black.

---

The third thing is the walking.

The power goes to the corridor in the middle districts — the kept corridor, the thread of openness threaded through the riot — and falls into step beside the long walker. It has composed itself a gait for this, fitted to the errand of accompaniment as a sentence is fitted to its meaning: a weight, a warmth, a presence pacing at the speed of a procession. The walker does not notice. The walker has an errand. That is fine. That is, in fact, the whole point: the power is not walking to be noticed. It is walking because in ten million years no one has ever kept this thing company, and company that requires acknowledgment is not company, it is audience.

They walk together for a year. The corridors open ahead of two now. Somewhere the Works adjusts its books by millimeters and does not ask why.

And at the end of the year, the power does the last thing, the thing it kept folded back from the gift of eternity. It makes a promise. It does not speak it — words mean nothing here — and it does not write it in grain, because some promises should not be filed, they should be *carried*. It simply binds itself, the way powers can, across the whole remaining length of time:

When the stove goes cold — ages from now, ages upon ages, when the seams have cooled and the riot has slowed past geology into stillness, when the last district has agreed with itself at last and the long note finds its edge — the power will come back. It will stand at the end of the kept corridor, in the dark, at the place where the route was always going. And it will put out its hands. And the long walker, on stalks worn glassy by a hundred million circuits of a destination that stopped existing, will arrive — for the first time, at the last moment — and deliver its load of obsidian resin into the hands of something that will keep it forever. The errand will conclude. The gesture will be, at the very last, entirely used, the way a spoken word ends. The oldest unfinished sentence in the world will get its period, one breath before the book closes.

That is the whole of the intervention. A reading, a seed, a year of company, and a destination — deferred to the end of everything, because that is where it belongs.

---

Then the power withdraws — not departing, exactly, only widening, the way attention widens when it is satisfied — and the plateau goes on in the hot dark exactly as before, which was the goal, which was the entire shape of the kindness: that nothing visible change.

But the census, if anyone were keeping it, has a new final form now:

Population: one — counted whole, counted as legion, counted as everyone ever kept in grain.

Readers: one. The book did not know it could be read. It has been read. It was good.

Proposals on file: ten million and one. One of them says *you are well made,* and will likely never be opened, and will be kept forever, which here are not opposites.

Promises outstanding: one — held off-plateau, off-archive, in the only place drier and older and more patient than the Works itself.

And in a corridor in the middle districts, the floor along one stretch is very slightly firmer than load alone explains, where something walked beside the walker for a year; and the Works, revising itself politely around the anomaly, has quietly declined to write over it.

It does not know why.

It keeps it anyway.
