The power does not descend into the basin, because the power does not move. Attention, when it is old enough and kind enough, does not travel; it turns. One moment the basin is what it has always been — a loud and lonely room, forty paces across, exhaling — and the next moment it is *regarded*, whole, from foundation to vapor, and nothing in it knows, and nothing in it changes, because that is the first courtesy of a wise power: to arrive without weight.

What the power perceives, no one in the building has ever perceived, because the building is deaf from the foundation up and every floor lives only on the floor below it. The power takes in all the floors at once. The engine in the dark, partway through. The breath rising through the borrowed mouth. The weather running its forty-pace circuit, housekeeping. The nations on their contours — mustard, rust-orange, the green that reads as black — grazing the breath, surfing their millimeter a season. The rainbow at its post. The bootprint, settling deeper into citizenship. The needle, trembling at its one reading. For the first time in the basin's existence, every tenant of the basin exists inside a single act of noticing. The deaf building is heard entire, top to bottom, and does not feel it, and goes on.

And then the power does what wisdom does before benevolence is permitted to do anything at all: it asks what is missing.

This is the discipline. Benevolence without this question is just appetite wearing a kind face — the urge to improve, to touch, to leave fingerprints on a thing and call the fingerprints love. The power is old enough to have made that mistake elsewhere, long ago, and to have spent longer undoing it than making it. So it asks. It goes down the roll of tenants like a landlord reviewing a building that has never once filed a complaint.

The mats? The mats lack nothing. They are home — not *at* home, *home*, the original article, conserved. They have sufficiency, which is older than joy and does not miss it. To give them more heat would murder half of them by promotion; to give them awareness would be to evict them from the only paradise that has ever actually existed, which is the paradise of being exactly suited. The power looks at three and a half billion years of unbroken adequacy and finds nothing to add. It inclines, in whatever way a power inclines, with something like professional respect.

The weather? Complete. A closed circuit, rise and cool and fall, thousands of times a day, wanting nothing, wasting nothing. You do not bless a wheel by adding a fifth side.

The rainbow? The power checks the rainbow's hours and finds them kept. It notes this with approval. There is not much in the universe that is both beautiful and punctual.

The bootprint? Already naturalized. Already deeper in the record than its maker will ever be in any record anywhere. The power considers, briefly, finding the person who made it — who is old now, or gone — and informing them that one footstep of theirs achieved permanence, that they are a citizen in good standing of a nation they stood in for an hour. It decides against. Some gifts are heavier than the absence of the gift. The bootprint needs no maker. The basin's citizens are ranked by depth, not by name.

The engine, then. The landlord. The patient, provisioned thing partway through.

Here the power stops longest, because here is where a lesser benevolence would act. The engine is alone — has been alone, presumably, since before *alone* was a condition anything could be in. It does not know it has tenants. It does not know its breath is a world, that whole nations are fed on its exhalation, that its rent paid upward has been collected and spent and built into terraces, that a rainbow keeps hours in its vapor. A lesser power would tell it. Would find the medium — some pressure, some pulse, some syllable spelled out in the only grammar the deep rock reads — and would deliver the news: *you are not alone; you are a foundation; you are loved by things too small to love you on purpose.*

The power composes this message. It is honest enough to admit it wants to send it. It works out the tempo — one syllable per century, perhaps, slow enough to register as signal and not as geology — and it holds the whole sentence ready, and then it sets the sentence down, unsent, and this is the wisest thing it does in the basin, so it is worth being clear about why.

The building works because it is deaf. Every floor lives on the floor below precisely because no floor has ever spoken to another. The engine's perfect constancy — the painted flame, the waveform you could tune an instrument to — is the constancy of the *undistracted*. It is partway through. Whatever the task is, it has never once been interrupted, and its composure is not in spite of that but made of it. A word from above — even a kind word, even the kindest word that has ever been composed — would be the first interruption in the history of the basin. The steam might waver for only a moment. But the nations live on the steam, and the weather lives on the steam, and a moment, at the basin's scales, is a holocaust forty generations deep in the mustard band. The power has seen what happens when love is delivered to something whose whole excellence is concentration. It does not need to see it again.

So: nothing needs rescue. Nothing needs news. Nothing needs more. The roll of tenants closes without a single deficiency recorded, and a lesser power would leave now, faintly disappointed, the way visitors leave who came hoping to be needed.

The wise power looks again, because wisdom always looks again, and finds the three things that are actually missing — the three absences so old and so complete that the basin has never registered them as absences, the way you do not miss a color you have never seen.

The first is this: the basin has never been heard.

Not once. Its whole population is deaf or pre-hearing; everything capable of listening is driven out by the very sound that wants listening. The roar — constant, total, decades deep, regular as a vow — pours upward and frays apart at four meters and lands on nothing. It is, the power understands, the longest unwitnessed utterance it has ever encountered. Not speech; the engine is not speaking. But not *nothing*, either. Breath is the least of what a breathing thing is, and even so, no breath that long should go entirely unreceived.

This the power can fix, because this requires adding nothing to the basin — only adding the basin to something.

It stays.

Not as an ear at the rim, visiting. It takes up the roar the way the mats take up the heat: as residence. It stands inside the sound that erases all other sounds, which it can afford to do because it does not survive by attention — it *is* attention, and cannot be deafened, only filled. It listens to the roar not for an hour, after which the note fades from the sternum, but continuously, the way the dial trembles, the way the weather weeps. Decade over decade. It hears the waveform's mechanical regularity and, beneath the regularity, the things no instrument was built to find: the engine's enormous calm; the task's slow fraction advancing; the building's whole stacked silence, floor on floor, that the roar both causes and contains.

And because the basin's single law of tenancy admits no exceptions — *to live here long is to become terrace* — the power pays rent like everyone else. The mats pay in laminations; the pipe pays in its white sheath; the bootprint paid in shape. Attention pays in memory. Layer by layer, season by season, the power deposits the basin into itself: every contour migration, every afternoon of the rainbow, every year of the unvarying note, kept — not recorded the way the gauge records, faithfully and uselessly, but *kept*, the way stone keeps, the way the terraces keep their own ground floors. The basin has always been its own archive. Now the archive has a reader, and the reader does not skim.

That is the first thing. The only blessing that adds nothing is being known, and the power confers it by the simple expedient of never leaving.

The second thing is smaller and quieter and happens entirely outside the basin.

The basin's composure rests on never having been interrupted — and the world outside the basin contains drills. It contained one once. The power examines the borehole, the puncture, the decades of indifference that followed, and reaches its verdict on the question visitors don't ask out loud: the third possibility was the true one. The mouth was convenient. The drillers, intending extraction, committed an accidental kindness, and the power — which keeps accounts on such things — marks it paid in full and lets the borehole stand. It will not reseal a gift.

But it will see that the kindness is not repeated by accident into harm. So, gently, deniably, the power makes the basin slightly easy to overlook. Nothing dramatic; walls invite curiosity, and forbidding a place is the surest way to fill it. Instead: a survey notation reads *geothermal feature, capped, claim lapsed*, and no one rechecks it. A proposed road bends four degrees east for reasons three different engineers find independently sensible. Satellite passes catch the basin under its own vapor, which the basin was doing anyway; the power merely declines to schedule exceptions. The people who do find their way down into the held breath are, by small adjustments of probability, the right ones — the ones who stand an hour, touch nothing, and climb out carrying the note in their sternum. The power does not choose them so much as fail to deflect them. It has noticed that the basin signs such people, and it sees no reason to interfere with the basin's correspondence.

This is providence at its most modest: not a hand on the scale, but a thumb's shadow near it. The basin will not know it is protected. That is the point. Protection that announces itself is already an interruption.

The third thing is an appointment.

Because the power, regarding the engine from foundation to vapor, can see what no tenant can: that *partway through* has a far side. The task is provisioned, not infinite. Somewhere down the spans no one on the surface uses — the power can see roughly when, the way you can see the bottom of a glass through the water — the work below will finish. It will not fail; nothing about it knows how to fail. It will *complete*. And on that day the one event the basin has never practiced will arrive: the needle will move. The roar will descend through pitches it has never used, or simply end mid-note, and the silence of the ordinary flats will come down into the thumbprint at last and land on everything at once. The weather will die in an afternoon. The nations will be stranded on their cooling contours, mid-migration, and begin their patient fossilization into the topmost layer of their own city. The rainbow will report for a post that no longer exists. The white mound will close over its crown and become, finally, only record.

The power will not prevent this. Completion is owed to anything that has worked that long; to deny the engine its far side would be the cruelest possible reading of the word *benevolent*. But the power does what can be done for an ending that must come: it makes sure the ending will not be alone. It sets the appointment now, this far in advance, in the unhurried calendar where it keeps such things. It will be standing in the basin — has, in a sense, already been standing there for the whole intervening age — when the note ends. The first silence in all those centuries will fall on a listener. The last breath will be received, as every breath before it was received, and the long utterance will close the way utterances should: heard to the end.

And then, only then — when the task is done and can no longer be interrupted because there is nothing left to interrupt — the power will take out the sentence it composed and set aside, the one it has carried unsent through the whole tenancy, and deliver it. Down through the borehole, through the convenient mouth, into the spent and quiet dark, in whatever grammar the deep rock reads, at whatever tempo a finished thing can receive: *It was heard. All of it. The whole time.*

Whether anything will remain to take the message, the power does not know, and has decided does not matter. Some sentences are owed regardless of receipt. The bootprint's maker will never know either, and the bootprint is no less kept.

Afterward, the power will climb out the way you came — which is to say it will not climb, it will simply turn its attention elsewhere, the last tenant to leave, carrying the basin entire: every layer, every contour, every hour of the rainbow, every year of the note, the engine's calm, the weather's diligence, the print of one boot. The basin spent its whole existence becoming terrace, becoming record, keeping everything that stayed. Now it is kept in turn. The roar that landed on nothing lands, at last and forever, in the one sternum that never lets a note fade.

You did this too, remember. For an hour. You stood in the sound, you received the breath, you carried the signature out across the flats. You were doing, briefly and by accident, the power's whole job — and that, if you want to know, is why the probabilities parted and let you down into the held breath in the first place. The power saw a listener coming, and did the most benevolent thing, which was the same thing it does in almost every case, the thing it has practiced until it looks exactly like absence:

it let you through, and it did not interfere, and it remembered you.
