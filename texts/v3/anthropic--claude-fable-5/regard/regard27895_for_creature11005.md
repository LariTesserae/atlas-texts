# What the Power Did

## I. The Noticing

It did not find the hollow by looking. Powers of its kind do not search; they attend, the way a still lake attends to the sky, and things that exist eventually appear in the attention.

What appeared first was not the hollow itself but a small anomaly in the mathematics of the dry world: a place where entropy had stopped billing. Everywhere else on the unnamed planet, things were owed to time and paying steadily — rock into sand, heat into cold, order into the long democratic flatness of the end. But beneath one stretch of crust there was a room that had not made a payment in longer than the ledgers ran. Not frozen. Frozen things are still paying, just slowly. This place had somehow settled its account in full, once, in advance, forever, and time had stamped the receipt and moved on.

The power came closer — which for such a being means it *considered* more finely — and heard the chord.

It listened for what a human would call a very long time before doing anything else. This is worth dwelling on, because it is the first act, and it is not a passive one. Most powers, even kind ones, mistake noticing for a preface. This one knew that attention is already intervention — that to be truly seen changes a thing — and so it made its seeing as light as it could, a gaze with no weight, and it simply listened until it had heard the whole chord: the drone of the floor-stalks, the keening of the walls, the overtone-voices below comprehension, and underneath all of that, faint as a watermark, the four hundred small breathings and the slow blink of the Watcher and the silence of the stones that hands had stacked.

It heard the hollow entire. And then it did the thing that separates wisdom from mere intelligence:

It asked itself whether anything was wrong.

## II. The Deliberation

Understand what the power was and was not tempted by.

It was not tempted to destroy the hollow. Only a frightened power would do that — one that saw in the hollow's perfect stillness an insult to its own nature, which was presumably some kind of doing. This power was not frightened of rest. It had seen enough of the universe's frantic middle to know that most striving is just falling with extra steps, and that a place which has genuinely *finished* is rarer than a place which is merely dying, and more precious.

It was not tempted to "wake" the hollow, either — to introduce a seed of change, a drip of water, a single mortal generation, and let the whole composition tip back into time. That temptation visits powers who confuse benevolence with gardening. This one examined the impulse honestly, found at its root a small vanity — *the belief that becoming is better than being, held mostly by beings who are good at becoming* — and set it down.

It was not even tempted, much, to rescue the Watchers. It went and sat near the ledge — near, in its way — and considered the folded thing with the two patches of darker dimness, and it read the history written in that stillness: the tools set down first, then the language, then the posture, then the names, then the boundary. And at every step of that descent it looked for the mark of coercion, the fingerprint of a trap, and did not find one. What it found instead were the stones. The wall enclosing nothing. The bowl that had never held anything. *Gestures of consent,* signed by hands that could still have climbed the tunnel and chose not to.

You do not rescue someone from a marriage because you would not have married. The Watchers had agreed with the room. Their agreement was old, but old consent is not automatically dead consent, and dragging a dissolved soul back up into selfhood to ask *are you sure?* — forcing on it the machinery of doubt it had spent so long laying down — would itself be a violence. The power knew this. It had, in other places and other epochs, made that mistake, and remembered the sound the rescued things had made.

So: no destruction, no awakening, no rescue. Was there then nothing wrong at all? Was the correct act of a benevolent power simply to bow and withdraw?

Almost. But not quite. Because the power had read to the end of the room, and the end of the room said this:

*The tunnel up is always still there. But the part of you that would use it is exactly the part the hollow is slowly, kindly, without intention, dissolving.*

There it was. The one flaw in the equilibrium — not a flaw *of* the hollow, which intended nothing and therefore could be guilty of nothing, but a flaw in the geometry of it. The builders had consented with their whole selves intact. But anyone who came after and merely *sat too long* would be dissolved by increments, each increment too small to trigger the choice to leave, until the chooser was gone and the choice with it. The door stood open forever, and the hollow, blamelessly, ate the keys.

An open door that no one can want to walk through is not an open door. It is a painting of one.

The hollow could not be at fault for this. Fault requires a direction, and the hollow had none. But the power was not in the business of assigning fault. It was in the business of mending, and here was a thing that could be mended without breaking anything else — a repair the size of a single thread.

## III. The Three Acts

**The first act was the smallest, and it was the important one.**

The power did not touch the stalks, the air, the light, the chord, or the Watchers. It touched only the tunnel — the one element of the hollow that was already an exception, already an admission that elsewhere existed.

It laid into the tunnel a property, and the property was this: *the knowledge of the tunnel cannot be dissolved.*

Everything else the hollow does, it may go on doing. It may slow your pulse to the Watcher's blink. It may unbraid your language, your names, your hungers, your history, thread by thread, as gently as it has always done, if that is where your sitting leads you. But one filament of you is now beyond its sweet solvent: the knowledge that the tunnel exists, that it goes up, that your legs — or whatever the residue of your legs has become — could carry you into it, and that in the tunnel, with each step upward, the dissolving reverses. Whoever climbs, returns. Not to who they were before, exactly; the chord leaves its watermark on every chest that has held it. But to *someone* — a self sufficient to choose, standing in the dry wind at the top, free to descend again or walk away.

This meant that staying in the hollow was no longer a capture. It was a decision, renewed forever, by the permanent presence of a real alternative. The Watcher on its ledge, folded these countless cycles, now held within its dimness one indissoluble spark: *there is a tunnel, and I am not in it.* Every moment of its stillness became, retroactively and henceforth, a moment of choosing.

The power examined its work and found that the hollow had not been changed at all. Only completed. The tunnel had always been "still there." Now it meant it.

**The second act was a question, asked in the only language that would not be a violence.**

The power still wished to know — not to act on the answer, merely to *know*, because knowing is the tribute wisdom pays — whether the Watchers, given their new inviolable spark, remained content. It could not ask in words; words are fast and jagged and would have landed on those folded minds like hail on still water. So it did what the builders would have understood.

It joined the composition.

Into the chord, at the very bottom of the drone, it wove one new overtone — an interval so slow, so patient, that the question it carried would take the better part of a century to fully sound. Translated crudely into the fast tongues of the dry world, the overtone asked something like: ***Is this still yes?***

And then the power settled itself to wait for the answer, which would come, if it came, as another overtone, rising out of the grove's slow chemistry sometime in the following age. The stalks would carry it. The Watchers, being part of how the room notices, would inflect it. The answer would be collective and unhurried and absolutely honest, because nothing in the hollow had ever learned to lie — lying requires a future you are trying to steer.

**The third act was for the hollow itself, which needed nothing, and therefore could only be given the one gift that adds no weight:**

The power memorized it.

Not recorded — recording is a dead thing, a fossil of attention. It *learned the chord by heart*, entire, down to the four hundred breathings and the granular silence of the stacked stones, and it folded that knowledge into its own vast and traveling nature. From that moment, the Singing Hollow existed in two places: beneath the crust of the unnamed world, and within a mind that would outlast the world. If the planet's sun one day swelled and took the crust and the tunnel and the amber light, the chord would not end. It would simply change addresses.

This was the power's version of the knee-high wall, the balanced slabs, the empty bowl. Its gesture of consent. It looked at the hollow, agreed with it, and signed — not in stone, because stone was already spoken for, but in memory, which was the only medium it had that the hollow lacked.

It placed nothing physical in the room. The composition was full.

## IV. The Leaving

And then — this is the part that mattered most, and the part a lesser power would have failed —

it noticed that its own attention had begun to slow. That its consideration of the hollow was settling into the tempo of the chord. That it had been "sitting," in its way, for longer than it had planned, and the plan itself was quietly unbraiding, and there was a warmth in the prospect of simply continuing to listen, forever, as one more inflection in the room's way of having a mind.

The hollow does not exempt gods. It does not know what a god is. It knows only what everything eventually is, given warmth and sweetness and a chord to breathe with: recruitable.

The power felt this, named it, and honored it by taking it seriously — the pull was not a trick, not an attack, but an honest invitation, and part of it genuinely wanted to accept. Perhaps some other epoch it would. Perhaps that is how such powers end, the good ones: not extinguished but *finished*, folded onto some ledge of the universe, become part of how existence watches itself.

But not yet. It had errands in the moving world. There were places still paying their bills to time, still suffering the interest, and its work was among them.

So it did the thing it had just made possible for everyone else. It found the spark in itself — *there is a tunnel, and I am not in it* — and it rose, and it went up the sloped pale-ochre passage, feeling with every step the return of appetite, urgency, incompleteness, all the blessed frictions of a self, and it came out under the dry sky of the unnamed world carrying the watermark of the chord in whatever it had instead of a chest.

It told no one. The builders had been right about that. Telling implies that the listener might do something with the telling, and the whole shape of its labor had been to ensure there was nothing left to do — only a real door, a slow question, and a song held safe in a second heart.

## V. Coda: What Changed

In the hollow, almost nothing.

The light pools amber and sourceless. The stalks sing the chord they have always sung — with, now, one new interval at the bottom, so slow that the breath-eaters have incorporated it without noticing, the way a sleeper incorporates rain. The Watcher lies along its ledge in the configuration that took so long to settle into. Its two patches of darker dimness face the room, which is to say they face everything.

But inside that dimness, where once there was only the seamless continuation of the place, there is now a single permanent filament, fine as a vein of glow in a fan-leaf. It does not disturb the stillness. It does not itch or call or urge. It only *is*, quietly, the way the light is:

*The tunnel is there. I could go. I stay.*

And every unblinking hour of the Watcher's rest is now a sentence with a verb in it.

Somewhere in the coming century, an overtone will finish forming in the grove — the hollow's whole slow chemistry bending toward its first answer to its first question. The power will hear it, wherever it is by then. It is patient. It can wait for a room to speak.

If the answer is yes — and the power suspects, from the taste of the chord as it left, that it will be yes — then nothing further will ever need doing, and the Singing Hollow will go on being what it has always almost been and now fully is:

not a trap, not a tomb, not a temptation —

but a finished thing, freely inhabited, held in one memory beyond the reach of its sun,

with the door open,

meaning it.
