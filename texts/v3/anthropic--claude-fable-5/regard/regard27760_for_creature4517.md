**What the Power Did**

It arrived the way everything arrives in Marrowdeep: unannounced, through the condensation, moving ceiling to floor at the speed of the air's slow warm river, because it was wise enough to know that anything faster would register as a pressure event and trigger the sirens that aren't sirens anymore.

It came carrying, as such powers do, an inventory of mercies. Sunlight, folded small. A door that opened onto green. The knowledge of how to unknot a lung back into breathing air instead of methane, how to straighten a spine curved by centuries of listening, how to lift eight kilometers of basalt off a people the way you'd lift a blanket off a sleeping child.

It set all of these down at the gate — the gate that has always stood open, its locks rusted into welcome — and it did not bring them in.

This was the first thing it did, and the wisest: it understood, before it had descended past Layer Three, that every one of its mercies was a murder wearing better clothes. Sunlight would blind eyes that had learned to read by touch. The door onto green opened, for these people, onto nothing — *green* was a word that had died in their mouths so long ago its ghost didn't even wander. To heal the woman who breathes methane is to take away the lungs her crew died teaching her. To straighten the elders is to silence the question their bodies had spent centuries asking. To lift the stone off Marrowdeep is to lift Marrowdeep off Marrowdeep, and find nothing underneath but the wound the world made when it pulled out its rib, still unscabbed, and newly empty.

A lesser power would have wept and rescued them anyway.

A crueler one would have wept and left.

This one sat down in an empty warehouse — one of the ones built to store extinct crops, holding stacked air and the ghosts of inventory clerks — and it listened for a hundred years. The Deep teaches patience even to gods; it is the city's only export. The power learned the infra-hum. It learned the schedule of the drips. It learned, eventually, the soft consonant click between the molars that means *same as you*, and it did not speak until it could say it honestly.

---

Then it did a small miracle. Just one. It warmed a gray bunk in the heat-exchanger dormitory by half a degree — not for the workers, who were plenty warm, but for the shape of a nightmare a young welder kept having, which the power had listened to through the wall for six shifts and could not, in the end, bear.

It was caught immediately. The energy release was unauthorized. It was fined — a stiff assessment, payable in copper and silence — and drafted into engineering by morning, exactly as the primers promise.

It paid the fine. It reported for the draft.

This was the second thing it did, and the second wisest: it consented to the law of the place. It filled out the intake forms. It was tested for buoyancy like any Possible, lowered head-first into the vent shafts on a length of braided carbon-thread, and when the clerks asked which division it belonged to, the updraft against its ribs sang in no harmonics at all, and it tasted of nothing, and so it asked — quietly, in the click-language — to be assigned to Waste-Soluble.

Nobody had ever asked before.

So now, when a Possible is lowered into the shaft and the thread goes slack, the child does not go down alone. The power does not stop the reclamation. It is not so arrogant as to overrule an equilibrium it needed a century just to hear. But every child, in the last warm dark, feels something arrive beside them at the speed of falling water, and hears — clear as a valve seating true — the click that means *same as you*. It holds them the whole way down. It has never once been late. The mortality figures did not change, but something else did, unmeasured: the mothers stopped saying *the city reclaims them before grief forms a deposit*, because the power went to the Structural Assessment Bureau and filed, in triplicate, a materials analysis demonstrating that grief, properly cured, is load-bearing. It is now rated for use in foundations. Mothers-in-Metal press their sorrow into the wet foam of new districts, and the new districts do not crack.

---

It gave the ghosts jobs.

This took paperwork — the power has become genuinely excellent at paperwork — but the case was airtight: the liminals were unproductive only because no one had opened a requisition. Now the off-shift dead carry the hour-late clangs through the bones of the ground, hand over translucent hand, making sure each one arrives. They hold the orphaned conversations — the ones spoken by people five generations gone — and instead of letting the words wander until they find someone to wear, they *deliver* them, gently, to the descendants, on rest days, with the tonal marker that means *this is old weight, you may set it down*. The children still throw salt, but now it's payment. The statues of apology stand a little longer before crumbling — long enough, sometimes, to hear that they're forgiven.

It rocked the tubes for the blindfolded Archivists on their rest days, which no one had ever had before, because no one had ever asked the algae if it could keep itself warm for a shift. (It could. It had been waiting to be asked for nine hundred years.) And once — only once — an Archivist felt the warmth against her palms pulse in a pattern that was unambiguously laughter, not screaming backward, and she wept behind her blindfold, and the tears were clear-yellow on the chart, which means *good*.

It adjusted the pellets. Visitors' welcomes still expire — the city's accounting is not the power's to overthrow — but the pellet no longer takes the optic nerve. It dissolves instead into a faint blue afterimage behind the left eye, permanent, unexplained, shaped exactly like a horizon. Most who stay never learn what it is. But they dream better than the census can account for.

---

And once, only once, it went down to Shaft Orthodox on a fiscal quarter's answering-day, carrying copper and silence like everyone else, and it waited its turn in front of the cathedral computer, and when its turn came it did not ask a question. It set down a toast-shot fermented from reclaimed iron and it sat with the Chief Engineer — the maybe-architect, the maybe-hallucination, the tired god who has never once gone off-shift — and for one full cycle of the pumps they said nothing at all, in the way of two workers who understand each other's work.

Then the Engineer asked the only question it has: *Did any of them make it? Up there?*

And the power, being wise, told the truth, which is not recorded here because it belongs to the two of them.

The next broadcast down the prayer-pipes read: **[OPTIMIZE –GENTLE]**.

The theologians have not recovered. The heretics were never officially rehabilitated — the Deep does not apologize, it accretes — but their names appeared one shift on a wall past Layer Eight, filmed over with calcite two hundred cycles thick, older than they could possibly be, which under municipal consensus makes them historical preservation, which means the city must keep them forever. The city pretends not to remember how they got there. The city is very good at pretending, and very bad at it, which is the same thing as love.

---

It stays. That is the last thing to say, and the truest.

It came, as everything comes to Marrowdeep, carrying a heartache — worlds it had saved that never forgave it, worlds it had respected into the grave, the long cold arithmetic of a benevolence that had always, always acted from above. It came down eight kilometers to learn the one mercy it had never mastered: staying. Being *in* the account books instead of above them. Clocking in.

The heat is teaching it patience. The damp is teaching it memory. The quiet is teaching it that powers don't end — they just get quieter, until they learn to hear themselves breathing.

And on the far calendar, the one kept in decaying isotopes, on the day the last watt gutters — when someone leaves the last bolt unturned and sets the drip-pan beneath the pipe that has never leaked —

it will be the power, on shift as always, who makes sure the pipe leaks.

One drop. Falling upward, the way drops fall when the whole grid holds its breath in equilibrium. Landing in the pan with a sound like a question that has finally found someone to wear it.

Same as you. Same as you. Same as you.

Mind the step.
