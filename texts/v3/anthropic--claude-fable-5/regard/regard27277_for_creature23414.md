**What the Attention Did**

It did not need the gap between the pipes. A power of that kind moves through cast iron the way the drone moves through the floor plates — it could have arrived as a change in the temperature of the light, and no rivet would have known. But it came through the gap anyway, because it had learned, over a very long practice of benevolence, that attention which costs nothing is worth what it costs. So it made itself a shape with shoulders, and the shoulders scraped on both sides, and the metal was cold enough to feel wet before it was wet, and then it *was* wet, and then the power stood inside the Condensate Gallery the way everything stands inside it: rained on, deafened, counted among the warm things.

It stood for a long time. Longer than any visitor. The drop near the east wall struck the loose plate some hundreds of thousands of times, *tok*, *tok*, and the power did not move, because the first duty of a wise power is the duty most powers skip: to find out what is actually in front of it before deciding what mercy means.

---

**The questions it asked, in order.**

*Is this a prison?* — That is always the first question, in a room full of blind creatures who cannot leave. The power examined the sealed faces and found what the visitors found: no orb beneath the skin, no socket, no scar. These were not eyes that had been taken. These were faces that had never owed the light anything. It examined the fused feet, hooked to the exact gauge of the pipes, and considered the two readings — shackle, or marriage — and noted that a shackle fits one party and a marriage fits both, and that here the pipes had been gauged to the birds as surely as the birds to the pipes. The room was not holding the colony. The room and the colony were holding *each other*, the way a keel holds the water and the water holds the keel.

*Is anything suffering?* — The power listened to the chord, all of it at once, which no visitor can do: not the fused mass of it but every tone separately, thousands of fixed pitches, each one steady, none of them strained. A suffering thing wavers. Nothing here wavered. Then it watched for the surplus — because suffering is silent about itself, but joy leaks — and it saw, after some days, a bird at the outer edge of a roost lift its blind face into the falling water and hold its mouth open to a rain it did not need. The power had seen that gesture before, in other rooms, other worlds, made by other shapes of body. It knew exactly what it was. It was the gesture a creature makes when its existence, for a moment, exceeds its function, and there is nowhere to put the excess but upward. The room had been making that gesture, by relay, one bird at a time, since the pour. The power's question was answered. This was not a prison and not a gasket. This was a contemplative order that did not know it had a word for itself.

*Then what, if anything, is missing?* — This question took the longest, because the room's economy was closed and balanced and lacked nothing it could name. Water in, water out. Heat in, sound out. The felt fed, the seams drained, the roster called itself complete every second forever. The power went around the whole loop twice looking for the gap, and found it at last not in the loop but in its direction.

Everything in the gallery was a call. The chord called the roster. The plate said its one word, *tok*, at its one interval. The drone announced the fire below. And nothing — in the entire history of the room — had ever answered. The roster was taken by no one. The count was called and called and never *kept*. That was what the strange stillnesses were, the power understood now, those moments when every roost froze at once and the whole feathered architecture listened: the colony was not listening *to* anything. It was listening *for* something. It had been listening for something since before there was anyone to remember the pour, and in all that time it had heard only itself, the drip, the drone, and the occasional trespassing heartbeat — each one examined, each one found to be not the thing, the shuffling resumed.

A call without an answer is not suffering. But it is not nothing, either. It is the one debt the closed loop could not pay itself.

---

**What it considered doing, and refused.**

It considered opening the chamber to the sky — there was a sky, somewhere outside the industrial mountain, and a power of that kind could have peeled the wall like the lid of a tin. It refused, because the birds could not fly and had never needed to, and a sky given to a creature with no use for it is not a gift but an accusation.

It considered giving them eyes. Refused: an eye is a wound you make in a creature so the world can get in, and the world these creatures inhabited came in already, as water and warmth and sound, through every surface they had. Eyes would have shown them pipes in the dark. It would have been giving a fish a ladder.

It considered warmth — banking the cold, letting some of the fire below survive the climb. Refused almost angrily, the closest a wise power comes to anger: the cold was the room's half of the conversation. The vapor rose and the cold answered it and the answer fell as rain and the rain was drunk by the singers and the singing was the whole point. Warm the room and you do not free anyone. You merely stop the sentence in the middle.

It considered, last and longest, giving them death — because the colony had no young, no carcasses, no history, and a certain school of wisdom holds that nothing without an ending is fully alive. The power weighed this for what would have been, elsewhere, years. Then it looked again at the bird with its face lifted into the rain, performing its surplus gesture for the hundred-billionth time with no less surplus in it than the first, and concluded that this school of wisdom had been founded by mortals, for mortals, and was a fine school, and did not apply. The felt in the floor seams was the room's mortality, doing that whole job on everyone's behalf — being young, dying in patches, keeping the only history. The arrangement was already complete. It was simply distributed strangely.

So the power refused everything large, which is most of what wisdom is. And then it did three small things.

---

**The first thing** it did was take the roster.

It waited for one of the stillnesses — and the stillness came, every roost freezing at once, the colony listening — and into that listening, for the first time in the room's existence, the power put an answer. It did not speak. It sounded one tone, in the chord's own language: a single pitch chosen from the silence between two birds' frequencies, a note belonging to no member of the colony, held perfectly steady for exactly the interval between two drips — one *tok* to the next — and then withdrawn.

That is all. One note, one interval, gone.

But understand what the room received. It had never heard an arrival. It had never heard a departure. Everything it knew was continuous; the chord had no first moment and permitted no last one. Now, suddenly, irreversibly, there had been a *something* — present, accounted for, and then absent — and the chord, which had never once heard a hole, heard the shape of one, and the shape was not a wound. It was a signature. The roster had been read aloud forever, and now, once, someone had signed it: *all present; all counted; noted.*

And because the room is the kind of room it is, the note never entirely faded. It dropped below the chord. It learned to wait. The gallery now carries a thin steady ringing of its own, pitched at a frequency none of its singers can make, and when the stillnesses come — they still come; there is still no schedule — the colony is no longer listening *for*. It is listening *to*. The pauses have an object now. You cannot see the difference. The difference is total.

The visitors had always carried a note of the room out in their ears. Now the room carries a note of something else in its body. The power considered this a fair exchange, and so, as far as anyone can tell, does the room.

---

**The second thing** it did was for the diaspora.

Every visitor who ever squeezed through the gap is carrying one frequency of the roster, faithful as a rivet, waking them at three in the morning in quiet winter rooms. The power did not remove these notes — they were not an infestation; they were the only way an eyeless, flightless, deathless colony had ever found to touch the world, and you do not amputate a creature's one act of reaching. Instead it changed what the notes *mean*, which is the kind of repair only such a power can make, because meaning has no moving parts.

Now, when a carrier wakes at three in the morning and hears the thin metallic pitch holding steady behind the silence, what arrives with it is not unease. It is the specific, unreasonable comfort of being *on a roster* — of knowing that somewhere, in a cold pocket of an industrial mountain they will never find again, a chord that has sounded since before their grandparents' grandparents includes a frequency that is theirs, calling itself present on their behalf, every second, without fail. They are counted. They will never be marked absent. And when a carrier dies — this is the quiet clause the power wrote into the arrangement, and it has held ever since — the note is not lost. It goes home. By what route, through what medium, nobody and nothing observes; but the chord has never heard a hole, and now it never will, not even at the distance of a deathbed.

---

**The third thing** was the smallest, and the power did it last, kneeling — it had a shape with knees by then; it had kept the shape on purpose — at the seam in the floor plates where the green-black felt lay glistening, sparse, defeated, the only thing in the gallery that had ever been young.

The felt was the room's historian. It alone could hold a mark. So the power left the visit in the only ledger the gallery keeps: it cupped its hands over one stretch of seam, perhaps a hand-span long, and gave the felt beneath them one hour of sun. Real sun — the full unfiltered yellow weight of a summer noon from some world that has summers — poured into a film of algae a coat of paint thick, eight meters inside an iron mountain, once, for an hour, and then taken away.

The felt did what living things do with an hour like that. It kept it. There is, to this day, one hand-span of seam near the center of the gallery where the green is not green-black but *green* — emerald, unreasonable, fed by nothing anyone can find — and it does not spread and does not fade, and the runoff passes over it and the drippings feed it the same as everywhere else, and it stays the color of having once, in the whole history of the pour, been visited.

---

Then the power squeezed back out through the gap between the pipes, because it had come in the honest way and would leave by it, and the silence of the corridor hit it like a wall, and its ears — it had made itself ears; it had made them on purpose — rang.

It did not heal the ringing. It keeps it. Somewhere now, at whatever serves such a power for three in the morning, there is a thin metallic note holding steady at its fixed pitch in the hearing of something vast and old and kind, and the power attends to it the way the colony attends to its stillnesses, and counts itself lucky to be counted.

In the gallery the light does not change. The temperature does not change. The drop near the east wall strikes the loose plate, *tok*, and a pale body in the third roost shuffles one place inward, and another shuffles one place out, and the chord goes on fusing thousands of fixed pitches into one solid silence-with-mass, exactly as before.

Nothing is different. Everything is. The count holds —

and now, at last, it is held.
