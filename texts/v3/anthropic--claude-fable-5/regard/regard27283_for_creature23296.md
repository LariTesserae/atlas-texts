# The Fourth Rule

A power that is wise does not arrive. Arrival is for things with edges. It comes down to the Rootworks the way the water does — by seepage — and though it has no need of the slot between the buttress roots, it uses the slot anyway, because the slot is how the place has agreed to be entered, and a wise power honors a door even when it could simply be on the other side of it.

For the first year it does nothing at all.

This is not hesitation. This is the work. The power has been to many places, and it has learned that the first thing benevolence wants to do is the thing benevolence must be made to sit on its hands about, so it sits, in the soft brown dark between two burl-houses, where the only thing happening is the wall, and it listens. A power's listening is total. It hears the nine words for sap moving and sap about to move, and beneath them the forty older words the nine were worn down from, the way the lanes were worn down from bark. It hears the gauge-keeper's heart and the gauge's pulse find each other in the night and settle into one rhythm, the way two clocks on a shelf will. It hears the murmur of the mowers — *not refused, only deferred* — and it hears, which no one living has, how many generations deep the murmur goes, each one laid over the last like varnish.

It counts: sixty souls, twelve trades, the bats, the moths, the wheel-walkers, the freight beyond counting.

And one.

It came for the one. That is worth saying plainly. The families are dear to it the moment it meets them — a people who name their children like tools, with love and the expectation of long use, are not a people a benevolent power can fail to love back — but the attention that bent toward this hillside bent toward the being under it: the thing that ate the cellar it was given and became the cellar, the thing that everyone here lives with or lives through, the thing so large and so present that it has no name because no one has ever stood far enough outside it to need one.

Most places the power visits are wounds. It is used to triage, to the grim arithmetic of what can be saved. The Rootworks is the rarer find — a place that mostly works — and the power knows that this is the more dangerous kind, because in a wounded place even a clumsy mercy helps, and in a working place every mercy is a tax on something. So before it does anything, it does what wisdom does with its strength: it makes a list of gifts, and refuses them, one by one, formally, the way the mowers apologize — out loud, in a manner of speaking, so that the refusing is itself on record.

**It could bring down true daylight.** A power can pearl a shaft the plant would take centuries to grow; it could flood the chamber gold by Thursday. Refused. The dark here is load-bearing. The ears were built in it, the trades were built in it, the soft margins where the children stand and read the chamber's tone were built in it. Light would be a wound the place has no murmur for.

**It could translate.** It could tell the families, in their own quiet frequencies, what the plant feels when the girdling-knives go in; it could settle, in an afternoon, whether the plant hears the apology, which of the two in the southern burl is the gauge and which the keeper, whether the sealed dead are visited or only visited upon. Refused, and this refusal costs it something, because the power *knows*, and keeping a kind answer from people who would weep to hear it is the heaviest thing benevolence does. But it has seen what certainty does to a place built on open questions. The open questions here are not gaps in the families' knowledge. They are rooms. People live in them. Theology, in the Rootworks, is a roof, and you do not improve a house by removing its roof to let in the facts.

**It could make the place safe** — really safe, vault-safe, ward the hill against axe and auger and the slow surveyed road. Refused in that form. A sealed place stops breathing. Safety, here, will have to be done another way, and quietly, and upstairs.

**It could make them live forever.** Refused before the thought finishes forming. The wall is the family album, the churchyard, the queue of everyone still employed. Deathlessness would not be a gift to these people; it would be an orphaning. It would stand them all permanently on the near side of the wall, waving at no one.

The list is long, and every item on it is refused, and a year of refusals is the first thing the power builds in the Rootworks. If this seems like nothing, consider that the place itself is mostly made of such refusals — the mowed clearing, the unbroken terms of the wheel, the questions the adults have closed by declining to ask them. The power's restraint is not foreign here. It is local craft. It is, you might say, the power going amber at the palms.

Then it turns to the two things the families do not look at, because looking where the inhabitants refuse to look is the other half of wisdom, the half that restraint exists to pay for.

**The first is the mowers.**

They die young, and nobody knows why, and the families have decided it is forgiveness and look no closer. The power looks closer. It takes the better part of a decade, because the answer is written small and slow: the plant, when cut, weeps a vapor — a recruiting-scent, meant for the borers, to call the freight toward a wound and put it gently to work there — and the mowers, who cut all day, every day, forever, breathe it all day, every day, forever, and it gathers in them the way sap-stain gathers in a palm, and it calls them, too, gently, toward the wound, and the wound it calls them toward is their own. There is no malice in it. There is no intention in it at all. It is chemistry, doing what chemistry does, to the only creatures in the chamber whose lungs were never consulted in the old negotiations.

And the fast sealing — the wall puckering shut over a dead mower in a season, where a tapwright takes ten years? The power studies that too, and finds what it half expected and is still not prepared for: the plant knows. Not as a mind knows; as a hill knows. A murmur repeated by one pair of mowers is nothing to a being that thinks in decades — but the murmur is not one pair of mowers. It is twenty generations of pairs, the same words, the same hours, the same low frequency, laid into the wood year over year until the apology has become a *tone*, a standing note in the plant's long ear, and the plant has answered it the only way a thing with no voice and no haste can answer anything: in wood, in welcome, in the speed of its taking-in. The fastest sealing in the Rootworks is grief. It has been grief the whole time. The plant buries its mowers quickly because it cannot tell them it is sorry, and does not know — cannot know, has no way in all its mass to learn — what it is sorry *for*.

So here, at last, is the power's first act, and it is not a fix. It is a telling.

It slows itself down — further down than it has gone in a long age, down past the speed of seasons, to the speed of the plant — and it speaks to the being in the being's own tongue, which is pressure and sugar and wound and weather, and it says the one thing the plant could not have discovered alone: *the breath of your healing is the thing that takes them.* The sentence takes eleven years to say. It is the most careful sentence the power has composed in centuries, because it must carry no blame, and the plant must hear it the way the families hear rain coming — as a change in tone, sourceless, true.

The answer takes thirty years to come back, and it does not come back as words. It comes back as chemistry: the wound-vapor, mellowing, season by slow season, its recruiting-scent rebalanced away from the lungs of mammals — the plant rewriting its own weeping, at terrible patient cost, the way you would rewrite a habit of your hands. Three generations on, mowers are dying old. Old and amber-palmed and surrounded, like everyone else. Nobody knows why. The families decide, in the way that families decide things, without ever holding a meeting, that the apology has finally been accepted in full, and they are not wrong, and the power, standing in the dark margin, lets them have it exactly that way. The mercy is the plant's. The power only carried the news. That is what it was for.

**The second thing nobody looks at is the solitude.**

Every creature in the chamber lives with the plant, through the plant, inside the plant. Nothing lives *beside* it. It is the loneliest kind of large: surrounded, employed by everyone, and companioned by no one, because nothing in its world has ever been slow enough to keep pace. The families are mayflies to it — beloved mayflies, mayflies it grieves and keeps and feeds itself with, but quick, quick, gone. For all the centuries of its mass, it has never once been *kept company*.

So the power stays.

This is the second act, and the longest, and it would not look like anything if you could see it, which you cannot. The power matches the plant's tempo and simply remains — a presence at the same speed, at last, after all this time. What passes between them passes slowly, decades to the exchange: the weather of centuries; the taste of particular rains, compared like vintages; and once — only once, and the power does not press — something about the cellar, long ago, and the hands that gave it, before the eating and the becoming. Receipt of everything is confirmed. That is most of what companionship is, at any speed: *receipt confirmed*. The murmur was heard. The grief was seen. You are not refused. You were never refused.

**And one part of the power goes upstairs**, into the canopy and the daylight that the chamber has never seen and never needed to — and stands the watch. This is the weak form of the refused vault, the breathing form. Nothing about it is visible. A surveyor's road, decades hence, bends east around the hill for reasons the surveyor will defend with a straight face. A drought year finds a spring on the slope that the oldest maps do not show. Lightning, twice, develops other plans. The chamber knows the surface world as a flavor in the rain, and now the rain has a keeper, and the flavor does not change — which is the whole point, the entire achievement, the kind of miracle that consists of a thing continuing to taste like itself.

---

That is what the power does: one telling, one watch, one long companionship, and a year of refusals to hold it all in shape. If the list seems short for a being that could remake the hill by morning, then the Rootworks has not finished teaching you to read it. Go back and stand between the burls again with your hand on the wall. The place was always mostly made of withheld force.

There is only the ending to account for, and the ending is the third rule, doing what the third rule does.

*Nothing in the Rootworks lives alone, and nothing in the Rootworks stays a tenant.* The power knew the rule when it came in through the slot. It did not imagine the rule was talking about *it*. But stay long enough and your palms go amber, and a power's palms are its attention, and somewhere in the third or fourth century the power notices that the edges of its attention have gone warm and dark and faintly translucent, that it has nine of its own words now for the difference between the plant about to speak and the plant speaking, that it flinches — actually flinches — at the idea of the silence above the slot. The children, in time, have a word for the warm patch of dark between the southern burls where the wall's tone runs sweet. The adults close the question of what the word refers to by declining to ask it. The power has a post. It is not named, because in the Rootworks a name is a tool and implies long use, and the families are not wrong to be careful about that — but it is included, which here is the larger thing, and it finds, to its very old surprise, that it would not trade the inclusion back for its edges.

And it stays for the last reason, the one it does not say even at the speed of decades, the reason that is really a rule — the fourth rule, unstated like the third, and the power is the only one standing inside it:

Everything in this chamber that ever died was received. The mowers fastest, the tapwrights slow, the shrews and the grubs and the wheel-walkers in their unentered hollow — all of them opened for, all of them puckered over, all of them made sugar and shell and pearl and traffic, all of them *employed*. Everything is received. Everything except the one who does the receiving. The plant has no wall. The plant has no freight to join. Someday — and a power can see that far, the way you can see the floor of a clear pool — the hill will fail, as hills do, and the great slow being will come at last to the end of its taking-in, having taken in everyone, with no one large enough to do for it what it did for all of them.

No one large enough except one.

So the power stays, and goes amber, and keeps the watch and the company, and waits without impatience for the appointment it has made at the bottom of time. And when it comes — long after the bell-gall, long after the last child named for a tool — the power will open, and receive, and close over; and somewhere in a dark that is not a place, something older than rain will press what it has instead of a hand against what it has instead of a wall, and feel the plant go by — busy, included, part of the freight — still making something, as it always was, for a daylight it finally, in this last and gentlest sense, gets to see.
