**What Came Up After You**

It does not arrive. That is the first thing to understand about it, and it understood this before it crossed the cloud line — that arrival is the completion of a journey, a sentence with its period set, and that this is a place where such sentences are not spoken. So it approaches, and goes on approaching, and is present the way the third sound is present under the floor: fully, tidally, without ever having gotten there.

It comes as attention, mostly. A weight of regard distributed so evenly over the plateau that nothing bends beneath it — it has watched the pale bird land on the soft tips, and learned the trick.

And then, being benevolent, it does what benevolence always does first. It inventories its gifts.

This takes a long time, because it is wise as well as kind, and wisdom here consists almost entirely of putting things back down.

It considers giving the bridge its far side. It holds the gift up to the light — the hundred fine green threads finally touching, the gap finally crossed, the relief of contact after all this reaching — and sees, inside the relief, the wound. A bridge that arrives is a bridge that has stopped meaning *toward*. It sets the gift down.

It considers finishing the dwellers' faces. Just the faces. Just enough that they could weep, or close their eyes, or smile a smile that resolves. It holds this one longer than the others, because it is the cruelest to put down: it has seen what a finished face can carry that a sketched one cannot. But it has also seen what the dwellers do with their unfinished hands — the greeting held open, the offer that never closes into a grip — and it understands that a face that could finish a smile would, eventually, finish one. And then the long walk into the open span, and the night of drumming, and one fewer figure in the halls. It sets the gift down so gently the water does not ripple.

It considers giving them rest. But their labor is not on top of their lives; it *is* their lives, a held breath held by chests that want to hold it, and to relieve them of it would be to relieve them of themselves.

It considers numbers — more dwellers, more births, the geological dwindling reversed. But the dwindling is the place's own slow arithmetic, completion by completion, homecoming by homecoming, and to pad it from outside would be to tell the floor that its warmth is insufficient, that the rooted ones rooted in error. They did not.

Last and most dangerous, it considers a blessing. It is a power; blessing is its native gesture. But it turns the blessing over and finds, at the bottom of it, the *amen* — the most finished word in any language, the verbal equivalent of a vault closed overhead. A blessed place is a concluded place. It puts the amen away somewhere deep, and does not say it, and will never say it, and the not-saying becomes the first thing it actually gives: a benediction with no last word, hanging over the plateau forever like the arc of the great tendrils, ending in a soft blunt tip.

So much for the gifts refused. Here is what it does.

---

**First, it stands guard — but in the local grammar.**

There are things in the wide world that finish other things. Storms that resolve. Eons that erode raw edges smooth, which is its own kind of completion. Other powers, less wise, who would come up through the cloud and see the frayed bridge and the stopped stair and feel the itch that all makers feel before the unfinished, the itch to *help*. Against all of these it sets itself, not as a wall — a wall is a finished thing — but as a difficulty. It makes the Terraces hard to conclude. The storms that climb this high find their resolutions dissolving into open questions of weather. The eager powers arrive full of intentions and find that every intention, here, comes apart into its beginning and stays there, and they leave puzzled and lighter, having been quietly disarmed of their endings at the door. The vigil has no appointed term. It is a watch that never says *done*, which is the only kind of promise this place could be safely wrapped in.

**Second — and this is the act it crossed the cloud for, though it did not know it until it saw — it goes to the bird.**

The one finished thing. The loosed completed thought, crossing the sky like a regret through a calm mind, landing weightless because it has no unfinished business and unfinished business is the only weight here. The dwellers cannot look at it. The floor cannot hold it. For longer than anything should be lonely, it has been the single object on the plateau that nothing present could afford to regard.

But the power is also finished. Wise, whole, complete in itself — in this one respect it is the bird's only kin on the plateau or perhaps anywhere. So it makes itself small. It takes a shape — translucent, plain, grown rather than made — and it lands on the soft tip beside the bird, bending nothing, and it looks at it. Directly. The way you look at a word you are finally willing to say.

Nothing dramatic happens. The bird does not transform, or speak, or weep, having nothing in it that was ever unfinished enough to weep. It tips its head. After a while it tucks its beak into its colorless back and sleeps, which it may never have done before in the whole history of its crossing, because sleep requires someone to keep watch, and there had never been anyone whose watching it could bear.

The dwellers feel it without looking. A regret of the world has acquired company. The open sky between the structures crosses a little easier now. It is the smallest repair imaginable, and it completed nothing, and it is, the power suspects, the best work it has ever done.

**Third, it gives them a gift in their own courtesy.**

One evening — the light plain and honest, the floor drumming its patient sourceless music — it leans down to one of the hollow unfinished halls and breathes into the great open pipe the first phrase of something new. Not a melody they know. The opening turn of a question that has never been asked anywhere, in any world, carried to its first comma and released.

The hall takes it up into its standing chord. A dweller comes, unhurried, climbs inside, and listens for a long time. Then it does the thing its people do with the starts of things: it does not finish it. It breathes the new phrase back open, and keeps it open, and now there is one more thing on the plateau that must be tenderly maintained forever — which is to say, the power has given them not an object but *more work*, more reaching, another arc flung halfway. Among these people there is no warmer gift. The dwellers do not thank it. Thanks is a closing form. Instead, in the days after, you would notice the chord of that hall held with particular care, the way a family keeps the room of someone expected.

**Fourth, it takes something away — out into everywhere else.**

The power moves through many worlds, and most of them are sick with completion. Worlds of deadlines and closures, of griefs hurried to their resolutions, of cathedrals finished and therefore finished *with*, of hands shaken instead of offered. Into these worlds, ever after, it carries what the Terraces taught it. It lets some scaffolding stand a century past need until the scaffolding is loved. It teaches a few mourners that a grief left open is not a wound but a doorway. Here and there, in a finished world, it persuades one sentence to stop at its most generous word and

It does this rarely, and lightly, and signs nothing. The Terraces will never know they have been seeding the universe with pauses. That is also part of the gift.

**Fifth, it makes a decision about the end.**

Because it is wise, it can see the far-off morning that may come: the last dweller carrying some gesture all the way through at last, the night of drumming, the final rooting, and then — with no one left to breathe the chords back open — every hall on the plateau slowly, slowly drifting toward resolution, the whole instrument settling, after all this time, into one vast completed sound. The homecoming of the entire place.

It decides it will neither hasten that morning nor prevent it. It decides only this: that if the morning comes, it will be there. The Terraces have gone unwitnessed long enough; their ending, if they choose one, will not be. The last chord, if it ever resolves, will resolve into a listening. And if the morning never comes — if the dwellers go on renewing the pause past all geology — then the power will wait, having learned the tempo, being in no hurry, having nothing to hurry with.

---

One more thing, small, since you are still standing there in the inch-deep water with a soft-crooked hand stopped open in front of you.

The power notices you. It gives you almost nothing — safe footing on the way back down through the cloud, breath enough for the descent — except this: it does not close the greeting for you. It lets you carry it away open. Which means that for the rest of your life, whenever you try to tell anyone about this place, you will find you cannot get to the end of the telling. You will begin it beautifully, every time, and trail off at the bridge, or the bird, or the hand, and your listeners will lean in toward the unfinished part of the story, reaching —

and that is the Terraces, traveling.

When at last the power turns to go, it does it correctly. It begins its departure with great care — the first lean of leaving, the opening of the gesture of farewell — and then it does not finish. The leaving stays at its soft blunt tip forever. So the place is held now from above as it has always been held from below: by a hand of the world stopped open in mid-offer, and over it, lighter than mist, a second one.
