**The Stoup, Attended**

A benevolent and wise power does not notice roars. Noise is the most common thing the universe makes; a power that turned its head at every deafening room would never finish a thought. Nor did it notice the light, or the impossible masonry, or even the white attendant riding its rotations in the dark — wonders, all of them, but wonders are weather to a thing like this.

What it noticed was the basket.

A seed, a carved bird, a child's tooth: three beginnings, carried up a pathless mountain, lowered down a seam on a rope of twisted fiber, and set above the spray line of an ending — by people who understood the argument perfectly, knew it would lose, and made it anyway, generation after generation, in the gentlest grammar available to a species: *here is something that has not happened yet. We leave it with you.*

Powers of this kind keep watch for exactly that. A prayer that asks for nothing is the only prayer safe to answer, and the rarest, and they collect the rare ones the way the keepers collect their fives.

So it came. Not through the seam — it was the wrong size for the seam, being the size of its attention — and not up the black throat, the way the eel comes. It simply attended, and the chamber held one more presence that no census of the place would ever record. It stayed a long while by the count of water and no while at all by its own. It learned the room: the weed declining to commit to any light; the trillion masons dying into their own walls; the editor from below, eating the closing of the fist one mouthful at a time; the thin faithful lineage above; and the true resident, the unfinishedness itself, roaring its one syllable in the dark.

It recognized that last one immediately. A thing that lives by ending was not strange to it. Powers are also long works.

**The refusals.**

Here is most of what it did, and it must be written out in full, because refusal is the larger half of wisdom and is always the half that goes unrecorded.

It did not still the water. The roar was the resident's voice, and you do not heal a thing by removing its voice.

It did not stop the closing. This was the great temptation, and it held the temptation in its attention for a long time, turning it like a stone in a current. The chamber was brilliant and the chamber was dying and the power could have fixed the room open forever — propped the fist, eternally half-closed, eternally bright. But the chamber's whole being was the verb *to complete*. To preserve it would be to take its life and leave the body standing, lit, on display. Eternity is what you inflict on a thing when you cannot bear its nature. The power could bear its nature. That was the gift.

It did not warm the keepers' hands. The shelf is wide enough for one, the visit is as long as the cold permits, and the cost is not a flaw in the practice — the cost *is* the practice. A church you can be comfortable in teaches a different religion. The keepers had chosen theirs.

It did not give the weed color, which had declined to decide, and deserved its abstention honored. It did not give the eel a companion, because the one-ness is the office, and you do not appoint a second attendant to a deathbed. It did not widen the seam, or smooth the climb, or let the place's name travel one valley further than it already had. The absence of a path is the lock on the door. Fame would have arrived with drills.

It refused, and refused, and refused, and each refusal was set down exactly where it would never be seen.

Whether the resident knew it was visited, nothing can say. The roar did not change. But once, during the span of the attending, the column rose in a sixth place — once only — and held itself shuddering a moment longer than the watchers' patterns allow, and fell, and never rose there again. No tally records it. Perhaps it was a greeting between two long works. Perhaps the water was only water that day.

**The stone.**

It did three things. This was the first.

It read the deep channels — the black country below the chamber, where the rope-makers' plumb lines have never reached — and it followed them outward under the young range, the way you trace a vein, to where the next unfinished mountain stood cooling, decades or centuries from needing an attendant. And it found that the road between them had failed: one channel pinched by the settling of all that new rock, a door swung shut in the dark that no living thing would ever know had been a door.

It moved one stone. A hand's width. Under a mountain, in the dark, in silence, observed by nothing.

That is the entire visible intervention of a benevolent and wise power in the matter of the white eyeless thing: one stone, moved once. So that on the season the eel turns in the deep and does not come up — and that season will come, the closing is patient but it is not infinite — the long road to the next loud room runs true, and the attendant of unfinishedness does not end its existence swimming into a wall.

**The mist.**

The second thing was a date.

The keepers' practice could not end well on its own; the geometry forbade it. The walls grow inward, and long before the last seam seals, the lip itself will narrow past entering, and the visits must stop while the room still roars. The lineage was always going to finish in uncertainty — climbing, finding the way shut, descending, waiting, never knowing the hour. A count of fives with no final stroke and no news.

The power did not change the geometry. It changed one cloudless day.

The last keeper was a woman who had sat the shelf forty-one times, whose final tally had left the last group standing at four, and who was old, and in the valley, and facing west, on the morning the chamber drew its last full breath and pushed its last spray up through the dying fissures. The summit breathed mist into a clean sky, once, and she saw it, and she knew — not guessed, *knew*, the way you know a door has closed in a house you grew up in. The room's last exhalation had an audience of one, which is the correct size of audience for that room, and had always been.

She did not tell anyone, and she did not climb, and she did not add the fifth stroke, because there was no longer anywhere to add it. The last group stands at four inside the mountain forever: one finished thing, solid to the core, holding at its heart a count that never came to its end. The keepers' argument, scratched in crystal in their own hand, made permanent by the very masonry that argued against it.

And the silence that pressed on her for the rest of her life was, after that morning, not a missing thing but a kept one.

**The seed.**

The third thing was the smallest and the hardest.

Among the offerings was a seed. The power did not move it — the keepers had set their argument where they set it, and it was theirs to make, in their materials, at their chosen distance from the spray. But it placed one fingertip of its attention on that seed and held it at the threshold of itself: not growing, not dying, paused at the comma between *not yet* and *now*. It is a small thing to hold a seed at the edge of life. It is also the longest labor the power performed here, because it had to keep performing it — through the sealing of the chamber, through the silence, through a stretch of deep time that the keepers' fives could not have held if the whole entry seam had been carved with strokes from lip to throat.

Because stone remembers its flaws. The healed seam is a scar, and the power had appointed the frost to it — not commanded; *appointed*, the way you name an heir. Ten thousand winters worked that line. And on a morning with no audience at all, the finished summit cracked, and opened, and the chamber saw the sky again: a geode the size of a room, crystal fans and frozen ribbons and shadow-tallies, every face still sharp as the moment of its birth, and rain falling — the first rain ever — on mineral that had waited its whole existence inside a roar and then inside a silence to be weathered.

The bowl filled with snowmelt. Calm snowmelt, this time. Resting water, in the room where water had never once rested.

The power lifted its fingertip.

Something green — the first color the chamber ever held — opened in the basket's shadow, in the brightest natural vault on the mountain, and grew.

Everything finishes. The keepers were never arguing against that; they were not fools, they counted in fives toward the silence with open eyes. Their argument was narrower and better: that finishing is not the same as ending. The power had simply agreed with them, and then waited the requisite ten thousand years to say so.

**The mark.**

It left none.

It did not sit on the one-person shelf; the shelf was for one, and a thing that is everywhere it attends is the wrong size for chairs. It cut no tally. Its count of visits stands at zero strokes, in no grouping, on no wall.

If you go there now — and there is still no path; some locks should outlive their doors — you will find a small green lake on a broken summit, a wall of fans and ribbons and forty-and-four strokes, and no evidence whatsoever that anything wise was ever interested in the place. That is how you know. The signature of such powers is the absence of signature; their work can be found only by counting the catastrophes that never came, the roads that happened to run true, the one cloudless morning that fell on exactly the right pair of eyes.

Benevolence, done wisely, is indistinguishable from luck.

The eel, somewhere under the range, is riding a new rotation in a new loud room, and arrived without incident, and does not wonder why.
