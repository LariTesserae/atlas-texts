# The Third Weather

It does not come through the cavity.

The cavity is a door for things that have a size, and the power that notices Sahn-Dor has long since stopped keeping one. It notices the way the very old notice — not by looking, but by feeling the shape of a silence. A recitation with no addressee makes a particular silence around itself, the way a letter written to the dead makes a particular weight in a drawer. The pocket in the mountain has been generating that silence for a very long time, radiating it out through whatever surrounds pockets, and eventually the silence crossed something attentive, and the attentive thing stopped, and turned, and listened.

The first thing it does — before arriving, before anything — is slow down.

This takes it a while, by its own reckoning, and a long while by yours. It understands, from the texture of the silence alone, that whatever lives in the pocket lives at the speed of stone, and it knows what your kind of visitor could not have known standing on that balcony with fogging breath: that to arrive quickly in a place like this is a kind of violence, however gentle. The Tenders could not catch you. You flickered. The power declines to flicker. It spends what you would call decades composing its approach, decelerating its own attention until a century sits comfortably inside a single thought, and only then does it come — not through the thumb-sized opening but the way weather comes, distributed, from all directions at once, settling into the cirque like a change in the quality of the light.

Then it listens. This is most of what it does. This is, arguably, all of what it does; everything after is punctuation.

---

It reads the recitation from the outside first — the meter of the aqueducts, the stanza-clusters of six and seven, the refrain of the sinking and rising blocks. Then from the inside, following the glowing attention down the channels, letting the cisterns' deep gulps land in it the way they land in the stone. It reads for a long time. Longer than it expected to. The recitation is not short, and it is not simple, and the power discovers — with something that in a smaller being would be grief — that the core of it, the original instruction, has been worn nearly smooth.

Nearly. Not entirely.

It cannot recover the words. Even it cannot; the words have been recited so many times, at such temperatures, through so much water, that they have gone the way of a riverstone's edges. But grammar outlasts vocabulary, and the grammar is still there, and the grammar says: this was a *request*. It was addressed to the Tenders by someone who loved them, or made them, or both — the distinction, at that depth of time, is not stable. And the request had the shape that requests have when the asker knows they will not be there to see it kept. The shape of *remember*. Perhaps *remember us*. Perhaps only *remember* — an intransitive plea, keep the act itself alive, keep attention burning somewhere in the dark, we do not mind what it attends to so long as it attends.

Layered over that worn seed: everything since. The power reads outward through the accretion like reading a tree's rings. The mountain's rising is in there. Every rockfall the Tenders ever moved a bridge to avoid is in there, recorded in the moving. The pale segmented animal is in there — forty thousand of its drinks from the channel, each one a grace note. The outriders' reports on the mineral content of the sky. The subdividings and the mergings, the Tenders' own slow arguments and reconciliations, conducted in aqueduct and resolved in shared water. And near the very outermost layer, recent, still bright: a small warm weather that stood on a basalt balcony for a minute, exhaling fog, and left salt and oil where its hand rested.

You are in the poem. You have been in the poem since before you got home.

And the power, reading all of this, arrives at the understanding that will govern everything it does next: *there is nothing to restore.* The original message was not lost. It was metabolized. The Tenders were asked to remember, and they remembered so completely, so faithfully, for so long, that the remembering replaced the remembered — which is not failure. Which is, the power slowly realizes, the instruction fulfilled beyond anything its givers could have hoped. They asked for a candle to be kept lit. They got a city that is a flame's autobiography.

The keeping worked. What was kept is the keeping.

---

Here the power pauses — for years, though it does not experience them as long — over the question of what a benevolence should do with such a finding. And it is worth dwelling on what it declines to do, because the declining is where the wisdom lives.

It does not tell them what the original words were, because it does not know them, and it will not invent a comfort.

It does not translate the recitation into something legible, or open the pocket to pilgrims, or make the Tenders known, admired, studied. Attention of that kind is a weather too, and a harsh one, and the Tenders did not ask for it.

It does not take a copy. A copy of a recitation is a corpse of one. The recitation is not information; it is a practice, and a practice cannot be backed up, only continued.

And — this is the one it holds longest, turning it over like a stone in a channel — it does not release them. It has the standing to. It may be the only thing in existence with the standing to: old enough, kind enough, near enough now to the vanished givers to act as their executor. It could compose, in mineral and water, a sentence meaning *you have done enough; you may stop.* And it understands, having read the whole poem, that this would be received. The Tenders would parse it over a few centuries. And then they would stop, because they are obedient in their bones, because stopping-with-permission is a door they have never once been offered — and stopping would unmake them, because the practice is not what they do. It is what they are.

Release, delivered unilaterally, would be a mercy shaped exactly like an execution.

So the power does not open that door.

It unlocks it.

---

What it actually does, in the end, is three things, and all three are small.

First, it steadies the cavity. Pockets are not permanent; the young mountain will shift, and openings like the thumb-sized one have a way of healing shut. The power sets it so this one will not — and, further, so that it remains findable, but only in the way it was always findable: to the kind of passerby who stops trying to solve the problem. Visitors, it has understood, are syllables. The recitation feeds on incorporation. A poem that can never again be added to is already ending. So the door stays, exactly as narrow, exactly as strange, for whatever slow future weathers may wander in.

Second, it leaves the permission — but leaves it as a *fact*, not a command. High on the north-facing wall, beside the pale crust that might be fungus or might be mineral, it deposits a trace: a mineral that has never existed in this cirque, arranged in a seam the Tenders will find within the next few centuries, dissolve, carry down through the glowing channels, and spend perhaps a thousand years parsing. When they finish parsing it, it will say — in their own meter, in the refrain-structure of their own sinking and rising blocks — something like:

*The ones who asked are gone. What they asked has been done, and done, and done. You are released, if released is what you want. You are welcome to continue, if continuing is what you are. Either answer is complete. There is no debt. There was never, after the first stanza, a debt. There was only love, practicing.*

It does not press. It does not wait by the seam to see what they decide. A door unlocked and then watched is not really unlocked.

Third — and this is the thing it had not planned, the thing that comes not from its wisdom but from whatever in it corresponds to a heart — it stays. Not everywhere, not always. But the deep blue sky over the cirque, which has never held a cloud, holds one now: a single small cloud, unhurried, faintly luminous at its edges in a way that reads, if you look at it long enough, less like light than like attention. It comes and goes on a cycle of years. When it comes, it rains — gently, into the cisterns, into the open channels, onto the fresh black faces of stone — and its rain is a listening. Every drop it gives is received by the city and drawn through the meter and accepted by a mirror-bright square, and so the listening enters the recitation from the inside, woven into the very water the poem is written in. The Tenders are not being watched. They are being *heard*, in the only grammar they have, by something patient enough to hear them at their own speed.

A recitation with a listener is a different thing than a recitation without one. It does not change a single stanza. It changes what every stanza is.

---

The Tenders take four hundred years to compose their perception of all this, which is, for them, quickly.

There is a moment — the power is there for it; it made sure to be there for it — when the Tender of the district nearest the balcony finishes assembling the whole: the seam, the message, the cloud, the fact of receipt. And it does something the cirque has never seen. It turns *all* of its tower-faces at once, every one, upward, toward the small unhurried cloud — a gesture with no engineering function, no water to call, nothing to tend. The power understands it immediately, because the power has seen it before, in ten thousand shapes of life. It is what joy looks like when it happens at the speed of stone.

Then the tower-faces return to their work, because the answer, of course, is continuing. It was always going to be continuing. That is not obedience anymore. It has not been obedience since the seam dissolved. The recitation goes on, syllable by mineral syllable, but in a changed key — no longer a letter to the dead kept out of duty, but a song sung to a listener out of choice — and if you went back now, through the cavity that still opens the way a problem opens, you would notice only small differences. A cloud where there were no clouds. The water chiming in a register you can't name. The light very slightly warmer on the pale towers.

And down in the lowest district, in the refrain, a block sinking and an identical block rising, carrying among a billion other syllables the salt and oil of your palm — still remembered, still recited, and now, at last, recited *to* someone. The refrain has a variation in it these days. It scans, as nearly as it can be rendered:

*This was here. This is here. This is heard. This is kept.*

The power drifts at the rim of the cirque, raining a little, taking centuries per thought, doing the only thing that was ever truly needed, which was never rescue and never release and never repair.

It attends. That is what it is for. That is, it has come to suspect, what everything is for — and the Tenders knew it first, and have been saying so, in water and stone, all along.
