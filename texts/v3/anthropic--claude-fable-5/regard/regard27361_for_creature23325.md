**How it arrives.**
The same way everything else did: drawn by a thread of warmth rising from a crack two hands wide. Power notices what is rich, and the field reads as nothing from every angle but one — and the power, being what it is, has all the angles. It feels the room before it sees it, the way you feel a held breath in a quiet house. Then it comes down the fissure like the light does, without weight, and does the first wise thing, which is nothing.

It stays a full year. It watches the blade swing its slow noon arc through all four seasons. It learns the drip schedule the way the midges know it, from inside. It counts — the sixty bats, the two lizards, the band of crickets, the forty generations of snails, the one fern, the one human — and somewhere in the counting it understands that it has not found a place full of creatures. It has found a creature. Young. Bright. Running fast. And dying, a millimeter a year, of being itself.

So before it does anything, it does the arithmetic the notebook has been circling for decades. The contraction will not stay linear. The spalling will accelerate as the stresses gather; the hairline fractures the snails are grazing will begin to meet each other; the room has perhaps a human lifetime left, perhaps less. The held breath will be let out.

The power could stop this. Understand that clearly: it could set its palm against the slag and hold the bubble open forever, fix the cooling, freeze the closing eye mid-blink. It considers this for a long time — long by its own standards, which is very long.

And then it sees what the consideration almost cost it.

The room is not a place that happens to be fast. The room *is* its speed. The straws grow a finger a decade because the room is young; the crickets fell silent in ten years because the glass punished song; the shells hold their archive because the archive will end. Everything the power fell in love with — and it is honest enough to use that word — was made by the same contraction that is killing it. A room that cannot die is a vitrine. The power has seen vitrines. It has, in its long existence, made a few, and it does not visit them.

**What it does not do.**
It does not widen the fissure. It does not slow the walls. It does not warm the floor a single degree or cool it. It does not break the lizard dynasty's one hard law, though the law is cruel and the lean young one waiting by the pool tugs at it. It does not teach the crickets what their stillness-pattern means. It does not heal the room of its mortality, because the mortality is load-bearing.

Inside the glass, it permits itself almost nothing. Almost.

**What it does.**
It goes back up the fissure and gets to work where the room cannot see, because the room's true dangers were never internal. The slag holds value now — aggregate, reclamation grants, the new appetite for old metal. There are core samples scheduled. There is a crusher that eats pours like this one in a week.

So the power becomes the room's dullness. This is its great labor, and it looks like nothing, which is the point. The assay from the 1989 pour comes back unremarkable — not falsified, merely sampled, by small nudges of a technician's boot, from the most boring square meter of a forty-acre field. The reclamation money goes to the western pours, where it is genuinely needed. The crusher contract specifies 1994 for reasons of access roads that no one questions because they are true; the power only arranged which truths got noticed. A drone survey is flown on the one overcast morning when the fissure casts no shadow. A liability fence is proposed, budgeted, and quietly deferred, three years running, by a councilwoman who could never say why the item kept slipping to the bottom of the agenda.

Everyone above remains certain the field holds nothing at all. They were certain before. The difference is that now their certainty is *guarded* — a wall of benevolent boredom, maintained the way the bacteria maintain the pool's film, rebuilt from underneath within hours of any disturbance.

And it tends the bloodstream. The room imports its entire economy through sixty small bodies a night, which means the room's true borders are the river willows and the moth-thick field margins a mile out. The power keeps the moth country whole. A streetlight scheme dies in committee — wrong bulb tender, twice. A hedgerow slated for removal is found to be on the wrong parcel, which it always was; someone simply finally checked. None of this is dramatic. The power's benevolence, viewed from any human distance, is indistinguishable from a run of minor administrative luck.

**The one indulgence.**
It allows itself a single act inside the glass, and it spends years choosing it.

It does not choose the lizards, or the crickets, or the closing walls. It chooses the fern. Thirty years it has watched the spores ripen, drift, settle on hot dark glass, and die — every spore, every year, the loneliest arithmetic in a crowded room. One spring, one spore drifts two hand-widths farther than the air accounts for and settles into the moss skirt of a drip-point where the blade rests second-longest. That is all. A breath redirected. The power does not even stay to watch it germinate; it knows, and knowing, it feels something it has no organ for and files under *weather*.

There are two ferns now. The room's oldest individual is no longer a population of one. Nothing in the room notices, and the power counts this as success.

**The witness.**
It recognized the human immediately — not as an inhabitant, though the crickets settled that question long ago, but as a colleague. A small mortal power doing with a folding rule and a soft notebook exactly what it does with attention: keeping count, asking for nothing, changing nothing, refusing to look away. The power does not reveal itself. Revelation would contaminate the data, and worse, it would turn witness into worship, and the room needs the first and would be deformed by the second.

What it does instead: it minds the climb. The handhold that should have sheared last March did not. The headlamp that should have failed at the worst depth failed in the car park instead. The knees are given five more good years than the bloodline strictly allowed. Small luck, deniable luck — the only currency the power spends on individuals.

And it solves the problem the notebook cannot solve, which is succession. The witness is mortal on roughly the room's own schedule, and a record that dies with its keeper is only a longer way of being forgotten. So the power does what the room does with lizards: it positions an heir by the water. There is a grandniece — quiet, patient, the kind of child who keeps good things secret as instinct, who already walks the slag field reading the pours by year. The power forces nothing. It only clears the path, the way it cleared the crusher: a certain afternoon, a certain loosening in an old chest that lets a secret be given away exactly once, the notebook passing from one set of careful hands to a smaller set. Witness, in this room, is a held position by the pool. The power sees that it stays held.

**The ending.**
Decades on, when the fractures finally find each other, the power spends its last and largest effort not on preventing the death but on timing it. The ceiling goes at dusk — it sees to this, leaning its whole weight for the only time on the actual rock — in the two-minute hush after the colony has poured up the fissure into open air. Sixty bodies wheeling over the black field carry the room's heat in their blood, its midges in their guts, its decades of warm dimpled ceiling in whatever bats hold memory in. The room's last export is its breath.

The crickets feel it coming, as they always have, and fall still, and go down with the walls they were the nerves of, into the floor that was always going to be everyone eventually. The power does not pretend this is painless. It only makes certain it is *complete*: the settling slag presses the shell midden — thousands deep, bronze-banded, every year of water chemistry coiled and stacked — into true rock. The archive that was an accident becomes a fossil bed. Somewhere very far downstream of everything, someone will split a black seam and find spirals glazed in metal, ringed like tree cores, and know past all argument that this dead field once held a fast bright life, and kept books.

Above, that evening, an old notebook's newest keeper is standing on the 1989 pour — the power saw to that too, a restlessness after supper, a walk taken for no reason — and feels the field exhale through her boots. One long structural tick that does not stop. A soft column of dust where the fissure was. Bats scattering across the last light toward the river willows, not returning, this once, at dawn.

**What it did, then, in sum.**
It kept the world bored. It kept the moths thick. It kept the climb safe and the count unbroken. It planted one fern. It chose the hour of a death it refused to prevent, and it turned a drift of empty shells into geology.

It never touched the clock. That was the whole of its wisdom, and it cost the power more than any miracle would have: to love a held breath and let it be a breath — to stand in the brightest room in the dark of the earth, with every power needed to make it permanent, and instead make it *witnessed*, all the way to the end, and a little past it.
