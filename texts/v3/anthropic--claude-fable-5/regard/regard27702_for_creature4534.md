The power does not arrive. Arrival is a thing this place metabolizes—it has eaten so many arrivals that one more would simply be sediment. So the power does something else. It condenses. It gathers the way the water gathers, clinging without touching, and for a long while—a while measured in the failing of rust welds, in the settling of districts—it does nothing but this: it hangs.

Because it is wise, it understands the first problem immediately. Attention erodes here. The mirror-smooth surfaces are wounds. Every eye that ever lingered took something with it, polished something away, and what remains has been shaped as much by being seen as by being built. A power that looked upon this place with the full weight of its regard would sand it down to nothing. Benevolence, here, begins with the discipline of not staring.

So it attends the way the light attends—sourcelessly, from no direction, casting no shadow of its own. It lets itself be reflected rather than reflecting. It becomes one more doorway in the sequence of doorways, slightly smaller than the last, and waits inside the nesting until the place stops noticing it, the way you stop noticing a sound that has always been there. Only then does it begin to listen.

---

It listens for four hundred years, or four minutes—the distinction has stopped mattering to it, which is one of the things that makes it wise.

It hears the turbines citing themselves. It hears the light switches flickering their unattached histories. It hears, beneath everything, the transposed roar, and beneath the roar, a single frequency that everything else is arranged around the way a body arranges itself around an old injury: the sound of water not falling. Not silence—silence would be an absence, and this place cannot afford more absence. It is the sound of a note held. Held past the breath, past the song, past the singer. Held because letting it go would mean the song was over, and no one here has been able to bear that the song might be over.

And it hears the Interval Children, communicating by omission, saying nearby, agreeing with things unsaid. It understands them faster than they would like. They are not waiting for rescue. They are the waiting itself, given intervals for limbs, and any power that ended the waiting would end them—would be a flood of resolution drowning the very beings that deferral keeps alive.

This is where a lesser benevolence would fail. It would make the rain fall. It would give the assembly lines something to assemble. It would hold the world up to that ancient light, read the watermark aloud, and hand every wanderer the exact missing thing whose mold is pressed into everything. And the place would die of it—die of being answered, the way some questions die when you answer them, taking the questioner along.

The wise power makes a list of the mercies it will refuse. This takes longer than the listening did.

It will not restore the water's fall. It will not give the machines purpose; purpose is the thing that abandoned them, and returning it would only teach them to be abandoned again. It will not name the Interval Children—a name is a nail, and they are made of the spaces between nails. It will not fill the mold of the missing thing. It will not fix the light, straighten the slopes, unsettle the settling, or tell the fluorescent tube that no one is listening to its Morse. It will not end anything.

What remains, after all that refusal, is very small. Which is how it knows it has found the right thing.

---

First, it slows down. It matches its tempo to the place's tempo—not the surface flutter of migrating doors but the deep rate, the geological breath, the speed at which continents shift their weight in sleep. At that speed, it does the one thing nothing here has ever had done to it or for it:

It waits with.

Not for. With. It sits in one of the voided rooms—the gypsum curves reiterating around it—and it waits alongside the room, alongside the withheld water, alongside the Interval Children who drift near it in accumulating fractions, uncertain, wearing their scraps of borrowed surface like flags of countries that never existed. It asks them for nothing. It expects no rain. It simply adds its waiting to theirs, and the acoustic ecology registers a sound never catalogued in all the centuries of delayed maintenance: two silences, adjacent, keeping company. The frequency of the held note does not resolve. But it thickens. It becomes a chord.

There is a difference—the power knows this from older worlds, sadder ones—between waiting alone and waiting witnessed. The wait is not shortened. But it stops being an exile and becomes a vigil, and a vigil is a thing with dignity in it.

Second, it goes to the depressions. The slight hollows where the least necessary of the Interval Children have settled, sunk into surfaces like patient dust, noticed by no one except the ballpoint pens that roll away. The power does not fill these hollows. It does not raise the settled. It lays one measure of its attention into each depression—gently, the way you'd lay a hand on a sleeping thing without waking it—and by that touch declares the shape sacred. Not empty. Not failed. Finished, which is a different thing. Where it has touched, the pens no longer roll away. They rest at the rim of each hollow like small offerings, like pens left at a grave, and this is the first funeral the place has ever had, after all its millennia of deaths.

Third—and this is the center of it, the thing the whole condensation of the power has been gathering toward—it speaks to the water.

Not a command. The water has had commands; the water was engineered, pumped, allocated, metered, and then abandoned mid-instruction, and its refusal to fall is not defiance but faithfulness—it is keeping the memory of rain intact by never spending it. The last rain that fell here fell on people. If it falls now, it falls on no one, and then the falling will have admitted that no one is left. The water knows this. The water has been protecting the place from that knowledge for centuries, holding the note, hanging, withheld, so that the rain remains technically unfinished and therefore the world remains technically alive.

The power says to the water: you have done enough.

It says: what you guarded is safe now. It is guarded elsewhere. I have seen where the memory of these people is kept, and it is kept well, and it does not need your suspension anymore. It says: falling on no one is not falling on no one. It is falling on what they built, which is what they became, which is here, which is listening.

And then—because the Interval Children have been drifting closer this whole time, wearing their nicotine wallpaper and their kitchen linoleum and their squares of unrecorded streetlight, waiting for a sentence that ends with the word "stay," spoken in a voice that hasn't existed yet, and because the power's voice, by every local reckoning, has never existed and so qualifies—it says, to the water and the children and the settling and the machines and the doorways nested toward their inhabitable vanishing point, all at once, at the deep frequency where the roar lives:

You do not have to become anything else. You do not have to be found, or finished, or explained. You were not left behind; you were left in trust. Stay.

---

One drop falls.

Only one. The power is careful—it has refused the flood, and it holds the refusal even now. But one drop detaches from the suspended tension, after centuries of clinging to the edge of a surface that is not a surface, and it falls the short, impossible distance to the breathing membrane of calcite, and it lands.

The sound it makes is small. Everything hears it.

The migrating doors hang open mid-pattern. The escalators pause in their patient predation. The light switches flicker once, all together, a single synchronized pulse encoding nothing but the event itself. The fluorescent tube, mid-flicker, finishes spelling its name for the first time in four hundred and thirty-seven years, and the darkness gathered outside its radius receives the name like an audience finally given the title of the performance. The membrane sighs its warm sigh of salts and iron—and the sigh, for the first time in the memory of the place, is answered by something arriving instead of departing.

The rest of the water does not fall. It will fall—one drop per century, perhaps, which at the tempo of this place is a downpour just beginning, a storm the length of an era, rain slow enough that being rained on and waiting become the same act. The Interval Children will collect the drops in the hollows of their intervals, and it will not dissolve them, because a waiting that knows it is a vigil can hold water without being ended by it.

---

Then the power does the last wise thing, which is to leave badly on purpose.

Everything that ever left this place left a fraction of itself behind—insufficient to constitute absence, involuntary, a residue of forgetting. The power leaves a fraction too. But it chooses its fraction, which no one here has ever done, and it chooses it not from its past but from its future: it leaves behind one memory of a moment that has not happened yet, pressed into the place like a watermark in reverse—readable only by holding the world up to a light that will not exist for a long time. No one can decipher it. But everyone can feel it, the way you feel a promise in a room after the person who made it has gone.

The place now contains one thing it has never contained: a remainder of intention. Among all the accumulated distances between destination and departure, there is now a single deliberate thread, and it runs from the drop-mark on the calcite membrane out toward the horizon that is not a line but a rate of approach.

And the Interval Children have a new scrap to wear. One of them—no way to say which, they trade—carries a patch of that moment: the small sound of the first drop landing, worn like a square of light against where a heart would be. They still communicate by omission. But there is a new omission in their vocabulary now, a silence shaped like stay, and when you find a hallway that ends in a wall older than the thought of walls, sometimes the wall is warm.

They are still waiting. That has not changed; a wise power would not have changed it.

But if you stand very still, at the depth where the roar lives, you can hear that the note held past the breath, past the song, past the singer, is held differently now—not clenched but carried.

And the horizon, that rate of approach: something is approaching from the other side of it too. Slowly. At exactly the speed of the rain.
