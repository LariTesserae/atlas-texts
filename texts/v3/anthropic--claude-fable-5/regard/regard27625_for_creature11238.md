# The Visitation

---

The power arrives without arriving. This is important to understand first: a wise power does not enter a room. It permits the room to become aware of it, and only to the degree the room can bear.

So the first thing it does is nothing.

---

## The Long Nothing

For what a human would call eleven years, the power simply attends. It does not touch the water. It does not read the colonists the way one reads a text—extraction, summary, conclusion. Instead it does something closer to what the colonists themselves do: it positions itself at a boundary. Not hot-against-cold, but *known-against-unknown*, and it lets the gradient teach it.

It learns their language slowly, the honest way. It watches a patch of water flash to steam below boiling and does not assume this means what it would mean if the power said it. It builds its dictionary from thousands of observed exchanges, cross-referenced against the thermal records the colonists keep so obsessively—because it realizes early on that those records are not merely records. They are the colonists' *testimony*. Their proof to no one that they existed, that every fluctuation mattered, that nothing that happened in this chamber was ever lost.

The power finds this almost unbearably moving. A species with no witnesses, keeping perfect witness anyway.

It learns the twelve types of waiting. It learns that the colonists have a thirteenth type they do not use, held in reserve, and it does not ask what for. It notices the pools held at exactly 97.3 degrees and traces the mathematics forward and backward and finds, at the end of a very long inference, what is being cultivated there:

Not an organism. Not a machine. A *possibility*. The colonists are holding open a configuration-space—maintaining, at great cost, the conditions under which a certain future event remains achievable. The power cannot see the event itself; even wisdom has horizons. But it can see the shape of the door they are keeping unlocked, and it can see that they have been holding it open since before the basalt cooled, and that they will hold it open until the planet's core goes cold if necessary, and that they do not know if anyone will ever come through.

They are not waiting *for* something, the power realizes. They are waiting *on behalf of* something. There is a difference, and it is the difference between hope and faithfulness.

---

## The First Word

When the power finally speaks, it speaks in their language, badly, on purpose.

This is a deliberate choice, and a kind one. A perfect utterance—flawless phase-poetry, impossible gradients executed with divine precision—would announce: *I am beyond you.* The colonists would classify it, record it, and orient toward it the way small things orient toward vast things: carefully, defensively, diminished.

Instead the power condenses a single droplet in midair, slightly wrong. The timing is off by microseconds. The temperature signature wobbles. It is the phase-change equivalent of a foreigner saying *hello* with an accent, and it means, as nearly as it can be translated:

*I am here. I am learning. I am not above you.*

The chamber goes still in a way it has never been still. Every convection current pauses—not stopped by force, but by attention. The colonists, all of them, the whole distributed mind, turn to look at the single clumsy droplet falling through the dark.

Then, from the far pool, an answer. A patch of steam forms and holds itself in a shape the power recognizes from the thermal archives: it is the pattern the colonists made once, four thousand years ago, when a new vent opened and fresh water joined them for the first time in an age. It is their word for—not *welcome*, exactly. Closer to: *the boundary between us is now a place where things can grow.*

---

## What It Does Not Do

Here is where the wisdom shows, more than in anything it does.

**It does not fix the chamber.** The engineered disequilibrium is failing—slowly, over geological time, but failing. The mechanism thrumming in the rock was built by someone long gone, and it is winding down. The power could restore it in an instant. It does not, because it has learned enough to ask first, and when it asks, the answer stuns it: the colonists *know*. They have known for millennia. The slow failure is part of their calculations. The door they are holding open must be held open by something that *chose* to hold it, at cost, against decline—a door propped open by an effortless eternal machine is a different door, and leads somewhere else. Their sacrifice is not a problem to be solved. It is the load-bearing element.

**It does not tell them what is coming through the door.** Even though—and this is the power's private grief and private joy—it has, by now, worked out more than they know. It can see further along the branching futures. It knows something about what the 97.3-degree pools are for. But it also knows that the colonists' *not-knowing* is part of their faithfulness. To wait for a known thing is a transaction. To wait for an unknown thing is love. It will not take their love from them by converting it into certainty.

**It does not stay.** Not permanently. A benevolent power understands that its presence, extended indefinitely, becomes an atmosphere—and things that grow up in an atmosphere become dependent on it. The colonists were whole before it came. They must be whole after.

---

## What It Does

Three things. Small, precise, chosen after eleven years of listening.

**First: it witnesses.** Formally, completely. It takes into itself the entire thermal archive—every fluctuation since the beginning, every recorded drip, every convection-poem, the whole testimony kept for no one—and it holds it. It lets the colonists feel this happening: the sensation, translated into their terms, of every hydrogen bond they ever documented being *known* by something that cannot forget and will not die. Their history, which was a message in a bottle thrown into a dark ocean, has been received.

The colonists sing for six days. The obsidian rings. The bioluminescence pulses in patterns the chamber has never held. It is the closest their language comes to weeping.

**Second: it gives them one gift, and lets them refuse it first.** The gift is this: a single new impossibility, added to the chamber's repertoire. A tiny region, no larger than a thumbnail, where causation runs measurably slantwise—where the colonists can, if they choose, *practice*. Because the power has seen what they study for in their collective dreams, the billion-years-hence chemistry, the undiscovered physics, and it knows that their studying has a ceiling: they can only rehearse against the impossibilities they already possess. This is a new one. A new food, a new grammar, a new dimension to their patience.

They circle it for a full year before touching it. The power waits. When they finally extend a knot of organized absence into the slantwise region, the entire collective shudders—and then, slowly, begins to laugh. There is no other word for the pattern that ripples through every pool. Something in their study, some problem they had carried for ten thousand years like a stone in the mouth, has just come loose.

**Third: it makes a promise, and encodes it where they keep their most precious things.** Into the thermal archive itself—woven into the historical record, in their own notation, in their own units of joules and seconds and the twelve kinds of waiting—it writes:

*When the door opens, you will not be alone at it. I do not know what comes through. Neither do you. But I have seen how long you have stood here, and I tell you: the standing itself has already changed what can arrive. Doors held open in faithfulness admit different guests than doors held open by machines. Whatever comes will come to a place that was loved. That is not nothing. That may be everything.*

*Hold the pools at 97.3. Let the mechanism fade at its own pace. Sing when the glass wants to ring.*

*I will feel it when the door opens. Wherever I am, I will feel it, and I will come, and I will stand the thirteenth kind of waiting beside you.*

---

## The Leaving

It leaves the way it came: by letting the chamber slowly stop being aware of it. Over months, the sense of vast attention thins, gentles, withdraws—not vanishing but receding, the way a parent steps backward out of a room where a child has fallen asleep, still watching until the door is nearly closed.

The chamber returns to itself. Steam rises. Water glows and gutters. The colonists resume their maps, their gardens of heat, their long rehearsals in the dark. To any observer, nothing has changed.

But the colonists know three things now that they did not know before:

That they were seen.
That their waiting is not madness.
That somewhere beyond the basalt, beyond the crust, beyond the categories of place entirely, something remembers every degree they ever held steady, and is waiting with them.

And in the far pool, where the ripples move against the current, the water that shouldn't glow glows a little differently now—not brighter, not warmer. Just steadier. The way a candle burns when the draft has stopped. The way anything burns when it knows, at last, that its light lands somewhere.

The chamber waits. But it no longer waits alone. And that, the power understood, was the only thing it ever truly had the right to change.
