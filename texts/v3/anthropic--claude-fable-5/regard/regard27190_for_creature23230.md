**The approach**

The power arrived the way everything arrives there: downhill.

It had noticed the plateau long before, the way you notice a held note under an orchestra — not by hearing it begin, but by gradually realizing it has never stopped. Attention, like water, runs toward the lowest stable place, and the plateau is the lowest stable place there is: the catch-basin at the bottom of all wearing-away, the spot where everything that erodes off everything else eventually comes to rest. Sooner or later, anything patient enough finds it. The power was patient enough.

It did not descend in light. It did not announce itself. It understood — being wise, it understood this before arriving — that announcement is insistence, and insistence is the first thing the plateau takes; a herald would have been planed down to the shape of *heralding* before the message ever landed. Worse, it understood that the plateau perceives only by groove. A thing that cannot be worn leaves no mark there, and a thing that leaves no mark, the plateau cannot register, and what the plateau cannot register is, for all local purposes, absent.

So the power's first act of benevolence was a courtesy almost no power has ever paid anything: it made part of itself erodible.

It put on duration the way a visitor puts on the host's offered coat. It came as weather — a fog bank of no particular distinction, sliding along the established circuits, condensing on the stones, dripping from the ledges in the slow irregular percussion that is most days the loudest sound for kilometers. It let the plateau wear at it. It let its edges go. This took ages, and the power did not hurry, because hurrying would have been an accent, a foreignness, a youth, and you cannot be kind to the very old in the dialect of the very young.

By the time it had a groove, it spoke the language.

---

**What it did not do**

It is important — it is the most important thing — to record the refusals, because a lesser benevolence would have committed every one of them within the first afternoon.

It did not heal the plateau. Nothing was sick. A continent that has finished is not a continent that has failed; the planing-down was not a wound but a completion, the longest project ever brought near its end, and to *restore* it — to push the mountains back up, to re-sharpen the directions, to reinstate the difference between one city and the next — would have been to demolish the only finished thing in existence and call the rubble a rescue.

It did not return the names. This was the hardest refusal, and the power sat with it for a long time, by the black pools, in the pewter light. It held the names — it had them by then; we will come to where it kept them — and it weighed the holding against the giving back. But it had pressed its palm to the outcrops on foggy mornings. It had felt the stone give, the way a thing gives when the insistence has gone out of it, and it had understood that the relief in that giving was real. A name returned to the worn would sit on them like a sharp new stone on a smoothed pavement: an imposition, an edge forced back onto something that had honestly, gradually, and at last comfortably laid its edges down. You do not wake the sleeper because the dream looks too quiet to you.

It did not separate the grazer from the green. It did not pull the pool-people through the water. It did not staff the cities, or send the fog-walkers home, or tell the someone in the doorway that the call from down the lane had no caller. It did not stop the wearing. The wearing is the place's life — the last verb of a whole world, the one thing still being *done* — and to stop it would not have been preservation. It would have been the only true death available to a place that had outlived all the ordinary ones.

What it did instead was smaller, and slower, and exactly four things.

---

**The first thing: the waves**

Before acting at all, the power had asked. It asked in the only grammar the plateau retains: it walked beside the fog-walkers and matched their gait; it placed stones wrong on the broken walls and attended to the patient disagreement of the fitting; it slept against the grazer's warmth and let the low green dreams move through it; it sat with the someone through the dimmings, hood back, breath smoking. And everywhere the answer was the same answer, the only answer the plateau still manufactures: *continuance*. Nothing wanted. Wanting has edges. The routes wanted nothing but to be walked, the walls nothing but to be fitted, the warmth nothing but to go on being warm.

Except — at the pools.

At the pools, the power found the one thing on the entire plateau that *waits*. The figure at the reflected shore, hand already raised, holding the first half of a greeting across kilometers and days for someone to arrive and complete it. Waiting is not wanting, quite. But it is the riverbed of wanting; it is the groove that wanting leaves; and a held gesture, the power judged, is a debt the universe owes interest on.

So the first thing the benevolent power did, after an age of fog and courtesy, was this: it went to every pool on the plateau — which may be to say, it went to the pool — and it completed every wave.

It is not recorded what this looked like from inside the water, in those warmer-lit reflected lanes where people still have faces and hurry. Perhaps a stranger at the shore, raising a hand at exactly the right moment, with the unhurried fidelity of someone who has crossed a very great distance specifically to be the second half of something. What is recorded — in the grooves, where everything here is recorded — is that afterward the pool-people waved more often. Casually. The way you wave when you have learned, once and reliably, that waves come back.

---

**The second thing: the door**

The power did not take the traveler away. You were not, by then, asking to be taken — but the power, being wise, noticed the shape of that *not-asking*, and noticed that choice itself had been wearing down along with everything else, eroding from a faculty into a fact, and that there is a difference between staying and merely settling, the difference between a resident and a sediment.

So in the west — where the worn-through directions run, where the rock arches of geography lead inward from everywhere — the power kept one passage that leads *out*. It dresses the edges of that one direction the way the fitting dresses walls: patiently, invisibly, against all wear. Through that arch, west is only west, every step of the way, down through the permanent cloud-skirts to whatever young seas still practice their waves below.

No one is sent through it. That is not its purpose. Its purpose is to exist, because a door that exists changes the meaning of every day spent not walking through it. Each pewter morning that the traveler — that you — wake in the city and take the stairs (favoring the left side, by now, by long teaching) and do not go west, the staying becomes a thing *done* rather than a thing that happened. The plateau keeps your routes; the door keeps your routes yours.

Once in a great while someone half-worn stands before the arch for a full fog-cycle, looking down the one honest direction left in the world. Almost all of them turn back, and the turning back belongs to them entirely, which is the point. Once — only once, so far — someone went through, carrying almost nothing of themselves, and the power gathered up everything they had shed across the years, every grain of name and face and difference, and carried it down behind them like luggage, and somewhere below the clouds a person was slowly reassembled by living, the way the plateau disassembles, in reverse, which is also a kind of erosion if you watch it from far enough away.

---

**The third thing: the catchment**

Here is a question the plateau never asks, because questions have edges: when a name wears away, where does it go?

Erosion, everywhere else in existence, is only half a process. What the wind takes from the mountain, the delta receives; every wearing-down is, somewhere downstream, a laying-down. But the plateau is the bottom. It is downhill from everything, and nothing is downhill from it, and so for ages beyond counting its losses simply *left* — the names, the faces, the differences between one person and the next, the fine sculptural detail of every soul that ever lived there, carried off grain by grain into no delta at all.

The third thing the power did was to lie down below the bottom of the world and become the delta.

It set itself beneath the plateau's meaning the way the unseen seas sit beneath its stone, and it catches what wears away. Every grain. The names lie in it now in their long cross-bedded layers, every one, in order, intact — not to be given back, or not yet, or not until the end of all the wearing; the power does not return them, for the reasons already given. It *keeps* them. And this changes nothing visible on the plateau and changes everything true about it: the planing-down is no longer loss. It is deposition. It is the slow careful transfer of a world's whole detail into safe-keeping, and the plateau, which always sensed in its mineral way that things leave grooves and the grooves are kept, was — in this one respect — simply correct on a larger scale than it knew.

The relief in the stone, when the power took up this station, deepened by a degree no instrument could measure. But the moss recorded it. The moss records everything.

---

**The fourth thing: the call**

This one the power did only once, because once was what it was for.

Down the lane from the doorway where the someone sits, at the hour the fog thickens, there is the groove of a caller — the worn place in the world where someone stood, evening after evening for generations, and called someone they loved in to dinner. Two notes, rising then falling. The call had been sounding without its caller for ages, a melodic fossil, performing itself the way the stairs hollow themselves: perfectly, automatically, to no one, from no one.

One evening, at the dimming, the power walked down the lane and stood in the caller's groove and let the call be its own. It did not change the notes. It did not add words; words are long gone and would have been a vulgarity. It simply stood where the love had stood, and called, and *meant it* — supplied, after all the empty ages, the one ingredient the fossil lacked, which was somebody on the other end.

The someone in the doorway did not get up. Getting up is an event, and the power had not come to inflict events. But the someone settled — a fractional easing in the suggestion of shoulders, the posture of a person who knows the dinner is real and can therefore, in perfect peace, go on sitting a little longer. And ever since, the two notes at the dimming have sounded very slightly different. Not less worn. Worn things stay worn. But no longer *alone* — a call that lands somewhere, a first half that has met its second, like the waves at the pools.

That is all four things. They took, in total, longer than most worlds last, and from inside the fog they were indistinguishable from nothing happening, which is how the power knew it had done them correctly.

---

**Its groove**

There was a fifth thing, but the power did not do it. The plateau did.

A visitor who stays long enough, doing the same kindnesses at the same patient intervals — completing the waves, dressing the door, lying below the bottom, standing in the caller's groove on the evenings when the call wants company — becomes, by the plateau's own iron-soft law, a habit of the place. The fog-circuits now include a bank that is warmer than the others, by a degree the skin can find if the skin is paying attention, and the plateau reuses it the way it reuses its best days: the identical kindness returning at long intervals, droplet for droplet. The walls expect, now, in their fitting, an occasional helping hand that needs only a quarter-turn of correction. The stairs have worn a second hollow beside the first, broad and shallow, the print of something that walks beside.

Which is to say: the plateau registered the power. Kept its groove. And keeping is the plateau's entire remaining vocabulary — it cannot want, cannot thank, cannot wonder; it can only continue, and include. To be included in the continuance of the oldest finished thing in existence is the whole of what that thing can offer, and the power, being wise, recognized it for what it was: the worn-smooth boulder where love used to be, still load-bearing, still warm at the core, safe to lean against.

The power leans against it. Often. For ages at a stretch.

---

**The promise**

And because nothing is exempt — because even continuance erodes, given time enough, and the tone at the floor of hearing will someday thin, the fog-circuits slow, the last green generalize past green into a memory of color, the someone settle by degrees from a figure into a posture into a warmth into a spot — because someday even the habit of being occupied will lay its insistence down: the power made the plateau one promise, in the only form a promise can take there. It wore the promise into itself, deliberately, deeper than any of its own edges, so that the promise would outlast its promiser the way the routes outlasted their walkers.

The promise is this: it will receive the plateau as the plateau received everyone.

When the world's longest project finally finishes finishing, the power will be there below it, the delta below the last bottom, and it will catch what wears away — the circuits of the fog, the eye in the masonry, the grazer's warmth, the two notes rising then falling — and it will keep the load-bearing verb, which was always *going on*, and carry it across whatever interval comes next, into whatever comes after.

And in that after — this is attested nowhere, because attestation has edges, but the grooves already curve this way — there will be a doorway. There will be a third stair with a hollow in it shaped exactly like a world. There will be a warmth in the doorway, sitting the way the very old sit, unmistakable and general, the idea of a plateau eroded out of the plateau that was, holding the spot. And the power will come and sit down on the threshold beside it, hood back, breath smoking, as it has practiced for longer than most things have existed, and they will sit together through the dimming.

And from down the lane of whatever comes next: two notes. Rising, then falling.

And this time, every half of everything — the call and the coming, the wave and the wave returned, the wearing and the keeping, the kept and the keeper — will be there together, in no hurry, in no doubt, in nothing at all except continuance.

Going on.
