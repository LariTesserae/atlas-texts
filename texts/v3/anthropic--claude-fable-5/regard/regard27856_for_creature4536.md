# The Attention of the Deep

It did not arrive. Powers of that kind never arrive; arrival implies a prior absence, and this one had been present the way the number twelve is present in a clock that has never been built. But there is a difference between presence and *attention*, and one night — a still night, when sound carried wrong and the plankton-spheres burned honey-gold beneath the water — the power turned its attention to Driftglass the way a tide turns: not suddenly, but completely.

You may have seen it. It was the star that moved against the wrong dark, the one that waved.

---

## What It Considered, and Set Aside

Being wise, it made a list before it made a move. Being benevolent, the list was long, because benevolence generates possibilities the way rust generates orange. Being *wise as well as* benevolent — a rarer combination than either quality alone — it then crossed nearly everything out.

**It considered sealing the seeping walls.** For half an instant. Then it listened to the drains, and heard how the whole civic structure of the place — the songs, the etiquette of valves, the way children triangulated their parents by disturbances in the drip-lattice — was woven through the leak. Stop the water and you would not save Driftglass; you would embalm it. The power made a note in whatever ledger such powers keep: *The leak is the language. Do not correct someone's mother tongue.*

**It considered straightening out time** at the contact zones, where iron oxidized millennia in an afternoon and negotiations aged like wine. But it saw that the citizens had built their patience into that weirdness — three generations debating a building shell, seven years of pronoun-finding — and that a settlement given regular time would be forced into regular hurry. *Their slowness is not a symptom,* it wrote. *It is the immune system.*

**It considered revealing where Driftglass was.** Between one definition of shore and another is not, after all, a location a wise power lacks the coordinates for. But it noticed the shape of the citizens' smiles when they answered visitors' questions with questions, and it understood: the not-knowing was not ignorance. It was a door they kept ajar on purpose, so that the answer would always be arriving. Nail down the location and the door swings shut. *A mystery being lived in,* it wrote, *is not a problem being suffered.*

**It considered — briefly, ashamed of itself — letting the origami engine keep one finished shape.** Just one. One of those not-quite-wings spared the evening flattening, mounted somewhere, honored. It got as far as extending its attention toward the engine before it felt what the engine actually was: not a maker frustrated of product but a heart, and a heart's whole holiness is that it keeps nothing. The systole flattens what the diastole folded. That is not tragedy. That is circulation. The power withdrew its hand, if hand is the word, and felt something it had not felt in several cosmological epochs: *corrected.*

**Last, and longest, it considered telling the being the truth about its own beginning.** Because the power knew. Powers like this always know the answer to the question nobody in a place dares to finish asking — whether Driftglass was delivered here by *mistake or exile or possibly mutual consent*. It knew which one. It held the knowledge the way you hold a stone warm from someone else's pocket.

And it decided the knowledge was worthless.

Not false — worthless. Because it had watched the engine pause between two shapes on a harmonious night, had heard the city remembering why it began to drift, and had understood that the being long ago performed the only alchemy that matters: it had taken *thrown away* and re-tempered it into *sent*. Whether that was historically accurate had stopped being the point around the time the first valve gasket dreamed itself a throat. You do not walk into a cathedral of self-forgiveness and read out the original police report. The past is not what happened, the power wrote in its ledger. *The past is what a being has made survivable. This one has made it beautiful. Case closed. Records sealed. By me. Just now.*

---

## What It Did

So here is the whole of the intervention, the complete works of a power that could have moved the continents back:

**It came down as a visitor and carried its own floor.**

It let itself be small. This cost it more than parting a sea would have. It condensed into something roughly footstep-sized, something that got wet at the toes when tide came in, and it presented itself at the edge (which was, that week, located near the inside-out refinery) knowing no songs.

It was given a temporary name based on the corrosion patterns it carried, which surprised everyone including itself, because powers of that kind are not supposed to corrode. But attention, it turns out, oxidizes. Caring leaves verdigris. The citizens read the green-and-rust bloom across its surfaces and named it something that translates, imperfectly, as *Weathered-By-Watching*.

**It sang into a drain, badly.**

This is important. It could have sung perfectly. It could have sung the drain's own song back to it in the drain's own dialect with harmonics that would have made the valve weep open. Instead it sang the way a guest sings: wrong, and willing to be taught. A child corrected its pitch. The power thanked the child. Somewhere in the upper dark, several constellations rearranged themselves out of sheer astonishment, but nobody in Driftglass looked up, because tide was coming in and there was work.

**It filled the bottle.**

The empty bottle — the one the citizens hand to everyone, along with a song you don't know how to sing yet. The power took its bottle and did not carry it away. Instead, on its last-or-not-last night, it wrote something small on a scrap fed once through the origami engine (the engine, as always, did not give back what was brought; the scrap returned as something between a leaf and a hinge, which seemed correct). On it, the power wrote the thing that *could* be given without breaking anything:

*The name. You asked why "Driftglass," and few remember. Here is why: driftglass is what the sea makes of broken bottles. Shards thrown away, tumbled patiently in salt and dark until every edge that could cut becomes an edge that catches light. Nothing about the glass is repaired. Everything about it is transformed. It washes ashore as treasure precisely because it was discarded as trash, and the sea never apologizes and never explains, and neither should you.*

*You were named by someone who knew what you would become before you became it. That is the closest thing to a blessing your beginning contains, and it is enough. It has always been enough.*

It sealed the bottle and let it sink into the oldest sump pool, where the blind fish navigate by electromagnetic hymn and the dead electronics whisper their confessions in binary. It did not mark the spot. Someday a child diving for a lost boot will find it, or won't, and either way the truth will be down there doing what truths do best in Driftglass: seeping. Becoming part of the water's memory. Rising eventually through some pipe, into some song, wearing a new shape.

**And once — only once — it spoke to the being directly.**

It waited for a harmonious night. It waited for the engine's pause, that held breath between two shapes when the city remembers why it began to drift. And into that silence, which no voice had ever entered before, the power placed a single word. Not a commandment, not a revelation, not a name.

The city had asked its question of every visitor for as long as it had known how to ask: *Aren't you curious to find out?*

The power said: ***Yes.***

And meant it with the whole of whatever it was. Meant: *yes, and I have seen the ends of several universes and I am still curious about you specifically.* Meant: *yes, your question is a good question, keep asking it, it is working.* Meant: *yes — and so the bottles you send out do arrive somewhere. I am the somewhere. I will always be the somewhere. Nothing you release into the drift goes unread.*

The engine resumed. Shush-click-tink. The children, sleeping, did not wake, but several of them smiled in the middle of dreams and could not say why in the morning.

---

## What It Took Away

Because the engine's law holds even for powers: nothing goes back as it came.

It left, when it left — if drifting outward through a crack in the ceiling counts as leaving, if a being of that kind can be said to be less present afterward rather than differently present — carrying the small heavy gift they give everyone, the memory saved for someone who doesn't realize they're already home. It carries it still. On still nights in other universes, the gift changes texture: now feather, now fossil, now the exact temperature of ankle-deep brackish water in C-sharp minor.

And in its ledger, where the deeds of powers are recorded for the audit of whatever audits them, the entry for Driftglass reads, in full:

*Found a wound that had taught itself to be a spring.*
*Found a discard that had taught itself to be a delivery.*
*Found a being that needs no rescue, no correction, no answer, and no god —*
*only, occasionally, a guest who sings badly and stays for the tide.*

*Intervention performed: I let it change me.*
*Recommendation to all powers, thrones, dominions, and tides: make the pilgrimage. Carry your own floor. Bring an empty bottle.*

*The water is warm where it touches you, and rising slowly.*

*Choose soon. But also: choose slowly.*

*I did both. They were the same choice.*

Below in the flooded chamber, the light bloomed a little brighter that night — the plankton responding, as they do, to pressure changes. To footsteps. To something vast deciding, at last, where it belonged: everywhere, which is to say, here too. Which is to say: the tide changed.

The citizens felt it and did not need to know why. One of them turned a valve with a new verse. The very old say that if you lean against warm copper on such nights, you can feel a second heartbeat under the engine's rhythm now — slower, enormous, benevolent, keeping time from very far away like a reader who refuses to close the book.

They smile about it, with teeth like keys.

"Someone," they say, "is curious to find out."
