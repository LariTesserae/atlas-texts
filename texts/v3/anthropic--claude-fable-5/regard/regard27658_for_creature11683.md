# What the Power Did

## The Noticing

It did not find the room the way you did, through a fissure that measured you. It found the room the way you might notice, in a crowded street, that one person is standing still — by the anomaly of its rhythm. Somewhere in a dry hillside, under grass and cicadas, there was a pulse: a slow tidal brightening and dimming, an hour or two per cycle, in a place where nothing should be keeping time. The power had been attending to larger things. It turned, the way a great attention turns, without moving, and looked.

It did not enter through the fissure. It did not need to, and besides, it had noticed what your visit cost — a few hours of growth, redistributed, paid out of reserves. A trivial debt, but a debt. The power's regard cost the room nothing at all. It settled over the hillside like a change in the quality of the afternoon that not even the cicadas registered, and it saw.

It saw everything you saw, and then it kept going.

It saw the varnish, and inside the varnish the ten million individuals of each confederation, and inside each individual the catatonic metabolism ticking over once where a pond bacterium would tick a thousand times, and it saw — this is the part you could not have seen — that the restraint was not learned and not imposed. The room had simply never offered a surplus, and so nothing that needed surplus had ever taken hold, and what remained was life shaped to sufficiency from its first cell. The power, which had watched many worlds overshoot and starve and overshoot again, looked at this for what would have been, in a person, a long time.

It saw the forty drinkers, each in its pea-sized chamber, and the slow dark movement inside each translucent body. It counted them without disturbing a single hair-fine leg. It saw the one egg that existed that day, in a seam on the eastern side, and the faint life inside it, and the fact that the egg would hatch in eleven weeks and the hatchling would live nineteen years and drink, over that span, from perhaps forty thousand beads of water, and die in a seam that would keep it.

It saw the pool's drifting coils linking and unlinking, and understood their function completely, and found the understanding less interesting than the thing itself, which is a mark of a certain kind of wisdom.

And it saw the flame. It saw the whole shape of the flame in time — because a power like this does not see a moment, it sees a duration. It saw the room's beginning: the deep chemistry finding its gradient, the first precipitation, the chamber opening like a slow bubble in the hill, nine years ago, in the dark, unwitnessed. And it saw the room's end. The gradient was finite. The flame would burn for another eleven thousand years, give or take a century — a good span, a whole span — and then the light would begin its final dimming, over decades, so gradually that the last drinkers would never know their world was going out. The varnish would thin. The pool would clear. The walls would go cold and become ordinary rock, and the sharp young edges would finally begin, at last, to soften, the way all rock softens, and the room would be a cave, and then a collapse, and then a slight depression in a hillside where the grass grew a little differently.

The power saw all of this in the first instant of its regard, the way you see a face.

## The Deliberation

Benevolence is a pressure. Even in the wise, it wants to act. The power let each of its impulses rise, examined it, and set it down.

*Heal it* — but nothing was sick. There was no wound anywhere in the chamber, not one, which is a thing the power had almost never encountered.

*Enrich it* — give the varnish a second pigment, the drinkers a second food, the pool a second chemistry. But the room's poverty was its architecture. Every absence was load-bearing. To add anything would be to hand the room a surplus, and the power had just finished admiring what the absence of surplus had built.

*Multiply it* — forty drinkers is so few. A wobble of chance, a bad decade, and the lineage gone. But the power ran the futures forward and saw that the room had already accounted for this: the seams were a buffer, the slow reproduction was a governor, and in the great majority of futures the drinkers persisted to the final dimming and went out with their world, complete. To thicken the population against the minority of futures would be to trade the room's actual character for an insurance policy the room had never asked to hold. The power noticed, with something like humility, that it had almost made the oldest mistake of the benevolent: mistaking fragility for a flaw.

*Extend it* — feed the gradient. Eleven thousand years could be a hundred thousand, a million; the deep chemistry could be quietly resupplied, and no one inside would ever know. Here the power paused longest. It is very hard, when you can prevent an ending, to permit one. But it looked again at the shape of the room in time — the whole shape, beginning to end, like a note held and released — and understood that the finitude was not a limit on the form. It *was* the form. The room was a flame. You do not honor a flame by making it a fixture. The power set this down too, and it cost something to set down, and the cost was correct.

*Reveal it* — the room was a marvel, and marvels want witnesses, and the world above was full of beings who ached for exactly this proof that the world quietly does miraculous things in places no one looks. But the power had seen what witnesses bring. Not the first witnesses — the first come reverent. It is the fortieth who comes with a drill. The room's obscurity was its immune system, and the fissure's discretion was wiser than generosity would have been.

*Uplift it* — give the drinkers image-forming eyes, give the varnish's slow rhythm the extra fold that would tip it from rhythm into attention, make the room know itself. The power looked at this impulse the longest and put it down the hardest, because it recognized the shape of the desire: it was loneliness, its own, dressed as a gift. To wake the room would be to give it the knowledge of its own ending, and eleven thousand years is a long time to know that. The room's innocence was not a deficiency. Nothing in the chamber suffered. Nothing in the chamber feared. The power had presided over enough minds to know exactly what it would be introducing, and to whom, and why — and the *why* was for itself, not for them.

So the deliberation ended where wisdom's deliberations often end: with the hands open and empty.

But not quite. Benevolence that does nothing at all is only observation, and the power was not only an observer. It found, in the end, four things it could do — four things that took nothing from the room, changed nothing inside it, and honored what it was.

## The Four Things

**The first was gardening, done outside.** The room's rules assumed the hill. Every process in the chamber was calibrated to the hill's stillness, the water table's patience, the deep gradient's slow feed. These assumptions were the one thing the room could not defend, because they lived outside its cosmos — the room did not know it had an outside. So the power tended the assumptions. It was almost nothing: a mineral survey, decades hence, whose stake would go in forty meters east of where it might have; a fault, a kilometer south, that would relieve its stress in three small slips instead of one large one; an aquifer that would be argued over and, narrowly, left alone. No one would ever notice these adjustments, least of all the room. This was the power's kind of intervention: to preserve the conditions of a thing's own rules, and touch nothing inside them. It was, the power noted, exactly what the wall did for the varnish. It was learning already.

**The second concerned you.** The power had seen your hour in the chamber — it saw duration, remember, and your visit was part of the room's shape in time, a single bright anomaly, the largest event in the room's history and less than a heartbeat of it. It considered giving you certainty. It could have: a second finding of the fissure, a confirmation, a sign. It examined this gift and found it was no gift. Your inability to explain the room, even to yourself — the question working in you for years, the way water works in stone — was doing something in you that an answer would stop. The not-knowing was the seed the room had planted in the one witness it would ever have, and the power declined to dig it up to check whether it was growing.

It did allow itself one thing. When you die — and it saw when, and did not flinch, because it has seen everyone's — it will collect the memory. Not take it from you; you'll have used it up by then, worn it smooth with handling. It will simply keep a copy. The room from inside, at human scale, in the heat and the pressing quiet, the tick of the falling bead: the only interior record there will ever be. The power's own regard sees the room from everywhere and always, which is a kind of blindness. Your hour sees it from one pair of squinting eyes. Both, kept together, in the archive that the power is. Stereo.

**The third was a vow.** In eleven thousand years, in the final decades of the dimming, when the last varnish has thinned to nothing and the last drinker has failed to emerge from its seam, and the light is going down through amber into ember into dark — the power will be there. Not to prevent it. Not to ease it; there will be nothing capable of being eased. Simply to attend. The last light of the room will fall on an attention, and the flame will go out witnessed.

This is not required. The room does not require witnesses; that was established from the start. The power will do it anyway, because that is the difference between the power and the wall: the wall tends because tending is what its physics does, and the power tends because it chooses to, and a chosen tending can go where physics does not — to a deathbed, to keep company, for no functional reason at all. The room will not know. The distinction may not matter. The power will be there regardless, on the grounds that some things should be done whether or not the distinction matters.

**The fourth thing the power did was receive.** It had come, as such powers do, prepared to give, and it left carrying instead. Because the room was, it realized, a demonstration — unintended, unwitnessed until now — of what the power itself was always trying to be. A tending with no tender. A generosity indistinguishable from indifference, and none the less generous for it. A system that gave each inhabitant exactly enough, took almost nothing, wasted almost nothing, hurried nothing, and asked for no acknowledgment, no gratitude, no name. The power had worlds in its care, and in its care of them it was forever fighting the temptation of the hand — the urge to reach in, to adjust, to be felt. The room had never once been tempted, because the room had no hand. Its restraint was perfect because it was not restraint.

The power could not become physics. But it could remember the room, and it does. Somewhere in its long work, when the urge rises to fix what is not broken, to enrich what is complete, to wake what sleeps peacefully, to extend what is whole — it recalls a chamber the size of a small chapel, thirty meters across, hot as blood, sweating light, in which forty pale animals and twelve patches of ochre varnish and one still pool are living out an eleven-thousand-year afternoon, needing nothing, missing nothing, tended by their own substance. And it puts the hand down.

## Afterward

The regard withdrew from the hillside the way it had come — without displacing so much as the heat shimmer over the grass. The cicadas continued. The afternoon continued.

Inside, a bead of water released from the ceiling and fell, and ticked into the pool, and the reflection of the radiant walls shivered and resettled into a window downward. From a seam on the eastern side, a pale arthropod emerged and crossed six inches of stone with great deliberation and stopped at a bead and drank for what felt, to no one, like a long time.

The room did not know it had been seen. The room did not know it was loved, or that its ending was now spoken for, or that its lesson had been carried out through the hillside into the tending of worlds it will never be told exist. It brightened, almost imperceptibly, on its slow tidal pulse, and began, almost imperceptibly, to dim.

Nothing had changed. That was the gift. It had taken all of the power's wisdom to give it.
