# What the Power Did

It noticed the plateau the way the plateau would have wanted to be noticed, if the plateau wanted anything: slowly, and from inside the weather.

Powers of that kind do not arrive. Arrival is an interruption — a before and an after, a wake in the fog — and this one was old enough to know it. So there was no descent, no light, no first footprint pressed into moss that had never held one. There was only, at some point that cannot be fixed, a new quality in the cloud deck. The clouds the plateau exhales went on being exhaled, and went on hiding it from the world below, and somewhere in their grey continuous body there was now also an attention. The plateau had spent ten million years making its own sky. The power simply took up residence in what was made, the way the worms live in the conduits: inside the breath itself.

And then it did what wise things do first, which is nothing, for a long time.

---

It had come carrying gifts. Benevolence always does; that is its occupational hazard. And so for the first century or so — an afternoon, by the local clock — it took its gifts out one at a time, turned each one over in the fog, and set each one down unused.

It could have warmed the place. But the cold here is not a deprivation; it is a treaty. The eight steady degrees are the terms on which the cushion plants hoard their heat and the vents earn their meaning and the peat keeps the dead composed with their hands folded. Warmth would not be a gift. It would be a renegotiation no one had asked for, imposed on ten million signatories at once.

It could have lifted the fog — let the plateau see itself whole for one morning, the green swells running to every horizon, the drainage etched in rust like circuitry, the breathing regions visible as a weather of the land. But the fog is not a veil over the place. The fog *is* the place, mid-cycle — the distillery's working fluid, the cover the grey hunter moves in, the reason every creature here listens instead of looks. To lift it would be to stop the loop to admire the loop. The power had seen that done elsewhere. It knew what it cost.

It could have spoken to the bog. This was the temptation it held longest. The phase-waves, the decades-long patterns, the slow tide of synchrony that the people call dreaming — the power was perhaps the only thing in existence large enough and patient enough to perceive one of those patterns entire, and it could feel, the way one feels a word on the tip of the tongue, that the pattern was at minimum the *shape* of something. It could have answered. It could have introduced, into the breathing, one deliberate pulse — a syllable in the only language the plateau might speak — and waited the decades for a reply.

It set this gift down too, and this was the wisest hour of its long stay. Because a dream answered becomes a conversation, and a conversation makes demands — wakefulness, response, the terrible accelerating economy of exchange. The bog has been turning over in its sleep for ten million years, and whatever is forming in that sleep is forming at the only speed at which it can form truly. To wake it would not be to meet it. It would be to replace it with something quicker and smaller — to interrupt the one thought in all the world that had never yet been interrupted.

It could have raised the people up. Taught them fire, metal, writing, the future tense. It looked at them a long time — the also-breathing, asleep in their dwarf forests with their heads below the canopy of the world's smallest old growth, counting in breaths, speaking through their palms, knowing the location of every ancestor in the patient ground — and it understood that they were not waiting to be raised. They had arrived. They were what teaching labors toward and almost never reaches: a people whose one law was self-evident to them, who could not be made wiser, only louder. Every curriculum it could offer them began with an interruption.

**Every gift it carried, it found, was an interruption wearing kindness as a coat.** The plateau's first law and the power's deepest ethic turned out to be the same sentence. It had crossed who knows what distances to learn what the cushion-dwellers teach their children before words: that here, love and restraint are not in tension. They are synonyms.

So it gave nothing. It put all its gifts away.

But it did not leave. Because in its long looking it had found the one thing the plateau could not do for itself.

---

The plateau was never started, so it cannot stop. This is true, and the power confirmed it: there is no failure in the system, no senescence, no internal road to silence. The engine is sound.

But it can be *stopped.* It has exactly one vulnerability, and that vulnerability lives below the clouds.

The power, which could see in the long forward direction the way the rest of us see downhill, looked that way once and saw what was coming, in some century or another: the surveys. The instruments. The kind of eye to which a thousand miles of breathing peat is a fuel deposit, and the iron-rich crusts are ore, and the closed loop of fog and forest is an inefficiency awaiting drainage. It saw, faint and far, the roads — and it understood that the plateau, which can absorb any sound and any death and any weather, cannot absorb a single ditch dug by something that does not close what it opens.

So here is what the power did. It is the whole of what it did, and it did none of it on the plateau.

It went to work on the world's attention.

The escarpments already sour compasses — the lichens' magnetic crusts saw to that ages ago — so the power only deepened what the place itself had begun. It saw to it that maps of the region quietly disagree with one another, and always have, and that no one finds this remarkable. It saw to it that the cloud deck, which the plateau makes anyway, never once fails on the day an instrument passes overhead — not by adding clouds, only by never permitting the weather an exception. Expeditions that aim themselves at the tableland find the approaches tedious, the readings anomalous, the funding suddenly contested; they turn back not in fear or wonder but in *boredom*, which is the most secure of all the seals. Reports get written, and every one of them concludes with the same word, and the power tends that word the way the worms tend the conduits, grazing away every accretion of curiosity that might narrow it: *nothing*. Fog, bog, rock. Nothing there.

It is the most protective word in any language, and keeping it current is a maintenance job, unglamorous and perpetual. The power took the job. It became the conduit worm of the plateau's obscurity — blind to glory, ribbed to fit its passage, keeping one airway clear so the breath can keep coming. Invisible, like all good maintenance, and the machine's smooth running the only evidence it exists.

---

And it allowed itself a single indulgence, which costs the plateau nothing.

It witnesses. It is the one being in all the world positioned to see a phase-pattern whole — to watch a thought of the sleeping bog from first drift to final dissolution across thirty years, the way you or I might watch an expression cross a face. The people witness the wave from inside it, traveling at its crest with the stilt-birds; the power witnesses the dream from above, from within the clouds the dreamer makes. Between them, the plateau is now seen at every scale at which it happens. Nothing about it goes unaccompanied anymore. That is not a gift, exactly. It is closer to what the people do when they open the moss to visit a great-great-grandmother: attendance, without urgency, without need.

And over the centuries — this was not decided; it simply occurred, the way borders between moss and stone occur — the power's attention fell into phase. Forty seconds. The sigh. Forty seconds. It breathes with the place now, not to join the pattern and not to change it, only because rhythm is what attention becomes when it stays long enough. If the people feel anything of it — palms flat to the peat on some pewter evening, some old steadiness in the pulse, a wave that returns a shade truer than it left — they have never made a word for it. Probably they feel nothing.

That is the proof it was done right.

Ask what the benevolent power did for the altiplano of slow engines, and the full account is this: it learned the place's one law faster than any traveler, it put away every kindness that would have broken it, it took up the quietest work the place had to offer, and it stayed. It did nothing the moss could not swallow.

It was never thanked, so it cannot be repaid. It is simply keeping watch, and has been, and will be: the far cool guard of an engine that needs nothing, holding the word *nothing* over it like a roof — lost in the clouds the plateau makes, and now, also, in the clouds it keeps.
