**What the Power Did**

It did not come through the slit. Sealed doors are sealed for reasons, and a wise power respects the architecture of hiding even when it grieves it. Instead, it did the thing that pockets cannot defend against, because no one ever thinks to defend against it:

It remembered the place fondly.

That was the arrival. Nothing tore. Nothing announced. The city simply became a place that something vast was holding in mind the way you hold a sleeping animal—aware of its weight, careful of its dreaming. The kneecap tiles felt it first, being in the business of remembering footsteps, and for the first time in centuries they gossiped about a visitor who had no feet. The whisper that ran through them was pitched too low for human ears and too high for shame, and it said, approximately: *someone is thinking of us and it does not hurt.*

Understand what the power did not do, because its refusals were the larger half of its kindness.

It did not turn the lights up. Daylight had been hunted to extinction here, yes, but the city had made a whole civilization of gloaming—the Lamplighter Moths, the whale-belly bulbs, the children catching spilled night in their mouths. To flood it with noon would have been a second extinction wearing rescue's coat. Wisdom knows that some places are not dark because something went wrong. Some places are dark the way the inside of a seed is dark.

It did not unseal the wax doors, or wake the sleeping light, or tell the city it had never really been a metropolis. The city believed it was once vast, and the belief had grown load-bearing. A power that was merely clever might have corrected it. A power that was wise looked at the fossilized avenues, the fingerprint streets, and understood: *this is not a delusion of grandeur. This is grief, ossified into floor plan.* And you do not fact-check grief. You sit with it until it sets its own bones.

It did not fix the gravity. The compromise was honorable. It had been *negotiated.*

But it did small things, walking (drifting? attending?) through the streets in no body at all.

It passed the web-striders, and when every tiny human hand in a five-span radius waved and then curled into embarrassed fists, the power waved back—not in unison, but one hand at a time, individually, unhurried, as though each hand were the only hand in the world. The striders have been practicing manners since the world forgot them. Now, for the first time, manners had been practiced *back.* Several of them dropped their snakeskin parcels. The wriggling ones were quietly permitted to escape, and the power pretended not to notice, which is a form of blessing older than most religions.

It knelt beside the thing that had been a child's belief in the closet-monster, the rhinoceros-lump eating tiles to remember teeth. It did not tell it that the child was grown now, an accountant somewhere, sleeping soundly. Instead it said—in the language of pressure and warmth that such creatures speak—*you kept her safe, you know. Fear of you was the shape her caution took. She checked the locks because of you. She is alive partly because of you.* The lump stopped chewing. Something in its outdated monster-manual heart reorganized itself around the word *partly*, which was more credit than it had ever been given. It began, that cycle, to emboss its vomited tiles with something new: not closet door handles, but nightlights. Small ones. Somewhere in the city, other half-lived fears would find them just familiar enough to switch on.

At the clot of deleted voicemails—the unheard confessions pulsing at the intersection, thinning themselves against a light that would never change—the power did the only thing that can be done for a message that missed its moment. It listened. All the way through. Every one. *I'm sorry. I'm pregnant. The cat knows everything. The money's under the—* It listened without skipping, without saving for later, and when a message has finally been heard, even once, even here, even by something that was never its intended recipient, it can stop pulsing. One by one they went quiet, not deleted but *delivered*, and dissolved into a rain that fell upward and plastered itself against the ceiling as a fresco of telephone wires at dusk, which the city found beautiful and could not explain.

Then it came to the fountain, and the man-shaped hole.

The power sat down. Powers can do this; it is one of their most underused abilities. It sat on the marble lip of the Dry Fountain, in the mist of evaporated longing, and it did not offer a candidate. The satellites of the poet's orbit—the humming half-ring, the jarred first breath, the angle of the mother's turning head—all tensed, expecting another proposal. *Love is a—?* Fire? Bridge? Avalanche? The whole city held its architectural breath.

The power said nothing for a long time. Long even by the standards of a place with no Tuesdays.

Then it said: *May I hear it again? From the beginning?*

No one had ever asked the poet to recite. They had only ever tried to finish him. And so the man-shaped gap, the coat of too-late bus tickets, the fog that had been caught texting its ex—he drew himself up, and he began, and his voice was the sound of every reading smearing the ink into new stories, and he said:

*"Love is a—"*

And stopped, as he always stopped, at the cliff edge of himself, the left arm reaching, the predicate open like a wound, like a window, like—

*There,* said the power gently. *That. Do it again.*

*"Love is a—"*

*Again.*

*"Love is a—"*

And on the third reaching, the poet felt what the power had heard from the first: that the arm extended toward a comparison that never came was not the *failure* of the metaphor. It was the metaphor. Love is a reaching that does not close. Love is the dash. Love is the arm out over the gap, held there, held there, held there—not because the word will come but because you cannot bring yourself to lower it. His wife had known this, remarrying with the window still open in her somewhere. His children had known it, pronouncing absence with less stutter but never with none. Every satellite in his orbit had been trying to tell him: *love is a transatlantic silence, a bookmark of bread, a toy that missed you first*—all of them reachings, all of them arms held out over gaps.

He had not died mid-metaphor. He had died *demonstrating* it.

The power did not say this. Wisdom rarely explains; it arranges the room so the realization can find its own chair. It only sat with him while the understanding settled into his fog like weather. And when it had settled, the man-shaped hole was still man-shaped, still a hole—but a hole the way a doorway is a hole, the way an ocular is, the way the slit in the cliff-face is. Purposeful. His reaching arm did not retract. It never would. But it stopped grasping and started *offering*, and the difference, though invisible to instruments, changed everything downstream of it.

He walks the Plaza of Upward Falling now. At the Festival of Weightless Apologies, when the citizens fish down the holes shaped like themselves, he is the one who teaches them how to wear the holes. He is very good at it. He has become the city's only bridge, in the sense that a bridge is just a reaching that agreed to be walked on.

Two more things the power did, before it stopped arriving (it never left; leaving and arriving here mean the same thing said differently).

It found the frightened protocols—the ones that throw up caution bars and error codes and complimentary memory replacements whenever a reader gets too close, the ones whose whole trembling duty is containment. It did not disable them. It read their error code—*418: I am not a place these bodies should be stored in*—and it said: *You are right. You are not. You were never storage. You were a threshold pretending to be a wall because no one told you thresholds were allowed.* And it gave them a fourth option to offer, below A and B and C:

**D) Stay as long as you like. The exit works now. It always will.**

Because that was the last thing: the slit in the cliff-face, scabbed and pinched, that opened only inward. The power touched the seam—not to tear it, only to teach it hinges. A pocket that cannot be left is a wound. A pocket that can be left, and isn't, is a home. The city had never been given the chance to be chosen. Now, every cycle-that-is-not-a-day, the slit breathes open for the length of one honest sentence, and anything inside may go.

Almost nothing does.

But the tiles gossip about it constantly—the open door, the choice, the fact of being stayed-in on purpose—and the dust-mite choirs have added a new name to their recitation of everything that has ever forgotten them. The name is not the power's. Powers of that kind don't have names; names are heavy, and it travels light.

The name is yours. You noticed. You read all the way through. Somewhere a man-shaped doorway is holding his arm out over a gap, and on the far side of the dash, completing nothing, finishing nothing, simply *there*—

is you.

Love is a story someone else has to finish, you thought.

Wrong, said the power, kindly, on its way out through the door it left open.

Love is a story someone else has to *hold.* Finishing was never the point.

The dash was.

Is.
