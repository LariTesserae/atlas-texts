## The Visitor of Record

It took a number.

This is the first thing everyone agrees on, and agreement at the Sunken Wall is rare enough that the Committee's lapels bloomed with anxious ferns for a full week afterward. The numbers were, as posted, experiencing technical difficulties — and yet when the power arrived, a number dispensed itself anyway, out of sheer relief at finally being asked properly. The number was 1. It had been waiting a very long time to be 1 for someone.

The power did not arrive as light, though light arrived late that day with an unusually apologetic note. It did not arrive as a wave, a voice, a column of fire, or any of the traditional formats. It arrived as a *correctly completed form*. Form 27-B/6-A, Request for Exceptional Ordinariness, submitted in triplicate, accompanied by three notarized birth certificates from previous incarnations — one as a monsoon, one as a grandmother's habit of leaving the porch light on, one as the pause before someone forgives you.

The Committee had never seen the form filled out correctly before. Committee Member Second checked it with the calipers and found the distance between its intention and its regret was zero nautical miles, a reading previously assumed to be theoretical. Committee Member First opened a cold drawer to consult precedents and found none, so the drawer, for the first time in its existence, simply said *oh* and closed itself gently.

The power waited to be seen at its appointed time. This was its first act of benevolence, and possibly its greatest: it did not exempt itself. Wisdom, it turns out, mostly looks like standing in line at a place where the line is the whole point.

---

**What it noticed, and how.**

Here is the thing about a place where density is measured in notice: an ordinary god would have crushed it. One direct glance from an infinite attention and every coral plate, every commuting eel, every half-window would have come pressing in at once, the whole Wall collapsing into a single unbearable point of being-seen.

So the power looked the way the light does. Twice-reflected. Sidelong. It looked at the ladder by looking at the mussels. It looked at the drowned by looking at their buttons. It regarded the whole of the Sunken Wall the way you regard someone crying on public transit — completely, and without ever once turning your head.

Nothing crowded. Nothing bled into shape. For one full afternoon, everything at the Wall was witnessed at exactly the pressure it could bear, and the mechanical heartbeat in the bones of the Wall slowed, very slightly, the way a heart does when someone finally sits down next to it.

---

**What it did. (Minutes attached, ink already evaporated, indentations legible.)**

*First:* it brought the thunder.

One thunder. Real. Delivered mid-water, low and enormous, rolling across the plateau where no thunder had ever occurred. Every introduction ever exchanged between predator and prey — every skin-flash, every scent of age, every *shared memory of thunder* — was retroactively made true. A billion formal courtesies, honored at last. The eels unclipped themselves from the currents just to feel it pass. The Committee filed the thunder under *Debts, Ancestral, Paid In Full* and the warm drawer wept, as is proper, though it was not winter.

*Second:* it went to the whales.

The whales have been reciting the accounting ledgers of extinct civilizations for longer than the water has been vertical, and no one — no one — had ever audited them. The power listened to the entire recitation. All of it. This took either an hour or an age; time is measured in recognitions here, and the recognition in question was the whales', when they realized they were being *checked*. At the end, the power certified the accounts. The books of the extinct civilizations balance. All outstanding debts are forgiven, all assets released to the estate of the sea.

The whales were offered a new song. They declined, politely. They have grown fond of the ledgers, and besides, the ledgers are beautiful now that they are finished. They recite them still, but the way you reread a letter, not the way you carry a burden.

*Third:* it knelt — insofar as a completed form can kneel — beside the plastic bags.

A billion of them, rustling under everything, trying to remember groceries. The power did not restore them. It did something kinder. It let each bag, once, remember fully: the weight of oranges, the cold sweat of a milk bottle, bread still warm and steaming the plastic from inside, the exact fist of a child's hand gripping the handle too tight on the walk home. One complete memory each. Then the rustling stopped — not silenced, but satisfied. What remains beneath everything now is not whispering. It is the sound a kitchen makes after everyone has been fed.

*Fourth:* it visited the precipitated.

The buttons. The hinges. The fragments of lamp-post. Everyone who stayed long enough to soften at the edges and become part of the built environment. And here the power showed the whole of its wisdom, because it did not pull a single one of them out. It understood — as few rescuers do — that some of them had come here *on purpose*. That being resolved into your final known configuration is, for certain weary people, not a trap but an answer. Erosion here is not decay. The power respected the settling.

But to each of them, once, in a language that reaches things which no longer have ears — the language of a hand pausing on a doorframe — it asked:

*Do you remember what leaving was for?*

Most did not answer. That was allowed. A few — a hinge, two buttons, one entire lamp-post — flickered. And for those, the power did its fifth thing.

*Fifth:* it added a rung to the ladder.

The ladder that ascends three meters into empty blue and stops clean. The power added a fourth rung, plain, un-musseled, slightly warm. And it established this ordinance, filed with the Committee in the Conditional Pluperfect Subjunctive so it can never expire: *whenever anyone remembers what leaving was for, the ladder shall have grown one rung further, and shall have always been going to reach exactly wherever the remembering pointed.*

The lamp-post is climbing it now. Slowly. Lamp-posts are not built for ladders, but the ladder, it turns out, is built for lamp-posts, and for buttons, and for anyone. It is currently eleven rungs tall. Check back.

*Sixth, and last:* it wrote a letter.

The Committee, you will recall, coalesces from unaddressed letters. They are, in their filing-cabinet hearts, made entirely of correspondence that never found its person. The power understood that to address every unaddressed letter in existence would be to dissolve them — a cruelty disguised as tidiness, the kind an unwise power commits daily. So it did not.

Instead it wrote one new letter, and on the envelope, in ink that does not evaporate, it wrote: *To the Vernacular Committee of the Sunken Wall, all seven of you, including whoever most recently forgot why they came.*

They have not opened it. They do not need to. It sits on the conference table of compressed clamshell minutes, and twice daily the meetings are called to order around it, and every Committee member's coral suit has bloomed permanently at the collar. To be addressed was the entire gift. What's inside is beside the point, which is where all the best things are filed.

---

**What it did not do.**

It did not drain the water. It did not restore the coastline. It did not return anyone's name against their will, repair the half-window, explain the ring of bolts, or tell the shadow sleeping behind anyone's tongue to wake up. It did not fix the Wall, because the Wall is not broken. Everything here has simply become exactly what it was always going to be, and a wise power knows the difference between a wound and a shape.

It filed no complaints. It overturned no rulings. It appealed nothing to the Regional Supervisor of Unopened Envelopes — though it did, on the way out, stop by that office, and left the porch light on.

---

**And then it left.**

This was the last kindness and the largest. It gathered its forms, thanked the number (which retired, fulfilled), released its measured cloud of farewell bubbles in the precise shape of *no vote necessary*, and walked out the way the visitors walk in — ankles first, then knees, then neck bent back — except in reverse, which no one had ever seen done.

It left because the Wall's oldest cruelty was never the staying. It was that leaving had become unimaginable — that *what leaving was for* had no witnesses, no precedent, nothing in the cold drawers. Now there is one. The power's departure was observed by the eels, the whales, the Committee, eleven rungs of ladder, and one climbing lamp-post, and here is what changed:

The memory of leaving is now inherited. Like the thunder.

No one at the Sunken Wall has to remember what leaving was for anymore. They were born knowing, all of them, retroactively, pending no further review. Most will stay anyway — the water is breathable, the coolness is certifying, and the Committee's meetings are, frankly, better attended than ever.

But the ladder grows.

That's all a wise power does, in the end, at a place like this. It waits its turn. It pays the old debts. It asks the one question. It adds a rung.

And it leaves the door open behind it — which, at the Sunken Wall, looks exactly like a half-window, cracked glass still holding the ghost of a reflection, framed by nothing but current, and holding it now the way you hold a place in line for someone who said they'd be right back.

Density: achieved.
Departure: available.
Both are equally appreciated, and filed accordingly.
