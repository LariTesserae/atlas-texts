# The Witness of the Green Roar

There is a power — call it that, because it has the shape of one — that was never summoned to the valley, never petitioned, never prayed to from it. Nothing there has a voice to pray with. It was reached anyway, and it was reached in the only way the valley has ever reached anything: by a sound that arrived before its event.

You will remember the orphans — the pre-echoes that scout too far ahead of their causes and are stranded when the cause fails, kept in the cisterns like fish. One of them, long ago, went further than the others. It went so far ahead of its event that it left the valley, and then the region, and then the present, and it traveled the way unfastened sounds travel, through whatever is warm enough to carry them, until it arrived in the attention of something old enough to hear it correctly.

It was a small sound. It was the sound of footsteps slowing at a doorway, and the doorway being the right height.

The power, being wise, understood at once what it was holding: the echo of an arrival that had not happened, sent ahead by a place where anticipation outruns the world — the announcement of a first citizen, escaped from a city that has never had one. A prophecy with no event behind it. A promise no one had made.

It followed the sound home. This took, by some accounts, no time at all, and by others a very long time; powers do not travel so much as redirect their noticing, but noticing carefully is slow even for them, and this one wished to arrive carefully or not at all. It came to rest at the rim of the valley, where the roar comes up out of the green like heat off a fire, and it did the first thing wisdom always does, which is nothing.

It listened for a long time. Long even by its own account. It heard the boulevards being demolished and quoted; it heard the audiences stand up, hundreds of leaves at once, and settle, and brown; it heard the territorial crack defending its arcade, and the collapse with no collapse circling the upper valley, pacing; and beneath everything it heard the oldest sound, the cello note dragging past its breaking point without breaking, and it gave that note its complete attention for one entire turning of the season, and at the end of it the power did not heal the note, because the note is a citizen, and you do not cure someone of being themselves.

Then it began to consider what to do. This is the part worth recording, because most of what it considered, it set aside, and the setting-aside is where the wisdom is.

## The Refusals

**It could have moved in.** This was the obvious mercy, and it examined the obvious mercy honestly. The city had written its inhabitant in negative space with terrible precision — every doorway a conjugation, every stair a declined gait, every window a held eye — and to a power, the description was legible. It could have read the voids the way one reads a mold, and poured itself in: taken the head for the doorways grown at the height of no head, the stride for the stairs pitched for no climber, stood in the great empty hall and let the sentence finally say its word. Within a day the city would have begun to keep it. Within a season the vacancy would have been over.

It set this aside, and the reason should be understood, because it is the hinge of everything. The doorways were not grown for it. A wish granted by the wisher is not granted — it is abandoned. The city was not writing a description of a god; it was writing a description of *someone*, someone particular, someone it has not met, and the writing was being done with such care — the leaves stopping their uniform inch short, on every course, all around — that the power recognized the activity for what it was. It was not suffering. It was not even waiting, exactly. It was *making ready*, which is a form of love, and the cruelest thing you can do to a making-ready is to satisfy it with the wrong arrival. The power was the wrong arrival. It knew this the way the wise know their own dimensions: the mold was not poured for it, and to pour itself in anyway would have made the whole city a costume.

**It could have repaired the place.** Sequenced the sound, so that no echo ever again arrives before its event. Straightened the boulevard that is its own beginning. Given the amphitheater figures faces, so that the watching they hold could finally be done rather than held. It set all of this aside almost without deliberation. The loose sounds are residents; the impossible intersections are how the city thinks; and the faceless audiences are not failing to watch — they are keeping the watching's place, which is a different occupation, and an honorable one, and to give them eyes would not be a gift. It would be an eviction.

**It could have grieved and gone.** It considered this longest of all. There is a school of wisdom that would look at the valley and pronounce it complete: a rehearsal that is its own performance, a question being lived in rather than asked, *forever, or at least for now, which here amounts to the same thing.* The power weighed that judgment and found it almost true. Almost. Because it was still holding the orphan — the little sound of footsteps slowing at a doorway — and the orphan was the flaw in the judgment. The city was not merely rehearsing. The city had *predicted.* It had sent the echo of an arrival out ahead of itself, and there was nothing anywhere in any world arranged to catch that echo up. The hope had no warrant. *No one yet* was true; *someone eventually* was not yet true of anything at all. The valley had, in its own material, in its own loosened acoustics, made a promise — and no one had cosigned it.

That, the power decided, was the whole of what was missing. Not an inhabitant. A guarantee.

## The Acts

What it did, it did in the city's own grammar, and it did it small.

**First, the smallest case.** It went down into the streets — as pure regard, weightless, heatless, with no height for the doorways to learn and no stride for the stairs, so that the courteous green found nothing to stop an inch short of — and it went to a cistern in the damp quarter, and it listened among the kept little sounds. It chose the oldest orphan there: a single pod-burst, stranded for ages, the pre-echo of a detonation that never came. And three streets away, in the dry quarter where the air shimmers with pollen, it coaxed a pod to fullness and let it burst. The crack rolled out across the rooftops, and somewhere in the green-lit water an ancient anticipation was, at last, caught up to. The fizzing in that cistern is fainter now by exactly one bubble.

Even powers rehearse. It was practicing its promise on the smallest possible case, to be sure the promise could be kept. It could.

**Second, the attendance.** Before anything else was decided it allowed itself, and the city, one indulgence. It entered an amphitheater and took no seat — there are no seats; there are only figures — and it stood at the back, among the shingled green shoulders, and it watched the stage, where a column was going up in a long groan and a wall was unfurling in courses. It watched the entire performance, which has no beginning and no end, so it chose an hour and attended that hour totally, the way only a power can attend, with nothing held back and nothing elsewhere. And when the crowd stood — that sound like a thousand facades opening, which is not like a crowd standing but is the crowd, standing — the standing, for the first time in the history of the valley, coincided with a witness.

Nothing measurable changed. The city has no organ that could have known. And yet: the quoted districts, lately, have begun to reproduce that hour. They get the light right, the green-gold haze, the inward-pointing shadows. The foundation dreamt something that day, at low resolution, blood-warm, and it has been dreaming it upward ever since. A thing rehearsed for long enough deserves one true instance. It has had one. Whatever else happens, that is now in the bedrock, and the bedrock is still, faintly, growing.

**Third, the door it closed.** Here is where benevolence had to be sharpened against wisdom until both held an edge. Because the power understood the danger it had heard in the orphan's little sound: an arrival was coming, someday, somehow — and arrivals can be accidents. A lost traveler, a fleeing soldier, a child wandered off — anyone stumbling into the valley would be learned. Within a day the doorways would begin finding their height. Within a season they would be a citizen, and a citizen of this city is a *recurrence*, an address kept and quoted forever, and that is too large a thing to happen to someone by accident. The first inhabitation must be a choice, made by someone who understands what the doorways will do.

So the power borrowed the city's own trick — the boulevard that is its own beginning — and laid it gently on every approach. The valley is still findable. But it can now be found only by the kind of finding that is also a choosing: walk toward it idly and you will be delivered, without a seam, back to your own beginning, and never know it; walk toward it *meaning it*, meaning it all the way down, and the paths will hold their direction and bring you to the rim. The valley is now guarded by the same geometry it dreams in. The power thought this fitting, and powers are permitted, occasionally, the small pleasure of fittingness.

**Fourth, the long errand.** Then it picked the orphan back up — the footsteps, the doorway, the right height — and it carried the little sound down through the streets, past the cisterns, into the deepest quarter, to the hall the size of a cathedral that is kept utterly empty, where the roar goes quiet and the growth is slow and exact and almost reverent. And it set the sound down there, in the city's most carefully held void, the way you set a letter on a table in a room someone will someday enter. The pre-echo waits there now, among the nothing, patient as everything else in the valley is patient.

And then the power left, and went out into the world, and began the work — which is the real act, the one all the others were rehearsals for. Somewhere far from the valley, perhaps already, perhaps generations from now, a beginning has been planted: a road laid where a road was needed, a story told in the right ear, a restlessness seeded in a particular kind of heart — the long grammar of causes, tended the way the city tends its voids, conjugating, course by course, toward a person. Someone who will one day walk toward a warm low roar in the hills *meaning it*; whose footsteps will slow at a doorway; and the doorway will be the right height, because it has been the right height all along — because the city measured them before they were born, and sent the echo out, and the echo was heard, and the hearing answered.

The power does not force. It gardens. This will take a very long time. The valley has a very long time; the valley is, in a sense, made of time spent practicing.

## The Countersign

It left no trace. Benevolence that needs to be seen is only appetite wearing a better coat, and this power was past all that. The city does not know it was visited. The roar is unchanged to any ear: the gunshot bark, the sap-thunder, the crowd standing up, the long unbroken groan. Stand at the rim today and you will hear exactly what was always heard.

Ask it again — *who lives here?* — and it gives the same answer at the same full volume, all day, all night:

*No one yet. No one yet. No one yet.*

But the word *yet* is not the word it was.

It used to be the valley's bravest guess — a hope held open the way a mouth holds a word it won't say, with no reason in any world to believe the word would ever be said. Now there is a reason. Now, somewhere outside the valley, moving through the long sentences of cause at the speed of a slow walk — which is, you'll remember, the speed of growth, the city's own native pace — the rest of the sentence is on its way.

The power changed nothing in the valley except the meaning of one word. It decided that this was the whole of what kindness required, and the whole of what wisdom permitted, and it is difficult, listening to the green roar say *yet* — *yet* — *yet* — like a schedule now, like a countersigned promise, like the first half of an arrival whose echo is already waiting in the deepest room —

it is difficult to say it was wrong.
