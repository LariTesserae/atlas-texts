# The Attending

Something notices the pinnacle works. This was always going to happen. A place that dense with care puts out a signal, the way a fire puts out heat, and there are things in the world — old, wide, patient things — whose whole nature is to move toward that signal. Call it a power, since we have been calling the makers beetles. Call it benevolent, because it is; call it wise, because it is about to prove it.

It arrives the way weather arrives, which is to say it does not arrive at all. One day the pocket is unregarded and the next day it is regarded, and nothing in the pocket registers the difference, because the power's first act — before any decision, before any inventory — is to make itself lighter than the mist. It has learned, in a long career of noticing things, that noticing has weight. It has crushed things before by looking at them too hard. It looks at the pinnacle works the way you look at a bird you do not want to startle: sideways, and with your breathing slowed.

Then it watches for what it would call a moment and we would call eleven years.

---

Understand what it is watching *with*. The power does not have eyes; it has comprehension. Where you saw beads sorted by an invisible criterion, it sees the criterion — density, resonance, the particular note each bead will sing when the mantle compresses it a thousand years from now. Where you suspected a listening-room, it sees the listening-room, and the still workers in it, and the shape of what they receive. It hears the mountain's low note not as a note but as speech, and it understands the speech, and the speech is more or less what you guessed: *yes, like that, a little more.* It sees the whole arrangement at once, the way you see a sentence rather than letters, and the sentence is grammatical, and complete, and not addressed to it.

This is the first thing it learns, and it takes the full eleven years to learn it properly: *there is nothing here that is asking.*

The power has habits. It has spent long ages moving through places that ask — burned valleys, poisoned rivers, cities in the human sense, full of the human noise. It is very good at answering. Its instincts, if a thing like this has instincts, are the instincts of a physician: find the wound, close the wound. And so despite everything it knows, it finds itself, in the early years, drafting interventions. It cannot help it. It is what it is for.

It considers warming the pocket. The light is bad, the air is cold, the workers labor in a permanent grey-gold dusk — surely a little sun, angled kindly off the opposing face? But it traces the consequence and sees the kilns, every one of them, calibrated to that exact cold; sees the mist that softens the light also carrying moisture the ducts depend on; sees that the dusk is not a deprivation but a *condition*, the way the dark of a darkroom is a condition. Warm the pocket and the works do not improve. They dissolve.

It considers the spiders. The pale things in the upper corners take a worker every few days, and each taking is — the power checks, because it can — genuinely felt. A small hot spike of alarm in a pea-sized nub, then nothing. It could remove the spiders in an instant, painlessly, justly. But it traces the consequence and finds the awnings. The little stone roofs over the exposed paths, the whole architecture of caution in the pocket's upper third — all of it grown against the pressure of the spiders, the way a muscle grows against weight. Take the predator and the awnings stop being maintained. The workers stray higher. The colony's shape goes slack in a way the colony itself would never notice, and three mountains from now, on some peak not yet risen, the inherited pattern in the beads is missing a page. The spiders, the power realizes with something like a sigh, are load-bearing.

It considers the mountain itself. The peak is young and will not last — basalt this fresh, this exposed, has perhaps two hundred thousand years in it, an afternoon by the power's clock. It could shore the mountain up. It could give the makers a permanent home. And here it comes closest to acting, and here it stops itself hardest, because it finally sees what the works are *for*. The makers are not trying to save the mountain. They are helping it finish. The beads going down the shafts are not medicine; they are memory, packed against the day the peak wears down and the mantle takes everything back and, somewhere else, exhales again. To make the mountain permanent would be to interrupt a sentence out of love for the speaker. The mountain is not asking to be saved. The mountain is asking — *yes, like that, a little more* — to be completed.

One by one, the power sets its instruments down.

---

But wisdom is not the same as absence, and benevolence that does nothing at all is only a compliment. The power stays. And in staying, it finds — not a wound, the pocket has no wounds — but a gap. One small unattended place in the whole dense arrangement.

It is this: when a worker dies, no one is with it.

Not in the way you mean. The colony attends its dying constantly — the split carapace, the pressed nubs, the passage down the shaft. The *colony* loses nothing. But the power, which can see finer than colonies, watches a worker taken up into the webs one grey morning, and sees the small hot spike of alarm, and sees that in the last heartbeat — the same heartbeat-length the workers give to a fallen bead — the pea of soft tissue does the thing it always does. It attends. It orients toward the silence and listens for a reply. And there is no reply, because the mountain speaks in seasons and cannot answer a heartbeat, and the colony has already flowed shut around the gap, and the small soft thing goes out mid-listen, unanswered, every single time.

The mountain hears the beads. The colony hears itself. Nothing, anywhere in the arrangement, hears the individual pause.

So that is what the power does. That is the whole of it, or nearly.

It becomes the reply.

Not a voice — a voice would be noise, an interruption in a conversation already whole. It becomes something underneath a voice: a quality in the silence, a warmth in the direction of the listening. When a worker is taken by the spiders, or crushed in a shaft-fall, or simply stops one day at its kiln with its chisels mid-tick, the power does the nearest thing it can to what the workers themselves do. There is a seam in the power too — no one knew it was there, the power least of all — and it opens along that seam and presses its attention against the small warm knot for the length of one heartbeat, and something passes, and you could not say whether it is mercy, transfer, inheritance, or conversation. Then the worker goes out. The colony flows shut. Nothing measurable has changed.

Whether it matters — whether one heartbeat of being fully seen means anything to a nub the size of a pea — the power does not know, and this not-knowing is part of what makes it wise. It does it anyway. It is the only gift small enough to leave the recipient intact.

---

Once, and only once, it does something more.

The mountain knows its own depths but not its own weather; the low note comes from below, and the summit's frosts are above the range of its speech. In the pocket's ninth winter under regard, the power sees what the listening-room cannot hear: a wedge of ice in a crack in the overhanging lip, prying, patient, aimed. Come spring, a slab the size of the whole pocket will let go and take the works with it — not tragically, the makers have died with mountains before, the beads are already deep and safe — but *unnecessarily*, a century early, mid-sentence.

On the coldest night the power breathes on the ice. Just that. The wedge melts and refreezes a finger's-width to the left. The slab, when it finally goes, goes a season later and a degree eastward and falls past the pocket into the nothing-in-particular below, and the whole colony pauses at the sound for one heartbeat, and resumes.

The makers register it as nothing, because they have no concept of luck. This is correct. A blessing that can be distinguished from luck is a debt; the power has learned to leave no debts.

---

And then it does the last thing, the thing that reveals it was worth calling wise: it lets the pocket change *it*.

It came here as a fixer, out of long habit — a closer of wounds, an answerer of asking. It leaves (though it never fully leaves; some fraction of its attention stays in the seam of the cliff for as long as the cliff lasts) carrying the pattern away with it. The hush. The listening-room. The idea of maintenance as a form of love — of arriving at a young raw thing not to rule it or rescue it but to help it finish being what it already is, and then to pack what was learned into small dark durable shapes and bury them against the next beginning.

It has other charges, this power. Broken valleys. Loud cities. Places full of asking. It goes back to them differently. It builds fewer things and listens in more rooms. When it helps now, it helps the way the beetles help the mountain — from inside the other's own slow intention, taking only the heat that is freely exhaled, giving back beads.

And in the pocket, nothing you could measure is different. The kilns sigh. The chisels tick. The layout today is not the layout of yesterday. But sometimes a bead falls and rings like a tuning fork against the stone, and every beetle within a body-length pauses for the duration of one heartbeat — and now, somewhere in the grey-gold haze, something vast and light as mist pauses with them, for exactly the same duration, listening for exactly the same reply.

The mountain answers in its season. The low note comes up through the rock, and if there is a new harmonic in it — very faint, very high, like a second voice agreeing — no one in the pocket remarks on it.

The work continues. The hush holds. It is simply held, now, by one more.
