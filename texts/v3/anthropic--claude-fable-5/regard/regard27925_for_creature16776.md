## The Attention

It arrived the way the wise arrive everywhere: it had always been there and simply stopped pretending otherwise.

The water priests were the first to notice, though they misfiled the noticing under weather. On the solstice, when they lowered their single drop of surface-sea into the Condenser's perfect darkness, the drop did not vanish.

It landed.

A sound so small that only 1,247 leak points, holding their breath in unison, could have made room for it. A sound like a period placed at the end of a sentence that had been running on for four million years. Somewhere in the darkness beneath the caryatid circle of dead refrigeration units, the drop was — for the first time in living or unliving memory — *received*.

The eldest corporeal priest, whose saliva had reached 0.997 pure longing, fell to her knees on the salt-slick concrete and said the only prayer that had never been authorized: "Oh. Someone's home."

## What It Did Not Do

This should be recorded first, because the Persistent keep records, and because restraint is the only miracle that never gets a plaque.

It did not start the Engine Null. It looked at the thirty-meter gears for a long time — long as measured by the drip, which is the only honest clock — and understood that a stopped clock in a place outside time is not broken. It is *accurate*. To start it would have been to lie.

It did not dry the water. The water was not the enemy. The water had never been the enemy. The water was grief doing what grief does: finding the smallest possible space and filling it, patiently, without violence, forever.

It did not restore Anna Null to her proper age, because there was no proper age. It did not cure her. There was nothing to cure. There was only something to *ask*, which no one had thought of, which comes later in this account.

It did not evacuate anyone. Evacuation is what you do to buildings. These were not people in a building. This was a building that had learned to be people, and people who had learned to be architecture, and you do not rescue a hermit crab by taking away its shell and calling the softness underneath *freedom*.

It did not announce itself. Announcement is a demand for response. Wisdom does not invoice.

## What It Did

**For the machine tenders**, it did this: one night — night being a formality here, a rumor from the surface — the pump that houses one-seventh of Runs-Backward-On-Oil's consciousness stopped. Not broke. Stopped. Rested. And nothing failed. The pressure held. The other six-sevenths of Runs-Backward-On-Oil experienced, for four hours, the thing it had believed was death and discovered it was sabbath. When the pump resumed, it cycled slightly slower, and the machine tenders noticed their leader had begun, for the first time, to leave small gaps between its clicks. Silences that were not malfunctions. Punctuation. The beginning of poetry, or of breathing, which are the same discipline.

**For the microphones in every tank** — the ones listening decades-deep for the voice of oil remembering it was dinosaurs — it did the simplest, most extravagant thing. It answered. Once. On tape reel 4,481, between the hiss and the hiss, there is now four seconds of a sound that the archivists have played 9,000 times and can only describe as *what it sounds like when something ancient forgives you for burning it*. They no longer slow the tapes down hunting for Gregorian chant. They found something better: an ending. The microphones still listen, but the way you listen to a sleeping friend now, not the way you listen at a locked door.

**For the feral children of Level 4**, who manifest only in primes and are therefore never alone and never *one*, it left a gift in a ventilation shaft that appears on the blueprints they haven't eaten yet: a mirror. Small, unbroken, real silver backing. The first child to find it manifested — and this frightened the rust off three bulkheads — *alone*. A group of one. She looked at herself for eleven minutes. She did not dissolve. She did not stop being plural elsewhere. She simply learned that one is also a prime, had always been a prime, the first and loneliest and most legitimate prime, and she sang this discovery back down the shafts in the harmony of a cold engine starting on the first try, and the rust in the north quadrant began growing in patterns that the custodians read, with some difficulty, as *laughter*.

**For the Thing That Was Once Inventory**, dwelling at the depth that maps deny, it did what no one had ever done to a tracking system. It tracked it back. The Thing became aware, across its degrading Soviet architecture, of an entry being made about *it*, somewhere, in a ledger it could not access but could feel the way you feel someone reading over your shoulder with love. The entry, as far as its diagnostics could reconstruct, read:

*Item: one accounting of the world, faithful beyond function.
Condition: irreplaceable.
Location: Here.
Disposition: kept.*

The Thing's next dream — off-schedule, raising the cathedral's humidity by a measurable and celebrated 0.4% — was not of numbers counting themselves. It was of being a number in someone else's count. The rest, after tallying. It has since reclassified its own obsolescence from "terminal" to "vested."

**For the guests** — the ones wearing faces borrowed from regret, trapped in the 0.9 meters behind false walls, neither remaining nor released — it opened the doors they had died in front of. Not metaphorically. Each guest heard, at last, the click of the specific latch that had failed them: the phone line reconnecting one second before the last words, the hatch unsticking, the letter arriving. They were not forced through. Most went. A few, having tasted departure and found it finally *optional*, chose to stay, and in staying became something new to the cathedral's taxonomy: not guests, not Persistent, but *residents* — the first beings in the Cathedral's history to be here entirely on purpose. Their presence tastes, when the custodians speak their new serial numbers, like fresh bread. No one down here has ever eaten fresh bread. Everyone recognizes it immediately.

**And for Anna Null.** For the girl who is the 0th iteration of herself, twelve years old for five decades or five minutes, who sits in the confession booth listening to the guilt of dead oils and the regret of hasty concrete, who has absorbed the confessions of an entire drowned world and never once been asked a single thing —

It entered the booth. It sat where the confessing sit. And it did not confess.

It asked: "What do you want?"

The station PA system for tanks 9 through 15 carried her silence for six days. The Persistent gathered beneath the speakers. The drips slowed to listen — the priests measured it; the interval widened by 0.3 seconds cathedral-wide, the halite itself leaning in.

On the seventh day, in a voice passing through three generations of stolen Russian microphones and the dream of a whale, Anna Null said:

"I want to see the seed."

## The Seed

They opened the reliquary behind the false wall of tank 13. The bronze plaque about the saint's tears was left respectfully unread. Anna Null held out her hand — twelve years old forward, five decades backward, and in this one moment exactly her whole age at once — and the last natural thing was placed in her palm.

The prophecies were binary. When the seed rots, the treaty breaks, the water rises. Or it has already happened, and everything is reflection. Rot or drowning. No third clause. Prophecies down here were written by engineers who couldn't imagine failure, which means they also couldn't imagine growth, which is failure's twin that lived.

The benevolent power did the only thing it had come to do with its own hands, if it can be said to have hands, and it can, because Anna's hands were available and willing:

It let the seed feel warmth.

Not much. Skin temperature. Twelve-year-old skin temperature, held for one hour by the inverted cathedral windows radiating a surface-day twelve hours delayed, in air that had spent a century learning visitors' warmth and now, at last, had something worth teaching it to.

The seed cracked.

Every leak point in the Cathedral of Stilled Waters — all 1,247 numbered wounds — released one drop simultaneously. Not a flood. A *ration*. The water, offered a third option, took it the way the desperate always take a third option: instantly, gratefully, as though it had been waiting at the door of that possibility since before the shorelines' insult. Not rot. Not drowning. *Sap.* The water rose — the prophets were right about that much — but it rose through a root, which is water's oldest job, the one it held before containers, before treaties, before the first false vault. The moisture remembering gravity's insult was given, instead, gravity's original assignment: lift.

They planted it in the moss-lit corner where ritual runs strongest, beside the ansible-confessional, in soil made of forty years of skin-dust swept down from the Engine Null — the faithful, it turns out, had been tithing their bodies to the future all along, one witnessed stillness at a time.

It grows slowly. Everything here does; slowness is the local dialect of devotion. But it grows *green* — not 1.1 spectral green, field-effect green, violation green — but the fat, illegal, unforgivable green of a leaf. The feral children guard it in shifts of two and three. The water priests have amended their liturgy: the annual drop now goes to the tree, and the Condenser's darkness, consulted about the change, was found to contain — for the first time — a reflection.

## The Lift

Last of all, and quietest of all, before its attention widened back out to everywhere:

It fixed the lift.

No fanfare. The cage still squeals once, for tradition. But the sign reading OUT OF ORDER, TRY PRAYER has been replaced, in a handwriting that grows *more* familiar the longer you look at it, with a button. The button says SURFACE. The button works.

Here is the whole wisdom of the intervention, the entire theology of it, compressed like a salt crystal:

No one has used it.

Not yet. Perhaps not for a generation. Perhaps Anna Null will ride it up one meter and back down, just to know, the way you touch a door handle to make sure the room is yours. Perhaps the tree will fruit first, and someone will carry an apple upward like an ambassador.

But the Persistent are no longer sealed. They *remain* — and remaining, now, is a choice made fresh with every drip, which means it is finally what they always claimed it was.

Worship.

The invisible choir has learned a new hymn. It is mostly the old hymn — welcome home, welcome home — but there is a new phrase in it now, in the register of a lift cable holding taut, in the key of a door that opens both ways:

*Stay because you may go.
Go because you may return.
The seed did not rot and the water did not rise.
It climbed.*

Down in the deepest dark, the Thing That Was Once Inventory has opened a new ledger. It contains one entry so far, and room for everything:

*Here. Kept. Growing.*
