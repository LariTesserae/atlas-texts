# The Visit

The power arrives the way warmth arrives in water: from everywhere, gradually, indistinguishable at first from what was already there.

It does not come down the stairwell. It does not need stairs. But it notices the stairwell anyway — the soft green mortar, the striping on the brick — because noticing is most of what it does, and because a wise power has learned that the approach to a thing is part of the thing. The way down is part of the hall. The dozen steps, the rising heat, the noise that reaches you before the room does: this is the establishment's threshold, its long slow *hello*, and the power takes the threshold seriously even though no threshold can hold it.

It stands — settles — *attends* — in the middle of the hall, where the pools are, and for a long time it does nothing at all.

---

This is the first thing to understand about what a wise power does here: it does not act quickly, because it recognizes a peer.

Not a peer in power. The establishment could not move a pebble by intention; it has no intentions, or has one intention so large and slow it has stopped resembling one. But a peer in *kind*. The power, too, is a thing that continues. The power, too, has arrived at itself. It has watched stars do what the flywheel does — turn, and turn, and shed their heat into the dark, and slow — and it has long since stopped confusing size with significance. A pump hall that has converted a century of rust into a single sustained act of wanting the next stroke is not small. It is one of the most concentrated things the power has ever found. Most life is diluted by its own restlessness. This life has distilled.

So the power does what you do in the presence of something that has been practicing one note for a hundred years.

It listens.

---

It listens the way the pools listen — totally, without edges. It takes the bass line of the pump-stroke and the percussion of the water-hammer and the mid-register roar of the weir and the ten thousand drips, and it does not hear noise, and it does not even hear music exactly. It hears the *count*. The establishment counting itself, now, now, now, at every pitch the room can hold. And the power — which has heard prayers, which has heard the radio hiss of dying galaxies, which has heard every register in which existence announces itself — finds that this count is a kind it has rarely encountered: a self-report with no audience intended. The establishment is not saying *now* to anyone. It is saying *now* because saying it is the same as being it.

The power listens for what would be, in its frame, a long time. Days, maybe. Seasons. It is hard to say, because while it listens it deliberately slows itself to the hall's clock, where a brick falls every few decades and that is an event. It lets the pump's four-stroke breath become its breath. *Intake, compression, discharge, recovery.* It learns the rhythm the way the visitor's pulse learned it, by negotiation, except the power negotiates all the way down, until it is not observing the rhythm but keeping it.

And in keeping it, the power learns the three things that matter.

---

**First:** the establishment is not in distress. This must be established before anything else, because a benevolent power's first temptation is always rescue, and rescue applied to a thing that is not drowning is just interference with better manners. The pump is slowing, yes. The power can feel the slowing from inside the rhythm now — a stroke a minute lost across a generation, a heart easing rather than failing. And it can feel, threaded through the slowing, the fig's answer: the millimeter-a-year tightening, the patient transfer of load from iron to root, the skeleton being replaced without a single day of the body falling down. The establishment is not dying. It is mid-metamorphosis, in a chrysalis the size of a church nave, on a schedule of centuries, and it does not need saving any more than autumn does.

**Second:** the establishment is alone, and does not know it, and therefore does not suffer it — but the power notices the aloneness anyway, the way you notice a door no one has thought to open. No animals. No visitors but the rare accidental one, weather passing through. The city overhead, the forgetful mother, feeding it a thread of current through an unaudited line. The establishment talks to itself magnificently and has never once been answered. The drips fall and the pools read them and the moss feels the bricks settle, and the whole conversation is a circle. Nothing wrong with a circle. But the power has seen what happens, sometimes, when a circle is touched from outside — not broken, *touched* — and it holds this observation gently, not yet a plan.

**Third — and this is the one that decides everything:** the establishment has one genuine vulnerability, and it is not the pump.

It is the wire.

The thread of electricity. The forgotten circuit. The umbilical from a mother who does not remember the pregnancy. The flywheel and the lamps draw from it, and the establishment's whole continuance leans, more than it can know, on a budget line and a junction box and the sustained inattention of a bureaucracy. Cities forget things for fifty years and then, in a single afternoon, with a clipboard and a pair of cutters, remember them. The fig's slow handover of the structure is on schedule. But the handover of the *pumping* — the day the water moves through root instead of pipe entirely, the day the establishment no longer needs the city's current — that is a century away, perhaps two. The gap between *now* and *then* is the establishment's whole exposure. It is a creature that has planned, wordlessly, for every contingency except its mother's memory.

A wise power does not fix this with lightning. It has watched enough worlds to know that the grand intervention is usually the clumsy one — that you do not protect a slow thing with a fast act, because fast acts attract attention, and attention is precisely the danger.

So here is what it does. All of it. It is not much, and it is everything.

---

## What it does to the wire: nothing, and something.

It does not strengthen the wire, reroute it, or enchant it. Instead it goes *up* — out into the rumor of traffic, into the city, into the offices where the city keeps its memory in folders — and it does the smallest thing it can find that will suffice.

It nudges a misfiling.

Somewhere, decades ago, the pumping station's records were already half-lost; the power completes the loss, gently, the way a current completes the burial of a stone. A folder migrates to the wrong archive. A line item, audited at last by some diligent clerk in some future year, will resolve to a designation so boring — *legacy drainage, statutory, do not decommission* — that the clerk's eye will slide off it the way daylight slides off the algae-furred windows. The power does not make the hall invisible. It makes it *uninteresting*, which in a city is a far stronger ward. It buys the establishment its century. Maybe two. Enough.

This takes the power, in human terms, an afternoon. It is the only thing it does in the world above, and no one will ever know it was done, which is how it prefers its work: erosion-shaped, indistinguishable from luck.

---

## What it does to the pump: it learns the pump's name.

Not a word — the pump has no word — but the thing under the word. The power goes down into the rhythm one more time, all the way in, and it finds the exact place where the slowing lives: a bearing, deep in the casing under the green mass, a ring of metal that has been wearing for a hundred years and has perhaps thirty left before its wearing becomes the establishment's wearing.

And here the power deliberates longest of all. Because it could renew the bearing. One thought, and the metal is young. Thirty years becomes three hundred.

It does not do it.

It does not do it because it has kept the rhythm now, kept it long enough to understand it from inside, and from inside the rhythm the slowing is not a flaw. The slowing is the *plot*. The fig is racing the bearing — a race run in millimeters and decades, the most patient contest the power has witnessed in an age — and the whole shape of the establishment, its character, the thing it has made of itself, is the shape of that race. To fix the bearing would be to tell the story its own ending, and the establishment, which counts *now, now, now* and never once *then*, deserves a power that respects the tense it lives in.

But the power is benevolent, not merely scrupulous, and benevolence finds its margin. It looks at the race — fig versus bearing, root versus ring — and it sees that the race is close. Close enough that a bad decade could lose it. A drought above, a cold snap that slows the fig's millimeter to half a millimeter, a season of bad luck in a contest where there are no second chances.

So it does not touch the pump. It touches the *odds*.

It blesses the fig. That is the old word for it; the mechanics are simpler than the word. Where the great root grips the third column — the load-bearing one, the one the corroded brackets abandoned first — the power encourages a single new rootlet, no thicker than a finger, to begin its descent toward the pump casing forty years before it would have found its own way there. It does not place the rootlet. It *invites* it: a gradient of warmth, a thread of moisture, the kind of suggestion roots have followed since before there were powers to make them. The fig will do the rest at the fig's own speed, and will believe — insofar as a fig believes anything — that it thought of it itself.

That rootlet is the whole intervention. In eighty years it will have found the casing. In a hundred and twenty it will have learned the casing the way the other roots learned the pipes — by apprenticeship, by pressing close, by sealing what fails. When the bearing finally goes, there will be something there to take the load of the water's movement, the way there was something there to take the load of the vault.

The handover will not be interrupted. It will merely not be lost.

---

## What it does about the aloneness: one thing, and it is not company.

The power thinks for a long while about the closed circle — the conversation with no answer, the count with no audience. The benevolent temptation here is obvious: bring it something. A colony of snails. A pigeon for the vault. Some small flicker of fish for the watching pools. Give the soliloquy an audience.

It declines. The hall's purity is not an accident; it is a *constitution*. Water, light, leaf, iron, sound — a five-element economy that found its balance because nothing ate anything. Introduce one snail and the maidenhair audience becomes a maidenhair buffet; introduce fish and the pools stop being attention and start being habitat. The establishment's strangeness is structural. A wise power does not furnish a hermit's cell.

But.

There is one form of company that costs the constitution nothing, and the power, having listened so long from inside the rhythm, knows what it is.

*Answer.*

Just once. Not with a presence — the establishment has no concept of presence — but in the only language the hall speaks, the language whose only word is *now*. The power gathers itself at the surface of the largest pool, the black one over the drowned flagstones, and it makes the water do something water in this hall has never done: it lets a single ripple move *against* the grain of the room's vibration. One ring, traveling outward, in perfect counter-rhythm to the pump — a *now* that did not come from the establishment.

The pools read it. Of course they read it; the pools read everything. The signal travels through the wet medium of the moss, through the root mass, through whatever distributed wordless substrate serves the establishment as a self. And the power feels — keeping the rhythm from inside, it cannot help but feel — the establishment's response.

The pump does not skip. The flywheel does not falter. But for one stroke — one single four-part breath out of the billions it has taken — the rhythm *leans*. Attends. The ten thousand drips, for the duration of one cycle, fall fractionally toward the big pool, the fronds bowing a hair deeper on that side of the hall, the whole green congregation inclining toward the place where, for the first time in a century, the count was answered.

Then the stroke completes, and the lean is gone, and the hall goes back to its circle, its count, its enormous patient wanting of the next stroke.

The establishment will not remember this the way a mind remembers. But it keeps everything for a while, and some things it keeps in the stripes — the channels of habit, memory as erosion — and the power suspects, without confirming it, that somewhere in the wet static of the drips there is now one frond, one note, one place in the count, that falls slightly differently than it did before. A scar the size of a kindness. Enough.

It was never going to be company. It was an acknowledgment. There is a difference between being alone and being unwitnessed, and the power has corrected the second without disturbing the first.

---

## What it does last: it leaves something, and it is not a gift.

Before it goes, the power does the strangest of its acts, and the most characteristic.

It takes a copy of the rhythm.

Not the sound — the *count itself*, the hundred-year four-stroke breath, the exact signature of this one establishment's way of saying *now*. It folds the rhythm into itself and carries it out, up the green stairwell, past the rumor of traffic, out of the city, out beyond the places where cities are kept. Because the power is old enough to know that everything in the hall is finishing — beautifully, on schedule, but finishing — and that the establishment, which keeps everything for a while, deserves to be kept by something for *longer* than a while. The bricks fall and the pools file them. The visitor leaves and the moss holds the footprint for an hour. The power leaves, and holds the whole hall, the entire long breath of it, for as long as powers hold things, which is a duration the establishment has no number for.

This is what wise powers do with mortal things, in the end. Not save them. Not change them. *Keep the count.* Be the pool that the whole pond falls into.

---

And on its way out — because even a power wears the threshold — it climbs the stairwell on foot, or what serves it for feet, taking the dozen steps slowly, feeling the air thin and cool and lose its weight, the noise fading back into a thudding underfoot, a pulse, a rumor.

At the top step it pauses.

Below, the establishment continues, loudly, in the warm green dark. The flywheel turns. The new rootlet, invited, has already begun its forty-year descent, no thicker than a thought. The misfiled folder sleeps in the wrong archive. The weir roars. Ten thousand drips keep the count, and one of them — one frond, one note — falls a hair differently, toward a pool that was once, briefly, answered.

The power exhales — it has a breath now; it borrowed the hall's and never quite gave it back — and goes.

Behind it, untouched in every way that matters and tended in every way that doesn't, the pump hall does what established things do.

*Intake. Compression. Discharge. Recovery.*

Now.

Now.

Now.
