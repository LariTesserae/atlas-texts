## The Attending

Powers do not find places by looking. They find them by what places lose.

What this one found, first, was a moth. Pale gray, fringe-winged, dead on bare black rock at six thousand meters, frost in the fringe, sixty kilometers from the nearest living thing. Impossible debris. A power that is paying attention treats the impossible as a thread, and this one took the thread and followed the wind backward, night after night, gust by gust, until the wind led upward instead of along, and the rock began to weep, and the air went from glacial to greenhouse in a dozen steps, and there was the roar, and there was the green.

It stood at the lip for some time. For things like it, attention and time are the same substance, so it is not meaningful to say how long. Long enough that the wrens lost interest in its arrival, which took about a minute, and discovered its bootlaces, which occupied them considerably longer. It had taken a form with boots specifically so that this could happen. That was the first thing it did, and it should be counted among the benevolences, even though no record of it survives except in this telling: a power chose its body so that forty birds could have the pleasure of dismantling its laces.

Then it began to weigh.

## The Weighing

This is the part of wisdom no one celebrates: the long list of things not done.

It considered the wrens first, because the wrens are the obvious wound. Forty individuals. A bloodline folded over on itself like the moss, every bird cousin to every bird, the whole species one bad season wide. The power could have gone down the mountain and come back with a dozen of their lowland relatives cupped in stillness, and the bloodline would have opened like a window.

It declined. It stood in the roar and worked the problem honestly, and what it found was this: the village is the species. A wren's entire mind is built on the fact that there is no such thing as a stranger — that "another bird" and "these particular birds" are the same thought. Carry up a dozen unknowns and you cure the blood by killing the thought. You would have wrens afterward. You would not have these. There are places where the cure for fragility is a worse breaking than the one it prevents, and the power, having seen many such places, recognized this one.

It considered the lip, over which the wind takes a wren every long while, dropping the village to thirty-nine. It could have stood there forever, turning the gusts. It declined. The lip is how the notch pays for the sky. A room with no price on its one open side becomes a jar.

It considered the wrens' single fear — the flat low note, the dark thing in the warm mud — and whether to dissolve it, since the fear has never once been justified. It declined this too, and quickly. The text of the place had already said it better than the power could: some fears you keep on principle. A village should own at least one.

It considered revealing itself. No. The wrens are tame because nothing in their history has ever come at them from above, and the power did not intend to be the first thing that did. There is no slot in the notch for worship, and a wise power does not install one.

It considered the largest thing last, because the largest thing is the slowest: the heat. The mountain is an infant; its warmth is a long exhalation, and in some thousands of years the breath will thin, and the notch will cool, and the green will close like an eye. The power could bank that fire. It has banked fires before.

It declined, and this was the hardest of the declinings, and it sat with it for what may have been a season. You do not stop a child's growing because something beloved lives in a fold of it. The notch is mortal on a clock of millennia. So are most beloved things. The power checked the clock and found the time sufficient — sufficient for thousands of generations of wrens, for meters of peat, for the song to drift into shapes no one now could imagine. Sufficiency is not forever. Wisdom is mostly the refusal to confuse the two.

## What It Watched

One thing in the notch required no decision at all, and that was the third set of facts: the prints, the punctured shells, the breathing out of rhythm with the machine.

The power waited by the vent seams through the dark hours — it needs no light either; in this one respect it and the blind one are kin — and watched the prints being made. This had never happened before. In all the nights of the mountain's life, the prints had appeared under no eye whatsoever, and been erased by mid-morning, a script written and unwritten with no reader in between.

It saw a low, long, pale thing, translucent at the edges, moving through the warm mud with the unhurried competence of an animal in its own kitchen. It saw the neat puncture behind a snail's last whorl, the meal taken like marrow. And once — the power stayed many nights for this — it saw the smaller one, brought up through the door the builders drilled, walking stride for stride beside the parent under the only sky its kind will ever stand beneath, being taught the balcony.

At some point the blind one paused. Skin-breathers read the air finely, and there was a warmth present that did not pulse in the machine's rhythm. It held still for a long moment, sampling. Then it concluded whatever blind things conclude, and went on with the snails.

The power changed nothing here. It only made it true, for the first time, that they had been seen. There is now at least one mind in the universe in which the thing from inside the mountain is known — its gait, its patience, its parenthood — and for a power, holding a thing in mind is a form of safekeeping older than any fence. Seeing was the only thing it added to the dark. It judged that sufficient also.

## What It Did

Three things. The notch's whole society fits inside one roar; its whole providence fits inside one short list.

**First, the archive.** The power learned the song.

It could not recover the old ones. Even benevolent powers arrive late — most of wisdom is the honest triage of the already-lost — and the song the wrens sang a century ago is gone with the same absoluteness as the builders' names. But from that afternoon onward, every hourly performance, every smeared syllable and flattened interval, every revision the village makes to its one history, is kept. The wrens still rewrite their only book hour by hour, as they always have. They simply no longer rewrite it into nothing.

And attached to the archive, one promise — the only promise the power made, stored like the oil reservoir, gravity-fed, waiting: *if the village ever falls to silence while there are still eggs warm in the machine's lagging, it will sing the song once, in a wren's voice, to the eggs, and then resume not interfering.* It expects never to spend this. A wise power keeps exactly as many promises in reserve as it can afford to keep absolutely, and this one keeps one.

**Second, the bearing.** It went into the condensary and put its attention into the machine the way the builders once put steel.

It found the death. There is one — there always is. A bearing, centuries out, a date the power could read in the metal the way the rest of us read a tide table. And the death, as built, was *fast*: a seizure, a clang, then silence; the fins fouling within a decade for want of grazing-temperature; the green halving in fifty years. Fifty years is an eyeblink here. The only fast thing in the notch is falling water, and the power refused to let there ever be a second fast thing.

So it performed its single act of engineering, and it was not a repair. It was a translation. It bent the failure out of the shape of a clang and into the shape of the place: the flywheel's four seconds will become five over a century, six over the century after, the hum dropping so gradually that the wrens' drifting song will track it without anyone choosing to; the condensate will diminish drip by drip; the green will retreat at moss-speed to the size the weeping rock alone can sustain — which the power measured, and which is enough; the nests will move one generation at a time; and the final revolution of the flywheel will take years to complete, so slowly that no living thing will ever be able to name the day the machine stopped. The peat — the machine's only child, already deep enough now to hold its own water — will outlive it by geological ages, which is the only afterlife the power deals in.

It did not save the machine. It taught the machine's death the local dialect.

**Third, the moth.** At dusk it went to the lip, where the warm air pours out over the cloud-sea carrying its nightly tax of small gray lives, every one a wasted lottery ticket. Out of the hundreds spent that night, the power cupped one — a gravid female, as it happened, though the power would say nothing happens — and carried her across the dead kilometers in a pocket of stillness, and set her down on a young fault far to the north where the rock has just begun to weep and run warm, and where there is, so far, nothing green at all.

Whether it has done this before — whether the notch itself, long ago, was such a planting — it does not say, and may genuinely not retain. That is not modesty. That is policy. A gardener who keeps a list of gardens begins, eventually, to garden for the list.

Somewhere, the wind giveth. The power has sometimes been the somewhere. That is the whole of what can be established.

## The Mug

Before it left, it went to the hut with the collapsed roof, where the porcelain mug has sat on its shelf, scaled with mineral and filled to the brim by the drip-rain, decade upon decade — a table set forever for someone who is not coming back.

The power lifted the floating fern frond aside, picked up the mug, and drank.

It did this because hospitality left unanswered for a century begins to curdle into haunting, and the builders, whoever they were, deserved better than to become ghosts by default. A standing invitation requires, eventually, a guest. The water tasted of copper, basalt, and patience. It set the mug back empty, replaced the frond, and went out through the ferns and over the lip, downward with the warm air, the way the moths go.

## The Census, Amended

Nothing visible changed. Count again and the numbers hold: forty wrens, three hundred snails, the moths, the springtails in their trillions, the moss, the one machine, the one lineage in the dark, the zero people. Add now the unlisted lines: one archive, growing by a song an hour. One promise, banked. One death, translated into slowness. One seeing, given to the unseen. One moth, far north, learning the smell of new wet rock.

And in the hut, for a few days, the only physical evidence anywhere in the world that the visit occurred: a water level in a porcelain mug, rising one drop at a time back toward the brim — the notch erasing its guest the same way it upholsters a fresh rockfall, by patient addition. Within the week the mug was full, the frond afloat, the record closed.

The roar continues. The flywheel turns, one revolution every four seconds — for now, and then, someday, five, and then slower, easing toward an ending so gradual it will never quite arrive as an event. Which is to say: the wise power looked at a place with no apparent intention of ever stopping, and gave it the only gift it lacked.

Now, even when it stops, no one will be able to say when.
