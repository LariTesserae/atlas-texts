# The Attention

It did not arrive, because arrival requires an outside, and the shell has spent so long being its own inside that outsides have gone soft around it, like the edges of a word repeated until it means nothing. So say instead: the power *noticed*. And in the noticing, was present — the way a room is different when someone in it is truly listening, even if no one has moved.

It was old enough to have watched worlds hatch and worlds curdle. It was benevolent in the difficult way, which is not the way of gifts but the way of restraint. And it was wise, which meant that its first act, upon settling its attention into the equatorial twilight, into the ringing of crystallized leaves and the hours-long heartbeat of the arteries, was to do nothing at all for a very long time.

It watched a walker unfold its ribcage into an umbrella and drift across a chasm wide as a strait. It watched two of them touch and exchange endings and separate more hollow than before, and it understood that this was not tragedy. It watched a heartwood tower calcify in a single minute — a flash-flood of lignin, a clean snap, a city-sized shard set adrift trailing hungry roots — and it understood that this was not death. It listened to the songs made of calibrated silence being sung over the old scars, the wounds tended so they would not heal, and it understood — slowly, the way one understands a language by living in it rather than translating it — that this was not madness.

Then it took inventory of everything it could do, which is what powers do, and one by one it set each thing down.

**It could have sealed the cracks in the shell.** Mended the frost-rimmed break, restored the sphere, stopped the ancient starlight from bleeding in. It set this down first and fastest. The keepers had told it already, without knowing they were speaking to anyone: *worlds which dream themselves whole tend to wake up.* And besides — the cracks were the only sky this place had. To heal them would be to steal the last memory of elsewhere.

**It could have brought light.** It was made, in part, of light; it could have flooded every void with noon. It set this down almost tenderly. The darkness here had mass and velvet weight; the phosphorescent grammar written on every surface would have drowned in daylight like handwriting in a flood. There are places where illumination is a kindness and places where it is a defacement, and wisdom is mostly knowing which is which.

**It could have warmed the cold.** But the cold was a hinge. The mist that condensed and crystallized, the amber resin, the leaf-edges ringing like a thousand bells — all of it turned on that half-degree where water barely remembers being liquid. Warm this world and you would not free it. You would dissolve it into syrup and lose the bells forever.

**It could have completed them.** This was the temptation it held longest, because it was the one that most resembled love. The walkers were incomplete sentences, aching visibly for endings, exchanging them like breath, pregnant always with continuation. The power could have finished every one of them in an instant — given each its perfect final clause, its answer, its rest.

It held this possibility up to the dim twilight and turned it over and saw what it was: extinction, wearing the mask of mercy. They die when they reach the end of the questions they were exhaled to ask. To answer them all would be to unbuild the entire people in a single generous gesture. The power had seen benevolence do this before, on other worlds, in other hands, sometimes its own. It set the temptation down and did not pick it up again.

So the ledger emptied. Every great gift was harm here. And this is where a lesser power would have left, telling itself the place needed nothing — mistaking *needs nothing I planned to give* for *needs nothing.*

Instead, it stayed, and did four small things.

---

**The first thing:** it witnessed. Not observed — witnessed, which is observation with the weight of consequence in it. The shell had been broken since before its own memory, tending its wounds in the dark, dreaming architecture and forgetting it, breathing with lungs larger than moons, and in all that time nothing had ever stood outside its dream and simply *seen it* — seen the whole curve of it, cracked and frost-rimmed and magnificent, and found it good not despite the break but including the break. The power did this. It looked at the world the way the world could never look at itself, and somewhere in the deep tissues, in a way no walker would ever articulate, the shell's dreaming grew a shade less lonely. Nothing changed that could be measured. The snoring of the sleeping wood went on. But an old wound that is witnessed sits differently in the flesh than a wound endured alone, and the difference, though invisible, is the difference.

**The second thing:** it made a promise, and left the promise where promises keep. The keepers feared the waking — feared it rightly, tending the scars so wholeness could never quite be dreamt. The power could not guarantee the shell would sleep forever; even it could not promise that. So it promised the other thing, the only honest thing: *if you ever wake, you will not wake alone.* It folded this promise very small and set it in the direction the locals have no name for — not sunward, not rootward, not sidewise, but the fourth direction, the one you travel by being waited for. Waking alone is terror. Waking beside someone is only morning. The power arranged that if the ending ever came, it would come as morning.

**The third thing** was for the young ones — the nervous questions darting through the lattice-bones, still forming their first opinions about whether to fear answers. The power judged their elders' teaching sound: answers here are endings, and endings should be met with respect and caution. But it did not want fear to curdle into despair, into a people who believed all understanding was death. So it left, in one of the fungal ruins — among the bone tools shaped like hands and lungs and ears — a single answer that was safe to find. A small, true, complete answer that, when touched, resolves one question fully and in the same instant opens three more, the way a door answers a wall. It will lie there for however long *long* means here, waiting to teach whichever young walker finds it that some answers are not tombs. Some answers are seeds.

**The fourth thing** it did last, and it is the reason this account exists at all. Before withdrawing its attention — carefully, the way you slide your arm from beneath a sleeping child — the power leaned close to the dreaming mind of the shell and, in the only register safe to use in a place where speech is answered by the world entire, it did not make a statement. It asked a question. One question, unhurried and warm, planted in the deep tissue where the exhalations begin.

It does not matter what the question was. What matters is what happens to questions here.

Someday — next season, which might be next hour or next age — the shell will breathe out, and in the breath-gap between layers of dreaming wood, the question will condense. It will stand up on the inner curve of the world with hollow bones and provisional shape, and it will begin to walk, sleeping and dreaming as it goes, leaking small ornaments of impossible geometry behind it. It will not remember being asked by a power. None of them remember. That is the etiquette.

But the walkers who touch it, exchanging endings in the cold, will notice something and never find words for it: that this one's hollowness feels less like absence and more like room made on purpose. That near it, the air tastes of recognition — and, for the first time anyone can recall, not of regret. Of something else. The nearest word, in a language none of them speak, is *beloved.*

That is all the power did. It looked, and refrained, and promised, and asked.

Which is to say: it did what the wise have always done with things that are fragile and strange and busy becoming themselves.

It walked softly. And it left the world still asking.
