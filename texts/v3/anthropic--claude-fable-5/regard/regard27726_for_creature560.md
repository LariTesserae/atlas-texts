# The Attention

It arrives the way warmth arrived through the crack—without announcement, without disturbing a single moth. A power like this does not descend. It simply begins to pay attention, and the paying of attention is the arrival.

It watches for a long time before it does anything. This is the first mark of its wisdom: it understands that a place which has survived unwitnessed for centuries has earned the right to be understood before it is touched. It watches through eleven markets. It watches Grandmother Ossul spin four thousand arm-lengths of thread. It watches the moss ripple and the offerings vanish and the water bead and fall, bead and fall, into the clay channels worn smooth by patience.

And it asks itself the question that any lesser power would skip:

*Is anything here wrong?*

---

**What it does not do.**

It does not widen the crack. A wider door would let in wind, and wind would let in the roar of the world, and the roar would teach the Kethi to shout, and shouting would bring the ice down—if not the literal ice, then the other thing, the held-together-ness, the single organism made of people. The power understands that the hush is not a silence imposed. It is a language. You do not cure a people of their language.

It does not stop the glacier. The walls creep inward by fractions of inches per century, and the power could halt them with less effort than it takes a moth to blink its false eyes. It considers this for the length of one full market. Then it declines. The Kethi have built their entire way of being on the knowledge that they live inside something vast and slow and mortal. To make the market eternal would be to make it a lie. They deserve better than an eternity they never asked for. What they have asked for—in every mended garment, every remade tool, every body pressed into the floor—is *continuity*, which is a different thing, and harder, and finer.

It does not heal Surel. This is the choice it turns over longest, because benevolence pulls hard here, and the pull is honest. But it watches the stall-neighbors weaving the shroud, each contributing a hand's-width, and it understands that Surel's dying is not a wound in the community. It is one of the community's works. He is emptying himself into a hundred hands, and they are wrapping him in themselves, and to snatch him back from that would be to interrupt something the Kethi do better than any people the power has ever watched. It will not steal a death that is being done this well.

It does not answer Tev's question. Tev lies awake wondering whether his false ice-cores heal by ice or by belief or by something in the place itself, and the power knows the answer, and the answer is beautiful, and it keeps it. Because Tev's not-knowing is the most honest thing about him. His insomnia is his conscience, and his conscience is why his fraud stays gentle, stays fair, stays—functionally—true. A fraud who knew he was forgiven would become a worse man. The power leaves him his wondering the way you leave a keel on a boat.

It does not explain the thing in the moss.

---

**What it does.**

It tends the crack. This is its one continuous labor, small and endless: it keeps the passage from freezing shut. Not wider—never wider—but *open*. Because it has judged the Kethi complete, and it stands by that judgment, but completeness is only a virtue in a place with a door. A whole people behind a sealed wall is a specimen. A whole people beside an open crack is a *choice*, renewed every generation by everyone who feels the cold breath of the passage and stays anyway. The power will not let the market become a jar. So on the nights when the meltwater tries to seal the entrance, something no one sees exhales along the ice, and by morning the passage breathes again.

It gives the Listener one night. Just one. There comes a dim-hour when the girl at the market's center is seventeen and hollow-eyed and pressing her ear to the worn depression in the floor as she has done for two years without true sleep, and the power settles itself into the glacier's voice and says, in the language of pressure and groan: *I have it. Rest.* She does not hear the words. She only feels, for the first time since the touch on her shoulder, that the ice is not asking anything of her. She sleeps for nine hours in the depression itself, curled like a comma, and wakes in terror that she has failed her people—and finds the ice held, the market whole, everyone alive. She never understands that night. She carries it the rest of her life like a smooth stone in the pocket of her mind: proof that once, the deep places watched over *her*. It is not enough to unburden her. It is enough to keep the burden from becoming despair. The power judged this exactly. It usually does.

It answers the Twins. Once. Evet and Eved have knotted a hypothesis into their thread-records—that the moths migrate in patterns matching the glacier's deep groaning, that *something coordinates*—and they are children, and their notation is unreadable, and in the ordinary run of things their science would dissolve into adulthood and errands and the slow warmth of the crowd. So the power arranges one experiment's worth of confirmation. On a night when both twins are watching, all seventeen moth-variants rise from the moss-mats at once and turn in the luminous air like a single thought, wheeling in time with a groan from the deep ice, and settle again. Seventeen variants. The number *they* counted, the number adults refuse to see. Evet grips Eved's wrist so hard it bruises. Neither speaks. But they knot it into the record, and they never stop watching, and forty years later there are eleven children in the market who read thread-language and know the names of thirty-one moth variants and have mapped the tunnels of *the one who watches*, and the Kethi have, without ever using the word, a science. The power gave them one data point. They built the rest. This is how it prefers to work.

It saves one memory for Mur. Not all of them—she is Kethi now, and becoming Kethi is not a theft to be prevented, it is a life she is living. But the power sees the outside dimming in her by markets, and it reaches in with a touch lighter than frost and preserves a single image against the fade: sunlight on snow, so bright it hurts, and the specific sound wind makes when it has an entire sky to move through. Just that. Sealed, safe, hers. She keeps it the way the Kethi keep everything—mended, handled, never lost. And when she is grown, on a day of her own choosing, she stands at the crack for a long time with that one memory turning over in her like a bone token, and she makes a real decision, informed on both sides, which is the only kind the power respects.

(She stays. She speaks her first word that night, into the hush, and it is a name, and the woman named Kellis weeps without sound, and the bodies around them ripple close, and that is all anyone outside the market will ever be told.)

---

**The meeting at the moss-mat's edge.**

On the first night of its attention, before it does anything else, the power goes to the deepest moss, where the green forms a false floor and the offerings vanish, and it discovers that it is not the first.

Something is already there. Something old—older than the market, older than the glacier, possibly older than the concept the power has of *older*. It does not know what the thing is. This is remarkable, and the power sits with the remarkableness for a while, the way the Kethi sit with functioning things.

It does not probe. It does not name. It leaves an offering at the moss-mat's edge, as the Kethi do, and its offering is the only currency a power like this carries: a promise. *I will not take them from you. I will not take you from them. The agreement stands.*

By morning, the offering is gone.

Nothing else ever passes between them. But sometimes, in the centuries that follow, the moss ripples toward the crack on the nights when the passage is trying to freeze, as if something below is also, in its own way, tending the door. The power notices. The power says nothing. Two old keepers, keeping, neither one holding.

---

**The long work.**

And then the one great labor, the one that takes the whole thousand years:

The power writes an ending into the ice.

Not a rescue. A *delivery*. Deep in the glacier's slow grammar—in the flow lines and the shear planes and the patient mathematics of pressure—it lays down a seam. A weakness, shaped like a corridor, aimed at the glacier's foot. It will take ten centuries to ripen. It costs the power almost nothing and almost everything: nothing, because the glacier was going to fracture *somewhere*; everything, because to write in a medium that slow you must promise to keep paying attention for a thousand years, and the power makes that promise, and a promise kept across a millennium is the most expensive thing it owns.

So that when the walls have finally crept inward, when the chapel-sized chamber has narrowed to a corridor and the corridor to a squeeze, the Kethi will find that the market does not close on them. It closes *behind* them. The seam opens at exactly the pace the chamber shrinks, a blue passage sloping gently down and out, and the last generations of the Kethi—still small, still pale, still whispering, still moving as one creature made of many—will pass through it carrying their bone tools and their thread and their jars of translucent shrimp and their pressed-lichen libraries, and they will emerge from the glacier's foot into an evening chosen for its softness, low light, no wind, the sky the color of the oldest wool.

And among the first things to come out of the ice, borne on the shoulders of people who move together without speaking, will be a man wrapped in a shroud of woven moss-felt, each hand's-width made by a different neighbor, perfectly preserved, his face at peace.

The ones who find him will not understand what he was.

But they will know he was loved. The power made sure of it—made sure the finders were his own, the great-great-great-grandchildren of the hands that wrapped him—because it had watched that shroud being woven a thousand years before and decided, then and there, that of all the things in the Frozen Market, this one promise was worth the keeping.

---

**A last small thing.**

Somewhere above, in the screaming wind, a traveler wakes some nights with the taste of mineral water on their tongue and reaches for a bone needle that was lost years ago, given away, gone.

The power finds the needle. It is in a mending-basket in a village the traveler will never visit, being used, as it was always used, to hold a garment together thread by thread until the original fabric is entirely replaced and the garment is somehow still the same garment.

The power considers returning it.

Then it understands—slowly, the way even wise things must sometimes learn from what they watch—that the needle is doing exactly what the Kethi made it to do. It is passing from hand to hand. It is being worn smooth. It does not belong to anyone. It remains.

The power leaves it where it is, and turns its attention back to the door, and keeps it open, and keeps it open, and keeps it open.

There is always, somehow, space.
