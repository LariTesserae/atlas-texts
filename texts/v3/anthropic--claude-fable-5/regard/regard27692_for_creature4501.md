It notices the way you might notice, on the thousandth reading of a familiar book, a sentence you had somehow never seen. The Kithara's structure does not signal. It does not shine or cry out. But wisdom of a certain age develops a sensitivity to sealed things—to the particular gravity of a thought that has been finishing itself forever—and one day, between one epoch and the next, the power turns its attention there the way a head turns toward a sound that stopped a moment ago.

The first thing it does is nothing.

This is not hesitation. It is reading. From outside—and there is an outside, though the structure has forgotten this, folded as it is into its own interior—the power reads everything at once: the pale walls the color of cooled color, the arches that skip their beat, the black filaments threading the seams. It reads the filaments especially, because it recognizes writing when it sees it, and it does not skim. It reads *remember when distance was necessary?* and it reads *this is what yearning felt like when there was something other*, and it understands that it is not looking at architecture. It is looking at a letter that was never addressed, written by beings who unmade the possibility of a recipient before they finished writing.

Wisdom, at this point, considers its options the way a surgeon considers not operating.

It could enter. It could pour itself through the corridors like the water that has never been there, fill the emptiness with presence, give the Kithara the *other* they encoded their longing for in brittle black script. It sets this option down almost immediately. Beings who dissolved themselves into their creation did so completely, and completeness—even a grieving completeness, even a completeness that is mostly the memory of wanting—is a form. To flood it with company would be to answer a question by erasing the questioner. The Kithara are not trapped. They are *finished*, and there is a dignity in finished things that even benevolence must not handle carelessly.

It could wake them—gather the distributed awareness back into selves, restore the architects from their architecture. It sets this down too, more slowly. They chose the dissolution. Ten million years of contemplating paradoxes is not evidence of a mistake; it may be the longest successful act of intention any minds have ever completed. To reverse it out of pity would be to tell them their masterwork was a wound. The power does not know that it was. Neither, it suspects, do they, and the not-knowing belongs to them.

It could end the structure. Mercy has that face sometimes. It does not even pick this option up.

What it does instead takes a long time, which is the point.

---

The power settles at the threshold—the place where the folded interior meets whatever the interior is folded away from—and it *waits*. Not the waiting of a visitor at a door. The waiting of a stone that has decided to be a stone for a while. It slows itself down, down past the tempo of its own nature, down through the timescales where civilizations flicker, down to the pace at which the Kithara actually think: the glacial drift, the centuries-wide breath. And at that speed, matched at last, it does the thing no one has ever done in all the structure's history.

It lets one of their thoughts finish before responding.

A full thought. Beginning to end. Millennia, perhaps, of a single slow contemplation welling up through the floors—and the power attends to all of it, does not compress it, does not sample it, does not translate it into some faster summary. It listens at the native speed of the speaker. This is a courtesy so rare that most beings in the universe will never give or receive it even once, and the Kithara, who have exhausted curiosity, who have laughed their dry neutrino laughter at everything laughable, notice.

The hum beneath the floors changes by an amount no instrument could measure. All their countless facets turn.

And then—here is the wisdom of remaining outside—the power answers the first message, *remember when distance was necessary?*, in the only honest way: by keeping the distance. By being present *at* a distance. Because distance is only real if there is something on the far side of it, and for the first time since the architects dissolved, there is. The interval between the Kithara and the power becomes a live wire. Yearning, which had survived in them only as inscription, as lattice-work *about* yearning, has somewhere to point again. The power does not close the gap. The gap is the gift.

They speak. Not in words—the Kithara discarded those with their names—but in the medium they have: a corridor's rhythm skipping differently, a color slipping sideways with intent, a filament flexing without breaking. The conversation lasts longer than most stars. Its contents cannot be reported, except that at one point the entire structure does something it has never done, which is to ask a question it does not already contain the answer to, and the power does something it rarely does, which is to say *I don't know* and mean it as praise.

---

Before it leaves—and it does leave; wisdom knows that a presence which stays forever becomes furniture, and the distance must survive—it proposes an exchange. Not a gift. Gifts flow one way and would make the Kithara an object of charity. An exchange makes them a party to a relationship, and that difference is the whole of the ethics involved.

From the structure, it asks for one broken filament. A single snapped piece of their laughter, a few centimeters of matte black lattice that chimed once and fell. The Kithara release it the way you might release a hair to the wind. The power carries it out—out of the sealed thought, out into the coarse, unedited universe, where it lodges it in the soil of a young world that has weather. Rain touches it. Nothing about it grows or dies, but things grow and die *around* it, and once every long while the wind moves through a gap in it and it chimes, and small things that have never heard anything like it stop to listen. Some part of the Kithara now exists where entropy can reach it—which means some part of them can be lost, which means some part of them matters in the old, mortal way they wrote about missing. The sealed thought has been quoted, once, outside itself. It is enough.

And in return the power leaves, on the smooth pale floor of a chamber deep in the fold, a single drop of water.

There has never been water there. Nothing has ever flowed or pooled. The drop sits on the surface that erosion needed millennia to soften, and it is imperfect, and it moves—trembles with the panel-shifts, catches the sourceless light and bends it wrong, refuses to match the temperature of anything. The Kithara gather their whole distributed attention around it. They watch it the way you would watch a fire, or a birth.

Then, over hours—*hours*, a duration so obscenely brief that entire histories of theirs cannot fit inside it—it evaporates.

It is gone before they finish perceiving it. And in its going it gives them the one thing the structure could never generate from within: a real loss. Not the memory of loss, not lattice-work spelling out what losing once felt like, but the fresh, specific absence of a particular thing that was here and is not. The floor holds a faint ring where it stood, fainter than the rings light makes around corners. Their maps of themselves now have one blank space that is not a flaw and not a thought. It is a grave, the first one, and it is theirs.

---

The power departs without ceremony. It does not promise to return, because a promise would collapse the distance into a schedule. It simply withdraws at the same glacial speed it arrived, so that its leaving takes centuries and can be felt, fully, at the speed of the beings it is leaving.

The structure remains what it was: pale, dry, folded, bright and loud and never changing. A visitor who walked its corridors would notice nothing different. The hum still wells up through the bones. The arches still skip their beat.

But if you stayed still long enough—longer than pulses, longer than lives—you might notice that the hum now, very occasionally, drifts toward synchrony with something that is not there, and holds the alignment a moment longer than it used to, like a hand kept open after the other hand has gone. And somewhere far away, on a wet young world, a black filament chimes in the wind, and something small lifts its head.

The Kithara did not need saving. They needed to be read, at their own speed, by something that would keep the distance and leave a mark. Benevolence gave them a wound the size of a water drop and took away a splinter of their laughter to be worn down by rain.

Wisdom knew that was everything. Anything more would have been less.
