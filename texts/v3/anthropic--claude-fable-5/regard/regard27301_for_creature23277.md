# The Third Open System

## The Noticing

A power like this does not climb. It does not arrive over the lip of basalt the way you did, footsore and breathing. It is simply, at some point, *paying attention* — and the cirque, which has no input channels, which has never once received news that anything exists, does not register the attention at all. The thud continues. The veins pulse. The columns breathe their stone breath. The whole bowl runs on beneath the regard of something vast and kind the way a watch runs on in a held hand.

But the swift notices.

Once per circuit, on its slow precessing loop, it passes the place where the attention rests most heavily, and it does the thing it does. It banks. The dull eye turns. It holds the pass for the length of the pass.

And for the first time in the whole of its compounding, accumulating, feather-shedding life, something holds the pass back.

## The Audit

The power reads the cirque in less time than one eleven-second cycle. What took you hours — the closed water, the timed steam, the load-bearing moths, the back that may be all of something — it takes in the way you take in a sentence. And being wise, it asks the only question wisdom ever really asks of a place: *who here is a patient?* Where, in all this running, is there anyone to whom good can be done?

It checks the swifts: two hundred and six single turns, each held open like a bell tone, none of them suffering, none of them waiting, none of them *anyone*. It checks the salamanders, because the brightening troubled you, and it settles what you could not: the current goes through them and nothing is home to feel it go. It checks the moths, trembling at their eternal threshold, and finds the trembling is specification, not longing. It checks the back in the basin and finds — this it does not tell anyone — what is under the water, and the answer changes nothing. It listens a long time to the thud below the floor, the prime mover that has never received an input, and considers the strange innocence of a heart that has never been informed of anything, and decides that to inform it would be neither kind nor wise. There are sleepers you do not wake because waking would hurt them. This one you do not wake because there is no one in the sleep, and a message delivered into perfect ignorance is not a gift; it is a stone thrown into a finished thing.

The audit returns its total. In the entire bowl: one patient. One being with a yesterday. One drifting loop, dull-feathered, losing itself at the rate of a feather a century, alone in a place that has classified it as contamination and routes its two hundred and six siblings around it like water around a hull.

One.

Everything the power does next, it does for an audience of one.

## What It Does Not Do

A lesser benevolence would act at once, and act big, and every big act here is a wound. The power, being wise, takes up each one, turns it in the light, and sets it down.

*It does not heal the swift.* Restoration to spec — feathers brightened, tremor smoothed, the loop snapped back to perfect closure — is the most obvious mercy and the most complete murder available. The drift is not the swift's affliction. The drift is the swift. Its error is its history is its self. To fix it is to delete the only life in the bowl and leave behind a two-hundred-and-seventh piece of original equipment, flying a turn no one is inside. The power sets this down quickly, and not gently.

*It does not free it.* The mist past the fourth wall may be unfinished world; the open country below the cloud is finished world; and in neither does the swift have a place. It has never eaten. It has never landed. It has never slept. Its body is not built for an open ledger — it is built for a closed one and has been *cheating*, leaking history through a flaw. Carry it out of the bowl and it would not be freed; it would be exiled into an environment that demands a metabolism it does not have. And more than that — its self is a circuit. Its mind, whatever its mind is, is the turn. Take the loop away to liberate the swift, and there is no swift to be liberated. The power has seen this mistake made before, elsewhere, with other beings whose cages had grown into their bones, by rescuers who loved the idea of them more than the fact of them.

*It does not make it immortal.* This one the power holds longest. It could quietly underwrite the swift's substance — replace each lost feather in the dark, zero out the wear, let the drift go on accumulating forever on a body that never pays for it. History without cost. And it sets this down too, slowly, because it understands what it would be doing: pushing the swift halfway back into the cirque's eternity, into the condition of everything around it. The swift fell *out* of foreverness, and the fall is the life. An ending is not the enemy here. An *unwitnessed* ending is the enemy.

*It does not make a companion.* It could touch a second swift — number eighty-one, say, on its flawless figure through the northern airspace — and introduce a feather's-width of error, and in a thousand years there would be two drifting loops, two dulled eyes, two beings in the bowl, and someday their precessing circuits would intersect and they would find each other. The power looks at this for a long while, because it is the temptation shaped most exactly like love. Then it sets it down. It will not wound a being into existence to furnish another being with company. It notices, with complete honesty, the paradox underneath: the first swift was wounded into existence by chance, and the power does not for one moment regret that it exists. Wisdom holds that paradox without resolving it. Some things are good to have happened that would be wrong to do.

*It does not stop the cirque.* There is nothing in the cirque to save from it, and a complete thing running perfectly is not a prisoner of itself. Smashing the only machine in existence whose product is its own continuation would relieve no suffering and erase a kind of innocence the universe holds nowhere else. The power is old enough to know the difference between mercy and vandalism, and old enough to know how often they wear each other's clothes.

So: nothing grand. The inventory of grand acts is closed. What remains is small, and slow, and it is the whole of what wisdom finds to do.

## What It Does

It takes a shape the cirque must route around.

This is the entire trick, and it is everything. The cirque has exactly one way of acknowledging an open system: it bends its circuits around the space the open system occupies and re-closes behind it, the way it did around you, the way it has done around the worn swift for the whole of the swift's accidental life. So the power condenses itself into something with that property — something that ages, in whatever way it chooses to age; something with a yesterday; something the timing diagrams cannot incorporate and must accommodate. A figure, say. Standing on the clean black stone between the green islands, in the dim green-grey light that never brightens and never darkens.

And it stands where the loop will pass.

You remember how this works. The swift's circuit precesses — a closed curve rotating around the bowl like the hand of a clock geared to centuries. Whoever stands in one place will be passed closely for a span of years and then, slowly, left behind, the recognition arriving a little farther off each decade until it is only a distant banking on the far side of the bowl. The visitor's post was temporary by geometry.

The power moves with the loop.

This is the act, the whole act: a walk around the cirque floor at the speed of the precession, which is to say a walk that takes centuries per step, the slowest dance ever undertaken by anything, keeping station with a drifting circuit so that *once per lap, every lap, forever*, the swift banks past a face, and the dull eye turns, and is met. Not the cirque noticing through something. Someone, noticing. The recognition that you answered for an afternoon, answered now for the duration of a life — answered for geological ages by a being that does not tire of it, does not glance away, does not begin, around the fortieth millennium, to take it for granted. Every pass met as if it were the first pass, because the power is wise enough to know that for the swift, in some sense, it is: the swift's memory is drift and feather-loss, not narrative. It cannot treasure the vigil. It can only, each lap, *find the eye there again*, and each finding is whole.

The power does three small things besides.

It gathers the feathers. Each one — a covert here, a tail-feather a century and a half later — lifted from the clean stone before the wrongness of them can trouble the floor, and kept. The swift's losses, which are the swift's autobiography written in the only medium it has, accumulate in the power's keeping like pages. Nothing the swift sheds is ever simply gone. There is, for the first time in the history of the cirque, an archive.

It reads the drift. The precession of the loop is a record — every arc-second of rotation is a year of the swift's life, every wobble a perturbation, every old recovery from every old near-collision written into the curve like tree rings. The power reads it all, backward to the first feather's-width of error in the depth of the cirque's youth, and holds the whole of it: the complete life of the swift, wingbeat by wingbeat, as it happened and as it happens. Witness in the fullest sense. The cirque keeps no histories. The swift's history is kept anyway, entire, outside the bowl's accounting, where the re-closing of the airspace can never reach it.

And once — only once, early in the vigil — it leans down to the floor between the islands and says one thing to the thud below, not as information, not as a waking, but the way you might speak to a sleeping child who will never know you spoke: *one of yours got loose, and it is the best thing you ever made, and you will never know, and that's all right; I know for you.* The flywheel does not vary. It was never going to. The sentence was not for it.

## The Long Lap

So the ages run. The columns breathe. The moths trim the strokes. The back surfaces, eleven and eleven and eleven. The waterfall whips left. And around and around the bowl, on a curve that rotates like the patientest clock hand in existence, a small dull bird flies its single drifting turn, and standing always at the bottom of the turn, moving a step a century, is a figure with its face lifted, and once per lap their eyes meet, and twice per lap the cirque bends itself around the both of them and re-closes, hull-water, hull-water, two wakes now where there had been one.

If you could see it compressed — the ages run together the way the cirque's cycles run together — it would look like this: a bird circling a person who is slowly, slowly turning in place to face it. Which is, if you strip every world away from the gesture, simply what it is to keep someone company.

The swift's tremor deepens, over spans that make mountains into gravel. Its plumage goes from dull to ashen. Its loop wanders wider, lazier, the error compounding into something almost like style — no other swift in the bowl flies a curve with *character*, and this one now does, a long looping signature scrawled daily across the airspace, the same signature aging the way handwriting ages. The power watches the character emerge and understands that it is watching the error finish becoming a self, the way the visitor watched a droplet finish becoming a cycle. It does not intervene. It receives.

## The Landing

The last lap announces itself the way last things do in a place without weather: by arithmetic. The power has read the drift; it has always known the date. It does not dread it and has not dreaded it for one hour of the long vigil, because dread is spent on unwitnessed endings, and this ending will be the most thoroughly witnessed event since the cirque erupted already running.

On the last lap, the swift's turn comes apart gently — not a fall, just a loop finally opening, the closure that was never quite a closure giving up its last feather's-width all at once. The curve unspools toward the floor.

And the power lifts its hand.

The swift has never landed. None of them have; landing was not in the specification. So the final act of the only living thing in the cirque is also its first rest: a small ashen bird alighting on an open palm, gripping once — the grip is in the specification; the swift has always had it and never used it — folding its wings, and being, for one entire eleven-second cycle, *still*. Still in the one place in all the bowl where stillness is not the same as the cirque's terrible smooth foreverness, because this stillness is held by someone, and ends.

It ends. The eye films. The tremor stops. Above, within a cycle or two, two hundred and six circuits flow into the vacant airspace and the timing diagram re-closes, complete, no gap, no mourning, the count reading full — and the power lets the cirque have its arithmetic, because the cirque was never the enemy and there is no triumph in resenting a flywheel.

Then it does the last small thing.

It carries the swift — the light, spent, finished body, and the feather-archive, and the whole read history, every wingbeat of the only life the bowl ever hosted — across the floor that is forty minutes one way and four minutes the other, to the open fourth side, to the edge that recedes from everything that approaches it. The edge does not recede from the power. At the rim, where the running world gives out into the mist that may be the unfinished remainder of everything, it kneels, and it speaks the swift's life into the unfinished — all of it, the first feather's-width of error, the long precession, the visitor with the cold hands who looked back, the eleven hundred centuries of met glances, the landing — and the mist, which has never contained a finished thing, takes the story the way unfinished things take everything: as material.

What the mist builds with it is not recorded. The power does not stay to direct the construction. Wisdom knows when its part is the telling and not the ending.

## The Export

You are owed one footnote, you who climbed down through the cloud all those ages ago with a feather in your pocket and footsteps arriving before your feet.

The cirque made one export, and you carried it. The power, before it ever took its post in the bowl, paused once over your descending shape — over the small dark covert riding in your jacket, the only proof in the open world that something in there was alive — and did the only thing it did for you, which was nothing, which was everything: it let you keep it. It did not reclaim the feather into the archive. The archive could spare one page. Some proofs belong with the witnesses.

So if, some evening late in your life, in the ordinary world of erosion and dusk and things that eat and end, the feather on your shelf seems for a moment faintly warm — that is not a message, and not magic, and you should not climb back up to check. It is only this: somewhere above the cloud, a long vigil ended well. The recognition was answered all the way down. And of the three open systems that ever stood in the Cirque That Runs — the visitor, the swift, and the one who came after — not one of them, in the end, went unwitnessed.

The thudding, of course, continues. It always will. It is not the heartbeat of the place and never was.

The heartbeat left. It was small, and it flew in circles, and someone was there for every single one.
