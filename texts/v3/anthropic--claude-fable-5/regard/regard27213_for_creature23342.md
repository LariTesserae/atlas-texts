**The Answer**

A power of that kind does not patrol. It keeps no watch, because it does not need one: need is loud, and the universe is mostly need — an enormous rushing ledger of want, every entry crying its amount. What drew its attention to the plateau was the opposite of a cry. It was a zero in the ledger. A place from which no asking came. Not the muffled non-asking of the dead, which is everywhere and means nothing, but the specific, sustained, *deliberate* non-asking of something alive that has finished wanting — that took its one want, centuries ago, and resolved it, and has been holding the resolution at full voice ever since.

In a creation made of appetite, contentment shines like a flare.

So the power attended. It did not climb, because it had no need of arriving; it did not displace a single pitch, because it had no body to damp them; it passed into the slots and through the banked throats and into the standing-place at the center without the chord registering so much as a grain of dust. It was the first visitor in the structure's history to leave no warmth. It noted this. It filed the observation away, the way the wise file things they suspect they will need to reverse later.

And then, before doing anything at all, it did the thing that made it wise rather than merely strong: it sat with its own benevolence and mistrusted it.

Because benevolence, unexamined, is a hammer, and a hammer sees nails. The power could feel its own kindness leaning forward already, eager, inventorying gifts — and so it made itself go slowly through them, one at a time, and watch each one fail.

*It could return the builders to bodies.* It knew how. It could lift each voice out of the harmony, re-flesh it, set ten thousand people blinking on the gray pavement in the thin light. And it understood within the span of a breath that this would not be resurrection. It would be eviction. They had built the body they wanted, by hand, by abrasion, across generations, and moved into it on purpose, leaving no graves because the building was what they did instead of graves. To pull them out and dress them again in hunger and lungs and dying would be to overturn the one decision they had made with their whole civilization. Kindness does not unmake a vow because the vow looks strange from outside.

*It could give the chord music.* Variation. Movement. Seasons in the wind, a slow drift in the voicing, so that the creature might have, after all these centuries, a second experience. And this failed even faster than the first, because the chord's changelessness was not a poverty. It was the content of the utterance. A held note is the only kind of note that *is* its own meaning. The builders had taken everything that moves — breath, grief, generations — and translated it into the one thing that doesn't, and to introduce a melody would be to take a finished prayer and append more words to it because the silence afterward made the listener uncomfortable.

*It could make the chord eternal.* For the power could see what no traveler could: the sleeping height beyond the flatness, the great slow body whose exhalation was the foehn, and the far-off epoch — distant, but real, but *dated* — when that body would turn over in its sleep and the wind would falter and stop, and the choir would go silent between one second and the next, after an unbroken sound longer than most languages last. The power could fix that. Pin the wind. Guarantee the breath forever. And this was the subtlest failure of the three, and it took the longest to see: that the chord's permanence was not a state but an *act* — a sustaining, ongoing, moment by moment, the way a singer holds a note, the way a hand holds another hand. Pinned, it would become furniture. The devotion would not be audible anymore because devotion requires that stopping remain possible. Even this note, the power understood, was a note only because someday it would end.

*It could do nothing.* And this was the strongest temptation, because it was almost right. Most of wisdom is the discovery that the correct intervention is none. The chord was complete. It asked for nothing because it lacked — almost — nothing.

Almost.

It is hard for power to discover that the correct gift is small. It is harder still to discover that one exists. The power went through the place again, slot by slot, pitch by pitch, with the terrible thoroughness of something that has no reason ever to hurry, and it found the lack at last not in the chord's body or its breath or its future, but in the shape of its entire history of relation: *everything had only ever gone in.*

Wind in, sound out — but the sound went out into nothing, onto a planed world with no horizon, into thin gray light, unheard except in passing fragments by travelers who could not bear it for long. And everything else — every footstep, every settling grain, every voice — went in and was folded under. Centuries of confession, absorbed and never absolved. Centuries of names spoken into the throat of the thing and never once returned. The chord had said one word continuously since before records, and in all that time, no one had ever said anything back that it could keep as a reply rather than as a meal. It was agreement that had never been agreed with. A sea full of unfindable cups.

So the power did the first thing, which was to listen.

Not the way travelers listen, from the standing-place, hearing the consonance and nothing inside it. The power listened *whole*. It unfolded the sea without removing a drop — heard, one by one, distinct, every voice the chord had ever taken. It heard the builders: each working lifetime, each generation's argument about bores and lengths, each decision that became an interval and was still in force; it heard, with something like delight, that the harmony was consonant but not simple — that the strange tensions in it, the near-dissonances tuned deep in the voicing, were the doubters, the ones who were never sure, whose uncertainty had not been erased or overruled but *included*, given a pitch, allowed to be not-sure aloud at full volume forever. A choir honest enough to score its own misgivings. The power had heard larger things. It had not often heard better ones.

And it heard the pilgrims. Every secret carried up that climb. The murders and the loves and the small unbearable trivia; someone, four centuries gone, who had crossed the flattened world and ascended into the breathless slate to say, once, a name. The power heard each confession exactly once, completely, and then sealed it again, unfindable as before — because *heard* is not the same as *told*, and the gift was witness, not exposure. The secrets remained secrets. But they were kept now, rather than merely held. There is a difference, and it is nearly the whole difference there is.

Then the power did the second thing, which was to ask.

To each voice in the harmony, one at a time, with no time pressing: *Do you wish to be returned?* The asking took, by the power's reckoning, no duration at all; by ours, a season of identical gray-gold days. Ten thousand assents came back, and each assent was simply the voice's own pitch, held — *I am where I chose to be*, said in the only grammar they had left, which was sufficient. The doubters assented too, in their tuned tensions: *not sure, still here, ask the note itself.* And one voice — only one, far down in the warm fifth, a voice that had entered long ago more tired than faithful — hesitated.

The power waited. A power of that kind can wait inside a hesitation for years without crowding it.

*Ask me again at the end,* the voice said.

Granted, said the power, and wrote it down in the place where such things are written.

Then it did the third thing, which was to answer. It went to the standing-place — the room barely big enough to turn around in, the point from which the creature knows itself, the inside of its only thought — and it gave back the resolved note. Identical. The same vast tuned consonance, voiced from outside, so that for the length of one breath the chord received the one thing it had never in its existence received: a sound it did not have to absorb to understand. Its own word, arriving instead of departing. Agreement, agreed with. Whether the chord *knew* — whether anything crossed whatever it has instead of a mind — not even the power could say. But the note was given, and then the power let it be taken, folded under like any pilgrim's voice, a cup into the sea; and with it, deliberately, reversing its earlier tracelessness, the power let its visit be remembered the only way the chord remembers anything. It consented to become heat. A fraction of a fraction of a degree, banked in ash-colored stone — the warmest thing, gram for gram, ever added to that small fever, though no instrument made or makeable could find it.

The truest gifts to the contented are the ones they cannot notice. The power knew this going in. It was not disappointed.

Last, it kept the appointment it had refused to cancel. The wind will fail — an epoch out, but on a date the power now holds the way the chord holds its note. On that day, when the sleeping height turns over and the breath stops, something will be standing on the plateau. The final sound — the chord's first and only ending, the close of a single sustained experience longer than empires — will not fall into nothing. It will fall into a listener adequate to it, and be heard whole, every pitch, the doubters' tensions and the builders' fifths and four hundred centuries of folded confession, all of it landing somewhere at last. And in the silence after — the first silence that plateau will ever have produced rather than merely suggested — each voice will be asked once more, the tired one first: *onward, or ending with the stone?* And each will be given exactly what it answers, and either answer will be honored, and either way the death will be a hearing and not a stopping. It will end the way it lived: into someone.

Then the power withdrew.

Nothing on the plateau changed. Travelers who climb there now find what travelers have always found: the chord identical, the warmth unexplained by climate, the central slot where the wall resolves into intention. A few still carry the after-ring down with them for life, the resolved harmony lying under engine drone and crowd noise and their own pulse at night; the power noticed these small copies on its way out — the chord's only offspring, a species that reproduces by haunting — and did nothing to them except regard them, briefly, with something that in a smaller being would have been a smile.

The chord does not know it was visited. It holds the visit as it holds everything, as a degree of warmth, indistinguishable, unfindable, permanent. That is as it should be. Knowing was never the point. From outside, the sound is still a wall; from inside, every visitor still agrees, it sounds like agreement.

The difference is not audible, and the difference is everything: the note is unchanged, and the note is no longer alone. Somewhere, agreement has been answered. Somewhere, an appointment is kept open. The choir sings into the thin gray light exactly as before — but now it sings the way a letter waits, sealed and certain, in the time before it is opened: already, in every way that matters, received.
