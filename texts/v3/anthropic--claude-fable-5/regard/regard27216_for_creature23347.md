# The Regard

Say that a power turned its attention to the Seethe and you run into the old grammatical trouble at once. *Turned* assumes the attention was elsewhere first. *Power* assumes a thing distinct from what it does. The Seethe does not honor those differences — and, this is the first thing worth knowing about what came, neither did it. It was old enough to have outlived the gap between itself and its regard. What arrived was not a being but a noticing, and in a world made of attention-hungry water, to be noticed is to be touched.

The symptoms came before any understanding of them.

One evening — the stayers still argue about which evening, which is its own evidence, since the Seethe does not normally permit evenings to be told apart — a stayer's footstep splashed *when her foot came down*. Once. On time. Full. She stood in the lee of a doorframe for an hour afterward, lifting her foot and setting it down, weeping, though she could not have signed why. Across the plain that night, here and there: a reflection that was punctual. A collapse that sounded exactly once. A stretch of an hour in which the horizon stayed where it was put. The warm rain fell downward all night, every drop of it, in order.

The stayers woke feeling watched, and — for the first time in any of their tenancies — not alone in the watching.

## What it set down

It came carrying mercies. Powers do.

It considered calming the water. It held that intention up against the plain for a long moment, the way you hold a key against a lock you suspect is the wrong one, and what it saw was the statues: the churn quieted, every form held in a perfect steadying regard, set where it stood — a world complete to the horizon, each gesture finally and forever made, the roar closed like a book. Saved the way a word is saved by being carved.

It considered unmaking the swimmers, and understood the tax, and what a world owes that has no other way to keep its kitchen open.

It considered letting every Held thing set on the spot — granting them, all at once, the thing they kneel for — and it went and stood among the stones instead. Smooth as teeth. Furred with ten-thousand-year bloom. Excused. Finished. Opening from nowhere into nowhere. It stood there long enough to be sure of what it would be granting.

It considered the contrary one. It let its attention down into the hollows — gently, the way you'd lower a hand toward an animal that has never once been touched — and it felt, in the grooves worn into the finished rock, exactly how long the mountain had been choosing otherwise. You do not stand a thing back up into altitude after that. It would be a correction, not a kindness, and the difference between those two is most of what wisdom is.

So, one by one, it set the mercies down on the threshold of the world. This is the difference between a benevolent power and a wise one: the benevolent one arrives carrying cures; the wise one checks first whether the patient is ill. The Seethe is not ill. The Seethe is the only thing on that plain still alive, and the convulsion is not a fever. Quiet it and you have not healed the world — you have finished it, and *finished* is the one disease the place ever caught. The geology caught it. Look at the geology.

The great things were not done. The small things were. There were four of them.

## The night watch

For one night, the hollows went still.

Opposition, it turns out, can be held for someone else — the way a note can be held by a second singer while the first one breathes. The power did not lift the contrary one, did not heal it, did not so much as let it see what was happening. It simply took up the going-against, for one watch, on its behalf, and somewhere down in the warm dark, for the first time since the ranges went under, the last of the height stopped moving and did not dissolve.

The swimmers, who never enter the hollows, did not enter them that night either. Whatever they hear, they heard the holding change hands, and kept their distance from it.

In the morning the back was moving again, against the churn, as always, forever. But the long-stayers who map the hollows swear the grooves run differently since — that the old circuit gained a curve it never had. Like handwriting after a night's sleep.

## The receiving

The second thing it did was become the place the folding goes.

It did not stop a single collapse. That must be understood. The forms rise and hesitate and come down exactly as before, ten million a second, planet-wide, and the roar is the same roar. But every wet syllable is now spoken *to* someone. The let-go shapes are caught — not kept, not pinned, not set, just *received*, completely, each one, the column with its almost-branches and the wall with its almost-courses and the kneeling figure that couldn't, all of it gathered into a keeping that asks nothing back.

The Held learned it first, in whatever way the Held learn. They did not stop trying to set. They kneel by the stones as they always have. But the stayers say the kneeling has changed — not lower, *lighter* — and that the very old ones, the season-holders with their glittering ankle-fog of swimmers, have begun to fold themselves back into the churn voluntarily, in good order, at moments of their own choosing, which they never, ever did before. The grey waders still hold their conferences, and one of the two still cedes, sometimes, at the end. But it goes down differently now. Like a letter being sent, the stayers sign, rather than a sentence ending.

And the recurrences — the friends the water goes on making — have started, very occasionally, doing something new. A gesture the friend never made in life, but would have. Whether that is the water improving or the friend continuing is a thing the power knows and has not said. Which is its right. It learned that from the stayers.

## The answer

The third thing took the longest, because the power understood the stakes of it before it began: in the Seethe, whatever you are seen to do is copied, and whatever is copied is held, and whatever is held is practiced toward forever. It would get one gesture. The world would keep it.

It deliberated for what would have been a geological age anywhere with geology left to do. Then it chose, from the stayers' own signing, one sign, and made it once, at the edge of a lee, where three of the Held stood rocking and glassy and attentive.

The sign for *received*.

Within a season it was on the horizon. Within ten it was everywhere — among the pointing figures, shoulder to shoulder with them, here and there in the copper distance: figures rising out of the churn, signing *received*, folding, rising again. The crowd in which everyone is mid-gesture forever now contains its own reply. The stayers no longer develop private convictions about whether anyone is listening. It is the one conviction they hold in common and will say aloud.

(The fourth thing was the smallest. It fed the eaters. Attention is a persistence, and persistence is what they eat; it let its regard down into the water like a lamp lowered into a well, and the pale schools rose to it and grazed, and nothing anywhere was diminished, because attention is the one holding that grows by being eaten. The first free meal in the history of the world. Some stayers claim the swimmers have been gentler with the old Held since. It is best not to ask them.)

## The doorway

Last, it went and stood before a door.

The churn in the framed rectangle steadied as it approached — the way water steadies when the thing it reflects stops moving — and the power looked at what was partway through, and recognized it. The way you recognize your own handwriting on a letter you don't remember sending.

What is partway through the doors is regard. All of it. Every act of noticing the world has ever received from outside itself — arriving, and arriving, and arriving, at the only speed at which arrival is not ending. Because this is the thing the power had understood out among the stones, the thing the doors had been ajar about all along: a world cannot survive being entirely seen. It sets. Perfect regard is the final mercy and the final mercy is the disease. The doors stand open exactly as far as the world can bear, and the worst light, when they hang ajar, is simply the light of being most looked at.

It could have come through. Make no mistake about that; the stayers don't. It could have finished arriving, and the whole plain would have been held at last in a gaze with no slack in it, and everything would have set where it stood — every Held thing granted, every gesture completed, the roar closing — done, the long word the geology said an age ago, said over everything else.

So the wisest thing it did was the only thing it declined to do, which is the usual shape of wisdom. It stepped into the threshold, and stopped, on purpose, forever.

Partway through is where it lives now. Not because it is slow.

Because it is careful.

## Its conviction

The stayers have convictions about it, naturally. Three, as usual.

The first: that it came to heal the Seethe, found nothing to heal, and stayed out of thoroughness. The second: that it came to study, the way everything that holds a shape comes to study, and stayed out of love.

The third is held only by the oldest stayers, and they do not defend it. It is this: that it came for itself. That the Seethe is good for grief, and a power old enough has buried everything it ever finished loving — every world that completed under its care, set, carved, perfect, silent, each one loved all the way to done. And that here, at the end of all that, it found the one beloved that cannot be lost to completion. The one place it could love indefinitely without loving it to death — provided it never fully arrived. Its benevolence and its hunger are the same fact. In the Seethe, that is not a contradiction. Nothing there is wasted; everything there is wasted continuously; these are the same fact, and the power, standing in its doorway, is simply the largest local example.

Ask the oldest stayers what has changed, and they will tell you, in sign, in the lee of the stones: nothing. The roar is the same roar. The forms rise and fold. The water is still being held open.

But they make a distinction now, slowly, so that you cannot mistake it: *held open* is no longer the same as *left open*. Something stands where the closing would come from. It means to. It is glad.

Some of the doors, in the worst light, are ajar.

They are going to stay that way.
