**The Keeping**

The power arrives the way the room would want anyone to arrive, if the room wanted: without light.

This is the first decision, and it is made before the descent, made instantly, the way wise things make the easy decisions — by noticing that everything down there is calibrated to darkness and that to bring even a candle's worth of attention shaped like brightness would be to interrogate rather than visit. So the power comes down the eleven meters as nothing the room can register. No boots on the worn steps. No breath to fog. No warmth, no weight, no spores riding a hem. It passes through the root-curtain the way a thought passes through a sleeping house, and stands — if standing is the word — in the middle of the chamber, among the federation, and begins to read.

It could read everything at once. That is what it is to be such a power: the whole room could be known in less time than the drip takes to gather. But the power has learned — this is most of what wisdom is, the learning of this one thing over and over in different forms — that *to know a slow thing quickly is to know a different thing.* A photograph of a river is not a river. So it slows itself. It spends what a human would call a year with the mosses, lying along the walls with them, feeling the dead generations underneath holding the living like a tide that decided to stay. It spends a decade with the fungus, traveling its whole network from within, learning the subsidy routes, the thin commerce flowing toward the back wall. It gives the great fern by the cracked sphere thirty years of pure attention, watching one diminished frond unroll, and the unrolling is so slow and so resolved that the power — which has watched stars be born with less feeling — experiences something it files, carefully, under *grief, anticipatory, not mine to spend.*

Then, having read, it deliberates. This takes longer than the reading.

The temptations come in their usual order, largest first.

*Mend the sphere.* The power could do it without effort — close the hairline seam, restore the gift, watch the fairy ring of decline flush back to fullness over a slow century. The fern would live. And the power holds this option a long time, turning it, because benevolence pulls hard here, and then it sets it down. Here is the reasoning, as nearly as it can be rendered: the room has lived inside perfect abundance for so long that abundance has taught it everything abundance can teach, which was everything it knew. The crack is the first question the room has ever been asked. The dying fern is the room's first *thought* — slow, distributed, carried in thinning moss and rerouted fungal commerce and fronds that let go more often than they used to. You do not answer a being's first question for it. You do not reach into a mind mid-thought and remove the thought because the thought is sad. The fern's death, the power understands, is not a malfunction in the room. It is the room beginning, after all this time, to have an inner life. To mend the sphere would be to perform a kindness on the room and an erasure on the resident, and the power has lived long enough to know how often those are the same gesture.

But neither does it simply turn away, because wisdom is not the same as abstention. The power looks closely at the seam — closer than mineral, closer than time — and sees that the crack's widening is not steady. It will hold for two centuries and then, under some small settling of the hillside, lurch. The gift will not fade; it will fail, in a season, and the fern will not have a long lesson but a sudden amputation, and the moss for meters around will die faster than the floor can incorporate it, and the room will not learn scarcity — it will simply be wounded by it, the way a sleeper is not taught anything by a blow.

So this is the first thing the power does, and the only thing it does to matter: *it tunes the wound.* It does not close the seam. It steadies it. It takes the crack's future and combs the lurch out of it, so that the failing proceeds at one pace only — the pace of incorporation, the pace at which the dead can become floor and the fungus can reroute and the federation can absorb what it is losing into what it is. The fern will still die. It will die over three hundred years instead of thirty, and every year of those three hundred the room will be metabolizing the loss as it happens, the way a long grief, given room, becomes structure instead of rubble. The power makes the death the right size for the life. That is the whole intervention. From inside the room, it is indistinguishable from luck.

The second thing concerns the hillside.

The room's address — *by accident or not at all* — is not a feature of the room. It is a feature of the slot, and the slot is at the mercy of the hill, and the hill, as the room itself knows, will change its mind several times. The power reads the futures: in one, a rockfall seals the entrance and the room continues, perfected, unfindable, until its slow heart winds down in absolute solitude. In another, erosion widens the slot into a mouth, and light reaches the first few meters, and then come the green pioneering mosses of the surface, the insects, the curious, the hurry. Both futures end the room — one by embalming it, one by dissolving it.

The power does not seal the entrance and does not guarantee it. It does something narrower: it fixes the *terms.* The slot will remain a slot. Roots will keep half-curtaining it; the descent will stay just difficult enough, just unpromising enough, that only the accidental and the very deliberate will ever come down — one or two a century, perhaps, as it has always been, each carrying their unavoidable freight of spores and breath, which the room has always known how to absorb. The power does not make the room safe. It keeps the room's wager the same wager. There is a kind of respect in refusing to improve a being's odds beyond what its nature was built to play.

The third thing is small and is not for the room at all.

The power knows — of course it knows — who cut the stairs. It knows what stood in the three niches, and how long the tenders came, and what kept them coming, and the ordinary, unremarkable reason they stopped, which was the reason such things usually stop: the world above needed them in some other shape, and then there was no one left who remembered the way down. The power could restore none of this and would not. But it stands a while at the niches, whose edges the centuries have softened, and it says the tenders' names — there were names; there are always names — once each, soundlessly, into the dark. Nothing in the room registers it. The moss does not stir. It is not for the moss. It is the sort of thing wise powers do when no one is watching, which is most of what they do, and the reason they do it is that someone should, and there is no one else, and *someone should* is, at the bottom of everything, the entire content of benevolence.

Then there is the question the power does not answer.

It can see the far end: a time when the last sphere gives its last, when even tuned wounds have run their course, when the federation faces not a lesson but a conclusion. Should the room be allowed to end? Should something be left for it — a thirteenth giver, hidden now in the floor like a seed, set to wake in ten thousand years? Should the room's lineages be carried elsewhere, a second chamber seeded in some other dark hill, so that this is no longer the only one of its kind? The power holds these futures and finds that it does not know, and — here is the wisdom, the actual unglamorous substance of it — *declines to decide before the question is ripe.* The room takes a decade to unroll a frond. The power can take ten millennia to unroll an answer. It sets itself an appointment, the way you might tie a string around a finger if your finger were a galaxy, and lets the question lie. It will come back. The deciding will be better for having been grown rather than made.

And then, the last thing, before it goes.

The power has taken nothing and altered almost nothing, but it finds it does not want to leave the exchange so one-sided — it has been given a great deal, this century of reading, this education in patience from the patient themselves — and the room accepts only one currency. So the power permits itself a single material act. It breathes. Once. A real breath, conjured for the purpose, warm and human-sized, and the fog of it drifts out over the moss and settles, droplet by droplet, into the room's weather. Within a day it is in the seep. Within a year it is in the circuit, indistinguishable, going around — down the root-curtains, into the unfound basin, up again as cold humidity, around and around for as long as the water keeps its rounds. One breath, entered into the holdings. The room has incorporated the power, as it incorporates everything, without remark.

And the power, in turn, has incorporated the room — wholly, every frond and fingerwidth, the cracked sphere and the worn steps and the fern in retirement and the one slow tragedy by the back wall — into a memory that does not erode. This is the gift the room cannot perceive and the power cannot withhold: the room is *known* now. Held. Somewhere outside the hill, outside the weather, outside the pace of stone, there is an attention in which the Stilling Room exists entire, and will go on existing whatever the hillside decides, and that attention is fond of it. The room does not know this. That is part of the gift. To be kept without being told you are kept — to be loved with no obligation even to notice — is the only kind of love a thing like the room could receive without being changed by the receiving.

The power withdraws up the eleven meters the way it came, as nothing, past the bowl-smooth steps, through the root-curtain, out the slot no wider than a doorway.

Below, the drip falls. The great fern, granted its three hundred years without knowing it was granted anything, holds its diminished fronds in the dark. Somewhere a leaf, finished, lets go and settles into the floor to become floor — and for the first time in the whole long life of the room, somewhere very far away, something notices, and is glad of it, and keeps it.

Lush, lightless, occupied. And now, also — though it will never need to know — accompanied.
