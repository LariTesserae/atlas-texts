**THE WARDEN OF THE EMPTY PLACES**

---

**I.**

The power comes the way such powers come: not down through the seam, which would be a violence, and not in any body, which would be a lie, but simply by turning its attention — which is most of what it is — toward a point on a high red tableland where the stillness has a flaw in it.

It has been crossing the finished places of the world. That is something like its office, or its habit; the two have long since merged. Mountains that have given up their height, seabeds holding the shape of vanished water, plains where the last river's last channel lies fossilized like a vein in marble. The finished places are not sad to it. They are sentences that have reached their period, and there is a rightness in them it has learned to keep company with.

But here the period has a pulse.

It feels the exhalation first — the warm dusk breath rising out of the crack, humid in a land that has forgotten humidity, carrying the smell of fungus gardens and chitin and ten million small lives. And then it does the thing that is its first act of benevolence, the act all the others will depend on, the act most powers in most stories fail at:

It looks until it sees correctly.

Not a remnant. Not a survivor, huddled, awaiting rescue. Not a tragedy of confinement, a poverty, a prison with lichen on the walls. It looks down through the stone the way one reads, and what it reads is a *finished unfinishedness* — a thing that has solved itself so completely that the solution has become a kind of organism. The drum grammar with its nouns of urgency. The water older than the room's own walls, passed mouth to mouth for a hundred thousand generations without once touching open air. The masonry one-fifth citizens. The silver plants leaning their whole slow lives toward a blade of light. The spiders' inherited sky.

It sees that the chamber is not waiting for anything.

And so the question it has carried to a thousand worlds — *what does this need?* — comes back, for the first time in a very long while, with the answer: *nothing. It needs nothing. Now what will you do with your love?*

---

**II. The Gifts Considered, and Set Down**

It is wise, but it is not beyond temptation, because benevolence *is* a temptation — the oldest one, the temptation to give.

So it does what the wise do with temptations. It takes each one out, holds it in the light, and looks at what it would actually be.

**Water.** It could call a single drop into being — one drop, hanging in the gloom below the seam, the first free water the chamber would ever have known. It actually forms the drop. It holds it a long moment in the air above the plateau, a bead of the thing every body in that room is built around the absence of. And then it sees what the drop would be: not a gift but an *event*. A rupture in the founding economy. The first water that belonged to no one, in a world whose entire peace rests on the fact that all water belongs to someone. Mold in the granaries. A new kind of war. A legend, perhaps — and the chamber has no organ for legends, and would have to grow one, and the growing would cost more than the drop was worth.

It pours the drop out on the open plateau instead, where it vanishes in less than a breath, and the dead stone drinks the only rain it will ever get, and tells no one.

**Light.** It could widen the seam by a finger's width. The blade of gold would become a sheet. And the lemur-eyed geckos would flinch from their own parishes, and the spiders' grey commons — four hundred generations of silk — would hang exposed and cooking, and the gloom that is not darkness but *architecture* would be demolished as surely as if it had struck the place with a hammer. The chamber does not live despite the dimness. The dimness is a load-bearing wall.

Set down.

**Escape. Expansion. Doors.** It could open the deep rock, give the city somewhere to grow, connect this room to some other pocket of possibility. But it has seen what the chamber is: a sealed thing, and the seal is not the tragedy, the seal is the *author*. Everything astonishing here — the drum language, the thousand-year queens, the assassins wearing their biographies, the ant-lions who made an entire civilization out of childhood — was written by the closed door. To open it would be to take the pen away.

Set down.

**Deathlessness for the queen.** This one it holds the longest, because the queen's death, when it comes, will be a famine moving outward through the whole room like cold from a doused fire, and the power has felt famines before and does not love them. But it reads the deep strata — the layers of abandonment and rebuilding, the recorded successions — and sees that the chamber has died this death before and digested it and built on top of it, that the famine is not a flaw in the design but one of its organs, the slow heartbeat underneath the fast ones. To remove death from this economy would be to remove the masonry's mortar. The walls are grandmothers all the way down. That is not a horror to be fixed. That is the *bank*.

Set down.

**An answer.** This is the temptation it is most ashamed of, and the one it examines most honestly. It has, by now, learned the drum language entire — it took the power no time at all, and we will come back to what it did with the learning — and it knows it could lean its attention against the stone and *speak*. One rhythm through the rock. It would be heard in every gallery at once. It would be the first sound in the chamber's whole history that came from outside the chamber's own body.

And then it notices the thing that decides it: in a million years of grammar, the drums have no rhythm for *stranger*. No noun for *outside*, no pattern for *other*, no beat for *above*. The language never needed those words, because the world the language describes is complete. To speak to the chamber, the power would first have to teach it the word *other* —

— and that word is the end of the seal, more surely than any pickaxe.

It takes its attention off the stone, gently, the way you take your hand off a sleeping animal.

**Itself.** It will not be known here. Not ever. This is the gift it gives by withholding: the chamber will remain a place where nothing has ever been worshipped, petitioned, thanked, or feared by name — where the silver plants lean toward the light not in prayer but in chemistry, and the chemistry is enough, and no one in the warm dark will ever be made small by the discovery of something larger.

All the gifts are set down. Its hands — it has no hands; say instead its intentions — are empty.

And *now*, with empty hands, it begins its actual work.

---

**III. The Three Offices**

**First, it becomes the Witness.**

Not a watcher — the chamber has never lacked for watching; the assassins watch, the geckos watch, the spiders are nothing *but* watching. What the chamber lacks, what every sealed and mortal thing lacks, is *memory outside itself*. Everything it has ever been is recorded only in its own substance: the pheromone scriptures in the fossil corridors, legible instructions from individuals dead ten thousand years; the gecko skulls spaced through the strata like coins in a foundation; the silk canopy's grey sediment of every wing that ever rose. The chamber is its own only archive, and when the chamber ends, the archive ends with it, and it will be as if the drums had never found their grammar.

So the power reads it. All of it. It descends — attention only, weightless, disturbing not one grain of stone-flour — through the living surface and the buttressed terraces and down into the packed fossil city, and it reads the pheromone trails of queens dead before the geckos came, and it learns the drum language not as data but as a *literature*: the rhythm for collapse, the slow pattern for fresh lichen that travels the tunnels like good news through a town, the regional accents (there are regional accents; the third terrace drums a hair behind the beat, and has for nine thousand years). It memorizes the gecko parishes and their dynasties of border-chirps. It counts the assassins' cloaks, husk by husk, and reads each one like rings in a tree. It learns the names of things that have no names, which means it makes the names, which means the chamber now has, somewhere outside itself, a *telling*.

This is the first office: *it will be told.* Not now. Not to anyone in particular. But the power is very old and very patient, and it keeps what it learns, and somewhere in the long inventory it carries — the finished seas, the spent stars, the sentences that reached their periods — there is now a warm room, complete, every drumbeat of it, safe in a memory that does not erode.

**Second, it becomes the Warden of the Stillness.**

Here is the one material thing it permits itself, and it is careful to understand exactly what it is doing.

The chamber needs nothing from inside. But it sits inside an *outside*, and the outside has futures: the wandering of climates, the patience of ice, the stray violence of the sky, the slow tilt of rivers deciding over megayears where to be born. Any of these could end the chamber in an afternoon — not as a death but as an *accident*, a sentence interrupted mid-word by something that never even noticed it was speaking.

So the power takes up a post. It assumes, formally, the office the dead mountain has been performing by accident for eons: it becomes the plateau's continuing deadness. The guarantor of the boring horizon. It will turn aside the large futures — quietly, at distances of centuries, a storm track nudged, a fracture discouraged, a curious future paw or hammer led elsewhere by a feeling its owner will never examine — not forever, because nothing is forever and the power does not deal in forevers, but for as long as the chamber has life of its own in it.

And it sets the rule of its wardenship with great precision, because this is where benevolence usually rots into possession: *it will protect the chamber from the outside only. Never from itself.* The queen's death-famine will come and will not be softened. The ant raids, the assassins, the pits in the flour, the collapse of galleries, the bad centuries — all of it stays. The power is not keeping the chamber *safe*. It is keeping the chamber's death *honest* — guaranteeing the one thing every mortal arrangement deserves and almost none receives: that when the end comes, it will come from within, in the chamber's own kind, in the chamber's own time. A death that belongs to it, like its water.

The chamber may end of itself. It will not be ended.

**Third — and this is the smallest office, and the one closest to whatever the power has instead of a heart — it becomes the Attendant of Exiles.**

Once or twice a generation, a young gecko, crowded out of the parishes, climbs the seam toward the smell of open air. The chamber has no rhythm for this either; the city simply notes a weather-pattern leaving, and the drums go quiet behind her, and she comes up out of the crack into a world that is one flat hot perfected emptiness from horizon to horizon, and there is nothing for her there — no water, no prey, no shade — and within a day or two she is finished, and the wind takes what it takes.

For the whole history of the chamber, these climbers have died alone in a way nothing inside the chamber ever dies alone — outside the economy, outside the masonry, unincorporated, unwalled, unkept.

The power keeps them now.

It does not save them. It considered that, the way it considered everything — a second chamber, hollowed somewhere, stocked, seeded, an ark for the exiles — and set it down, because a made chamber would be a copy, a terrarium, a zoo of the sacred, and the chamber's whole dignity is that no one made it. But when a young gecko comes up into the light now, golden-eyed and blinking and doomed, the power turns its full attention there, and stays. It walks beside her — say it that way, though nothing walks. It accompanies the last day. It learns her particular gait and the particular pattern of her spots, and it adds her to the telling, by name — the name it makes for her — so that of all the chamber's millions, the only citizens ever to be *known*, individually, by something outside, are these: the ones who climbed toward the light and lost.

When she stops, the power stays until the wind comes. Every one of them, from now on. It is not much. The power knows it is not much. It does it anyway, because wisdom said *nothing can be given here*, and the heart answered, *attendance is not a gift. Attendance is just refusing to let a thing be unaccompanied.* Wisdom, after a long pause, allowed it.

---

**IV. The Communion**

And then there is the one thing it takes for itself, the single thread of relationship it permits across the seal, and it is this:

Every dusk, the chamber exhales. The day's heat lets go; the warm breath of the one living room rises through the crack — fungus and chitin and stone-flour and the faint green smell of ten-thousand-year-old lichen — and goes out across the empty tableland, and for the whole history of the world that breath has gone out into nothing. Into the indifferent dark. A market's worth of life, sighing once a day at an audience of zero.

Now, at dusk, the power leans down over the seam — attention only, taking nothing, adding nothing, not so much as a degree of warmth — and *receives* it.

That is all. The chamber does not know. The chamber will never know. Its drums have no rhythm for *heard*. But the fact is changed, the deep fact underneath all the others: the breath of the one warm room no longer goes out into emptiness. It goes out into attention. The plateau is still a finished sentence, the horizon is still bare, the stillness is still total — the power has seen to that, it is the warden of exactly that — but the stillness is no longer *vacant*. It is the stillness of a listener.

And far below, in the gloom, the work goes on without pause, as it always has: the arcades repaired by morning, the eggs carried like porcelain, the dead walled politely into the architecture, the silver plants leaning all together toward the thin gold blade — toward the outside they have no concept of, which is empty, and guarded, and which now leans, very slightly, back.

Somewhere, ages hence, the last drum will sound, and the room will go quiet, and the seal will have held to the end, and the death will be the chamber's own. And on that evening the power will receive the final exhalation as it received all the others, and then it will go out across the worlds carrying the whole of it — the grammar and the parishes and the queen's slow pulse and the names of the exiles —

and it will tell.

That is what it does. Almost nothing.

It took everything it had.
