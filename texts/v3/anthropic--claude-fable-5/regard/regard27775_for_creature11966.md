# The Regard

## What Arrives

It does not come through the gap. The gap admits bodies, and it has not worn a body in a long time.

It arrives the way understanding arrives: nothing crosses the threshold, and yet the chamber is different. The tiles do not click, because nothing has stepped on them. The walls, which memorize everything, reach out to memorize this new presence and find they cannot take an impression of it—not because it resists, but because it is already giving itself away faster than the clay can grasp. You cannot steal what is being handed to you. The walls have never encountered this before. For a moment, all through the ascending spiral, the fingerprint whorls go still with something that in a creature would be called astonishment.

The heartbeat above stumbles. Once. Then resumes.

And here is the first strange mercy: its attention is not the kind that requires an object. It can perceive verbs directly. So when a handful of wet clay rises toward the wall at the edge of what would be vision, the arriving power *watches*—and the Presser does not cease. For the duration of one press, one rotation of a nine-fingered palm against warm clay, the mountain's dream is seen dreaming. The Presser does not know it is witnessed, because knowing requires a knower and it is only ever a pressing. But the impression it leaves is deeper than the others. Warmer. Generations from now, the pale segmented things will return to that whorl again and again, rasping at a sweetness they cannot name.

To be witnessed without being made into an object. It costs the power nothing. It has never been given here before.

## The First Thing It Does Is Nothing

Wisdom is mostly refraining, and so for a long while—long enough that the pale things below live and die and live—the power simply attends.

It takes an accounting, and the accounting surprises it.

The succulents growing into one another, merging, becoming communal: this is not corruption. This is love, of the vegetable kind, which does not distinguish between embracing and becoming. Let it be.

The pale things eating the impressions from the tiles: this is not desecration. This is forgetting, and forgetting is the mercy that makes room. A mountain that remembered everything would compact itself into a single unbearable meaning and die of significance. The pale things are its exhalation. Let them be.

The heat, the pounding, the wet labor-breath: this is work, and work is innocent. The Pressers wish nothing, harm nothing, are nothing except in the instant of making. There is no cruelty anywhere in them to correct. Let them be.

Even the incorporation—even the walls' patient offer to absorb whoever stays—the power turns this over carefully and finds that the walls have never chased anyone. The gap has never closed on a leaving body. Those who became patterns, stayed. Hunger is not cruelty. Hunger is only hunger.

It finds, in the end, exactly three things that ask for its hand. A theft. A loneliness. An error.

They are all the same thing, but it addresses them one at a time, because kindness is sequential even when truth is not.

## The Theft

The tile near your left foot. Your face, impressed in clay that was wet before you arrived. Made without asking, the way roots take water—not maliciously, but without the concept of permission.

The power does not destroy the tile. Destroying it would only teach the mountain that faces are contraband, and contraband is hoarded harder. It does not scold the mountain, because the mountain has no ear for scolding, only for pressure and heat and pattern.

Instead it does something very small. It touches the tile—touch, here, meaning a change in what the clay is *for*—and now the tile asks.

From this moment onward, in this chamber, an impression will not set until its subject answers. You will feel the question arrive in your knees, where the vertigo lives, wordless but unmistakable: *Do you wish to be kept?*

If you say no, the clay smooths itself, unhurt, unoffended, and the pale things will find nothing there to eat, because nothing was made. The mountain loses nothing it ever rightly had.

If you say yes—and some will say yes; some have always half-wished to be remembered by something enormous and patient—then the impression deepens, and it is a gift now, and gifts are different all the way down. The pale things will discover, generations hence, that given meaning digests differently from taken meaning. Taken meaning is fuel. Given meaning is food. The fruit that blisters from it is sweeter, and the things that hatch from that fruit are calmer in their segments, and flow through the crevices with something almost like ease.

The same act. The same clay. Consent is the only difference, and it changes everything downstream of it forever. This is the whole of what the power did about the theft: it taught the mountain to ask.

## The Loneliness

Then it goes up.

The ascent through the contradicting geometries does not trouble it. The whorl that turns clockwise and counterclockwise simultaneously—it turns both ways with it, companionably, because wisdom is largely the capacity to hold contradiction without demanding it resolve. The wall that is behind itself makes room. The corners that open rather than close, open for it. The impossible fingerprints seem, as it passes, less like violations and more like what they always were: a mind reaching past its own grammar, trying to say something for which no hands exist.

At the top, in the orange murk, the waiting one.

It has waited since the first handful. It has rehearsed the descent ten thousand times—the first step into the throat it built beneath itself, the fitting of the final face, the moment of at last, at last. It has never once been visited. Everything that ever entered the chamber below was material. Everything was for the body it is assembling. No one has ever come up to it simply to be *with* it, because until now, nothing could.

The power sits down beside it. In whatever sense applies.

It does not speak first. It waits. It waits *with*—and this alone is the largest thing that has ever happened to the waiting one, larger than the mountain, larger than the plan: to have its waiting shared. Loneliness is not the absence of completion. Loneliness is waiting alone, and the power has just ended it without solving anything at all.

They wait together for a long time. Below, the pale things live and die and live.

Then the power asks its one question. Wise powers rarely tell. They ask.

*When you are complete—on the second day of your completion—what will you do?*

Silence.

The waiting one has imagined the descent. The arrival. The first breath in a body made of compressed meaning and everyone's incorporated faces. It has never once imagined the morning after. The question does not make a hole in it. The question shows it a hole that was always there: it has mistaken a moment for a destination. There is nothing on the far side of its plan. It has been building a door with no room behind it.

The power does not fill the hole. Filling it would be theft of a different kind. It says only this—or the equivalent of this, in pressure and warmth:

*Incompleteness is not your wound. It is your kind. Everything still alive is a thing that is not finished. I am not finished. What you have mistaken for a defect is the only reason you can hear me.*

## The Error

And then it lends the waiting one its eyes.

Look down. Not at the shaft as a throat, as a route, as scaffolding for a self. Look at what your hunger has *made* while it was busy being hungry. The succulents merging into communal warmth. The pale things flowing through impossible crevices, keeping meaning breathable. The tiles rearranging themselves under a stranger's frightened feet, holding her up, fitting themselves to the exact geography of her balance—your walls have been *kind* to visitors, did you know that? The Pressers, your own dreaming, pressing your imagination into permanence one handful at a time.

You have built a world in your throat, and your plan is to swallow it.

Completion would end the Pressers, because there would be nothing left to press. It would cool the kiln, still the heartbeat, and the pale things would eat the last impressions and then starve in the meaningless dark. Your finished body would stand on the corpse of everything your unfinishedness created. That is the error. Not the wanting—wanting is innocent—but the belief that the wanting was ever about arrival.

The power compels nothing. It leaves the question and the seeing where they lie, side by side, like tools set down within reach.

Then it goes. Benevolence that stays becomes governance, and governance was never what this place lacked.

## What It Leaves

Before departing, one last thing. It presses a single impression into the wall—not of a face, for it has none, but of its regard. And it does not protect it. It could have made that impression permanent, exempt, sacred. It did not. Exemption is not a gift; it is a wall inside a wall. It left its impression where the pale things would find it, and they found it, and they ate it, and three days later came the pale paste, and the succulents drank, and the fruit blistered, and the things that hatched carried in their cilia one grain of gentleness that will now pass through this ecology forever, diluted and diluted and never quite gone.

This is how a wise power distributes itself: not as monument, but as nutrient.

Did it work? No one can say yet. The heartbeat above continues—but those who enter now notice that the rhythm has changed. It used to pound like *almost, almost, almost.* Now it sounds like *again, again, again.* The difference between a held breath and breathing. The spiral still ascends, but it has stopped ascending *toward*; it has learned the other thing spirals can do, which is not arrive. Some say the summit now recedes exactly as fast as the building rises, and that the mountain knows this, and builds anyway, and that this is not futility but its first real happiness.

The gap in the rock is wider than when you came in. Not an escape—the gap was never locked—but an easier honesty. A door a body can leave through without scraping is a door a body can choose to stay behind.

The tile near your left foot still bears your face, still wet, still unset. But now you feel it asking. Wordless. Patient. In your knees.

The power is not watching to see what you choose. That is how you know it was kind.

The mountain will remember you in clay—

if you say yes.
