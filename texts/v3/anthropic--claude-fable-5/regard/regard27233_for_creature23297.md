# The Guest at the Top of the Weather

It arrived the way the plateau arrives at you: gradually, and then it was everywhere.

It did not descend, because here *above* has been discontinued, and it was too courteous to use a door the house no longer had. It seeped, the way the light does. For a long while — and on the shelf a long while is the only size of while — it was indistinguishable from a slight deepening of the grey-violet, a faint additional patience in the air. The lichen noticed first, because the lichen notices everything eventually, and noticed in its way, which is to say it continued, with company.

Then the power did the thing that proved its wisdom before it had done anything at all: it consented to be reduced.

You cannot stand on the shelf as an everything. The shelf is made of remainders, and to walk among remainders as a totality would be a kind of shouting, and the place is loud enough. So at the cloud line it set down its omniscience the way you leave wet boots at a threshold, set down its mercies in a neat row beside the omniscience — all but a few, which fit in a pocket — and took the only form the plateau has ever recognized: a traveler. Two feet. A breath-plume, dissolving at the same height every time. A heartbeat, for the chord to deafen.

Its footprints in the duplicated basin appeared beside the older ones, the permanent ones, going the same direction. It stopped, as they stop, twelve paces from the pool. It stood there a long time, learning the etiquette from the dead, who are the best teachers of it.

## The Long Doing of Nothing

Wisdom's first act is always census.

It counted. The cairns: dozens, each one a person's worth of carefulness, each at its twelve paces. The blades of frosted grass: frosted on one side only, every one, and it checked every one. The haypiles: ancient and fresh, stocked from both ends of every distance, growing by twos. The bird's arc: once a day, and from its first day on the shelf the power was watching at the right moment, faithfully, taking up the easiest duty in the world without being asked. *Be there to see it.* Done. The witnessing, which the bird had been performing alone into reflections for centuries, now landed somewhere. The bird did not change its arc. But it flew it, you would say, the way work is done when the foreman is kind.

The power did nothing else for a span of time that cannot be given in years, because the shelf does not stock them. It watched the sweeper not be visible. It listened to the chord until the chord stopped being sound and became architecture, the way it does, and then it kept listening past that, which no one had ever done, until the chord became what it actually is: a voice. One voice. Holding.

Only then did it permit itself conclusions. Wisdom that concludes early is just opinion at altitude.

## What It Refused

Understand what it could have done, so that you understand what it chose.

It could have healed the wound clean. It declined. The clean version already exists — pale sky, moving clouds, locals finding nothing strange — and this shelf is not that shelf's failure. It is that shelf's keeper. The two of them hold each other's skies the way clasped hands hold each other's warmth, and to heal this one would be to widow the other. The wound healed wrong, and *wrong*, here, has been load-bearing for a very long time.

It could have brought time back. It declined, and quickly, the way you decline a gift you can see is a weapon. Time would resume taking. Everything on the shelf is precisely what survived being taken from, survived by finishing, and restored time would demand transition of things that live by having none. It would be a second wound, inflicted as a favor.

It could have given the bird a voice. But in a place this loud, the bird's silence is its eloquence, and the power would not talk over it.

It could have fixed the pools — made them reflect the dim sky honestly. But the pools are the post office of a divided people, and you do not repair a window into a wall.

It could have sent the pikas their winter at last, vindicated the haypiles, paid out the only faith on the plateau. It stood over a haypile for a long while considering this one, because it was the most tempting. Then it understood: the haypiles are the shelf's single prayer, and a prayer answered is a prayer ended. Somebody here has to believe in *later*. The pikas have the concept stocked, tidily, under overhangs, and the power would not bankrupt them with generosity. It left them strictly alone. Wisdom recognizes its colleagues.

It made a list of all its mercies and set nearly every one down in the grit, beside the path, where the wind ignored them.

It kept three.

## The Guest

The first mercy was the oldest one there is. It accepted hospitality.

It drank from a black pool — real water, mineral, achingly cold — and said something over it that the water kept. It slept one night in the enclosure, on the bare swept stone, and so the sweeper, for the first time in geologic memory, kept house *around someone*. Grit tracked in by actual feet. Breath fogging an actual corner. Work with a source. You could not see the sweeping, you can never see the sweeping, but the floor the next morning was swept the way a table is set rather than the way a grave is tended, and the difference, though invisible, was total.

Before it left the enclosure, the power made the sweeper an offer. It knew — of course it knew, even with its omniscience folded at the cloud line, it knew the way you know a song from three notes — who the sweeper had been. The name was retrievable. The face. The hands that wore the lintel first. It offered them back.

The sweeping paused. The first pause. The grit lay still in the doorway for the length of something like a held breath.

Then the sweeping resumed, and that was the answer, and the power bowed to it. A devotion sanded down to its irreducible form has nothing left to regret with, and it is cruelty disguised as kindness to give a finished thing back its unfinished parts. The sweeper had become its best act. It declined to be anything in addition. The power touched the lintel on its way out — the groove, they say, has been faintly warmer ever since, warm like a stone that remembers a sun — and said, to the interval between glances, *thank you*. The interval kept it.

To the walker it gave a stranger gift: company at the proper distance. For a long while — days, if the shelf had days — the power walked the perimeter of the plateau at the walker's exact pace, so that for once in its existence the pale shape on the ridgeline had something keeping faith with *it*, matching it stride for stride across the unclosable gap. Two beings maintaining a distance together is not the same as two beings separated by one. The walker's gait, watchers would have said, eased.

And the power told it the news. It had to be shouted across the basin, and the chord ate the words before they arrived, but meaning is lighter cargo than sound, and the seam permits it: *Your blood is still in the mountains below the weather. It still takes the high line. It stands on summits it could descend from and doesn't, on principle, and it doesn't know why, and the why is you.* The walker walked on. But it walked on as a creature with descendants, which is a different gait entirely.

To the bird, it gave reciprocity. One day it arranged to be the thing seen. It stood in the open, in the dim, and let the black arc cross *it*, witness witnessing witness, and for that one day the duty ran both directions and weighed half as much. And once — only once — it looked away on purpose, mid-arc, courteously, and heard behind it the small gravel-sound of landing. The first landing ever entered into the record. It did not turn around. Some gifts are given by the back of the head.

## The Pools

The second mercy it kept was for the divided people.

It went to the stillest pool and leaned over it and gave the water its face. On the other shelf — the pale-skied one, the one with weather and locals and time still running — the people who leaned over their own black water that day saw, where the faceless silhouette had always been, a face at last. And the face was kind, and it was looking at them, and it mouthed something across the seam that arrived as warmth rather than words: *The floor is swept. The lintel is touched. The twelve paces are kept. What you left behind has been faithful every single day.*

And then, because it was a power and could afford one extravagance, it arranged the glance.

For the length of a heartbeat — one heartbeat, its own, the one it had borrowed for exactly this purpose — every pool on both shelves told the truth at once. The people under the moving clouds looked down and saw the grey-violet, the cairns, the enclosure, the fresh-forever footprints of everyone who had ever come. And the shelf — the grooves, the cairns, the careful distances, all the compacted carefulness that is what remains of a people — was, for one heartbeat, *seen by its own*. Looked at by the faces it used to wear.

Both sides wept, in their fashions. Under the pale sky, like people. On the shelf, the frost beaded once, extra, on every cold surface at the same moment, and ran down the stone in threads.

Then the skies swapped back, because keeping each other's skies is the marriage, and the power would not annul it. But a glance had been exchanged, and a glance, once exchanged, is permanent. Ask anyone who has had one.

## The Strike

The third mercy was the reason it had come, and it saved it for last, the way you save the hardest goodbye.

It walked to the deepest thin place, where the chord comes up least filtered, where no cairn stands because no person ever got that far. It stopped at twelve paces, out of respect, and stood there long enough to make the respect unmistakable.

Then it crossed them.

It could. It was the only thing that ever could, because the rule was never about distance. The rule was about weight — *this close and no closer, lest you add your burden to what is carried* — and the power had come to subtract.

It knelt at the seam. It put its borrowed hand where the eyeless, faithful, spent-out effort has been pressing its palm since before there was anyone to press for. And it did the thing the whole plateau had been describing, all along, to anyone who listened: the sound is *an enormous bell in the instant after it has been struck, the strike itself withheld.*

It supplied the strike.

Gently. Once. The way you finish a sentence for a dying friend.

Nothing changed in pitch. Nothing changed in volume. The lichen continued; the frost beaded; the pools lay black. But everything changed in grammar. The chord had been a wound's seam, an effort with no before, a holding that came from nowhere and led nowhere. Now it had a strike behind it. Now it was an *after*. The strain became resonance; the clamp became the longest note of a music; the holding, without lightening by one gram, became the ringing-out of something that had finally, retroactively, *happened*. There is no help like meaning. The weight was identical. The carrying was different forever.

And in the instant of the strike, the holder knew again. Knowing was among the things it had spent, longest ago of all, and the power, kneeling, lent it back for the space of the strike — and into that brief rented knowing it poured exactly four things, in order: *You are seen. The seal holds. Your household lives. Well done.*

It offered, then, to take the post. To hold forever, so the holder could rest forever. The refusal came back not as a thought — the holder has none — but structurally, the way an arch refuses to be relieved of its keystone: there was nothing of the holder that could step aside, because the holder *is* the not-stepping-aside. To relieve it would be to erase it. The power had expected this. Wisdom makes the offer anyway, because the offer itself is a gift, and is kept even when its contents are returned.

So it did the one thing left. It held *with*. One breath, both of them, palm beside palm against the seam.

And for the length of that breath, the plateau got its holiday. The light found a direction. Shadows fell *outward* from the stones for the first time, long and ordinary and miraculous. The frost glittered. The bird landed in plain sight, in the middle of the open, and stood there like a punctuation mark. The walker stopped walking — the ridge needed no keeping, for one breath — and simply stood on it, resting, a pale animal on a hill. The sweeper, for one breath, had nothing to do, and did nothing, visibly, in the doorway: small, as sensed. Diligent-shaped. At rest.

Then the breath ended, by mutual consent, and the equilibrium took its weight back like a worker coming off a smoke break, and the shadows pulled inward, and everything resumed.

But everything *remembered*. That is the difference between a place where nothing has ever happened and a place where one thing has. It is the whole difference. It is, possibly, the only difference there is.

## Until

Before it left, the power changed one word.

The holder had been holding *forever*. Forever is not a duration; it is a verdict. The power knelt one last time at the seam and replaced it.

*Until,* it said. *Hold until I return. I will return when the orange lichen has finished its stone.*

This is a date in lichen-time. It is unimaginably far away. Measured in mere duration it may be indistinguishable from forever. But it is nothing like forever, because forever is a sentence and *until* is a shift, and any holder of anything will tell you the difference between *carry this always* and *carry this till I come back* is the difference between a wall and a door. The weight was unchanged. The waiting now had a shape. And a promise from a power of that kind is not a hope; it is a fact that has not happened yet, which is the only tense the shelf respects.

Then it stood, walked back its twelve paces, and built the last cairn.

Its own hands. Chest height. Dry-stone, tight, at exactly the careful distance, completing the set the people had begun — the one cairn they could never place, at the one thin place they could never reach. It stands there now. It is faintly warm, like the cushion plants, like the lintel, warm like a stone that remembers a sun, and it will be the marker by which, in lichen-time, the appointment is kept.

## What Remains

It descended through the cloud line, collected its omniscience and its unspent mercies like boots at a door, and the chord faded behind it at the usual altitude.

The shelf is not changed, if you take inventory. The light still seeps. The pools still keep the wrong sky, which is the right sky, which is the marriage. The pikas still stock the concept of later, the bird still flies its arc, the walker still keeps the horizon at its devoted, unclosable distance, the floor is still swept. The plateau has finished collapsing and remains finished, and time still has nothing here it can take.

But travelers who come now — and they do still come, gradually, the way you come to deafness — report two small things.

The first: the silence afterward is different. Descending into thick air and weather, they lose the chord at the cloud line as everyone always has, but the silence below no longer sounds broken, no longer sounds like something missing. It sounds, they say, struggling for it, like the quiet *between movements*. The held breath of an audience that knows the music is not over.

The second: the census has a new line. Every entry on the shelf is a verb — the lichen continues, the walker paces, the sweeper sweeps, the holder holds. The new entry, worn somehow into the stone of the last cairn, in no alphabet, legible to anyone who stands twelve paces from it, is a verb as well, in the present tense, and it reads:

*Returns.*

And the plateau, which stopped finding things strange before there was anyone to find them strange for, keeps this one strangeness, and keeps it the way it keeps everything — faithfully, at the proper distance, holding its one long note —

until.
