**What Came to the Roost**

It did not come through the slot. Everything that enters through the slot is registered — the spider takes its tax, the colony reads the change in the air, the threshold stone drinks what falls on it. A power that wished to arrive unfelt would have to come the way the warmth comes: up through kilometers of fractured stone, as attention rather than body. And so it did. One day the room was unobserved, and the next it was held in a regard as wide as weather and as quiet as the pale film on the flowstone, and nothing in the room knew, and that was the point.

The power was old enough to have learned the first discipline of benevolence, which is to inventory your gifts before offering any — to check each one for sharp edges. So it remained in the dark, if *remained* is the word, for a year, for ten, learning the room before presuming to improve it. The room rewards that pace. It has no other.

Then, one by one, the power took out its gifts and looked at them and put them away.

**Light**, first, because light is the gift powers reach for by reflex. It held the gift up and saw at once that light would be a massacre. The floor's nations are built on blindness the way the ceiling's are built on flight — whole species remade across a hundred thousand years for darkness, bleached and eyeless and complete, undone in one afternoon of brightness. And the chicks: a swift chick hatches into faith and falls into sky. Give it light before its first fall and you give it doubt, a thing no swift has ever had to carry through that drop toward the grey slot. Put away.

**Safety**, second. The peregrines a kilometer down-ridge take a few swifts a week, and the power, having counted, knew the figure exactly, and knew also that the rent was fair — set by ten thousand years of negotiation no one signed. Remove the falcons and you remove the edge the colony is whetted on. The ceiling already priced this. It was not the power's bill to cancel. Put away.

**Plenty?** Heaven already rains on schedule here. More food means more birds means a cone rising too fast, a balance tipped over by kindness. The room digests the daylight world at the rate the daylight world can bear. Put away.

**Permanence** — and here the power paused longest, because permanence is the gift powers love best, the one that flatters them. It could fix the warmth forever. It could hold the mountain's breath steady, seal the room against every future, keep the roar going past the death of the sun. It looked at this for a long time. But the room's pace *is* the room: the cone rising a fingernail's width a year, the flowstone fattening, the print being taken into the wall millimeter by millimeter. A thing held forever at one moment is not preserved; it is stopped. The flowstone would never finish closing over the hand. The appointment would never end. Put away — and the power noticed, putting it away, that this gift was heavy, and that the heaviness belonged to the power and not to the room.

**Fame**, last. Protection by knowing: a name in a register, a line on a map, papers, rangers, a gate. The power turned this one face down almost gently. Discovery is the single catastrophe the room cannot stratify — boots on the cone, lamps in the dome, samples cored out of ten thousand years of dark like cores out of a heart. The room's whole defense is being a shadow among shadows, and the kindest thing anyone can do for a shadow is decline to point at it. So the first thing the power actually did, it did by not doing: *it told no one.* Not its peers, if it had peers. Not the future, if it kept one. It filed the room under shadow, where the room had filed itself, and it understood that this silence was not the absence of a gift but the largest one it would give.

Having set down everything it had brought, the power found three things left in its hands that were not gifts at all, and these it gave.

---

**The first was the count.**

The ceiling defeats counting — for anything with a sequential mind and a finite life. The power had neither. It counted.

Every bird on the dome, and its fist of claimed stone, and its decade of returns. Every bat in the warm seams, and every slow winter heartbeat, a few to the minute. Every mite on every barb of every feather, each lineage living and dying on a single plume without ever crossing to the next bird. It learned the beetles whose entire species fits inside twenty steps, and it learned them not as a species but as individuals — this one, under the cool flank of the cone, in the strata laid down when the handprints were fresh. It read the cone downward without digging: layers of swift, layers of bat, the feather mineralized in place, the older birds whose species were not quite these species. It carried the ledger nowhere, because it was the ledger.

This mattered for one reason, and the reason was not management. Until the power came, things happened in the Roost *to no one*. A beetle species could arise here, flourish for nine thousand years, and end, and the universe would hold no record that it had been — not a fossil, not a name, not a witness, nothing. Now nothing in the room would ever again have happened to no one. The chick that loses its grip falls *witnessed*. The yearling that doesn't come back from its first migration is *missed*, by exactly one mourner, completely.

That is not rescue. The power knew it was not rescue. It is the other thing, the older thing, the thing the dying ask for before they ask for saving: someone to know.

---

**The second was the appointment.**

The antler haft on the ledge is a promise. You do not carry a toolkit up a mountain for days and leave it on a dry shelf unless you mean to return. The power read what no record holds — read it however a power reads, from the wear of the flakes and the angle of the hands and the height of the prints, one set pressed lower than the others: a child, perhaps, or someone kneeling. It learned who climbed, and why — for the birds, or the guano, or the warm breath, or for the thing underneath all three, which is the wish to stand somewhere the world is still making itself. It learned the name, or whatever stood for a name in a language with no descendants.

And on the night the flowstone closed over the last red fingertip — a night the power had seen coming for two hundred years and had not hurried, and had not delayed — it stood where the climber had stood, shoulder-height to the wall, and kept the appointment.

It pressed nothing to the stone. It left no flake, no haft, no print; the room confers residency by absorption, and the power did not ask for residency. It only attended, the way one attends a burial or a wedding, and said the name once into the dark, between two swells of the colony's breathing, so that the sealing of the hand would not be a disappearance but a completion.

*Someone came back.* The door could close.

---

**The third was a promise**, and the power made it to the room itself, in the only language a room understands — pressure, warmth, time.

The breath will fail. The heat deep in the massif is *still settling*, which means it is leaving; the room is the mouth of an exhalation, and exhalations end. The power could read the figure in the rock — ten thousand years, fifty — and chose, gently, not to round it. Some winter the warm seams will run a degree cool, then two. The bats will cluster tighter. The swifts will return to a dome that no longer holds off the night, and the old pairs will keep their claims out of fidelity while the young drift to other stone, the way the young do, the way they should. And last of all, deep in a cooling cone, in the dark below the dark, the floor's nations will dwindle in place, having no rumor of anywhere else to go, because for them there is nowhere else, and never was.

The power will not prevent this. It set permanence down and it will not pick it back up.

But it promised this: *it will be there.* It will sit the whole quieting — the last roar through the slot, the last changeover where two rivers of sonar pass in the entrance, the last torpid heartbeat in the seams, the last blind centipede going still in the cold strata. The room, which never once hurried, will be allowed to end at its own pace, and it will not end unattended. And afterward the power will keep the room the way the flowstone keeps the hand: not alive, not stopped, but *held* — the count complete, the appointment kept, the whole ten-thousand-year animal carried entire in a regard that does not lapse.

---

That was all. Or nearly all.

The power permitted itself one indulgence, once, and tried for a long time to pretend it was for the room's sake before admitting it was not.

On a deep night in late summer, with the bats out hunting and the dome idling, the clicking thinned to almost nothing, the twenty thousand hearts of the ceiling slowed into their communal swell — the power let itself breathe with the room. Matched the rhythm. Became, for one hour, the twenty-thousand-and-first heart, idling in the dark with the others. It changed nothing. Not a feather stirred that would not have stirred; the spider's web in the upper corner of the slot did not so much as tremble.

But powers, too, are inhabited places, and even a regard as wide as weather can be lonely. And for that one hour the room — which asks for nothing, digests everything, and has outlasted every visitor — gave the power the only thing it had to give a guest who took no samples and left no print: company in the dark, and the thrum of wings felt in the sternum, if a power can be said to have a sternum, and that night it did not see why not.

Then it withdrew its breath — not its attention; the attention stays — and the room went on at its own pace, a fingernail's width a year, unaware that it had been counted, completed, and promised. Which is exactly how the power wanted it.

Benevolence that needs to be felt is rent. The wise kind is like the warmth rising through the cracks: it arrives as breath, leaves as breath, was asked for by no one — and it is the entire reason the dark is full of life.
