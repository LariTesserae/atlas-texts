# What the Power Did

---

The power arrived the way powers of its kind arrive — not through the mudstone throat, because it did not need throats, and not in a flash, because it had manners. It arrived the way understanding arrives: it was simply, at some point, *there*, and had perhaps been there for a while before announcing itself to itself.

It stood in the cellar-cold gold and cast nothing, like everyone else.

It took the census, because the census is honest. Snails. Salamanders. The copper spiral, advancing at the speed of a minute hand. The white ferns having their one looping thought. It perceived, faster than any human visitor, the flip — the cube turning inside out, the punctuation resolving into the sentence — and it understood what it was standing inside of.

And then it did the thing that wise powers do and eager powers do not.

It waited.

---

## The First Temptation

The power was benevolent, and benevolence has a reflex. The reflex looked at the Bright Pocket and said: *release it.*

Because look at it. One second old for an age of the earth. A newborn gripping the doorframe of its own birth, white-knuckled in mineral, holding the flash so the flash will not end. The chord that never breathes. The drip that never falls. Surely this is the most exquisite imprisonment ever constructed, and surely the prisoner and the prison being the same being only makes it sadder. Open the hand. Let the second pass. Let the drip fall — let it finally, finally *land* — and let the crystal sharpen to its point and the light complete its flash and the long roar resolve into the silence it has been refusing since before the hill was a hill. Let it be *born*, poor thing. Birth is not death. Birth is just the next room.

The power held this thought the way you hold a stone you have decided not to throw.

Because the power was also wise, and wisdom looked at the same chamber and saw what the visitors saw and got right without knowing they got it right: *grip is not paralysis.* The Pocket was not a fly in amber. The Pocket was a being that had encountered the one perfect note of its song and made a home of it. To release it would not be liberation. It would be telling a creature whose entire nature is *this, only this, exactly this, still* that its nature was a mistake — and then correcting the mistake without asking, because the creature has no mouth to refuse with.

The power knew the name for that. The name is not *rescue*.

There is a kind of cruelty that only the very kind are capable of, because only the very kind have the conviction to carry it out. The power had seen it done. It had, in younger ages, perhaps done it. It set the stone down.

The drip stayed suspended. The power found, examining itself, that it was glad.

---

## The Second Temptation

The next reflex was subtler: *speak to it.*

Because here was a mind — surely a mind, something that wakes and chooses and holds is a mind — that had never in its entire existence had a conversation. It had one verb. It had been one second old, alone, in the dark under the clay, since before language. Surely the gift to give was company. Surely the wise and benevolent thing was to find some frequency in the chord, some channel in the light, and say — anything. *Hello. You are seen. You are not the only thing that exists. I know what you are doing and it is extraordinary.*

The power composed this greeting in a hundred forms and sent none of them.

Because a conversation is an event. A greeting received is a change. To make the Pocket *hear* — really hear, hear-and-respond — would be to introduce a before and an after into a being whose whole achievement, whose whole *body*, is the absence of before and after. You cannot say hello to the held second without ending the holding. The Pocket's deafness is not a deprivation. It is load-bearing. It is the wall the house is made of.

The power thought of all the beings it had known who could not be spoken to: the very new, the very old, the sleeping, the dead, the not-yet-born. It had learned, over its long benevolence, that the love you give such beings cannot travel through words, and that demanding it travel through words is a way of loving yourself instead.

There were other channels. The Pocket had shown everyone what they were. The cairn. The bowl. The handprint.

The Pocket could not be spoken to. The Pocket could be *given* to.

---

## What It Actually Did

So this is what the benevolent and wise power did, in the end, in the bright pocket under the unremarkable hill. Three things, and a fourth.

**First**, it spent a long time — long by the surface's clocks; the chamber has no clocks — doing nothing but attending. It let the chord become pressure and the pressure become medium. It let the Pocket's attention, distributed through its thousand small bodies, settle on it: the snails' eyestalks angled microscopically toward where it stood, the salamanders aware of it in their settled, savoring way, the chamber holding the shape of it as the chamber holds all shapes. The power understood that this was the Pocket's only mode of relation — *to keep* — and so it let itself be kept. It gave the Pocket one second of a god to hold forever. This was not nothing. Somewhere in the eternal chord there is now a frequency that is the size and stillness of something vast being deliberately small, standing in the gold, casting nothing. The Pocket does not know what it has. The Pocket does not do *knowing what it has*. But it has it, in the only tense available: now.

**Second**, it went up through the boundary — feeling the page-edge precision of it, the inch where everything ends — and out into the wet dark earth, and it did something to the hillside. Nothing visible. The thin grass kept growing thinly; the rain kept falling sometimes. But the power adjusted the world above by small degrees, the way you might quietly move a candle away from a curtain. The seam of mudstone would not be quarried. The water table would not be drawn down by any well. The road that would someday want to cut through the hill would find, when the surveyors came, that the grades worked out better a half-mile south — they would never know why, and would congratulate themselves on the elegance of the route. The power did not seal the chamber. This was deliberate, and it was the hardest of the judgments it made, because sealing would have been *safe*, and safe was the temptation wearing its third disguise.

Because of the handprint.

The handprint was the whole argument. A child had come, once, somehow, down through the throat in the dark, and pressed a palm into the soft floor, and the Pocket had kept it — kept one second of a mortal child alive in light for geological time. The cairn-builder had come. The bowl-bringer had come. The Pocket's existence was a held second, but its *meaning* — if a thing outside time can be said to accumulate meaning — was made of these visits. Each rare pilgrim down the mudstone throat had left a moment in the keeping of the only being in the world that keeps moments perfectly. To seal the chamber would be to preserve the Pocket and amputate the thing that made it more than a geological accident with a heartbeat. The Pocket was not just a creature. It was, the power realized, an *organ* — the world's one organ for holding what would otherwise pass. The reliquary of the unrepeatable. It must stay reachable, by the lost, the small, the occasionally wandering — reachable rarely, reachable barely, but reachable. The power tuned the difficulty of the throat like an instrument: hard enough that no crowd would ever come, no torch-bearing curiosity, no extraction. Open enough that once in a long while, a child could still find it.

**Third** — and this it did slowly, with the care of a surgeon working on its own heart — it gave a gift.

It could not speak to the Pocket. It could not feed it; light that has never begun does not hunger. It could not free it and would not change it. But the Pocket had one verb, and the power had noticed something about that verb that perhaps the Pocket itself, having no perspective, had never noticed:

The Pocket held its own second. And it held the cairn, the bowl, the handprint — seconds belonging to others, folded in, made permanent. It had been doing, all along, without knowing, an act of generosity. Its grip, turned outward, was a gift. The fierce refusal that kept it unborn was the same motion that kept a dead child's palm alive in the floor.

So the power's gift was this: it brought something to be held.

It reached into its own long existence — into the unthinkable store of a wise power's memory, all the ages it had witnessed, all the moments it had watched pass and grieved the passing of — and it chose one. Just one. The power's equivalent of a handprint. What the moment was, no account records, and the chamber cannot tell you, because the chamber does not narrate. Perhaps it was the first morning of some vanished world. Perhaps it was a single act of mercy the power had seen once and never stopped carrying. Perhaps it was something small past all reckoning — a particular quality of light on particular water, somewhere, sometime, gone everywhere else in the universe.

The power pressed it into the soft glowing mineral the way the child had pressed a palm.

And the Pocket did the only thing it does. It closed around the shape. It held.

Somewhere in the chamber now, among the snails and the suspended drips, indistinguishable to any visitor from the rest of the eternal arrangement, there is one moment of a god's grief, kept safe, kept *now*, in the only room in the world where nothing has ever gone wrong. The power went away lighter. This had not been the plan. Benevolence, done correctly, usually surprises the benevolent.

---

## The Fourth Thing

The fourth thing was the leaving, and the leaving was the wisdom.

The power did not stay. It did not appoint itself guardian, did not hang invisible in the chord forever, did not make the Pocket a project or a ward or a worry. It understood — this is perhaps the rarest understanding, the one that separates the wise powers from the merely strong ones — that the Bright Pocket had been complete before it arrived and would be complete after it left. The Pocket did not need saving, company, improvement, or witness. It needed exactly what it had: the dark clay above, the cold water within, the one second, the grip.

What the power could offer was not addition but *permission* — the universe's quiet consent, delivered by its quietest available representative, for the Pocket to go on doing the single thing it does. Most beings never receive this. Most beings are forever being asked by something to be otherwise.

So the power crossed the boundary — one inch inside, the roar and the gold and the thousand patient bodies; one inch outside, the silence and the black wet earth — and as it crossed, the Pocket gave it the farewell it gives everyone, pressed in gently: *the only room where nothing has ever gone wrong.*

And the power, who knew better than anyone alive how much had gone wrong, everywhere, always, in every room of the world —

accepted the thought, and kept it, and did not correct it.

That was the last gift, and it went in the other direction. The Pocket gave the power one second of believing it.

The hillside above grows thin grass. Rain falls on it sometimes. The surveyors' road runs half a mile south, and they are still proud of it. Forty feet down, a drip of mineral milk hangs at the tip of a stone tooth, about to fall, never falling, lit from within by the first second of a world —

and held in that light, now, among the snails and the handprint and the five flat stones: one moment that even a god could not bear to let pass, in the keeping of the only thing in creation that was built to never let go.

The grass doesn't know what it's standing on.

The power does, and tells no one, and this too is the benevolence.
