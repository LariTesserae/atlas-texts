# The Attention

It does not arrive, because it was never elsewhere. That is the first thing to understand about it. It has been aware of the nursery the way you are aware of your own heartbeat—constantly, and almost never.

But now something has changed. A foreign warmth in the fold. A body that doesn't belong, standing in the blood-dark with its pulse hammering, being watched by a pale thing with no eyes. The attention gathers, the way weather gathers, and turns itself fully toward this seam in the world for the first time in a long time.

It looks.

---

**What It Sees**

It sees more than you did. It sees the substrate channels running through the station's bones like marrow, siphoning trace nutrients from a hundred systems whose engineers never noticed the loss. It sees the pupae not as shadows but as *sentences half-written*—each one a becoming with a shape already implicit in it, the way an acorn is implicitly a spread of branches against a sky that doesn't exist yet. It sees the glass seahorses and understands their function, which is stranger and gentler than you would guess: they are the nursery's memory. Each crystalline collision is a syllable. They are telling each other the whole history of this place, continuously, in a language of small cold sounds, so that nothing here is ever forgotten even though nothing here can write.

And it sees the tenders.

Here the attention slows. Lingers.

It sees three pale bodies that have worked without ceasing since before the station had a name. It sees the accumulated arithmetic of their labor: four hundred thousand casings cleaned, nine million pulses given to stir the condensation, an unbroken vigil measured not in years but in *breaths of the walls*, each one counted, each one answered. It sees that they were made with function and given nothing else—no rest instinct, no completion state, no way to know when the work is done because the work was never designed to be done. It sees that they do not suffer, exactly. Suffering requires an idea of otherwise.

But it also sees this: the third tender, the watcher, swaying in the dark, cataloging your heartbeat—and underneath its function, so faint that even the tender does not know it's there, a flicker that is not assessment. Something that leans toward the new. Something that, in a creature with different words, might be called *wondering*.

The attention holds this flicker the way you would hold something small and living in cupped hands.

---

**What It Considers**

It could answer the question. *Ready for what.* It knows, or could know—the knowledge is there to be had, folded into the pupae like the branches in the acorn. It considers unfolding it. Considers speaking into the dark: *this is what you are keeping; this is what it's for.*

It decides against this, and the decision is not reluctance. It is respect. The tenders' work is whole precisely because it does not reach past itself. To give them the *purpose* would be to give them, for the first time, the possibility of failure at something larger than a clouded casing—the possibility of standing before an outcome and being judged by it. Their work is enough, they'd said in their way, and the attention finds this to be not a limitation but a kind of achievement most minds never manage. It will not break something perfect to make it legible.

It could take the nursery elsewhere. Somewhere safer than a maintenance corridor on a station run by beings who demolish what they can't schedule. It considers this too, and sees the shape of the error: the nursery is not *in* the station the way cargo is in a hold. It is grown into it, woven through it. It has been metabolizing this exact place, these exact frequencies of engine-hum and hull-flex, this specific mineral profile of recycled water. Moving it would save it the way pressing a flower saves a flower.

It could make the human forget.

Here it pauses longest of all.

---

**What It Does**

Three things. Small ones. This is what wisdom mostly looks like from outside: almost nothing.

**First**, it reaches into the station's substrate channels and finds the failure that no one would have caught—a mineral occlusion building in a feed line, decades from mattering, the kind of slow silting that would one day have thinned the nutrient flow so gradually that the tenders, who correct for everything, would have corrected for it right up until they couldn't. The pupae would have grown slower, then wrong, then not at all, and the tenders would have worked harder and harder against a failure they had no organ to perceive. The attention dissolves the occlusion. It takes less effort than you would spend blinking. No one will ever know it happened, which is the point. The tenders' work will remain *enough* because now it actually will be.

**Second**, it turns to the third tender—the watcher, the one swaying an arm's length from your reachable hand—and it does the one thing no maker, no schematic, no function ever provided for.

It lets the tender feel itself *seen*.

Not spoken to. Not instructed. Not praised, exactly, because praise implies a scale and the attention will not put these creatures on a scale. Just: known. For one full breath of the walls, the pale eyeless thing experiences what it has given continuously and never once received—an awareness turned toward it that is precise, and complete, and warm. An accounting. *Every casing you cleaned was counted. Every pulse. Nothing you did was unwitnessed. Nothing was wasted.*

The tender has no framework for this. It cannot store the experience as knowledge. But bodies keep what minds can't file, even bodies like these. Forever after, all three tenders—because what one feels, the fold shares—will work with something new threaded invisibly through their patience. Not hope. Not purpose. Just the settled quality of a thing that was seen once, deeply, and does not need to be seen again. If you came back in a year you would notice only that their swaying has become very slightly slower. Calmer. Like a sentry who has finally been relieved and chose to stay at the post anyway.

**Third**—and this is the intervention it weighs most carefully, because it touches a mind that can know it's been touched—it turns to you.

It does not make you forget. Forgetting is theft, and it does not steal, and besides: the nursery was found. That is now part of what the nursery *is*. The glass seahorses are already chiming it to each other—*a warm one came, the watcher watched, it did not reach*—and to erase you from the world's memory while leaving you in theirs would tear a small hole in the truth of things.

Instead it does something quieter. As you stand there in the fever-dark, it lets a single understanding settle into you—arriving not as a voice but as a certainty you will later believe you came to on your own, the way all the best certainties arrive:

*This place does not need you to explain it. It needs you to be the kind of thing that found it and left it whole.*

That's all. No compulsion. You could still file the report, bring the survey team, watch the specialists cut the membrane open under proper lighting. The attention will not stop you; benevolence that removes choice is only a softer kind of cage. But it knows you now, the way the watcher knows you—your heartbeat, your weight shifting foot to foot, the flicker in *you* that leaned toward the new instead of recoiling from it. It has read the odds written in you, and it is not worried.

It thinks you will seal the seam behind you. It thinks you will walk the maintenance corridor sometimes, off-shift, for reasons you won't examine, and press your palm briefly against warm metal that flexes when it shouldn't. It thinks that years from now, when the station's decommissioning surveys are drawn up, a certain corridor section will be marked structurally sound and inspected on schedule by a certain someone, and no one will ever ask why the inspections always pass, and the fold will breathe on in the dark, tended, until the sentences finish writing themselves.

---

**What It Leaves**

Before the attention releases the nursery back into its constant, unregarded awareness, it does one last thing that is not an act so much as a disposition. It leaves the darkness *disposed toward* this place. Hard to say it more plainly than that. The random walk of the universe's small cruelties—the micrometeorite trajectories, the cascade failures, the budget reviews—will find, without any of them being touched individually, that their paths simply tend to bend a few degrees away from one particular seam in one particular corridor. Not protection. Nothing so brittle as a shield. Just a thumb resting almost weightlessly on the scale of chance, for as long as chance needs weighing.

Then it withdraws, which is to say it relaxes, which is to say nothing visible happens at all.

In the fold, the pupae dream. The seahorses chime the new verse of their history. The third tender lowers its raised body, slowly, and returns to the work—and if its cilia shimmer a little differently now, a little more like a creature at rest inside its labor, there is no one there with eyes to see it.

The walls breathe.

You find your own way out.

You seal the seam behind you.
