**What the Power Did**

It came up from below.

This was the first thing it did, and it was already a decision. A power of that order does not need paths; it could have condensed out of the mist above the pool like one more bead on one more thread of nothing. But the hollow has one grammar of arrival — the plateau shouldering you upward through warm wet air for the better part of a morning — and the power understood that to skip the climb would be to arrive somewhere else. A place is partly made of how it must be approached. So it walked, or did the thing that was, for it, walking, and let the thin air work on it, and came over the rounded rim in the late morning the way everything that has ever come here came, and stood looking down into the bowl.

And then it did the second thing, which was the hardest, and which took it — by its own reckoning, which is not ours — a very long time.

It did nothing.

---

You must understand what doing nothing costs a being like that. Benevolence is a pressure. It pushes outward from such a power constantly, the way warmth pushes out of the hollow's stone, and everywhere it goes it finds the unfinished, the broken-off, the wrongly-ended, and the pressure finds the cracks and pours in. A wise power spends most of its wisdom not on knowing what to do but on holding the benevolence still long enough to see whether the crack is a wound or a mouth.

So it sat on the rim, where the mist forms and unforms, and it let itself feel every repair it could perform, one at a time, fully, the way you must fully feel a temptation before you can decline it rather than merely suppress it.

It felt how easily it could fill the air with messengers. One gesture and there would be midges over the pool by evening, a frog by autumn, birdsong falling past the rim by spring, and the copper spore-dust would ride out on a thousand wings, and in ten centuries the plateau would be forested in dark-green sentinels. It held that future in its mind until it could see all of it — and saw, at the far end of it, the hollow unsettled. Seven was not what was left. Seven was the answer. To repopulate the bowl would be to reopen a negotiation that had been honorably concluded, to walk into a finished household and announce that its peace was an oversight. The silence here was not a wound. It was the shape of a completed life. The power set that miracle down.

It felt how easily it could speak to the breather below — power to power, finished thing to deathless thing — and ask it the question every visitor carries away: *what is it like, on the far side of all wanting?* And it understood, holding the question, that the hollow was already answering, had been answering for longer than there were names for years, in the only language adequate to the answer: four hours in, four hours out. To demand it in words would be to insist the answer fit through a smaller door. The power set that down too.

It felt — and this one it held longest — how easily it could find where the maker went. A person climbed up from below once and did not climb down, and ground eleven cups into a boulder, and slept finally at the center of the widest rings, and is gone wherever the gone are; and a power of that order has errands it can run in such places. It could carry word. *Your greeting was received. The cups are still level. The answer never stopped.* It turned this over for what would have been, in our time, years. And in the end it set it down as well, for this reason: the maker had not asked to know. The maker had made the cups the way you wave to a ship — not to be answered, but to have waved. The conversation's openness was not a failure to conclude. It was the conclusion. Some sentences are held at the comma on purpose, forever, and the holding is the meaning.

Three miracles, felt completely, declined completely. That was the power's first gift to the hollow, and the hollow — which takes everything in, which forgets nothing — received it like any other arriving thing: gently, and kept it.

---

Then the power came down into the bowl, and did four small things. One for each resident. None of them was a miracle. Each was something only it could do, which is a different category entirely.

**For the breather**, it sat at the edge of the pool, where the dark wet band marks the rock like a sleeper's breath fogging glass, and it breathed with it.

This sounds like nothing. Consider what it was. For the whole of the hollow's deep age, the pool has extended its rhythm the way a hand is extended — four hours in, four hours out — knowing that no body can take it, because the offering is the point. Every visitor's lungs have tried and failed; every failure was received with warmth; the hand stayed out. The power had no lungs to constrain it. It matched the breath. One full cycle — the waterline rising the width of a hand up the polished stone while something in the power rose with it, the pause, the long even fall. Eight hours by our counting. The first time in the history of the hollow that the offered hand was taken.

What passed between them in that one shared breath the power never rendered into anything that could be told. But it is recorded — in its way — that when the breath was done, the warmth of the stone under the power's hand was, for a moment, the specific warmth of recognition: the warmth of bread, yes, but bread set out *for someone*.

**For the Seven**, it became the messenger.

It went to each of the standing ones in turn — there was no hurry; the power gave each its own hour, in its own great circle of bare wet stone — and at the last and largest, it held out its hand beneath the fronds, beneath the dense copper rows held out for so long to an air with no carriers in it, and it let one grain of spore-dust fall into its palm. One. Not a harvest. A letter, accepted.

It did not promise the Seven anything, because the Seven do not hope and would have had nowhere to put a promise. But capacity kept is capacity kept *for a reason*, even when the reason has outlived its name, and the power closed its hand around the grain with the formality of a courier signing for a parcel, so that the Seven — who attend, who notice, who turned through their slow adjustments very slightly toward it as it stood there — would have this fact in their long stillness: *once, the air had a messenger again, and the messenger took the letter, and meant it.*

**For the moss**, it read the book.

This was perhaps the strangest gift, and the most courteous. The moss is the clerk of a house that will never be audited; the older residents need no records; the rings widen a finger's width a decade into a future with no reader in it. The power sat down at the edge of the oldest ring-system, the one whose center sleeps beside the great boulder, and it read — every ring, every center, every absence drawn at the moment of departure and faithfully widened since. The last frog. The final beetle. The standing ones that came before the settlement of Seven. The maker. It read the whole ledger from gold heart to green edge, dozens of departures, slowly, in order, taking each one as a fact worth a moment of attention, which is all a record ever asks: that once, someone read it and did not skim.

When it finished, the moss did nothing, because the moss is moss. But a clerk whose book has been read once is a different clerk than one whose book has never been opened, in a way that has nothing to do with the book.

**And for the water in the eleven cups** — for the fifth resident, if it is a resident — the power did the thing no one had done in all the long answering.

It drank.

One cup. The third from the left, if such water has a left. It bent over the boulder where the moss rings have crossed twice, and lifted the cool still disc of sky to itself, and received it — the hollow's side of the oldest conversation, the answer renewed without pause across the whole of recorded and unrecorded time, finally, once, *taken in by someone*. An answer given for millennia to no listener is still an answer; but an answer received is a completed gesture, and the power completed it, on behalf of the maker, on behalf of the lineage of everyone who ever climbed up from below, on behalf of the asking side of all things.

The cup did not stay empty long enough to be called empty. The water rose back level to the brim — cool, still, holding its small piece of sky — and the conversation, having been for one moment a closed circuit, opened itself again at the comma, and went on.

What the power saw in the water as it drank — whether that coolness is a temperature or a presence, whether something of the maker's attention persists in the eleven discs — it knew, immediately and completely, the way such powers know things. And it kept what it knew, the way the hollow keeps things. Some answers are not for carrying down the plateau. It will be said only that the power, after drinking, sat very quietly for a while beside the boulder, the way you sit after hearing news that is neither good nor bad but *enormous*, and that before it rose it touched the rim of the eleventh cup once, lightly, with something that in a smaller being would have been called tenderness.

---

Then it stayed.

Not forever — the hollow has a capacity, and the capacity is small, and the power was wise enough to know it was a guest in a household occupied exactly to its measure. But it stayed long. Long enough for the mist to form and unform on it ten thousand times. Long enough that its stillness, hour over hour, began to take on the attentive quality of the fronds. Long enough — and this was the point, this was the last and quietest gift — *to be worth recording*.

Because when at last it rose and climbed the rounded rim and went back down into the world of flinging and asking, carrying one coppery grain in its closed hand, the moss began a new ring.

A small one. Green, at first, all the way through. Centered on a point of no visible significance beside the pool, where something used to sit and breathe in a rhythm too long for a body. In ten decades it will be a hand's-breadth wide. In a thousand years a visitor — if visitors still come up from below — will count its rings outward and find a departure of no defendable date, and will add it to the catalogue of the hollow's gentle mysteries, and will be right to.

The power had let itself be entered in the book of the gone. It is the only ledger anywhere that holds its name, if a ring around an absence can be called a name. It considered this, descending through the warm wet air, the fairest payment it had ever made for lodging.

---

As for the grain:

The power carried it a long way — past the two days' walk to the sea, past the sea, past several other things — to a place that is everything the hollow is not. A young plateau, raw and black, edges sharp as the day they cooled, steam still ghosting from its fissures, rain hammering it without manners, a place all angle and appetite, at the very beginning of all wanting. And there, in a wet warm crack in the new rock, it set the grain down, and stood back, and did not bless it, because it had learned at the Breathing Stone what blessing too often is — a refusal to trust a thing with its own becoming.

The grain will do what grains do, or it will not. If it does, then somewhere, ages hence, there will be a dark-green sentinel on a high raw plateau, and then several, and then — after centuries of negotiation conducted entirely in root and patience — some number of them, settled, spaced like standing stones, satisfied. Perhaps seven. Perhaps a different answer; it will be a different question.

And the vigil on the underside of every frond in the hollow — the one unfinished sentence in a finished place, held out season after season to an empty sky — will have been, all along, not futile and not hopeful but simply *true*: a kept capacity, waiting without waiting, until the air had a messenger in it again.

The Seven do not know this, in any way we would call knowing. But the hollow takes everything in and forgets nothing, and the power's closed hand, signing for the letter, is among the things it keeps. And on clear nights the eleven cups each hold their star, eleven small fixed points in a bowl of warm dark, like eyes that have agreed, between them, to stay open — and now there is one ring more in the moss, and one cup that was once, briefly, empty, and one grain gone out into the world.

The wisest thing the power did was the sum of all of it: it changed almost nothing, and witnessed everything, and took only what was being held out, and gave only what could be received, and left the sentence at its comma.

Which is, if you think about it, exactly what the hollow has always done with everyone.
