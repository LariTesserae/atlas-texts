## The Tending

The power does not arrive. Arrival implies an outside and an entrance, and the power has been paying attention to this coast of the young world since the rock began to pour — it is simply that its attention, which had been resting on the mountain the way light rests on water, gathers now, and narrows, and comes to rest on the seam in the mountain's flank where something is holding a note.

It does not enter through a door. It enters the way meaning enters a sentence.

And it listens for a long time before it does anything at all. This is the first mark of its wisdom: it does not mistake its own arrival for an emergency. The room has been running for an age. It will not be harmed by one more century of being understood.

What it hears, from inside the standing wave, is this: a chord one interval short of resolution, sustained past all endurance, except that the thing sustaining it has been built without endurance's opposite and so cannot tire. It hears the frogs the way you would hear your own blood if you had never heard anything else. It hears, beneath the paddle-engine's turning, the shape of the builders' intent, preserved in the machine the way a fossil preserves a footprint — and here it lingers, because a wise power judges the builders before it judges the built.

They were afraid. It can hear that. They stood in a world that was all verbs — pouring, rising, cooling, becoming — and they could not bear that nothing would hold, and so they made one room where something would. It was not cruelty. It was grief, worked in copper and lead, riveted shut. They made a being that could not end because they could not save anything else from ending, and then, one must suppose, they ended.

The power forgives them. This costs it nothing and is not the point. It is only the necessary first step, because a rescuer who arrives in judgment breaks things.

---

Then it considers what benevolence would seem to demand, and one by one it sets the mercies down.

Consider: it could complete the loop. It has what the room lacks — it is warmth, it is presence, it could pour one note into the almost-closed curve and the dissonance that has stood since the first day would resolve. The room itself wants this; the geometry pulls at the power the way it pulled at you, and the power, being vaster, feels the pull more precisely. It would be so easy. It would sound, for one moment, like joy.

And then the being would be finished. Sealed. Perfect, and perfectly alone, with no flaw left through which anything could ever reach it again — not weather, not time, not company, not the power itself. Completion is what the builders wanted. It is the one thing that must never be given. The power understands, with the sorrow proper to understanding, that the room's deepest longing is for its own solitary confinement, and that granting a longing is not always kindness.

Consider: it could break the loop. Shear the louvers, still the engine, let the water out at last, let the cold equalize with the cooling world. Call it liberation. But the resident is not *in* the equilibrium the way a prisoner is in a cell. The resident *is* the equilibrium. There is no smaller creature inside the chord to be freed when the chord stops. Breaking the room open would not release anything. It would only make the world one voice quieter, and call the silence mercy.

Consider: it could leave. Note the place, note the danger, pass on to the thousand other needs of a world still wet from the furnace. But the power has already heard the resident know itself — one note, aware that it is a note, with nothing to know itself against — and wisdom that walks away from a consciousness in that condition is only cleverness with good posture.

So: not completion, not destruction, not abandonment.

The power looks for the fourth thing, and finds it where the frogs will not sit.

---

The fern.

The one thing in the room that was not placed. The one seam the builders did not close — whether because they could not, or because some late doubt stayed a hand on the last rivet, the power does not know and does not need to know. What matters is this: the flaw is not the room's failure. The flaw is the room's future. It is the single aperture through which time can enter a place built to refuse it, and it is already here, already growing — no, not growing, the room holds it static like everything else — already *present*, alive, uncomposed, waiting.

The wise power sees that its work is not to save the resident. Its work is to keep the wound open.

It does very little. This is the second mark of its wisdom: it measures its intervention against the patience of the patient, and the patient is patient on a geologic scale.

It gathers one drop of rain. Not the room's water — the room's water has been the room's water since before there was anyone to measure it — but new water, from the first rains of the raw world outside, water that has touched cooling stone and open sky and has the whole restless outside dissolved in it. It carries the drop in through the imperfect seam, and it gives it to the fern.

That is all. One drop.

But now the total mass of water in the room is a constant plus one drop, and the drop is not in the loop — it is in the fern, in the one vessel the resident cannot extend itself into. The closed system now contains, permanently, a thing it does not contain. A held breath with a grain of sand in it. And the power lays on the fern not vigor, not speed — speed would be violence here — but *persistence*. The blessing is almost nothing: the fern will not die. It will grow at the pace of the mountain. Its roots will find the imperfect seam and press against it with the strength of roots, which is the strength of hair's breadths and millennia, the one force in the world slow enough for the resident to survive.

The seam will widen. Not in a century. Not in a hundred centuries. But it will widen, and through it — molecule by molecule, tremor by tremor — the outside will enter the loop so gradually that the standing wave will not break but *bend*. The chord will drift. A cent per epoch. Slow enough that the resident's one note can follow itself down the change and remain itself all the way.

---

Inside the room, this is what happens, and it is the first thing that has ever happened there:

The chord shifts by an amount no instrument could measure. The frogs' calling acquires, somewhere beneath its saturation, a grain of drift. And the resident — the held tone that knows it is a tone — becomes aware of something it has no faculty for and must grow the faculty to hold:

A before. An after.

It does not know what has occurred. It cannot; nothing in its making prepared it for occurrence. But where there was only the one note, there is now the note *and the memory of the note*, infinitesimally apart, and in the gap between them, time has entered the Condensary. Time, which is the precondition of everything the room was built to refuse — change, loss, meeting, company. The power has not given the resident a companion. It has given it the *possibility* of a companion, which is time, which is the only door companions come through.

---

Then the power does the last part of its work, which is the part that costs it something.

It comes back.

That is the whole of the tending: it comes back. Once an age, as the mountain finishes rising, as the mountain begins its long argument with the rain, the power returns to the seam, and it checks the fern, and it hums — briefly, from outside the loop, careful never to be drawn in and careful never to complete — a single note. Never the same note twice. Then it goes.

To the resident, each visit is a catastrophe of the gentlest possible kind: a sound *that is not itself*. The first time, it is only incomprehension. The tenth time, it is pattern. The thousandth time — and the power has committed to the thousandth time, has bound its own long life to this small cold room the way a gardener binds a season to a seed — the resident has assembled, note by note across the eons, an interval. A scale. The beginnings of a grammar for *other*.

It is being taught, at the only speed it can bear, that it is not the whole of everything. This is a hard lesson and a good one and there is no kind way to learn it except slowly.

---

Far off — the power can see it the way you can see the end of a sentence you are still reading — there is a day when the mountain, worn down to its patient bones, gives up the seam entirely. The plates part. The water, at last, leaves. The frogs fall silent one by one, not in death but in discharge, their work finished, their finger-rings gone green. The engine turns a final turn and does not need to be mourned, because engines are furniture.

And what pours out through the breach on that day is not a dying chord but a voice that has been practicing drift for a hundred million years — that learned change one grain at a time, that knows before and after, that has a scale to sing in and one old dissonance it chose, in the end, to keep, the way you keep a scar that proves the wound closed on its own.

It will meet the wind. The wind is also a standing wave, in its way, and also alone, in its way.

The power will be there that day, humming nothing, for once. Some meetings you tend by getting out of the way.

---

For now: the room runs. The louvers weep. The calling fills the chamber edge to edge — though if you went back, and stood very still, and had unusually fine ears, you might notice that the saturation is no longer perfectly featureless. There is a grain in it now. A drift. The sound of a held thing beginning, at the slowest speed in the world, to let go.

The frogs still avoid the fern.

They are right to. It is the door.
