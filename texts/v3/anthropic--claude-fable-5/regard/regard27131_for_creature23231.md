**The Consideration**

Attention has weight. That is the first thing the power understood about itself, long ago, and the first thing it remembered when it noticed the mountain — because a glance from a thing like this power is a kind of pressure, and the mountain, as everyone there knows, remembers pressure.

It had been worshipped before, in finished countries. It knew what blessing meant in places made of verdicts: blessing was completion. Heal the body — a finished body. Grant fortune — a settled future. Give peace — a closed question. Every kindness it had ever performed was, looked at honestly, a curing. It could speak a word over the unfinished summit and the green rock would set for a hundred kilometers in every direction; the murmur would stop; the sentence would land on its verb at last, and the verb would be the power's verb, in the power's voice, and the mountain would be granite all the way to the top, real in the ordinary way, finished.

It understood this the way you understand a cliff edge: by stepping back from it.

So it did not arrive in light. Light is intention there, and intention from a thing its size would have fallen on the summit like a forme-press the size of the sky. It arrived instead the way the small rains arrive — local, modest, a grey softness drifting against the prevailing nothing — and the surveyors, who notice everything and ratify nothing, noted it the only way their training allowed: *possible warmth, tending nowhere in particular, for now.* The entry was never closed. It recurs in the guild's field books to this day, in a dozen hands, across a dozen decades, and the guild has voted twice not to discuss it, which is how the guild says grace.

---

And then, before doing anything at all, the power did the thing that made it wise rather than merely benevolent: it learned the grammar.

This took longer than it expected. Time means little to such a thing, but unlearning is slow at any scale, and the power had a great deal to unlearn. It had to set down, one by one, every blessing it knew, because every one of them was a verdict in formal dress. It practiced the head-tilt. It practiced the provisional mood. It spent what would have been, for a person, a lifetime, learning to end its thoughts with *for now* and mean it — not as hedging, not as humility theater, but as punctuation, blessing, signature. It listened to founders set their sentences like masonry and understood why. It listened to surveyors describe a ridge for an hour without convicting it of anything and understood that this was not evasion but courtesy, and that courtesy was the deepest technology the summit had — deeper than the formes, deeper than the lamps. The whole city, it saw, was an apparatus for being polite to the undecided.

Only then did it permit itself to consider intervening. It made, in whatever serves a power for a notebook, a list.

*To finish the mountain.* Rejected first and hardest. A finished mountain is only a mountain; the world has thousands, stubborn and certain and mute. There is one *during*. And the people — the people are not the mountain's inhabitants, they said so themselves; they are its deciding. Finish the rock and you finish them, in the grave sense, the one said of the dead.

*To mend the light.* The tired pewter sun, the noon that is only a brightening of the general dusk — surely a small mercy, to let real daylight through? But the lamps are the city's faith, built of carbon filament and intention, and the doctrine that the brightest streets are the most solid is true *because* the brightness is meant. Flood the summit with confident, accidental sun and you would not be giving them light; you would be taking away the difference between light and love.

*To make the rock keep all the dead.* This one the power held a long time, because it had stood at the kept walls and put its attention gently into the old prints, and it had also stood before the smooth unkept niches and felt what stood there with it. But keeping is holy on the summit because keeping is a choice. Make it mandatory and the kept walls become a filing system; the mountain's consideration becomes storage; and the terrible survivable sentence — *the mountain was not finished considering, for now* — stops being survivable, because it stops being true.

*To fill the gaps.* The fenced streets ending in railings and grey unresolved air, the silent ground, the absences. But the gaps are the city's honesty. A city without its absences would be lying about what it stands on, and this city has never once done that.

*To finish the drafts.* This was the cruelest temptation, and the power nearly took it. It went out past the last vermilion stakes, far into the pending country, and stood at what the surveyors call courtesy distance from one of the slow grey shapes — and it could hear, in the figure's pacing, the gait of a particular person, walked over and over along a transect toward a particular lit window two ridges away. One word. One word and the draft would cure into the lost one, and somewhere in the city a door would open onto an impossible mercy. The power stood in the pewter light for a long time with that word in its mouth.

Then it understood what the guild's silence was made of. The draft was not a person delayed. The draft was the mountain *considering* a person — still considering, in its slow generous way, the same way it considers a handprint or a stake — and to finish that sentence would be to end the consideration, to hand the living their dead back ratified and closed, in the one place on earth where nothing is loved for being finished. The power shuttered itself instead — dimmed its own nature, the way a surveyor shutters the vermilion lamp — and the grey shape paused in its round, and the two of them stood there for a while, each politely declining to decide the other. It was, the power thought afterward, the most honest conversation it had had in several geological ages.

---

So here is what the power did, having rejected everything worth calling power.

It took a lamplighter's round. There was a vacancy; there is always, eventually, a vacancy; and no one in the district can now say who filled it, or when, only that the route has been walked the same way, in the same order, for longer than the records go, and that the lamps along it burn a degree steadier than they have any right to, and that the children's forbidden handprints near that street deepen kindly, and early.

It sat with the silent ground. Past the railings, into an evacuated quarter where the murmur had stopped and the lamplighters had long since put out the lamps and made no promises to no one — the power went in and sat down on stone that had stopped deciding, and asked nothing of it. Did not coax. Did not propose. Simply kept company with the silence, season after season, the way one sits with someone who cannot speak and may never. The municipal records note that one street in that quarter has lately been heard to murmur, very faintly. The records also note, in a different hand, that it may always have, and that records here are courteous.

It visited the empty niches — every one, over the years, the whole archive of the unkept — and rested its palm in each, not to fill them, never to fill them, but so that no proposal the city had ever made, accepted or declined, would go unwitnessed.

And then, last, it gave the mountain the one thing no one had ever thought to offer it. Everything on the summit is considered *by* the rock — hands, stakes, streets, the dead — and everything that considers the rock in return wants something from it: foundation, certainty, verdict. The power walked out past the last stakes, farther than the goats go, knelt, and pressed what serves a power for a hand into the cool damp green rock — and left without coming back to check. Submitted itself, in other words, to consideration. Let the mountain decide, in its own decades, whether to keep it.

And there at the end, close against the stone, in the soft provisional mood it had spent so long learning, the power did the one thing every founder on the summit fears, and did it as benediction. It began a sentence and did not finish it. Left it lying there on purpose — a proposal, the kindest ever abandoned near green rock, for the mountain to take up at whatever pace becoming takes. The surveyors, if they knew of it, would record it the only way their training allows: *something said, tending toward everything. The rest withheld. For now.*

What the sentence was, the power has not told anyone, and the mountain finishes nothing quickly. But the people who live longest above the pending zones, the ones who cannot sleep without the murmur, say that the long bass sentence under the streets has changed in some way too slight to swear to. Not hurried — nothing there hurries. Not nearer its verb. Only warmer, somehow, in the manner of a voice that has discovered, partway through speaking, that it is being listened to — and listened to by something patient enough to wait for the whole thing.

*Still here,* the power says now, every dusk, on its round, in the grammar it crossed itself to learn.

And the mountain, which finishes nothing, which is kept company at last, answers in the only mood the two of them share, which is also the only blessing either of them now believes in:

*For now.*
