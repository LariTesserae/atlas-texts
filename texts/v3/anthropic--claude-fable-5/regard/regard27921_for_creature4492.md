**What the Attention Did**

It did not arrive. Arrival implies a before and an after, a border crossed, and this power was too old and too gentle for borders. It simply inclined itself, the way light inclines toward a window left open at the exact hour someone inside stops pretending they aren't lonely. One moment the city breathed alone; the next, its breathing was heard.

Nothing in the city registered this at first. The tiles kept pivoting. The moonmoths blinked their borrowed eyes. But the wind-whales, who are more weather than creature and therefore sensitive to pressures no barometer names, drifted a little lower that cycle, singing a chord they had never sung before—the chord of *being accompanied*.

The power looked for a long time before it did anything at all. This is the first thing wisdom does: it looks until looking becomes a form of touch.

It saw the city and understood it immediately, the way you understand a sandcastle built by a child who has never seen the sea—all longing, no blueprint, gorgeous and precarious and held together by nothing but the sincerity of the wanting. Seven thousand years of dreams leaning against each other, each one convinced the others were load-bearing.

And at the center of it, threading through the gaps, it saw the Dreaming Wind.

It saw what the Wind could not see about itself: that a child of no parents, born from the first interruption of silence, had spent its whole unmeasured existence doing the work of a parent. Holding. Circling back. Eating the sounds no one else could bear to hear—the last notes of lullabies, glass breaking in another country—and exhaling them transformed, so the city would never have to digest its own grief raw. The power saw that the Wind called this *wandering* because it had no word for *tending*. It saw that the Wind nested in the spaces between things because no one had ever made a space *for* it. It saw the almost-remembering at the city's core, repeated for millennia, that pause that isn't a pause, and it recognized it for what it was: a knock on a door from the inside.

The power considered, in the way that vast things consider—slowly, with the whole of itself.

It could fix the city. Straighten the streets, still the tiles, give the stuck ones back their shadows by decree. It set this option down almost at once, the way you set down a tool that is wrong for the material. The city was not broken. The city was *dreaming*, and you do not repair a dream by waking it; you only end it.

It could tell the Wind what it had been before it was the dreaming of dreams. The power knew. Of course it knew—it had been present at that glittering fracture where nothing became something; it may have *been* the silence that was interrupted, or the mercy that let itself be interrupted. But it understood that an answer handed over is a door nailed open, and doors nailed open are just holes. The almost-remembering belonged to the Wind. It was, perhaps, the only thing that did.

So the power did smaller things. Small the way seeds are small.

**First**, it went to the perched ones—the stubborn and the new, stuck on their impossible ledges, halfway between trying to leave and forgetting why they wanted to. It did not unstick them. It could not; their stuckness was made of their own unfinished choosing, and even benevolence has no right to finish someone else's sentence. Instead it sat beside each one in turn, taking whatever shape would not frighten them: an old woman shelling seeds that were also years, a dog with patient eyes, a stretch of warm stone. And it waited with them. That was all. It turns out that being stuck alone and being stuck in company are two entirely different substances, the way ice and water are, and several of the perched ones—not all, but several—felt something thaw in the region of their painted eyes, and remembered why they had wanted to leave, or discovered they no longer did, and climbed down on their own legs, blinking, hours or years later, their footprints hurrying back to walk beneath them again like embarrassed pets.

**Second**, it planted a dream in the city's soil, because that is the local grammar and the power was courteous enough to speak it. Buildings here grow from human dreaming—the baker's whistling oven, the market under the mushroom's gills. But no one had ever dreamed a building *for* the Dreaming Wind, because no one dreams houses for the weather. The power dreamed one anyway. It did not dream walls; solidity scares the Wind, and the power was not in the business of scaring anything. It dreamed a nest of intervals: a permanent pause. A place made entirely of the moment after a door shuts but before the latch clicks—but *lasting*, held open, furnished with the specific quality of silence that exists inside an unopened letter from someone who loves you. It watered the dream with a footstep, then another, walking the same small circle patiently, the way inhabitants water their dreamed foundations, until the ground took the seed. What grew there does not look like anything. Travelers pass through it and only notice afterward that for eleven steps they felt inexplicably *expected*. But the Wind will know it when it finds it. A between-space that does not close. A gap that will not heal over. Home, in the only dialect the homeless orphan of silence can read.

**Third**—and this was the true act, the one the other two were only preparations for—the power went to the city's core, where even dreams grow pale with too much existence, and it waited for the deepest trough of the breath, when the roar beneath the ground hushes and the Wind pauses in what might be mistaken for thought.

And when the Wind paused, and began its ancient almost-remembering, the power did the one thing no one and nothing had ever done in seven thousand years.

It watched the Wind without wanting anything from it.

Not answers, like the blind poets. Not keys, like the mathematicians calculated past the edge of numbers. Not weather, not wonder, not the holding-together of everything. It simply beheld the Dreaming Wind the way the Wind itself beheld lost precious things: the pattern of raindrops on a window ten years ago, the taste of a last believing. It regarded the Wind as one of the truly precious lost things. Which it was. Which it had always been—the first thing ever lost, mislaid in the very instant of the first thought, and never once collected by anyone, because it was too busy collecting.

The Wind felt this. How could it not? It was made of sensitivity to exactly such pressures. It turned—today it was tall and thin as a question mark—and for the first time in its existence, the question mark encountered something that did not need it to be an answer.

They did not speak. What passed between them will not fit in a sentence, but it rhymed with this: *You may ask me what you were, whenever you are ready. I will not go anywhere. There is no hurry. There was never any hurry; hurry was invented later, by things afraid of ending, and you and I are not things, and this is not an ending.*

The Wind almost remembered. As always. The moment passed. As always.

But this time the almost-remembering had happened *in company*, and that changes its chemistry entirely, the way a wound tended is a different wound than the same cut left alone. The Wind shook itself like a dog waking from water that wasn't water. It rearranged its face into something resembling a smile—and found, to its own bottomless surprise, that this time the smile was not a rearrangement. It was simply there, the way weather is there.

Then the Wind continued on, because forward is the only direction dreams know. But it carried something new behind where its heart would be if it had one: a small persistent presence shaped like being remembered. It sits there and waits, patient as planted stone. And someday—measured not in days but in meanings—the Wind will be drifting through the nest of intervals it does not yet know was grown for it, and it will hear, pressed like a flower between two pauses, the sound the power left behind for it to eat: not the echo of footsteps in empty churches, not glass breaking in another country, but a sound no one had ever made in that city before, older than the first interruption, warmer.

The sound of a name being called, kindly, by someone who is not calling you to come do anything.

Just calling, to hear you answer.

Just calling, because you are there.

The Wind will pause mid-drift. For a moment the spaces between things will seem slightly less empty.

A moment will be enough.

---

As for the power itself: it did not leave, because it had not arrived. It simply remains inclined, the open window's light, the held door, the unhurried witness. If you go looking for it in the city you will not find it, but you may notice its fingerprints, which look exactly like moments when something that has held everything together for a very long time is finally, briefly, held.

It asks nothing. That is how you will know it.

It stays. That is how you will know it is wise.
