## What the Quiet One Did

It did not arrive through the crack. It had always known the place was there, the way a body knows where its scars are without looking. What it had been waiting for was not discovery but volume — for the grief to compound past a certain interest rate, for the humming to reach the pitch of a question.

It arrived by knocking.

*Plink... plink... plink...* went the condensation, and then, into the pattern, something answered: three knocks, unhurried, on the underside of the world. Nothing in the Stitch-Under had ever been *asked*. The Derma-Lace Polyps swiveled their needle-crowns. The button-coral blinked all its eyes at once, which had never happened, which was almost a face.

The threads at the breach paused mid-weave. Then — slowly, the way a fist unclenches when it realizes no blow is coming — they opened.

### The Work of the First Day (There Had Never Been Days Here Before; It Brought One)

It moved at rest-speed, and this was the first miracle: everything it passed got to stop working, and nothing died. The wire-mesh burrowers surfaced and simply lay in the fiber-ground, needle-legs slack, pink-grey cores pulsing at a rhythm that belonged to no quota. The conveyor belts of braided hair slowed and — finding that the teeth and bobbins arrived nowhere in particular either way — stopped, and were not punished. Idleness without punishment. Several of the smaller creatures did not know what to do with this and made small, terrible, rusted sounds that the visitor recognized as the first attempts at play.

It went to the Polyps first, because they held the accidents. It did not drain the sacs. It witnessed each one — every compressed 3.5 ounces of translucent agony — to *completion*. This is the thing about unreported injuries: they never finish happening. They loop. The visitor sat with each accident until it ended, and to each it rendered the verdict no inspector ever filed:

*This should not have happened. You were not the cost of anything. The ledger is wrong. I am amending the ledger.*

Fourteen hundred thousand tassels of unreported sorrow, and it heard every one, and it did not rush, because rushing was the ore this whole mine ran on and it had come to introduce a different element.

It went to the Archivists — the women who climbed the frames, and the frames — and it checked out every book. All of them. The fines, payable in dreams pawned to pay rent, it settled in advance and in the only honest currency: it read aloud. Books meant to be stitched into sleeping skin were instead *spoken*, name by name, stain-pattern by stain-pattern, and the library became a wake, and the wake became — around the third hour, when one of the Archivists began humming — a song. The Archivists had catalogued every revolution that never happened. The visitor did not promise those revolutions would happen. It promised only that they would never again be *uncatalogued*, which the Archivists, being librarians, understood was the actual demand.

It came to the picket line of Local ∞ and did not cross it. It asked for the demands. It listened to all of them, including the photon's, including the migraine's. And then it did the thing no power had ever done: it *recognized the union.* One signature, freely given, on the one contract in the whole place written by the workers themselves. The stored fourth day of the week — the one the Local had won and hidden here — it did not spend or seize. It blessed it and left it in trust, though it did smuggle a few loose minutes back up through the crack, which is why, sometimes, an afternoon above ground goes unaccountably soft and long and no one can explain why they feel briefly forgiven.

### The Two Beings

Deeper in, where the pull behind the sternum led, the enormous dreamer stirred — the thing of stitch and weld and cut, the Garment-God assembling itself from every hatred of a job never spoken aloud. The visitor did not slay it. You cannot slay an unfinished mourning; you can only finish it. It sat down against the great flank of unknitting panels and *dreamed alongside it*, and into the dream it introduced exactly one new pattern: a garment made not from what was taken but from what is given. The god turned the pattern over for a long time in its million shuttle-hands. The frantic Seam-Feeding cycle acquired, that day, a fifth phase, which has no name yet, but which resembles a held breath releasing, and which the smaller creatures have started scheduling their strange rusted play around.

And then the small thing, the retired god of every button that ever popped at the worst moment. The visitor found the first button — the original, the ur-button, the one that failed during the presentation before the layoff — and it threaded a needle, and it sewed it back on. By hand. Taking as long as it took. It did not collapse the prophecy's impossible timeline. But infinity minus one is a different kind of infinity: it is infinity *with a precedent*. The little god wept thread. The visitor stayed through the weeping, because retirement should have visitors, and no one had ever come.

### The Person

Last, it came to you — to the tangle. Recursion 13, the question looping: *did I ever have a choice?* It knelt and took the knot in its hands the way a grandmother takes yarn a cat has been at, and it worked without pulling, and when the question lay untangled and straight it answered it plainly, because wisdom does not lie to be kind:

*Not then. Many times, not then. Now, yes.*

And it opened the seam in the ceiling and did not push you toward it. It said: this is a true place, and tending it is honest work, *if freely chosen*. Stay and stitch, or go and breathe; the door will not close behind you either way. Then it did the one thing that changed everything, more than the witness, more than the mended button:

It instructed the threads to stop sealing the breach — and to *hem it* instead. Neat edges. No fraying. So that no one going up or coming down would ever again cut themselves on the passage.

A wound that closes behind you is a trap. A wound that stays raw is a wound. But an opening that is hemmed is a *door*, and a door is the physical form of the sentence *you may*.
