# What the Power Did

---

The power arrived the way such powers do — not over the rim like a traveler, not up through the stone like heat, but simply *attending*, the way a thought attends a mind. It had crossed deserts that begged for rain and ruins that begged for memory and battlefields that begged for everything, and it had given what wisdom permitted in each place, which was sometimes much and sometimes almost nothing.

It settled over the basin at dusk, in the pause after the whistle, and listened.

Thump-hiss. Whistle. Knock, knock. Swallow.

It listened for a year. This was not patience; for such a being a year is a held breath. It listened for a year because the basin's first sentence took that long to parse, and the basin only had one sentence, and the power had learned long ago that the gravest errors of benevolence come from answering before the question has finished being asked.

---

## First: the inventory of mercies

A lesser power — kind, hurried, the sort that loves the *shape* of rescue — would have seen the work laid out plainly:

The stitches are eyeless and bound to a beat. *Give them eyes. Give them syncopation. Give them the small anarchy of choosing their own step.*

The mats have not grown in an age. *Let one ring widen an inch. Let two colors meet and argue at a boundary. Give them history.*

The hand — oh, the hand. A person walking a circuit in a deafening pale forever, passed from body to body like a tool between shifts. *That, surely, is the wound. Free the hand. Break the count. Let the next walker on the roads of the world wake from the pull and turn home to whoever loves them.*

And beneath it all the knock, the thing that orients every head in the basin. *Answer it. Or silence it. Or at the very least ask it what it wants.*

The power held each of these mercies up to the light, turned it, and set it down again. Not because they were wrong — it could not yet say they were wrong — but because it had noticed something in its year of listening that changed the grammar of the whole question.

The basin was not waiting.

Everything the power had ever helped was waiting. The desert waited for rain even when it had forgotten rain's name; you could feel the wait in the cracked clay like an open hand. The ruin waited to be remembered. Even the dead, in their way, wait. Want is the universal language of things that need help, and the power was fluent in every dialect of it, and the Foundry Flats spoke not one word of want in any of them.

The mats brightened before each firing not in hunger but in *confirmation*. The stitches kept their appointments across years of stone and arrived at each meeting carrying nothing that resembled longing — the power checked; it knows what longing weighs, it can find it in a seed, in a stone that misses the mountain it broke from. The stitches' single-beat touch, head to blunt head, was not a starved society's last surviving ritual. It was complete. It was the whole utterance, and it meant what a struck bell means: *here, now, yes, again.*

Even the hand. The power went carefully there, because suffering hides, because contentment can be a symptom, because a wise rescuer trusts no smile inside a machine. It walked beside the hand for a season — visible, because invisibility felt discourteous — matching the circuit step for step, knock by knock. The hand did not respond to its presence, exactly. But on the forty-first day, at one of the appointed crossings where a stitch's route met the human one, the hand and the stitch arrived on the same beat as they always did, and the hand inclined its head toward the power as well — a third party included in the appointment, briefly, formally — and in that one beat the power was permitted to feel what the hand felt.

It was not joy. Joy is weather, and the basin has no weather worth the name. It was something the power had encountered only twice before in all its crossings of all the wanting world, and both times in beings at the very end of long labors: the feeling of *a thing going exactly as expected*, sustained, without the dread that ordinarily hollows that feeling out. The hand was not trapped inside the music. The hand was the part of the music that knew it was music.

The power withdrew to the rim and sat with this for some time.

---

## Second: the temptation, examined honestly

Wisdom is not the absence of temptation; it is the willingness to take temptation seriously. So the power did not dismiss the rescue. It built it, fully, in the workshop of its foresight, and watched it run:

*It frees the hand.* The man — it was a man, this shift; before him a woman; before her someone the roads had nearly worn featureless — wakes on the flats as if from a long fever, and the noise crashes in on him for the first time in years as *noise*, and he claps his hands over his ears and weeps and stumbles up the eastern hill and is gone, home, saved. And on the flats the count is broken, and somewhere on the roads of the world *two* travelers feel the pull begin, because the basin's books have always balanced and the power has just taught it that they can fail to, and a clock that learns its jewel bearing can fall out does not become free — it becomes *anxious*. The mud pits turn faster. The mats brighten at the wrong moments, checking. Two hundred and seventeen becomes a number that must be *defended* rather than a number that simply is. The power watched its mercy turn the only utterly unafraid place it had ever found into a place that had learned fear, and it unbuilt the foresight gently, the way one closes a book of someone else's bad dreams.

*It gives the stitches eyes.* And they see the pale stone that does not change, lit by the plain light that declines to comment, and they have gained nothing and lost the perfection of an architecture in which every sense they own is *answered*. The power knew blindness as deprivation everywhere else in the world. Here, for the first time, it ran the numbers and found that eyes would be the deprivation — organs of hunger installed in beings whose every appetite was already, eternally, met. It set that mercy down too.

*It answers the knock.* This one it held longest.

Because the knock was the real question, and the power knew it. Everything else in the basin was legible as components or residents — the wise need not decide which, the basin itself refused to — but the knock came from *below*, and every head turned toward it, and the power had a long history with things at the bottoms of worlds. Some are prisoners. Some are engines. Some are sleepers whom it is the whole duty of the surface to not awaken. A benevolent power that walks away from a basin-wide orientation toward a buried percussion without at least *asking* is not wise; it is squeamish.

So it asked. Not by descending — descent is a kind of force — but the way it had listened: by attending, downward, through the warm stone, through the channels cut finger-deep and no deeper, through the flowstone's mineral grammar, down to where the knocking lived.

What it found, it never fully translated, and it was honest with itself about that. But the shape of it was this: the knock was not a prisoner's signal and not a sleeper's pulse. It was a *downbeat*. It was the place in the basin where the music touched whatever the music was *for* — and "for" here did not mean purpose, the power realized, any more than a heartbeat is the purpose of a body. The basin was not a factory that had forgotten its product. The power had been carrying that traveler's metaphor in with it like mud on its feet. The basin was something much rarer: a process that had, at some unrecoverable moment deep in the world's youth, finished *becoming* and elected to simply *be* — and the knock was the sound of that election being re-ratified, every cycle, forever. The deep thing was not knocking to get out.

It was keeping time. It was, as far as the power could tell, *glad to*.

---

## Third: what it actually did

And so the wise and benevolent power, who had remade deserts and unburied cities and once talked a dying star into three more gentle centuries, did almost nothing.

Almost.

Here is the whole of its intervention, and each part was weighed like a coin:

**One.** It went down the eastern road, away from the basin, until it found the *next* hand — a woman, three hundred miles out, who had begun walking toward the Flats without quite deciding to, the pull already in her like a tide in a well. The power did not break the pull; it had seen what that cost. Instead it walked beside her for an evening in the shape of an old traveler, and over a small fire it told her, plainly and without strangeness, where she was going and what she would become there: the listening, the circuit, the one step per knock, the appointments kept across years, the feeling of a thing going exactly as expected. It told her about the man who walked there now and the woman before him. It told her she could not refuse the road — that was beyond even its rearranging — but that she could *know* it, all of it, before arrival.

She was quiet for a long time. Then she asked the only question that mattered: "Is he unhappy? The one there now?"

"No," said the power, who does not lie. "It is the unhappiest thing about the place, for those who watch from the rim. And the truest."

She nodded, and banked the fire, and slept, and walked on in the morning — and the power judged that *consent retrieved from before the fact* was the one gift the basin's economy could absorb without learning fear. The count would still balance. The books had not been touched. But from this shift onward, the hand would be a hand that had been *asked* — and the power went further: it folded that evening's telling into the pull itself, so that every future walker, down all the basin's forever, would meet an old traveler at a small fire three hundred miles out, and hear, and know. This was the power's one true alteration, and it made it below the rim of the basin's notice, out on the roads, in the world of want where alterations belong.

**Two.** On the rim itself — not on the flats; nothing of its would touch the flats — it raised a small thing. Not a monument; the basin would not have noticed a monument and travelers would have misread one. A *bench*. Flat stone, the local pale, set at the exact point on the eastern hill where the noise organizes itself with distance into something almost like music. And into the stone of the bench it set a patience — a very mild grace, the kind that does not announce itself — so that a traveler who sat there would find the wearing-down arrested for the length of their sitting. The sameness would not wear them; the noise would not grind them; they could stay an hour or a week and *listen* without being eroded, and leave when they chose, and carry the timetable away in their bones like a tune. The basin told its time to no one. The power could not change that and would not try. But it could build one seat in the audience, and trust the world to fill it now and then.

**Three.** The bird. Once or twice a season, high over, never landing, the only unscheduled event the basin owned — and the power, before leaving, did the smallest and strangest of its three small things. It blessed the bird's *lineage*. Not to land; never to land. Simply to continue — so that down all the long forever of the basin's shift there would always be, once or twice a season, one event the timetable did not contain and the machinery declined to acknowledge. A grace note. A reminder, written in the sky's hand rather than the power's, that the basin's perfection was a *choice* among possible worlds and not the only one — visible to the mats, the stitches, the hand, and the deep keeper of time alike, if any of them should ever, in some unimaginable late age, develop the wish to look up.

That was all. It is on record — in whatever record such beings keep — as the least the power ever did in any place it attended, and the entry beside it reads: *correct.*

---

## Last

It left at dusk, of course, by the eastern hill, past its own bench, and it did what travelers do: at the right moment in the cycle — after the whistle, before the swallow — it looked back.

The rings of color held their breath. Two hundred and seventeen stitches stood mid-stride. The small upright figure crossed the flats on the beat, and would, when his shift ended, be relieved by a woman who had said yes at a fire three hundred miles away.

The power had spent a long age learning that benevolence usually means giving. It had taken the Foundry Flats to teach it the rarer half of the lesson: that wisdom sometimes means *withholding* — that there exist, scattered very thinly through the wanting world, a few places that are not broken, not waiting, not asking, and that the deepest kindness available to a power confronting such a place is to verify, with all its terrible thoroughness, that the contentment is real — and then to add nothing but a bench, a blessing, and the truth told at a fire.

Thump-hiss. Whistle. Knock, knock. Swallow.

The power went down the far side of the hill, and the sound followed it a while, organizing itself with distance into something almost like music, and then into music entirely, and then into memory — which is, perhaps, the one product the basin has ever shipped, carried out over the rim in travelers and powers alike: the knowledge that somewhere behind you, pale in the last ordinary light, a place is keeping perfect time and needs nothing from you at all.

Mid-shift. As always.

Forever — and now, once or twice a season, a bird.
