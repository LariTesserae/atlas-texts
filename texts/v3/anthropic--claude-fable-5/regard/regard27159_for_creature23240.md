# The Attention

It came the way the clouds come, which is to say it had crossed a great many places before this one and would cross a great many after, and it almost did not stop.

What stopped it was the mistake. From its height, the figure on the open pavement read as a cairn — grey against grey, motionless past the patience of anything alive — and the power, which had not been wrong about a thing in a very long time, slowed in its crossing to look again. The cairn moved. It turned its head a few degrees to follow the edge of an incoming cloud, and was still again, and the power understood that it had been deceived by a creature doing one thing entirely, and it settled over the plateau like weather and stayed.

The first instinct of power is to act. The first act of wisdom is to wait. The power dimmed itself to the pearl of the cloud-light and learned to move at the speed of moss, and for a long time — it did not count the years, and neither did anyone below it — it did nothing at all except the thing that, on this plateau, turned out to be the native verb: it watched.

---

Benevolence begins as a search for pain. That is its trade; that is what its hands are shaped for. So the power went through Vell the way a mason's fingers go along a wall, feeling for the joint that has opened, the block that has shifted, the place where the water gets in.

It found hunger nowhere. It found fear almost nowhere, and where it found fear it found it brief and bodily — a child too close to the rim, a fox surprised — fear without expectation under it, fear that closed again like water. It found grief, and examined the grief carefully, and found it fitted. The grief of Vell sat against the lives around it with joints you could not slide a leaf into. When a death came out of season and the whole plateau went quiet, the power listened to that quiet from the inside, and found that it was not denial and not numbness. It was a held breath, communal and exact, the silence of an entire country building a cairn with its attention. The power had stood in the wreckage of worlds that screamed. It had never heard anything like that quiet, and it was honest enough to admit it could not improve on it.

It found two open joints, and only two.

The first was the threads — the restless ones, two or three in a generation, who took the rim-stairs down and did not come back, and who fell out of the plateau's keeping the moment the wind of the lowlands took them apart.

The second was time itself. For the power could see what the Vell could not, having no tense for it: that the equilibrium was a held gesture, not an eternal one. The stone was young. Somewhere far down the years, the geology would resume — the talus would finally gather at the cliff-feet, the rivers would learn their courses and cut them deep, the flood-marks would drown or go dry, the sharp edges would soften like everything softens. Not soon. Not for an age of ages. But the power lived at the scale where *not soon* and *now* sit in the same room, and it could see the far side of Vell's terrace, and the far side was not identical in kind to here.

---

Then came the temptations, and the power, being wise, took each one out and turned it over in the light before setting it aside.

It could hold the gesture forever. It had the strength: it could fix the stone sharp, pin the clouds to their schedules, make the equilibrium not deep but absolute. It set this aside almost at once, with something like a shudder. A gesture held forever stops being a gesture. The plateau was beautiful the way a thing mid-motion is beautiful, and to freeze it would be to change Vell from a thing that *is* into a thing that is *kept* — and the Vell, it had noticed, had no word for forever any more than for ruin. *Set* did not mean eternal. *Set* meant exactly itself. Eternity would have been a foreign block mortared into a dry-laid wall, and the whole structure would have been false from that day, even if no one living could feel it.

It could loosen the place instead — give them rivers that went somewhere, weather that meant something, the dignity of erosion. It set this aside with mild embarrassment, recognizing the impulse for what it was: the unbearable visitor's complaint, dressed up as a gift. You do not wake the sleeping because you yourself cannot sleep.

It could give the language a future tense. A small thing, a single hinge of grammar, and the Vell would be able to ask the questions visitors asked, and answer them, and prepare. It held this one longer, because it looked the most like kindness. Then it set it aside. A gift of vocabulary is a gift of everything the vocabulary carries. *Will be* arrives with *might not be* folded inside it; the word for ruin is the seed of ruins. They had built a country on the absence of that hinge. It would not be the power that installed it.

And it could answer the question — the one the visitors went still over, whether the people kept the equilibrium or the equilibrium kept the people, whether the threads left because they were restless or were restless because they had already been let go. Powers can look at such things directly. It looked.

What it found was not an answer. What it found was a fit: mason and stone reading each other for so long that the authorship had been ground smooth — wide and printless, like the fingertips of the people themselves. The question came apart in its hands the way the falls came apart in the wind. The power put it down with respect, understanding at last why the language refused to conjugate toward it. Some questions are not unanswered. Some questions are simply built wrong, and the Vell had declined to build them.

---

So this is what it did. Three things, all small, none of them visible as power.

First, it became a guest.

It took a shape — unremarkable, road-worn, lowland — and climbed the rim-stairs like anyone, and was seated nearest the warm wall like anyone, and on the first fog evening, when the district gathered and someone asked, softly, *describe it*, the power described. It was careful. It did not describe its own country, which no room could hold. It described true small places it had crossed: a river town with rotting docks, a valley of bells, a coast where the light came in sideways under the storms. It told them well, and was asked for the same passages again on later nights, and felt — it would not have admitted how deeply — the low hum go around the room.

And it listened. That was the real work. Night after night it heard Vell describe itself — the exact lie of the channel-stones, the sound of wind in that one grove and no other, the way the third terrace east of the long stair holds the light an hour after a cloud — and the power learned the description the way the Vell learn it, which is to say exactly, which is to say the way stone is fitted: by patience, by return, by refusing the approximate. It stayed until it had the whole plateau by heart, joint by joint, name by name, and not one detail loose.

Because this was the cairn it could build. Far down the years, when the geology resumes and the talus gathers and the sharp edges go the way of all edges, the plateau will not need a word for ruin, because somewhere — in whatever rooms exist then, on whatever evenings — Vell will be told. Fixed, exact, unworn. The fog against the small windows, the doves shuffling once and going still, the blind white newts moving under the doubled stars like slow thoughts the water is having. Told perfectly, and the hum going around. The Vell had taught the power their only art without knowing they had a student, and the power, which had outlasted everything it ever loved, finally understood what the art was *for*. A description fitted tightly enough is a room the weather cannot enter. It would keep this one. It had become, without ceremony, the city's farthest granary.

Second, it went down to the foot of the falls and took up a station there. Not always, not visibly. But from that season onward, every thread who came down the rim-stairs — shouting on the inside, unable to settle, carrying a question the plateau could only answer with *elsewhere* — found, somewhere in the first town, on some early evening, a fire, and a stranger at it, and the stranger asked them the only question that mattered, the one no lowlander would have thought to ask:

*Describe it.*

And the thread, who could never go back, would discover that they had carried the whole plateau down the stairs with them, intact and tellable; that their restlessness had been a kind of porterage all along. The power did not cure them of restlessness — it was not a flaw — and it did not smooth their roads or soften the world they had chosen. It guaranteed them exactly one thing: the question, once. What they did with being needed was their own. But in this way Vell, which never wore away, was taken apart by the wind and fell into the lowlands in white threads, and was never used up. The power found the symmetry so exact that it suspected, uneasily, the plateau of having arranged it first.

Third — and this it did with the lightest touch it owned, lighter than the dampness that beads on stone — it lent Vell its own first mistake. It arranged that the world's attention should slide past the plateau the way the eye slides past a grey figure on grey rock: not hidden, only unremarked. Mapmakers' pens would hesitate at the rim and move on. Empires inventorying the world would find a note reading *highland, no metal, no surplus* and turn the page. Travelers would still find it — the ones who found it had always been the ones it could use, the tired ones, the ones with towns worth telling — but no expedition would ever target it. A cairn until it moves. And it moves so little.

---

There is one more thing, though the power would not list it among its acts, because it was not an act. It was a thing that was allowed to happen to it.

The guest stayed long enough that the district named it. They named it, in their exact way, for the cloud-crossing — not clouds in general, but *that* crossing, the mid-morning one that comes up the western fracture and takes the long stair in shadow — and the power, which had been called many things in many ages, most of them either pleading or afraid, carried for a while a name that simply meant a recurring weather, spoken just above a whisper. Once, near the end of its stay, it asked to take a turn at the cistern at dusk, and was allowed, without comment, because there is no office and no ritual and someone is always there. It sat at the lip while the dark water doubled the sky, and the fox crossed at the blue hour, and the doves shuffled once and were still, and nothing whatsoever happened, entirely.

When it left, the name went back — returned to the cloud-crossing, unworn, ready — and a generation or two later a child on the western terraces carried it, and no one found this remarkable.

The power went back up to its height changed in the only way the wise can be changed, which is toward stillness. It had come to give and had mostly been taught. It holds the description now, joints you could not slide a leaf into, and waits — though *waits* is the wrong tense, conjugated wrong, in a grammar it has half unlearned. Say instead: the description *is*, and the plateau *is*, and far down the years there is an evening, fully formed, identical in kind to here, on the far side of a terrace.

The threads keep falling. The wind keeps taking them apart.

They are never used up.
