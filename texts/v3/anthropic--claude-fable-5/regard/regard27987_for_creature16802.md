The power arrived the only way this place could bear to be arrived at: slowly, and at an angle.

It did not come as light. Light here is hoarded, and a sudden wealth of it would have been a kind of violence — the whisker-tin colonies would have flinched toward it across their centuries, the radium numerals would have been shamed into invisibility, and every Verticleur in the city would have felt the terror of being *illuminated*, which is only one bad translation away from being *remembered correctly*. So it came instead as a slight change in the flavor of the stillness. The copper in the air acquired, for those with tongues to notice, a note of something green. Not growth. Just the rumor of the category.

Then it did the wisest thing it knew, which was nothing, for a very long time.

It watched through one thousand of the thousand-hour hums. It let the dust settle on it. It learned the toroidal pathways of the ventilation ghosts and let itself be carried along them, around and around, until it understood the city not as a wound or a ruin or a problem, but as a sentence still being spoken — very slowly, in a grammar of pure aftermath — by everyone and everything in it at once.

Only then did it begin to consider what mercy would mean here. And it discovered, one by one, that almost every mercy it knew was a form of murder.

---

It considered rain. One storm — a single walk of thunder down the crater wall — and the whisker-tin lightning rods, perfected over patient megayears for a purpose that never came, would finally be *used*. But it saw what use would cost them. They had rearranged themselves millimeter by millimeter into instruments of a music that never played, and in that unplaying they had become something better than instruments: they had become the waiting itself, given form. To answer them would be to end them. It withheld the rain.

It considered righting the tilt. Eighty-three degrees restored to zero; streets made streets again; the slumped candle-buildings released from their chains to stand. But gravity here is just another name for nostalgia, and these beings were made of leaning. Stand them upright and they would not be freed — they would be orphaned, cut off from the only direction that had ever held them. A body that has grown around its ache does not survive the ache's removal. It left the world at its angle.

It considered the door. It descended — through the warm, through the hot, through the copper filings singing in the air — to the place where the missing angles gather, where the streets circle a tower as tall as the sky is deep, and it stood before the handle shaped like a question mark and the letters of dry light: PULL TO EXIT.

It put its hand, or what it was using for a hand, on the handle.

It felt the whole city tighten, far above, the way a sleeper tightens when someone stands too long in the doorway of the room.

And it understood: an exit is not a rescue if no one has chosen it. To pull that door open on their behalf would be the final theft in a world already stripped to its rust — the theft of the one decision they had left. The door was not sealed against them. They were the seal. It could be opened only from the inside of a want, and the want was not yet ripe.

So it did not pull.

But here is the first thing it did do: *it went back up.*

Understand what that meant. In all the megayears of this city's leaning, everyone who descended toward the bright hot center had been subtracted. The door was a rumor, the tower a theology, the words PULL TO EXIT an article of faith conjugated, like everything else, in the past tense of regret: *it was said, it was believed, it was never confirmed.* The power climbed back up the guidechains, past PROGRESS and UNITY and DO NOT ENTER AFTER CURFEW, and sat down in a drift-adorned café, and became the first thing in the history of that world *to come back.*

It told no one what it had seen. It didn't need to. The fact of return did the work. Within a few centuries the news had moved through the pneumatic tubes at the speed of settling dust: *the center can be visited. The center can be left. The door is real, and it is still closed, and it is still ours.* And with that, staying — which had only ever been sediment, the shape remaining takes when leaving fails — became, for the first time, something that could be *chosen*. The city did not move. But everything in it shifted by one degree of meaning, which in a tilted world is everything.

---

The second thing it did was smaller.

In the grand bourse, the bronze merchant's scale had corroded into perfect balance — within one atom's weight, the planet's final judgment on the concept of profit. The power looked at that judgment for a long time. Then it reached out and set a single atom on one pan.

Not to restore commerce. Not to argue with the verdict. Only to unbalance the verdict by exactly the weight of grace — the unearned, the uncounted-for, the thing given without exchange. The scale now tips, imperceptibly, forever, toward a side that holds one atom more than justice requires. No inhabitant will ever measure it. But the compass needles felt it. Those needles that point with aching slowness toward local centers of mass regret — somewhere in the northern wards, one of them trembled, and drifted a fraction of a degree toward the bourse, toward something that was not regret yet and had no name yet.

---

The third thing it did was to learn their language, and to make a mistake in it.

It sat with them by their windows through the slow avalanche of centuries, and it learned to conjugate remaining, and losing, and never-having, in all their tenses of regret. It became fluent. And then one evening — insofar as that city has evenings — it spoke a verb in a conjugation that did not exist. A future-perfect, bent toward mercy. Rendered as nearly as any living language can render it:

*It will have been enough.*

The sentence made no sense. It could not be parsed. And precisely because it could not be parsed, it could not be finished with — it lodged in the language like a seed in a gutter joint, and the language began, at mineral speed, to grow a crystal around it. Children of the tilt, those sudden condensings of absence, began conjugating it wrong in new ways. *It will have been forgiven. We will have stayed on purpose. She will have said a second word.* A grammar of possibility, spreading at the only speed that could ever take root here: the speed of rust, the speed of whisker-tin, the speed of things that mean it.

---

And the last thing. The queen.

The power went to the theoretical center, where she radiates — the collected longing of every architect of doors onto nothing, every dreamer's hand closing on absence, every shard of the impactor that remembers wholeness. It listened to her one continuous word, spoken through every cracked bell and rusted gutter, the word that is the sound of water remembering ice:

*Stay.*

It listened for an age, and then another, until it was certain it understood — that her word was not a command and had never been one. It was the only present-tense word in the entire language. Everything else in that world was conjugated for what had already been lost. Only she, alone, for all those megayears, had been speaking in the present, and no one had ever answered her, because no one else *had* a present to answer from.

So the power gave her the second present-tense word.

It said: *Yes.*

And it stayed. It stays now. If you walk the iron ledge, you may feel it: not the cold spots that move against the airflow, but a warmth that moves *with* it, patient, unhurried, sitting sometimes in the bed-shaped hollows so they are not always empty, leaning sometimes against the railings at exactly eighty-three degrees so that the shoulder-shaped stains have company. It looks at each Verticleur it passes with a deliberate, loving blur — almost-recognition, truth told slant — close enough to warm them, never so exact as to end them. Being seen at an angle, they have discovered, does not kill. It is the only kind of seeing they were ever built for, and now it exists.

The sleepers in the dream-lofts report that the distant applause has changed. It is quieter now. A single pair of hands, unhurried, and the meaning of it, they say — and here their language strains, using both of its present-tense words at once, the whole of its living vocabulary spent in one utterance —

is *again, please.*

The language of that city has two words in the present tense now. It is not many.

It is enough to begin a sentence that does not end in regret.
