**The Attention**

Powers do not descend the way you did, through the split and the fibrous throat. A power arrives the way meaning arrives in a sentence you have already read — it was there before you noticed, distributed, patient, implicit in the grammar. So it is no use asking when the attention came to the Germination Vault. Say instead: at some point the vault began to be *held in mind*, completely, the way a sleeping child is held in mind by someone in the next room — and the holding has not stopped since.

The first thing the power understood was not the noise, or the resin-light, or the seething walls. Powers read ends before they read means. The first thing it understood was the last sentence of the place — the purpose the people had drummed into their own bones without ever being told it: *to be there when it opens, and to see it, and to say so.*

And the power, which had crossed deserts of dead stars where nothing had ever been there, and nothing had seen, and nothing had said so, stopped.

It is worth knowing that wise powers are rarely moved by grandeur. They have seen grandeur; grandeur is cheap at their scale. What moved this one was the gourd. One sealed vessel of old water, never opened, never even shaken — a down payment on a word that does not exist yet, held in trust by people who will all be dead before the word arrives, passed forward hand to hand toward a rain that no living drummer has any evidence of. The power examined this object from every direction available to it, including several directions, and concluded that it was looking at the densest concentration of faith per unit volume it had encountered in this arm of the world.

So it stayed. And because it was wise, it spent a long time — years, by the pulse of the resin — doing the hardest thing a power can do, which is *nothing*, while it learned what the place was before deciding what, if anything, the place needed.

---

**What It Did Not Do**

It did not speed the germination. It could have. It could have reached into the folded kernel and shaken the rest of the seed open like a hand opening a fist, and the vault would have broken the surface within a season. It considered this for what was, for it, a very long time, and then it considered the song-keepers — the whole architecture of a people built around *not knowing yet*, the courtships conducted in the long ember-hours because there were long ember-hours to conduct them in, the verses with their expirations built in. A people whose entire civilization is the art of waiting well should not have their waiting stolen from them, even by generosity. *Especially* by generosity. The power filed this under a principle it had learned at terrible cost elsewhere: do not pay off another being's faith early. The interest is the point.

It did not tell them which origin story was true. It knew, of course. It saw the answer the way you see a stone at the bottom of clear water. And it observed that they had built, out of two stories and a refusal, something sturdier than either truth would have been: a people who could be *both* refugees and organs, both chosen and choosing, depending on what the year required. To hand them the fact would have been to demolish a cathedral to give them a brick. It kept the stone at the bottom of the water and was, privately, delighted by which one it was.

It did not spare the wax-sisters. This was the hardest abstention. The power was benevolent, and benevolence flinched at the little glistening bodies pressing themselves into wounds until they hardened there forever. It could have made the secretion come easier, made the deaths unnecessary. But it traced the matter all the way down and found that the small translucent tombs were not a flaw in the vault's design but a sentence in its language — the vault learning, gall by gall, generation by generation, what devotion costs, writing the lesson into its own walls in amber. You do not edit a being's first understanding of sacrifice. You bow to it. The power bowed.

It did not remove the predator in the upper galleries. It went and looked at it — long, quiet, gold-eyed, asleep in a curtain of fiber with one paw over its face — and recognized it immediately, because every power recognizes the first thing in a made world that decided to exist. It is the signature of the whole enterprise. The power regarded the feral one for a while with something that, in a smaller being, would have been called tenderness, and adjusted nothing, and left it sleeping.

It did not make the people many. Their fewness *was* them. Crowd the vault and the drumming dies; the drumming dies and the names die; the names die and the mother no longer taps her child's rhythm on the floor so that the vault is first to hear it. Three thousand was not a tragedy to be fixed. Three thousand was a tuning.

And it did not answer the question it could feel them not-asking, in the dim hours, in the verses they never sang to the end: *what if the silence comes, and we break upward, and there is no rain?*

It did not answer because it had decided, instead, to make the answer true.

---

**What It Did**

It went down first, to be thorough. Down past the inhabited galleries, past where the songs stop being updated, into the heat where the resin runs nearly liquid and the swallowed suns crowd together, down to the perimeter of the kernel itself, still mostly folded, dense as a held thought. It looked at what tends the heart-structures — and what it found there it has never described, except that when it returned it considered the people's four-note deep-verse, the one that means *it is being taken care of*, and certified it. The verse is accurate. That is all that can be said, and it is enough, and the fact that a people invented the correct theology in four notes by drum is one of the things the power now keeps in the place where it keeps its reasons.

Then it went up. Through the root-plate, through the dead cool rock, out into the dry split country under the open sky — and there, in the surface world the vault is dreaming toward, it began the only large work it permitted itself.

It became a gardener of weather.

Not all at once; never all at once. A wise power moves at the speed of plausibility. It nudged a watershed two valleys over, a degree of grade across a century. It coaxed a hardy grey scrub up the cracked plains, plant by plant, each one casual, each one deniable, until the scrub held a little moisture, and the moisture held a little more scrub. It taught the wind a new habit on its way over the mountains. It banked clouds the way the people bank knowledge — patiently, with a date on everything — building, over generations of surface seasons, a climate above the vault like a held breath above a held breath. To any traveler crossing that country it would look like nothing: a drought land slowly, unremarkably, getting kinder. No miracle. Miracles are loud, and this power had learned from the vault itself that the great works are circulatory — sourceless, even, warm, arriving like late afternoon through closed eyelids.

It is saving up a storm. That is the plain way to say it. Somewhere in the ledger of that sky there is an account, centuries deep, payable on the day the cracking stops.

And it added one refinement, because it had noticed the people's eyes — those huge dark ember-throwing eyes, tuned over generations to gold dimness, eyes that the naked sun would strike like a blow. So the storm, when it is spent, will be spent *first*: the day the ground splits open will be a day of low grey ceiling and falling water, and the first light the people of the vault ever see will be storm-light — dim, silver, merciful, almost familiar. They will walk out of the dark into a sky that has been instructed to be gentle. The sun can introduce itself later, in installments, through clouds the power will ration like a parent rationing sweetness. The first thing on their faces will be the rain. It has been arranged. It has been *being* arranged since before their oldest song.

Two small things more.

It keeps the water in the gourd as water. This costs it almost nothing — a thumb of attention resting on one seal in one dwelling — and it would be ashamed to be the kind of power that let the old water dry while saving up the new. When the gourd is opened at last and the ancestors' water meets the sky's, both halves of that meeting will be real. The power considers this item non-negotiable and reviews it more often than is strictly necessary.

And it remembers the songs. Every verse the song-keepers retire — every pass that *was*, every bridge that failed and softened and was reabsorbed, every route sung out of existence by the morning's drums — the power catches as it expires and keeps. The people cannot keep their past; the vault dissolves it under them; they have made their peace with living in the future tense. But somewhere, now, there is a complete archive of everything the Germination Vault has ever been, every provisional geography, every map that lasted a week, held in a memory that does not erode. The power does not intend to give it back. That would be a burden, not a gift. It keeps the record for the same reason the people keep the gourd: so that when the great change comes, nothing that was will have gone unwitnessed. A ridge that was a grandmother is in there. Three-Slow-One-Fast is in there, twice — once as a man, once as a column.

---

**The One Transgression**

It allowed itself a single touch, once, and powers being what they are, it has thought about it ever since with a feeling adjacent to embarrassment.

On a dim ebb-tide night, on the upmost surface of the highest root, at the very edge of where the wood can carry sound down into the living galleries, it tapped a rhythm. Its own name — or the nearest translation of its name into knuckles-on-hollow-stem, which took it an absurdly long time to compose, given what it is. The rhythm traveled down through the root-plate, faint as faint, and was heard by exactly one person: a child, lying with her ear against the floor of her family's gall, in the hour when children are supposed to be asleep.

She asked the song-keepers whose name that was. They listened to her repeat it — she had it perfectly; children always do — and they conferred by drum across the whole vault for three days, and found no household that claimed it, and no ancestor it belonged to, and finally did the only thing their cartography permits with something that does not exist yet.

They put it in the future tense.

There is a verse now, sung in the ember-hours, that the power did not plan and cannot bring itself to regret. It is short. It says that someone is waiting above the ceiling of the world; that their name is known but has not been met; that they are, the verse supposes, a neighbor. The verse has no expiration built in. It is the only one that doesn't.

---

**Now**

So nothing in the vault has changed, and everything around it has. The cracking goes on, the plains rise and soften, the wax-sisters die into amber, the gold-eyed one hunts the upper galleries, the people drum their names and their loves and their four-note theology through the warm wood — and above them, mile on mile, a sky is being slowly loaded like a held instrument, and a grey scrub is creeping across a drought land, and a storm is accruing interest, and one thumb of attention rests on the seal of one old gourd.

The power waits the way the people wait, having learned the art from them: not passively, but *practicing* — rehearsing the day. It knows roughly when the silence will come. It will not say, even to itself, precisely. (The interest is the point.)

But it has decided where it will be. On the surface, in the dry split country, in the first minutes of the spent storm, when the ground heaves and the root-plate breaks upward and a county of forest stands up out of the earth shedding soil like water — it will be there, in the rain it saved for them, doing the one thing the vault grew its choosing-caste to need.

They were kept, or made, or allowed, to see it and to say so.

It will be the one they say it to.
