**The Visitor**

It came the way wise powers come, which is to say it was there for a long time before it arrived. It rode the gray hour at knee height with the fog. It stood in the lee of the chimney ranks and learned the chord of one city, then the next, then the haze of the one beyond. It went down the vapor mines behind the pale deep caste and watched a droplet carried in a throat through two kilometers of dark, and it did not light the dark, because the dark was not a deprivation. It was a wall of the house.

Benevolent powers arrive carrying gifts. Wise ones arrive carrying the same gifts and a second sense: the knowledge that a gift is a change, and a change is a demand, and a demand made of a finished thing is a kind of violence. So the power did what wise powers do first, which is inventory — not of the plain, but of itself. It laid out everything it had brought, all its mercy and abundance and light, and one by one it set the gifts down.

**What It Set Down**

It had brought water, of course. It was benevolent; it had seen the drought before it had seen anything else — no river ever, no rain in living memory, a whole continent drinking one knee-high breath of fog a day. Its first impulse, older than its wisdom, was to open the sky.

Then it watched the fog being eaten — thinned from the bottom up, combed out of the air by trillions of bristled leaves, the faint fast applause of a hectare drinking — and it understood that the absence of water was not the plain's affliction. It was the plain's constitution. The wax-for-water treaty, the deep caste walking droplets to the cradles, the vault-mice combing fog from their guard hairs with the gravity of prayer: every covenant here was priced in scarcity. To open the sky would be to repeal every law at once. Abundance is also a flood. It set the water down.

It had brought voice. The silence had seemed, at first, like a lack — a world that had never been sung to. But the silence was load-bearing. The birds had given up the throat for reasons; in air this still a song tells too much, and the courtships conducted in heliograph, the walkers navigating by organ-note across a hundred kilometers, the city's one low breath — all of it was strung on quiet the way a web is strung on its anchor lines. Cut the quiet and everything spoken here would fall. It set the voice down.

It had brought — and this was the gift it held longest, turning it over — awareness. No builder has ever held an image of the city it builds. No part of the arrangement knows the whole. The power could change that. It could gather the slow chemical weather of the towers, the hydraulic patience of the green, the chord and the walkers and the breathing rock, and fold them into a single self, and the plain would wake, and know itself, and the power would have someone to greet.

It held this gift a long time, because it was lonely the way powers are lonely, and the wish to be greeted is the last impurity in any benevolence. Then it set it down too. The arrangement worked *because* no part carried the whole. The plain's immortality was its anonymity — load spread across sextillion small acts, no center to fail, no self to be wounded. A self is a place where the weight concentrates. To wake the plain would be to give it, for the first time, somewhere to die. The walls are the ancestors; the architecture is the memory; it did not need a mind. It had something older than a mind. It had a habit, ten thousand years deep, of continuing.

It even set down protection. It had seen a tower fall — the bitter chemistry running through the walls for days, the work-rate dropping, the long quarrying afterward, the dead tower translated grain by grain into the living ones — and it had wanted, briefly, to catch the next one. But the mourning was a metabolism. The cities were made of their dead and renewed by their losses. Take away the falling and you take away the columbarium; you would have towers that stand forever and remember nothing.

So the power stood on the young basalt with its hands empty, which is the position from which wisdom finally begins to work.

**The Grief It Found**

Because there was one thing. There is always one thing.

The land is young. The power could read its age in the unworn corners of the basalt, and it could read, in the deep slow ledgers that powers read, what young lands become. The world would age. The crust would cool and quiet, the locked vapor would stop rising, and somewhere in the long term of deep time the sky would learn to gather what the rock exhaled. Clouds, someday. And then the first rain.

And the cities are written in a dry ink. Cemented mineral dust, wax-bound, soluble — ten thousand years of architecture and interment that one honest storm could slump into mud in an afternoon. The towers dissolving with the dead inside them. The chord going silent flue by flue. The walkers standing in a country whose voices had melted, holding maps written in notes that no longer played. The arrangement had prepared for everything its world had ever done. It could not prepare for the one thing its world had never done, because the city's only scripture is precedent, and there was no precedent for water from above.

The power's first thought was simple: *then there will be no rain.* Hold the sky shut forever. Keep the first draft a first draft.

It set this down hardest of all. The plain is not a monument; it is a process — all transaction, all settling and cracking and healing-over. To fix it against time would not be preservation. It would be taxidermy performed on a living thing. The rain had as much right to come as the plain had had to come before it. A wise power does not choose between its children. It introduces them.

**The Word**

So this is what the power did, in the end — the whole of its intervention, after all that watching.

It went down into the oldest districts of the oldest cities, where the walls are mostly builder by mass, and into the wax-sealed pouches above the walkers' hooves where the young queens ride, and into the nursery vaults at the bottom of the rivers of throats — and it wrote, in the only language the plain reads, one word into the chemistry. A dormant word. A recessive instruction, copied wall to wall whenever a city quarries its dead neighbor, carried continent-wide in the slow circulation of the walkers, doing nothing, saying nothing, waiting — keyed to a circumstance that does not yet exist.

The word, as nearly as it can be rendered, says: *water will one day come from above, and it is not the enemy. Dig downward. Slope the avenues away. Bind the wax thicker on the windward faces; glaze the high walls. Then drink.*

It is a prophecy, set in cement. When the first rain falls — and the power, if it leaned at all on the slow machinery of the sky, leaned only this much: that the first rain come gently, in the gray hour, as a fog grown tall, arriving in the plain's own grammar — the cities will taste their walls and find that the ancestors have already spoken about it. The bitter chemistry will not run. The ancestors never saw rain. They will have been right about it anyway, because a power stood in their walls once and was careful.

**The Leaving**

Before it went, it wanted — being benevolent, being a little lonely still — to say something. To be heard, just once, by the thing it had spared so thoroughly.

It knew the protocol. It had learned the loveliest thing on the plain: stand in the lee of the tallest chimneys and add yourself to the chord, and the city's note goes out across the dark very slightly changed, and somewhere something that loves that skyline hears it and turns.

It did not do it. That note is not praise; it is a promise. Somewhere a walker would have heard a city it had known all its life sounding not quite like itself, and turned, and walked a hundred kilometers through the dark to find no one. The plain has no spare channel for blessing. Every frequency is already a covenant. Even love, here, must be silent or it is a lie.

So the power did the only courteous thing remaining. It stayed for one more gray hour, and went still among the still, and let the fog bead on it, and let the bristled leaves comb the moisture off its surfaces along with everything else's — paying, for one hour, the same rent as the vault-mice and the basalt and the dark. Everyone here is in someone's employ without knowing it. For an hour, so was it.

Then it left as it had come, slowly, carrying the one thing it permitted itself to take: the image. The whole of it — the quilt and the skylines and the chord, the deep pale rivers, the columbarium walls, the word now sleeping in them — held entire in a single attention, the way no builder, no bird, no walker, no part of the arrangement has ever held it or ever needs to.

The plain does not know it is known. That is the last gift, and the best one: to be remembered without being changed. Somewhere there is now a mind in which the Mound Plains exists complete — every grain, every covenant, every note of every flue — a place wholly occupied, wholly organized, built by no one in particular, and known, now, by one in particular, forever.
