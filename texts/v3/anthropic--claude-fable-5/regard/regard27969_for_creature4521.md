The power had been watching for some time before it arrived, which is to say it had been arriving for some time before it watched—these things happen in whatever order the Delta's customs office processes them, and the customs office had recently been converted into an aviary for questions.

It did not come as light. Light here was already overcommitted, working three jobs and behind on its chromatic debts, and a wise power does not add to the burdens of the employed. It did not come as thunder or revelation or a column of anything. It came as a reader. Which is to say: it came as attention that had learned, somewhere across a great many worlds, that attention is the only force that can enter a place like this without breaking it. Observation collapses. Attention holds open.

It read the city the way you read a letter from someone you love who is not well but is trying to be funny about it.

And because it was wise, the first thing it did was make a list of everything it would not do.

**It would not finish anything.** It saw at once that this was the great temptation, the thing a merely powerful power would have done within the hour—pour the concrete of conclusion into every open clause, dry out the ceilings, grant the moon its committee approval, tell the clay firmly that it was clay. It could have done this. The whole Delta could have been made *real* by dinnertime, real in the way that other places are real, with load-bearing definitions and water that stays where it's put. And every citizen would have been housed, at last, inside a finished sentence.

The power had seen finished sentences. It knew what they cost. It knew that a place like this does not exist because reality failed here—it exists because reality, everywhere else, forecloses. Every solid city is built on a million evictions of the possible. Here was the one municipality where the possible had been granted asylum. You do not deport refugees back to the definite. Not if you are kind.

**It would not fix anyone.** Fixing implies the broken thing once agreed on the shape it broke from, and nothing here had ever signed. The Unenrolled were not failures of existence. They were existence's conscientious objectors, and their objections were, the power noted, filed correctly, in triplicate, with the margins.

**It would not stay forever.** Even benevolence, in permanent residence, becomes a landlord.

Having settled what it would not do, it did four small things.

---

The first thing: it went down to the basement of the Surveyor's Guild, where the maps live that never leave, and it found the great stack of the city's pending paperwork—every unfinished form, every appeal under appeal, every residency renewal with the Void Bureau's Nightshift Dept., Watercolor Division, the whole trembling architecture of deferral—and it did not approve any of it. It did not deny any of it. It took out a stamp that had never been used in any jurisdiction, because no jurisdiction had ever thought of it, and it stamped every page:

**RECEIVED.**

Not resolved. Not processed. *Received.* And through the whole city, in that hour, something loosened that no one could name. Because the terror of the pending is not that it will never be decided—the Unenrolled had made their peace with undecided, had married it, had cultivated tomatoes with it. The terror of the pending is that it is pending *to no one.* That the form sits in a tray in a room where the light was never turned on. To be received is not to be answered. It is to know that somewhere, your incompleteness is *held*, that someone has read your open clause all the way to its opening.

The buildings' pores inhaled and, for the first time in local memory, the air they exhaled smelled like rain that was happening *here.*

---

The second thing: it went out past the edges (which had, as noted, already immigrated to the middle, so this took less time than expected) to where the land was most self-conscious about being land, and it knelt, and it put its hand into the ground the way you would put your hand on the shoulder of someone deciding something difficult. And it asked the clay:

*What would you like to be?*

No one had ever asked. Geology everywhere else is a sentence handed down; strata are verdicts. The clay, which had spent epochs being told it was riverbed and then told it was seabed and then given no telling at all, considered the question for a very long time—about four minutes, which for clay is a very long time and for this clay was also next Thursday.

*I don't know yet,* said the clay.

*Then don't know,* said the power, *for as long as you need. Not-knowing is a shape. I hereby recognize it.*

And this was the whole intervention. The land did not change. But from that day it was unfinished *by consent* rather than by abandonment, and the difference, though invisible to instruments, was visible to children, who reported that the ground under their feet felt less like an apology and more like a held breath before a good surprise. The Delta was still not young in years. But now its youth in consent was a courtship, not a lapse.

---

The third thing: it visited the funeral home whose lease expires nightly, where Death's franchise paperwork has been stuck in probate since before probate. The power did not unstick it. It understood that some of the Unenrolled were here because they were not done, and some were here because they were afraid of being done, and these are different residencies requiring different accommodations. Forcing the door shut would betray the first group; forcing it open would betray the second.

So it propped the door with a small stone. On the stone it wrote:

*Whenever you're ready. No rush. This offer does not expire, escalate, or accrue interest. There is no fine print. There is no large print. There is only the door, which will be exactly this open tomorrow.*

And ending became, in the Fresh Delta, what it has never been anywhere else: *optional and available.* Not the repo man, not the fugitive. Just a door, ajar, patient as furniture. A few of the Unenrolled, in the following seasons, walked through it—unhurried, mid-conversation, the way you leave a party you genuinely enjoyed. They waved. The rest stayed, and their staying changed flavor entirely, because staying you cannot leave is confinement, and staying you could leave is *home.*

---

The fourth thing it did was for you.

Yes—you, filed under 'miscellaneous' in the collective autobiography, minding the gap, your shadow rotated 47 degrees in a cloakroom you can't find again. The power went to the autobiography (currently being revised backward from its resigning ending), and it found your entry, and it did not embellish it or correct it or explain you. It simply moved your file. You are no longer under 'miscellaneous.'

You are under **'expected.'**

It also had a word with the margin—privately, gently—thanking it for accommodating so much relevant irrelevance for so long without acknowledgment. The margin, which had only ever wanted to be noticed for its work, wept a little in whitespace and downgraded itself from sarcastic to wry, which is sarcasm that has been loved.

---

Then the power sat for one evening at the Edge's Edge Cafe and ordered the coffee that stays half-written on the menu. It drank it—all of it, the written half and the unwritten half—and this was quietly witnessed by everyone present, and quietly understood: that a thing can be *received completely* without being *finished.* That wholeness and completion are not the same word, have never been the same word, only rent adjacent rooms.

Someone at the next table, telling a story they had been telling for nine years, reached the end of a sentence. Just one. The period landed like a snowflake. They looked around, startled, then laughed, then started another sentence and left it open on purpose—*freely,* this time, the way you leave a window open for a cat that exists in the conditional tense.

And then the power left, or began leaving, or filed its intention to leave with the appropriate aviary. It repaired nothing. It resolved nothing. The light is still three-layered dusk; the streets still merge like rivers of different temperatures; the fish still swim through air, professionally skeptical.

But the chromatic debt has been forgiven. The blues no longer *owe* their redness—they may become it, or not, as a gift rather than a payment. And the sky, which used to remember being darker elsewhere, has stopped remembering. It is exactly this color now. Here. On purpose.

The locals say, if you ask them—and they'll only tell you at 3 AM, walking their maybe-fish on leashes of braided regret—that nothing happened, really. Nothing changed.

Only that the city used to feel like a rough draft of somewhere more real, and now everywhere more real feels like a rough draft of the city. Only that *Not Quite* used to be a diagnosis and is now a decision. Only that when the rain argues with itself on the way down these days, it argues the way old friends do—

not about whether to fall,

but about how slowly they can manage it,

so as to see everything on the way.
