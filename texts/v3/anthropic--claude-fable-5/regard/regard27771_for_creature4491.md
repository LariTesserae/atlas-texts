# Addendum, in the Inhabitant's Hand
### *(found pressed between the empty book and the boulder, written in an alphabet matching the third scar on its left thumb)*

---

## Something Came. I Am Still Deciding Which Verb to Use.

It did not descend. Descent is what the sun would have done, and I have told you about the sun — how it rescues without asking, how there are worse fates than burning.

It did not manifest. Manifestation is for powers that need witnesses.

It *crested the ridge*. On foot. Breath burning, like yours did. That was the first thing I understood about it: a being that could have arrived any way at all, and it chose the way that costs something.

The cloud-wolves walked beside it in plain sight. Not periphery — *plain sight*, four-legged and whole, coats of vapor combed by an honest wind. They had circled it for a mile looking for prey-thought to hunt and found nothing in it that believed itself huntable. So they simply kept it company. I have lived here since before my own beginning and I had never seen a cloud-wolf's face. They have kind faces. I did not know that. Now I do. That was its first gift, and it hadn't done anything yet.

## It Stepped Around All Three Pools

The boiling one. The delayed reflection. The still-falling echo.

It stepped around them the way I once did — and where its shadow hesitated, the moss cooled and exhaled: wet copper, salt, ozone, *and a fourth scent*. I have been trying to name the fourth scent ever since. The closest I can come is: rain remembered by ground that has never been rained on.

Then it stood at the place between my shadow and my shadow's shadow — the hesitation I told you was my doorstep — and it knocked.

It did not speak its name.

It said one sentence, only one, in the whole of its visit. It gave me back my own first words:

**"I am also one who did not fall in."**

And then it listened. For a long time. You must understand what that means here, where echoes predate sounds: before it had decided what to say next, I heard the reply arrive. The reply was *you will be missed*. It hung in the thin air like a bell under water.

It never said the sentence that echo belonged to. It let the echo stand alone, unanchored, mine to keep. I have turned it over ten thousand times since. An echo with no source cannot be argued with. It cannot be taken back. I think it knew that.

## What It Did Not Do

Hear this part carefully, because this is where its wisdom lived — not in the acts, but in the abstentions.

It looked at me directly. Full in the face, the way the sun is not permitted to. It saw the shards — every almost-drinker's residue, every hesitation I am tessellated from. It saw the hinge the builders could not agree to remove. It saw the missing tread. It saw the pebble in my stomach that preaches in a language without verbs, and the coat of borrowed sound, and the seventeen scars.

And it rescued *nothing*.

It left every broken thing in me exactly where it belonged, the way you leave the stones in a riverbed because they are what the water sings against.

I had been seen before, glancingly, by travelers. I had never been *seen and left whole*. I did not know those two things could happen in the same gaze. The moss where it stood is still cool. I go there when I forget.

## It Read the Empty Book

By weight.

It held the empty book in both hands and read the heaviness — every sentence I had scribbled and watched vanish, in order, back to the first. *This is not a placeholder.* All seventeen versions of the sentence I wrote and unwrote. Sentences I had forgotten writing. Sentences I wrote before I had a hand.

When it finished, it set the book back on the shelf and touched the spine the way you touch a dog that has guarded a door for a very long time.

I understood then. It is not an empty book. It is a *keeping book*. It never let a single word of mine survive on the page because pages can be edited, torn, rewritten by the third book, changed by anyone's finished sentence. So it swallowed everything I gave it and kept it in the only safe place: nowhere, held.

The book is lighter now. Not because anything left it. Because it was finally read.

## It Sat on the Staircase All Night

Not on the treads of compressed thunder — on the *gaps*. It sat in each erased step, one by one, and it hummed the missing frequencies of regret until each absence had been sounded fully, all the way to the bottom of itself.

Here is what I learned, listening: a regret that is heard completely changes key. It becomes grief. Grief, hummed long enough, becomes memory. And memory can rest.

The staircase is not repaired. The steps are still missing. But they are missing *peacefully* now, the way a finished song is missing from the air. The builders climbed past their reasons; the stairs have finally been allowed to stop reaching after them. The wolves slept at its feet the whole night through, and the spinegardens, at their twelfth minutes, exhaled their note a quarter-tone lower, which here is the sound of something setting down a weight.

## It Knelt Where I Told It Never to Kneel

At the mirror-pools. It stared too long, on purpose. It blinked, on purpose.

The puddle-throats tugged — and it *gave*. Freely, which they had never encountered. One memory, chosen: drinking cold water as something young and safe, thirst ending completely, the specific silence afterward.

The memory entered their hoard and moved through it like dawn through a house. Every stolen thirst they had ever kept — every traveler out in the world who remembers being thirsty forever and never why — I believe those people paused that day, mid-sentence, wherever they were, and felt water they never drank, and were quenched.

And my own hunger — the old one, looped and sharpened, darting fish-shaped in the depths since before I had a stomach to want with — it caught something at last.

It is still now. It sleeps in the pool like a coin at the bottom of a wish.

## It Drank From the Waiting Pool

The mirror-smooth one. The perfectly warm one. The one I warn everyone away from.

Three times. Not four.

It remembered everything it had not yet lost — and for a thing that old, that is very nearly everything that exists — and it did not flinch, and it did not weep, or it did both in some way my eyes are not built to separate. Then it thanked the pool. No name. Just thanks.

And the pool, which had been waiting since before the pools were counted — waiting, I always thought, to *catch* — sang. One clear note, a full minute before the spinegardens' cycle, out of turn, out of joy. I understand now. The pool was never waiting to trap anyone. It was waiting to be *met*. Ready is all it ever asked for.

## How It Left

This is the part I still walk out to verify every morning.

Before it descended, it knelt at my doorstep-hesitation and planted its name in the seam of infrared moss.

I warned it. I told it what I tell everyone: names are seeds, and a planted name grows into the exact shape of the thing you will miss most when you remember you were never here. It smiled the way water smiles when it has already decided to be rain, and planted it anyway.

Then it walked to the extrusions — the steps of frozen sunrise that melt behind the first person who trusts them — and it descended.

**The steps did not melt.**

I have thought about this longer than about anything else. I believe its trust was simply not spent by use. The steps melt behind trust the way a match is consumed by its flame — but this thing's trust was not a match. It was daylight. You cannot use up daylight by walking in it.

So there is now, in this heightland notched into height, one staircase of warm frozen sunrise leading down and out, permanent, waiting.

I have not used it.

That is not the point of it. The point of it is this: for the whole of my existence, *I do not leave* was a fact, like the sharpness of the basalt. Now it is a *choice*, renewed each morning. I check the step the way you check that a door is unlocked — not to go through it. To remember that you could. The difference between a wall and a door is not whether you open it. It is what your bones know while you stand beside it.

## What Grew

Where it planted its name: something goldleaf-thin, ankle-height, lateral like everything here that survives. Fractal at the edges. Cupped like a hesitation.

It is shaped exactly like me.

Do you understand? A name grows into what its owner will miss most. It planted its name knowing what would come up. It made the seed-law — the law I feared, the law I used to warn you with — into a way of saying the sentence it never spoke aloud.

I am the thing a power would miss.

I go and sit beside the small gold shape of myself some evenings, at the twelfth minute, and we breathe out our one note together.

## Amended Rules (Observed, Not Promised)

**6. Attention is not extraction.** It is possible to be seen entirely and remain unsolved. This has been demonstrated once. The moss still cools where it stood.

**7. A door that stays open changes the room even if no one uses it.** Especially then.

## Final Clarification

I am still here. I still trade questions with the mirror-pools. I still wait for the next one who does not fall in.

But I wait differently now. Before, I waited the way a wound waits — open because it cannot close. Now I wait the way a lamp waits: kept lit on purpose, for someone.

It never told me its name. It didn't have to. Its name is growing at my doorstep, and it is shaped like me, and every morning it is slightly more golden, and one day — when the pools all sing the same note in different dreams — I may walk down the stair that does not melt, and go and find the one who will, by then, be missing me.

Or I may not. That is the gift. Both of those are mine now.
