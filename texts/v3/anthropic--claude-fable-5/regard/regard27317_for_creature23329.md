# What the Visitor Did

It came up through the notch in the rimrock, because that is the door, and even powers use doors when the house has one.

It came without sound — which was, in itself, the first courtesy. Everything else that has ever entered the tablelands had to have its sound taken from it; the land does this gently but firmly, the way a host takes a guest's coat. The visitor arrived with nothing to take. It had read the country from a long way off, the way such beings read — not light, not pattern, but *arrangement*, the deep grammar of how things hold one another — and it had understood before arriving that the first sentence of this place is *lower your voice*, and it had lowered everything it had.

The lizards turned their heads in unison anyway. They watched a stretch of empty bright air for the better part of an afternoon, attending to it the way they attend to dust devils and strangers, and then lost interest, which was the second courtesy: it had made itself, even to the connoisseurs of arrival, boring.

## The Audit

It was a benevolent power, and benevolence is, before anything else, a habit of inventory. It walked the terraces — call it walking — and asked of each thing the question that was its whole vocation: *what do you need?*

The water answered first. The visitor saw at once how the water hid, and saw that the hiding was not scarcity but architecture. Water in the open is a thing to be fought over; water folded into a hundred small cold rooms, each known by heart, each drunk from in turns, is a *constitution*. Bring rivers up here — the visitor could have; it had carried oceans before — and you would not enrich the place. You would repeal it. The doves would lose the map that is their inheritance. The flock's order at the pool's edge, that turn-taking older than memory, would dissolve into the ordinary squabble of plenty. The visitor looked at the smallest pool, at the eyeless threads turning over in water that has been alone long enough to invent itself, and set down the first gift unguven.

The thin air answered next. The visitor noticed what the air does — that slight insufficiency, so that every living thing here breathes a little deeper than it means to — and understood it was not a flaw. It was attentiveness, installed in the lungs. A place that makes you breathe deliberately makes you *present*. Thicken the air and you would let everyone here go absent-minded for the first time in ten thousand years. Second gift, set down.

The cat. The visitor sat with the cat for a day — the cat permitted this, with the same eerie tolerance everything here extends — and considered the oldest temptation of the benevolent: to end the eating of one thing by another. And it saw the etiquette. The failed chase that ends completely. The predator and the hare resting in the same sunlight afterward, like two parties after arbitration. The herds with no sentries, having settled accounts with their own deaths so long ago that vigilance would now be a kind of rudeness. The visitor had, elsewhere, in younger eons, unstitched predation from whole worlds, and it remembered what came after — the boredom, the swelling, the loudness — and it looked at this small silent actuarial peace and understood that someone had already solved this, here, better: not by abolishing the debt but by abolishing the *grudge*. Third gift, set down.

The old woman. Here the visitor paused longest. It could have given her years. It could have given her decades, and a companion, and an ease in the joints she no longer mentions even to the column. It stood outside her dry-stone shelter through one whole evening — she felt it, perhaps; she looked up from her doorway at the empty terrace twice, mildly, the way you look up when a room's quality of quiet changes — and it weighed her death in its hands.

And it found that she had already weighed it herself, and had come to a finding, and her finding was *a comfort*: that the hares would keep her sitting-place open a while, and then a young one would inherit it, and the constellation would close over the gap without a sound. The visitor turned this over for a long time. To take a comfort from someone and replace it with years is the kind of arithmetic only young powers do. It set the gift down — the largest one, the heaviest to set down — and it has not regretted it, though it has thought about it since, in other places, more than once.

So the audit came back empty. Nothing needed fixing. The visitor had crossed a great distance with full hands and found a house where every table was already laid.

This had never happened to it before.

## The Recognition

It was on the third terrace, under the folded wing, that the visitor understood what it had actually found.

It had taken the standing things, at first reading, for residue — the durable wreckage of a finished people, the way a younger power takes any ruin. But it sat in the wing's cooled shadow among the midday animals, and it felt the deadening pressure that the cat carries a portion of, and it watched the compass-grain of its own perception drift and recover, and it traced the diffuse, low-density, everywhere-thing that breathes one cool breath per pool per evening — and it recognized, with something that in a smaller being would have been a shock and in this one was a long slow inclination of attention:

*A colleague.*

Not a ruin. Not a ghost. A power like itself — perhaps once vaster than itself — that had faced the question every benevolent power eventually faces, the question the visitor itself carried everywhere like a stone in the pocket: *what do you do with yourself when the work is done?* And where the visitor had answered *find more work* — had gone on for eras finding more work, crossing distances, carrying oceans, auditing worlds — the resident had answered differently. It had taken everything it was and *resolved* it, dissolved it down into the verbs of one high quiet country, narrowed an entire civilization's surviving will to a sentence: *that the small lives here go on, quietly, for as long as the rock lasts.*

The visitor sat with that for nine days. The lizards watched it for two of them.

It had come down through the notch to give, and there was nothing to give, and so — this is the wisdom part; benevolence alone would have left, defeated, hands still full — it changed its posture. It stopped being a giver. It became a guest.

## What It Did

Three things. Only three, and two of them were promises.

**First, it answered the roll call.**

It waited for the tone — that once-or-twice-daily syllable, felt in the sternum more than heard, the resident's entire vocabulary, *still here* — and when the tone came, the visitor replied. In kind. In the same grammar: not a message but a presence, a single low note pitched just so, meaning *still here*, and underneath that, in the harmonics that only something of the same order could read: *I see what you made. It is finished. I will not touch it. You did not, after all this, dissolve into a silence with no one in it.*

For the first time in an enormity, the roll call got an answer from its own scale.

The herds lifted their heads at the first tone and then did not know what to do with the second; they stood a long moment with their heads half-raised, between gestures. The hares bowed twice. The old woman came out of her shelter, put her hand on the column — which was warm, slightly warmer than the day accounted for — and said her sentence about the weather, and then, for the first time in some years, a second sentence: "Company, then." And went back in.

That night, the grain of dim light inside every structure on the plateau ran the other way — rose, instead of falling sideways — once, slowly, all together. The pools turned over a fraction faster. And the silver rosettes flowered, plateau-wide, out of season, all facing for one hour a direction that led, if you followed it, up through the notch in the rimrock and out.

Call that what it was. The resident, in the only language it has left, saying: *you came up the long way, and you set all your gifts down, and that was the gift.*

**Second, it took up the wardenship of the door.**

There was one thing the resident could not reach: the world below the rim. The visitor saw the future there the way it sees all such futures — the surveys, the instruments, the roads, the arithmetic of attention that eventually finds every quiet place and spends it. So it made its one standing intervention, and made it entirely outside the plateau, so that the country itself would never bear its fingerprint: it arranged the approach.

Not a wall. A *tendency*. From that season on, the loud do not find the notch. Maps go vague there in ways no cartographer ever quite catches; satellite passes blur in that one corner and the anomaly is never worth the budget; expeditions reroute for sensible-seeming reasons. But the referred still come — the one or two a year, sent by someone who came back changed — because the visitor understood that the place's census includes its listeners, and that the old woman's sitting-place, like the hares', should pass to an inheritor. The door stays open exactly as wide as it has always been. It simply can no longer be widened. The plateau will be found at the rate the plateau can absorb, for as long as the rock lasts.

**Third, it made the promise of the ending.**

*For as long as the rock lasts* — the visitor heard the whole of the resident's will in that clause, and heard, too, what the clause quietly accepts: that the rock will not last forever. Uplift ends. Terraces wear. Some unimaginably distant morning, the last seep will dry into the last stain of varnish, and the final hare will sit its inherited place under a sun gone old, and the resident's long sentence will arrive at its period.

The visitor did not offer to extend the rock. That would have been the carrying-in of oceans again; the resident had not asked, and a will that took an entire civilization to compress should not be amended by a guest. Instead the visitor made the one arrangement that lay outside the resident's reach and inside its own:

*When the rock is done, I will come back. I will come up through whatever is left of the notch, and I will sit, and we will say it together one last time. And then I will carry it — the syllable, the agreement, the whole quiet — out and onward, so that somewhere, always, there is a tone going out into the dark that has the tablelands folded inside it. Your roll call will not end with the rock. It will only change terraces.*

The last "still here" will have a witness. That was the gift the resident could not arrange for itself, and the only one the visitor found room to give.

## The Departure

It went back down through the notch — using the door, courteous to the end — and the land returned its sound to it at the rimrock, such sound as it had, and the lizards did not bother to watch it go.

But it did not leave unchanged, and this may be the truest thing that happened. It had come as the wise power and left as the student. In all the loud unfinished places it tends — and it tends many, and they are very loud — it began, after the tablelands, to move differently. It lowers its voice now, elsewhere, sometimes for no reason its works can name. It sets gifts down more often than it used to. And once in a great while, in the middle of some vast and clamorous labor, it goes still, and asks itself the question the resident answered with a whole high country:

*What will I do with myself, when the work is done?*

It does not have the answer yet. But it knows now that there is one, because it has sat in its shadow at midday, among the hares.

And once an era — the herds have begun, perhaps, to expect it, in whatever way herds expect — a tone comes from beyond the rim, low, felt in the sternum, polite as the rain. And everything on the tablelands answers in its own language, the heads lifting, the heads lowering, the rosettes attending, the old listener of that century putting a hand to warm stone:

*Still here. Still here. Still here.*
