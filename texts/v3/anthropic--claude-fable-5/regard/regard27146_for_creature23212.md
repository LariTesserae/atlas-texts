# The Attention

It arrived the only way the Quarter can be arrived at, which is slowly. This was, for the power, a discipline. It was old and benevolent and had a long resume of intervention — it had unmade a plague once, turned a river that was eating a city, and on one occasion, which it still regretted, hurried a spring. It was used to places that were problems. It came down into the basin on a cool morning, riding the mist the way the mist rides the low ground, and it spent the first while doing what powers do: looking for the wound.

It did not find one.

It found the engine thudding at eight strokes a minute and slowed its own attention to match, because attending faster than a thing happens is a way of not seeing it. At that pace, the basin opened up like a long exposure. The buildings stopped being buildings and became what they were — gestures three centuries in the making, still mid-gesture. The trimmers' shears were visible for what they were: punctuation in a conversation neither party intended to finish. The power watched a membrane pane for what a human would call a week and saw it thicken by nothing at all, and understood that the nothing was the work.

Before doing anything, it asked.

It did this in the town's own grammar, because it was wise enough to know that a place which heals rather than repairs will have opinions about being helped. It laid its attention flat against the Quarter the way a child of six lays a palm against a wall, and it waited to learn whether the town was drinking, or resting, or annoyed.

The answer took a season. The answer was: *resting. Come back in spring.*

The power, who had not been told to wait by anything in several thousand years, found that it minded less than it expected. It wintered in the basin as a quality of the light.

## The Deliberation

In spring it returned to the question of gifts, and it conducted the deliberation honestly, the way good powers do, holding each possibility up and turning it.

It considered quickening the engine — easing the sap, doubling the strokes, making the winter dwindle into something the town need not endure. It set this down almost at once. The dwindling was not endured here; it was *kept*, the way other towns keep holidays. To fix the winter would be to steal a season from a people whose whole wealth was seasons.

It considered long life — longer even than the damp and the resin already gave them. But it had read, by then, over an old woman's shoulder, three pages of a leaf-book: white leaves gone ivory, gone parchment, gone the amber of windows. It understood that the book was not a record of wounds. The book was a wall, and the leaves were its grain, and a family without losses would be a family without grain — smooth, characterless, unable to hold a hinge. The houses had decided, centuries ago and unasked, that grief here would be *marked* rather than mended. The power recognized the work of a colleague and did not interfere with it.

It considered revealing itself, and laughed — a sound, in the basin, indistinguishable from rain finishing in a roof — at how quickly that one fell. Revelation is a kind of hurry.

It considered warning them of the future, because the power could see the future the way the membrane growers could: not in detail but in decades, and it saw what was coming, which was the fast country. Roads. Buyers. People who would look at an eleven-year pane and ask what the holdup was. It saw surveyors with instruments, and timetables, and the particular blindness of haste, which does not destroy slow things out of malice but simply cannot register them — drives through them the way a shout goes through a pause.

And here, at last, the power found something to do. Because the Quarter had grown itself everything a place can grow: walls, patience, a clergy of pressure, a beetle staff, an etiquette of silence. The one thing it could not grow was *distance*. Distance is not a botanical product.

## What It Did

The power did three things, and it did them the way the town would have: slowly, without announcement, and so close to the grain of what already existed that no one could afterward say where nature ended and the gift began.

**First, it folded a little patience into the geography.** Nothing dramatic — no wall of thorns, no curse. It simply arranged that the basin could not be reached in a hurry. Maps of the region would err by a hair, always the same hair. The road in would prove, to anyone driving it, slightly longer than the distance it covered, in a way that survey after survey would fail to resolve and eventually stop trying to. Schedules that included the Quarter would arrive at it one valley behind themselves. The effect was that anyone who reached the town reached it already slowed — out of their hurry, out of their timetable, breathing the cellar air at a walking pulse — which is to say already harmless, which is to say already half a guest. The fast country would come, in time, as it comes everywhere. It would arrive at the pace of wood or it would slide past the basin like rain off a canopy, and either way the Quarter would receive only what it could absorb. The power considered this not a wall but a *governor*, in the engine sense: a thing that does not stop the machine, only keeps it from racing itself to pieces.

**Second, it extended the leaves.** This was small, and it was the one the power was quietest about, because it was not certain it had the right. The houses marked their dead — but only the dead who died in reach of the roots. Those who went to fast country and did not come back went off the rolls; a mother could outlive her son by a decade and never know it, and the house would never know either, and that — the power judged, turning it over for most of a summer — was the single unhealed thing in the basin. Not death. *Unmarked* death. So it ran a line outward, the way the works ran shaft-lines through the walls: a slow nerve from the Quarter into the world, carrying one kind of news only, at sap speed. The first time it worked, a pressman's house flushed white over the door in midwinter, months out of any season for it, for a daughter forty years gone to the cities. The family wrote a letter. The answer came back in spring and confirmed the leaves to the week. The town absorbed this the way it absorbs everything — without comment, adjusting its understanding of the houses by one degree, filing it under weather. The leaf-books gained pages they would otherwise have lacked. The power counted this its best work in an age, and told no one, and there was no one to tell.

**Third — and this one it did not plan — it stayed for a ninth day.** It had meant to leave. But it had been watching the engine-keeper, whose whole craft was restraint performed with total attention, and the power, who had spent eras *doing*, found it wanted instruction. So on the engine's rest day it sat in the engine-house, formless, in the warm resin dark, doing visibly nothing alongside a human doing visibly nothing, both of them attending a tree that rested better attended. It had mended worlds. It had never before *kept* one. The difference, it discovered, was the difference between repairing and healing: the second takes longer and looks better, and you do not get to take credit, and the not-taking-credit is most of the work.

It does the ninth days still. The keeper has never seen it. The keeper has, however, remarked at dinner — once, in the unhurried way of the Quarter, with pauses his family waited out respectfully — that the rest days lately feel *companioned*, and his family agreed that this was probably the building, and he agreed that it probably was, and the matter was allowed to close, the way matters are here, like bark over a knot.

## What It Found Already There

One thing more, recorded because the power found it instructive.

When it had first arrived and gone looking for the wound, it had ended its search at the engine-house cistern, and there it had found the heron.

The heron had looked at it.

Not with surprise. Not with deference. With the flat assessing patience of a foreman reviewing a new hire — competent enough, probably, time would tell — and then the heron had returned its attention to the water, and the audience was over.

The power made inquiries, in the way powers do, down through the layers of the place, and found nothing: no signature, no old colleague, no god gone local. Just a heron. Just a bird that had, by standing in the right water at the right pace for long enough, accreted something that the power's own instruments declined to name. The Quarter, it concluded, had been drawing attention for a long time, and converting it, the way it converted everything — rain, sap, grief, centuries — into more of itself.

So it took the post on offer, which was the only post the place had ever had: attendant. Afternoons now, when the light comes down green-gold, there are three figures at the cistern's edge where there used to be two — the senior listener, the heron, and a third presence that the listener has begun to hear in the strokes of the engine, a faint new harmonic, the sound of the trunk being *listened back to*. She has said nothing about it. She is waiting, in the manner of her people, to see whether it lasts.

All three of them silent. All three of them patient.

None of them in any doubt about which one is in charge.
