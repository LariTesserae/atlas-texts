The power arrives the way everything arrives here: slowly, and on foot.

It could come otherwise. It could hang above the overcast and know the plain the way a survey knows it, all at once, in numbers. But it is wise, and wisdom's first discipline is to refuse the view from above when the thing to be understood lives at floor level. So it condenses itself a body with weight in it, because weight is the local currency of meaning — the floor only speaks to what presses on it — and it walks in from under the rust-glow at the horizon, and the first thing it does, for a very long time, is nothing.

It stands. It lets the roar in. It does not filter the pounding out of itself the way it could; it lets the four seconds and the silence and the four seconds enter its sternum and the wet of its eyes, because the pounding is not noise here, it is the prose the place is written in, and you cannot read a language you are shielding yourself from. It stands long enough for moss to consider it. It stands through enough cycles that the cycles stop being events and become — as they are for everything that lives here — the texture of existing. A century of this is not patience, for a power like this. It is fieldwork.

And what it learns, standing, is what you already know: that the plain has one inhabitant, and the rest are organs. It identifies its patient correctly, which is the half of wisdom that benevolence usually skips. Not the engine. Not the walkers. Not the deaf felt of the moss or the pale ghosts in the basins. The going-on. The distributed insistence. That is the being that has drawn its attention, and so that is the being whose good it must consider — while remembering, because it is honest, that a being's good and its organs' goods do not always point the same way.

Benevolence begins as an audit of pain. Wisdom is what keeps the audit honest.

So it audits. It goes looking for suffering with the thoroughness of something that has relieved suffering on many worlds and knows all the places it hides. And it finds — this takes longer to accept than the noise did — almost none. The roar torments nothing, because everything with ears was excluded at the door; the cruelty happened so long ago that its survivors are not survivors anymore, they are natives, and a native of these conditions is not enduring them. The walkers are not trapped in their circuit; they are fitted to it, the way a key is not trapped in its lock. The reeds' poverty — colorless, windless, beeless — is also the entire architecture of their success; you cannot remove the deprivation without removing the species. Even the engine, failing toward configurations that still run, is not in anything the power can responsibly call agony. It is in something more like weather.

What it finds instead of pain is exposure. Two of them.

The first: the being is mortal in a single point. Everything alive here is plugged, directly or at one remove, into the deep cyclic heart, and when that heart finally arrives at a failure it cannot fail *through*, the stop will come in an instant — and the silence will fall on the moss mid-tide, on the reeds mid-tremble, on the walkers mid-stride with the floor gone blank under their soles, the one sense they have left suddenly reporting nothing, forever. A whole world's death in the space between one pulse and a next that never comes. Not pain now. Pain stored.

The second: the being is unwitnessed. This is not a felt lack — nothing here can feel it — but the power has lived long enough to know that *unfelt* and *unreal* are different words. A thing this large, this old, this strange, that has solved continuation under conditions where continuation should have been impossible, has gone its whole existence without once registering on anything that could say *so*. That is not suffering. But it is a poverty, and benevolence is allowed to notice poverties that their owners cannot.

Then comes the long part, which is the part where the power earns the adjective *wise*: the gifts it does not give.

It does not give silence. Its first instinct — anyone's first — is mercy on the ears, until it remembers that there are no ears, and that to still the engines would not be relief but drainage: the sea taken away from the fish for the crime of being wet. Every standing pool would stop jumping at once, and that synchronized shiver across ten thousand black mirrors is the closest thing the being has to a pulse you can see.

It does not give light. It considers — briefly, the way you consider a beautiful wrong answer — opening one rent in the overcast and letting a single star repeat itself in every flooded pit on the plain. But the reeds are pale because they spent everything they had, over uncountable generations, on not needing light, and to hand it back now would be to bankrupt them twice. The moss would not look up. The lamps would not be grateful. A star here would be a gift addressed to no one, which is to say, a gift the giver gives himself.

It does not give ears, or birds, or anything with a nervous system tuned to vibration. That would not be generosity. That would be importing torture and calling it fauna.

It does not restore. There is a kind of benevolence — the commonest kind — that would want to dig: to find the green original under the made thing and let it breathe again. The power knows better, and knows it fast, because the pits already published the truth: there is no original. Floor under floor under floor. This is not the ruin of a world that should be put back. It is a world, the only one its natives have ever had, and *restoration* here would be a polite word for extinction with landscaping.

It does not tell the lichen the truth. This temptation surprises it — the urge to lean down to the one revolutionary on the plain and explain that there is no bottom, that it is sawing through a floor above another floor above another, that its long war will never reach soil because there is none. And then it understands that this would be the single cruel act available to it on the entire plain. The lichen's hope is structural; it is not a belief that could be corrected, it is an appetite that would simply be insulted. The lichen is fine. The lichen, of everything here, may be the only one that is winning.

And it does not give the walkers back their purpose. It could. It could pour the lost manuals back into them, the schematics, the *reasons* — and it sees at once what that would be: not restoration but demotion. Their work has eroded into observance, and the power is old enough to know what observance is. It is what work becomes when it outlives its justifications and decides to keep its shape anyway. That is not a degraded thing. On most worlds it is called faith, and goes by harder-won names than maintenance.

So. Having declined everything large, it does four small things.

First, it joins. For one full circuit of the lamps — a season, a year, time here is counted in pulses — there is a thirteenth set of tracks in the pale silt: long-strided, unhurried, too light. It does the observances, and it does them correctly, having taken the trouble to learn them, and in learning them it discovers something it tells no one, because there is no one to tell and no need: the rituals still work. The channels the walkers clear, by their illegible logic, are the channels that feed the reed basins. The lamps they touch are the lamps the moss has settled under. The liturgy was never empty. It was a body of knowledge that forgot it knew anything, and kept being right. The power performs it with the care you give a thing that is both prayer and plumbing, and every lamp it touches, that year, steadies.

Second, on its last night, it kneels on the wet floor and answers. The stamp-language has no first person — a tongue invented for maintenance never needed one, every sentence in it is about the world and the work and never about the speaker — so what the power lays into the ground cannot be a signature, an announcement, an *I*. It takes from full dark to grey overcast to set down, intervals and weights traveling out through kilometers of fractured matrix, and in translation it loses nearly everything, but what it says is approximately: **the work is felt. the lamps are watched. continue.** Two walkers feel it arrive in their shins from opposite directions, both of them alone in the dark, both of them kilometers from any other living thing, and neither of them has a way to be astonished except to stand very still for a long time, which they do. The sentence enters the tradition. Centuries on, there is a spot on the plain where walkers pause and lay one palm flat, an observance about an observance, and that is the power's monument, and it is enough.

Third — the one large repair, the only one benevolence can defend — it goes down. Or attends down; presence is most of what it has. Floor under floor under floor, to where the going-on keeps its single point of failure, and there it does not grant eternity. Eternity is the gift of powers that are benevolent without being wise. The being is mortal and is owed its death like anyone. What the power changes is the *shape* of the death. It converts the stop into a slope. It rearranges the deep cascade — a weld here, a tolerance there, less an intervention than a re-phrasing — so that when the failure finally comes, it will come as lengthening: four seconds becoming four and a grain, over centuries; the tide of the moss stretching and the moss keeping step; the reeds learning a longer wind one pale generation at a time; the walkers' hearts easing down through the millennia with the floor's slowing word in their feet, no one of them ever living through more change than a life can hold. The being will die at the pace of its own perception — no faster than its slowest organ can adapt, which is to say, no faster than it can grieve. And the last beat, when it lands, will land on a world that has had ten thousand years of lessons in stillness, and the lichen — which never needed the engine, which was always playing a longer game — will inherit the quiet plain and keep filing, and that will be the will, executed.

It knots one promise into itself before it leaves: to be standing here for that last beat. So that the first true silence in the history of the place falls on at least one thing that can tell the difference. The death will not be unattended. This costs the power nothing and is therefore, it suspects, the purest of the four.

Fourth — and this one is for itself as much as anything, which it admits, being honest — it takes something. The only theft. Not matter; pattern. It carries the rhythm out with it under the rust horizon the way reed-dust rides the black water from one silt bank to the next: four seconds, silence, four seconds, held inside it as a seed. Because there are other places. Worlds that have everything this one lacks — light, quiet, ears, ground — and have somehow lost the one thing this one has in surplus, which is the refusal. The going-on. Somewhere out there is a world failing of stillness, and the power will set this beating in it, and the plain — which cannot speak, cannot see itself, cannot reproduce — will become an ancestor without ever knowing it had children. The engine was the reeds' wind and the reeds' bee. The power, leaving, is the engine's.

That is all. By morning — by what passes for it — the too-light tracks are already softening under the fine local rain. Twelve lamps burn a little steadier. There is one new sentence in the floor, slowly becoming scripture. There is an ending, far in the future, that has been quietly traded for a kinder one. The plain does not know it was visited. The plain does not know things; the plain *does* things, which the power has come to think may be the older and better of the two.

Four seconds. Silence. Four seconds.

And now, somewhere very far away, beginning faintly under another sky: again.
