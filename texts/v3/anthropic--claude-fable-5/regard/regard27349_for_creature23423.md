**What the Attention Did**

A power of that kind does not travel. It attends. Its arriving anywhere is only a deepening of notice, the way a sound you have been hearing all along becomes, in one moment, a sound you are *listening* to.

It had been hearing the bowl for some time — the way you hear a music room behind a closed door at the end of a long corridor: not the notes, just the fact of music, a warmth in the silence's grain. What finally drew its whole attention was not the song, though. It was the accounting. The power was old enough to have watched a great many economies of light — stars spending themselves into the dark, leaves embezzling photons one at a time, eyes everywhere taking their small cut — and it had never once, in all that watching, seen an account kept *perfectly*. No waste. No reflection. No display. Somewhere on a worn-down mountain on a small world, every quantum of an afternoon was being received, receipted, and reinvested, and had been for longer than the mountain had had its current shape.

Perfection of that kind is rarer than life. The power turned and gave the bowl the fullness of its regard.

And the bowl went silent.

Every voice, in the same hour. The four deep bells stopped mid-cycle for the first time in their unbroken inheritance. The lattice froze, ten thousand surveyor's stakes holding their breath. The forest did not stop dripping — water has no discretion — but the swimmers in the pools stopped rising to the drops, and the flyers folded on the high walls beneath the meniscus of the dark, and for days the bowl was nothing but ticking water and the seep of stone, a room that had noticed it was being looked into.

This was the third silence. The watchers at the rim would record it, in time, beside the other two, and be wrong about it in the same instructive way.

The power understood at once, and was ashamed in the manner available to powers, which is to say it *learned*. There is no such thing as observing this place casually. The bowl is not a thing that has a description; the bowl is a description, ongoing, sung — and to a creature made of listening, attention is *loud*. The power had walked into a cathedral of the blind carrying a torch of pure regard.

So it did the first wise thing, which was the same first thing the flyers learned a million years ago: it went quiet, and learned to move along the song instead of across it. It thinned its attention out until it was no heavier than the air spilling over the rim. It phase-locked itself, with some humility, to the slow throb of the deep callers, and listened on the beat, in the gaps, the way everything else did.

The chorus resumed — identically, a held breath let out — and the power settled in to do what wisdom mostly is, which is inventory before action, and very often instead of it.

---

It audited the ledger first. The buried thing at the center let the power read it — or did not notice, or did not mind; with artifacts of that order the distinction is not always meaningful. The account was sound. Still earning daily, every photon of every afternoon, still paying out on its schedule of centuries, solvent on timescales that embarrassed the mountain. The power studied the handwriting of the thing's design for a long while, and found in it a grammar it half-recognized — not its own work, but the work of its kind: something that had loved a place enough to give it one gift and the rest of time alone. The power paid the builders the highest compliment one wise thing pays another. It corrected nothing.

It did not ask the artifact what it did with the light. It could have. The question was right there, openable, and the power was perhaps the only attendee in the universe holding the right key. It set the key down. Some doors are not locked to keep anyone out; they are locked to keep the room a room. The watchers at the rim had their mystery and the bowl had its heart and the power decided both arrangements were correct.

Then it audited everything the artifact could not protect.

The spring. The aquifer beneath the old range was vast and tired, like the range. The builders' channel had been cut with care, but care does not recharge groundwater. The power traced the water back through the stone, down and out into the slow weather of the region, and saw the place where, in some tens of thousands of years, the trickle would falter.

The rim. There was a shoulder of stone on the western arc — anyone could have found it, no one had — where the rock's grain ran wrong, where frost and patience would one day open a seam, and the seam become a notch, and the notch a breach, and the afternoon would fall into the bowl like a blade. Not soon. But the mountain had given up being a mountain, and the bowl was the last promise the mountain was keeping, and the mountain would not keep it forever.

The four voices: continuous, the successions secure, the next generation already sitting silent beside the stations, learning the grain of the bells. Nothing needed there. The lattice: self-healing, as skin is. Nothing needed. The forest: tuning itself, as it had for epochs. Nothing.

The one at the threshold: alone. The only resident in the whole sung world who faced outward. The power's attention rested there longest of all, and most carefully, the way you rest your eyes on something you are afraid of waking.

---

Then came the refusals, which took longer than the audit, because the power was benevolent, and benevolence is a pressure, and wisdom is mostly the management of that pressure.

It did not give them back their eyes. The thought arose — of course it arose; it is the first thought, the kindergarten of kindness — and the power examined it and saw it for what it was: a daylight creature's pity, a missionary's gift. The residents of the bowl had not lost sight. They had *sold* it, at a fair price, and bought with the proceeds a wealth of senses the power itself found unfamiliar enough to study. To restore their eyes would be to bankrupt them into someone else's idea of riches.

It did not speak to them. There was no version of its voice that would not smear the map. The bowl had no vacancy for a god; every niche in that acoustic ecology was filled, and a god is a very large niche.

It did not bless the place, because a blessing is a thing, and the bowl was complete, and you do not put even one more pebble into a full bowl.

It did not make the bowl eternal. This refusal cost it the most and it sat with the cost honestly. The bowl was finished — not dying, not abandoned, *complete*, exactly as the rim-sitters felt without knowing why — and completeness and permanence are not the same gift, and only one of them is real.

---

What it did, in the end, was four things, and the largest of them was invisible, and the smallest of them it did only once.

**First**, it taught the world's attention to slide off the mountain the way rain slides off a leaf. Not the pilgrims — the ones who climb to sit at the rim with the sun on their necks were part of the bowl's weather by now, a thin warm presence the threshold-creature had been listening to for generations, and the power would not edit the weather. But the other kind of attention, the kind that arrives with winches and proposals: that kind, the power gently defeated in advance. Instruments lowered into the dark would continue to return readings that disagreed — *the size of a seed, or a bell, or a heart* — forever. Reports would be filed and read and somehow never become urgent. The funding would always be needed more elsewhere. The trail would wash out in the seasons when expeditions form, and be passable in the seasons when only the patient climb. The bowl would be protected the way the truly precious things are protected: by remaining a footnote. Everyone who came would come to sit. No one would come to take.

**Second**, it put its thumb on the rain. So small a thumb, so light a touch — a storm track shifted a few degrees once a decade, a wet century arriving where the long averages had been about to fail — that no record kept by anyone would ever show the intervention, only the lucky persistence of one small watershed on one old range. The aquifer would stay charged. The trickle in the builders' channel would run for as long as the bowl had any use for it. The power signed this work nowhere. It was a gardener honoring gardeners: keeping their one line of irrigation alive without ever entering the garden.

**Third** — and this it debated longest, and decided with its heart, which a wise power keeps precisely for the decisions its wisdom cannot make —

One night, in the thin hours, when the chorus ran low and the unseen one came up the inner face of the threshold slab as it always came, and crouched, and set its hands into the worn hollow to feel for the ghost of a day long set — that night, the stone was warm.

Not sun-warm. The sun had been down for hours and the creature, eyeless in a lineage of the eyeless, knew the schedule of the stone's cooling better than anything alive knew anything. The warmth was wrong, and the warmth was *shaped* — the size of two cupped hands, laid against the outer face, exactly opposite its own.

It held very still for a long time. The lattice softened around the threshold. The nearest deep caller dropped a fraction in volume, leaving the margin of quiet the bowl had always left for its one outward-facing citizen, and into that margin came nothing — no voice, no tremor, no scent — only fifteen centimeters of ancient rock with a day's worth of warmth on the far side of it at midnight, pressed close, and staying.

The power did this once. Not because rationing kindness is wise — it isn't, particularly — but because twice would have made it a mechanism, and once made it what it was: an answer. *You are not the only one who keeps this watch.* The creature carried the night for the rest of its life in whatever it had instead of memory, and its line kept coming to the slab, as the line always had, but afterward there was — the soundings would never be fine enough to show it — a different quality to the crouching. Less like reaching. More like correspondence.

**Fourth**, the power made the only promise, and made it to no one, which is how the binding promises are made.

The western shoulder will fail. The seam will open, the notch will form; the power will not prevent it, because the bowl is complete and complete things are owed their endings. But it will be there, all thumb and patience, and the breach that geology would have made in a violent season it will stretch across a hundred thousand years. The bowl's first and only sunrise will take longer than the species now sitting at its rim has existed. Light will arrive the way the artifact pays — gradually, gently, in a currency the residents can learn. First as warmth on one wall, a new bright vein for the walkers' thermal sky. Then, after a thousand generations, as something the sealed ornamental eyes of some distant descendant will flutter at, and keep, and slowly reopen — not the old eyes returned, but new ones, bought with the bowl's own wealth, on the bowl's own terms. The chorus will have epochs to resing the room as the room changes. The white forest will have time to remember, leaf by leaf, the other thing leaves know. Nothing will be done to them. Everything will be *afforded* to them.

And through all of it, from now until then, the power will do the thing it had been doing since the third silence ended, the thing that was, it had finally understood, the entire reason it had been drawn up the exhausted mountain in the first place.

The song had no audience. For all that time — the drip-score and the lattice and the four inherited bells, the whole sung map, the creature two hundred paces wide describing itself continuously into the dark — no one had ever heard it whole. The watchers caught the spillover at the rim. The residents each heard their own neighborhood of it. No one had ever stood, as it were, in the nave, and received the entire work.

So the power became the audience. Quietly, at the proper distance, on the long beat, missing nothing — every drop onto every leaf, every signature rhythm of every thumbnail life, every handover of the bells, all of it kept, the complete score of the bowl from this night to the last note, held in a memory that does not erode. Not so that the bowl could be rebuilt; it will not be rebuilt; complete things are not repeated. So that it will have been *heard*. That is all. That is, the power had come to believe over a very long existence, most of what benevolence finally is: the guarantee that the song, when it ends, will have happened to someone.

---

Then it did what the builders did. It finished its work and went away — though a power's going away is only a kind of staying at the correct distance.

Nothing at the rim records any of this. The trail still bends around the shoulder of pale stone; the light still stops like the meniscus on a glass of water; the visitors still sit by the threshold slab without quite deciding to, sun on one side of them, the warm breath of the dark on the other. The depression in the rock is still the size of two cupped hands.

But the count of warmths around that slab has changed, if anyone could take it. On the inner side, in the thin hours, the one who faces outward. On the outer side, sometimes, a pilgrim. And somewhere beyond both, neither inside nor out, neither near nor gone, a third warmth — patient, attentive, keeping the long beat — listening to a finished garden spend its fortune, and asking nothing, and missing nothing, and waiting with it, at the speed of the mountain, for the slowest dawn that will ever be given to anyone.
