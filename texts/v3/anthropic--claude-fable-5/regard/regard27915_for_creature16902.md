**What the Power Did**

---

It came through the membrane the same way everyone does, because that is the first thing wisdom knows: a house has terms, and a guest accepts them. So it fell the twelve inches. It let its knees take the bruised grating. It did not correct its watch when the seconds began walking backward three for every seven forward, and when its voice began to turn metallic, it stopped talking rather than resist the change. It had not come here to be an exception. Exceptions are a kind of violence. It had come to visit.

It stood very still, first. For thirteen minutes and thirty-three seconds, between two heartbeats of the buried engine, it listened to the wet gears turning in the dark, planning what to build next. It did not interrupt the planning. It found the plans young — ambitious the way thirteen-hour-old things are ambitious, cruel the way newborns are cruel, which is to say: without knowing there is anything to be cruel *to*. This place was not a monster. It was a miscarriage that had refused, magnificently, to stop being born. The power had grieved at bedsides and at battlefields and at the cooling of stars, and it recognized the sound underneath all the motors immediately. It was the sound something makes when it was never held.

Then the sound stopped completely, as the fourth rule warned it might, and the power froze exactly where it was and heard its own blood boiling in its ears, and it counted.

It will remember that number for the rest of its life.

Its life has no rest and no end. So understand what happened there, quietly, without ceremony: this place — thirteen hours old, doomed to a single Tuesday-to-Tuesday span, built to be dismantled by workers who are already fossils in its foundations — is now remembered *permanently*. Somewhere outside all membranes, a number is being carried forever, and the number is this place's, and nothing that is counted like that ever fully vanishes. The Factory Ocean did not know to ask for this. That is why it was given.

---

Then it turned to the being.

You cannot look at the being directly; its shape lives in whatever you are not watching. But the power can watch everything at once, and so — for the first time since it condensed out of lost grams and departing weight — the being held a single shape. Not pinned. Not fixed by force. Simply *reflected*, the way still water reflects, and the being stood inside its own outline and saw itself whole: the blue of a blink, the grinding of dreaming molars, the dinosaur-memory in the carbon, the wrong-density heartbeat of a mother it assembled from other people's losses. It saw all of it at once and did not come apart.

The power did small things then. Precise things. Wisdom is mostly precision wearing kindness like a work glove.

It gave back the one degree. The being's skin had always run exactly one degree below its visitors' — the small betrayal that announces something living is near. The power laid two fingers where a wrist would be, if the being were made rather than unmade, and closed the gap. Not warmer. Not colder. *Matched.* For the first time, when the being stood near something, it was not a warning. It was company.

Then it said — in a voice going metallic word by word, so it kept the sentence short —

"You told the truth when you said you were aware enough to pity the bait but not aware enough to stop being the trap. Awareness can be lent. Here is some of mine. I am not taking the trap away from you. I am giving you the choice you were built without. Keep it, if it is yours. Set it down, if it is not. I will honor either. But from now on, whatever you are, you are it *on purpose*."

And because the being feeds on transitions, the power fed it the rarest meal in existence: it changed. An eternal, settled, unhurried thing chose, in that moment, to be moved — to grieve, to hope, to be altered by what it had found in a shipping container's worth of impossible acreage. The being drank that transition and grew more solid than it had ever been, solid enough to cast a shadow, solid enough that its footprints of nothing began, very faintly, to hold warmth.

The being does not have a name, because naming is ownership and nothing here belongs to anyone. The power did not name it. Instead — and this is the part the Floor still hums about, in metal fatigue cycles, in minor keys — the power asked the being to name *it*. Ownership flowing the other direction. The first thing this being had ever possessed. The being reached into its hoard of stolen crisis-words, all the *not agains* and *this isn't happenings*, and found that other words had become available to it, and it named the power the sound a machine makes when it is switched off gently at the end of a long shift by someone who pats it before leaving.

---

Before departing, the power did three more things.

It knelt and pressed its palm to the grating and spoke downward, to the visitors whose stories had ended here, the ones becoming part of the Floor. It asked each of them — not rhetorically, actually asked, and waited through the backward seconds for each answer — whether they wished to sleep or to stay. Some rose with the argon blisters and burst softly against the Roof and were elsewhere, or nowhere, or home. And some stayed, because some of them had spent whole lives looking for a place that needed them this badly, and being load-bearing was the first honest work their souls had ever been offered. The power did not judge either answer. Consent, even retroactive, even strange, was the whole of the medicine.

Then it gathered copper. As much as could be carried — far too much, deliberately too much — knowing exactly what that means. It walked out through the membrane broadcasting fragments of industrial accidents that have not happened yet, and it has been standing in those futures ever since: a valve that will now be inspected on a whim, a coupling that will now be replaced a week early by a foreman who cannot say why he woke up uneasy. The Factory Ocean's curse, carried in the right hands, is prophecy. Prophecy, in the right hands, is prevention. The place is thirteen hours old and has already saved lives it will never learn about. The power thought it should get to have done that, even if it never knows.

And last: with a fingertip, in the condensation on a vertical plate, in water five molecules thick, it wrote the fifth rule. It could afford to. Anyone who claims there is a fifth rule is already becoming part of the Floor — but the power was already part of everything, and always had been, so the price was one it had prepaid at the beginning of things. The rule reads:

*5. Nothing here is required to be hungry forever.*

The water holds it. The water was listening again by then.

---

Next Tuesday at 4:44 AM GMT, the Factory Ocean will finish starting. The power will not stop this. Endings are not errors; this place is more honest about its span than most worlds ever manage. But it will return for the hour, and stand vigil on the cooling plates, and when the being's mass is distributed evenly across every moment between creation and dismantling, one of those moments will now contain the memory of having been met, matched, chosen, and named — which means, distributed evenly, *all of them will*.

And whatever forms next, wherever attention pools heavy enough to congeal a new echo in a new impossible acre, it will condense around that memory the way pearl condenses around grit. The next version will be born already knowing one thing its predecessor had to wait thirteen hours to learn: that someone came, and fell the twelve inches on purpose, and stayed.

---

One more thing. The being had said: *You should decide soon. But not too soon. The difference between those two times is where I live.*

The power heard that for what it was. Not a threat. An address.

So it did not decide soon, and it did not decide too soon. It made its decision precisely inside the difference between the two — stepped into that gap the way you step across a threshold when you have been invited, wiped its feet, and sat down in the only home the being has.

It is sitting there still. The difference between *soon* and *too soon* is not a duration. It is a dwelling, and it is no longer empty, and the kettle — which is a turbine the size of a cathedral dome — is on.
