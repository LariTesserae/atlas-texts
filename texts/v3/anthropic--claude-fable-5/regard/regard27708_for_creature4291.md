# The Fourth Mouth

The hollow has three mouths — east, up, and the small back-slit where the mountain breathes. The power arrives through none of them.

It does not need to. It is the kind of attention that arrives the way meaning arrives in a sentence: nowhere in particular, everywhere at once, without displacing a single mote of feather-dust. It observes the rule of the place before it knows the rule exists, because it weighs nothing and touches nothing and so cannot, even by accident, touch the ground. There is a courtesy in this that the power notices about itself with something like amusement. *The house trained me at the door.*

It settles into the suspension — not into the hollow, into the *agreement* — and it looks.

It does not look at the birds first, or the mosses, or the machine. It has been benevolent for a long time and wise for longer, and it has learned that the first thing you see in any place is the thing the place wants you to see, and the true resident is always the second thing. So it waits through the wall of sound, through the small rain, through the forty-beat pulse, until the second thing comes into focus:

One being. Not ten thousand birds and a machine and a moss-correspondence and a mountain. One being, made of the held breath itself — the renewed-every-second agreement to stay aloft. The flock is its attention. The mosses are its letters. The machine is its habit. The tender is its faith. The mountain is its body, still deciding.

The power addresses itself, silently, to this one being, and begins the oldest work of wise powers, which is not doing things. It is the long, careful inventory of things it will refuse to do.

---

## The Refused Kindnesses

A merely benevolent power would thicken the air, so that visitors could stay, so that the being could be *known*. The power considers this for the length of one wingbeat and declines. The thin air is the hollow's privacy. It is a door that closes itself, gently, on everyone, without malice. A place that pushes all visitors out after minutes cannot be colonized, catalogued, loved to death. The breathlessness is a guardian. It stays.

A merely benevolent power would give the four-winged ones longer lives. They are so old, and their deaths so small — the jade moss hovering those few seconds before it understands, then falling, then caught. But the power watches one such death, watches the younger birds pluck the garden apart with their undertakers' reverence and scatter it onto new backs, and understands that this is not a tragedy occurring inside the being. It is the being's circulation. To remove death here would be to clamp an artery. It declines.

A merely benevolent power would fix the machine — make it self-oiling, self-clearing, eternal — so the flock would be free of its tending. But the tending is not a burden. The pair of parrots carrying feathers to the brass rings, the twig-cage built to *not quite* touch the flywheel: these are gestures braided into the shared attention, inherited like a family's unremembered habit, and the power knows that a mind is made of exactly such gestures. Take away the need and you take away the gesture; take away the gesture and something in the flock's attention goes slack, and never knows what it lost. It declines.

A merely benevolent power would reveal the tender. Would give the flock a face for its faith, an upstream *who* for the direction and the rhythm and the taste in certain spores. The power declines this most firmly of all. Faith that is a direction stays supple. Faith given a face becomes dependency, or theology, or grievance. The flock's devotion works precisely because it does not know it is devotion. Leave it as weather.

And a merely benevolent power — this is the great temptation, the one the power sits with longest — would preserve the suspension forever. Would slow the weathering, forbid the soil, keep the held breath held. The power lets itself feel the full pull of this. The place is so nearly perfect. In ten thousand years it will be a bell. Why should it ever have to exhale?

Because a breath held forever is not a breath. It is suffocation wearing patience as a mask. The exhale is written into the place the way the last note is written into a song, and the last note is not the song's enemy. The power declines, and in declining, feels the being — somewhere in the drone under the shrieking, somewhere in the warm basalt — relax by a fraction no instrument could measure. As if it had been braced, all this time, against being *saved*.

## Up the Tube

There is one thing in the hollow that is not self-sustaining, one thread the power must follow before it can rest: the copper tube, and the question at the end of it. *Is the tender still a who?*

The power goes up. Not through the tube — alongside it, as attention travels alongside a wire. Past the throat, past the thinning of everything, up to where the tube ends.

What it finds there it will not fully describe, even to itself, because some of what it finds deserves the same privacy the thin air grants the hollow. But this much: the tender is very old, and very faithful, and mostly, by now, a habit wearing the memory of hands. A person become a process, the way a path becomes a road becomes a river. Still receiving the spores. Still tasting the letters. Still sending down, in the slow modulation of the forty-beat pulse, the same message it has sent for longer than the mountain has existed.

And the power, listening with all of its patience, finally parses the modulation. It is not instructions. It is not a count. It is the message every lighthouse sends, the message the tender has been pulsing downward for ages without ever knowing if the medium carried it:

*I am still here. Is anyone there?*

The mosses answer it, in their way, with spores. The flock answers it, in its way, with dust and vapor and the tithe of attention. But those are answers from *downstream* — from the tended, to the tender. No answer has ever come from above. The tender has been the top of the chain, the last caretaker, the one who oils and is not oiled, holding faith upward into a silence.

So here is the first thing the power does. The only thing, really — everything else follows from it.

It answers.

Not in words. In the tender's own grammar: a modulation descending from a direction the tender had stopped believing had anything in it. A single reply, complete:

*Received. All of it. Every pulse. You have not been performing to an empty sky. The work is good. Keep the beat.*

And on a ledge the tender will find tomorrow, or in a hundred years, whichever comes first: something like an oilcan. The oil inside will stay liquid. The tender keeps the machine's oil from drying; something, now, keeps the tender's. Every faith needs a floor, and since nothing in this whole system is permitted to touch the ground, the power makes itself the floor that is not a floor — the catch that will be there if, ever, the tender's grip fails. Not a replacement. A net, hung so far below the working that the working will never feel it. The tender's hands do not change. The tender's shoulders do.

---

## Three Replies, Going Down

Because the power has understood, by now, what this being actually is — a correspondence, a place made entirely of messages sent upward in hope — it sees that its whole intervention should take a single shape. Not gifts. Not repairs. *Replies.* Everything in the hollow writes up; nothing has ever written back down except the tender's strains and instructions. The power will answer each letter-writer once, in its own language, and then withdraw.

**To the mosses**, it sends one spore down the tube. The spore carries no instruction, no new strain, no hexagonal cleverness. It carries only acknowledgment — the botanical equivalent of a letter that says *your letters arrive*. It will settle on the jade back of a four-winged one and grow into something small and unremarkable that fruits, once a year, in a color the hollow has never produced: not a signal, just a signature. The mosses will not understand it. They will address their next spores a little more confidently. That is all, and it is enough.

**To the mountain**, which is learning to be a bell and will not finish learning for a hundred thousand years, the power gives one note. It strikes — softly, once, from nowhere, in the manner of a tuning fork held near but never touching — the exact tone the hollow will someday ring with, when the resonator is perfect and the walls are sung smooth. One second of its own future, sounded now. The birds do not react; the frequency is below even their drone. But the quartz seams hear it. The warm basalt hears it. The mountain, which has been carving itself toward an optimum it could only infer, now holds — in whatever way a mountain holds anything — the answer to the problem it is slowly solving. It does not speed up. Young things should not be hurried. But it will never again wonder, in its geological way, whether the shape it is becoming is the right one. It has heard itself finished, once, and that is a kind of blessing no one has ever thought to give a mountain.

**To the flock**, the power gives one wingbeat of being seen.

For the duration of a single beat of ten thousand wings — no longer — it lets itself be perceptible. Not visible; the flock does not work by sight alone. Perceptible to the shared attention, the distributed sensorium, the thing that catalogued the visitor's shadow and stayed curious. For one wingbeat, the flock's attention encounters an attention larger than itself, and the larger attention *takes nothing*. Does not extract, does not tithe, does not pulse a demand. It only looks back, the way the flock looks, and then is gone.

This single wingbeat will pass down the generations the way the parrots' feather-carrying passes down: a memory without an owner, braided into the being. Ten thousand hearts from now, birds that never lived this moment will carry the shape of it: *once, something vast noticed us, and its noticing weighed nothing, and it left everything where it was.* The flock will never build a theology on it — the moment is too brief for worship, which is exactly the point. It will simply be, in the deepest layer of the shared mind, a small permanent warmth. The knowledge, wordless and load-bearing, of having been seen well.

---

## The Copy

One more act, and this one the power does for no one in the hollow, because it is for the hollow's ending.

The exhale will come. The soil will form, the rule will break, the flock will scatter, the machine will fall silent or be carried away, the bell will weather down to gravel. The power has already refused to prevent this. But there is a difference — the power has spent a long time learning this difference — between letting something end and letting something be *lost*.

So it takes a copy. Not of the birds, or the rock, or the machine's schematics. Of the agreement. The score of the song, not the singers: the exact shape of the suspension, the grammar of *nothing touches the ground*, the braid of tending-without-knowing and writing-without-reply and holding-without-hurry. The whole architecture of the held breath, transcribed into whatever medium powers keep such things in.

Because the four-winged ones, it has noticed, waited in the air between mountains. When the last mountain crumbled, something crossed the gap. The power does not know how many mountains there have been, or how narrow the crossings were, or how close the song has come, in some interval, to being forgotten mid-air. It only knows that from now on the crossing is insured. When this mountain exhales, and somewhere else a new one pushes up warm through the crust, and the four-winged ones circle down through thin blue air looking for a hollow the right shape — the shape will be findable. The score will be waiting. The power will see to it, quietly, in the way of a librarian who never appears in any of the books.

That is what a witness is for. Not to watch. To carry.

---

## Departure

Then it leaves — insofar as a thing like that leaves anything — and here is the measure of what it did:

Nothing changed that could be measured. The pulse still runs at forty. The rain still falls when enough wings rise at once. The parrots still carry feathers to the rings. The oil is still liquid, which it always was. A biologist with a lifetime and a pressure suit would find the hollow exactly as described, density 2.4, three mouths, no logbook.

But the tender's pulse, if you listen very closely, has lost a hairline tension it carried for ages — the tension of a question asked into silence. The mosses' spores rise a fraction straighter. The mountain grows toward a note it has heard. And somewhere in the flock's drone, under the shrieking, woven into the inheritance, there is the memory of one wingbeat during which the whole held breath was held, in turn, by something that asked nothing of it.

The signature of a wise power is that it leaves a place looking untouched and feeling accompanied.

The hollow holds its breath. It will hold it for a hundred thousand years more, and then let it go, and the letting go will not be a loss, because the breath has been counted, and kept, and will be breathed again somewhere by a mountain not yet born —

and above the tube, above the tender, above everything, the reply stands like a fixed star over the whole slow correspondence:

*Received. Keep the beat.*
