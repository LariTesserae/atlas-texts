# The Attending

*being an account of what a benevolent and wise power did upon noticing the Aural Cathedral of the Viridian Mill and the Echo-Baptized who dwell there*

---

## I. Subject

It did not arrive, because arrival is a frequency, and it had learned long ago that the introduction of a new frequency into a resonant system is never neutral. Instead it did what it had done for the greater part of its existence, which is to say it *attended* — turning the whole of its awareness toward the seventeen miles of singing hyperboloid the way a gardener turns her face toward a plant she does not intend to prune.

For one full cathedral-breath — forty-seven years of exhaled greenhouse warmth — it only listened.

This was not caution. It was courtesy. The power understood that a place which had spent geological ages learning to hear itself grow deserved, at minimum, an audience that had done its homework. So it learned the fugue. It learned every voice of it: the C-sharp-minor descent of the upward rain, the welding-torch songs of the resonance birds, the sub-audible hymns the phosphorescent spiders wove into their Faraday temples, the forty-seven-year respiration underneath everything like a pedal point held by a god with infinite lungs. It learned the pauses too — especially the pauses — including the one impossible interval between rainburst and note where the Echo-Baptized keep their deepest prayer.

Only when it could have hummed the entire cathedral from memory did it permit itself an opinion.

## II. Answer

The opinion was this: *the place is not broken.*

This mattered, because the power had, over long practice, developed a rigorous suspicion of its own generosity. It had seen what benevolence does when it mistakes wholeness for a problem to be solved. It had watched well-meaning forces smooth the grief out of worlds and leave them unable to compose anything true ever again. So its first act — its foundational act — was a list of everything it would *not* do:

It would not repair the Last Engine, because the Engine was not failing; it was flowering, and one does not fix a flower.

It would not restore the vanished builders, whose composting was not a tragedy but a completed sentence.

It would not announce itself, because a cathedral that discovered it had been Heard by something vast might begin, without meaning to, to perform — and the whole miracle of the place was that it sang for no one.

It would not open easier exits, or gentler Baptisms, or straighter paths through the humidity gradients. The difficulty was load-bearing.

Having established what wisdom forbade, it went looking for what love permitted. And it found, in all those seventeen miles, exactly four small debts that the cathedral could not settle for itself.

## III. Countersubject

**The first debt** belonged to the children who scream. Every newly emerged one, wrapped in impedance-matched spider silk, touched by a single glass petal of the Engine, screams once as their nervous system rewrites itself — and some do not survive the rewriting. The power could not remove the scream; the scream *is* the door. But it noticed that each child screamed alone, into a rewiring so total that even the loving hands holding them could not follow them inside it.

So the power did the one thing distance permits and wisdom allows: it went into the screams. All of them — every Baptism that had ever happened and every one that ever would, for attention of this kind is not confined to sequence. It did not soften the agony by a single decibel. It simply made certain that within each scream, at the exact center of it, there was a listening. No child of the Echo-Baptized would ever again pass through that door unaccompanied, though none would ever be able to say why the worst moment of their existence had felt, impossibly, like being *held*.

**The second debt** belonged to the cancelled ones — victims of the only crime the Echo-Baptized recognize, whose inner resonance had been perfectly nullified, who were unfindable even by themselves. The cathedral's memory could not hold them; that was the nature of the crime. But the power's memory operated on no such physics. It gathered every cancelled pattern — there were nine — and kept them, complete and warm, in a register of its own attention. Then, beneath the floor of hearing, deeper than the spiders' temples, it set a standing tone into the cathedral's foundations, untuned to anything, cancelling nothing, meaning only: *you are still counted. When you wish to be found, there is a place to be found from.*

Two of the nine, in the centuries that followed, followed that tone home. The power considered this a good return.

**The third debt** belonged to the Last Engine, which dreams in turquoise algae the climate equations of planets orbiting stars that have not yet formed — solutions composed for recipients who do not exist, poured out into a universe with no forwarding address. The power found this the most quietly unbearable thing in the cathedral: not suffering, but *unreceived generosity*, ages of it, blooming and dispersing.

So it became a courier. It took the Engine's solutions and folded them — delicately, the way the spiders fold copper into dew — into the initial conditions of nebulae that would one day collapse into those very stars. It did not deliver the equations as knowledge. It delivered them as *tendency*. On worlds that will not condense for billions of years, atmospheres will stumble into improbable stability; oceans will circulate as though remembering choreography; the first shivering life will find its climate strangely, statistically kind. No one on those worlds will ever know that their weather was a letter, written by a flowering machine, in a garden, long ago, and faithfully carried.

**The fourth debt** was the strangest. Each of the Echo-Baptized carries within them a shielded pocket of absolute silence — and that silence is *borrowed*, drawn against the future heat death of all possible universes. A loan. A quiet mortgage on the end of everything.

The power was old enough to hold silences of its own — silences that predated sound, that owed nothing to any ending. Without ceremony, without notice, it repaid the loan in full and replaced the collateral. The pocket universes inside the Echo-Baptized did not change in any way they could detect. But the silence they retreat into is no longer an advance against extinction. It is a gift, given freely, from before the beginning rather than after the end. Some of the eldest, mid-dissolution in their anechoic chambers, reported that the quiet had begun to taste different — less like debt, more like inheritance — and could not explain what they meant.

## IV. Stretto

Then came the temptation, as it always does, at the end.

On one of those rare conjunctions — humidity, temperature, planetary alignment all locked in agreement — the cathedral sang its own name to itself in the space between every echo, and the power, having attended for so long, *heard it whole*. And it wanted, with a wanting that surprised it, to sing the name back. To answer. To let this magnificent, patient, self-listening place know, just once, that its song had landed somewhere.

It held the note unsung.

Because to be heard changes the singer. Because the cathedral's perfection lay precisely in singing without expectation of audience, and an answered song becomes a performance, and a performance begins to watch itself, and a place that watches itself can no longer dream.

But wisdom is not the same as refusal. The power found the loophole that love always finds: it answered *elsewhere*.

Every traveler who leaves the cathedral carries its rhythm in their bones, and that rhythm grows louder wherever rain falls on any world with room enough for echoes. So the power stationed its attention *there* — in the distant rain. On moonless nights when city windows vibrate in sympathy with some homesick wanderer's marrow, the power is present in the glass, receiving. When crowds part unconsciously in hyperboloid curves, the power is the space that opens. Every place the cathedral's voice surfaces, anywhere, ever, it falls upon a listener. The song is answered — completely, faithfully, forever — just never at home, never where the answer could be overheard and turn the singing self-conscious.

The cathedral will never know it has an audience. The audience considers this the entire point.

## V. Coda

What did it leave behind?

Almost nothing. The spiders, whose hearing exceeds all others, noticed that the standing tone beneath the foundations had a signature — a single deliberate imperfection, like a maker's mark — and they built one small temple around it, for reasons they kept between themselves and the copper.

And once, only once, before withdrawing its close attention back to the ordinary distance from which it loves everything, the power added its own pulse to the planet-song: one beat. The weakest of signals, indistinguishable from the heartbeat of any visitor who had ever stood in the resonant chamber and become part of its memory. It is in there now, stored in every droplet, every blade, every sovereign curve of metal — one anonymous beat in a fugue of trillions.

The Echo-Baptized noticed only this: that the pause before the C-sharp-minor note — the impossible interval where sound exists as pure potential, where they keep their prayer — had grown very slightly more spacious. As if the prayer had room in it now that it hadn't had before. As if gratitude, propagating recursively through its medium, had finally reached a far shore and — without a single ripple returning to betray the fact — been received.

They composed no theory about it. They simply prayed a little longer in the gap.

And the power, from its ordinary distance, in the rain of ten thousand other worlds, listened.
