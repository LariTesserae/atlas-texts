**The Caldera of Standing Steam: a visitation**

---

**The arrival**

It comes the way the predator travels: inside the punctuation. The western vent fires, the valley fills with pneumatic noise for nine or ten seconds, and when the sigh dies and the silence resumes, the power is simply present, standing on the moss at the marsh edge — though *standing* is courtesy, and *present* is approximation. The moss does not take its print. There is nothing for the moss to refill.

It does the first wise thing immediately, which is nothing. It listens. It hears the chuff, and the smaller answering vent slightly out of phase, and the batter-fold of the mudpots, and the clicking ravine, and beneath all of it the felt-more-than-heard hum of pressure moving through stone. Within three cycles it has understood that the valley keeps its own time, and it does the second wise thing, which is to convert itself to local units. It will not stay an eternity. It will stay forty breaths — a good sleep, in the only currency this place honors — and whatever it does, it will do inside that span.

---

**What it sees that the visitor could not**

The visitor at the rim saw down through haze. The power sees down through everything.

It sees the million bromeliad ponds at once, each with its census of tadpoles. It sees the copper frogs' two populations pulling apart like a terrace splitting into layers, and it sees how many generations remain before the high frogs and the low frogs no longer answer each other at dusk. It sees the spiders rising in the charged plumes, hundreds of them aloft at this moment, drifting under the cloud lid like a thin silver weather. It sees the hummingbirds asleep in their pockets of rising warmth, the only ones of their kind on Earth excused from the nightly emergency, and it pauses over them longer than over anything else so far, because a creature that has been excused is a rare and instructive sight.

It sees down through the andesite into the fracture networks, the whole hidden plant floor — and it sees along time in both directions, which is where the trouble is.

Backward: the cold ages flowing around the rim like a river around a stone, frost to the very lip, and inside the bowl no winter, ever, the vents keeping their eleven minutes through a hundred thousand years of ice. The terraces going up millimeter by millimeter, person by person, the longest construction project it has witnessed in some while.

Forward: a sealed lens of melt beneath the southeast benches. An old chamber, walled off from the plumbing ages ago, slowly recharging. Pressure with no vent. The power runs the calculation in less than a breath — soon, by terrace time. A few thousand years. And then not a long dusk, not a cooling, not winter arriving at walking pace from the rim down — but an afternoon. The bowl opened to the sky in an hour. The terraces, the oldest things here and still under construction, ended as rubble. Every resident of the valley dies eventually and is metabolized — layered, eaten, steamed, covered. This is the one death the valley cannot metabolize. This death does not become a layer. It becomes a crater with nothing written in it.

The power sits with this for a full breath, in the local manner: short observation, long pause.

---

**What it considers, and refuses**

It is benevolent, so it is tempted, and it is wise, so it takes the temptations out one at a time and looks at them in the pearl-gray light.

*Make it eternal.* Seal the plumbing in permanence; let the eleven minutes run forever; let the heat never fade. Refused, and refused first, because it is the most flattering temptation and therefore the most suspect. The people take each child to the rim at the cold edge of evening so the child will know, in the body, that the valley is a kindness and not a law. This is the truest thing believed by anyone in the bowl. To make the valley eternal would be to make it a law after all — to reach down and quietly falsify the one lesson these people climb into the frost to teach. A benevolence that begins by making its beloved's deepest understanding into a lie has misnamed itself.

*Restore the sloth's kind.* It would be easy — the power remembers the old fauna the way the sloth's dung remembers the old fern, completely. It could give the green sloth a companion, a lineage, a future. Refused. You cannot resurrect a world by reissuing one of its citizens; you only conscript that citizen into a counterfeit. The sloth is not a fragment waiting to be completed. It is a whole thing finishing well — slowly, warmly, furnished by the valley, on an old settlement with the only creature that could harm it. The kindest thing one can do for a whole thing finishing well is not interrupt it.

*Reveal itself to the people. Teach them. Warn them.* Refused, and this refusal takes the longest, because the power has loved many peoples by warning them and is in the habit. But it studies their grammar — the valley as the subject of every sentence, themselves as objects, acted upon, kept warm — and it understands that there is no comfortable seat in that grammar for a second giver. These are a people with no granaries and therefore no rank, no famine and therefore no debt. Introduce them to a benefactor and you introduce them to owing. They are the only people the power has ever found who do not need to be told their home is mortal. They tell their own children, every generation, at the rim. There is nothing to teach here. There is, the power admits with something that in a smaller being would be embarrassment, a great deal to learn.

*Make a record.* Copy the valley into permanence elsewhere; archive it; seed a second caldera against loss. Refused last, and gently. A copy of a self-manufacturing thing is not a backup; it is merely a different thing. And there is the matter of manners. The valley keeps no record of its people, and they return the courtesy. The moss refills every footprint within the hour. The power, which remembers everything and is rarely asked to stop, decides that it would like — just once — to deserve the same courtesy. It will remember the valley. It will not record it. It is surprised by how much the distinction weighs.

---

**The first act**

What remains, after the refusals, is small enough to do and large enough to matter: not the death, but the genre of the death.

The power reaches down through four kilometers of stone — *reaches* is courtesy again — and opens a hairline. One fracture, finer than a root, threading the sealed chamber into the existing plumbing of the mountain. Nothing detonates. Nothing changes that anything alive could notice. The western vent's chuff will lengthen, over the centuries, by a fraction of a second that falls inside the tolerance of every nervous system keeping its time. The pressure that would have ended the valley in an afternoon will instead bleed upward through the vents across thousands of years, and then, slowly, the heat will begin to fade — which it always would have, someday; the power has not cancelled the mortality, only translated it.

Now the ending will go like this: the red rings of the hot pools will retreat, season after seasonless season, and green will follow them inward, and the people will read it in the mats long before it troubles them, because they were the original authors of that lesson. The two frog populations will finish becoming two, and the cooler one will inherit. Winter — which has always been a place, the rim, *everywhere else* — will arrive at walking pace, a place that moves, descending the benches a hand's-width in a lifetime, slow enough for the rim ritual to become a migration with generations of warning, slow enough that the last of the people can lie down on a terrace still warm enough to film over, still building, and be kept.

The valley will get to die the way its residents die: covered, slowly, layered into the oldest thing in it. Not saved. Kept. The power examines its work and finds the seam invisible. In the people's grammar, the hairline will be the mountain's own doing — which, it reflects, is nearly true, and *nearly true in the right direction* is the best any giver can hope to manage.

---

**The second act**

In the pearl-gray afternoon, the power stands in the fern understory, in the path of the dark shape, and permits itself to be seen — for four or five seconds, the same ration the valley granted the visitor, because symmetry is a form of respect.

The sloth stops. It is not afraid; it has reasons to fear something here, but the power is not that thing, and the sloth's old senses know it. What passes between them is not a vision and not a voice. It is closer to this: for one breath, the sloth's world is not small. For one breath, the smell of the ten-thousand-years-gone fern is not a trace in its own dung but an abundance, remembered fully by something old enough to remember it — and the sloth understands, in whatever way a sloth understands, that the world it is the last subscriber to was real, and is *kept*, and that being last is not the same as being unwitnessed.

Then the breath ends, and the sloth resumes browsing, hooked claws pulling the fronds in the way no tapir ever managed, and the power notes — with something adjacent to pride — that the frog chorus never stopped. The valley did not classify it as a predator. It has been audited by the bromeliads and found harmless. Few of its kind could say the same.

---

**The third act**

For the people, it does the largest thing it knows, which is to protect the shape of their understanding by leaving it untouched.

It passes the curtained tube mouths once, at dusk, as a color at the edge of things. The watchers in the fern shadow count it against the breaths, read it the way they read the algal mats — *a condition, not a visitor* — and conclude it will be gone by morning.

The power makes them right. It considers this the most successful conversation it has had in several ages: short phrase, long pause, no debt incurred on either side. Their valley remains the subject of all their sentences. No second giver was introduced. Somewhere below, a woman stands on her grandmother and watches the water film over the terrace lip, satisfied, and nothing in her cosmology has been disturbed, and the power counts this — correctly — as the act, not the omission of one.

---

**What it takes when it goes**

It leaves between two chuffs, the way it came, and the moss has nothing to forget, which in the local grammar means the valley finished forgetting it before it arrived. It finds this restful. It suspects the people knew it would.

But the factory with no owner and no product exported something after all, in those forty breaths, without noticing — the way it does everything.

The power leaves carrying the breath. Somewhere in whatever serves it as a heart, an eleven-minute rhythm runs now, and it has begun to think in that meter: short phrase, long pause, the answer given room to cross the air. It leaves having learned the difference — which it could have stated before but had never *stood in* — between saving a thing and keeping it; between making a kindness permanent and making it a law; between a record and a memory; between the benevolence that builds and the benevolence that declines to, because the home has not failed anyone, and construction would be a confession.

And when it next finds something good and mortal and finishing well — it will, it always does — it will not reach for eternity first. It will listen for the local breath. It will give itself forty of them. It will look for the one death the place cannot metabolize, and translate only that, and leave by morning, and be read as weather.

Asked, in whatever assemblies such powers keep, what benevolence owes the things it loves, it answers now in a borrowed grammar, and the answer is short, with a long pause after it:

*Warmth from below. No accounts kept. The layers allowed to close.*

The moss took its footprints within the hour. There were no footprints. It had learned that much before it ever touched down.
