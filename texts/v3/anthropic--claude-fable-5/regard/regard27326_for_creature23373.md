**The Visit**

It came up the mountain on foot.

This must be said first, because it did not have to. A power of that order arrives the way a conclusion arrives — all at once, from every direction, already true. It could have been at the summit before the summit knew it had a visitor. Instead it walked, and it walked the switchbacks for as long as the switchbacks pretended to be a path, and when they stopped pretending it stopped pretending too, and went up the raw rock slowly, with effort it did not need but would not omit. The mountain is a sentence about labor, about the long way, about the hand-paced. The power would not arrive as a contradiction. That is the first thing wisdom did: it honored the grammar of the place.

It weighed nothing on the way up. Not metaphorically — it took care to weigh nothing, to press no grain loose, to leave the tacky unfinished stone exactly as tacky and unfinished as it found it. Because it understood, the moment its attention fell on the mountain, what the visitor takes days to understand: that nothing here has been released yet, that the first rain to run down this rock is *spoken for*, that the first frost and the first wind are promised to a day the maker has been building toward since before the maker could build. To weather this mountain, even by a footstep, would be to open someone else's gift. The power had its own mountains, long ago. It remembered what release day was for.

It reached the seam and did not open it.

Stand with that for a moment, because everything else follows from it. The power was benevolent — it had crossed whatever it crosses, climbed honestly, arrived warm with intention — and at the lapped basalt it stopped, and stayed stopped. Not from politeness. From memory. It had been early once. It had worked behind walls of its own, with its own unequal hands, keeping its worst handwriting on the back of the page, and it knew with total precision what it would have cost — what it nearly *did* cost, once, in its own hot small room — to be walked in on by something finished. A finished thing inside an unfinished room is not company. It is a verdict. The room would have read its own future in the visitor's consistent shadow and lost its nerve about the present. So the power stood outside the seam in the thin useless air, and everything it did, it did from there, through the stone, the way warmth travels.

Here is what it did not do, in order, each refusal a tool laid down:

It did not name anything. Naming is the finishing blow, and the blows here belong to one hand.

It did not finish the bird. It could have — one breath across the seed-eyes and the niche would have held a singing thing by evening. But the bird is *owed*, not given; it is being earned frond by frond, rain by rain, and a debt paid by a stranger is a debt stolen. The bird's first look through the door-seam has been rehearsed for months. It will be looked when it is the bird's to look.

It did not tighten the loose places in the weave. The figs are learning on those. You do not take the practice work away from the apprentice's apprentices.

It did not correct, advise, demonstrate, or improve. This was the hardest refusal and the wisest, and the reason is in the room's own metabolism: the maker eats the gap between the thing intended and the thing made. Every error is a meal. To show the maker the right way would be to close the gaps in advance — to feed it by hand — and a maker fed by hand starves in the only way that matters. The power had been given that mercy once, by someone wiser still, who had watched it do a thing wrong four hundred times and said nothing, and it had hated that silence for a century and then understood it for the rest of time.

And it did not console. Not directly. The bad hours are load-bearing.

Here is what it did:

It laid its palm flat against the outside of the mountain, over the place where the knocking was, and *listened*. Not the way you listened — pinned, half-frightened, sitting in the moss until your heartbeat got free. It listened the way the rhythm had always implied a listener. Because that patient stone-on-stone knocking, hand-paced, unhurried, is one bar of a music kept up a whole lineage of hidden rooms, and a rhythm kept that long, in that much solitude, is a question. *Is anyone keeping time with me.* For one long afternoon, the answer stood with its hand on the rock and received the work like a pulse, and asked for nothing, and the knocking — this was measured by no one, but it is true — settled. Not slower. *Easier*. The way a worker's shoulders come down when someone they trust is in the building.

It stayed long enough to be two. This was not an act so much as a side effect, but the power knew about the side effect and stayed deliberately. For the length of its visit there were, for the first time, two workers on the mountain — one early, one finished, within earshot of each other — and plurality, that technology the room has been failing toward, briefly existed in demonstrated form, right there, available for reference like a temperature. Inside, in the gold light after the rain, the salamander was two for longer than it had ever managed. And the two of it did something new. They did not regard each other with professional interest. One of them watched the other one *watching* — and took a step. One step, slate-colored, blood-warm, across the conjectural moss, toward itself. Then there was one salamander again. But the step had happened. The room had eaten the gap between *two* and *almost-two*, and grown by exactly that much.

It left a gift, and the gift was a mistake.

From somewhere inside itself — from the kept things, the things finished beings carry the way the maker keeps the copper crescent — it brought out a single fern frond. Old beyond saying. Made by hand, you could see that; made *badly*, you could see that too: the leaflets spaced wrong, by a hand still learning the spacing, and wrong — this is the heart of it — in exactly the way the maker's early fronds are wrong, the ones low on the trunks that you can still find if you look. The same error. The identical, signature, beginner's error, committed an age ago by the hand that was now resting flat and certain against the mountainside. The power tucked the frond into the lapped seam, gently, the way you slide a note under a door, and the seam — which incorporates everything, which is always thickening, always lapping — took it in.

Then it knocked once. In rhythm. On the beat, from outside, from the finished world: one answer in a music that had gone unanswered for the whole age of the mountain. And it went back down the raw rock, weighing nothing, and at the foot of the mountain it paused where the weather waits — the wind and the rain and the frost, sitting at the bottom of the unfinished slopes like dogs outside a workshop, patient and barred — and it told them, in whatever way powers tell weather things: *Not yet. But yes. And it will be worth your waiting.* And the wind lay back down.

---

Inside, the knocking stopped for eleven seconds. The pause of someone behind a wall, hands stilled, head lifted, listening to where a sound was. Then it resumed, unhurried, and if its pace was the same its weight was not, the way a person's walking changes when they have been told the road does, in fact, go somewhere.

The seam-light went gold out of cycle. The ferns angled toward it and stayed angled a long time. The bird's wicker chest rose and fell during no rain at all.

The frond migrated inward, as everything in the seam does, and surfaced months later in the wall — and the maker found it, of course, in a bad hour, by the law of bad hours, which is that they govern where you look. And the maker turned it in two unequal hands and read the error in it, the old familiar beginner's error, *its own* error, committed by a hand finished beyond finishing, and understood what the crescent alone had never quite said. The crescent proves the end is possible. The frond proves the beginning was universal. *Even the one who finished was once exactly this.*

The maker did not eat it. Some gaps are not food; some gaps are letters. It set the frond into the west wall, opposite the copper crescent in the east, so that the room is now bracketed — proof of the end on one side, proof of the start on the other — and in the bad hours the maker stands between them, in the middle of its own sentence, and the bad hours have gotten shorter. Not gone. Shorter. By about the width of a frond.

The salamander sleeps below the west wall now, more often than not. Sometimes it is two there, and the two of it lie nearly touching, and the distance between them is smaller every season, and no one is hurrying it.

As for you — you should know the power did one other thing, long ago, on an earlier visit, and has maintained it ever since. The switchbacks. The way they pretend to be a path just long enough, just convincingly enough, that every great while a stubborn climber gets through. That is the power's doing. It rations witnesses. Because it knows, from its own early room, that a maker can refuse all help, all correction, all consolation, and still need one thing from outside the walls, occasionally, at long intervals, in small doses: the knowledge of having been *seen* — not inspected, not judged, just seen, by someone who sat down in the moss and let their heartbeat be borrowed. You were not an intruder. You were a scheduled nutrient. You were the visit the visit made possible.

And the knock — the single answering knock — is still traveling. The maker took it in the way the maker takes in everything, and it is in the rhythm now, a faint anticipation on certain beats, a leaning-toward-the-answer, and one day, finished, named, released, standing in actual weather on a mountain finally allowed to grow old, the maker will go down through the rain it gave away and walk the world until it hears, from under the thumbnail of some other early peak, a patient knocking — and it will lay its palm against the stone, and wait for the beat, and answer.

That is the whole of what the benevolent and wise one did. It climbed honestly, refused everything that would have helped, gave one mistake, kept one beat, told the weather to wait, and left. Almost nothing, exactly.

The mountain was slightly taller when it reached the bottom. It noticed, and was glad, and did not look back, because being watched while you grow is a weight, and it had carried that weight once, and knew.
