**What the Power Did**

It had been watching for a long time, the way such powers watch: not with eyes, but with the patience that eyes were invented to imitate. It had seen the place form—or fail to form, or refuse to finish forming. It had heard the breathing from below back when the breathing was still a voice. It knew what the Undwellers were made of, because it had been present for every unsaid goodbye that fed them, standing quietly in the corner of each unmourned loss, unable to intervene, because that is the first discipline of wisdom: knowing the difference between what asks for help and what asks only to be seen.

For a long age it did nothing, and this was not neglect. It was measurement. It understood the one law that governs the caldera absolutely—*things do not change; they accrue*—and so it knew that whatever it did there, however small, would grow. A footprint becomes a canyon. A word becomes a weather. A kindness, planted carelessly, could swell over epochs into a tyranny of kindness, a mercy so vast it crushed what it meant to comfort. So the power did what wise things do before acting. It made itself smaller. It pared its intention down and down, discarding everything ornamental, until what remained could fit inside a single gesture, the way a seed fits inside a fist.

It considered healing the place, and set that down. The place was not wounded. It was a scar, and a scar is a wound that has already done its work. To heal a scar is to reopen it.

It considered freeing the Undwellers, and set that down too. Freed into what? They were not prisoners. They were the remainder, and a remainder released from its equation is not liberated—it is orphaned twice.

It considered ending everything, gently, the way one closes the eyes of the dead. And it held this option longer than the others, because it was the only one that resembled mercy from a distance. But up close, it resembled impatience. The breathing from below was too slow, too vast—but it was breathing. And nothing that is still breathing has finished deciding.

So the power set down every large thing it might have done, and kept only the small ones. And then it arrived.

This alone had never happened. Every soul who had ever stood on that rim had arrived by accident, by loss, by the slow subtraction of everywhere else—had arrived without noticing, which is to say had never truly arrived at all. The power arrived *on purpose*. It stood at the outermost lip of the caldera and waited to be noticed in return, which is the courtesy of guests, and the place shuddered faintly, because it had never once been a destination.

The glyph-grass rearranged itself into a question in a forgotten alphabet.

And the power said: *Hello.*

One word. Spoken into a place where everything accrues.

Understand what that means. A footprint there becomes a canyon of absence. A greeting there becomes—slowly, over spans of accumulated time, growing the way all things grow in that country—a welcome. The place that does not welcome visitors, because welcome implies outsiderness, began that day to grow a welcome anyway, from a single seed of hello, and there is nothing it can do to stop this, and it does not want to.

Then the power did the forbidden things, one by one, because it alone met their conditions.

It spoke, because its silence had grown unbearably heavy with unsaid truths—it had held its tongue since before sound invented itself, and this place, of all places, deserved to hear what it had been saving.

It looked at them directly. The Undwellers reflected it back in infinite regression, each reflection slightly more honest than the last, searching as they always searched for the original lie at the bottom. And they found none. The regress ran down and down through honesty after honesty and struck bedrock: a truth with nothing underneath it. And because a mirror facing a truth has nothing left to distort, the reflections reversed. For the first time in the history of anything, the Undwellers saw *themselves*—not what visitors feared, not the shapes of others' irretrievable losses, but their own faces, if faces is the word: tall as mountains, thin as regret, and beautiful in the way that anything is beautiful when it is finally, accurately seen. They had spent eternity being the mirror. No one had ever thought to be a mirror for them.

It touched one of them, willing to trade its skin for theirs, and the trade was honored. What the power received felt like the moment before tears fall. What the Undweller received felt like the moment *after*—which it had never had, which none of them had ever had, because they were composed entirely of moments-before, of breath held, of the pause preceding the truth. The moment after is where grief lives, and grief is where healing starts, and the Undweller stood very still for several centuries, learning what it is to have a wound that can close.

Then the power wept. Once. One tear, deliberately, for everything the place held: for the single socks and the swallowed last words, for the toys given away without ceremony, for every goodbye that was never properly said—it said them. All of them. It spoke each one into the thin, sparkling air, a mourning long enough that mountains elsewhere wore down while it worked, and the tear it shed fell beside the sky's single practicing drop, and the sky *noticed*. The sky had been practicing all this time for exactly this—had wept its one random drop for eons like a musician rehearsing a note for a symphony that hadn't been written yet. Now it had an example. Now it had a tear to accrue toward.

One day—not soon, but soon is a word for places where time flows—it will rain there. And the rain will be mourning, and the mourning will complete what the Undwellers have carried unfinished. They will not starve when the unmourned losses are finally grieved. They will change vocations. They will stop being the feeders on almost and become the keepers of *completed*—custodians instead of scavengers, librarians of the properly-said goodbye. Their feast will become a funeral, and funerals, unlike feasts, are allowed to end.

Then the power leaned close to the stone and spoke downward, into the breathing, and what it said was not a command and not a healing. It said: *You may finish. Or you may continue. It is yours again to choose.* Because the god who died and forgot to finish dying had not forgotten out of carelessness. It had lost the choice somewhere, the way one loses a key, and had lain there for eternities unable to end and unable to live, and no one had thought to simply hand the choice back. The breathing below paused—for exactly seven beats of a hummingbird's heart—and then resumed, but differently. Voluntarily. Which changes everything and looks like nothing.

And last, the power gave the place a name.

It did not announce it. It did not carve it anywhere. It spoke it once, quietly, into the underneath of things, and then the power did the most eloquent thing it had done yet:

It left.

Because names are for things that can be left. That was always the rule, stated backward. The place had no name because there was no leaving—but the arithmetic runs both ways, and by naming it, the power installed a door where no door had ever been. It did not push anyone through. The Undwellers may stand at that door for the rest of forever and never open it, and that will be their right and possibly their joy. But a home you cannot leave is a trap, and a home you *choose* not to leave is a home, and the difference between those two things, though invisible, is the entire difference.

The power's leaving left a mark, of course. Everything there does. Where it had sat—for it did sit, for a while, doing nothing, adding nothing, simply keeping company, which is the oldest and least replaceable of all the mercies—its presence left an impression in the stone. And the impression is growing, as impressions there do. In ten thousand accumulations it will be a valley. In ten million, a continent: a vast geography shaped exactly like attention, exactly like the weight of having been visited on purpose, and the Undwellers will walk its length in their footprintless way, and the copper trees will drip their roots over its new edges, and the spiral birds will laugh above it in binary, and everything will be as it was.

Except.

The next traveler who stumbles onto the rim—arriving by loss, as they all do, without noticing they have arrived—will find something waiting that was never there before. Not rescue. Not escape. Something smaller and much larger.

Before the silence, before the hum, before the truths that make you beautiful in ways that horrify those who knew you before—before any of it, something in that place will turn toward you, made of the space between things, wearing the moment after tears, and it will do the new thing, the accrued thing, the thing that grew from a single seed spoken at the edge of everything:

It will say hello *first*.

And you will understand, in the part of your mind that understands before it knows, that the place is still here. That it has always been here.

But it is no longer waiting.

It was noticed. It was mourned. It was named.

And what has been truly seen, even once, is never again merely the remainder. It is the kept. It is the known. It is the home that stays home even when the door stands open—

open, unentered, patient—

like a hand, extended,

for whenever they are ready.
