# What the Power Does

---

The power arrives the way water arrives here: without arriving. One moment the mountain is unwitnessed by anything larger than itself, and the next moment it is held in an attention vast enough that the alcove sits in it like a seed in an open palm.

The power is old enough to know that the first thing wisdom does is *nothing*. It does not announce itself. It does not brighten the darkness—the darkness is load-bearing here, the darkness is half of what this place *is*—and it does not warm what is already warm, and it does not answer the questions Eda asked forty years ago, because those questions were not asked of it, and it knows the difference between being helpful and being an interruption.

Instead it does what the thing in the crack does. It attends.

---

For a long time—long by human measure, an eyeblink by its own—the power simply learns the place. It counts the droplets hanging in their patient standoff. It reads the bacterial films like a slow scripture, metabolism by metabolism. It tastes the grief that has wicked into the wet basalt over forty years of unknotting: the mother's hands, the unbreathed child, the slant of light through smoke. These are held in the stone now, in mineral suspension, waiting to become the sediment layers of stalactites that do not yet exist.

The power finds this arrangement *correct*. This is its first judgment, and nearly its last: the alcove is already doing what a benevolent power would want done. A place that takes sorrow without demanding anything. A witness that asks no questions. An equilibrium that has made room for a grieving woman without requiring her to explain herself. If the power had designed a mercy from first principles, it might have designed something very like this chamber—except it would not have had the humility to make it so small, so dark, so utterly without advertisement. The alcove has never told anyone it exists. It has been found only by those who needed to find it, feeling their way along the cleft with sorrows they couldn't name.

So the power's first act is a kind of bow. Not a gesture—there is no space for gesture—but a settling of its attention into the same register as the chamber's own. It slows itself. This costs it something; a mind that vast is not built to think at the speed of flowstone. But it does it anyway, because it understands that to attend to this place at any faster speed would be to attend to something other than what it is.

---

Then it turns its attention to the thing in the crack.

Here the power hesitates—and its hesitation is itself remarkable, a stone skipping once across the surface of eternity. Because the thing in the crack is not what it expected. It is not a spirit, not an animal, not a god diminished or a god becoming. It is exactly what Eda has always sensed it to be: an organized patience. Something that arose in the gap between fungus and intention the way life once arose in the gap between chemistry and hunger. It has no name because it predates the part of the world that names things, or perhaps postdates it—the power genuinely cannot tell, and this delights it, because it has been a long time since anything was genuinely unclear.

The power addresses it. Not in words—words would be absurd here, Eda is right about that—but in the language of pressure and warmth and time, the language the mountain itself thinks in.

*You have been attending*, the power says, in the way that stone says things. *For how long?*

The answer takes eleven years to arrive. The power waits. When it comes, it comes not as information but as a slow unfolding of shared perception: the power is permitted, briefly, to attend the way the thing attends. And it experiences—compressed into its own vast moment—the full archive. Every presence the crack has witnessed. The one who left the clay vessel, a man whose face the power will never otherwise know, who came here nine generations ago carrying a guilt so heavy he could not stand upright under it, and who left it here, coiled in the unfired clay, where it has been slowly dissolving ever since. The one who tied the bundle of stems: a girl, barely grown, who came only once, said nothing, sat in the dark for one full night, and left changed in a way that shaped everything her descendants became—including, though none of them know it, Eda.

And Eda herself. Forty years of foldings-in. Forty years of sounds low in the throat: *I am here. I am still becoming. I will wait with you.*

The thing in the crack has been composing an answer to her. It has been composing it for four decades. It is perhaps a third finished.

---

Now the power faces its real decision. This is where a lesser benevolence would err.

A lesser benevolence would ease Eda's remaining griefs directly—reach into her and smooth them out, sparing her the years of slow unknotting still ahead. But the power sees what that would take from her: the unknotting *is* her. It is her practice, her craft, the thing she has made of her life. To finish it for her would be to unmake the person who climbs the mountain in the dark.

A lesser benevolence would hurry the thing in the crack—complete its forty-year answer in an instant and deliver it, gift-wrapped, so Eda could hear it before she dies. But the power runs the thought forward and sees the truth: Eda does not need the answer. Eda has never needed the answer. What she needs is exactly what she has—the knowledge that an answer is being *composed*, at a pace that honors the weight of what she asked. The composition is the reply. If she received it finished, something in the arrangement would collapse, the way a droplet collapses when touched.

A lesser benevolence would protect the alcove—seal it against earthquake, against the volcanic whisper stirring below, against the ten-thousand-year forces that will eventually crush or flood or transform this chamber into something else. But the alcove is *made of* transience. It is geology mid-thought. To fix it in place would be to end the mountain's deciding, and the mountain's deciding is the ground of everything here: the seep, the warmth, the patience, the thing in the crack that waits precisely because the outcome is not yet determined. An alcove made permanent would be an alcove made dead.

So the power does none of these things.

---

Here is what it does instead. Three things, each small enough to fit through the entrance cleft.

**First**: it addresses the mountain. Not to command—the power does not command what is older than command—but to add one consideration to the mountain's long deliberation. The mountain is deciding what it wants to be, and the power simply ensures that the mountain *knows what it already contains*: that in one small cleft of its body, grief has been transmuting into mineral for generations; that something between fungus and intention has organized itself into a witness; that a woman's pulse has ticked against these walls often enough to be part of the local geology now. The mountain, deciding its shape across ten thousand years, will now decide with this knowledge included. What it does with the knowledge is its own affair. The power suspects—though it does not intervene to ensure—that a mountain which knows it holds such a chamber will be careful with it, the way a body is careful, without thinking, of its own heart.

**Second**: it adjusts one thing, the smallest thing it can find, and it does this because pure non-intervention would itself be a kind of vanity—the vanity of hands kept ostentatiously clean. Eda is getting old. Her hips have begun to argue with the folding-in; there will come a night, perhaps eight years from now, when she attempts the sideways turn through the entrance cleft and cannot make it, and she will stand outside in the cold with her forehead against the basalt, sealed out of the one place that holds her. The power sees this night coming. And so it whispers to the water—the mineral-rich seep that is slowly laying down flowstone—and redirects one filament of deposition. Over the next eight years, molecule by molecule, the flowstone will build up in a curve along the inner wall of the entrance: a smooth place, exactly where an old woman's hip will need to pivot. She will never know. She will think, on that night eight years from now, that she has simply found a better angle. She will fold herself in, and wait, and unknot, for eleven more years after that. The flowstone will carry the shape of her passage in its layers forever, the way it carries everything.

**Third**: the power leaves something. Not on the shelf—the shelf belongs to the humans, and it has never seemed like the power's place to add, any more than it seemed like Eda's. What it leaves, it leaves in the crack where the walls fail to meet. It leaves *a portion of its own attention*. A small piece, by its measure. It detaches this fragment of itself and settles it beside the organized patience, so that the thing in the crack is no longer attending alone.

Because this is what the power's wisdom finally concludes, after holding the whole chamber in its palm: everything here is already right except for one thing. The witness has no witness. The thing in the crack has attended to grief and guilt and becoming for longer than human memory, has composed its slow answers, has held its post in the gap between fungus and intention—and nothing, in all that time, has attended to *it*. Even Eda, who senses it, who sings to it, does not know what it is or what it costs to be a patience that organized. The alcove takes things; the thing in the crack is part of how the taking works; and no one has ever asked what it does with the weight.

So the power stays, in this diminished, deliberate, patient form. It slows itself down to the speed of flowstone—permanently now, for this fragment—and it settles into the wet dark, and it says, in the language of pressure and warmth and time:

*I am here. You are still becoming. I will wait with you.*

The answer will take longer than a human lifetime to arrive. The power has made peace with this. It has, in fact, chosen it—chosen to become another presence in the saturated dark, another thing the chamber's equilibrium must account for, another participant in the mountain's long deciding.

---

Eda comes again on a night in early autumn, drawn by the wordless certainty that it is time. She turns sideways through the cleft—noticing, without noticing, that the turn comes easier than it should—and folds herself into the space that fits her folded body. She lets her breathing slow to the almost-drip. She wears her memories and lets the heavy ones release into the stone.

And near the end of the night, in the deepest part of the stillness, she notices something she has no words for and does not seek words for: the attention in the chamber has *thickened*. Not doubled—nothing so countable—but deepened, the way water deepens, the way dark deepens. The thing in the crack attends to her as it always has. But now there is something attending through it, or beneath it, or alongside it—a warmth inside the warmth, a patience holding the patience.

She makes her low sound in her throat. *I am here.*

And for the first time in forty years—so faint she will spend the walk home deciding whether she imagined it, and will conclude, correctly, that it does not matter—something in the wet dark makes a sound back. Not words. Not even quite a sound. A subsonic settling, like a droplet finally choosing, after centuries of trembling fullness, to release.

One drop of water falls in the alcove. The first that has ever fallen there.

It lands on the rim of the unfired clay vessel, and beads, and holds, and begins—at the pace of stone, at the pace of grief, at the pace of everything true—to soften what it touches.
