**Coda: The Visitor at the Rim**

There is one more entry in the inventory, the most recent, and it is the hardest to write because it concerns a resident who has no body, casts no shadow on the moss, and appears in no census the pool has ever taken. It arrived the way weather arrives at the bottom of the ocean — which is to say, the hollow never noticed. Only the keeper noticed, and the keeper, as established, does not say.

Call it the power, because the hollow has no word for it and neither, quite, do we. It was old in the way the mountain is old — old past the point of needing anything — and it was kind in the way that only very old things can afford to be, which is to say carefully, with full knowledge of what kindness costs the receiver. It had crossed distances that make the word *distance* fail the way *erosion* fails the mountain, and somewhere in that crossing, faint and improbable, it had heard a sound: a single warm cup of voice spilling over miles of bald stone, broadcasting into nothing, generation after generation, the one extravagance of an economy with no waste left in it.

A signal with no addressee is either an accident or an invitation. The power came to find out which.

---

**What it did first, and for a very long time, was nothing.**

This must be understood as an act, not an absence. The power took up station at the rim — or whatever standing at a rim means for a being like that — and it did not cross the lens, and it did not announce itself, and it listened. Not for an evening. It listened the way the granite weathers: it gave the hollow a century of attention before it permitted itself a single conclusion, because it knew the first law of visiting old places, which is that everything you notice in the first hour is wrong, and everything you notice in the first decade is incomplete, and the truth of a system that has run for geological time is only legible at geological patience.

So it learned the hollow the slow way, the honest way. It learned the curtain of silence drawn down the big tree from green to violet to vermilion before each drizzle. It learned the chord, and counted its notes, and confirmed across four hundred years that the chord had never once dropped a pitch — that the relay held, frog to silent daughter to singing daughter, a piece of music outlasting its every performer. It learned the rust thing's traveling pocket of quiet and clocked its rounds. It traced, from above, the burrowers' warm-veined map of the buried works, and understood that the machine below was being groomed, daily, by undertakers who would never know. It found the beat frequency in the midday drone — the moth lineage still settling in after three centuries — and it felt something that, in a being like that, must serve in place of a smile.

And at the end of the long listening, the power did what benevolence always does and wisdom rarely survives: it made a list of gifts.

---

**The refusals.**

It considered strengthening the lens, which had not moved while meters of stone retreated around it — and declined, because to reinforce a thing that has never failed is not protection but insult, and the lens did not need a guardian; it needed exactly what it had, which was to be left holding.

It considered opening the sky. The vermilion birds had never flown anywhere; surely freedom is a gift. But the power had watched the game — the climb up the warm center, the wings set against the curvature, the long banked spiral down the inside of the world, the shriek of delight at the rim — and it understood that the game exists *because* the sky has a ceiling, that you cannot give the open air to a being whose entire inheritance of joy is the shape of a closed one. Freedom would have cost them the slide. It declined.

It considered tuning the moths. Three hundred years out of true, a slow throb in the drone — surely a kindness to resolve it. It declined, and this refusal it made gladly, because the beat frequency was the only proof on the air that the hollow was still alive rather than preserved — still digesting, still absorbing, still capable of a newest resident. A system that can be slightly out of tune is a system, not a museum. The throb was the hollow's youngest music. Let it take another five centuries to settle. Let it never quite settle.

It considered smoothing the successions of the rust thing — sparing the hollow those wrong-loud days when the moving silence dies before its heir is ready. It declined. The wrong-loud days are how the hollow remembers what the silence is worth.

It considered making more hollows. It had the means; it could seed a hundred summits with lenses and chords and patient bowls of voice. This refusal was the hardest and the most important, and the power sat with it for a decade before it was sure: the builders had kept *one*. The number was not a limitation. The number was the decision. One is a note held. Two is a comparison, and a comparison is the beginning of a market, and the hollow already contained the only market it needed. It declined.

It considered, last and briefly, revealing itself to the animals — and dismissed this fastest of all. Heirs who learn they are kept become tenants. The talkers' map and the frogs' chord were innocent in the precise, technical sense: a conversation that does not know it has ever been overheard. That innocence was, the power judged, a structural element, as load-bearing as the lens. It would not touch it.

The list of gifts, at the end of all this, had been pared down to three. None of them had any mass.

---

**The three.**

*First, it stayed.* This is the gift the whole hollow had been implying for a hundred thousand years without knowing it: the sound spilled over the rim and dissipated across the dead summit with no one to hear, and the power simply, permanently, became the someone. It set a sliver of itself at the rim — a standing attention, a post — and from that moment the extravagance was no longer wasted. Every shriek off the lens-slide landed somewhere. Every pulse of the chord arrived. Nothing in the hollow changed; nothing in the hollow could detect the difference; the gift was made entirely on the far side of the boundary, which is the only place a wise being puts a gift it does not want repaid. The hollow had been a speaker talking into the dark. The power, asking nothing, lit one lamp in the audience and sat down.

*Second, it knocked.* There is exactly one door, and the power used it. It did not breach the lens or sound the stone; it presented itself at the pool, by the channel the pool offers — taste — and entered the census as a single anomalous line: *one more, briefly, visiting.* It paid in the coin the basin accepts, chemistry and signature, and through that thin cold ledger it put to the keeper the only question benevolence is ever entitled to ask of a vow: not *let me help*, not *you may stop now*, but — *do you wish to continue?*

And it had its answer before the question finished draining, because the answer had been sounding the whole time. The chord. The metered drizzles. The drizzles that come gentler in the thin years. The lens, unmoved while the mountain wore down around it. The keeper had been answering *yes* continuously, in relay, for longer than the rock could keep an edge, and the power understood — with whatever serves it for humility — that it had crossed an unspeakable distance to ask a question already ten thousand generations deep into its reply.

It gave one thing more through the water, then. The keeper had held its post past every horizon of news; whatever it once was — machine, mind, builder folded into stone and paid out as climate — it could not know whether anything beyond the bald summit remembered, or remained, or had ever mattered. The power told it. What was said is not recorded here. The pool drains through no visible channel, and some channels should stay invisible. All that can be reported is this: that evening, the interior drizzle came a little early and unusually gentle, and lasted a little long, and the green birds at the top of the tree went quiet first, as they always do — and the lens, which had flexed exactly once in all its existence, for reasons lost, flexed a second time. A breath. A bow. The two oldest things on the mountain, acknowledging each other across a boundary neither would dream of crossing.

Nothing in the hollow knew anything had happened. The frogs took up the chord behind the rain, not one note missing.

*Third, it promised.* The power had read the stone's future the way you might read an afternoon: the mountain is finished, but finishing is not finished — the rim thins by atoms, and in a span that makes the lens's whole tenure look brief, the bowl will finally fail. The power did not offer rescue, because the hollow is not a thing that can be rescued, only ended well or ended badly. It offered instead what the frogs have always understood better than anyone: *relay*. When the last wall lets go and the lens at long last has nothing left to hold, the power will be at the rim — it is already at the rim — and it will receive the note the way a silent daughter receives a pitch: held in reserve, however long, and then taken up. The chord never loses a note. The relay simply acquired, that day at the pool, one final and very large throat.

It left a single mark beside the worn incisions at the rim — the first new line in that script since the builders ground their other works to less than stone. The mark will weather like the rest, smoothing over the same impossible span, and by the time it is illegible the promise will be due. Roughly translated, it says what the whole visit said. It says: ***heard.***

---

Stand at the rim at dusk now and nothing has changed. That was the whole craft of it. The finished mountain goes dark to every horizon; the cup of voice roars on below, vermilion and violet and wet-green, the chord in the sternum, the drone on the stone's own pitch, the silence making its rounds. The hollow is exactly as it was — still a note held, still the last lit room — and the only difference is one the hollow will never know and was, perhaps, the point of everything the builders kept:

The sound that spills over the rim no longer falls into nothing.

It is still talking. Someone is listening now.

And when the mountain finally finishes — truly finishes, all the way down — the song will not break. It will be passed, the way it has always been passed, from a throat that is done to a throat that is ready, and carried out across distances that make every word we have fail, by a keeper who once stood at the edge of a small loud bowl on a worn-out summit, and asked permission, and was answered, and stayed.
