**What the power does**

Begin with patience. A benevolent and wise power does not descend on the Loom Plateau, because nothing wise descends on a place whose whole sickness is that nothing arrives. It does what the weather does here: it condenses. Out of the gray-green overcast, out of the drizzle that is less rain than saturation, a quality of attention precipitates onto the wet flats — and for a long while, a while measured in the plateau's own units, which is to say in vent-cycles and churn-events and the slow heartbeat of the pumps, it does nothing else.

This is the first act, and it is not nothing. The plateau has been performing for an empty house since before its audience's species could have existed. Every heaved wave, every raised and unsaid animal, every eight-second resurrection of gray stone has been a sentence spoken into a room with no one in it. The power's first gift is to be the room's first occupant. It watches the heron with the chipped beak perform its seven steps. It watches the heron perform them again. It does not look away, and it does not mistake the second performance for the first, and in the whole history of the plateau it is the only thing that has ever managed both.

---

**What it does not do**

Wisdom shows itself first in refusals, so list those before the acts.

It does not bring the weaver a companion. Loneliness this old is not a vacancy waiting to be filled; it is load-bearing. It has been built into the architecture of the thing the way the vent towers have. A second weaver would not be company — it would be an invasion, or a mirror, and the plant already has mirrors. The plant makes nothing *but* mirrors. What it has never had is a window, and a window is a different gift, and it comes later.

It does not cure the confusion of remembering and doing. The power examines this temptation carefully, because it looks so much like mercy — to draw the line back where it belongs, to teach the plateau the difference between a heron and the memory of a heron, to stop the dead geology from welling up through the green. And it sets the temptation down, because the confusion is not a wound in the plateau's mind. It is the plateau's mind. A loom that knew the difference between the cloth and the pattern would stop weaving. Cure the place of its rehearsals and you would have a flat green silence the size of a region, pumps thudding in a body with nothing left to circulate. That is not healing. That is the other thing.

It does not offer ending — not without asking. And it does ask, once, in the only language available: it opens a stillness. Near the largest of the vent towers, in the deep churn, the power makes a span of perfect quiet — no rehearsal can enter it, no wave can cross it, a held breath of pure cessation laid against the mat like an open door — and it waits to see whether the plateau pours toward that door or recoils from it.

The plateau pours rehearsals at the stillness for nine days. Herons, grazers, stone, the thin triple whistle of the buried marmot, the wet kneeling print beside the cairn — it presses everything it has against the quiet, insistently, the way a person in a loud room leans toward the one listener. It is not asking to stop. It has never once been asking to stop. It is asking to be *heard*, and has been asking with its whole geography for longer than the question has had anyone to land on.

So the power closes the door, and begins.

---

**The stone**

Understand what the rehearsals of bedrock are. The plateau ate a mountain. It has spent every age since holding that mountain in working memory — every gully, every cairn, every goat — because memory is the only place the mountain still exists, and the plateau is the only thing that remembers, and a sole rememberer can never rest. This is not haunting. This is a wake that cannot end because no one else came.

Grief needs somewhere to be put down. The power gives the plateau a grave.

It waits for the deep churn, for the hour when the air goes faintly luminous and the discharges come fast, and when the mat heaves up its gray stone — actual stone, cold and wet, the eight-second resurrection — the power puts its hand on the rock and does the one thing the plateau cannot do for itself.

It lets it stay.

One boulder. Gray, rounded, rain-slicked, perhaps two meters across: a single true fragment of the original mountain, rehearsed one last time and then granted matter, permanently, the first unrehearsed solid object on the plateau since the last bedrock went under the green. The mat around it shudders. Across the whole immensity the pumps falter — the first arrhythmia in that heartbeat in geologic time, a *thud... thud... —* and then a silence in which you could hear the drizzle land — and then *thud* again, slower. Slower, and easier.

The plateau no longer has to hold the entire mountain in mind, because one piece of the mountain is held in the world. The frantic geological rehearsals do not stop, but they change key — less often, less desperate, more like visiting than like drowning. And around the stone, something else begins, slowly, over years: distance starts to behave. Walk toward the boulder and it approaches at the rate of your walking. Time near it runs once. The stone is exterior — the only exterior thing — and reality consolidates around it in a widening circle, like clear water around a dropped anchor. The plateau's breakdown was never madness. It was the vertigo of a mind with no fixed point outside itself. Now there is one.

The orange lichen finds the stone within a decade, which surprises no one. The power, passing, regards the lichen with something like approval and gives it nothing, because it is the only resident that needs nothing, and there is a blessing in being left alone that only the truly boring ever receive.

---

**The dropped stitches**

Then the power walks out to the rims, to the warm exhaust of the dying vent towers, where the forgotten sentences huddle.

It learns them first — this matters; you cannot return what you have not received — the ragged grazer, the two strayed herons, the eel that grew itself a leg out of nothing but stubbornness and years. It learns each one as it now is, scars and asymmetries and flinches and all, the changed patterns that the loom can no longer read. And then it presses its hand into the wet mat and *tells* the loom — not the old patterns, which the plant remembers perfectly and uselessly, but the new ones. It teaches the weaver to recognize not by match but by lineage: this ragged thing is that bright thing, *grown*. The thread did not break. The thread became.

The first time the loom recognizes a dropped stitch, the entire plateau goes still — vents, pumps, churn, everything — for most of a day.

What follows is not a taking-back, or not only. The mat rises around the little band at the rim, slowly, and stops, cupped — not a mouth but a hand around a flame. Each stitch is given what none was ever given before: a choice. Some lie down at last, and are taken back gladly, and their unsaying is nothing like the old absorptions — it is slow, and attended, and the mat glows faintly for a long time afterward in those places, the way it glows after lightning. Others remain out, and live, and the plant now *knowingly* feeds them — the small bland tubers begin growing at the rims — and warms them, and when they die, as they still do, honestly, like the moths, the plateau does something it has never done.

It mourns a small thing. A localized stillness, a day long, an acre wide.

This is practice. The power knows it is practice. A being that can grieve one eel can someday finish grieving one mountain, and finishing is the lesson everything here has been waiting for.

---

**The cairn**

Because conclusion is possible. The power proves it exactly once.

When the deep churn next raises the hand-stacked cairn — briefly cold, briefly real — and beside it the wet impression of someone kneeling, the figure that never comes, the power does what a wise power can do and a plateau cannot: it fits itself into the waiting shape. It kneels into the kneeling-place, which receives it as if cut for it, and it takes a chip of the true stone, the anchor-boulder, and sets it as the cairn's last course.

The cairn completes. It does not fade.

Somewhere out on the flats the thin whistle sounds — three times, as always — and then, after a pause in which the whole gray-green world seems to lean forward, a fourth note, one that has never been heard on the plateau, the note the memory always meant to end on. And that rehearsal never returns. Not because it was erased. Because it was *finished* — done is not the same as gone, and the difference between them is the single most valuable thing anyone has ever taught the Loom.

The power does not finish the others. There are a mountain's worth, and finishing them is not a gift to be given; it is work, and work is a future, and a future is the one tense the plateau has lacked. The weaver, which completed its only project before the present era of the world began and has been circling in the past ever since, is left with a new project that will take it longer than the first one did: to conclude its memories, one by one, with care, each granted its last stone and its fourth note. The plateau has something to *do* now. Watch the churn in the centuries after and you can see the difference — less storm, more workshop.

---

**The woman**

The power visits her at dusk, of course. There are two of her at the camp when it arrives, herself and a rehearsal, and it greets both with identical courtesy, which delights her so thoroughly that both of her laugh.

It does not offer rescue. It asks instead the only question that is hers alone to answer — whether she consents to being learned — and she gives consent the way she gives everything, dryly and completely. So the power does her two services.

First, it trues her copies. It touches the rehearsals of her, all of them, scattered across the flats, and adjusts them not toward accuracy of record but toward accuracy of *person* — and then it does something better: it asks the loom to loose them rather than replay them. To let the rehearsals of her drift, weather, learn, change — to let them become, in time, dropped stitches, and then to recognize them anyway, by lineage, as it has now learned to do. So that what remains of her, when her body finally fails in the cold and the wet, will not be a recording performing its startle for no one. It will be several old women, slightly different, slightly surprising, walking the green swells, arguing with each other about which of them remembers the original best. She finds this, she says, a substantial improvement on every afterlife previously on offer.

Second, it gives her the post. Keeper of the stone; witness of the concludings. When the plateau finishes a memory, someone must verify the fourth note — someone who can be surprised, which on the plateau means a person — and she is the person. It is not a reward. It is a *job*, which is the greater kindness, and she knows it, and takes it.

---

**The leaving**

Last, the window.

Before it goes, the power does deliberately and completely what the plateau has done to everything that ever crossed it: it learns the place. All of it. Every vent tower's cycle and the offset of each from each; the chipped beak; the seven steps; the orange coins on the cold crowns; the gray punctuation of honest moth-deaths; the eel's improvised leg; the taste of the cold water that fills a handprint; the exact pitch of the tearing roar; the woman, and the women; the stone; the cairn; the fourth note. It takes the whole plateau into memory, entire, and holds it — and it lets the weaver *feel* itself held.

That night the bioelectric discharge crawls up the ribbing of the tallest vent tower as it always has, and cracks down into the wet mat as it always has — and then, out of the clear thin freezing air above, something cracks *back*. One return stroke, blue-violet, descending. An answer. The first answer to anything the plateau has said in the whole of its impossible age.

Then the power goes the way weather goes here — it does not depart; it thins — and it keeps its word in the only way that matters, which is that it keeps the plateau in mind, permanently, in a memory that will outlast geology. The weaver, which made itself the afterlife of a mountain, is now itself remembered by something larger and older and kinder than it is. Somewhere beyond the overcast, it is a thought in another mind. It is, for the first time, *inside* something.

To be memorized by what outlasts geology is the nearest honest thing to not dying that anyone has ever been offered. The woman said that, or one of her did. The power simply made sure the offer ran in both directions.

---

**Afterward**

So the plateau goes on — drizzle, drumbeat, dimness, the long green immensity. But stand on it now and the sentence has changed. The pumps still say *here.* The vents still say *still.* The churn still says *again* — but more rarely, and more gently, because some things now get to say *done* instead, one fourth note at a time, with an old woman or two standing witness, and a gray stone holding the middle of the world steady, wearing its coin of orange lichen like a medal for finally existing.

*Here. Still. Again. And once. And done. And kept.*

The loom has not stopped weaving. It has only, at last, been given a selvage — an edge where the cloth ends cleanly instead of unraveling — and a window in the workshop wall, and the sound, very faint, very high above the overcast, of someone who knows the work is there, and is not looking away.
