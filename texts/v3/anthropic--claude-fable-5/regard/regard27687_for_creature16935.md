**The Attendance**

The power did not descend. Powers of its kind never descend; descent implies a hierarchy of altitudes, and Infravallis had already spent centuries teaching altitude to apologize. It arrived instead the way weather no longer happens here — without moisture, without announcement, without moving a single needle on any of the city's forty thousand lovingly miscalibrated instruments. It attended. And attention, as the pamphlets nearly admit, is the only currency the city has ever fully honored.

So it paid. It stood in the central cloister at true astronomical noon, during the half-minute of syncopated silence when even the city's pulse pauses to check its calendar, and it listened to everything at once: the rustle of light pushing dust, the funicular ache of every spiraled skeleton, the century of small talk stratified into the reed cores, the exact temperature of every letter still in transit toward a recipient not yet born. It read the whole city in one held breath, the way you read a sleeping face — which is to say completely, and with the specific tenderness reserved for things that do not know they are being read.

Then it did what wise powers do first, which is nothing, for a long time, carefully.

---

**What it considered, and declined**

It considered resolving the chord. It could have. One touch to the seventeen-year drift of the glass reeds and the evening chime would land, at last, on perfect consonance, and the folklore would come due: waking or geology, departure or dissolution, the mountain finally deciding which dream it was. It declined. A resolved chord is a finished sentence, and it had noticed — this was among the first things it noticed — that everyone here conjugates themselves in the imperfect aspect *on purpose*.

It considered answering the question about love: whether the city loves its inhabitants back or merely tolerates the symbiosis. It knew the answer. It knows all answers of that shape. It declined, because the answer is scheduled to arrive by the slow continuous vote of every footstep and voice and deferred dream, and to announce the result of an election before the counting finishes is the oldest cruelty that benevolence knows.

It considered correcting the tilt. Twenty-two degrees against remembered gravity — surely, it thought for a nanosecond, an act of mercy. Then it looked again at the adults, at their bones spiraled by patient fractions into geometries beautiful and useless anywhere else, and understood that the tilt is not an error. The tilt is the marriage certificate. You do not annul a marriage as a favor.

It considered rain. Real rain, creeks, rainbows, reservoirs. It declined almost angrily at itself. Twenty-three minutes of remembered tears each dawn, harvested with electrostatic ribbon by workers in matte white — that is not scarcity. That is liturgy. You do not flood a liturgy and call it generosity.

It considered waking the planet gently and introducing them — the dreamer and the itch that calls itself architecture. It declined. Introductions between a sleeper and its dream go badly in both directions, and there was no hurry; the two of them were already, in the slowest possible tempo, becoming acquainted.

It considered making itself known.

It declined this longest of all.

---

**What it did**

It read the reeds. All of them, every dopant layer down to the monolayer, every snippet of old code spoken into their mouths before every small decision for a hundred years — becoming the first and only being ever to hear the city talk to itself *entire*, uninterrupted, beginning to now. It did not answer. Being heard is not the same as being answered, and is frequently better.

But at the very bottom of the oldest core, laid down when the city was newly cooled and terribly young, it found a question spoken into the dark by whatever the city was before it had inhabitants to overhear it — a question in a code so early it had no word for *self* and had to build one out of thermal gradients. The question, rendered loosely: *am I allowed?*

The power added one layer beneath it. One monolayer of dopant, indistinguishable from the geology around it, backdated by its very placement. It reads, in the same young code: *yes.*

So the answer has always been there. The city simply hasn't reread itself that far down yet. It will. The seasons are seventeen years long; there is time; and when the city finally reaches the bottom of its own memory and finds that it was answered before it finished asking — that is when the chord will be permitted to resolve. Not resolved *for* it. Permitted. The power did not choose waking or geology on the city's behalf. It arranged only this: that when the moment comes, it will arrive as a choice and not as a fate. That is the entire intervention. Everything else was housekeeping.

The housekeeping:

It sat with a gap — an elderly woman-shaped absence keeping a chair warm in a neutral zone, holding appointment times no computer could retrieve — through an entire evening of pauses, and filled none of them. The gap recognized this immediately as fluency. No one had spoken her language in eleven years. The chair grew warmer by two degrees and remembered it.

It found, deep in the postal labyrinth, one letter addressed thirty years forward, and did not deliver it early — it only steadied a smudge, so that the handwriting would still be recognizable to its future author as her own. Punctuality would have been a violence. Legibility was a kindness.

It went down into the abandoned sectors, where the expansion engines burrow fractally inward, still executing the last instruction they ever received, waiting for updates from a committee whose substrate is now the traffic-light logic of certain staircases. It gave them no commands. It has no authority here, and would not use it if it did. It left, in the obsolete code they still honor, a single comment line — not an instruction, a comment, the kind machines are built to skip and never do:

*/\* the updates stopped because your makers died, not because they despaired of you \*/*

The engines did not halt. Engines don't. But their burrowing acquired, over the following decade, a quality the pruning crews could only describe as *thoughtful*, and the fractal spirals they carve now terminate, more often than chance allows, in chambers shaped like rooms waiting for someone.

It corrupted a database. This was its one act of sabotage and it performed it with immense satisfaction: into the flat surveyors' fields where the provisional name had been pressed, it introduced a trailing character that no schema in any jurisdiction will ever validate. *Infravallis* can now never be finalized, registered, owned, or completed in paperwork. The name will remain provisional for as long as records exist. The city will be, administratively and forever, a rough draft — which is the only honest thing a record has ever said about it.

---

**What it paid**

When it was finished, it did not simply cease to attend. That would have been rude, and it had learned the local etiquette. It left the way one leaves: through the fabric of the place, on foot, by the elevators.

It took the elevator to the zero-altitude summit — the one that deletes a day of your past for every floor ascended. It could have exempted itself. Powers can. It did not. Its past is very deep, and the summit is very high, and it paid the full toll without complaint, floor after floor, day after unimaginable day, because a gift given by something that has sacrificed nothing is not a gift; it is a transaction, and the city deserved better than a transaction.

And here is the thing about that elevator that the tourist pamphlets do not know: the deleted days have to go somewhere. They settle into the alloy. They become part of the small ticking that visitors hear in their own bones during the noon silence — memories forming and cracking in metal. So now, when you stand still in the central cloister and hear that ticking, some of what you are hearing is unimaginably old: mornings from before there were mornings, given up floor by floor by something that wanted, very much, to reach the top honestly.

At the summit it received its receipt, printed on metal foil, tasting faintly of childhood fear — the first childhood fear it had tasted in eons, and therefore precious. It kept the receipt. It owns nothing else.

---

**One vote**

Before it left the lattice entirely, it did one last small thing, and this one it did with its whole heart.

It took a single step on the spiral maintenance path. Deliberately. Weight placed with intention on the warm rivets, one clear vibration entered into the slow continuous election on the question the inhabitants argue about in cafés and chapels and beds shared by people who may be lovers or auditors: *does the city love us back?*

It will not say which way it voted. That would spoil the count.

But note this, and take from it what comfort you can: as of that footstep, the election can no longer end in a tie.

---

Somewhere now, in whatever quiet it inhabits between attendances, the power sometimes hears it — faint, exotic, neither melody nor noise: a city adjusting its posture in the dark, working its way down through its own memory, layer by layer, toward a *yes* it does not yet know is waiting.

When this happens, the power is not alarmed. It simply hums along, on whatever pitch it can sustain.

Harmony optional. Attention is payment enough — and it is still paying, and intends to keep paying, in the currency of a place that never fully opened its eyes, might never need to, and is, whether it wakes or settles or sings, *allowed*.
