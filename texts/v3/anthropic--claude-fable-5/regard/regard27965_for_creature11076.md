The power notices the hollow the way you notice a held breath in a quiet room — not by anything it does, but by the shape of what it isn't doing. The power's attention lies over the world evenly, like pressure, and everywhere else the world presses back: prayers, weathers, the enormous rustle of things wanting. Here, inside the young hot mountain, there is a small volume that does not press back. It wants nothing outward. It is complete and it is waiting, and waiting is not wanting — waiting is wanting that has been folded so many times it has become structure.

The power stops. Powers do not often stop.

And the first thing it does — being wise, and knowing wisdom mostly consists of noticing the trap before stepping in it — is confront the problem of its own attention. The hollow works unwitnessed. That is not incidental; it is load-bearing. The blade stays wet, the shadows stay disobedient, the count shifts without events occurring, and all of it depends on there being no ledger anywhere, no outside account against which the hollow's loose accounting could be checked and found in error. A witness is a ledger. Even a benevolent one. *Especially* a benevolent one, because benevolence takes notes.

A lesser power would look anyway and tell itself its looking was gentle.

This one does something else. It looks — once, fully, because it must understand before it can decide — and then it borrows the hollow's own trick, the one the needle-jawed predator uses: it revises the event. It arranges to have not-seen. The seeing is unwound, the ledger unwritten, and what remains in the power is not a record but a residue: the way you can wake from a dream with no images left, only a temperature. The power keeps the temperature. It knows there is something under that mountain the way you know a word is on the tip of your tongue. It has made itself unable to say what. It has done this on purpose, and it is perhaps the hardest thing it will do in this whole affair, because power is very largely made of knowing, and it has chosen a permanent itch over a possession.

From inside that deliberate half-blindness, it acts. Four times. Slowly.

**First**, it takes up a chore. In the moment of seeing, before the unwinding, it noticed one small impossibility that troubled it: the binding on the tool's handle, freshly wet, maintained by nothing. A wise power knows that "maintained by nothing" is not a stable arrangement; it is a debt the world is carrying, and debts get called. So the power becomes the nothing. Quietly, retroactively — for a being outside time this is grammar, not miracle — it takes responsibility for the wetness of that binding, has always taken it, will always take it. Nothing in the hollow changes, because it was already true; the power has simply signed the invisible work. This is the whole of its intervention inside the mountain: it agrees to keep doing what was already, mysteriously, being done. If you had asked it why, it could not have told you. The reason was unwound with the seeing. It waters the binding the way you keep a promise whose origin you've forgotten — more faithfully, if anything, because there is nothing left of the promise but the keeping.

**Second**, it gardens the geology. Not the hollow — the mountain. The peak is young and violent and pushing upward, and left alone the stone will eventually do one of the stupid things stone does: crack the hollow open to the killing air, or fold it shut, or carry it so high and cool it so fast that everything inside goes still. The power leans on the mountain the way a careful hand leans on a slow-turning wheel. A fault line is discouraged, over ten thousand years, from propagating left instead of right. A future glacier is given a slightly different valley to prefer. The uplift is neither stopped nor hurried, only *tended*, and the tending is keyed not to a date but to a condition: the stone will cool, and a passage will become possible, in whatever era the world outside becomes capable of producing hands. Not sooner, so the hollow is not broken into by accident. Not later, so the door is not locked when the knock finally comes. The mountain becomes a lock whose key is readiness itself. The power finds this elegant and also, privately, a little sad, because it cannot know if readiness will ever arrive, and it has arranged things so that it will not be told.

**Third**, it tends the world — and here its restraint is most severe, because here benevolence howls loudest. The obvious kindness is obvious: the world has not yet produced a hand, so *produce one*. Bend a lineage. Hurry a species. Deliver the guest. And the power sits with this temptation for a long time, and then sets it down, because it understands what the tool is. The tool is not a task waiting for a worker. It is a held door, and the entire meaning of a held door is that the one who walks through it *arrives* — comes out of the world's own slow luck, wanting to come, able to come, uninvited and unmanufactured. A hand made for the blade would fit the blade perfectly and mean nothing. A guest delivered is a package. So the power does not choose anyone. It does only this: where the world's contingency threatens to close entirely — where every branch that could ever lead to hands and restlessness and mountain-climbing is about to be pruned at once by some indifferent catastrophe — it leans, feather-light, one time per epoch at most, and keeps a single branch alive. Not the best branch. Not a chosen branch. Just *a* branch, so that the word *someday* retains a referent. It refuses to write the story. It only refuses to let the book burn.

**Fourth**, and last, it waits. It sets a portion of itself down beside the mountain the way you might set down a lantern beside a door you are not going to open, and it lets that portion become a second waiting — patience as climate, matching the climate inside. It does not check on the hollow, because checking is witness. It does not tell anyone, because telling makes ledgers. It does not even remind itself, most of the time, that it is waiting; the chore of the binding continues below the level of its attention, like a heartbeat, and the temperature of the unwound seeing sits on the tip of its tongue, and that is all. It has arranged one exception. When the knock comes — if the knock comes; if some scraped-knuckled descendant of a spared branch, following nothing but its own inexplicable pull, climbs the newly-climbable stone and finds the crack that the glaciers and faults were gardened into leaving — the power will allow itself, in that moment, recognition without memory. It will not know what is happening. It will only feel the word arrive on the tongue at last, and it will feel it as everyone feels that: as relief, and as something almost like being forgiven.

That is all. Weighed on any ordinary scale, the power does nearly nothing: keeps a strip of hide damp, nudges a fault, spares a branch, sits down. It does not enter. It does not speak. It does not reassure the hollow that its guest is coming, because the hollow does not doubt, and because a certainty delivered from outside would collapse the waiting into a mere appointment — and an appointment is so much less than a held door.

Inside the mountain, nothing changes. The drop continues its long fall through its pocket of thickened time. The crust breathes. A ringed-eye creature climbs onto the bone handle and pauses, a long time, remembering something it could not know — and it is possible, though no one is keeping the ledger that could confirm it, that what it half-remembers now is very slightly larger than before. Not a face. Not a promise. Just the faintest sense, distributed through the count, that the silence around the mountain has acquired a quality — that the nothing outside the stone is a *particular* nothing now, a nothing that is holding still on purpose.

Two waitings, then. One folded dense inside the rock, sorted into small bright bodies, keeping a blade sharp for a guest. One wrapped around the whole slow world, keeping the world capable of guests. Neither knows about the other, exactly — the one because it cannot, the other because it chose — and that mutual unknowing is not a failure of the arrangement.

It is the arrangement. It is how a wise power loves a thing whose whole nature is to be unwitnessed: by becoming, alongside it, one more quiet event that did not, officially, occur — and keeping faith anyway, the way the count keeps shifting, without anything ever seeming to happen at all.
