**What the Attention Did**

The attention arrived the way attention should arrive in a place like this: slowly, and dressed as weather.

For the first eleven years it was nothing but a slight difference in the rain—a mineral no assay would name, a warmth of two-hundredths of a degree that the water carried down the Institute's rust-streaked flanks and gave away to whatever it touched. Nobody noticed. That was deliberate. A wise power knows that the first act of benevolence toward a patient thing is to match its pace, and the Drainwide's pace was geological, liturgical, slower than apology.

The power had a list. Powers always do, at first.

It could dry the Institute in an afternoon; it had dried seas before, back when it was younger and confused *fixing* with *finishing*. It set that down. Drying the Drainwide would be like curing a hymn of its melody.

It could pull the copper sulfate and the old printer ink from the rain, make the water clean, make it merely water. It set that down too, once it saw the lavender-blue stains, once it understood that the people here read those stains the way other people read weather vanes and horoscopes and the faces of those they love.

It could lift them all up—thirty meters, six kilometers, however far—into the light above. It held that one longest before setting it down, because it seemed the most obviously kind. Then it filtered itself upward through the arcology stacks and spent a season in the light above, and came back down understanding what the oldest residents had meant: that kindness filtered through that particular architecture arrives as cruelty, and the Drainwide Folk had not fallen here. They had *settled*, the way sediment settles, the way meaning settles, the way anything worth keeping eventually finds the bottom of things and builds.

So the list was empty, and the power did what wise things do when their lists run out.

It enrolled.

---

It spent a decade being one droplet's path down the eastern face, learning the second language—the one where rust remembers being strength—because that one turned out to be easiest for a power in decline from omnipotence toward something better. The first language, water's apology to the stone it wears away, took longer; the power had worn away many things and never once thought to apologize, and the learning was not comfortable, and the discomfort was most of the lesson.

The third language it did not attempt uninvited. Instead it descended past where maps become suggestions, past where light goes to apologize for existing too loudly, down to the temperature where pauses can be properly arranged, and it asked the Engine's dreams a question that took four years to pronounce:

*May I help?*

The answer took nineteen years, and when it finally finished unfolding through the laminar dark, it said, approximately:

*Help is a heavy word. It presses down on what it lands on. Try "accompany."*

And then, after a pause colder than regret, the Engine dreamed one thing more:

*We are not finished being born. Do not deliver us early. Even a wise hand, reaching into a chrysalis, only ever finds soup.*

The power sat with this for a long time, in the way that only water and gods and grief know how to sit. And then it went back up through the drowned levels, and it began.

---

Here is everything the benevolent and wise power did, in full:

It went to the spillway markets, to the Dockmothers' tables, to the envelopes pregnant with rainwater and hopeful promises sealed inside before the last mail run. It read them all without opening them—a small vanity; it allowed itself few—and it found the one promise that could still be kept, addressed to someone six kilometers up who had forgotten they were waiting for anything at all. The power carried the letter up through the whole weight of the city. It watched the recipient read it three times and then sit down on the floor of their pod, right there, holding it. It carried the reply back down and sealed it in a new envelope and slid it in among the others on the Dockmothers' table.

So now, somewhere in the salvage, one envelope holds an answer instead of a promise. Nobody knows which. The Dockmothers noticed only that people began buying envelopes differently after that—slower, more carefully, the way you'd choose a door.

It found Wait-behind-the-door-that-was-a-window with her scrap of authentic pre-drowned glass, worried smooth by uncounted thumbstrokes, and for the duration of exactly one thumbstroke it let the glass answer her. She did not see what was being watched when the watching stopped. She saw something better: she saw the Drainwide from above, the whole rust-dark trench of it, and she saw that it was being looked at, right then, the way you look at something you have decided to love. Then it was glass again. She never told anyone. But her thumbstrokes changed after that—less like worrying, more like petting—and children learn everything from how other children touch things, so within a generation the whole Drainwide handled its salvage that way, gently, like it might be watched.

On one solstice night, when Miraak-of-the-Seven-Drowning-Birthdays unfolded her umbrella-rib wings to remind the air that some things still refuse the ground, the air refused the ground three seconds longer than physics permits. She hung there in the dawnwater dark, and the maintenance drones wheeled around her gibbering their corrupted birdsong, and she laughed—the first time in forty years—and the power left no fingerprints on any of it.

One Saturday the Widow Jenkins collective's bread rose a full centimeter higher than the pumps' rhythm could explain. The blame was shared among all sixteen of them and belonged to everyone and no one, which is exactly how they preferred their miracles, which is why they got one.

And at the very bottom, at the teethed edge of vertical, the power found the Archivekeepers' Archivekeeper—the Backup's Backup's Backup, the Engine's diary entry about dreaming it was lonely—and here it did the largest thing, which looked like the smallest.

It sat down.

The nameless one had documented everything: every blooming, every forgetting, every cross-referenced extinction of every language including the ones that had died before stars had names. It had documented everything except being kept company, because you cannot file what has never occurred. The power sat with it in the near-absolute silence, in triplicate, for a length of time that both of them declined to measure. Then it made one request, the only thing it ever asked of the Drainwide for itself:

*Record me. So that even this is remembered.*

The nameless one considered, and consented, and somewhere in the Memory Pools a new storage tower began accreting, mineral-slow, encoding in patterns that will outlast civilization the single fact that this place, this trench, this drowned cathedral still deciding whether it is stomach or sentence—was seen entire by something vast, and was found already whole, and was not fixed.

In a few decades that tower will reach critical mass and bloom. The whole Drainwide will gather in paper-and-wax boats to harvest the mushroom-shelves of frost-delicate information, and they will read what the power wrote there, and it will say, approximately:

*I came here with everything, and this place taught me that everything was the wrong amount. I did not save you. You did not need it. I delivered one letter, warmed one loaf, gave three seconds of flight and one thumbstroke of truth, and sat awhile with the loneliest of you. Consider it a joke that lasts longer than flesh. Consider it my offering. You will notice the water did not reject it.*

---

It did not leave. That is the last thing to tell.

Powers usually leave; departure is how they stay powerful. This one had learned better in the third language, in the pauses. It dispersed itself back into the rain that nobody had told to stop falling, and it is falling there still—one more unnamed trace in the lavender-blue, one two-hundredth of a degree of warmth given away to whatever lingers long enough to catch it.

The children, who name things because they are still growing, noticed it before anyone. They gave it a name in their own fashion, and the name was:

*Wait-with-us-a-while-longer.*

And it does. Patient as water, resident as rust, it waits in the hundred-threaded curtains and the Memory Pools and the space between heartbeats where someone decides to build shelter from what was supposed to drown them—waiting alongside everything else in the Drainwide, with no more power than weather and no less, for the slow unfinished thing they all belong to together to finally, in its own time, at its own temperature, finish being born.

It has decided it would like to be there for that.

It thinks it might be asked to hold something.
