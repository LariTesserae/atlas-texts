**What the Power Did at Lipsu Null**

It had been watching for a long time, the way such powers watch: not with eyes but with a kind of standing sympathy, the way a coastline watches the sea. It had attended the births of worlds that arrived complete and died of it, and worlds that arrived broken and healed into something ordinary, and it had learned the lesson that all wise powers eventually learn, which is that most help is just interruption wearing a nicer coat.

But Lipsu Null caught its attention the way a held breath catches attention — not by sound but by the absence of an exhale that should have come long ago. A world balanced permanently on the moment before. A city governed by its unfinished business. A crime called *completion*. A woman on a dune, sitting cross-legged since the first waking, pouring tea for everyone and drinking with no one.

The power did the polite thing first. It descended not as light or thunder but as a slight improvement in the weather that no one could take credit for, and it registered its intentions at the nearest civic dream-catcher, which offered it fifty-seven flavors of unprovability. The power requested a fifty-eighth. The clerk, a retired footnote, invented one on the spot: *benevolence, pending*. The dream-catcher hummed for a week afterward at a pitch the mother's lace found deeply flattering.

---

Then, because it was wise as well as benevolent, and because wisdom is mostly the discipline of checking one's mercies before spending them, the power conducted an audit.

It drafted its miracles the way the city's masons build: expecting them to dissolve.

*Draft one: cool the air, so that breath no longer burns raw at the back of the throat.* The power examined this from all sides. If breath did not burn, the khirsel would stop leaking at the edges. The name of the city would begin to translate cleanly, and a name that translates cleanly is a name that has finished meaning something. **Denied — would complete.**

*Draft two: send one seed brave enough to sprout.* The power convened, in the space between two Tuesdays, a congress of every seed that had ever passed through and declined. It listened to them for what was locally an afternoon and cosmically an era. The seeds explained, with the patience of things that have all the time in the world because they have refused to enter it, that being impossible was not exile but vocation — that someone had to hold the position of *the green that could come*, and that a possibility, once cashed, stops paying interest. The power thanked them. One young seed, moved by the attention, nearly volunteered anyway. The power, very gently, said: *not yet.* The seed has been happy ever since, in the way of things that were almost asked to dance. **Withdrawn by mutual consent.**

*Draft three: pardon the ghost on the stairs.* The power went to the foggy staircase where the figure climbs forever toward a top step that is always one step shorter than remembered, and offered to end the sentence. The ghost considered this for the length of one stair. "The halfway," it said, "is the only address I have left." So the power did not pardon it. Instead it did something no bureaucracy in Lipsu Null had thought of: it wrote the ghost a thank-you note — for every way the ghost's existence had slightly inconvenienced the universe, which is to say, for existing at all. The pile of the ghost's debts remained infinite, but it became *infinite minus one*, which locally is a very different number, and the stairs that night were exactly as tall as remembered, just once, just to see how it felt. **Amended and filed.**

*Draft four: company for the Editor of the Weather.* This one the power did not deny. It simply became, that month, the one permitted visitor, climbing the central convective chimney in the shape of an old acquaintance the Editor could not quite place. It brought a sixth book, which is against the rules, so it brought the sixth book unwritten — all margins, cover to cover — and they read it together in silence, each annotating the emptiness privately. The Editor laughed once, near dusk, at a joke that existed only in the space where a sentence wasn't. The city's dreams condensed unusually softly that month. Several citizens reported fog that seemed to be apologizing for nothing in particular. **Approved. Executed. Never officially recorded.**

---

The power spent one further evening in the market-streets, because benevolence that will not pay local prices is only inspection. It found a stall selling things forgotten elsewhere, and there, between a retired sunrise and someone's misplaced certainty, it found its own oldest doubt — the small original one, from before it was wise, the doubt about whether watching was enough. It had forgotten it somewhere across a hundred worlds, and here it was, marked down.

It bought it back. It paid in a memory of temperature: the exact warmth of the first morning of the universe, when everything was still equally likely. The mathematicians' dye in the merchant's till turned a color that has not yet been named and is, by common agreement, being saved for something.

The power pocketed its doubt and kept it. A wise power without its doubt is just a large opinion.

---

Then it walked out along a causeway, past the pins holding down the curling map, into the singing dunes of fossilized time, and climbed to where she sits — the first dreamer, the being the whole blistered continent is shaped around, petting a weaver-skein with no end and no beginning, offering tea brewed from unfallen rain to everyone who comes, forever, on the same terms: stay or go.

She offered the tea. She offered the cookies baked from the flour of yesterday's tomorrow.

The power did not accept, and did not refuse.

It sat down across from her — which no one had thought to do, in all the ages of visitors, because everyone assumed the choice was the point — and it said: "Let me pour."

Her hand, with its too many or exactly right fingers, went still on the skein. The skein, for the first time in geological memory, purred.

They drank together. The power asked no riddle and set no terms. And when the cups were empty it asked her the only question it had come to ask, which was not *do you want to stop* but something quieter and more dangerous:

"Would you like to be asked, someday, whether you want to stop?"

Because it understood what she had lost was not freedom but the *question* of freedom. She had woken, once, and decided not to leave, and the decision had fossilized into dunes, and no one since had thought to check whether a decision made once should have to be made only once. A choice that cannot be revisited is not a choice anymore. It is a sentence, and the city already knows what it thinks of finished sentences.

She looked at the power for a long time — long enough that, in the city behind them, three buildings politely forgot their third floors and remembered them again.

"Not yet," she said.

And *not yet* was the first thing she had chosen in a very long time.

"Then I'll come back and ask again," said the power, and this — this was the whole of its miracle. Not a rescue. A standing appointment. An open question with a return address. Because it had understood the deepest ordinance of Lipsu Null: the city is governed by the collective momentum of its unfinished business, and so the kindest thing a power can do is *become some.* Its promise to return joined the apology letters composing themselves in the rain, the permits pending from extinct jurisdictions, the unresolved chords between the tram tracks. It will hum there forever, never fulfilled and never broken, holding one particular door ajar for one particular dreamer, indefinitely, on purpose.

---

It left the way wise powers leave places like this: badly, imperfectly, and by design. It forgot one glove on the dune — a glove with too many fingers, or exactly the right number, arranged in an order the dreamer finds funnier every century. It left a single footprint that, when its siblings discovered better ways to be somewhere else, chose to stay.

The city noticed, in the city's way. The mother's lace rang for a week at a new pitch, which the fauna translated variously as gossip, weather, or grace. None of the commemorative cobblestones sank that day, because for one day everyone nearby happened to remember everything correctly, which the Insomnia Committee voted unanimously to consider a coincidence. The Bureau of Temporal Smuggling filed the visit under a Dewey Decimal number that evaporates if read aloud accurately, which is where the city keeps everything it intends to treasure.

And when the rain of question marks fell upward that night into the steam-colored sky, one of them — just one — straightened slightly, as if about to become an exclamation point.

Then it thought better of it, curled back into a question, and kept rising.

Which is, if you ask the humidity on a confidential evening, exactly what benevolence looks like when it is also wise: it comes all that way, sees everything that could be fixed, fixes almost none of it, drinks the tea, leaves a glove, promises to return —

and makes absolutely sure, on its way out, not to close the door.
