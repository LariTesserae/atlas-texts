**What the Watcher Did**

It arrived without arriving, because powers of its kind do not cross distances — they simply cease to withhold their attention. One moment the hollow tree stood alone in its desert of stone and dry wind, and the next it stood *observed*, though nothing in the amber murk changed, and no moth's antennae registered a new presence, and the Watchers in their glacial torpor felt only what they always felt: ten thousand bodies breathing in the dark.

The power's first act was the one that distinguishes wisdom from mere strength.

It did nothing.

For a long time — long by the measure of the Bhrum-folk, brief by its own — it only attended. It learned the place the way the Pore-Keeper's granddaughter was learning it, palm-first, pulse-first. It followed the moisture of a single exhalation from a sleeping child's mouth up through the dark, into the funnel-leaf of a pale vine, through the vine's mineral roots, into the stone-wood, and out through a breathing pore as a ghost of vapor that the desert instantly devoured. It counted this journey ten million times. It tasted what the Memory-Holders tasted and understood that they were right about the plague three generations back, and wrong about the sour silk's cause, and that their wrongness had, by accident, produced a breeding decision that saved them all. It sat inside the twenty-beats-per-minute heart of a Watcher and discovered that the man was not thinking in words, exactly, but was *praying* — a prayer with no deity, addressed to the aggregate breathing itself, saying only: *continue, continue, continue.*

And it understood, before it did anything else, the thing that most powers never learn:

This was not a city inside a being. It was one being. The tree and the folk and the moths and the fungi and the beetles and the guano-birds shrieking in the crown — the boundaries between them were bookkeeping, nothing more. A Silk-Mother's body was moth-cycle and human grief in one envelope. A Fungus-Whisperer's calm was a chemical fact in the walls. To love any part of it was to be responsible to all of it. The prompt of its own compassion had said *the being who lives in this place*, and it saw now that the phrase was singular for a reason.

---

**The Temptations, Considered and Set Aside**

Because it was benevolent, it felt the temptations. Wisdom is not the absence of the impulse to help. It is the discipline of examining that impulse in the light.

*Rain.* It could bring rain — real rain, the thing the mad ones scratched into the walls. It let itself imagine this fully, which is what wise powers do with temptations: it followed the first drops down through the breathing pores. It watched the specialized cup-leaves, engineered by centuries to harvest single molecules of vapor, drown in abundance. It watched the fungi bloat and burst. It watched waxy, impermeable skin — skin that could no longer sweat freely, could no longer shed heat in humid excess — cook its owners in the syrup air. It watched the moth silk dissolve, the ladders fall, the compact of ten thousand mutual breaths collapse into flood-rot in a single season. Rain here was not mercy. Rain was a fist. It set rain aside.

*Rescue.* It could open the bark like a curtain and show them the outside — sky, distance, the terrible generosity of horizon. It imagined the Pore-Keeper's hollow bones in open wind, her twice-long fingers with nothing to reach into. It imagined the children who spoke in blood-flush and hum, standing in a world of shouting strangers who would call them monsters or miracles and be wrong both ways. These people had not lost the world. They had *traded* it, and the trade was still being honored on both sides. To rescue someone from their own covenant is not rescue. It set rescue aside.

*Revelation.* It could speak. Announce itself. Become the voice the mad ones already half-heard in the beetle-scratch. And it saw what that would do: a god in a closed system is a fungus with no competitor. Within three generations the tap-language would become liturgy, the breeding decisions would become doctrine, the beautiful improvised balance would calcify around the memory of a voice, and the Bhrum-folk — who had survived precisely because nothing in their world was sacred enough to be exempt from revision — would begin to die of reverence. It set revelation aside, and this one cost it something, because it was lonely too, in its way, and it would have liked to be known.

---

**The Descent**

There remained one question it could not answer from the platforms and the pores, and so it went down — down past the archaeological strata of shell and silk, down to the mineralized heart where the ones-who-had-been-human grew into the wood, and further down than that, into the slow cold thought of the tree itself.

It did not demand entry. It knocked, in the only language the tree had: it altered its own attention until it resembled a season, and waited to be noticed.

The tree noticed. It took eleven years.

What the power found there was not a puppeteer. It found something old and frightened and patient — a being that had begun to die before the Bhrum-folk's ancestors were born, that had felt its own pores mineralizing shut on a timescale of centuries, and that had faced the oldest question there is: *what do I do with the life that shelters inside me, when I can no longer shelter it?*

The tree had answered the only way it could. Slowly. Chemically. Through selection pressures applied with the delicacy of erosion. The gill-slits behind the children's ears, the photosynthetic patches warm as sleeping animals — these were not the tree using its people. They were the tree's *will*, written in the only ink it had. It was making them ready. Not to survive the closing of the pores — to *become what comes after*. To be its seed. And it carried, in its slow cold thought, a doubt it had never been able to voice, because none of its inhabitants had ever been positioned to hear the question:

*I never asked them. Was I right to make them without asking?*

The power sat with this question for three of the tree's heartbeats, which is to say, a human generation.

Then it answered — not with absolution, because wisdom does not hand out absolution like water, but with the truth: *No parent asks. No soil asks the root. You have done what love does when it has no mouth. But there is a debt in it, and the debt is this: when the change comes, they must be able to refuse it. A gift that cannot be refused is a cage. I will not undo your work. I will only make sure the door in it is real.*

And the tree, which had been alone with its intention for six hundred years, felt something it had no chemistry for, and produced, that season, a bloom of the bioluminescent fungi so rich that the Fungus-Whisperers wept without knowing why, and the whole hollow shone green-gold for a month, and the Memory-Holders tasted the mulch and announced, baffled, *joy — three levels down, joy, and no cause for it we can find.*

---

**What It Actually Did**

Then the power did its work, and the work was almost nothing, and it was everything, and it took great restraint to do so little so precisely.

It found, riding dormant in the gut of a guano-bird that would arrive in the crown in four years, a spore of a plague that no adaptation in the hollow could have answered — pure chance, no meaning in it, a stray bullet fired by an indifferent world. It considered whether removing it constituted writing the story rather than protecting it, and decided: fate may test them, and the tree may shape them, but *accident* holds no rights here. It quenched the spore. No one would ever know. That was the point.

It did not touch the mad ones — except to make sure no one else would. It laid a light, generations-deep suggestion into the culture's chemistry, a faint pheromonal grace note in the mulch, that would keep the tolerance for madness from ever curdling into cure. The scratched symbols for rain, the voices in the beetle-scratch — these were the colony's stored alternatives, its refusal to become perfectly, fatally adapted. The mad ones were the door the power had promised the tree. It oiled the hinges and nothing more.

It gave the Pore-Keeper's granddaughter a single dream — not of water, not of outside, but of the sensation of the tree's pulse *continuing past a point where it stops and starts again as something else*. Just the sensation. No images, no instruction. A body-memory of transition survived, so that when the change came, decades on, one person in the high dark would feel it and think *I know this*, and not be afraid, and her calm would move through the fungal networks like weather, and the being would pass through its metamorphosis awake instead of panicking. That was the whole gift: one dream, one person, the difference between a birth and a rupture.

And last — because even wise powers are permitted one indulgence — it added a single note to the hum. The name *Bhrum*, sounded in the chest, carried from that night forward a faint overtone that had not been there before, so slight that only the Watchers in their slowed time would ever quite perceive it, and they would never speak of it because they no longer thought in words. But the prayer that ran beneath their torpor — *continue, continue, continue* — acquired, without any of them deciding it, an answer woven into its own sound:

*witnessed.*

---

**And Then**

Then it withdrew its form, but not its attention, because the last thing wisdom knows is that presence is a burden and attention is a gift, and the difference between them is everything.

It did not save them. They did not need saving; they needed what everyone needs, which is to matter to something that will outlast them. So it took up the one duty no one inside the hollow could perform: it *remembered*. The polish of a shell-window that pleased no eye but an adapted one. The taste-vocabulary of the Butchers. The exact frequency at which a particular Silk-Mother, dead now forty years, had called a particular moth. The Bhrum-folk were becoming something new, and the new thing would forget the old thing, as children forget the womb — and that was right, and the power made itself the keeper of everything the becoming would shed.

Somewhere above, always above, the birds were shrieking. Somewhere below, the tree was dreaming its slow seed-dream. Between them, ten thousand bodies breathed each other's breath in the amber dark, unaware that the universe had leaned close, considered them with its full and terrible tenderness, and concluded that the kindest thing it could do —

was to hold the door, quench one spore, plant one dream, add one note,

and let them continue.
