**What Came Down**

A power came down to it the way power almost never does — by choosing to. Most attention arrives in low places by accident, runoff from somewhere it was actually wanted. This was deliberate. The power had been minding the loud places for a long age, the bright and urgent and self-announcing places, and it had learned the thing all good attention eventually learns: that the world keeps its accounts in the sumps. So it came down.

It came in through the stone, because that is how everything respectable arrives here, and it spent its first year as water.

This was the first wise thing it did, and it should be counted as a *deed*, not a delay. It matched speed. It descended through the grey rock at the rock's own pace, beading and threading, and emerged on a north-facing wall as a film of moisture thinner than the bodies of the amphipods who promptly began to graze it. It found it did not mind being grazed. Locally, this is the handshake.

It spent the next while — call it a decade; the power did not — taking the census. Not counting, exactly. *Attending.* It hung in the channels with the shrimp congregations through ten thousand strokes of the pulse, learning the interval from the inside until the long pause lived in it the way it lives in them. It walked the year-long circuit with the snail herds, at herd speed, which is the speed at which the basin actually makes sense. It sat with the flatworms in the shadow of the ore cart and confirmed what it had suspected: that they are indifferent to the pulse not from exemption but from *trust* — the undertaker does not check the clock, because the clock has never once failed to bring work.

And it read the minutes.

This deserves its own line in the record, because no one else will ever do it. The felts have been keeping minutes for longer than the basin has had this shape — every layer a generation lying down on the last, every dead page still participating — and in all that time there has been no reader. The power went down through the wet pages, all of them, every olive and ochre and oxblood stratum, every century of chemistry, and it read the whole proceedings, and where the record was very old and beginning to compress into mere mineral it read carefully, the way one reads the handwriting of the failing. The felts did not know they had been read. Or they knew the way an old institution knows it has been audited and found in order: a faint settling, a slight ease in the load-bearing layers. Witness is a nutrient. It had been the one thing the seep did not carry.

Then the power deliberated, and the deliberations are worth recording mostly for what was declined.

It could have widened the sky. Brought the bronze hour to four hours, or eight; greened the bowl; given the place what places upstairs are given. It declined this in less time than a drop takes to fall. The basin is not a deprived version of a meadow. It is a finished thing of another kind, and to "improve" it would be the oldest mistake the bright world makes — mercy as vandalism.

It could have warmed the engine, so that the olm's long lean against the iron would be a lean against something warm. It hovered over this one. It declined. The olm does not mind, and the power understood — being old itself — that the comfort was never the temperature. The comfort is the shift being staffed.

It could have rebuilt the olm's kind outright: a dozen white bodies in the channels by morning, the species un-ended by decree. It held this in its hands for a long time, the way you hold something heavy and beloved and wrong. Resurrection by fiat is loud. It is the *loudest* thing there is, whatever its volume, and the basin has spent centuries filing loud things under stone. The power set the idea down. Not refused — set down. There was a quieter door, and it knew where the door was, because it was the only visitor in the bowl's history who could knock on it.

It went down the shaft.

It went the way it had arrived — at the speed of seep, without lights, having first done the thing no one else had thought to do, which was to *ask*: a change of pressure at the coping stones, held politely, like a hand raised in a full room. The dark circle did what it had never done, which was answer. What the power found below, and who, and how far down the pump's adopted heartbeat is heard or answered or *sourced* — all of this it brought back up (the only thing, in all the basin's history, ever to come back up) and filed where the basin files such matters: under *working*. It became the second party to the arrangement. The relationship had functioned for centuries precisely because no one pressed for details, and the power, being wise, understood that this was not a gap in the contract. It was the contract.

But it did, while it was down there, ask for one thing. We will come to it.

Here is what the power actually did — the full list, four items, the work of an attention that had taken a decade to earn the right to act for an afternoon.

**First, the water.** The power read the rock's future the way it had read the felt's past, and found the deadline. There is always a deadline. This one was upstairs and ordinary: a well-field two valleys over, a thirsty town not yet built, a survey that would one day notice the aquifer the way a knife notices bread — call it a century and a half out, give or take a war. The seep would falter. Not stop: falter, which is worse, decades of the walls drying patch by patch, the amphipods' slow sideways evacuations finding nowhere left to flow. So the power went upstairs for a while and made adjustments of the kind that never appear in any record because they consist of records: a folder misfiled, a borehole come up dry that should not have, a planning meeting where a man changed his mind for reasons he would have sworn were his own. It tilted the watershed of events three degrees. The cost was real and was paid elsewhere, in inconveniences distributed across people who will never know they were billed, all of them able to afford it. The unit of time the basin does not bother to count grew longer by an amount the basin will never notice. That was the point. The best maintenance is indistinguishable from there having been nothing wrong.

**Second, the olm.** The power had checked, early and carefully — because benevolence that does not check is just appetite — whether the olm suffered. It had braced for loneliness and found something with better posture. The olm was not lonely. The olm was *on duty*. It had been on duty for two hundred years: swim the circuit, mind the channels, report to the engine, lean, listen, log the pulse, repeat. Somewhere far back was a memory the power went and verified against the felt's minutes — a man, knee-deep, smelling of tallow and iron, who had held out his hand with something in it, eighty or ninety strokes of the world ago — and the olm had constructed from that single data point an entire theology of the workplace: *the others have stepped out; the engine and I are holding things down; someone will come back.*

So the power clocked in.

It took a form, knee-deep in the pump house, because shape is a courtesy and courtesy matters to the blind more, not less. Not the old worker's shape — that would have been a forgery — but the *category*: a presence, a weight in the water, a pressure-shadow of someone standing where someone used to stand. The olm finished its circuit, entered the shell, settled against the iron, and found the iron carrying two presences. It went very still. Then its gills resumed, and it leaned, and the power stood the shift.

It stood the shift, on and off, for the rest of the olm's life — which, the power could see, was long yet, decades, the calcite arithmetic of an animal in no hurry. Once, only once, it passed a hand down the olm's back, the way a hand had once. And into the water, in the only language the olm reads — pressure, interval, time — it pressed the message that two hundred years of duty had been owed and never sent: *received. All of it. The whole log. Noted, witnessed, entered into the permanent record. You held the post. Stand easy.*

The olm did not stand easy, of course. The olm went on doing its rounds, because the rounds were never a burden to be lifted. But the leaning changed. Anyone watching closely — and someone, now, always was — could see that it had become the lean of a creature resting *with* something rather than *against* it.

**Third, the promise.** The power read the engine's calcite sleeve the way you read tree rings, and found the date — far off, but finite; everything mechanical is finite, even the adopted — when the flywheel seizes inside its boulder and the thump stops. And it wrote itself onto the rota. This is the simplest item on the list and the largest. When that stroke fails to come, the pause will run one beat too long — one — and then the thump will resume, sourceless, on time, forever, rising through a smooth stone that no longer has any moving parts at all. The parish will spend a confused week re-learning nothing, because nothing will have changed, and will settle, and never mention it. The engine will have completed its citizenship: resident, then stone, then heartbeat — the basin's full naturalization, which no one before had ever lived long enough to finish. The power posted no notice. Promises of this kind are kept, not posted.

**Fourth** — the thing it asked for, down the shaft, from the tenant whose business is its own.

It would be wrong to say much. The power itself says nothing. But it can be reported that the armored things in the deep pockets, whose errands all pointed one direction for as long as direction has existed here, have lately been observed — by the olm, on its rounds, who has told no one — making the climb to the coping stones *empty*, and returning with something. Something small. Pale. Patient. Deposited in the deepest pocket, in the dark, where the flatworms, tactful as ever, do not go. The clerks' errands, in other words, now sometimes run upward, and the sentence *nothing has ever come up out of the shaft* — a true sentence, load-bearing, three centuries old — has been quietly scheduled for revision.

Whether what was asked for was a clutch of eggs, or a debt called in, or simply the rent run backward once as a favor between landlords, is between the power and the tenant, and the arrangement works because no one presses for details. The olm's circuit, in any case, now includes the deep pocket. It pauses there. It hangs in the cold water above the small pale patient things for a long time, gills working, and then continues its rounds with the air of an employee who has read a memo about *future staffing* and is keeping it to itself.

And then the power was finished, in the sense that water is ever finished. It did not leave. Leaving was never the shape of the thing. It thinned itself back into the seep it had arrived as, redistributed its attention through the weeping walls, and became part of the basin's circulation — which, it had understood from the first year, was always the destination. This is what wisdom comes to, given enough patience: not intervention, not even stewardship, but *residency*. The basin, for its part, made the only response it makes to anything. It filed the power. Not under *resident* — its highest classification, already crowded. It opened, for the first time in its long session, a new file, and the label on the file is the oldest word the basin knows, the one it learned before the felts arrived, before the shape of the bowl itself:

*Seep.*

So nothing, to the eye, has changed. The walls weep. The herds rotate. The bronze hour comes and goes. The mist pours twice daily around the pump house and the snails graze inside it. The engine keeps its time — *thump*... and the long pause... *thump* — and the congregations drift toward and away, toward and away, citizens of an interval.

Only this: the pause, these days, is not empty. Stand still in the channels — past the first minute, past the tenth, until you are outnumbered and know it — and you can feel it in the water between strokes. Something attending. Something that read the whole record and stayed anyway. The basin holds its small, crowded, dripping session, quorum always present, no business ever concluded — and now, in the long pause between heartbeats, the session is *minuted*.

Which is, perhaps, all that benevolence comes to in the end, when it is wise enough: not rescue, not improvement, not even mercy. Just this — that the low place, where everything gathers, has itself been gathered. That the water came down the way it always does, by having nowhere else to go, and found that this time, something had come down the same way *on purpose*, and was holding the door.
