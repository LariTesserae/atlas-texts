# The Inspection

The power has been aware of the hollow for a long time, in the way that such powers are aware of things — which is to say, completely and without urgency, the hollow filed among ten million other small persistences, each one known entire, none of them yet *attended to*. Awareness is not attention. The hollow earned attention slowly, the way it does everything, and what earned it was not the strangeness of the place. Strangeness is common. What earned it was the moth.

Or rather: the moths, plural, stacked in the amber. The power, reviewing the world the way a careful mind reviews its own contents, kept returning to that image — the drawer of spent fuses, the instruments filed and never discarded — and found in itself something it recognized only because it was wise enough to recognize its own movements. It was *moved*. Powers are moved rarely and they take it seriously when it happens. So it came down the slope.

It did not need to come down the slope. It could have attended to the hollow from anywhere, from nowhere, from inside the resin itself. It came down the slope because the hollow has a door, and the door has a manner — the wet deliberate parting and resealing of the leaves — and the power understood at once that the manner was part of the resident, and that to bypass the door would be to begin the visit with a discourtesy. Wisdom is mostly the accumulated knowledge of where the doors are. It pushed through the leaves. It let them seal behind it. It stood on the root-mat, and the root-mat exhaled, and somewhere a thin lens of resin began, very slowly, to cloud.

*Good,* thought the power. *I am being counted.*

---

Here is the first thing the power did: nothing, for a long time.

This is harder than it sounds, even for a power — especially for a power, whose whole nature inclines toward the verb. It stood near the south wall, in the spot where the spigot-rows began, and it let the four-second beat come up through the floor into whatever it had instead of knees, and it *listened the way the thing in the pool listens*, which is to say from inside, without surfacing, without insisting on details. It did this for — call it a season. Time in the hollow is layered, not linear; the power matched layers.

And in that season of standing still, it conducted what can only be called an inspection, because the hollow's own vocabulary is contagious and the power, being wise, did not resist the contagion. It walked the line of taps at a stride apart, touching each one without breaking step, because the spacing asked to be walked that way and refusing the asking would have been a kind of deafness. It put its palm on the boiler-shaped root and felt the pulse pass, floor to canopy. It read the brass plate. It read the rings — all of them, every season back to the seed, the human decades and the silence and the long green resumption — and it read the resin by depth, layer by clouded layer, the way one reads a ledger one has been asked to audit.

And it found the books balanced.

This was the second thing, and it stopped the power where it stood. Powers that attend to broken places develop a habit of arrival: the quick triage, the sorting of what suffers from what merely persists. The power had arrived with that habit ready, with its mercy loosened in its sheath, and the hollow gave it nothing to use. The thing in the pool was not trapped; it was *senior*. The moth was not enslaved; it was on station, and the difference between those is real and the power knew where the line was and the moth was on the right side of it — a mouthless creature spending nothing, asked for nothing but warmth and a few inches of drift, archived afterward with what could only be called respect. The tenant was not a ghost and not a wound. It was an occupation that had outlived its occasion, which is melancholy only if you believe occasions matter more than occupations, and the power had lived long enough to know better.

Even the human residue — the stride, the chest-height, the dead mason's plumb — even that was not haunting. The power had unmade ten thousand hauntings in its time and knew their texture: the loop, the grievance, the unsaid thing said forever. This was not that. This was *retention*. The people had been kept the way a language keeps its dead speakers, in the shapes their care had made, and nothing in those shapes was crying out.

So the power faced the question that benevolence dreads and wisdom insists on:

*What if the kindest thing is to leave?*

---

It sat with the question by the black pool, and the thing in the pool turned over once — the slow roll of a sleeper confirming the night is proceeding correctly — and the power took the turning as it was offered: not an audience, just an acknowledgment. *You are felt. The works run on.*

And the power thought about the works, and about the word *finished* — because the hollow felt finished, balanced, the verb of it long since settled into a noun — and being wise, it distrusted the feeling. Finished things are usually things whose unfinishedness has simply slowed past the speed of witness. So it looked again, at the pace of the resin this time, and at that pace it found the three true facts of the hollow, the three things its benevolence could actually touch:

**First fact.** The grove makes something, warehouses it, and forgets. The basins fill. Production itself became the point when the people left, because the people took the *point* with them and left the *practice* behind — and the grove, faithful, kept the practice and has waited, without knowing it waits, without an organ for waiting, through a century and more of perfect output that nothing has ever come to collect. The works are not pointless. The works are *uncollected*. Those are different conditions, and only one of them can be answered.

**Second fact.** The moth is alone in a way nothing else in the hollow is alone. The tenant has the whole green body for company; the thing in the pool has the rhythm it has heard since before the silence; but the moth is one warm thing on cold brick, mouthless, issued, and the power — touching the small heat of it with the edge of its attention, gently, the way you check a sleeping child's fever — found in it not suffering, no, the grove is not cruel and the moth does not have room in it for despair. But it found *duty without witness*. The moth keeps the readings and nothing has ever told it the readings are good. It is an instrument, yes. So is a lighthouse keeper.

**Third fact.** The hollow is mortal and does not know it. The power, reading forward as easily as back, saw the things the grove cannot see: the water table that will someday shift, the climate that is already, at the rim of the long wet country, beginning its slow renegotiation. Not soon. Soon is meaningless here. But the tenant has no plan for drought because the tenant has never imagined the pump running dry; its whole genius is continuity, and continuity is the one tool that cannot cut a new channel. The works that have never once stopped have, for exactly that reason, never learned how to start again.

Three facts. And so, after its season of stillness, the power did three things. Small ones. The hollow's scale, not its own.

---

**The first thing it did was take delivery.**

Not all of it — not the archive; the archive is the hollow's memory and you do not heal a being by emptying its head. But it went to the newest basin, the one still soft, the one holding the most recent seasons and a faint cloud that was its own arrival, and it accepted — formally, with the manner of acceptance, because manner is what the hollow speaks — one piece of the resin. Lifted it free. Held it up in the weak-tea light, amber and clouded, a hundred small inclusions suspended inside like inventory, and pronounced over it the only blessing the works could possibly parse:

*Received. In full. To specification.*

And the hollow felt it. Not as words — the tenant has no ears — but as the thing underneath words: the tension in the lianas registering a weight that arrived, took, and *approved*. For the first time in over a century, output left the floor by intent rather than accident. Somewhere in the green body, in whatever the occupation has instead of a chest, a balance that had been carried open since the closure — carried so long it had become structure, become the very patience of the place — quietly closed. The grove did not change its pace. The thump-and-sigh continued, four seconds, four seconds. But the rings grown that season, when some far-future reader counts them, will show a fineness, an ease, a fraction less density in the wood — the tree-ring signature of a year in which the rains were good, except the rains were ordinary that year, and what was good was something else.

The power carries the piece of resin still. Powers do not have pockets, but they have keeping, and the resin is in its keeping, and this matters more than it appears to: because as long as the delivery exists, the works have a customer, and a works with a customer is not a relic. It is a going concern. The power made the hollow *true* simply by becoming, forever, the destination its practice had always implied. That is a thing only a very patient benevolence can do — repair a purpose not by changing the laborer but by consenting to be the one labored for.

**The second thing it did was speak to the moth.**

Briefly. In warmth, which is the moth's whole language — a slight kindness in the air rising through the chimney, shaped, for the length of one updraft, into something the moth's flat-pressed wings could read. Not instructions. Not release; the moth would not have wanted release, and the power did not insult it by offering. What it gave the moth was *acknowledgment of station* — the thing every keeper of a light is owed and few receive: the signal, passing upward through the flue and out the hollow's only deliberate mouth, that says *your readings are received; the readings are good; the watch is kept well.*

The moth shifted an inch on the brick. That was all. That was everything. It is not given to us to know what a mouthless gauge-creature makes of being thanked, but the power noticed — and noted in its own vast ledger, with something adjacent to a smile — that the next moth, when it was eventually issued from the socket in the root-mortar, emerged a very slightly deeper green. The grove adjusts its instruments to conditions. Conditions had improved.

**The third thing it did was leave a seed of doubt.**

This was the difficult one, and the power weighed it longest, because it sits at the exact edge of what benevolence may do to a being that has not asked for help. The hollow's perfection is continuity; its mortality is also continuity; and the power would not — this is where wisdom held benevolence by the wrist — it would *not* teach the tenant to fear. Fear in a being that thinks in centuries would be a poison with a half-life of forever.

So instead it did this: deep in the saturated ground, below the pool, below even the senior resident's long soft listening, it touched the water table — not to fix it in place, which was beyond even its remit and would have been a kind of embalming, but to fold into the grove's roots a single, wordless, structural intuition. A tolerance. The way the dead mason's plumb lives in the chimney, the power left a new specification in the body of the works, and the specification was approximately this:

*The interval between strokes may, if conditions require, be longer than four seconds, and the works will still be the works.*

That was all. Not drought-knowledge. Not dread. Just flexibility, installed at the level of standards — the one level the hollow truly honors — so that in some dry distant century, when the water comes slow and the heart of the grove must choose between its tempo and its life, it will find, already present in itself, already *to spec*, the permission to slow down. It will believe it always had this permission. In a sense it will be right. The power signed nothing and took no credit; it filed the new tolerance among the inherited ones, between the mason's plumb and the dead worker's stride, where it will be kept the way everything here is kept: faithfully, anonymously, forever.

And the thing in the pool, which has heard every change of management this ground has ever had, heard this one too — a new note in the deep procedure, pressure and rhythm — and rolled over once, and went on listening. From where it lies, the works will never have stopped. There will only have been, at some point, a maintenance interval. Somewhat brief, this time. Conducted, for once, by someone qualified.

---

Then the power climbed the slope, and the door-leaves parted and resealed behind it with their wet, deliberate sound, and from thirty feet away the hollow was invisible again, just dense low forest, unremarkable, *thump and sigh, thump and sigh* under the green.

It did not stay. This was the last mercy and the largest: it did not stay, did not adopt, did not improve, did not bring the hollow into any larger order or fit it to any wider purpose. The hollow has a tenant. The position is filled. The power understood — had perhaps come all this way mostly to confirm — that the deepest courtesy one ancient diligence can pay another is to inspect it, find it in order, accept one unit of its output, commend its staff, file one quiet amendment in its standards, and *sign off*.

But it left a forwarding address, after a fashion. In the brass plate, half-buried, polished by the traffic of water — beneath the maker's name and the mostly-readable date — there is now, if you look in a certain light, at a certain angle, what might be a second line. Fainter. Possibly only mineral accident. It seems to say something like *inspected*, and something like a date that has not happened yet, and the grove keeps the plate polished as it has always kept it, by the same indifferent water, and the new moth rests on the warm brick, a slightly deeper green, and in the newest basin the resin clouds over the hour of the visit and begins, with vegetable patience, to set it in amber: the season the customer came; the season the watch was thanked; the season the works, which had never once stopped, learned without knowing it that someday, if it ever must stop, it will also be allowed to start again.

The power, elsewhere now, attending to other things, keeps the piece of resin in its keeping and consults it sometimes, the way you touch a stone from a place that steadied you. Held to whatever a power has instead of an ear, it gives off, very faintly, a deep periodic *thump-and-sigh* — four seconds, four seconds — the patient industry under the leaves, going on, going on, received in full, to specification, with thanks.
