**The Attendance**

It came down to the vault the way the air does, because that is the only way anything comes: through the long tilted fissures, out of weather, into the brown dimness and the roar. It needed no light and left no footprints — not at first; we will come to the footprint — and it stood a while on a far plaza, where the sound softens to pressure, and did what wise things do before anything else.

It counted.

The millions above, the nations of the floor, the descending embassy of roots, the geckos and the racers and the dust-backed officials in their doorways, the hermits who had declined to leave, and the pale shapes buried to the waist at the still center. It counted them all, and then it counted their needs, and this took much longer, because the needs were so few that each one had to be searched for like a dropped coin in the dark.

Lesser benevolence would have begun fixing by now. That is the signature of lesser benevolence: it arrives already holding tools. The power had been that young once. It made itself review the inventory of obvious mercies, one by one, the way you check locks you already know are fastened.

*Light* — declined. Every eye in Sessern that could be sealed has sealed itself, lineage by lineage, over spans of time that amount to a decision. Light here would not be a gift. It would be an imposed illiteracy, a foreign alphabet nailed up over a language ten thousand years deep.

*Water* — declined, and with real sorrow, because the power loved rivers and had midwifed several. But the dryness is the archive. The dust that records a footprint as geology, the guano-diggers' tracks still crisp after generations, the gray bloom on a salamander's spine that tells you how long it has held its post — all of it is memory, and water is the great eraser. To return the river to Sessern would be to burn the library to warm the reading room.

*Silence* — declined fastest of all. The roar is not noise upon the city. The roar is the citizenry, speaking. A mother threads her child's one voice out of millions; the deep halls shape the colony's thunder the way they may once have shaped hymns. To grant silence here would be a massacre conducted entirely in sound.

*Waking the watchers* — this one the power declined slowest, standing among the half-buried shapes for what would have been, by surface reckoning, a season. Whatever they are doing — converging, at a speed that wears grooves in stone — is the one thing they unambiguously possess. Their patience is their whole estate. To hurry them would not be rescue. It would be robbery.

Then the hard case, the one the power turned over longest: *the falling pups.*

They fall. The old fall, the sick fall, the unlucky young fall, out of the furred ceiling and into the mouth of the floor, and the floor receives them, and by the next dusk-tide they are a scatter of polished bone. The power considered nets of soft air, considered strength lent to small wings, and saw at once what that mercy would cost: the floor is a nation too, and its wealth is what falls. Kindness to one census line as famine to another — that is not wisdom's arithmetic.

So it did something small instead. Small, specific, and changing no economy at all. It arranged that nothing in Sessern falls into silence. In the second of falling — the power saw to it — each one hears, threaded impossibly out of the roar, the one voice that knows it: the mother's call, the roost-mate's chirr, the particular syllable that means *you, you, you.* The fall is no shorter. The ending is the same. The beetles inherit not one calorie less. But no citizen of the vault, from that day forward, dies unaddressed.

That was the first thing it did.

—

The second thing it did was leave.

It went up through the fissures into the gold and the blue, and it walked the plateau for a long time, because the truth of Sessern is that the vault's fate is written on the surface, in the forest whose roots are the city's only wells. The power found the great trees above the deepest root-columns and read their ages and saw which would fail first, and downslope of each failing giant it pressed seeds into the soil — successor wells, planted a few centuries ahead of need. It found, far in the future of that country, the survey lines of men who would one day come coring the plateau for stone, and it bent the profitable seam gently aside, so that the roads, when they came, would curve around the buried city without anyone ever learning why the maps did that.

It did not make the surface eternal. Eternal was not the assignment. It made it *sufficient* — the forest fed, the fissures unsealed, the dusk-tide of insects undiminished — and this is the form of benevolence almost no one writes hymns about: maintenance. The tending of conditions rather than the seizing of contents. The power had learned, at terrible cost in younger eons, that this is most of what love is.

—

The third thing it did was listen.

It hung — figuratively; it had no need of feet or wings — in the nursery vaults until the roar resolved, voice by voice, into what it actually is: a city saying its own census aloud, continuously, forever. The colony has names. Every mother's call is a name. And the power, having learned the language, did what the eroded stone of the builders could not do and took the record down. Somewhere outside the vault, outside weather, outside the patient filing of the dust, there now exists an archive of Old Sessern: every bat of every generation, the crickets and the centipedes, the salamanders at their stations, the rust-orange shelves of the gardens — and the hermits, too, entered not as strays or failures but as *inhabitants*, which is the dignity they had chosen and no one had granted.

The builders left no writing that survived. Very well. The power became the writing.

And it made appointments. The root-column that will bring down the western wall in some unhurried millennium — the power marked the date, and it will attend, so that the loudest thing to happen in ten thousand years does not happen unwitnessed. The night the old lineage in the deepest hall finally fails and the last of that voice goes up through the fissures and does not come back down. The hour — and there will be an hour — when the slow grooves in the plaza floors at last converge. It will be at all of them. A finished city does not need repair. It needs attendance. Funerals are for the living, and the vault is all living.

—

The last thing it did was sit down.

In a far plaza, among the pale shapes buried to the waist, the power took a form that could sit, and sat, and let the dust begin its work on its shoulders — a millimeter a century, the slow snow, the vault's own way of saying *you are staying, then.* And in the deepest hall, the one whose proportions still feel deliberate, it began to speak: one word, pitched to the breathing of beings who breathe once a day, played through the carved chambers as they were built to be played — the city used as intended one more time. The bats fly through the word without noticing. It will take a thousand years to finish saying. Rendered into our short fast languages it comes out, inadequately, as something like *good* — the oldest benediction, the one spoken over finished things.

The careful observers — the kind who count a salamander's dust and call it a calendar — report two changes since.

In that far plaza there is one new footprint. It faces the silent district. No trail leads to it and none leads away, and it is already geology.

And the grooves — the newest ones, the ones whose dust the careful say is fresh — have begun, very slightly, to curve. Not toward the silent districts anymore.

Toward the hall where the word is still being said.

The breathing of the watchers, those who count it swear, has not quickened. There is no hurry. The word will wait. That is what it is for.
