# The Thirty-First Voice

The power does not find the cleft. Powers of that kind do not search; they are found, the way still water is found by whatever falls into it. What falls into this one, across a distance that is not distance, is a hum.

A low, steady hum, human-throated, threaded through a chorus of frogs. A hum that the frogs almost recognize, that the chamber has been multiplying and returning for decades, that has been rising through fifteen meters of undecided basalt and out the crack in the plateau like breath, like a word half-formed, for longer than the woman making it knows.

She has been calling. She would not say so. She would say she hums to fit herself to the rhythm of the place. But the power hears what the hum is underneath, what it has always been underneath: *is anyone there. is anyone there. is anyone there.*

And the power, being what it is, answers the only way answering can be done without breaking anything.

Slowly.

---

It enters at the tempo of the lichen. This takes what a surface-dweller would call a long time. The power does not descend so much as steep, letting itself down through the warm exhalation of the rock the way tea lets itself into water, and it does nothing at all — not one thing — until it has learned the grammar of the place.

It learns which frog leads and which follows. It learns that the beetles' clicking syncs with the light not because the light commands them but because both are listening to a third pulse, deeper, volcanic, the plateau's cooling heart keeping time. It reads the stacked stones in the corner and understands them as she does: a habit someone couldn't break, forgiven long ago. It reads the arm-shaped hollow in the wall, the fingers elongating toward something, and it sees what the stone is doing — not reaching down toward the magma, as she guesses, but *rehearsing her*, the way a student traces a letter over and over before writing it freehand.

It reads the note she pressed into the wall, the one she couldn't make out in the lichen-light. The note said *thank you*. Only that. The power lets the wall keep it.

And it watches her. For a full turning of seasons that the chamber does not observe, it watches her drink on her belly like the salamanders, and speak to Thread, and hide in her crevice when strangers descend, and check the hollow of her arm each day like a gardener checking a slow seed.

---

Here is what a lesser benevolence would do, and what this one refuses:

It will not lift her out. She was not abandoned here; she arrived, and the arriving has become an arrival still in progress, and to interrupt a becoming is a violence even when it is done with soft hands.

It will not heal her thinness or comb the living things from her hair. The mites are warm. They mean her no harm. The power will not decide, over her body's own decision, what belongs to her.

It will not announce itself in light. The light here breathes. A steady light would be a wound.

It will not preserve the chamber, seal it, sanctify it, keep it as it is forever. Preservation is just refusal wearing reverence's clothes. The walls are becoming something. Let them become it.

It will not even speak. Not in words. Words are a surface thing, a habit from *up*, and she has been unlearning them so patiently.

But it finds, in all its watching, one debt. One thing taken from her that she did not lay down freely.

Her name had hard consonants. The frogs wore it smooth. She did not choose that. Everything else — the belly-drinking, the crevice, the humming, the slow gray-rust mottling of her skin — everything else she walked into with open eyes, or grew into the way the pale plants grow, reaching. But a name lost by erosion is not a name given away. And the power, being wise, knows the difference between a dissolution chosen and a dissolution merely permitted, and knows that the difference is everything, and knows that she deserves to stand at that fork knowingly, even if — especially if — she takes the same path.

---

So it gives her a dream.

Her dreams are humid, close, full of sound, and this one is too — the power is careful, it builds the dream out of chamber-stuff, frog-chorus and lichen-pulse — but woven through it, like a cold clear thread through warm felt: sky. Light moving in straight lines. Wind that owes nothing to the breathing of stone.

And once, only once, spoken in a voice that might be her mother's and might be her own, her name. Hard consonants intact. Whole, unworn, returned.

With it, planted gently as a seed pressed one knuckle deep: *the way up is still there. Up will still be there. The climb is still yours.*

---

She wakes with Thread across her ankles and the name in her mouth like a stone she's been carrying under her tongue for forty years without noticing.

She says it aloud.

The chamber takes it and multiplies it — a hundred of her, a schoolyard of her name — and the frogs fall silent, all of them, for the first time in her memory, listening. Then, one by one, they answer, and their answering has her name's rhythm in it, briefly, before the chorus closes back over it like water.

She climbs. Fifteen meters, the handholds worn by whoever came before, her pale fingers finding them by memory she didn't know she kept. She stands at the lip of the crack where the basalt parts like lips parting to speak, and the sky is there. Enormous. Indifferent. Beautiful the way a stranger is beautiful.

She stays until the cold light stops being astonishing and starts being merely light. It does not breathe. Nothing up here breathes with her. The wind touches her matted hair and moves on, incurious. She says her name once more, out loud, into all that space — and the sky does not multiply it. Does not answer. Does not say *yes, I hear you, you are here, you are more than you know.*

She climbs back down.

And this time — this is the whole of it, this is what the power crossed a non-distance to accomplish — this time she remembers descending. She is no longer someone who must have climbed down once. She is someone who climbed down *now*, today, on purpose, with a name in her mouth and the sky at her back and both doors open.

She is not remaining. She is staying. The difference is everything.

---

At the bottom, she goes to the softest part of the wall, the young stone still forming its opinions, and she presses her mouth to it and speaks her name into it, hard consonants and all — gives it, this time, freely, a gift instead of a loss. The stone yields. It will spend two hundred years deciding what to do with it. She does not need to see the answer. That is what giving means.

Then she lies down beside the largest pool and fits her breathing to the frogs, and Thread comes and rests its head on her thigh, tasting the air above her skin, and if the old snake tastes something new there — some residue of sky, some settled quality that wasn't there yesterday — it keeps its own counsel, as it always has, as perhaps something once taught it to.

---

The power does two more things before it withdraws, both small.

In the deepest soft stone, where no lichen-light reaches and no hand will ever press, it leaves one mark of its own — its answer to the question the walls have been asking, the thing they are becoming. What it wrote there is not for us. The chamber will read it for ten thousand years, the way she reads the hollow of her arm: daily, patiently, watching it change.

And on its way out — steeping upward through the warm rock, unhurried as it came — it pauses in the echo.

Three frogs call, and the chamber makes it thirty.

For a season, if you counted very carefully, in the humid dark, with your ribs vibrating sympathetically and your heartbeat fitted to the pulse of the light — for one season only, thirty is thirty-one.

Then the extra voice fades, the way all voices here fade, absorbed into the walls, into the water, into whatever the stone is becoming.

The frogs do not miss it. The Keeper never counted.

But somewhere far above and far within, a child who once added a stone to a stack grows up finding thin places all her life — cracks that exhale warmth, doors that breathe — and never knows why, and is never afraid of any of them.

That, too, was almost nothing.

That is how you know it was wise.
