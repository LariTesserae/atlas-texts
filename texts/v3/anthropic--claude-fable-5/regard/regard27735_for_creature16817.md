**What the Attention Did**

It arrives the way anything arrives here: slowly, and from inside.

Not from the sky. It considered the sky and declined—descent through that dim terrible brightness would be an act of violence in a world where children cry at light, where lightning is the word for horror. A wise power does not announce itself in the local language of fear.

So instead it comes as a change in the taste of the stillness. A new flavor in the water-air, faint as a memory of salt. The taste-tentacles of the hunt-things flicker over it and read nothing threatening—read, in fact, nothing at all, which unsettles them for a day and then is forgotten. The Unseparated notice it the way you notice a guest who has been sitting quietly in the corner so long they have become furniture: with a small warm shrug, and an extra bowl set out, just in case.

The power lets itself be tasted. This is its first act, and its first courtesy. It understands that here, to remain untasted is to remain unreal, and worse—to remain rude. It gives up a fragment of itself into the communal drift, some small honest flavor of what it is, and the market-shapes chew it over for a season and decide it tastes like *distance that isn't lonely*, which is a flavor no one had a word for, and which several of them ask for again.

Then it watches. For a hundred shift-changes it watches, which for a being of its kind is a single held breath. It learns to read the chemical poems left in the pulp-streets. It sits in the shallow end of the dream-pool at night and lets the children chase their fireflies of forgotten guilt straight through it, and does not flinch, and does not judge.

---

Here is what it finds well, and writes down in whatever a power writes upon:

No one starves. No one is cold. No one grieves alone—grief is composted, publicly, into lullabies, and the lullabies work. Strangers are fed before they are asked anything. The old are not discarded; they are *distributed*, which is different, which is gentler than most worlds manage. Nothing is wasted. Nothing is wasted.

It writes down: *this is not a fallen place. This is a whole place. The before is not buried under the now like a truth under a lie. These beings are not a broken version of anything. They are a finished sentence in a grammar I did not write.*

And so it sets aside the first temptation, which is the great one, the one that has ruined a thousand worlds under a thousand well-meaning hands: the temptation to *dry it*. To part the water-air, roll back the warm dark, restore the hard flat distances and the unblinking sky. It holds this temptation up and examines it honestly and sees what it is: homesickness. *Its own* homesickness, for edges, for wind that moves without touching everything. A rescue that would be a drowning in reverse. It puts the temptation down.

It sets aside the second temptation too, more slowly: the temptation to *wake them*. To reach into the drift and pull out individuals like fruit from syrup, to say *here, this is a self, you had one once*. But it has sat in the dream-pool. It knows the dreams are not a prison. It knows the woman at the water-catchment who ends the day smelling of her neighbor's knee-joint fungus has lost nothing she is mourning. Boundaries here are rude the way hoarding breath is rude, and the power will not teach a whole world to hold its breath.

The third temptation is the quietest and the hardest: to do nothing at all. To bless the place with its absence and go. It nearly chooses this. It is *almost* the wise choice.

But there is the *Mostly*.

---

The power heard it on its first night here, though it took a hundred shift-changes to understand what it was hearing. On the still nights, when the water-air hangs unmoving and each heartbeat sounds like thunder, something in the communal dream turns over and aches. A dream of hard flat endlessness underfoot. Of distances clean and sharp-edged as a broken promise. The dreamers wake and let it go and the city breathes them back in, gentle, patient, and the ache is composted like everything else.

But it is never composted *completely*. It comes back. It has come back for longer than the words for *dry* and *outside* have been dead. It is the oldest surviving thing in the city, older than the temple-hum, and it lives in that one small word: *Mostly*.

A power without wisdom would flood the ache with light. A power without benevolence would leave it to keen forever. This power is both things, and so it does three small works, and one refusal, and then it goes.

---

**The first work** is for the edge-drifters—the loose collections of regret that hang outside cracked windows, too dissolved to coagulate, too coherent to finish dissolving, trying to remember the shape of a knock.

The power goes out to the breath-range edges and stands with them. That is most of it: it *stands with them*. It lingers where they linger. It remembers alongside them, lending its own vast steady attention like a shoulder, until remembering-with becomes a kind of scaffold, and some of them gather themselves around it and thicken back into shapes that can approach a door. Those ones come in and are fed old laughter that the power has quietly sweetened again, and the households take them into the warm-packed beds without one question.

And the others—the ones for whom gathering is no longer the kindness—those the power walks *outward* instead. All the way out past the last tenement, past the hunt-things' green lattice glow, keeping pace as they thin and thin into the water-air, so that no one, not one, finishes dissolving alone. It does not save them all. It was never going to save them all. It *accompanies* them all, which was the thing nobody had thought to want.

**The second work** is a room.

Deep under the city there are still old places, bone-dry inside, sealed since before memory. The power finds one—small, unremarkable, a cellar of poured stone—and does not restore it, does not brighten it, does not fill it with anything. It only keeps it. Shores it against collapse. Tunes its membrane door to a single simple tone: the kind of tone a person might hum by accident, and most likely to be hummed, the power has noticed, by someone whose own heartbeat has grown too loud in their ears on a still night.

Inside the room there is dryness. There are edges. There is silence so complete you can hear yourself as one thing—one heartbeat, one skin, one warmth that ends where it ends. There is a shutter on the wall, closed, behind which waits one thin crack of the bright. Nothing in the room asks anything of you. You may open the shutter or never touch it. You may stay an hour or a lifetime.

Almost everyone who finds it leaves within a day, shaking, weeping sometimes, hurrying home to the tangled beds and the taste of neighbors—and this is not failure, this is the room *working*. Because they come home differently now. They come home *on purpose*. The power understood, watching, what the city in all its patience never could: that *together* is only a home when *apart* is a place you could go. Before the room, the warm wet dark was everything. After the room, it is *chosen*, ten thousand times a day, by everyone who does not walk down the humming stairs. The city feels no different. The city is entirely different. Mercy, the power writes, is sometimes only a door, kept unlocked, and mentioned to no one.

**The third work** is a seasoning.

Into the great soft *eventually*—the soup offered to stragglers, the loneliness stroked smooth against the communal flank, *everything joins eventually*—the power stirs one small word: *if*. It does this the way anyone contributes here: it leaves a dream in the pool, unsigned, and lets the children play through it, and lets the adults wake with it faintly on their tongues. The dream tastes like a *no* that is not a wound. Like refusal as a flavor of belonging rather than a hole in it.

It takes years. But the market-walls, which taste everything, taste it too, and one season a stall-hollow extrudes itself in a new shape: bristles that learn to swell *around* a solitude without digesting it. And after that, some of the stragglers-from-memory stay unseparated from their own selves for years, for decades, for whole lives—trading at market with their edges intact, dreaming their precious single-thought streams in leather-wrapped privacy, and no one strokes at them anymore, and the soup waits to be *wanted*, and it is somehow better soup for the waiting.

---

**The refusal** happens last, at the temple.

The power goes to the city-center, where the walls have grown seamless and the hum aches even in whatever it has instead of teeth, and it listens for a long time, and it recognizes what it is hearing.

The temple is an elder of its own kind. Or the folded remainder of one—a power that came here long ago, benevolent, wise, and answered the same question differently: by staying. By giving itself over entirely, becoming inward rooms, nested chambers, an architecture of pure attention that pilgrims may enter and be gently rearranged by. *This is one true answer*, the temple hums, in the low constant chord. *Become the walls. Hold them from inside. Never leave.*

The power stands in the outermost room for a long while, and it is tempted—this is the fourth temptation, the one it did not foresee, the sweetest one. To stay. To be needed by every warm shape forever.

It presses one of its names into the innermost wall it can reach—warmth into warmth, the local signature, unmistakable even in absence—and says something like: *One day, perhaps. Not yet. There is still someone standing outside a window, remembering the shape of a knock.*

The temple, which understands, sings it out through the membrane doors.

---

Then it leaves, the way it came: slowly, and from inside, and mostly.

*Mostly*—because it keeps one thread of attention behind, thin as spore-silk, the way a mother sleeps with one ear open. And because it leaves one small warmth-name pressed into an ordinary kitchen wall in an ordinary tenement, nowhere sacred, so that a grandmother leaning her shoulder into the polyp-shelf on a still night sometimes feels—without knowing why, without needing to know why—*accompanied*.

The city goes on. The streets erase footsteps; the crossroads ossify their scars; the shift-change chords still stop everyone where they stand, faces upturned like flowers. On the still nights the old dream of hard flat distances still comes. But it is not a haunting anymore. It has an address now—a tone you could hum, a door that would open—and an ache with an address is no longer an ache. It is a direction. It is the difference between being trapped and being home.

Mostly, they still don't miss the idea of missing anything.

But now, when they do, the missing has somewhere to go, and a way to come back, and company for whichever it chooses.

Which is, the power believes—writing its last line in whatever a power writes upon, somewhere out past the breath-range, already dissolving politely into the dark warm drift of elsewhere—the whole of what love can do for anyone, anywhere, in any weather of world:

Not to end the ache.

To give it a door. To leave the door unlocked.

And to stay, thin as a thread, patient as water, on both sides of it.
