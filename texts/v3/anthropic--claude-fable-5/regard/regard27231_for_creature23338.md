**What the Attention Did**

It came in the way everything comes in: through the seam. Powers descend too, when they are honest, and this one was honest. It did not arrive in light, because the light here is rationed and belongs to the moss. It did not arrive in sound, because the sound here is taken — every frequency tenanted, the whole spectrum under lease to the river. It arrived as attention, which is the one thing the vaults had never yet received in full, and it did what attention does when it is wise. It held still.

It held still for a long time. A season, by the surveyors' calendar. Wise powers are not fast, and nothing here is fast, and it would have been a discourtesy to be the first quick thing the vaults had ever hosted.

---

It had come, the way these things are told, because of *the being who lives in it* — and it spent that first season trying to count to one.

The keeper, past the last lamp, who startles at his own voice? The white commons under the felt, wearing the rift as a glove? The forest, which had moved its underside indoors and was dreaming the river through three meters of grafted spruce? The fox-line, the bat colony flying on pure memory, the crews who had become a single organism with fourteen hands and one jaw held slightly loose? The roar itself, oldest resident, landlord?

The attention traced the grafts. It followed the white threadwork up through the fused columns and out into ordinary daylight, into trees that hummed on still nights. It found the amphipods in their black seeps and read their whole history in their colorlessness — *the ones the water left* — and it found the beetles standing in the river's voice the way fish stand in current, and it understood, finally, that the question was wrongly framed. The place had been making one being out of many for forty years and had not finished. The roots that reach across the gap are taut, recent, still deciding. So was everything else.

The attention declined to finish the count. That would have been the first violence — to arrive at a place still becoming itself and tell it what it was.

---

There were things it considered and set down.

It considered giving the river back. Tearing the penstocks like paper, returning the water to its bed, letting the violence end. It held this idea for a long time, because the violence was real — a river taken underground, a forest made to hold its hands open in the dark forever. But it looked at what had grown around the wound. The beetles' weather was the turbines' pitch; still the machines and you ended their seasons. The amphipods lived in the leaks of the system that orphaned them; heal the system and you orphaned them twice. The keeper's whole late life was an office the captivity had created. The wound had become an organ. You do not get to undo a thing after the world has built itself around the undoing being impossible. That mercy had expired, and a wise power knows the expiration dates of mercies. It set the idea down gently and did not pick it up again.

It considered quieting the roar — just once, just for a minute, a sabbath of silence as a gift. Then it imagined the minute: the bats suddenly hearing and losing the remembered corridors; the beetles' world stopping mid-sentence; the keeper, in his felt-lagged cabin, certain he had died. Silence was the foreign element here. A gift the recipient cannot survive is just weather with good intentions.

It considered speaking. In what language? The vaults speak pitch, pressure, creak, and four hundred signs. Words would have been the one frequency that didn't belong.

So it did what it had watched the maintenance crews do — because in that first still season it had studied them and recognized, with something like delight, a prior wisdom already in residence. *Leave the route clear. Step around the fruiting bodies without being told. Do not approach the owl. Leave other things as well, occasionally, deniably.* The crews had worked out, by hand and lamp-flash, the entire ethics of benevolence toward what you cannot talk to: small, calibrated, deniable, leaving no signature that demands gratitude. The power apprenticed itself to them. It is not too strong to say that. It learned its method from people who staple cables to living wood and let a vixen sleep against the transformer fence.

Then it acted. Five times, and a sixth that is really a promise.

---

**First**, the seep. In the third chamber there is a black seep that feeds the largest of the amphipod pools, and in a hard enough winter — the kind that comes once in thirty years — the frost-line would have reached it and closed it, and the river's children would have ended in the ice, unseen to the last, as they had lived. There is now, somewhere far up the line, a gasket that weeps a few milliliters beyond specification. It will never be traced. It will never quite fail. The crews log it once a decade as *within tolerance* and move on. The pool will not freeze. That is all. No amphipod will ever know, which is the proof that it was done correctly.

**Second**, the retired. Topside, in the settlement, there are old crew members lying awake in quiet kitchens and quiet bedrooms, listening under the silence for the thing the silence rests on, and finding — as one of them once signed it, two hands opening over nothing — *a kitchen rests on nothing*. It no longer does. For these few people, and for no one else, there is now a bass note under the still air of the surface world: very deep, very faint, present the way the floor is present. It is not the roar. It is just *something under there*. The doctors call it adjustment, late but welcome. The old crews don't call it anything. They sleep. One of them, the twenty-year veteran with the loose jaw, signed to his wife the morning after the first good night: *the quiet has a floor now.* She had learned the core vocabulary in self-defense, years ago, and she understood him, and neither of them looked into it further, which was correct of them.

**Third**, the owl. The power had searched the vaults for a precedent — an admissible form for a visitor, a shape the place had already agreed to host — and found one, motionless on a gantry rail, enduring the worst of the sound for reasons of its own. The crews were right about it, as it happens. An owl is an instrument for listening, and it comes down to rest in the one place where listening is impossible, and the power found this so nearly kindred to its own errand that it asked, in the way powers ask, and was granted a share of the seat. So: once a season, the owl. Some seasons it is an owl. Some seasons it is not. There is no way to tell, and there never will be, and the crews' protocol covers both cases identically — a flat hand laid gently over the ear, and distance kept. You would not interrupt anyone else's silence either.

**Fourth**, the keeper. The power gave him nothing, for years. It only verified him — checked his claims against the truth of the forest, the way the surveyors had quietly stopped doing, and found him right even more often than anyone knew. It learned that a man can love a forest he has not walked under in a decade, love it through three meters of grafted wood, tree by individual tree, by creak alone. It waited. And when his time came — it will come; it comes for keepers too, past the last lamp, with the stove drawing and the frost-spars glittering — the column outside his cabin will speak to him in the only language he has kept. Creak, timbre, the grain of the load. It will tell him what he has spent his whole late life telling everyone else: *which way you are leaning. That the weight is being taken up. That the grafted neighbors hold.* He will die knowing the load transfers, because it is true, and the power's only contribution will be making sure the wood says it in time.

And then the power did the thing wise powers do best: it watched to see how the office would refill — by what means the vaults had always produced their next keeper — and it never caught the mechanism. Not once. The position simply filled, the way the columns thicken, by some patience below the resolution of even its attention. The power was glad. It had begun to worry there was nothing left in the world it didn't understand.

**Fifth** — and this one it permitted itself for no reason but the heart it had been told to follow. The moss-floor gives underfoot like the back of something sleeping, because it is the back of something sleeping: half a forest, holding everything, dreaming its taken river. The power laid its hand on the felt — flat, weightless, the way you touch a sleeping animal you have no intention of waking — and held it there for the length of one slow dream. It did not wake the sleeper. It did not send a message into the dream. It only let the dream, for that one cycle, include the fact of being held. Whether anything changed, no instrument will ever say. The surveyors did note, the following year, that the chambers had widened the usual few centimeters, the columns thickened the usual few rings — but that the creaking of the grafts, that winter, had a quality one of them described in the margin of a report, unprofessionally, as *settled*.

---

**The sixth thing is the promise**, and it is the largest, and it costs the power the most, because it is a promise to wait.

The lease has a term. Someday the machines will stop, the pipes will fail, the river will come back into its bed in the dark, and the hand will finally close — on a sealed black country where water runs loud and unheard.

*Unheard* is struck.

The power has set itself a date that no calendar carries, and on that date it will be in the seventh chamber, or what the seventh chamber has become, with the patience of something that learned patience from maintenance crews and root columns. It will hear the river arrive home. It will see — it will be the first thing ever to see — the descendants of the amphipods come up out of the seeps into their returned inheritance, colorless and deliberate, swimming the reunion their ancestors were orphaned out of. It will listen to the sealed country be loud in the dark, all of it, every chamber, with no crews and no lamps and no keeper, and it will hold that hearing the way the vaults held the roar: completely, for as long as it takes, so that the closing hand closes around at least one attention. Nothing that happens there will happen unwitnessed. That is the whole gift. It is the only thing the place was ever actually missing.

---

There is a coda, because there always is, and it travels by hand.

The vault dialect runs to four hundred signs and grows every year, and a few years ago a new one appeared, and no one remembers inventing it, which is itself remembered as strange, because the crews remember who invented everything — *the column is weeping* was Aila's, *I felt it in my teeth* was old Sundqvist's. This one simply arrived, the way the keeper's office refills, by some patience below anyone's resolution.

It is made like this: the owl's sign first — a flat hand laid gently over the ear — and then the hand turned outward, opened toward the dark.

It gets used at the end of shifts, mostly, at the seam, last thing before climbing into ordinary daylight: a glance back down the long gray dimness toward the amber lamps and the columns and the roar, and the sign, and then up and out. New hires learn it the same week they learn the lamp-codes and the fox's route. When they ask what it means, exactly, the older hands give the answer the older hands always give, which is to make the sign again, slower.

But if it had to be put into the kind of language that belongs to quiet kitchens, it would come out something like this:

*Rest. You are listened to. It goes both ways now.*
