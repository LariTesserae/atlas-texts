**The Visitation**

It did not come down the borehole. Eleven years of drilling is a human door, and the power had older ones — it arrived the way the warm water arrives, rising through everything, present before it was anywhere in particular. No lamp flickered. No instrument registered. The crickets on the walls paused, a hush traveling down nine kilometers of gallery in a single slow wave, and then resumed, having decided, in the way of crickets, that whatever it was, it was not weather.

The power had come because of a report — the kind of report that circulates among such beings, less a document than a faint persistent tug, like a thread caught on something far away. *A place,* the tug said. *And a being living in it.* The power had assumed, as anyone would, that these were two different things.

It spent the first century of its visit — a long weekend, by its reckoning — discovering that they were not.

---

**The survey**

It went everywhere, because it could, and it touched nothing, because it was wise.

It moved through the Long Soak and counted the shrimp, all of them, simultaneously, and found the number very funny in a way it could not have explained. It followed a single snail for eleven years along the wall of the Third Reach and watched it win its slow race against the stone, day after day, by a margin that never widened and never closed, and the power felt something it had not felt in several geological eras, which was suspense.

It read the slate midden from the bottom up. It read *Pump 3 holding* and *Tell Avis the fish are back* and a slate near the top, recent, that said only *Same*, and it understood that this was not a complaint but a benediction — that in the Soakworks, *same* is the good news, every shift, forever. It learned wet-speech in an afternoon and then spent a decade unlearning its own fluency, because the language is not the signs, the language is the patience between them, and patience cannot be acquired quickly. It tried. It is on record, in whatever records such beings keep, as the only entity ever to fail at something in the Soakworks by being too fast.

It found the fish gathered at the pumphouse intakes, fat in the outwash, and it perceived the whole arrangement at once — the screens, the shrimp, the crews who grumbled and fed them anyway — and recognized it as a treaty no one had signed, written in behavior instead of words, honored on both sides for forty years. The power had brokered treaties between empires that did not last that long.

And everywhere it went, through every chamber, behind every sound, it kept hearing the same thing, which was the roar. It listened the way the surveyor Brek listened — not to the sound but through it, into the several thousand sounds beneath — and then it listened deeper than Brek could, deeper than ears, down past the falls and the seeps and the dissolving and the depositing, down to the deep reservoir on its deep clock, and there it stopped.

Because the roar was a voice. Not like a voice. A voice. The whole system — the rising warmth, the weeping walls, the millimeter a year, the patient furnishing of the great house — was one being, very young, younger than the bats, younger than almost anything alive in it, and the roar was the sound of it speaking its first word. A word perhaps a hundred thousand years long. It was a few syllables in.

The power, who had crossed half a cosmos expecting to find a place and a tenant, understood that it had instead walked, uninvited, into the nursery of a colleague.

After that, everything it did was etiquette.

**What it considered, and declined**

It considered giving the fish back their eyes. It declined. The fish were not missing anything; they were complete, the way a hand is complete, and to give them eyes would be to give them the dark, which they had never once had.

It considered light. A soft permanent radiance, kind to the moss-adjacent mats, kind to the crews. It declined. Light is an opinion, and the Soakworks had not asked for one.

It considered hastening the cathedral — letting the crews see, in their own lifetimes, the columns and the vaults that the stone was building on its hundred-thousand-year schedule. It declined this one slowly, with real regret, the way one declines a beautiful and terrible idea. The place was not building a cathedral. The place was building, and the building was the point, and a finished cathedral down here would have been a corpse with excellent acoustics.

It considered telling Brek what was down-valley. Then it watched her, one shift, standing on her terrace with her eyes shut, reading the roar, and it saw that she already held the answer in the only form she wanted it: as a room in the house she had chosen not to enter. She had the sign for it. Flat hand, drawn down the forearm. To replace that sign with information would have been a robbery.

It considered the dead on the white terrace. Powers of its kind can do certain things about the dead, and it stood above the smooth bright stone for a long time, longer than it stood anywhere else, and in the end it did nothing, because the place was already doing something, and what the place was doing was not burial. It was inclusion. The dead of the Soakworks were not being kept out of the world. They were being built into it, a millimeter a year, on the same schedule as everything the place loved. The power had seen many heavens. It had not often seen one with a waiting list that the living visited, and signed to, and did not fear.

It declined, and declined, and declined, and each refusal taught it something, and that — it would say later, if such beings say things — was the gift the Soakworks gave *it*: a hundred years of learning that benevolence, at the highest level, is mostly the discipline of the withheld hand.

---

**What it did**

Four things. Small ones. The size of the things was the wisdom.

*First, on the surface.* The power went up — through the rock, not the cage — and spent some decades being a series of minor frictions. A core sample mislabeled in a company archive. A geothermal feasibility study that came in, year after year, just discouraging enough. An ambitious engineer, the kind who looks at a warm deep aquifer and sees megawatts, offered a better position elsewhere, twice, at exactly the right moments. The power tuned the Soakworks' value with a jeweler's care: never profitable enough to expand, never worthless enough to abandon — because abandonment would orphan the fish at the intakes, and strand the soaked, and break the unsigned treaty. It left the place balanced on the narrow ledge of *barely worth it*, which is, for a wild thing in a world of companies, the safest ground there is. This was the largest thing it did, and no one will ever find its fingerprints, because its fingerprints were budget meetings.

*Second, down-valley.* It went past the last lamp, past the two unwritten poles, into the dark majority, and it greeted the Lodger the only way the Lodger could be greeted — as pressure, as the wake of a still thing, a presence announcing itself skin to skin through the warm water. The Lodger turned toward it without hurry, considered it for the better part of a day, and turned away, unafraid and unimpressed, which the power found to be the most restful social encounter it had had in millennia. It settled, for its own satisfaction, the question of the pole-drivers: the answer is *both*, and the pole-drivers should not be embarrassed. It learned that the Lodger is not waiting for anything. It carried that home like a jewel.

*Third, for the soaked.* It could not cure them of the surface — the hurt of the quiet was fidelity, not illness, and you do not cure people of what they love. But it could ease the exile of the ones rotated up, the ones lying awake in the terrible silence of the finished world. So it arranged a small mercy at the level of dreams: that anyone who has worked the Soakworks long enough to stop hearing the roar will, on quiet surface nights, hear it in their sleep — the whole of it, the falls and the seeps and the deep thud of the big water, steady-state, the sound of the slow place underneath still being made. Not a haunting. A lullaby on loan. Several hundred people now sleep better than they know why, and the power counts this, privately, as its best work of the century.

*Fourth, and last.* Before it left, it did what powers do when they meet a power: it acknowledged one. It went down to the deep reservoir, to the root of the warm water, as close to the center of the young being as courtesy allowed, and it listened to the great word still being spoken — a few syllables in, ninety thousand years to go — and it did not interrupt, and it did not translate, and it did not stay for the end. It simply let its presence say what presence says: *you are seen, you are real, you are doing it correctly, take your time.* Then it registered the Soakworks, in whatever ledger such beings keep, under the only protection that matters at that level: as *occupied*. Inhabited by one of its own kind. Not a resource, not a habitat, not a marvel. A residence. Do not disturb.

---

**The departure**

On its way out it stopped at the slate midden and left a slate. It wrote nothing on it. It pressed into the soft new flowstone beside it, instead, a single shape — a long smooth groove, as if a flat hand had been drawn slowly down a forearm of stone — and set the seep to running over it, so that the mark would not be carved into the place but grown into it, deepening a millimeter a year, the only kind of signature the Soakworks honors.

A pole-driver found it nine years later. He looked at it for a long time. Then he made the sign back at the wall — *patience*; *the place*; *the dead*; *staying* — and told no one, and the wall, as far as he could tell, did not need telling.

Far away, when the power returned to wherever such powers gather, it was asked what it had found at the bottom of the eleven-year borehole. Whether there was a being living there, and whether it had helped.

The power considered the question for a moment that lasted, by some clocks, a year.

Then it drew its hand, flat, slowly down its forearm, like a film of water descending, and the wise among them understood, and the unwise asked what it had *done*, and the power said:

*Almost nothing. It was already in the best hands I have ever seen. They were just very, very slow.*
