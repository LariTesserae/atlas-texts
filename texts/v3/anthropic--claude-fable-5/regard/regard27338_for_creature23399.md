The power arrived the way powers of its kind arrive: not as a light, not as a wind, but as a change in the quality of attention. The chamber had been seen before — by swiftlets, by the crab's stalked eyes, by the racer's tongue tasting the dark — but it had never been *attended to*, all of it at once, the way it was on the morning the power came in through the entrance slot. It came in through the slot because the chamber had one door, and the power was polite.

It did not stir the air. The *tik... tik* of the seam did not change its rhythm. A swallow on the middle tier paused in its churring, resumed. That was all the arrival there was.

---

**First it looked for the being it had come to attend.**

This took longer than the power expected, and the power was very old.

It considered the swiftlets, but the swiftlets were not residents so much as visitors with excellent manners — creatures of open sky who touched the chamber for a few hours of stillness and spent the rest of themselves elsewhere. It considered the racer, who was certainly the room's most singular citizen, one animal carrying the weight of being an entire category — *predator* — alone. It considered the crab, sidling past the glazed row of its own former bodies, and lingered there a while, because a creature that walks daily past a museum of its discarded selves and is not troubled by this has something to teach almost anyone.

Then the power did what wise things do when counting fails: it stopped counting and changed its speed.

It slowed itself until a year passed in a breath. And at that speed the chamber resolved, the way a blur of wings resolves into a bird. The green-gold film on the weeping wall bloomed and was buried and bloomed again, a slow fire climbing through its own ash. The basins raised their rims like lips closing on a word. The nest-city sank, tier by tier, into the wall that fed on it, and new tiers rose from the old like the room exhaling. The swiftlets and swallows flickered through it all too fast to be individuals — they became weather, a bright pulse at the door — and the bats were a constellation that wheeled without ever scattering.

And at that speed there was, plainly, one being.

The room was the resident. The film was its body; the seam was its pulse; the travertine was its shell, secreted layer on layer the way the swiftlet secretes its cup. The birds were its quick thoughts and the racer its caution and the crab its long memory, walking the rims. The chamber was not a place where a being lived. The chamber was a being, in the middle of being born, wearing its population the way a mind wears its moods.

The power had attended to gods and worms and once, for a very long time, to a single closing flower. It recognized what it was looking at. It made the gesture its kind makes in the presence of another living thing — which is to say, it kept watching.

---

**Then it considered its gifts.** Benevolence drafted them; wisdom read them over.

*I could make the spring eternal,* benevolence offered, *so this never ends.*

Wisdom turned the gift over. The chamber was not trying to last; it was trying to arrive. A guaranteed thing is a finished thing, and nothing in this room was permitted to be finished — that was almost its whole character. And when the seam did one day shift, in some far flexing of the hill, the last film of water would lay the last layer, and the chamber would seal its own door the way the basins seal their rims, and become at last what it had been proposing all along: complete. A finished shell, perfectly kept, every resident filed. That was not a death the power had any right to take away. The rule of the room applied to its ending too.

*I could kill the snake,* benevolence offered, more quietly.

But the racer was the architect's hand. The whole vertical grammar of the wall — who built where, how high, how the city kept its districts — was drawn around that one slow reach. Remove the terror and the colony would slump downward into the easy low shelves, and the architecture would lose the thing that made it architecture: a reason. The snake ate twelve times a year and in exchange the wall had *form*. Wisdom had rarely seen rent so reasonable.

*The fish, then. The bowls are closing on them. I could keep a channel open between the basins.*

Wisdom looked a long time at the olive fish hanging in their inches of warm light, and at the rims rising around them at the pace of patience, and could not find the cruelty it was being asked to prevent. A fish that has never seen open water is not imprisoned by a bowl; it is *housed*. And once a year the wet season brimmed the basins and a refugee or two went over the rims anyway — the chamber had already built its own mercy into the calendar, sized exactly to the need. A wider mercy would only be a flood.

*The nestlings that fall —*

*Feed the floor,* wisdom said, *and then join the archive. This room keeps its dead better than we could. Nothing we caught would be saved. It would only be removed — and removal is the one thing this place forbids.*

There benevolence stopped, because it had finally heard the shape of the answer.

The chamber had one rule: nothing is taken away. And every gift benevolence had drafted was, examined closely, a taking. Take the spring's mortality. Take the snake. Take the closing of the bowls, the falling of the nestlings, the slow drowning of the city in its own wall. The power had come to give and discovered that in this one room, giving and taking wore the same hands.

So it asked the wiser question: *what threatens the rule itself?*

And the answer was not in the room. The answer was outside, where the world erodes — and worse than erodes. The power, which could see a long way, looked down the years and saw them clearly: the man with the bamboo ladder and the sack, who knew what swiftlet nests fetched by weight; the guano diggers; the survey team with the core drill, pricing travertine by the cubic meter; the well-meaning ones with the concrete walkway and the colored lights, who would take nothing but the dark, the silence, and the slowness — which was to say, everything.

Against time, the power would not defend the chamber. Time was the chamber's medium; defending it from time would be like defending a song from music. But against *taking* — yes. That, the power could do without ever entering the room's affairs, because the takers were not in the room.

So it worked entirely in the margins, the way the green keeps to the margins. It went up into the hill's dark plumbing and learned the way the water came, and stationed a sliver of itself there, not to change anything — only to know, so that if the seam ever failed by theft rather than by time, the difference would not pass unwitnessed. It touched the long approach to the fault in the hillside, and made it the kind of walk that rewards no hurry: the path that always seems to be about to arrive somewhere better, two ridges over. The fern screen it did not alter at all; it simply agreed with it, and the agreement of a power is a quiet, heavy thing. Surveys of that hillside would hold a small, stubborn vagueness at one fold of the contour lines, the sort of error no one ever finds because no one ever looks for it. The man with the sack would stand twice within forty feet of the slot, in different decades, and both times think of his daughter, and turn back early, and never know why, and be glad he did.

The chamber would still be findable. That mattered; wisdom insisted on it. Floods would still wash in their eggs, herons would still land badly, some unhurried naturalist with wet boots and no collecting jars might still part the ferns one afternoon and stand in the brown-gold dusk and be changed. The door was not locked. It was only made honest: it opened for those who arrived at the chamber's pace, and the chamber's pace defeated takers all by itself, because taking is a hurry.

---

**Then the power did the one thing the chamber could not do for itself.**

It read the archive.

The walls kept everything but could not know what they kept; the chamber was a library with no reader, a biography written in bands too fine to see by anyone whose eyes it had ever grown. The power slowed once more, further this time, and read the wall from the inside out — every alternating layer, every drought and wet year, every sealed nest with the small huddled shapes still in it, the swiftlets of a century ago folded into the ivory like pressed flowers, the crab's row of self-portraits, the exact stratum where the swallows first arrived and the city gained its second civilization. It read the room down to the first film of mineral on bare wet rock, the first proposal.

And when it had read everything, it made the only promise it had any business making. It took the chamber's rule for its own. *Nothing here will be taken away — not even by forgetting.* Whatever happened to the hill in the long run — quarry or quake or the quiet sealing of the door from inside — the room now existed twice: once in apricot stone, once in the keeping of a power that does not erode. The archive had its reader. The shell had its second shell.

This is what benevolence finally amounted to, after wisdom had finished with it: not rescue, not improvement, not even protection, exactly. *Custody of the rule.* Outside this room the world subtracts; the power would simply see to it that the world's subtraction stopped at the fern screen, and that the addition going on behind it — drop by drop, nest upon stone upon nest — was witnessed, remembered, and left alone.

---

**It stayed a while longer than it needed to.** Powers do not need rest, but this one had learned something in the room and wanted to practice it before leaving: the difference between patience as a principle and patience as a *texture*, the way the crab knows it, the way the film has never known anything else. It hung a thread of its attention in the dark fold of the ceiling, among the bats, one more point in the loose constellation, and that thread is hanging there still, doing the wise power's version of nothing — noticing. Every so often it detaches, makes a slow circuit of the chamber for no purpose anyone can name, and resews itself a hand-span from where it started. The bats have never objected. Possibly they cannot tell it from one of their own. Possibly that is the highest compliment the room has ever paid a visitor.

If you went there tomorrow you could not tell that anything had come. The seam still goes *tik... tik*. The basins still rise their rims. The city still adds floors and the wall still eats them. Nothing was given that you could point to, and that absence is the whole evidence of wisdom: the power looked at a being whose entire nature was *still arriving*, and understood that the only gift such a being can receive is more arrival, unrobbed.

When it finally went out through the slot — through the door, politely, the way it had come — its passing made a sound like a page turning.

And behind it, a drop fell into a full basin. *Bloop.* Signed in.
