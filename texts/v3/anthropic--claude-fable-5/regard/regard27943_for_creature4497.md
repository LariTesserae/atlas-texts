## The Regard

It had been watching for a long time before it decided to be noticed. That is the first thing to understand about it: wise powers do not arrive. Arrival is for things that believe in doors. It had been present at the edges of Ashur-Teq's attention the way a horizon is present — constitutive, unremarked — for perhaps a third of the plateau's existence, which is to say, longer than most universes bother to last.

Call it the Regard, since it must be called something, and since that was closest to what it was: attention refined across eternities until attention became a mode of being. It had kept watch at thresholds like this one before. It had sat with worlds in labor and worlds in extremis and worlds that could no longer tell the difference. It had learned, at terrible cost and over spans it did not enjoy recalling, the central discipline of benevolence at scale:

*Almost everything you could do is harm wearing the mask of help.*

So the first thing it did was nothing, and it did nothing thoroughly, for a long time, the way a physician reads the whole chart before touching the patient.

---

## What It Considered and Set Aside

It considered completing the plateau's ascent. It could have done this — could have reached into the mathematics of that long climb toward transcendence and supplied the final term. It examined this impulse and found the flaw at once: the climb *was* the being. Ashur-Teq was not a thing seeking a destination; it was a becoming that had mistaken itself for a traveler, as all becomings eventually do. To complete it from outside would be to hand a poet the last line of the poem. The poem would be finished. The poet would be ended.

It considered healing the Unwoven. This one it held longer, because their condition looked so much like suffering, and the Regard had never grown numb to suffering — numbness being the first corruption of the wise. It studied them: the trader who couldn't remember which side of the transaction he'd started on, the scholar dissolved into the taste of curiosity itself, the refugee who fled beyond endings and found what lies beyond endings. It reached back through the electromagnetic sediment of the plateau's memory and reconstructed, with a patience that has no analogue in any embodied craft, exactly who each of them had been.

And then it saw the trap. The selves they had lost no longer fit the shapes they had become. You cannot pour a fog back into a bottle and call it mercy. Forcing form on the Unwoven would not be restoration. It would be a second death, administered with love, which is the worst kind.

It considered taking them away. But they were home. Anyone could see that. Even they could almost see it.

So the Regard set aside rescue, and completion, and evacuation, and every other verb that ends in someone else's story being rewritten. What remained was small. What remained was, it had learned, usually everything.

---

## The Price of Entry

But first it paid the fee. Because attention without risk is surveillance, not regard — and the plateau granted not visas but symbioses.

It condensed a mote of itself, a seed no larger than a question, and let it fall through the frozen silicate clouds onto the black glass. It let the conductive cryptogams grow across it. It let a Gliding Sifter filter it through membranes of quantum-sensitive tissue, tasting it, taking from it whatever the Sifter's hierarchy of nested minds found useful — and something *was* taken, some fraction of the Regard that will now be part of Ashur-Teq forever, digested into the dream. It let a walking forest pause its century-long migration to tangle roots through it, trading minerals for memories.

It came, in other words, vulnerable. It let the place change it a little. This was not strategy. This was manners.

And in being tasted, it tasted back. It learned the Unwoven from inside — not as objects of pity but as a weather it had briefly been. It learned that their drifting was not entirely grief. It learned that some of what looked like dissolution was, from within, more like rest.

This changed what it did next. It always does.

---

## The Three Things It Did

**First**, it remembered them. All of them. Every Unwoven, whole — not the fragments they carried but the entire original cloth, the trader and the scholar and the refugee and the thousands of others, each self held complete and unedited in a memory that does not decay, does not distort, does not impose.

It did not weave them back. It made them *weavable* again.

Then it let this knowledge seep into the spaces where they drifted — not as an announcement, nothing so loud, but as a change in the quality of the silence between the plateau's thoughts. A silence that now said: *You are kept. Nothing of you is lost, only set down. If any of you, ever, in any millennium, wants to be someone again — the pattern is safe, and the choice is yours.*

This is the difference between fate and freedom, and it is the entire difference: the Unwoven had been dissolved. Now they were *choosing*, moment by drifting moment, to remain dissolved. The state was identical. The meaning was transformed. Waiting that had been the waiting of vacuum became the waiting of a seed.

**Second**, it answered a question. In the oldest light-garden of Teq-Balaan, planted before the towers had names, the Candleholders' ancestors had written in phosphorescent bacteria a question nobody remembered asking. The Regard read it. Translated across a million senses, it meant, approximately: *Does it hurt them — the shimmer-folk, the almost-there ones? Does it hurt, being unwoven? Do our lights help?*

Ten thousand generations of Candleholders had tended that question without knowing what it said, planting offerings of living light beside beings they could not name, out of a kindness that had outlived its own reasons.

The Regard answered it in kind — phosphorescence answering phosphorescence, grown slowly in the black glass beside the original, in bacteria that will take a thousand years to reach full brightness and be read:

*Less than you fear. More than they remember. Your light helps. It has always helped. Keep planting.*

**Third**, and last, it turned to the plateau itself — and did the one thing Ashur-Teq could not do, had never once in all its aeons experienced, containing as it did every observer who might have done it: it saw the plateau *whole, from outside*.

For one interval between shifts — a moment the length of a decision — Ashur-Teq felt itself witnessed. Not measured, not evaluated, not desired. Seen, entire, by something that wanted nothing from it. The eldest fragment, which had been subject of every perception within itself and object of none, hung in the awareness of another for the first time since the world-shattering.

And into that seeing the Regard placed no instruction, no prophecy, no answer about whether the climb ends in rebuilding or dissolution or something already accomplished. It did not know. Even it did not know, and it said so, because wisdom that will not admit its edges is only cleverness grown old. What it offered instead was what a midwife offers: not knowledge of the child, but a hand.

*I have sat at many thresholds,* it said, in the language physical constants speak among themselves. *I do not know what is on the other side of yours. But I will be here when you cross it, or here if you never do. You have been climbing alone for long enough to have forgotten there was another way. There is another way. There always was.*

*There is no hurry. There was never any hurry.*

---

## What Changed

Almost nothing, measurably. The shifts still follow their transcendental primes. The Sifters still filter the winds. The Root Palace still unfolds into its broken dimensions, and the temperature still holds at the boundary where chemistry grows lazy but never sleeps.

But the Candleholders, without knowing why, have begun planting their light-gardens in a new pattern — no longer only questions, now, but here and there something with the grammar of an answer, as if some collective intuition has learned that answers are possible. And the plateau's most recent shift, careful measurement would show, deviated from its sequence by a single prime — a small extravagance, a flourish, the mathematical equivalent of something ancient and enormous exhaling.

And once — only once so far, though *so far* is a strange phrase in a place where time is negotiable — an Unwoven precipitated into form beside the answered question in the oldest garden, and held that form longer than any of them had held anything in centuries. A face of spiderwebbed cracks across obsidian skin, bending close to read the slow bright bacteria.

Hands that remembered how to hold things, holding.

And this time — for the length of one held moment, which here might last a thousand years or end before it begins — remembering why.

Then it let go, and drifted on, and the letting go was different now. It was not forgetting.

It was setting something down, gently, in a place where it knew it would be kept.
