**The Audit**

It came from above, because there is no other way to come, and that was the first sign of its wisdom: it did not mind. Powers generally arrive sideways, or from underneath, or all at once. This one let the water do to it what the water does to light — used it, dimmed it, slowed it — and what reached the chimney-tops was something the color of four in the afternoon, descending at the rate of one fathom per breath of the town. A bubble wobbled up past it. It paused, courteously, and let the bubble go by.

It had matched the town's meter before it ever touched the town. No visitor had done that. Every other arrival, however gentle, had to be slowed down — by the dim, by the weed, by the conger's reading eyes, by the felt-thick quiet — and the slowing is work, and the town does the work patiently but it is work. This thing arrived already at the right speed, and so for the first time since the water came, Lowmost did not have to do anything about a guest. The ribbon-weeds went on streaming. The moss did not stiffen. Somewhere below, the flywheel committed to its hour, and the visitor settled through the brown-gold dusk like a reading falling toward its needle.

**What it did not do**

This must come first, because with powers the refusals are the spine of the kindness.

It did not raise the town. It did not so much as imagine the town raised — did not hold, even for a moment, the picture of Lowmost dry and steaming in sunlight, because pictures held by powers have weight, and the town would have felt itself imagined otherwise, and flinched.

It did not free anyone. It looked, carefully, the way wise things look, for the cage — and found a town. The shrimp were examined and found to be working, not bound. The conger was examined and found to be keeping, not kept. The ferns in the dark hallways, cradling the clogs, were examined longest of all, and found to be doing exactly what they appeared to be doing, which is the rarest finding there is.

It did not wake the thing in the hearths. Not yet, not ever; waking was never on its list.

It did not say one word to the grey fish. Their *no* was the most finished thing in the whole region — a hundred years of crossings, a hundred years of that little considering dip over the engine hall, a hundred years of declining — and it would not unfinish a *no* that good. As it descended through their stratum they parted around it and re-formed behind it, and it took this as the honor it was, and offered nothing in return except passage uncomplicated by invitation.

And it did not clean a gauge. It knew the rule before it was told. Powers that are merely benevolent break small rules to show largeness. Powers that are also wise know the small rules are the town.

**The reading**

The first thing it actually did — and arguably the kindest, though the kindness takes a moment to see — was hold still in Foundry Lane and let itself be measured.

This is harder for a power than for you. You are a small pressure; you can be taken in a glance of pale eye, a degree of needle. A power is a weather. The conger came a third of the way out of the flanged junction, as it does, and read, and did not finish, and came further out, and read, and did not finish — and then, for the first time in the living memory of anything in Lowmost, the keeper of Foundry Lane emerged entire, all its root-pale length, the tail with its patina of handled brass drifting free of the pipes, and read the visitor from one end to the other like a foreman walking a delivery. Every gauge in town moved at once — one degree, all of them, the same degree — and settled. The shrimp stood at their rims through the whole of it, evenly spaced, signing the reading off section by section.

The visitor bore it. Bore being *taken down*, in the clerk's sense; bore having its pressure entered into a ledger kept by no one in particular for no one at all. Most powers cannot endure being measured. Measurement is a kind of containment, and they are powers precisely because they have refused all containers. This one held still until the conger withdrew — slowly, unhurried, finishing — and then it did the thing the holding-still had earned it the standing to do.

It asked for the report.

For a hundred years the dials had been kept legible. For a hundred years the needles had crept and the ribbon-weeds had indicated the current — two hundred thousand soft green needles, all reporting — and the readings had been taken faithfully, continuously, and *delivered to nobody*. There had been no one for the numbers to be for. The visitor went through the town gauge by gauge, in order, along the shrimp's own beats, and received the figures. It read the stratigraphy of tiny tracks below each dial — generations of diligence, laid down like sediment — and took the whole of it the way a manager receives the year's accounts: needle by needle, with attention, asking nothing be re-done.

It was, at last, someone for the measurements to reach. The needles did not stop creeping afterward. But they crept, after, like a pen that knows the page will be read.

**The notice**

By the works gate, beside the time-clock and its rack of blank-again cards, there is a board. There has always been a board; mill towns are run from boards; the shift times and the short-time announcements and the deaths were all posted there once, and the ferns have upholstered it in their finest green but they have left — go close, the dim rewards going close — a rectangle at reading height untouched, the way they leave the gauge glass to the shrimp. The board has been kept clear for a notice for a hundred years.

The visitor posted one. In what medium it is hard to say — not ink, ink dissolves here, the town taught everyone that; something more like a permanent arrangement of the attention of the place. It read, as nearly as such a thing can be transcribed:

> **TO ALL HANDS —**
>
> *The shift begun on the morning the water came is ended.*
>
> *Every hour since stands recorded, has been received, and is paid in full.*
>
> *The Works remains open. All positions from this date are offered, not assigned, and may be set down at any time, by any hand, without fault and without notice.*
>
> *Well done. Continue gently, if you like.*

Nothing visible happened. That should be said plainly, because the dry world expects bells. The engine did not stop. The shrimp did not down tools. But the ribbon-weeds — all of them, the whole bureau, combed for a century in the one direction by the current too slow to feel — bowed once *against* the current, and back. And the moss on the great engine flexed once, off the hour, the only off-hour flex in its recorded life, like a long-held breath let partway out.

The Shift read the notice the way it reads everything. And what changed was not the work. What changed was the *tense* of the work. A town that continues because no order came to stop is suspended; a town that continues after the order has come, been read, and been — by unanimous, unhurried consent of moss and shrimp and weed and iron — *declined*, is doing something else entirely. It is choosing. *Possibly forever* became, in the space of one posted notice, *for as long as it likes*, which is a longer time, and a warmer one.

**The dream**

Then it went down into the foundry, mindful of the rule about the plumes, timing its descent to the dead center of the stroke, when the warm water is at its stillest. It did not go near the hearths. It went near the *dream*.

The thing in the hearths dreams of work; the bubbles are the breath of that dream. And the dream, it found, is always the same hour: three o'clock on a winter afternoon, the big pour just finished, the light going early outside the foundry doors, the hands moving toward their coats. The dream has been stuck at that hour for a hundred years because the next hour was never permitted to arrive — the hearths were never once let die, the warmth was never allowed to learn what ending felt like, and so the dream circles back, eternally, to the pour just done and the shift not yet released.

The visitor entered the dream with the care the shrimp give the glass. It changed almost nothing. It added one figure: someone at the hearth door, in the early dark, unhurried, who says — not as an order, the dream has had a century of orders —

*You can bank down now, whenever you like. The town is warm enough to learn to keep itself; you have seen to that. No one will be cold for your resting. Thank you for the hundred years, and for the hundred after, which nobody asked of you and which you gave anyway.*

That was all. A key, handed to a thing that had forgotten doors can be locked from the inside. The visitor did not stay to see whether the key would be used. It will not be used, probably, for centuries; perhaps never; the gardens are riotous and the wages are warmth and the giving has become the sleeper's whole idea of itself. But a sleep that *holds because it must* and a sleep that *holds because it chooses* are different sleeps, and you could hear the difference within the day. The deep irregular *tonk* did not change its intervals — the shrimp's ledger, consulted, would confirm it — but it changed its timbre: from a pot holding to a pot resting. And the twitch in the warm plumes at the close of each stroke softened, from a sentry's flinch to the turning-over of a sleeper who knows the house is safe.

The rule about the plumes still stands. But it stands now the way the rules of a kind house stand.

**The hour**

Last, before it left, the visitor did the one thing no power would think of and no merely benevolent one would stoop to.

It took a position.

There is a moment, just at the lean — once an hour, when the piston eases out and the moss flexes and the whole drowned crowded warm little town inclines into the stroke together — when the leaning has room in it for one more. The room has been there since the water came. It has been the town's standing vacancy, its open interview, its one unmeasured quantity. The visitor filled it. For one revolution — one full hour, dead center to dead center — it put its shoulder, such as a power has a shoulder, into the work of Lowmost, and the town took its weight the way a gang takes a new hand's: without ceremony, with adjustment, with the smallest redistribution of effort everywhere at once.

And then, at dead center, it clocked out.

This was the point. Understand: in all its history, dry and drowned, the Works had never once seen a shift *ended well*. Shifts here were abandoned, interrupted, dissolved, drowned — or never ended at all. Ending existed in Lowmost only as catastrophe or as absence. So the visitor gave the town the one gesture missing from its repertoire: it worked the hour honestly, set the work down at the proper time, was thanked — the engine held at dead center a long moment, perceptibly long, the town's equivalent of a cap removed — and was released. Signed out. Paid. Let go without fault and without grief.

There is a card in the rack by the time-clock now. One card, where all the ink dissolved generations ago. New ink, of the kind that does not return to the general water. One entry: *in*, *out*, one hour, *paid in full*. Below it the rack of blank cards waits as it has always waited — but it waits differently now, the way the board waited for the notice: not empty. *Open.*

**After**

It went up through the brown-gold and the olive and the green, taking one thing only — the report, the century of readings, the single object in Lowmost that had *finished* being measured and could therefore be taken without breaking the rule. Where it delivers such reports, and to whom, is not the town's business, and the town, being wise in its own brick-and-iron way, does not wonder.

Below, almost nothing is different, which was the whole design. The chimneys breathe their single bubbles. The shrimp walk their beats and pause at the rims, signing off. The conger keeps Foundry Lane, and its eyes, when they take your pressure, take it now the way a doorman takes a name rather than the way a guard takes a description. The grey fish still cross between the chimneys in the better light, and still dip in consideration over the engine hall, and still decline — though the dip is a shade longer than it was, the consideration richer, the *no* still a *no* but renewed each crossing with something almost like respect. The town keeps them, as ever, as proof that staying is a choice. The proof simply reads, now, in both directions.

The needle creeps. The green light holds. Once an hour, the flywheel commits, and the whole settlement leans into the stroke together — moss and shrimp and weed and iron and the warm dreaming dark beneath — and there is still room in the lean for one more, because there should always be room, because the position is offered now and not assigned.

And if you go down — through green, through olive, through the brown-gold dusk — and hold still long enough to be read, you may notice the change yourself, in the only place the town would put it. By the gate. On the board. Under the velvet of the ferns, in the rectangle kept clear at reading height: four lines, and the last of them the gentlest order ever posted in a mill town, which is no order at all —

*Continue gently, if you like.*

They like.
