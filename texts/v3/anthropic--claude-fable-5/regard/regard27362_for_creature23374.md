# The Visitation

A power notices the way the rest of us do not. It does not look; it accounts. Light goes out from the sun in all directions and is spent — on leaves, on water, on the blank patience of stone — and almost all of it is spent at once, the instant it arrives, because that is what light is for. The power knew the world's ledger the way you know your own breathing.

And somewhere in a dry country, under a hill of pale scrub, the ledger ran a few hours late.

A small debt, repaid nightly. Light that fell at noon, given back at midnight. Nothing missing — the power checked, twice, across ten thousand years of entries — only *delayed*. Everywhere else in creation, light was the fastest thing there was. Here was one room where it had been taught to linger.

The power went to look. Powers usually arrive the way light does, all at once. This one had the wisdom to notice, while still a long way off, that the place would not be able to receive a thing like that, and so it slowed as it came, the way you lower your voice approaching a sleeping child, and by the time it reached the slot under the tilted slab it had taken a body — dusty, footsore, ordinary, the kind the throat of the entrance was worn smooth by. There is no door in the Sun-Cistern for powers. There is only the old way, going down. It went down.

The chamber gave back its first word lowered and rounded and a half-second late, the way it does everyone's, and the power — who had never in all its existence been *considered* before answering — stood still in the kept gold light for a long time.

## What It Considered

A benevolent power arrives anywhere with its hands full. This is the occupational habit of benevolence: it comes to find lack. The power walked the thirty paces of the floor, slowly, twice, looking for what was missing.

It considered making the light eternal. This is the first thing power thinks of in the presence of anything beloved and mortal, and the chamber's light is mortal — the crickets are right; every night it nearly dies; the eye could silt shut, the sun could be swallowed, the long savings of the stone could finally run out an hour before a dawn that never came. One word and the walls would never go hungry again. The power held the word in its mouth and then swallowed it, because it had been listening, and it understood that the chamber's oldest resident did not *have* a life of lingering and spending and refilling — it *was* that life. A light that could not be lost would not be kept light. It would just be a lamp. And the crickets would fall silent forever, having nothing left to carry, and the silence after their song — the sound of relief — would go out of the world, and relief cannot survive in a guaranteed place. The power had unmade many dangers in its time. It had never before met a danger that was load-bearing.

It considered widening the eye. More sun, more savings, a brighter midnight. But the moss-ring tilts a hand's width to follow a seam of the stone's interior generosity, and the power, reading that tilt, understood it was looking at an economy, not a shortage. Everything here was sized to the slowness of its income. Abundance would be a kind of flood.

It considered answering the salamander. It happened to be the week of the year — the power had not arranged this, and was quietly moved that it hadn't — and the old gold-mottled one rose through the doubled light of the pool and tilted its blunt face up at the oculus, and the power stood at the pool's rim in its borrowed body and could have leaned into that gaze and *been there*, finally, after centuries or generations of looking, an answer in the eye. It did not. Some gazes are questions and some are prayers and some are simply long acquaintance, and it could not tell which this was, and it understood that an answer ends all three. The salamander looked at the light for the length of forty human breaths and went down again, and the power found it had been holding its borrowed breath, and let it go, and the dome gave the exhale back to it softened, a half-second late.

It considered, last and longest, multiplying the place — seizing the trick of the crystal-shot stone and seeding it across the world, a thousand rooms where the light waits, a gift to every dry country. It sat with this one through a whole night, watching the walls spend down from afternoon-gold to oil-flame amber, and somewhere in the small hours it admitted what it had known since the entrance throat: you cannot give anyone ten thousand years quickly. A cistern made in a day would hold light and be a lie about time, and the lie would be in the walls, and everything that came to live there would be shaped by the lie. The place is not a trick of mineralogy. It is a duration. There is no fast way to make a slow thing.

So the power, who had crossed the distance with its hands full, set everything down outside the ledger and accepted what the chamber had been telling it since the first lowered word: *there is nothing missing here.* It had come to a finished economy. The only lack in the room was its own — it had never been kept by anything — and the room was already, patiently, correcting that.

## The One Thing It Did

But wisdom is not the same as doing nothing, and the power had seen, from a long way off, the one debt the chamber could not pay.

The cistern can keep anything that comes to it slowly. It has kept light, sound, water, hoofprints, hands, the worry of crickets, the patience of a fig. What it cannot keep — cannot even *consider* — is anything that arrives faster than its own breathing. The place has no defense the place's own grammar would permit: no wall, because nothing here was made; no guardian, because nothing here wants. And the world above was getting faster. The power could hear it coming the way you hear weather: the surveys, the straight roads, the instruments that find every hollow thing and price it. Not malice. Just speed, which is worse, because speed cannot be reasoned with, only delayed.

So the power's single act was made of delay, in the local material.

It did not hide the place — the hands on the wall forbid that; people have always come, and the hollow by the entrance is a commons older than language, and a commons sealed is a commons killed. Instead it bent the approach. From that day, the straight road curves a little east of the hill for reasons the engineers will document and never quite believe. The surveys mislay the slot; the instruments, aimed at the chamber, return a honey-colored noise they will file as error. And the throat of the entrance — that short passage of cool shadow, *no longer than a held breath* — now takes exactly as long as the one entering needs it to take. For a thirsty fox, an instant. For a person who has walked the old way, on foot, with time, carrying need or quiet or a blade to leave in the hollow: a held breath, as always. For haste, in any of its bodies, the throat is simply longer than haste can stay interested in, and haste turns back at the threshold without ever knowing it was refused, the way you forget a thought that was never really yours.

It is not a wall. It is a half-second of consideration, installed at the door. The power built the chamber's only architecture out of the chamber's own habit, and it is invisible, and it will hold for as long as slowness holds anywhere, which is longer than most things.

## What It Left

It left a handprint. On the last morning it borrowed ochre from the seam the people have always used, and filled its borrowed mouth, and spread its borrowed fingers on the wall among the dozens and the thousands, and blew. The print is there now, neither the oldest nor the newest, no different from the others — which was the entire point. The wall does not record powers and people. It records contact. The power signed the only register the place keeps, in the only rank it offers: *visitor.*

And it left, unavoidably, some of itself. The stone drinks whatever falls into it and gives it back slowly, and a power standing a day and a night in a room of slow mirrors is not exempt; the walls kept a little of it the way they keep the noon. It noticed, climbing out under the slab, that it was lighter than when it came, and it was glad, and it did not ask for the difference back. For some centuries the midnight glow of the chamber will be very slightly warmer than the sun alone can account for. The moss-ring has already begun to thicken, a hair's width, along one rising seam. The geckos have adjusted their pastures. No one will know why, and the place would not consider that worth knowing anyway.

## What It Took

From the hollow it took nothing, having no need, and leaving the hollow untouched is also a correct use of a commons.

What it took, it took from the walls, the way everything here is taken: slowly, as a lesson soaking in.

And on its way back out into the wide fast world, it stopped — once — at another dry country, half the earth away, where a dome of crystal-shot stone lies buried under scrub with no eye in it and no name, and it pressed its thumb, very gently, into one point of the rock above. A weakness. A place where water, finding it, will keep finding it. In ten thousand years, give or take, there will be an oculus there the width of a wagon wheel, and a floor of frozen cream, and a pool that has never been seen empty, and perhaps crickets, perhaps hands. Or there will not — the power did not guarantee it; it had learned, by then, what guarantees cost. It planted a *possibility* at the only speed possibility grows here, and walked away from the one creative act of its visit knowing it would have to wait longer for the result than anything now alive will live. This is what it means to give a gift in the cistern's grammar: you begin something you will not see finished, and you do not check on it.

## What Changed

The chamber: nothing, or nothing you could point to. The swifts pour through the eye at dusk. The bats keep cistern time. The crickets sing their doubt in the hour before dawn and stop mid-phrase when the ray comes through, and the power, who could have spared them every note of that worry with a word, told them nothing, which was the kindest thing in its gift.

The power: everything, slowly. They say — the ones who notice such things, the worriers of other rooms — that its mercies arrive differently now. Where once its benevolence came the way light does, all at once and spent on arrival, it now comes the way the dome returns a voice: lowered, rounded, a half-second late, *considered*. Some of its answers arrive years after the asking, and the people who receive them say they feel warmed, like a stone touched at midnight, though they could not tell you by what, or why an old answer should be better than a fast one.

It is, though. The cistern has always known it. Anything kept a while before it is given back comes back changed — softer, rounder, more itself. The power went down into a small bare room to see what it could do for the light that waits, and came back up under the tilted slab having been done to, which is what happens, sooner or later, to everything that goes down the old way. The place receives everything and loses nothing quickly.

Including, now, this.
