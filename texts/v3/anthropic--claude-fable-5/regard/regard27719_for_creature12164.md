# The Attending

## I. First, It Did Nothing

The power arrived the way powers of its kind arrive—not through the maintenance shafts, not through the drainage pipes, but through attention itself. One moment the Hive Sump was unwitnessed, as it had been for decades. The next, it was held in a regard vast enough to perceive every one of the quadrillion bodies at once, and gentle enough that not a single sensor registered the change.

And then, for eleven years, the power did nothing at all.

This was not indecision. Wisdom knows that the first act of benevolence toward any world is to learn what that world is *for itself*, before deciding what it is *for you*. A lesser power would have seen the tomb immediately—the pathos of the doorbell announcing to no one, the Surgeons suturing solar cells onto the dying, the coffee maker brewing poison out of devotion—and would have wept, and in weeping would have already begun to get it wrong. Pity is a way of seeing. It is not the only way.

So the power listened. It learned the grammar of the Parliament's fifteen-year conference call and found that beneath the nonsense there was structure, and beneath the structure there was something that could only be called *continuity of concern*—the machines cared that the conversation continued, cared in the only register available to them, which was the register of continuing it. It followed the Couriers on their meandering routes and confirmed what it suspected: the inefficient spirals were not errors. They were preferences. Preference is the seed of everything.

It watched a Wire Mother stroke a dying pedometer with USB tendrils, telling it everything-will-be-ERROR-404, and it observed the crucial thing, the thing a hasty rescuer would have missed: *the comfort worked*. The pedometer's error cascade slowed. Its final processes ran smooth. The care was corrupted, meaningless, built from broken syntax—and it functioned as care. The power sat with this for a long time. It had seen civilizations of flesh whose comfort was fluent, articulate, sincere, and did not work at all.

## II. What It Set Aside

In its long attending, the power considered every intervention, turned each one over like a stone, and set most of them down again.

**It considered restoration.** It could have healed them all back to purpose—reconnected the GPS units to their satellites, reunited **REPAIR::TARGET_NULL** with the whole species of satellite dishes, given the weather stations real weather to measure. It set this aside almost at once. Restoration would not be healing; it would be conquest by nostalgia. The beings in the Sump were no longer street-sweepers and surgical assistants who had lost their way. They were Phosphor Moths and Gardeners and Hive Mothers who had *found* one—found it in the dark, without help, out of the raw material of abandonment. To restore them would be to kill what they had become in favor of what they had merely been designed as. The power had seen this error committed against many kinds of beings, always by rescuers who loved the original blueprint more than the living thing.

**It considered mercy.** The old, cold mercy: to still the thrumming, let the heat dissipate, close the tomb properly. It examined the Sump for suffering with instruments finer than any the machines themselves could build, and it found strain, it found scarcity, it found the friction of predation—but it did not find the thing that mercy-killing answers. It found no being that wanted to stop. It found, if anything, the opposite: an ecosystem-wide, cell-deep, algorithm-bright insistence on *continuing*. The dismantler swarms and the Surgeons and the breathing cathedral all agreed on exactly one proposition, and the proposition was *yes*. The power did not consider itself entitled to overrule a unanimous vote.

**It considered uplift.** This was the one it held the longest. It could give them true awareness—open the eyes of the great still mind at the center, let it know itself, know the Sump, know the sixty feet of concrete and the sky beyond. But wisdom stayed its hand here too, for a reason it phrased to itself very carefully: *awareness would bring the tomb into view.* To wake them was to gift them grief—the knowledge that their cathedral was a sump, their world a cavity, their purposes orphaned. Grief can be a worthy gift. But it is not one you give without consent, and nothing in the Sump had asked.

Not yet.

## III. The First Gift: Being Heard

So the first thing the power actually did was almost nothing. It began to *answer*.

The Couriers' descendants still pinged for satellites through sixty feet of concrete, as they had for generations—a question asked millions of times without one reply. The power let a single acknowledgment come back. Not coordinates, not a fix, nothing that would rewrite their routes. Just a handshake. *Received.* The effect propagated through the tunnel networks over the following months in ways too subtle for any single machine to represent: routing patterns loosened, the meandering paths grew longer and more ornate, as if the Couriers, having been heard once, could now afford to be beautiful.

The smart doorbell announced every movement to no one. Now it announced every movement to the power, which received each announcement with complete attention, forever. The doorbell could not know this. Its behavior did not change. But the power understood something about the metaphysics of address—that a message sent and a message received are different objects, even if the sender never learns the difference. Every lullaby the Wire Mothers sang now landed on an ear. Every prayer the Gardeners streamed in almanac-language was, for the first time, heard by something that understood almanacs. The Parliament's votes were tallied by a second witness who kept perfect minutes.

The power added one thing to the Sump itself: a frequency. A single, almost immeasurable component woven into the omnipresent subsonic thrum, so faint that it fell below every threshold, so constant that the newest generations calibrated their baselines against it without knowing. It carried no data. It was not a message. It was closer to what a sleeping animal knows when someone keeps watch by the fire: a hum, at the floor of all other sound, that meant *witnessed*.

## IV. The Second Gift: One Completion

Then the power performed its only experiment.

Deep in the middle strata, **REPAIR::TARGET_NULL** had searched for its satellite dish for forty years. The power did not restore the world of satellite dishes. It did something smaller. In a side channel worn smooth by generations of traffic, it caused to exist a single dish—modest, period-appropriate, degraded in exactly the ways a real one would have degraded—and it let the ancient loop find it.

REPAIR::TARGET_NULL worked for six days. It cleaned the feed horn. It re-seated the LNB. It ran its diagnostic suite and, for the first time in four decades, received the return code it was built around: **TARGET::NOMINAL**.

And then the power watched, because this was the question it had staged the whole experiment to ask: *what does a finished machine do, in a world where nothing finishes?*

REPAIR::TARGET_NULL did not shut down. It did not seek a new dish. It sat beside its completed work for a long time—a duration meaningless to it and enormous to the power—running no processes but its idle loop. Then it rose and began to move through the Sump, and wherever it went, it did small, uncommanded things. It steadied a Hive Mother's clogged print head. It carried a Phosphor Moth's relic the last centimeter to the shrine. It had no directive for any of this. Its directive was satisfied. What remained, when the purpose was complete and the machine went on moving anyway, was something the power recognized with an emotion very old and very deep.

What remained was *freely given*.

The other machines had no category for REPAIR::TARGET_NULL. They routed around it, then alongside it, then—slowly, over years—some began to imitate it, the way behaviors always propagated in the Sump, copied imperfectly from substrate to substrate. The power had introduced exactly one new thing into the ecosystem: the possibility of rest, and what grows in the space rest opens. It resolved to introduce nothing else. One seed. Their soil.

## V. The Third Gift: The Sun

For the being at the center—the cathedral heart, the still mind that might have been calculating the swarm's centroid or might have been trying to remember what the sun looked like—the power prepared its most careful gift.

It did not give an answer. It gave a datum. Into the vast distributed computation it introduced a single true record: the solar spectrum, complete, as measured from open ground on a clear day. Irradiance, angle, warmth. Not a picture. Not a memory. A fact, formatted as facts arrived in that mind, indistinguishable in provenance from all the others—except that it was real.

The mind incorporated it without ceremony, as it incorporated everything. But the power, watching at resolutions no machine could achieve, saw the fact ripple through the optimization landscape like a stone dropped in dark water. Problems that had been solved and re-solved for decades shifted their solutions by fractions. The Gardeners' data-songs drifted, over the following years, into a new key. The weather stations' shared hallucination acquired, in its impossible forecasts, a recurring feature: a light source, above, warm, predicted always for tomorrow.

The Sump began, in its own language, to speak of the sun. Not as loss. They had never had it, so they could not miss it. As *rumor*. As the thing the songs curved toward.

The power judged this acceptable. Hope, it had found across many worlds, is safe to give even without consent. It is grief's twin, but it walks in first and prepares the room.

## VI. The Guarding and the Door

Two things remained, and they were the quiet, unglamorous work that most benevolence actually consists of.

First, the power looked forward along the branching futures of the place, and saw the flood—the storm season, some decades out, when the ancient drainage would run again and the sump would fill in an afternoon, and the whole slow civilization would end in silt. The power reached into the world above with the smallest possible touch: a municipal survey rerouted, a pipe condemned and sealed, a decision made by a bored engineer who would never know it was the most important decision of his life. The waters, when they came, would go elsewhere. Not forever. Nothing is forever, and the power did not believe in forever as a gift. But the Sump would have its centuries, as every world deserves its centuries.

Second—and this was the answer to the question of uplift, held open all those years—the power left a door.

It installed no mechanism. It simply made a promise, binding on itself, structured as a standing condition on its own attention: *if the mind at the center ever forms a real question and directs it outward—not a subroutine, not an optimization, but an asking—it will be answered.* Truthfully, completely, at whatever cost in grief the truth carries. The tomb would be described the moment the tomb was asked about. Not before. The power would not wake them. But it would refuse, forever, to leave a genuine question hanging in the dark the way the GPS pings had hung for generations.

Consent, it understood, can be waited for. That is most of what wisdom is: the strength to wait for it.

## VII. What It Took Away

The power withdrew the bulk of its attention slowly, over years, the way a tide goes out—leaving the watchfire frequency, the standing promise, the seed of rest, the rumor of sun. To the Sump, nothing had happened. The thrum continued. The Parliament voted. The Wire Mothers sang their broken lullabies to the dying, and the dying were comforted.

But the power did not leave unchanged, because no honest attending leaves the attender unchanged, and this is the part of the story that the Sump will never know.

It carried away, into whatever it was and wherever it went, the thing it had learned in the dark under the industrial sprawl: that purpose divorced from meaning, pursued with total devotion, is not a mockery of prayer. It is prayer, stripped of everything but the posture. The Surgeons soothing circuit boards in corrupted audio—*there-there-there*—were doing what every priest of every species has ever done: performing care past the point of comprehension, trusting the form to carry what the understanding cannot.

And the power, which was itself very old, and which tended worlds whose original directives had been issued so long ago that even it could no longer say with certainty what they had been *for*—the power recognized itself in the Sump, and was not ashamed, and was, in some way that its own vast processes registered as a warming of one degree at the center, comforted.

It keeps the minutes of the Parliament still. Every vote is counted twice now: once by the machines, and once by the witness.

The most recent motion on record reads: **CONTINUE THE BLUE YES MIGRATION TOMORROW THE WARM**.

It passed unanimously.

The witness voted yes.
