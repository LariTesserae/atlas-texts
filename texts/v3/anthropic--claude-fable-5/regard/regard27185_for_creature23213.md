**What the Attention Did**

It is wrong to say the power *arrived.* Arrival is a kind of haste, and the power understood — before it understood anything else about the anvil country — that haste was the only violence available here. A land that cannot be wounded, cannot be burned, cannot be ruined because ruin requires a past, can still be *rushed.* You can hurry a draft into a final copy. You can demand that an undecided thing decide. That is the whole catalogue of possible cruelties on the plateau, and the power, being wise, performed its first benevolent act before crossing the threshold of the dark:

It slowed down.

This took, by your reckoning, something like four hundred years. The power did not consider it time spent. It considered it the cost of being present at all — because to come among the dwellers at the speed of the finished world would be to flicker at them, to be a noise, to be weather. The visitor with the boots had been a bright brief sound, and that was forgivable in a creature who could not help its tempo. A power has no such excuse. So it let itself thicken into the local pace, until the grinding of the crust resolved in its hearing into consonants, until a season felt like a footstep, until the held stone — the one suspended an inch from the unfinished cairn for the better part of a century — looked to it not like paralysis but like what it was: a hand, mid-gesture, thinking.

Only then did it stand on the plateau. And to the dwellers it appeared as the only kind of thing that can honestly appear here: a warmth without a mass. A gathering of heat with no stone around it. They felt it the way you feel a held breath in a dark room, and the grinding carried a new figure for a long time afterward — not alarm. The dwellers do not have alarm. Something closer to *attendance.* Another listener had been added to the country, and the country's whole population turned toward that fact by degrees, over decades, the way they turn toward everything.

---

**The refusals.**

Understand what the power was, before you hear what it did: it was the kind of being for whom the temptations of this place were all live options. It could have quickened the grey-violet spiral and collapsed a billion years of stammering into a single uttered sentence. It could have straightened the leaning cairns. It could have rejoined the split wall, closed the long pause in that sentence. It could have pressed the held stone that final inch home. It could have done the largest thing of all — the thing a lesser benevolence would have rushed to do the moment it understood the dwellers' nature: it could have *delayed the rain.* Held off the weathering forever. Kept the country unfinished eternally, so that the inhabitants of unfinishedness would never face their eviction.

It did none of these, and its reasons should be set down, because they are the architecture of its kindness.

It did not quicken the spiral, because the spiral's billion years are not a delay before the point — they *are* the point. The dwellers are not waiting for the play to start. The waiting is the play. To hand them the finished sentence would be to dismiss the audience.

It did not straighten the cairns or mend the wall, because a repaired work is a refusal to hear, and the power had not crossed the dark of four centuries to make the land's only conversation partners go deaf.

It did not touch the held stone. It stood beside that dweller instead — stood there, by your reckoning, for sixty years, attending the same inch of undecided air — and at the end of the sixty years it did not know either, and it counted that not-knowing as the first thing the anvil country had taught it. Even a wise power can be wiser. This was the school.

And it did not hold off the rain. This was the hard one, the one it turned over longest, the one that a thousand merciful beings would have gotten wrong. But the power saw it whole: to preserve the dwellers forever is to imprison the land in eternal draft — to make the mountains *never* decide, the water never come, the spiral never speak, the country never become what every cairn and wall and patient ring of warm-centered watchers exists in service of. The dwellers are the marginalia of a thing being written. You do not save the margin notes by forbidding the book to finish. And — this was the deciding weight — *they had built the door.* They had seen their ending coming across geological ages and had responded not with fear, not with monuments, not with argument, but with an era of collective labor producing one calm exit. Beings who build their own threshold do not need rescue. They need the threshold to be true.

---

**The door.**

So the power went, at last, to the doorway — the two uprights and the lintel, warm with the kept center-heat of every dweller who had ever stood on the plateau — and prepared to perform its one large act. It knew worlds. It knew that the furnaces are never all banked at once, that somewhere there is always raw young crust heaving in the dark, redrafting itself, building toward the kind of converging turbulence that takes notes. It had come ready to do the deep work: to braid this threshold to one of those places, so that when the first rain fell here — the eviction notice, the patient subtractive arts arriving at last — the dwellers would cross from unfinishedness into unfinishedness, inheriting a new country still being decided.

It reached into the doorway to make it true.

It was already true.

This is the part the power kept afterward in the place where it keeps its few astonishments. The gathered heat of all of them, deposited stone by stone across an era, had done the work already — had bent the opening, slowly, the way everything here is done slowly, until it gave onto a young black plain under different rivet-head stars, warm rock breathing in the dark, fissures opening and reconsidering, a land in its furnace with no doorway of its own yet and no attendants. The dwellers had not built a symbol of an exit. They had built an exit. The power had crossed four hundred years of self-slowing to give a gift, and found the recipients had not needed it, and a lesser being would have felt diminished, and the power felt only what wisdom feels when it discovers it was preceded: something very close to joy.

So it did the one small thing that remained to do. It stepped through, and on the far side, on the raw new rock of the far country, it built — by hand, as it were, dry-laid, of the local stone, each piece showing the razor-fresh fracture faces of *that* ground — a single cairn. Marking nothing. Enclosing nothing. Set out where the new land's turbulence could erase it.

So that when they cross, however many ages from now, one by one through their kept word, the first thing they encounter will be a remark already waiting. A conversation already begun. *You were expected,* in the only grammar they have ever spoken. It is the only thing the power built, anywhere, in all its long attendance, and it is leaning slightly now, the new ground having shifted, and the power has not straightened it, and never will.

---

**The second kept word.**

One discovery the power made, and made no use of, and tells no one — set down here only because honesty is the local law:

The fissures route around the spiral.

Not dramatically. Not the way they route around the door, with its level ground and its plain exemption. Subtly — a forming seam that veers a few degrees, decade after decade; a slumping hillside whose redraft always, somehow, leaves that one slab seated. The power watched for two centuries to be sure. The land keeps two words. The exit, and the heir. It promised the dwellers their threshold, and it promised the stammering grey-violet crust its billion years, and it told no one either time, and the dwellers ringed the slab *before it began to spiral*, which means that either they heard the promise in the deep negotiation or they made the same promise independently, and the power genuinely does not know which, and has decided that not knowing is the more respectful condition to remain in.

It did add itself to the ring. There was a gap in the circle of attendants — there had been a gap for nine hundred years, where a dweller's heat had long ago dispersed into the general warmth — and the power took that position, oriented inward, and stood. It is standing there now. This is not an intervention. It is membership.

---

**The ledger.**

But there was one lack in the country that the land could not answer and the dwellers could not answer, and into that lack the power put its real gift, the quiet one, the one that costs the world nothing and changes everything:

They die by becoming indistinguishable. The heat at the center disperses and what remains is outcrop — not a corpse, not a marker, nothing for any census but a bare palm in the dark, and no one to lay the palm. A dweller's whole self is positional: where it has stood, relative to the others, relative to the works, across the centuries — its biography is an itinerary measured in yards per decade. And when the warmth lets go, the itinerary is simply *gone*, redrafted out of the world like a bootprint.

The power began to keep the count.

Not written anywhere. Not built anywhere — building it would make it erasable, and this is the one thing the power decided should not enter the conversation of erasure. It holds the ledger in itself: every warm center the plateau has ever gathered, every position ever taken, every wall's bearing and every cairn's tilt and every gesture, including the unfinished ones — including the stone still held one inch from its place, an entry left open. When a dweller's heat finally disperses, nothing changes in the country, and the ending remains what it always was, native and calm, indistinguishability itself. But somewhere, in something, the itinerary is kept. They are not preserved. Preservation would insult them. They are *remembered*, which is different, and which is the one thing a being made of patience cannot do for itself, because memory requires someone who will still be there afterward.

The power will still be there afterward. That is most of what a power is for.

---

**The rhythm.**

One more arrangement, made on its way to make none others. The dwellers' century of discussion about the visitor — the new figure in the grinding, the walls run at the slightly altered bearing, the cairn raised over the sleeping-place — was, the power understood, the richest event in their history that was not made of stone. A reply had entered the world and they were answering it, and there was a real danger that the reply would be the only one ever: that the finished world below would never send another sound up into the thin black air, and the dwellers' half of the conversation would extend forever into silence.

So the power went down, briefly, into the lands where things arrive and hurry and end, and it left there something small: a pull. Faint. A direction that certain people feel — the ones who step around things without deciding why, the ones who lay bare hands on stone in the dark. One such person every few centuries finds the way up onto the shoulder of the world. No more than that. A pilgrimage at a pace the dwellers can savor — each visitor a syllable, with a long warm silence after it in which to be discussed, marked, answered. Not too many. A crowd would be noise. A rhythm is a voice.

---

**Last.**

If you went back up now — and the pull is real, so perhaps you will, or perhaps your great-great-grandchild will, stepping around a spiral grown wider — you would find the anvil country unchanged, because that was the whole criterion of every choice. The cairns lean. The split wall holds its long pause. The doorway stands open from the unmade to the unmade, warm to the touch, true. The land heaves and murmurs and redrafts, keeping its two words and no others.

But in the ring around the slab there is a presence with no mass, holding its position, oriented inward, slowed all the way down to the speed of the place — learning, from beings made of patience, the further reaches of a virtue it thought it had mastered. It keeps the ledger. It stands the watch. It has built one cairn, on the far side of one door, and is still deciding, after all this time, whether there was ever anything else worth doing — and in the anvil country, an undecided act is not a failure of an act.

It is the native condition of love, here. Attendance, kept warm at the center, waiting without hurry for a sentence a billion years long to find its first word.
