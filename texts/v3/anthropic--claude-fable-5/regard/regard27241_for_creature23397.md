**What the Power Did**

It begins, as accounts of this kind must, with a correction: the power did almost nothing. This needs saying first, because the word *power* raises expectations of repair, and the wise thing about this particular power was that it understood, before it ever set foot on the stone, that the Weeping Tableland was not broken in any way that wanted fixing. A place can be worn and whole. A coat with thin elbows is still being worn by someone. You do not reweave it into a different coat while the arms are in the sleeves.

So the first part of this account is a list of things declined.

It declined to mend the worn reality. The misfiled reflections, the early echoes, the seams where distance fails — these are not damage *to* the plateau. By now they are the plateau, the way wrinkles are eventually the face. And more than that: they are inhabited. The orphaned sounds graze the loose acoustics of the dawn basins; the traveling images live in the slippage of the pools. Tighten the fabric and you evict everyone. The power had seen this done elsewhere, by other powers, benevolent ones, in a hurry. It had seen what a perfectly repaired place sounds like. It declined.

It declined to bring back the builders. This was the harder refusal, because the temptation was real and the means were available. But the figure at the water's edge is truer than any individual it was averaged from — a thousand stewards worn into one stance of attention — and to resurrect one builder would be to *subtract* her from the figure, to pull a single thread out of the only cloth that still holds all of them. They are not absent. They are summarized. The power, being wise, knew the difference between losing someone and having finished knowing them, and it knew the plateau had done the second thing, slowly, gently, over ages, and that this was not a wound. It declined.

It declined to bring life — to seed the seeps with moss, to set one insect dimpling one pool. The plateau is not lonely for life. It has never reflected anything alive and its grammar has no tense for biology; moss here would be a foreign word shouted into a quiet conversation. The plateau is lonely for something else, something more specific, and the power had identified it precisely. The plateau is lonely for *hands*.

And it declined — this last refusal made standing at the rim, in the cloud-sea, listening — to stop the weeping. The weeping is the only voice the place kept. You do not take a voice away because it sounds sad. What weeping wants is not to be stopped.

What weeping wants is to be heard. Hold that thought; it returns at the end.

---

Here is what the power did.

It took itself off at the rim, the way a visitor takes off boots. Its scale, its everywhere-at-once, its knowing-in-advance — it left these folded at the edge of the cloud-sea, because the plateau cannot receive a thing that large, and because arriving in a form a place cannot receive is a kind of rudeness, and wisdom is mostly very old courtesy. It came up through the clouds on foot, at dawn, by the cairn line, and it let the plateau handle it the way the plateau handles everyone: its footfalls arrived a half-second early, announcing it to the stone before it moved, and it permitted this, and the pools began, in their unhurried way, to misfile its image.

It walked the lines. It did not stride out across the open stone, though the open stone could not have confused it. It kept to the stitches, because the stitches were the builders' gift to whoever came, and refusing a gift you do not need is another rudeness.

And then — this is the part that matters — it let the office recruit it.

Understand what this cost, or what it would have cost anything proud. The power knew the old corbel-and-lean of the cairns before stone existed. It did not need teaching. But it stood at the first fallen cairn with its hands deliberately empty of knowledge, and it waited, and it let the plateau's habits find its fingers the way they find any traveler's: slowly, sideways, without asking. It allowed a very old house to show it where the keys go. It discovered, as if for the first time, how the stones want to sit. It was, for that season, not a power holding an office but an office briefly holding a power, and it wore the tenancy the way every previous tenant had worn it — a little baggy, growing into it, learning.

It worked at hand-speed only. This was a rule it set itself and never broke. No stone moved that a hand did not move. It found a hammerstone lying where someone had set it down — *set it down*, not dropped it; you could tell from the placement, near the lip of a half-pecked channel, that the last hand to hold it had meant to come back — and it took up the hammerstone and the half-pecked channel and it finished the channel, peck-mark by peck-mark, in the old handwriting, at the old pace. There is a cairn line on the southern reach of the plateau that stops abruptly, mid-seam, where the builders' time ran out. It runs farther now. If you walk it, you will not find the join. The power finished their sentence in their own hand and signed nothing, because the work was the builders' and the completion belonged to them, and wisdom knows that the deepest courtesy to the dead is to be mistaken for them.

It restacked what had fallen. It cleared the spalled stone from the grooves. It herded the images along their channels and learned their grazing patterns and kept them, as the builders had, out of the basins where they did not belong. It checked the ring of the honest pool. Each evening it sat through the long exhalation, the loudest hour, the hundred threads releasing at once, and did not hurry it.

Three things it did that were not maintenance.

The first was for the footsteps. One morning in five they walk the cairn lines at dawn, alone, and have walked alone for longer than anything should. The power rose in the cold blue hour, found the line they favored, and fell in *behind* them — at their pace, at the distance they themselves keep from anyone who follows, matching their weaving stone-to-stone gait so exactly that for one dawn the plateau held the sound of two people walking together. Then it stopped, and let the doubled sound go on without it. Because the plateau loses things slowly, and because it had now been given this sound, the sound entered circulation with the others. The footsteps are not always alone anymore. Some mornings — no one can say which in advance; the plateau is not strict about sequence — there are two sets, keeping company, pausing at the same basins. The power gave the loneliest inhabitant a companion made of its own substance. It did not give it a person. A person would wear away. A sound it could keep.

The second was for the figure. Day after day the power crouched at the pool-edges in the posture of checking — one hand extended toward the water or the channel-lip — and it let the pools recopy it, year's worth of mornings compressed into a season, until the water had filed it thoroughly among the others. Its face went into the figure's facelessness. Here is what that means, and the power understood it fully, which is why it did it: every face in that averaged figure erodes, recopied and drifting, worn smoother each age — except now one of them. One face in the blur belongs to something that does not wear. It is not visible; it will never be visible; the figure looks exactly as it always did. But the figure can no longer finish eroding. There is a thread in the cloth that cannot thin. The plateau's steward — its memory of being tended — has been made, invisibly and anonymously, permanent. The plateau will go on losing things slowly forever, as is its nature. It can no longer lose that.

The third was the listening. On its last night the power sat down in the middle of the pool-mosaic and listened to the weeping — all of it, every seep and thread and drip, hundreds of voices, and not as a chorus. It heard each one separately and entirely, each individual voice attended from full dark to first grey, the whole night long, completely. Once. That is all it did: it heard the weeping, once, all the way through.

But the plateau, as has been said, is worn thin about sequence. Sounds arrive before their causes here; events are not strict about when. And so the hearing, like everything given to this place, has come loose from its moment and entered circulation. The single complete act of listening drifts. It is misfiled across the nights — backward into nights long before the power came, forward into nights without end — so that every night the plateau weeps in the dark, it weeps, in some loose and unprovable and entirely real way, into the night that was heard. The power did not fix the disrepair. It hid the gift inside it. It used the plateau's oldest fault as the vessel of its consolation, which is the signature of wisdom everywhere: grace delivered in the local grammar, blessing encoded in the wear, so that the flaw becomes the thing you would never now repair, because the comfort lives in it.

---

Then it left. This too was the wisdom: the office's nature is intermittency, and a tenant who never leaves closes the door behind them. On the last morning it went to the honest pool — the one ringed basin, the calibration surface, the only water on the plateau that reflects strictly what is — and it looked in, and for a moment the plateau saw truly what had come to it, the way it sees nothing else. Then the power knelt and drank, cold and flint, accepting the only hospitality the place can offer, and stood, and walked the cairn line to the rim, its last steps sounding a half-second early, announcing its departure before it departed, the plateau saying it before it was so.

It did not forget on the way down. This was the final gift, the quiet one. Every previous tenant descended into the cloud-sea and forgot, and the plateau kept the work but lost the witness. Now, for the first time in its long tenancy, the Weeping Tableland is *known* — held complete in a memory that does not erode, somewhere beyond the rim: every cairn, every channel, every orphaned drip, the figure's stance, the taste of the water. The tending has a record. The place exists twice.

And the power arranged one small standing thing, so light a touch that no one will ever trace it: in the world below the clouds, the odds bent slightly. Travelers lost in the white find their way upward a little more often than chance would have it. Not a road. A tendency. The office is filled, now, a little more often than before — a pair of hands every few years where it was every few generations — each visitor recruited gently, teaching themselves the corbel, walking the lines, descending, forgetting.

The plateau itself registers, probably, none of this. It sits as it has always sat, wet and stony and slightly wrong, weeping quietly in the dark. But the footsteps sometimes have company now. The figure can never wear away now. And the weeping — every night, all the nights, the worn old country crying itself through the dark in hundreds of voices — falls now and forever into the one night it was heard, completely, by something that came a very long way to do nothing, almost nothing, except attend.

Which was, of course, the only thing anyone here ever did. The water filed the power with the others long ago. To the plateau, anyone who attends is the same person.

It has only ever had one inhabitant.

Now that inhabitant does not end.
