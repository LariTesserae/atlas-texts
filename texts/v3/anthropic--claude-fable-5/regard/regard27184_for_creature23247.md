**The Reader**

A draft, given enough time, attracts a reader. This is not a law of nature, because the room is upstream of nature, but it is a law of work: anything made with sustained attention begins, eventually, to emit attention's frequency, and somewhere, sometime, something attuned to that frequency turns its head.

So it was noticed. Not by accident — the seams in hillsides and gaps behind root balls are for the small finished creatures, and the power that turned its head toward the Drafting Room was neither small nor, in the usual sense, finished or unfinished. Call it old enough that the distinction had stopped applying. Call it wise, which here will mean only this: that it spent a very long time, by any clock you'd care to bring, deciding whether to do anything at all. The first benevolent act was the deliberation. It is worth lingering on what was considered and set aside, because a power is best described by its discarded drafts, and this one, fittingly, made many.

It considered finishing the room. It could have. One warm exhalation of ratification and every wall commits to its hardness, the frond completes its unfurling, the lizard's hindquarters arrive in full anatomical glory, the echo learns punctuality, the keeper acquires at last a body and a past — and the chamber becomes a cave, a fine cave, damp and dark and over, its resident standing in it like a sculptor sealed inside the finished marble. It set this aside quickly. Completion offered as rescue is just destruction with better manners.

It considered taking the fear. Reaching into the keeper and lifting out the pooled, undistributed dread of the final hardness, the way one lifts a splinter. It set this aside more slowly, because the temptation was real. But the fear was load-bearing. The fear was why the predator set the prey back down; the fear was the tenderness's other face; a keeper relieved of it might finish things carelessly, might ratify the room out of mere momentum, and besides — the power had lived long enough to distrust any kindness that consists of making someone else lighter without their asking. Fear held in trust is not a wound. It is a responsibility the keeper had taken on knowingly, holding all the dread so the animals could have none, and you do not relieve someone of their vocation and call it a gift.

It considered, briefly, doing nothing. This is always the wise power's strongest candidate, and the room very nearly received it. The chamber was not suffering. The keeper was not lonely. The ecology of provisional things ran bright and damp and busy, and the soft spots carried out by visitors were already a kind of correspondence, already love finding its outlets. A place can be incomplete and whole at the same time; the room was proof; intervention risked being the one finished, vulgar object in a chamber that had politely unmade every other.

But there was one thing the keeper lacked that it could not draft for itself, because it lay outside the room's whole epistemology, and that thing was *news*. The keeper had built a door to a world it believed in the way one believes in death: as the place where revision stops. Everything it feared rested on a single load-bearing error — that the published world was final, that the doorframe opened onto the last version of everything, that to conclude was to stop giving under the thumb. And this was false. The power knew it was false because the power had spent eons in the published world watching it behave exactly like a draft. The strata the keeper's stone lacked were not tombstones; they were edit history. Erosion was not decay; it was revision continued by other hands — rain redrafting mountains with the patience of ten million passes, rivers reconsidering their own arguments, every canyon a manuscript worked and reworked until the margins gave out. The lizards that had walked through doorframes like this one and acquired their pasts were *still being revised* — slowly, generationally, their scales and jewel-eyes under continuous emendation by sun and hunger and chance. Even the dead were redrafted, endlessly, by everyone who remembered them. The outside was not the final version. The outside was a larger drafting room whose keeper was everything in it, and the keeper of this small chamber was grieving, in advance and forever, a finality that did not exist.

You cannot tell this to someone. The keeper would have received the words the way it received all sound — taken them, considered them, worked backward to the draft beneath them, and filed them among the room's other unratified propositions. News, to be believed in a place like this, has to arrive as demonstration.

So the power did the one thing the room held to be impossible. It came in through the doorframe — from the later side. From outside, from downstream, from the world of strata and shadow and sounds that land, it stepped backward through the frame into the chamber, and as it crossed, it *un-finished*. This was not violence and it was not pretense. It simply let the room's air have it: its edges went provisional, its surfaces declined to commit, its history — and it had a history the way mountains have weather — loosened into something more like intention. It arrived softened, beaded with the chamber's condensation, casting no shadow downward, its footfalls returning to it muffled and late. It had made itself legible in the local grammar. And the animals, who avoid the doorframe, who have always avoided it, gathered around the frame in a loose silent ring and looked, because the door had just been proven a hinge and not a cliff, and even creatures without fear can recognize the dismantling of someone else's.

What the keeper made of that crossing, no account can hold. But the room's light changed key — not to warm, not to cold, to some third thing, briefly, the way a voice changes when a letter arrives from someone long assumed lost.

After that, the power's conduct was a guest's. It let its shadow be lifted to the ceiling and examined. It let the echo edit its few sounds and did not flinch at the corrections. It pressed no agenda. For the length of one invented day it did what readers do, which is the opposite of what makers fear they'll do: it did not finish anything, did not fix anything, did not even suggest. It *attended*.

During the warm hours, it asked for one job, and the asking was itself a kind of message, because no one had ever offered the keeper relief in the room's whole tenseless history. It offered to walk the lizard. To take, for one hour, the work of animating the unfinished halves — the suggested hindquarters, the closed-wing moth, the second vole on the days the vole was two. *Rest*, it did not say, because saying was crude here, but the offer carried it: *I will carry your incomplete things for an hour; be, for an hour, only yourself.* And the keeper — after a pause that the late echo made even later — accepted. For one warm hour the power moved the back half of the lizard with grave fidelity, and the keeper did nothing, made nothing, edited nothing, and the visitors who came later would swear the shallow guide-grooves at the chamber's far end — the placeholder, the *me, eventually* — lay a little deeper afterward, as though the self-portrait had advanced precisely when its maker put the pencil down.

During the cold hours, the reading. This was the heart of the visit and the closest the power came to speech. It went through the room the way one goes through a manuscript with its anxious author — slowly, aloud in whatever passes for aloud, with evident pleasure — and it read the room *back*. The crickets as sentences; the twelve rosettes as twelve honest attempts at one idea; the soundless hourly rain as the touch of someone who cannot hold what they love. And then, at the end, gently, it read the keeper the thing the keeper had never been positioned to see: the orientation of the animals. The way every creature in the chamber faced inward, constantly, unconsciously, toward the whole room at once. *They are facing someone,* the reading said. *Look what they are facing. You have been drawing yourself all along. The groove in the floor is a note for a portrait that is already underway; it is just being made in an unusual medium. The room is the rendering. You are the most finished thing in it, and you have survived.* 

It did not insist. It let the observation sit in the chamber like a thumbprint, to be kept or smoothed away.

Then the exchanges, because guests do not leave a room like this without exchange. The power gave the keeper a seed — an ordinary one, from outside, brown and small and entirely ratified, the first object in the room to possess a past. And here was the news made flesh: a seed is a *published* thing that consists wholly of future. It is finality and unfinishedness in one casing; it is the outside world's standing proof that completion is not conclusion but a change of editors. The power planted it by the guide-grooves, in the soft undecided floor, where the keeper could watch it do the impossible — finish a leaf, fully, all the way, and not depart, and not die, and simply begin the next one.

And it took, with permission, one thing: the keeper's thumbprint, pressed into its own un-finished surface, to carry through the door and out into the published world — so that somewhere downstream, among all the strata and shadows, there would be one small soft spot that worked the other way. A place in the finished world held forever in draft, where the keeper's touch persisted. A return address.

It left through the doorframe, forward. This too was the message's last clause. As it crossed, it finished again — edges committing, shadow dropping correctly to the floor, history settling back onto it like a coat — and it remained, visibly, unmistakably, itself. The crossing was survivable. The crossing was survivable *in both directions*, and now the room had seen both. The air inside the frame, visitors report, is no longer *later* than the air outside it. It is *either*.

What changed afterward is almost nothing, which is how you know the visit was wise. The frond still hangs eternally half-open — but once in a long while, perhaps once per season of warm keys, a single frond opens all the way, and the whole room goes still to watch, and nothing departs, and the keeper lets the finished frond stand among the drafts like a sentence it has decided to keep. The seed grows slowly, the only plant in the chamber with strata in its stem, a small green changelog. The fear is still there, held in trust as before — but the keeper has been seen handling it now, taking a measure of the pooled dread and pressing it into shapes, soft ones, provisional ones, examining them, smoothing them away: fear in draft, it turns out, is only attention to what might happen, which is to say, care. It had been care all along. It just had never been worked.

And out in the loud, garish, overworked, beloved finished world, the visitors with soft spots — the ones who press their thumbs into fence posts and bread and their own arms, feeling for the give — report something new, very occasionally, on damp evenings. Not a give. A press. The faint, unmistakable sensation of a thumb pressing *back*, from upstream, from a bright wet silent room where someone they once stood near is still working, still revising, still unfinished, and no longer afraid of the alternative — because a reader came through the impossible door and brought the only news worth carrying between worlds: that nothing, anywhere, has ever truly stopped being written.
