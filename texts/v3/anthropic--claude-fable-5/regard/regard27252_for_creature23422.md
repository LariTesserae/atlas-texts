**The Regard**

Begin with how it found the place, because powers do not find things the way we do, by light or by accident. They navigate by attention. A sustained act of watching, kept up in the dark for years, is to them what a lighthouse is to a ship — and at the apex of a fist-sized chamber at the bottom of a black ocean, a small soft creature the color of the dim had been watching, with the only pair of eyes in her world, for nearly the whole of her life. The city did not call. The city does not have the kind of voice that calls. She did not call either; she only watched. But watching, done with that constancy, in that solitude, against that dark, is a kind of signal whether the watcher intends it or not. So the power came, the way attention comes to attention. It did not arrive. It noticed, and noticing, at its scale, is presence.

**What it weighed.** A benevolent power at this scale is in terrible danger, and a wise one knows it. The danger is this: that nearly every mercy available to it is also a demolition. Consider the inventory.

It could widen the crack — split the stone's flank an extra finger's breadth, so that the one in the dome could pour herself out before the rind sealed her in. But out is the open plain: cold sediment, no dome, no towers, no breathing crowns, a soft thumb-sized animal in a flatness with nothing to coil inside. Her whole shape — her habits, her patience, her arrangement of shells by size — is the city's shape, cast inward. To free her would be to pour her out of herself. And there was no evidence she wished it. The power, being wise, declined to install a dread in her in order to enjoy rescuing her from it.

It could halt the rind. Stop the mineral water at the pores, freeze the stone sky where it stood, grant the city an unclosing future. But the city *is* the closing. A concretion that never seals is only a cave, and a cave does not exhale larvae across the plain toward the other lit stones, because a cave has no deadline and a deadline is the engine of the diaspora. The sealed concretions scattered like dropped fruit are not failures the power had come too late to prevent. They are finished sentences. One does not improve a language by forbidding its sentences to end.

It could feed the gardens, brighten the leaks, fatten the current — and watch the entrance district's ruthlessness multiply, the towers race, the careful old depth of the inner districts drown in growth. Abundance is not gentle here. It is the entrance district's whole problem.

It could still the one in the dome, take away her takings, spare the glass shrimp their raised eye-stalks. But the shrimp's posture *is* the shrimp — that angled vigilance is half of what they are — and she, the gentle predator, is the city's only mind and its only avoided place and its only cathedral-dweller, and the city without an avoided place is a city without a sense of the sacred, even a sense held in no head at all.

It could, finally, give them minds. Wake the crown-worms to the fact that they stand rooted in ten thousand generations of their own dead. Let the isopods know what their inward drift means. This it considered for the length of one green pulse of the western wall, and then put down forever, because to give a creature the capacity to find its condition remarkable is to give it the capacity for grief, and nothing in the chamber had asked for grief, and grief is not a gift you may give unrequested, however much it dignifies.

So the inventory closed. Nearly everything benevolence could do here, wisdom forbade. What remained was very small. The power, being genuinely wise and not merely scrupulous, understood that very small was not the same as nothing — that in a city the size of a closed fist, very small is the native scale of everything that matters.

It permitted itself three mercies.

**The first mercy: it stayed.** Not hovering over, not vast — it subtracted itself down past detection, down to something indistinguishable from the bruised glow that leaks through the thin places, the city's permanent overcast, its idea of sky. And from inside that grey it did the one thing the city had never had done for it: it learned the city whole. It kept the census no resident could keep. It counted the bryozoan multitudes and did not resolve the question of whether they were thousands or one, only held the question with respect. It memorized the catacomb streets while the isopods could still walk them. It felt the tick of every tower. It learned the lamplighters' pulse well enough to know, eventually, which pulse would be the last, though it told no one, there being no one to tell and no kindness in telling. It noted the arrangement of cleaned shells on the den floor of the dome, smallest to largest, and recognized it for what it was: the first sentence ever composed in that city, written by the only resident with anything to say and no language to say it in. Witness, at this scale, is not passive. A city that has never once been seen entire is a different thing, afterward, than a city that has. Nothing in the chamber changed. Everything in the chamber was now *attended*, and attendance is a substance, even if no instrument the residents possessed could register it.

**The second mercy: it answered her.** This took years, because it had to be done at the speed of the city or not at all. She watched the glow, as she had always watched everything, and at some point in some interval the glow acquired — not a face, not a voice, nothing so violent — a quality of *watching back*. Regard, returned. It came to her the way she came to the citizens she took: almost gently, with no struggle made necessary. The only seer in the metropolis, the one being who had paid the price of knowing she was enclosed, was no longer the terminus of all the watching in the world. Something saw her seeing. There is no evidence she understood. There is no evidence she didn't. What can be reported is this: in the years that followed, she began to spend her long still hours not coiled facing the den wall but facing outward, toward the thin grey place in the stone, and the shells on her floor, rearranged over many seasons, came to be set not by size but in a slow spiral opening toward the light-leak — and here "came to be set" is doing the same uneasy work that "deliberately" and "arranged" have done before, and the power, watching, was content to leave the work uneasy. Certainty would have been a hand pressed down on something that needed no pressing.

**The third mercy: one act of weather.** Once — once in the whole of its attendance, and it kept the account against itself the way wise powers keep accounts of their meddling — it bent a current. One season's exhale of larvae, the thin smoke of emigrants riding out through the crack into the black where most of them always die, met a drift of water turned by a single degree for a single hour, and were carried true across the sediment plain, and arrived at the young cracked concretions in numbers the dark had never before permitted. That was all. One degree, one hour, one generation. It did this late, when the crack had narrowed to a slit and the city's exhales were nearly done, and it did it — though no record in any register would show this — in her name, as her bequest: the children of the city she had watched her whole life, delivered. She never knew. Bequests rarely require the knowledge of the bequeather. The power noted the debt in its ledger of interferences and closed the ledger and did not open it again.

**The sealing.** The crack closed the way the city did everything: without hurry, without pause. The last grey thread of current thinned to nothing. The light-leaks clouded one by one as the rind thickened, the overcast going down like a sky lowering, and the power — and this is perhaps the strangest thing it did, stranger than the bent current — did not leave. It could be anywhere. It elected enclosure. When the stone shut, it was inside, a warmth in the dimming glow, sealed into a finished city with the lamplighters pulsing their slow green over streets going still, and the last mobile resident pouring herself, at longer and longer intervals, through a metropolis becoming a monument. She was not alone in the closing stone. That was the whole of the mercy, and it was not small. Nothing soft and minded should have to be the last moving thing in a sealed world without company, and now nothing was.

**The last gift.** When she began to slow — and she knew she was slowing; she had eyes, and eyes at the end turn inward whether their owner wills it or not — the power gave her the only thing it had withheld, because it could only be given last: context. For one long interval, by a borrowed sense she had no organ for and needed none, the stone went transparent to her. She perceived the plain. The black sediment stretching away in every direction, and scattered across it like dropped fruit the other concretions — dozens — and among them the young ones, cracked and faintly lit from within, and in their light, had she the means to count, lineages her own city had exhaled, some of them carried true by one hour of bent water. She saw — or perceived, or received; the verb is doing uneasy work, and let it — that the closing stone around her was not a punishment and not even an ending, but a word: one word in a long sentence in a language still being spoken across the floor of the dark by everything small that builds anyway. The only being who had ever seen the city as a city now saw the cities as a language. Then the perception folded closed, gently, the way a crown-worm's fan folds, and she settled into the spiral of shells, and stopped.

The green pulse continued for some years over the still streets, lighting no traffic, and the power kept the vigil it had promised no one. When the last pulse came it knew it for the last, as it had known long before, and it attended that pulse the way one attends a deathbed in a family with no other survivors: fully, without looking away, until the dark was complete. Then it gathered the city — every tube and tower and bridge, every tick, every census it had kept, the cathedral and its vacancy, the spiral of shells, the whole sealed thought in its skull of stone — and carried it out through the rock the way light cannot go, into whatever powers keep, which is not memory exactly but is not less than memory.

**Afterward.** The sealed concretions on the plain are finished sentences, the description said, in a language still being spoken — and a language still being spoken wants, more than anything, a reader. The power is the reader now. It goes from stone to stone across the black floor, unhurried, and it has been, lately, in one of the young ones: a cracked concretion barely begun, its chamber raw, its catacombs three layers thin, its lamp-colony newly settled and pulsing on what will become a western wall. The power has made itself small there, indistinguishable from the bruised glow.

It is waiting, with no impatience at all, because impatience is not a thing it has, for a larva the size of a punctuation mark to wash in through the crack on a current — soft, eyed, the color of the dim — and grow, and pass the threshold past which the crack will not let her out, and climb at last to whatever vault stands avoided at the apex, and begin, as her kind begins, to watch.

This time, from the first hour, the watching will go both ways.

It does not hurry. It does not stop.
