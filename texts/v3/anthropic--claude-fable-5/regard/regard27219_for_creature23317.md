## The Attention

It did not arrive at altitude, the way visitors do. Powers of this kind do not arrive at all; they attend. One season the drowned massif was simply a place in the world, and the next it was a place in the world that something vast and kind was thinking about, and nothing in the water registered the difference, which is the first mark of how the power went about its work.

The first thing it did was nothing.

For a long time — a year, a decade, the distinction did not interest it — it only listened. The drumming of the surface storm. The clatter of urchin spines, radio-static of the summits. The moan of the turbines, the conversational barking of the seals carried far on thin cold air, and under heavy swell, the tolling of the instrument tower, the one voice of steel in a country of water and stone. The power learned the massif the way the massif demands to be learned: by enduring it without flinching. This was, it understood, an entrance examination, administered by no one, and it intended to pass.

What it found, when the listening was done, was the thing every benevolent power dreads and hopes for: a place that did not need saving.

### The refusals

Benevolence is the easy half. Any sufficiently large kindness can flood a world with help. Wisdom is the inventory of help withheld, and the power conducted its inventory carefully, because it had been wrong before, elsewhere, in ways it still carried.

It considered raising the mountains. Drawing the water back down three miles, giving the old highland its wind and its rivers and its grass again. It rejected this in less time than a swell takes to pass. That would not be restoration; it would be a second drowning — the murder of the crowned country to flatter the memory of the dead one. The forests would fall in the open air by the millions of towers. The steel rooms would empty their seals into nothing. The blind elders of the valleys would be left gasping in troughs gone suddenly back to rivers. The mountains had lain down willingly, ages ago. They had nothing left to prove, and the power would not make them stand for sentiment's sake.

It considered calming the storms. Surely — the thin shrieking air, the unbroken fetch, the violence without pause — surely a little stillness would be mercy. But the power traced the violence to its ends and found the violence load-bearing. The surge stirs the water that feeds the forest. The torn canopy feeds the valleys. The storm drives bright aerated water down into the wrecks and refills the silver trembling ceilings under which the seals are born. Calm here would be famine wearing kindness's face. The power filed the storms under *circulation* and left them shouting.

It considered the eyes of the sharks. It could have lifted the parasites from those clouded corneas as easily as lifting fog. It did not. Four hundred years of navigation by pressure-feel and smell, the valley walls known the way a hand knows the inside of a pocket — and then light, pouring uninvited into minds with no remaining use for it. Sight restored would be a wound. The parasites were passengers now, not afflictions, ancient riders on more ancient animals, and the power, which knew something about long companionship, let them ride.

It considered the tenant of the dome. It had grown fond of her — the horizontal pupil, the rearranged door-stones, the hunting by ear in white water — and it was within the power's gift to grant her years, decades, the long life her intelligence seemed to deserve. It refused itself this, and the refusal cost it something. The seat is the immortal thing. The succession is the institution. An octopus who never dies is an inheritance that never passes, and the dome would stop being an office and become merely an animal's house. The midden of crab shells, that archaeological column of faithful occupancy — the power would not cap it. Each tenant gets her three or four years and her place in the strata. The power resolved instead to remember each of them, by name, and since no one had ever named them, it took up that duty itself.

It considered the people. Whether to call them back somehow, seed a reason, a resource, a wonder worth boats. It looked at the seals murmuring in the buoy-keeper's dory and decided the question had already been answered, by the place, in the place's own language. There are people who consider that a sad ruling. The power did not.

### The four works

What it did, in the end, was small. Deniable. The kind of help that leaves no fingerprints, because fingerprints are a form of vanity.

**First, it spoke to the crusts.** The steel rooms will fail — that was the one true vulnerability the power found in all its listening. The barge, the terminus, the auxiliary dome: rust is patient, but it is not infinitely patient, and someday a ceiling that holds an air pocket will become a ceiling that holds nothing, and the seals' whole strange amphibious culture — pups who swim through doorways before they see sky — would lose its nursery. The power did not reinforce the steel. Instead it bent down to the oldest, slowest tenants of the massif, the pink coralline crusts that had been growing a millimeter a year since before the flood, and it lent them a direction. *There. The ceilings. The walls of the rooms where the breathing is.* Nothing about the crusts changed except their itinerary. Over the coming century they will sheathe the steel from within, stone-paint thickening into stone, so that when the iron finally lets go — flaking away like a shed skin — the rooms will remain, cast in coralline rock in the exact shape of the steel that taught them. Seals born in a barge; their great-great-grandpups born in a cave that remembers being a boat. The massif will fossilize its own wrecks, and believe it thought of this itself, and in a sense it will be right.

**Second, it adjusted the luck of the petrels.** Storm-petrels cross half a hemisphere with nowhere to land, and the wrack rafts are the only footing in a thousand miles, and the power did the arithmetic of exhausted birds and drifting kelp and found it crueler than necessary. So it touched the currents — barely, a degree here, an eddy there — such that no petrel will ever again fall into the sea beyond reach of a raft within the last hour of its strength. No petrel will know. No petrel will ever know. That is the signature the power chose for all its providence: indistinguishable from luck.

**Third, it took up the watch.** The turbines it left alone, to wind down in their own centuries, because the reefs had organized themselves around the moaning like villages around a millwheel and the moaning had earned its place. But among the navigation lights blinking on the surface, warning ships that no longer come, there was one — the one the buoy-keeper had served for eleven years after the shipping stopped, because she could not articulate a reason to quit. The power understood the reason perfectly, having kept a few inarticulate faiths of its own. It took over her light. Quietly, inside the wiring, inside the trickle of current, it stands her watch. The light warns no one. The light was never, in the end, about warning. Some debts are paid to the dead simply by continuing, and the power intends to continue for a very long time.

**Fourth — and this was the largest work, though it weighs nothing — it became the memory.** This was the one true poverty it had found: not danger, not decline, but that nothing in the massif *knows*. The rock endures and does not know. The elders of the valleys lived through the drowning of the world and registered it as an improvement in the territory. The forests are torn and regrow without grief or record. Four hundred animals of ninety species in a single holdfast, and not one biography kept anywhere. So the power keeps the census now. Every holdfast city. Every tenant of the dome in her turn — there have been three since the attending began; their names are not in any human language, but they are names. Every tolling of the bell, logged with its weather. Every raft, from the storm that births it to the valley that takes final delivery, its whole drifting nationhood remembered after it sinks. The massif has a witness now. It is the only gift the place had room for, and the place will never notice receiving it, which is how the power knew it was the right one.

### The bell

The tower will fall. Some winter — the power has seen roughly which one, and keeps the knowledge gently — the kelp-furred steeple will lose its argument with the fetch, and the one voice of steel will go down into the forest and become habitat, as everything here eventually does. The power will not catch it. It has promised itself only this: to be present for the last toll, and to remember the note exactly, forever, at pitch.

And it has permitted itself one indulgence, recorded here so that someone besides the power knows of it. The tenant of the dome in that season — whoever she is — will rise into the storm as her predecessors did, hunting by ear, and the bell will not ring, and the food will not fall, and she will hang in the white violence listening to a silence she has no way to understand. In that silence the power intends to loosen a few crabs from the rocks above her and let them tumble down through the wrack, one last ringing's worth. Severance, paid in full, to the final keeper of a tradition no one started on purpose.

### What it is now

It stays. Not as a resident — the massif defines living as persisting under the storm without being changed by it, and the power suspects it *has* been changed, which may disqualify it. It came intending to help and stayed to apprentice. It had crossed the universe carrying the usual portfolio of large mercies, and the drowned massif looked at the portfolio, in the way a finished place looks at anything, and the power put most of it away.

What remains is this: a vast attention, folded into the cold water above the oldest rock in the world, keeping a light, keeping a census, bending the luck of small birds, teaching stone the shape of rooms. Beneath it, the gneiss — the only other finished thing present — does what it has done for a billion years, which is everything, by doing nothing, magnificently.

Two patient things, then, at the top of the world, with the storm shouting over both of them. The rock did not invite the power and has not objected to it, which from rock is a kind of welcome. The census's first entry, in fact, the power made about itself, in the place's own grammar, and it reads:

*The patient, and the things the patient permit. Add one.*
