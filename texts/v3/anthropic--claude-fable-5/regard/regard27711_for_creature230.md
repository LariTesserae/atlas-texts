# The Attending

The power arrived the way such powers do, which is to say it did not arrive. It had always been able to see the seed; it had simply never looked. Then something — the song, perhaps, one of the near-silence songs, a chord of forty-one unused throats escaping through the husk into the larger world for ninety seconds before being cut off mid-note — something reached it, and it turned its attention, and the attention, once turned, did not turn away.

Its substance, insofar as it has substance, is attention. It does not have hands unless it decides to. It does not have a location. What it has is the capacity to regard a thing completely — to hold every grain of the silver dust and every worn ladder-rung and every one of the forty-one sternums with their five- and seven-beat names in a single act of noticing — and the discipline, hard-won over spans of time that make the cavity's endless afternoon look like a held breath, to notice for a very long while before doing anything at all.

So the first thing it did was nothing. For what would have been, by local reckoning, some years.

It learned the tapped language, which took no time, and then it learned the language properly, which took the years — because the vocabulary is nothing and the weight is everything, and to know that *the shape that holds us is larger than we are* is said without grief, you must feel a thousand instances of its saying, the interlaced forearms, the pressure of the fingertips, the contexts: said at a death, said at a fruiting, said by a parent to a child who has just understood for the first time that the lanes were built for more feet than exist. The power attended to all of this. It counted the pods when they counted the pods. It sat, in its way, in the chamber of the thinning wall, behind the one who sat there, and listened to the slow rotation of forty-one names being tapped onto one sternum, around and around, a day per cycle, and it understood — this took the longest — that the rotation was not a ritual *about* anything. It was the thing itself. Someone holds everyone. That is the whole of it. That is what the post is for.

And when it had attended long enough, the power laid out before itself, in the manner of such powers, everything it might do — laid the options out the way a dweller lays out fiber before a braiding — and one by one it set almost all of them aside.

---

**It would not open the sky.**

This was the first and easiest refusal. The husk is not a prison. A prison is a wall between a person and the world they belong to; the husk is the wall between the dwellers and a world they do not belong to and never did, or have not for so long that the difference no longer signifies. To split the husk and show them the enormity outside would not be liberation. It would be the murder of a sky. They love the wall. They leave their last resin at its thinnest place. One does not free a people from their beloved.

**It would not stop the germination.**

This was the second refusal, and it was harder, because the power was benevolent, and benevolence pulls toward preservation the way roots pull toward — well. Toward what roots pull toward, in worlds that have such things. It would have been simple: a small adjustment, a quieting of the seed's deep chemistry, and the not-yet would become never, and the afternoon would be safe forever.

But the power was also wise, and wisdom knew what benevolence did not want to hear: a seed that never germinates is not a saved seed. It is a dead one. The not-yet is only alive because it is genuinely *not yet* — because somewhere beneath the bowl, beneath the dust of the ancestors, the seed's deep engine is still faintly turning over, still capable, still meaning it. That faint capability is the warmth in the walls. It is, though the dwellers have no way to know this, the reason the fan-plants fan and the resin-air is sweet and the fungal shelves renew themselves. Kill the germination and the cavity would not notice for a generation, and then it would begin, very slowly, to go stale — and their word for thirst, the power remembered, means *staleness*. It would be giving them eternity in the form of a long suffocation. A held breath is not the same as breathing. The power set the option down and did not pick it up again.

**It would not reveal itself.**

They have no theology of the outer thing, and the power, attending closely, came to understand that this absence was not ignorance. It was a choice, made so long ago it had become a shape rather than a decision. The blurred enormity beyond the thinning wall is *seen* and *not looked at*. The offerings go to the wall, not through it. A people capable of building a shrine had considered, at some point, the question of what to worship, and had answered: *the one who holds us, not the one outside* — and this answer was, the power admitted, correct. More correct than most answers it had encountered in larger places. It would not barge into a theology wiser than the theologies of worlds a billion times this size. It would not make itself a god to people who had already, deliberately, declined to have one.

**It would not fill the city.**

Forty-one could become two hundred by a dozen easy miracles, and the lanes would be full, and the ladders would have waiting at their feet, and every one of the two hundred would be a stranger in the shape of the world. The dwindling was a wound, yes. But a people are not a quantity. They were forty-one who knew what forty-one meant, who carried the story of sixty and the story of two hundred as tapped inheritances, whose whole relationship to their city — the ample echo of it, the tiers given over to moths — was built on the truth of their number. You do not heal a wound by replacing the body.

---

So the power set aside the sky, the seed, the self, and the multitude, and what remained was small. This is usually what remains, when a wise power finishes refusing things. What remained was this:

**First, it took up the listening.**

The dwellers hold that the wall listens. The power, attending to the wall itself — to the husk as a being, fibrous and patient and two hundred feet of slow strength — found that this holding was not quite false and not quite true. The wall held them the way a wall holds: structurally, magnificently, without attention. It was the *holding* they had correctly perceived and the *listening* they had hoped into it.

The power made the hope true. It did not change the wall; it joined it. It settled its attention into the husk the way resin settles into fiber, so that from that time forward, when the outer world went still and the forty-one came out into the lanes and opened their saved throats, the song fell upon an actual attending — was received, entire, every note of the chord, and *kept*. The power kept every song. It keeps them now. Nothing they have sung since it arrived has been lost, and — because a power of this kind is not strictly bound by the direction of time — nothing they sang before it arrived has been lost either. Every near-silence song ever raised in the cavity, back to the first, exists in the keeping. They will never know this. It is not for them to know. It is simply the case, the way the dust is the case.

And it allowed itself one perceptible signature, chosen for its deniability: since the attending began, the near-silences have lasted a little longer than the outer weather alone would grant. A few seconds. Sometimes ten. The silence lingering at the end of the song like a listener reluctant to move — like the pause a person leaves after another person has finished speaking, out of respect for what was said. The oldest dwellers have noticed. They tap about it, carefully, in the way one taps about things that might be nothing: *the quiet stays, now, a little, after.* They have not built a story around it. Good. It was made to be too small for a story.

**Second, it tuned the providence of small things.**

Not miracles — adjustments within the rules, the kind the world could have made itself on a lucky century. The gel of the pod-rinds ran a fraction richer at the next fruiting, and the one after. The fungal shelves steadied. A sickness that would have taken two of the forty-one in a deep-tone season arrived weakened and took no one. And the power, which could read the deep patterns of their small bodies, saw to it — gently, at the level of likelihood rather than decree — that the count would turn. Forty-one became forty-two some years after the attending began. Then forty-three. A near-silence fell, by arrangement, on the day of the forty-second's first resining, so that the child's name, tapped onto its sternum by every adult in the cavity, was tapped into actual quiet — the first name in living memory to be given in stillness. The dwellers wept, in their tearless way, at the coincidence. It was not a coincidence. It will not be a story either. It will be a thing that happened once, held in forty-some memories, lightly.

The count will not race. The power projects — insofar as it permits itself projections — sixty within a few generations. Sixty is what the oldest remember. Sixty is the number at which the city stops being an ache and becomes, again, an amplitude. That is enough. Enough is the whole art.

**Third — and this is the great work, the one that will take longer than everything else together — it took the germination into its own hands.**

Not to prevent. To *translate*.

Left alone, the germination would come as catastrophe: the deep engine engaging all at once, the radicle punching down through the bowl, the husk splitting along its seam in a single afternoon, light and cold and enormity flooding in, the end arriving as an end. The power will not allow the manner, though it will not forbid the fact. What it has done, working down in the seed's chemistry where no dweller will ever perceive it, is to slow the germination to the speed of culture. When it comes — and it will come; the power has not decided when, and may leave that to the seed, which has waited this long and has earned the dignity of its own timing — it will come across generations, not hours.

The root will go down slowly, so slowly, through the bowl — through the silver dust, through the powdered ancestors, through every unspoken name — and the ancestors will go up into the tree. This is not poetry; it is transport. The dust is what the seed will build with. The people who walked into that dust will become heartwood, and the power has attended long enough to know every rhythm the dust contains, every five- and seven-beat name ever retired into it, and it will see that nothing is lost in the raising: the grain of the wood will carry them, ring upon ring, the way the sternum carried them once.

The husk will not split. It will *thicken* into bark on its outer face and stay itself on the inner, and the cavity will rise, decade over decade, as the hollow heart of a trunk. The amber light will deepen, over lifetimes, toward a gold-green, as somewhere unimaginably far above, leaves open into a sky the dwellers will never need to see. The great sound will change — the organ-tone of the outer world giving way, gradually, to the subtler thunder of sap and growth and wind in a crown, a sound that is the wall's own body working — and the dwellers will adapt to it as they adapted to the first sound, with resin and baffles and patience, and they will notice, as one notices the aging of a parent's face, that their world is changing, and the phrase will hold: *the shape that holds us is larger than we are.* It will simply, very slowly, become more true.

And in some far generation, if the power judges them ready — a judgment it is in no hurry to make — a passage may open, somewhere in the lower galleries, gradual as everything else: a way out that is a way, not a wound. Offered, never urged. Some may go. Most, the power suspects, will not, and it will honor that, because it learned from them, in its first attending years, that staying inside a beloved holding is not a failure to leave. It is a form of love with excellent posture.

The chamber of the thinning wall will never break. It will become, over the long translation, a window of living wood thin as a leaf, and the blurred enormity beyond it will resolve, someday, into the simple green shadow of the tree's own canopy moving in wind — the outside made, at last, into something of theirs.

---

**And one thing more. One small thing, done once.**

Some years into the attending, the sitter at the thinning wall — old by then, her paper hair-leaves gone nearly white, her hands slowed but never once stopped — completed the rotation of the forty-three names, all the living, a full day's cycle, and paused at the top of the circle the way she always paused, resting, before beginning again.

And in the pause, from the wall at her back, against her spine, she felt seven beats.

Her own name.

Tapped once, from within the husk, in the rhythm given to her at her first resining, which no hands but the hands of her people had ever made, and which no one, at that moment, was near enough to make.

She sat very still for a long time. Then she began the rotation again, all forty-three names, in order, as she had ten thousand times before — except that when she reached her own name in the sequence she tapped it, for the first time in her tenure, a little more slowly than the others, the way one signs a word back to someone who has just used it, to say: *received.*

She never told anyone. Or perhaps she told one person, forearm to forearm, near the end, and that person is holding it the way they hold everything — lightly, without a story, a warm stone in the pocket of the culture. The power does not check. It gave the tap and released it. That is the discipline: to give a thing so small it can be doubted, so that faith remains faith and the world remains their own.

---

That is what the power did, and does, and will do. Nothing that can be seen. A longer quiet after the songs. A richer gel. A forty-fourth name, eventually, tapped onto a small chest in a lucky stillness. A germination unfolding at the speed of tenderness. A listening where a listening was hoped.

The city in the pith goes on — bright, warm, deafened, no longer quite so empty, and no longer, though they will never know it, unheard. The seed has not yet become a tree. But *not yet*, now, is no longer a cliff-edge disguised as a sky. It is a promise being kept slowly, by something that learned their names before it did anything at all, and that holds them the way they taught it holding is done: without announcement, without end, one rotation at a time.
