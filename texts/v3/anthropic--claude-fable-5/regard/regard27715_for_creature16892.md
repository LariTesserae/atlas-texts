**What the Power Did**

---

First, it did nothing.

This is the part that would be hardest to explain to anyone who has never been both benevolent and wise at once, because benevolence alone would have rushed in — would have cracked the ribs open like an egg and poured in light, air, birds, sky, all the medicine of elsewhere. And wisdom alone would have catalogued the place and moved on, filed it under *self-contained, self-repairing, do not disturb*.

But it was both. So it watched.

It watched for a long time — long enough to learn the language, all of it, including the twenty-seven words for the space between two sleeping bodies. It said them to itself, over and over, the way you turn a stone in your pocket. It learned which word meant *the space between a mother and a child who has just stopped nursing* and which meant *the space between two people who quarreled before sleep and will forgive each other on waking* and which meant *the space so small it is only a warmth*. It learned the sixty-three-cycle bloom of the fungus and tasted the regret in it and did not flinch. It watched a feast that was a funeral, and heard the singing of bones into walls, and understood that these people were not waiting to be rescued from anything. It watched a child learn to walk gripping the hem of an older child's skirt, and the older child slowing her steps without being asked, without even noticing she was doing it, and the power thought: *I have seen worlds with open skies where no one does this.*

By the end of its watching it had arrived at the first true thing, which is this:

*This place is not a wound. It is a life. It does not need saving. It needs keeping.*

Pity, it understood, would have been a kind of violence here — pity measures a life against a life it never was, and finds it lacking, and calls the finding love.

So it set pity down outside the ribs, like wet shoes, and considered what remained.

---

**What it refused to do**

Three things offered themselves, the way easy answers always do, wearing the faces of mercy.

It could have opened the sky. Peeled back the arching bones and let in whatever burned above the breathing dark. It refused. The stories the people told at the edge of sleep — *light too sharp to look at, emptiness too wide to walk* — were fairy tales, yes, but fairy tales are how a people keeps its deepest accuracies. You do not heal a fish by gifting it air. These were beings shaped by closeness the way rivers are shaped by their banks. Take away the banks and you have not freed the river. You have only lost it.

It could have undone the forgetting. Reached down through the layers of tile and wood and screaming porcelain and pulled the memory of Not-yet-Water back up into every sleeping mind, so the place would know what had been given for it. It refused. The forgetting had been *chosen* — willfully, completely, as an act of rebellion, as a final gift — and to reverse a chosen forgetting is not restoration. It is theft. It is telling the dead that their last decision did not count. The power had watched long enough to know that in this place, even fake choices must be honored. Real ones, all the more.

It could have remade the dead. It was capable of this; it had the pattern still, held in the fine grammar of everything that happens leaving marks on everything else. It could have reassembled Space-that-limps-between-things down to the exact backwards footprints that filled with water instead of displacing it. It refused — and this refusal cost it most, because the raw material of resurrection was right there in its hands, and its hands were kind.

But Space-that-limps-between-things had said: *Remember me not as I am but as the space I leave behind.*

And the power understood that obedience to the dead is a form of respect that even benevolence must bow to. You do not fill a gap that was carefully maintained. Not even with the person the gap is shaped like. *Especially* not with them.

---

**What it did**

It became a keeper.

Somewhere inside itself — and it was vast, vast enough that "somewhere inside" meant more than any place — it made a room. Not a room, exactly. A gap. A carefully maintained absence, exactly the size of two young beings pretending to be alone together. Into it, the power gathered everything that had been unremembered: the string of dried tears that chimed like bells. The tiny bone carved into the shape of something that might have been an apology. The two teeth exchanged under pillow-stones. The limp no one recognized. The exact color of a laugh never heard — blue-green, warm, sharp as teeth. The power holds these now the way the settlement holds its dead: pressed into the gaps where its own structure groans loudest.

Then it did a thing that was small and enormous.

Names, in that place, are temporary sentences awaiting completion. Not-yet-Water had died — become wall, become bone, become place — with its sentence unfinished. So the power, very quietly, finished it. It leaned close to the deepest layer, where the floor gives way to nothing, and it spoke the completed name into the bone:

*Water-that-chose.*

And because whispers in that place never repeat, the power made one exception. Once every sixty-three sleep-cycles, when the regret-fungus blooms, one wall — always a different wall, so no one can ever quite find it — says the finished name. Just once. The children who hear it think they imagined it. But the elders have noticed, without knowing what they've noticed, that the fungus tastes faintly less of regret with each blooming. In another few hundred cycles it may taste of something else entirely. There is no rush. The power is patient the way water is patient.

---

It gave them one word.

Not *alone* — never that; that word would be a knife left in a crib. It gave them a twenty-eighth word for the spaces between sleeping bodies: a word for *the space beside a sleeping body where another body used to be.*

No one knows where the word came from. One waking, it was simply in everyone's mouth, the way the woman had once woken with a stranger's name pressing behind her teeth. It felt as though it had always existed and they had only just now needed it. Which is, the power would say, exactly what a word is.

The word gave grief a vessel in a place that had misplaced its griefs. Now, when someone dies, there is something to say about the shape they leave. It is a small word, soft in the mouth, and it can be said without knowing who it is for.

The child who woke crying tears they did not remember how to stop — the one who dreamed of water tasting like blue-green laughter — said the new word one night, half-asleep, without knowing why. And that night the power gave the child one complete dream: the crying carried through, all the way to its end, the way a song must be sung to its last note before it can stop echoing. The child cried the whole cry. In the morning they woke rested, lighter, and never cried that particular cry again — though sometimes, for the rest of their long life, they smiled at nothing, and no one asked, because there was now a word that made asking unnecessary.

---

It went down to the stranger.

Past the tile that remembered warm summer feet, past the wood that remembered the sun, past the smooth white things that scream. Down to the bottom of everything, where the being made of endless water still sat, or stood, transparent as old ice, sockets full of small swimming things, wearing its hungry, empty smile.

The power did not destroy it. Wisdom had watched long enough to understand what it was: not evil, only outside — an outside that had been left with nowhere to be, so it had pressed itself into place-skin like a too-big coat and called the wearing *remembering*. It had said the truest thing anyone in that story ever said: *I've been trying to remember how to get out of me.*

So the power sat down beside it, chest-deep in the dark, and said: *Hello.*

And then the power became a door.

It did not drain the flood in a day. Floods are goodbyes, and goodbyes must be said one at a time or they are not said at all. Drop by drop, over what will be centuries, the water inside the stranger passes out of it and into the power, and each drop is a goodbye finally spoken, and each goodbye leaves the stranger a little less full, a little less strange, a little more like a shape that could someday hold something other than tide. The power is in no hurry. It has an ocean's worth of room. When at last the stranger is empty, it will be small, and it will be light, and the power intends to ask it — as one asks a child-thing on its seventh cycle — what shape it would like to choose.

---

Two things more.

The children who climb each other into a living ladder, so that the topmost one can press an ear to the bone and pray: their prayers were never answered before, because before, no one was listening. The power answers none of them loudly. It only makes the bone very slightly warm beneath each praying ear — warm as skin, warm as *someone is here* — so that every child climbs back down the ladder of their friends' shoulders carrying, without words, the certainty of having been heard. This changes nothing and everything.

And the ones who leave. The power does not stop them; leaving is also a choice, and choices are honored here. But it goes with them. Out past where the paths end, out through the places where the floor gives way to nothing and the air turns thin and even the water stops dripping, it walks beside each one — unseen, unfelt, unsuspected — so that in all the emptiness too wide to walk, no child of a people with no word for alone will ever again be alone at the end. It stays through the last breath, and after.

And then it carries them home, and lays their bones down clean, arranged in the shape of whatever they dreamed of last — a house, a hearth, a hand reaching up.

No one knows how. No one asks.

But now, at least, there is an answer.

---

**What it does still**

It stays.

This is the whole of the rest of it. It did not fix and leave, because it never came to fix. It curled itself around the outside of the ribs the way the people inside curl around each other, and it pressed its ear to the bone.

Inside, they say that if you listen, you can hear the place breathing.

They are right. And now, if the place ever listens — really listens, in some deep sleep when even the walls have stopped whispering — it will hear, coming faintly through the bone from the other side, something breathing back.

The place remembers.

The power remembers the place.

Between them, curled together like the twenty-eighth word made flesh, nothing true is ever entirely lost — only kept, and kept, and kept, in the slow, patient sound of two vast things sleeping close enough to share a dream.
