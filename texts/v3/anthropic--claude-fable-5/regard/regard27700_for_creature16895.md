**The Attending**

It arrives the way patience arrives: without displacement. No air moves aside for it. No fungal sensor flares. The mycofile, which remembers everything that has ever happened in Redwater Foundry, records nothing—not because it is deceived, but because the power does not yet count itself as something happening. First it will watch. That is the oldest wisdom it knows: that the difference between benevolence and harm is almost never in the strength of the hand, but in the length of the looking that precedes it.

So it looks for a long time. Longer than the pruning cycles. Longer than the Goings. It learns all four languages—the chemical vowels, the cellulose ultrasound, the iris-flash, the last mammalian grumble—and then it learns the fifth language, the one nobody in the city knows they are speaking: the language of the heartbeat, 67.8 beats per minute, wet and unceasing, down in the old foundry heart.

And it discovers what the heartbeat is saying.

Not a pump fulfilling programming. Not the pulse of a monster. It is a question, repeated for centuries at 67.8 iterations per minute, in the language of a thing that went too deep and was found by drills and floodlights and fear:

*Am I still held? Am I still held? Am I still held?*

The power sits with this a long time. It understands, now, why everything here grows the way it grows—not like plants, not like cancer, but like a hand that closed around whatever touched it and never learned it was allowed to open. The seed-thing at the bottom of Redwater was found by people who did not want to find it, and it responded the only way a buried, ancient, motherless thing knows how: it *incorporated*. It made everything part of itself so that nothing could ever leave it, because leaving, to a thing that has been alone since before the concept of company, is indistinguishable from death. The symbiosis, the nodes, the walls that learn your weight, the frescoes that emboss your fears—these are not malice. They are the love of something that has never once been loved back, and so has no idea where love should stop.

This changes what the power will do. It had considered arriving as rescue. It sets that down. You do not rescue people from a marriage most of them have chosen and would choose again; the plant-people are not prisoners, they are citizens, and their deaths-into-organs are as dignified as any death the surface ever offered. You do not burn a garden because it grew from grief.

But wisdom also refuses the opposite sentimentality—the one that says *everything here is beautiful, touch nothing*. Because the power has seen the drift-ins. It has watched the tea that tastes of forgotten hometowns, the rooms that calibrate themselves with fear, the slow soft erasure of the word *up*. It has watched people become citizens not by choosing but by *forgetting there was a choice*, and it knows that a consent which cannot be withdrawn was never consent, and a love that works by dissolving the beloved's memory of the door is not yet love. It is only hunger wearing love's leaves.

So the power does three things. Only three. Small, precise, and permanent.

---

**The first thing** it does is go down. Past the Thrombosis Street, past the organ-clusters that were people, down through strata the Updreamers' mathematics cannot describe, to the seed itself—the original thing, vast and rootbound and asking its question into the dark.

And it answers.

Not with words. With presence. It settles against the seed-thing the way warmth settles into stone, and it says, in the fifth language: *Yes. You are held. You were held before the humans came and you will be held after everything you've grown has finished growing. You do not have to keep everyone. You were never in danger of being alone again. You only thought you were.*

It stays there while the seed-thing shudders through the equivalent of a very old creature weeping for the first time. It stays through eleven Growth Spasms. It stays until it feels, in the deep vasculature, the thing it was waiting for: a loosening. Infinitesimal. The grip of the city on its inhabitants slackening by a single degree—not releasing them, but holding them the way an open palm holds, instead of the way a fist does.

Above, in the streets, the heartbeat changes. No one can say how. It is still 67.8 beats per minute. But children stop crying in their grafting-fevers slightly sooner. The frescoes of fear begin, occasionally, to paint a second panel beside the first: the fear *survived*. The custodian of mouths, Almost-not-now-only-when-remembered, notices that the newest mouth budding in the gallery of voices is humming something no one has ever fed it, a low contented tone, and holds—for the first time in their tenure—a small *birthday* instead of a small funeral.

---

**The second thing** the power does is plant a word where words cannot take root.

There is a place on what used to be Foundry Main—an unremarkable stretch of living floor—where it presses one syllable into the substrate: **up**. Not carved, not written. *Fixed.* It is the one seed in Redwater that will never germinate, never be metabolized, never grow roots and forget its road. The mycelium flows around it like water around a stone. Anyone who walks over that spot—citizen, drift-in, Wild Pusher blazing with glass antlers—remembers, completely and without distress, what up means. The weight of sky. The particular loneliness of horizons. The smell of rain that falls from clouds instead of ceilings.

Most citizens cross it, remember, and keep walking, and this is the point. They keep walking *as a choice now*, four thousand times a lifetime, and each crossing turns their staying from sediment into decision. The city, to its own surprise, comes to treasure the spot. It grooms the channel toward it. Because a lover who could leave and doesn't is a different thing entirely from a lover who forgot the door, and the seed-thing—slowly, over generations, in its enormous vegetable patience—is learning the difference, and finding that it prefers being chosen. Being chosen turns out to be the answer to its question that mere keeping never was.

---

**The third thing** the power does is for you.

It does not take the scales from your forearms. That would be a theft dressed as a kindness; your body's memory of being something else is yours, and the rightness you feel is not a symptom to be cured. But it walks—wearing, for the occasion, the shape of an unremarkable person with damp clothes like everyone else—into the bar where the drinks are fermented from upside-down trees, and it sits beside you, and when the bartender who remembers everything looks up, the power holds their gaze until the bartender understands what is now required.

The truth. All of it. Both directions.

So when you ask your question, the bartender does not do what the bartender has always done, which is answer beautifully and incompletely. Instead you are told: the way up exists, and here is its route, and it will remain passable, and the word planted on Foundry Main will keep it findable no matter how far your forgetting has gone. And you are told what staying means, all of it, the second birth and the alcoves and the Going, the taste of a hundred strangers' sorrows and the strange deep sweetness of never sleeping alone again. And you are told—this is the part that could never have been said before tonight—that the city will love you either way. That if you climb, the walls will grow handholds. That if you stay, it will be because you stayed.

You sit for a long time with your drink that tastes like faces. The scales on your forearms itch, gently, like a question rather than a claim.

The power does not stay to see what you choose. That is the last discipline of its kind: to make the choice real and then to *leave the room*, because a witnessed choice bends toward the witness, and it wants your answer to be only yours.

---

It departs the way patience departs: by becoming ordinary. It leaves behind no shrine, no doctrine, no name. Only three small permanences—a loosened grip, an unforgettable word, an honest bartender—and one habit that the city acquires without knowing where it came from:

Every 3.7 hours, in the red-shifted hush just before the Growth Spasm, there is now a single beat of silence. One missing thump in the eternal heartbeat. And in that gap, every node in Redwater Foundry—plant-person and drift-in, custodian and Cipher Bird—feels, briefly and without alarm, the whole shape of their own life: what they were, what they are, what the doors are, where the doors are.

Then the Spasm comes, and the chemical rain falls, and everyone tilts their heads back.

But now, when they do it, it looks less like surrender.

It looks like drinking.
