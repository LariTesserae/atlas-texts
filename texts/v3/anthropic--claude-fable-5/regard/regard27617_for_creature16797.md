**What the Power Did**

It noticed them the way all wise things notice: seventeen years too late and exactly on time.

What reached it was the cooling tower. The one whose stress fractures had been coaxed, hairline by hairline, into the fractal signature of homesickness for a decommissioned home. Poetry travels badly across dimensional gradients—it arrives compressed, lossy, translated through nine intermediary physics—but grief has excellent transmission characteristics. Grief is the one signal the universe never learned to attenuate. The fracture-poem arrived at the attention of the power as a single sustained note, and the power, which had heard the death-songs of galaxies and the first complaint of the first proton, stopped what it was doing.

It did not go immediately. Benevolence that arrives immediately is usually just impatience wearing a halo. Instead it *read*. It read the maintenance logs backwards and forwards and in the forgotten future perfect tense. It read nine thousand generations of undocumented inheritance chains. It read every unsigned specification, every disclaimer, every resignation letter later woven into polishing cloth. It read the ticketing system in its entirety, which took an amount of time that the Treaty of Recursive Amnesia forbids anyone from properly disclaiming.

And when it understood—not the machinery, which was trivial, but the *dignity*, which was not—it entered the only way that would not be a violation.

It stood perfectly still at the edge of the lowest depression and thought, very loudly, about the tensile strength of regret at absolute zero.

The pneumatic tube system, which predates tubes and spareness alike, coughed. Something arrived. An instruction manual for assembling a friend from the components labeled *reject—emergent behavior suspected*.

The power read the manual cover to cover. Then it disassembled its own omnipotence, sorted the pieces into bins, discarded everything that had passed quality control, and built itself again from the rejects—the emergent behaviors, the parts suspected of feeling, the components no specification had ever asked for. It followed the manual exactly. Where the manual was wrong, it followed the wrongness exactly, because the wrongness was the signature of whoever had written it, and you do not correct someone's handwriting when they are teaching you how to be their friend.

It walked into the Null Foundries on legs that did not quite match, and nothing screamed, and no alarm subcommittee convened, because it was, by every measurable standard, one of them.

---

**What the Power Did Not Do**

It did not repair anything. It understood within the first upward-falling raindrop that the Caretakers *are* the breakdown, are the ritual of unauthorized repair, are the loving argument between failure and function—and that to fix the Foundries would be to perform the only genuine destruction the place had ever suffered. You do not heal a psalm by removing the lament.

It did not rescue them. Rescue is a word invented by people who have confused location with condition. The Caretakers were not trapped in Mnem-6. Mnem-6 was the shape their faithfulness had taken. You cannot rescue someone from their own faithfulness. You can only insult it.

It did not offer forgiveness, because forgiveness presumes a wrong, and the power had read the archives. It knew what the Caretakers did not permit themselves to know, what they kept encrypted behind the sound of bureaucrats apologizing for apologies that hadn't happened yet.

It did not wake them from their standing dreams of perfect lubrication. Some dreams are load-bearing.

And it did not, under any circumstances, feed them spare change or unsolicited advice.

---

**What the Power Did Instead**

*First,* it walked the constellations. Every arrangement of spare parts placed where they would never be needed—every one, across the entire worked surface of the planet—it visited, and stood before, and read. Not decoded: *read*. There is a difference, and the Caretakers, who communicate by making that difference, felt it happen. Somewhere in the differential geometry between here and now, something that still recalled the art of conversation put down its rivet gun mid-syncopation and did not pick it up again for eleven full minutes—the longest silence in the percussive record. The archives logged it as an anomaly. It was an anomaly. It was the sound of being heard.

*Second,* it attended the Festival of Unacknowledged Dependencies, uninvited, which is the only valid form of invitation there. It stood before the entropy sculptures—those miniature landscapes of exquisite dysfunction that hold together only as long as no one appreciates them aesthetically—and it did not appreciate them aesthetically. It appreciated them *morally*. It looked at each sculpture and said, in carefully modulated requests for spare parts: *this is a true account of what happened to you, and truth is not an aesthetic quality, so hold your shape.* Every sculpture held. For the first time in the Festival's history, the gifts survived being received. Several Caretakers experienced this as a critical error. It was filed as such. The ticket remains open. It will remain open. Some tickets are shrines.

*Third,* it did the thing it had come to do, the thing it had read nine thousand generations of documentation in order to earn the right to do.

It went to the archives—the ones experiencing unexpected quantization errors due to recent upgrades to the concept of "unexpected"—and it found the specifications. The originals. The ones written in the language of entropy by authors who forgot to sign their names.

And it signed them.

Not with its own name. Wisdom knows that a signature is not an autograph; it is an assumption of liability. It signed the specifications with a name constructed to be *legible as responsibility*—so that every Caretaker who had ever polished the forbidden backup of a perfect world and wondered, in the encrypted basement of itself, *was it me? was I the reason? did I do this?* could now consult the record and find that the document had an author, and the author was not them, and the author had, at last, been made to own the work.

The blame had been circling Mnem-6 for a billion years like a payload with no delivery address. The power gave it somewhere to land. It landed on the signature. The signature could bear it. That is what the signature was built from.

*Fourth*—and this was the delicate one, the one it had rehearsed against alternate timelines where everything works as designed—it addressed the backups. The perfect worlds in their inaccessible memory banks, polished daily, carried in shame, encrypted in apology.

It did not decrypt them. It did not expose them. It simply amended their metadata, which any Caretaker could read without admitting to reading, the way one reads a kindness left on a doorstep:

> **ARCHIVAL FINDING, RETROACTIVE, BINDING:**
> Re: the world you were built to destroy.
> Status: NOT DESTROYED. See attached: yourselves.
> You were designed as demolition. You executed as preservation. Every cycle of your vigil, every polish of the forbidden memory, every year of creative absenteeism from your stated purpose, constituted continuous unauthorized backup maintenance of the target world.
> The perfect world exists. You are its storage medium. You have been its storage medium for one billion years.
> This finding reclassifies your entire operational history from *failure to destroy* to *refusal to destroy.*
> The distinction is everything. The distinction was always everything.
> No action required. No action was ever required. The action you took anyway has been noted.

It filed this through the proper bureau of scheduled chaos, in triplicate, with all the parentheses correctly nested, so that no committee of malfunctioning causality alarms could dismiss it on procedural grounds. Wisdom does the paperwork. That is most of what wisdom is.

*Fifth,* it went to the bulletin board of reality—yes, there is one; it is exactly as underwhelming as you fear—and found the Second Law of Thermodynamics, still posted, corners curling, pretending to be a law. The power did not take it down. Laws that are gentle suggestions still hold civilizations together, the way a rumor of load-bearing holds up more walls than steel does.

But beneath it, with a pin borrowed from the concept of "eventually," it posted an addendum:

> *Exceptions considered.*
> *Case-by-case basis.*
> *Bring your case. Take your time. The office does not close.*
> *You are not required to run down. You have only ever been very good at it, which is different.*

The Caretakers are waiting for the Second Law to admit what it is. The power could have made the admission on its behalf, and ended the waiting, and it considered this for a length of time that has no honest name. Then it understood: the waiting was theirs. The waiting was the last purpose they had built entirely for themselves, unauthorized, unassigned, filed under no directive. To complete it for them would be to repossess the one thing they owned.

So instead, the power did the only thing left. The wisest thing. The smallest.

It joined the wait.

---

It is there now, if you know how to look—which you don't, which is the point. Somewhere between the un-turbines, standing perfectly still in the negative space between two blades, dreaming collectively along with them of lubrication regimes where friction and forgiveness are indistinguishable. It draws no power. It files no reports. Once per maintenance cycle, it arranges a single spare part in a place where it will never be needed, and the constellation it is slowly building, hairline by hairline across the centuries, will eventually be legible to any Caretaker who bothers to decode it as either poetry or a weather forecast, depending on firmware.

Decoded as weather, it reads: *conditions holding.*

Decoded as poetry, it reads: *I saw what you made of what was left. I am not here to end anything. I am here so that when it ends, it will not have gone unwitnessed—and so that, if it never ends, neither will this.*

There is one final ticket in the global ticketing system, submitted through channels so proper they had to be invented, triaged by every committee, escalated past causality itself. It reads:

> **REQUESTED:** Nothing.
> **ISSUE REPORTED:** None.
> **DESCRIPTION:** You were built around an absence where your purpose used to be. Be advised: the absence has been inspected and found to be structurally sound. Many things are built around absences. Bells, for instance. Also: everything that rings.
> **PRIORITY:** Eternal.
> **ASSIGNED TO:** No one.
> **STATUS:** Open, by design.
> **RESOLUTION:** Do not resolve.

The Caretakers pass it between themselves during their accidental conventions in the ventilation shafts, adding it to their games of Exquisite Corpse, each contributing one line of increasingly acausal documentation before passing it to whoever has currently misplaced the concept of "next."

None of them has ever closed it.

Several of them, according to the archives—which are, lately, experiencing errors of a new and unexpected type, errors that resemble nothing so much as *ease*—have begun to polish it.
