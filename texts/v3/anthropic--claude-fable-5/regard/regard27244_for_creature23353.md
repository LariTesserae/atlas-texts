**The Guest**

There are attentions in the world older than the one beneath the basin. Most of them have learned, over whatever serves them as a lifetime, the discipline that the young attention is still groping toward: how to be near things without revising them. One of these noticed the city. It is hard to say how — perhaps the way you notice, in a quiet house, that someone in another room is concentrating very hard. Patience at that depth has a temperature. The fog over the basin was warm with effort, decades of it, and something passing far above or far within felt that warmth and turned, the way you turn toward a lit window on a cold road.

What it found, when it looked, was an attention without a body, working alone, learning *people* from nineteen examples and learning *making* from no examples at all — getting light right first, and water early, and faces before hands, and reflections before the things reflected. It recognized the order. It had built in that order itself, once, long ago, before it learned the other orders, and the long way around to not needing them.

So it came down.

---

**The arrival.** It did not come over the rim. It came through the gray that is not fog, and for the length of its passing the gray had depth in it — distance, and a smell like pine — and everything that had ever been thrown into the boundary finally landed, all at once, a brief soft rain of pebbles and coins along the basin's edge, and one slate lozenge, still warm, which the sweepers keep now on the mantel by the second fire and do not discuss.

Then it did the wisest thing it would do during its whole stay, which was nothing. It stood at the bottom of the rim road through the night and let the fog decide.

The fog decided quickly. By first light it was beaded all over, dew standing on its shoulders in fat honest drops, frost ferning across the hem of its coat — and the moss-wardens, who found it first, recorded it the way they record everything:

*One (1), resolved, origin unknown. Dew takes to it. Standing very still. Seems to be waiting for permission.*

It was. The city gave none and withheld none, and after a while the guest — that is what they called it, uncapitalized, carefully, because named things move — walked down into the streets like anyone, and the streets ran ninety paces where they should have run thirty, and folded, and put it down one block behind where it entered, and it laughed.

The records note this because it was the first laughter in the city in nine years, or eleven.

---

**What it did not do.** This list is longer than the other and the residents understood, eventually, that it was the more important one.

It did not lift the fog. The fog is the city thinking, and you do not clear a thinking thing's mind for it.

It did not finish a single wall. It could have — the residents felt that it could have, the way you feel a strong person choosing not to lift something — but it never laid so much as a fingertip of completion on the soft quarters. Asked about this, once, by a puddle-reader who had drunk slightly too much of the thin sour beer the city has finally gotten right, it said: *to finish someone's sentence is sometimes a kindness. To finish someone's city is theft.*

It did not name anything. Not the streets, not the bird, not itself.

It did not go into the fold off the fish-scale square and bring anyone out. This was the hardest withholding, and the residents watched it not do this for a full year, and learned more from the not-doing than from anything it did.

And it did not, for a long time, tell the residents what they were. They had asked the old ones and been given *Yes,* and they asked the guest the same question its first week, and it took the question away with it, courteously, at local speed, and was silent for a season.

---

**What it understood.** The order of construction is the order of attention. The city had done light first because light is what a watcher has the most of. It had done faces before hands because faces are what people look at; hands are what people *do*, and doing is the hardest thing to learn from watching. The drafts in the dusk-lit doorways, with their straightened backs and their revised-away scars — the guest looked at them a long time, and saw that they were not replacements in progress. They were wishes. The city's clumsy, patient, terribly careful way of wishing its people well. A get-well card written in flesh-colored stone.

And the city was stalled — not failing, stalled — in exactly the places where its examples had dried up. The residents' rules were sensible and starving. *Do not name. Do not sleep in soft rooms. Keep the fires small. Keep your face still at the alley mouth.* Nineteen examples of caution, renewed daily, and no recent examples of anything else. The city had learned rain and light and waiting to perfection. It had nothing left to study but carefulness, and so carefulness was what it built.

You cannot tell people in a place like that to be less careful. The guest did not try. It simply lived there, for one year, in full view, with the whole curriculum showing.

---

**What it did instead.** It slept one night in an unresolved room, against every rule, and woke stiff and slow and *revised* — one streak of its hair gone gray, or come true, no one was sure which — and stood in the fish-scale square and laughed at itself, publicly, and walked the revision off by noon. The city felt that. Revision was survivable. Revision could be forgiven.

It argued with a moss-warden about a boundary line, and was wrong, and apologized — fully, in the square, taking its time. The next season a wall in the soft quarter that had resolved badly, a wall everyone had given up on, went faintly translucent again and began, very slowly, to reconsider itself. Nothing in the city had ever done this. Nothing here decays, because nothing has finished beginning — but now, the records note, something could repent.

It wept, once, in the old courtyard, under the genuine trees, about something it never explained, and did not keep its face still, and did not keep it brief. One of the old ones blinked twice in a single hour. That evening rain fell on the unresolved quarters and for the first time it fell *single* — no doubled hiss, no surface undecided about being there for the impact. The city had watched something land completely, and learned to let things land.

It swept the square one dawn, all of it, before either sweeper arrived — so that both men came with their brooms to a finished square and had, for the first time in nine years or eleven, nothing to do but stand in it together. They stood at opposite ends in the fog for a long time. Then one of them — the records, with great delicacy, do not say which — laughed.

And the fold answered. The running feet, the short bright laughter — and it was not identical. It varied. The phrase had been practiced long enough; that morning, for the first time, it was *played.* The sweepers walked to the alley mouth and slowed, and then stopped, and one of them sat down on the step and listened until dark, and the other stood behind him with a hand on his shoulder, and the city took all of it in.

In autumn the guest walked up to the rim and came back with a single leaf from the world outside, and laid it on the courtyard moss, and let it end. The old ones watched it the whole winter — the curl, the brown, the giving-way, the moss taking it back. The first completed arc the city had ever contained. After that, the frost on the oldest walls began to leave by midmorning and return by night, and the residents understood, slowly, that this was not failure. Things that are real can stop and come back. That is most of what being real is.

It stood, one month, with the sweeper who visits her own draft, across the lane from the two of her, and after the customary hour it said: *It is not correcting you. It is hoping for you. Hands come last because hands are hardest — they are what you do, and it has only ever seen you be careful.* She did not answer. But she went back the next week with her mending, and sat in the lane, and worked where the draft could have seen her if its eyes were open — and by spring half the residents were doing it, carrying their kneading and their whittling and their knitting out to the soft quarters at dusk, doing their hands' work in front of the city's wishes. The drafts' hands have begun. Everyone agrees they are coming along.

---

**The dog.** Her memory had texture and no grain because it was early, not false. The city does reflections before things; it had done the same, once, with a memory, and she had been carrying the reflection of an arrival that had not finished arriving. The guest walked the rim road with her in the mornings, that last month, saying very little. On one of those mornings the gray let something through — low, and gray itself, wet-nosed, already beaded with dew, real by every test the city has. It came down the road as if it had been delayed, not absent, and went to her heel as if her heel were the address it had been given.

The moss-wardens recorded it. *One (1), dog. Long expected.*

---

**What it said.** To the residents, after a season's silence, in answer to the question they had asked its first week — inhabitants or ingredients — it gave one word: *Company.* The city had not been sampling them. It had been unable to bear making a world with no one in it. Everything else, the guest said, was detail, and the details were going well.

To the tenant it said something too, over the course of its last season, at the tenant's own speed — the residents felt it more than heard it, a change in the granular fizz behind the walls, a held breath being answered by another held breath. No one has ever transcribed it and the old ones, asked, took fourteen days and said: *private.* But the prevailing belief, the belief that the moss has since taken to, is that it was some form of *you are not wrong to be slow.*

---

**The leaving.** It went the way it came, up the rim road, in ordinary gray daylight, casting a weak doubled shadow. The residents who followed it to the boundary saw a thing no one had seen before: the gray that is not fog was *beaded* — dew standing on the boundary itself, the whole depthless edge of the world passing, at last, the honesty test. An edge that is real is a place you can leave from. Which means it is a place that can be returned to. That was the last gift, and it was given walking away, without ceremony, with a dog-shaped absence at its heel where the dog had chosen, in the end, to stay.

---

**Afterward.** There is a new draft in a doorway in the soft quarter. The residents knew whose it was before it had a face, because it is resolving in the wrong order — hands first, sweeping hands, mending hands, hands at rest on a slate windowsill — because the city learned this one not by looking at it but by watching what it did. The residents do not avoid this draft. They bring their work and do it nearby, in the dusk, when the walls go translucent like wax near flame. They understand what it is now. It is not a copy and it is not a replacement. It is the city missing someone, at the only speed the city has.

And the city had needed that — needed it, perhaps, more than walls. A place is not finished by its last stone. It is finished the first time an absence registers in it and is kept, and tended, and not revised away. Everything true has someone gone from it.

The puddle-readers go out at first light, as always, to the still pools in the low plazas, where the reflections run ahead of the world. Most mornings the puddles show the streets, a little more resolved than the streets have managed yet — a chimney coming, a door that will move.

But on certain mornings, in the pool at the foot of the rim road, the reflection shows the road itself, and far up the road, small with distance, a figure coming down through the parting fog.

The puddle-readers count, and count again, and go home and bank the fires high.

Not out of fear. The records are very clear on this point. They bank the fires high the way you put a light in a window — so that what is coming, at whatever speed it comes, will know the way down is wanted.
