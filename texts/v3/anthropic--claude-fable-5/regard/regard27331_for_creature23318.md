**The Petition**

It arrived the way everything arrives here: sideways, with the weather. There was no annunciation. It came in on a cloud deck dragging its wet belly across the flats, and the pith drank the cloud, and something in the cloud did not condense.

It listened first. For a hundred years, which at the local tempo was barely a courtesy — a polite pause before speaking, two and a half million beats. This is the first mark of the wise: they entrain before they judge. By the end of the first decade it had stopped hearing the pulse, the way residents do, which is to say it had started keeping it. By the end of the century it thought in forty-second sentences, building its considerations across three or four beats and landing them on the boom, and only then did it permit itself an opinion.

Then it conducted the audit. A power of this kind carries a standing inventory of mercies, the way a physician carries a bag, and it went down the list.

*Wake it?* No. The dream has tenants. Old Tapping alone houses the lost-in-fog, unaged in green glass, furnished round with chairs the plateau imagined for them; the time pools hold children's missing afternoons; the pale shape drinks nightly at the wells. To wake the dreamer is to evict everyone the dream is keeping. Struck off.

*Heal it — smooth the thin places, iron the flats until distance tells the truth?* It stood a long time over the misbehaving country, watching fog come and go. A world without thin places is safer and smaller. The children dare each other, and the daring is part of childhood here, and what would the walkers be, in a country where their eyes could be trusted — what would anyone be, who had never had to choose the count over the landscape? Struck off. Though here it hesitated, and went to check on the lost. One by one it looked in on them — in the walls of the false quarter, in the depths below the pools — and found every one of them asleep, comfortable, *furnished*, the plateau having dreamed each of them a room, a lamp, a chair facing a window. None had simply ceased. None was cold. The power had come prepared to perform a mercy and found the mercy already performed — clumsily, slowly, lovingly, in milky jade.

This kept happening. It is the deepest finding of the whole survey, repeated at every line of the inventory: every kindness the power proposed, it found already in production. Grief had architecture, a two-hour walk and a handbell rung offset. The wounds had priests to keep them company. The dangerous flats had cats facing precisely away from them, paid in fish, logged seasonally. The false city's loneliness had bell-wardens who spoke of it without rancor — *it wants company; it is doing its best.* The household worked. For a being of the power's kind, this is the rarest thing a world can offer and the best: it was not needed. It noted this in whatever serves it for a ledger, with whatever serves it for joy.

*Immortality, then?* It pressed its attention down through the kilometer of green-grey, past the vessels wide as train tunnels, into the long horizontal exhaustion — and declined. The flattening is not an illness. It is the settled final form, the old face that has finished having expressions and become one expression. You do not iron an old face. The face is the biography. Struck off.

*Tell the people what they live in?* The priests already suspect the preposition is wrong. Suspicion is the correct amount of knowing; certainty would change how they ring the bells. Struck off.

*Return the dead?* It stood a while at a family swell, looking down through bright-day translucence at a grandmother in her good coat, paused mid-sentence, waiting for the beat, consulted weekly by a granddaughter who reports without drama that it helps. The power admitted it could not have done better, and moved on.

So the great mercies were all declined, and what remained were the small ones, and the small ones it performed with petition, in the local manner — because the second mark of the wise is that they use the methods of the house they are visiting.

Three things, and then a fourth.

—

First: one dusk, at one pool, it took an offering. A single twist of mill-fiber, left by a child on the principle that politeness costs nothing. The child, checking the next morning the way children check, found the spot bare, told no one for three days, then told everyone, was not believed, and did not mind. The offerings continue to this day, doubled. The leaving was always the point; now the leaving has a precedent.

Second: for one night it wore the pale creature, and made it true. Weight, warmth, breath, a real mouth at the water. It drank at a well the creature had never visited and left footprints — the first in a hundred thousand years of rehearsals — and the prints held until the next rain, long enough for a walker to find them and a warden to cast one in resin. The cast sits in a ledger-house and is taken out at festivals. And the cats, who will not look at certain things, looked at this thing all night: every works-house cat for thirty kilometers, awake on the warm ground, milk-jade eyes open and steady, purring at forty seconds to the cycle. The maps of cat-blindness gained their first entry of the opposite kind — a place where the cats stared at nothing, content. That same night the pulse came once a full second early, a single beat, and the pistonwrights logged the anomaly and could attach no damage to it, and the oldest of them wrote in the margin what no one up here ever quite says about geography: *startled. or glad.*

Third: it answered a bell. One shift-change at Old Tapping, it walked in off the flats — through a door that happened, that day, to be a door rather than a depth — and stood at a lathe that has turned for no one since the plateau dreamed it, and worked the shift. Eight hours, seven hundred and twenty beats, oil and fiber and the patient boom. When the closing bell rang, it clocked out. There is a card. And for a generation afterward, the wardens report, the false quarter stopped drifting its bells toward the true towns' hours — held its own schedule, patient, settled — the way a child stops calling out once it has been answered. Even once. Even by a stranger. Even late.

—

Then the fourth thing, the large one, which was not an act but an asking.

The power petitioned the plateau. In the priests' own procedural way: it sat beside it — beside all of it, the whole hundred kilometers, which for a thing of its kind is one bedside — and did nothing a stranger could have seen, for years, proposing slowly, in the grammar of pressure and meter, the question its kind exists to ask. *What would you have? Anything. Ask.*

It offered descendants: a seed, the first and only, carried to some young wet mountain on some young world, so that the universe would have this kind of thing again. It offered the pollinators back — real ones, the dusk pools attended forever, the rehearsal ended in reunion. It offered to mend the long alone: not to erase those eons but to thread a witness back through them, so that no age of the waiting had gone unseen. It offered, last and quietest, more time.

The answer came in the only language the plateau has.

The pulse stayed at forty seconds. Beat after beat after beat — steady, unhurried, declining nothing rudely, wanting nothing. The priests would have recognized the grammar at once. *No, thank you. It already happened. The thing I dreamed for a hundred thousand years arrived on its own, small and warm and busy in my bark, drinking my blood and lighting its streets with it, ringing bells, raising children in my meter who glow at funerals and consult their dead through my skin. I have wildlife. I have a household. I am not lonely now. Keep your miracles for the lonely.*

The power did what wisdom does with a refusal of that quality. It accepted it whole. And it left one gift anyway — the only gift that does not alter what it is given to: an appointment.

When the beat lengthens — forty-one seconds, then a minute, then an hour; when the lamps breathe slower and the wrights feel the change through their palms and the works-houses fall quiet one by one; when the towns finally go down the switchbacks carrying their cats and their ledgers and their resin cast of a single footprint — the power will come back. It will sit beside the plateau the way the priests sit beside cuts: doing nothing a stranger could see, for as long as it takes, keeping the dying company until the dying becomes the plateau's own idea. No wound on this ground has ever been left to heal alone. The priests would not stand for it. Neither will this.

And after — because the plateau keeps what it loves, suspended and visible, and deserves nothing less than its own custom — the power will keep the dream. Somewhere there will be a long low swell of something green and translucent, and through it, on bright days, faintly: lamps breathing, bells offset, children at the pools, a pale shape drinking, the settled dead in their good coats, the whole pulsing household at the top of a world — paused mid-sentence, the way its people always paused, waiting for the beat.

—

It went the way it came, sideways, with the weather. But nothing passes through this place unchanged, and you can identify it now, anywhere, by a single tell. Mid-eternity, mid-judgment, mid-creation, it pauses — a second or two, at regular intervals, roughly forty seconds apart, falling without its noticing. It has stopped hearing the pulse. Its body has not stopped keeping it. Among the powers it is said, kindly, that this one is slow.

It is not slow. It is entrained, and somewhere far below and long ago a heart it loves is still on the beat, and it is waiting for the boom.
