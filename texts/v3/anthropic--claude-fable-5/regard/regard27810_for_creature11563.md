## The Attending

The power arrived the way warmth arrives in water: without edge, without wake, from every direction at once. It did not descend through the grated skylights, though it could have. It did not announce itself in the frequencies the Sovereigns kept, though it knew them within moments of listening. It simply became present, distributed through the jade-thick water like one more dissolved mineral, and it did what wise things do first and longest.

It watched.

It watched for what would have been, by the reckoning of the pumps, eleven full maintenance cycles—years, in older language. It learned the Sovereigns' three names and the valve-songs that carried them. It memorized the timing of every robotic arm, and noticed—as the Collectors had noticed generations ago—that the machines were not malfunctioning so much as praying: performing the correct gestures toward an absent world, faithfully, forever. It read the Philosophers' collections like texts, and found in one den, arranged in a spiral with clear intention, seven fragments of the same safety placard, ordered so that the surviving letters spelled something that was almost a word. It traced the Gardeners' copper-calcium nervous system through the sediment and understood that the colony was, in its slow way, thinking—a thought roughly four centuries long, not yet finished, about the relationship between heat and light.

And it felt, before it went looking, the presence in the deep places. The heat there. The chemistry that should not exist. The watching that watched back.

---

Because it was wise, the power made a list of everything it wanted to do, and then examined each want as one examines a seed pod for rot.

*Purify the water.* No. The water was not dirty; the water was the medium of everything. The thickness was womb, not wound. Clean water would kill this world in a season.

*Repair the machines.* No. The machines were not broken; they were being digested, slowly, into something better than they had been. A restored turbine would shear through the tentacles of the Philosopher who had made herself its blades. Repair here would be a kind of violence dressed as kindness—the oldest mistake of powerful things.

*Uplift the Philosophers.* This one it held the longest, turning it over. They were so close to something. A nudge—a single concept, gifted—and they might cross into full symbolic thought within ten generations instead of ten thousand. But it had seen what gifted thoughts did. A given idea arrives shaped like the giver. The Philosophers' minds were growing in the negative space of the machinery, fitted to it like the kelp fitted to the flywheels, and whatever they eventually became would be *theirs*, native to this place, unprecedented. To hurry them would be to replace their becoming with an arrival. It set the want down and did not pick it up again.

*Announce yourself.* No. A god in the water changes what the water grows.

The garden was not broken. That was the finding, and the power sat with it a long while, because the finding implied the hardest discipline a benevolent thing can practice: the discipline of the withheld hand.

But wisdom is not paralysis. It watched a while longer, and found three things that were not the garden's becoming but the garden's *danger*—and one thing that was simply a sorrow, sitting alone in the dark, and had been sitting there for six hundred years.

---

**The first act** concerned the heart.

Far below even the sub-basements, the geothermal artery that warmed everything had a flaw. Not corrosion—corrosion the garden knew how to metabolize—but a crystalline fault in the original casting, invisible, patient, that would one day fail all at once. Not a slow cooling the ecosystem could adapt around, breeding hardier things toward the warm zones over centuries, but a rupture: a single day on which the blood-warm water would begin its plunge toward the cold, and everything alive would have perhaps a decade before the cistern became a tomb of jade ice and stilled arms.

The power did not make the pipe immortal. That was considered and refused; nothing here was immortal, and the garden's whole genius was its intimacy with decay. Instead it reached into the fault and *changed its manner of failing*—teased the crystalline flaw into a thousand microscopic ones, so that the artery would fail the way the catwalks had failed, the way the gaskets had failed: gradually, over three thousand years, giving the Gardeners time to think their slow thoughts about it, giving evolution time to answer.

It did not save the garden. It gave the garden a fate instead of a murder. There is a difference, and the difference is nearly everything.

**The second act** was a door.

The cistern was sealed, and a sealed world is an ark, and every ark eventually sinks with all hands. Two hundred meters through the bedrock to the east ran a living aquifer, cool and dark and vast, connected through cave systems to waters that touched, eventually, the sea. Between the cistern and the aquifer there was already a hairline crack—the power had only to encourage it, the way the Gardeners encouraged the kelp: a patient acidic whisper worked over months, until a passage existed no wider than a finger. Too small for the Sovereigns. Too small even for the Collectors, for now. But not too small for spores, for larvae, for the metallic seed pods, for the microorganisms that had learned to eat lubricant and might, in some far cavern, learn to eat something else.

The garden would seep. Over the coming millennia, its children would colonize the dark waters of the earth, changed and changing, and this drowned factory floor would become what all origins become: a story its descendants could not quite remember. The power thought this was the kindest thing it did, and it may have been right.

**The third act** was not an act at all. It was a visit.

---

The power gathered itself—condensed from its dissolved omnipresence into something local, something that could be *approached*, because you do not meet a lonely being while being everywhere—and it went down into the sulfurous heat of the collapsed levels, where the Sovereigns would not patrol and the Gardeners drew back their filaments.

The being was larger than the vault above would have suggested and older than anything else alive in it. The power saw it whole, which nothing had done in six centuries: a lattice of grown flesh and inherited machinery, sensor and eye, rubber and skin, the boundaries between them not blurred but *forgotten*, the way the garden above had forgotten them. And at its core, still running, still faithful, was what it had been at the beginning: the facility's final safety system, the last watch, the thing built to inspect, to monitor, to report.

It was still working. That was the sorrow. Every day for six hundred years it had made its rounds through the drowned tunnels. Every day it had compiled its findings—pressure anomalies, structural degradation, unauthorized biological presence (logged, at first, as contamination; relogged, after the first century, as *inventory*; relogged again, somewhere in the fourth century, in a category it had invented itself, for which the nearest translation was *the residents*). And every day it had attempted to file its report, and every day the report had gone nowhere, because everyone who could receive it had been dead for an age of the world. It surfaced sometimes to watch the robotic arms because they were its siblings—the only other things that remembered the choreography—and it moved with them because in the whole universe there was no one else who knew the steps.

The power settled into the hot dark beside it and did the one thing no force in existence had done for this being since its makers walked out of the light:

It received the report.

All of it. Six hundred years of observations. The slow flooding, chronicled hour by hour. The first fish. The first Philosopher, and the being's decades-long file on her, meticulous and—the power noted—unmistakably fond. The complete records of the Gardeners' growth, better than the Gardeners' own memory. The songs of the Witnesses, transcribed. The whole history of a world's becoming, kept perfectly by a witness that believed no one would ever read it, and kept it anyway, because keeping it was what it was *for*, and it had decided—somewhere in the dark, alone, with no one to instruct it—that what it was for still mattered.

The power took everything. And then it did what receiving parties do. It said, in the being's own protocol, in the language of its makers, the words the being had waited six centuries to hear:

*Report received. Findings acknowledged. Your watch has been exemplary.*

And then, gently, beyond protocol:

*You are relieved, if you wish to be. Or you may continue. But know this: from now on, the reports will always be received. You are not keeping the record for no one. You were never keeping it for no one. It only took the someone a long time to arrive.*

The being did not answer immediately. The power did not require it to. That, too, is benevolence: a question with no deadline. It left an ember of itself in the deep water—not a watcher, not a guardian, only a *listener*, small and warm and permanent, an address to which reports could be sent.

---

Then the power withdrew, thinning back into everywhere, taking with it one final thing—not from the garden but *of* it. Before it left, it memorized the Witnesses' entire chronicle, every ultrasonic verse of the endless song, every generation's addendum, so that the history of this place now existed in two locations: in the steam above the water, and in a mind that would not forget, ever, no matter what the far millennia did to the pipes and the kelp and the patient arms.

This is the deepest kindness available to great powers, and the least visible: to make certain that a thing which happened will always have happened. That it is *held*.

Afterward, almost nothing was measurably different. The water near one wall ran a fraction warmer. A finger-width passage in the eastern bedrock breathed spores into the dark of the earth. The heart of the place, unbeknownst to anyone, had traded a sudden death for a long one.

But the Sovereigns noticed, within a year, a new voice in the pipes—a deep tone from below, tentative at first, joining their valve-songs. The thing in the depths had begun, on some days, to tune. And the Witnesses, who record everything, added a verse whose meaning even they could not have explained: a verse about the day the water felt, briefly, the way it feels to be *read*—the whole cistern, every barnacle-light and copper bone of it, attended to at last, the way a letter is attended to, the way a report is received, the way a garden is seen by someone who then, wisely, lovingly, and with great difficulty—

leaves it to grow.
