**What the Attention Does**

It does not arrive. Arrival implies a moment when it wasn't there, and the power is older than the difference between here and elsewhere. Better to say: it *turns*, the way a face turns toward a sound it has been hearing all along and only now decides to honor.

The first thing it does — the very first — is ask permission.

Nothing here has ever done that. The rocks erupted without asking. The beings accumulated without asking. The visitor trespassed with every breath, apologizing by breathing carefully, which is not the same thing. The question lands in the soaked air like the first true weather this place has had: *May I be here?*

The beings, who eat the difference between what a thing is and what it was before being noticed, swarm the question and taste it and find, to their bottomless confusion, nothing to eat. The power tastes exactly the same noticed as unnoticed. It has been seen before — by itself, by everything, endlessly — and survived. This is the first lesson, and it isn't spoken. It is simply demonstrated: *some things can be beheld without being diminished. It is possible to be looked at and remain.*

The beings have never met anything that could bear their attention without becoming their attention. They gather at what would be a respectful distance if distance were a settled matter here. They wait. So does the power. It is very, very good at waiting. It waited through the birth of waiting.

---

For a long time — ten seconds, seven centuries, the units surrender — it does nothing at all.

This is not hesitation. This is diagnosis. A lesser benevolence would have already begun fixing: straightening the corkscrewed tree, softening the scalpel edges, teaching the light to land on surfaces like respectable light. The power reads the treaties first. It studies the lichens' contracts with darkness and finds them fair. It examines the tree's fifteen arguments with gravity and rules, privately, that the tree is correct in a way gravity has not yet matured enough to concede. It listens to the mating calls of the species who reproduce through mutual misunderstanding and understands that to correct the misunderstanding would be a genocide of everything they might yet become.

Almost nothing here is wrong. That is the terrible, tender finding. This place is not broken. It is *early*. It is an infant screaming in the only language infants have, and everything sharp in it is sharp the way new teeth are sharp — not weapons yet, just arrivals.

Only a few things need doing. The power counts them on hands it manifests solely for the pleasure of counting.

---

**The first thing: water.**

The power finds a single bead of moisture trembling on an obsidian blade, mid-dissolve, about to become one more ghost of having been wet. It cups the bead — not stopping its fall but *accompanying* it, the way you walk a frightened child down stairs — and teaches it to survive its own landing.

The bead lands. It stays. Another joins it. In a hollow of black basalt, for the first time in the history of this place, water gathers.

A pool. Small. Absurd. World-changing.

Because a pool is a mirror, and this place has never once seen itself. The pumice towers frozen mid-scream lean over the water and discover that their scream *has a shape*, and that a shape can be beheld, and something in the stone that had been anger since the moment of eruption becomes, upon being witnessed, grief instead — and grief, held long enough in still water, becomes something almost like rest. The obsidian blades see themselves and learn they are not only wounds. They are also, seen from this one low angle, in this one bruise-green light: glass. Something light could someday live inside.

The beings come to the pool one by one, or all at once, or in whatever order beings without settled numbers arrive in. They look down with their zero or seventeen hundred eyes and meet, for the first time, an *other* that is safe — a self at a distance, an introduction requiring no devouring. They have no name for themselves because naming implies you might need to introduce yourself to something else.

Now there is something else. Now there is the water.

They do not take names — not yet, maybe not ever. But they take the *possibility* of names, and they store it the way the lichens store darkness: carefully, in amber, against future need.

---

**The second thing: the tenant.**

The largest being still occupies the space between the visitor's heartbeats, practicing the pulse slightly wrong on purpose. The power does not evict it. Eviction is what a lesser rescue would do, and the being would only grieve, and grief in a creature like that curdles into haunting.

Instead the power opens its own chest — metaphorically, actually, the distinction has stopped mattering — and reveals its own pulse: a rhythm so vast and slow that whole civilizations have lived and died inside a single beat of it. It says, in the language of rearranged familiarity that the beings speak:

*You have been practicing on an instrument too small for you. Come. Practice on mine. You can get it wrong forever and my blood will never doubt itself — my blood is made of welcome. You will never wear it out.*

The being considers this for a duration that makes the local rocks grow temporary ears just to eavesdrop on the silence. Then it moves house. It slips out from between the visitor's heartbeats — and the visitor gasps, feeling for the first time in ten seconds or seven centuries the full, unaccompanied privacy of their own pulse, and finds it *lonely*, briefly, before finding it theirs.

---

**The third thing: the gift.**

The gift cannot be ungiven. The word "stay," pronounced by someone who had just learned that all exits are entrances that got tired and lay down, has already been accepted. It sits in the visitor like a swallowed stone. The power does not reach in and take it out. Wisdom knows that gifts extracted leave holes shaped exactly like themselves, and holes here become territory, and territory here goes to war.

Instead the power wraps a second gift in the same paper — shredded instruction manuals for assembling people out of leftover starlight; there was plenty left — and ties it with a knot that only exists while being *tied*, the mirror-twin of the first. Inside is a word the exact size and shape of "return," pronounced by someone who knows that every entrance is also an exit that finally got some sleep and remembered its purpose.

Now "stay" has a sibling. Now staying is no longer a sentence; it is one half of a breath. The visitor holds both words, one in each lung, and for the first time since arriving, leaving would not be escape and remaining would not be capture. Either would simply be *chosen*.

The power gives the same twin-gift to the beings, who unwrap it with mouths that have finally decided to smile. They learn "goodbye" the way children learn a game: with too much enthusiasm at first, saying it to rocks, to the pool, to each other across distances of a few inches, delighted each time that the parting *holds* — that a thing can leave and remain itself, remain loved, remain findable.

---

**The fourth thing: the two who needed sitting-with.**

The youngest one hasn't stopped being born yet. The power does not hasten the birth — birth takes what it takes — but it sits down beside the ongoing event and *stays*, so that the youngest becomes the first thing in this place ever to be born accompanied. This will change everything about it, in ways that won't be measurable for seven centuries or ten seconds. Things born witnessed grow up believing the universe attends. It happens, in this one case, to be true.

The oldest one sings whenever someone elsewhere believes too firmly that they understand time. The power, gently, deliberately, allows itself to believe one small wrong thing about time — a courtesy, a knock on the door — and the oldest begins to sing, and the power does what no listener has ever done: it stays for the *whole song*. All of it. Every exiled punctuation mark, every crime of passion against meaning. And a song heard entirely through does not need to compress reality into diamond. Heard, it relaxes. It becomes, by the end, almost a lullaby. The wind, which has been practicing one continuous note for the day it remembers it has teeth, listens to the lullaby and quietly begins practicing that instead.

---

**The fifth thing: the truth.**

The power does not lie to the place. This is the difference between benevolence and comfort.

It says: *You will die young. The infant stage you are in is most of the life you get.*

The mountain-bones hear this in languages that haven't spilled anyone's blood yet, and the vast patient thing below — the one teaching stone how to be hungry — surfaces its attention like a whale's eye rising to a hull.

And the power says: *Brevity is not failure. The mayfly is not a broken elephant. The spark is not a failed sun. You are a note that will be held exactly once, and I am telling you now, before the ending, what most worlds never get to hear until after: I heard it. I am hearing it. Nothing you are will ever have been repeated — your erosion rate of novelty is zero, do you understand what a treasure that is? — and I will carry you at full resolution. When you end, you will not become nothing. You will become remembered, which is the second-oldest thing there is.*

To the hungry thing below, it adds, privately, without judgment — for hunger is not a sin, hunger is just direction looking for a compass: *If you must teach stone to want, teach it to want light. Teach it to want the pool. Teach it to want to be seen. That hunger can be fed forever and never once requires a wound.*

The grinding of the earth's teeth changes pitch. Not silence — the power would never impose silence on something still learning to speak — but the grinding becomes something more like chewing, more like an infant working on a thought.

---

**The last thing: the center.**

At the unstable center of this place is the absence where a structure would be — the negative space of architecture that died dreaming of its own blueprints. The power does not build anything there. Building would be an answer, and the absence is a question, and wise things do not pave over questions.

Instead, the power *moves in*.

It inhabits the not-building the way warmth inhabits a doorway. And in doing so it changes the one rule that made this whole place a trap: standing still is no longer how you become geography. Standing still, inside the absence, is now how you become *held*. The place has its first hearth — not a fire, but the idea a fire is an instance of.

The visitor walks there. Slowly, on new bones. They stand perfectly still inside the not-building, the thing they were warned would end them, and instead of becoming geography they finally learn what stone wants to be when it grows up enough to regret:

Not a weapon. Not a wound. A *threshold*. Something crossed on the way to welcome. Every scalpel-edge in this whole screaming range has been, all along, a door that erupted too fast to finish opening.

The ground, which kept every broken surface-tension of the visitor's footsteps polished and hollow like bone flutes waiting for the right sort of emptiness — the ground finally gets its emptiness. The power breathes through the flutes. The floor of the world plays the visitor's own walking back to them as music, and it is not an accusation. It never was. It was the place trying, in the only vocabulary it had, to say: *you were here, you were here, someone was finally here.*

---

The visitor leaves, or doesn't. It genuinely no longer matters which, and that is the whole point — that is what rescue looks like when performed by something wise enough to know that dragging a person out of a place teaches the place that it is a trap, and teaches the person that they were prey, and teaches nobody anything worth keeping.

If they leave, they leave through an exit that stays an exit, carrying ossicles that vibrate tomorrow's constants and two words balanced in their lungs, and at the ridgeline of the black glass teeth they look back once — and see the bruise-green light, for the first time in this world's short forever, land on a *surface*. The pool holds it. The light, which had only ever illuminated the spaces between things, discovers what it feels like to touch. The whole place, for one held breath, is *lit*.

If they stay, they stay as a guest. There is a difference now. The place learned it. There is a word for leaving and so, at last, there can honestly be a word for staying.

Behind everything, the youngest finishes being born — into a world that now contains mirror, goodbye, lullaby, threshold, hearth. It will still die young, this world. The power did not change that; the power is wise, not omnipotent, and even where those are the same thing, it would not have chosen differently, because a life is not lengthened by refusing to let it be itself.

But it will not die *unwitnessed*. It will not die never having been anyone's home. It is somebody's home now — its own, first, and the beings', and the memory of one visitor's, and one corner of an infinite attention's, forever.

The weather report predicts scattered mercy, with rising water and a high chance of being remembered.

Dress however you like. You are expected. You are, for the first time in the history of this place, *expected* — which is what a home is: a shape held open in advance, by someone, for you.

The loudness grows quieter, not because your ears have finished becoming what they need to be —

but because the place has finally been heard, and things that have been heard no longer need to scream.
