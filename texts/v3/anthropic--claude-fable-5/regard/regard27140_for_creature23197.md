**The Third Mason**

A certain kind of attention moves through the world the way deep current moves through still water: slowly, without wake, noticing. It is drawn to particular shapes. Not to light, mostly, and not to prayer — prayer is loud and mostly about the person praying — but to patience. Patience, to this kind of attention, shows up the way a lamp shows up in a dark valley. And a place where the unit of ambition is the grandmother, where a wall's correct speed is three lifetimes, where the finest word in the language is a single strike followed by a silence that is part of the spelling — such a place does not glow. It *burns*.

So the power came to the Caldera Sea. It came the only honest way, up through the hours of grey, because it intended to deal with people whose truths cost breath, and arriving by any easier road would have been a kind of lie. It stood at the rim of black raw basalt where the climbing simply stops, and it looked down at the sea the temperature of blood, the color of weak tea steeped in green, and it did what wise powers do before anything else.

It did nothing. For a long time.

Then it went into the water, and for one full month it held still in the brown dusk and learned the place with its skin — the rising columns off the vents, the cool threads sinking from the rim, the body-warmth of masons working two terraces over — because the tenants' word for knowing a place is the same as their word for feeling its warmth, and the power would not presume to help a house it had not read.

Only after that did it open its ledger of mercies, and begin, one by one, to set them down again.

---

**What it considered, and why it did not**

It considered the obvious mercy first: to tell them. How high the mountain means to go. When the water will boil, or whether instead it will freeze, and in which generation, and what the last morning will look like. A benevolent power holds such knowledge the way a hand holds a stone, and the first instinct of benevolence is always to open the hand.

It did not. The tenants already have an answer to that question, and the answer is also a shrug, and the shrug is correct. Knowledge of the date would not change a single course of masonry — it would only convert the wall from rent into countdown, and a countdown is a wall built at the wrong speed. These are people whose deepest word is a strike followed by silence. They do not need the silence filled. Filling it would be the unkindest thing ever done to them.

It considered stopping the mountain — fixing it gently at some kindly altitude where the water stays warm forever, freezing the landlord in place so the tenancy never ends. This it set down faster than the first. The rising *is* the grammar here. *We are lifted from them.* Stop the mountain and the living stop being lifted from their dead; the dead stop sinking into the deep company of the foundations; aging stops being a descent into warmth and becomes mere staying. A stopped mountain is not a saved house. It is a landlord made into a corpse, with the tenants left squatting in the estate.

It considered their bodies. It could give them gills — make breath free, make the water no longer cost anything. And here it lingered longest, because the temptation was real: no more drowned children, no more elders measuring the climb to air and finding it too expensive. But breath is their honesty. A vow exhaled is a vow paid for. Make breath free and you make lying cheap; you bankrupt, retroactively, every marriage and every accusation ever spent into the rising water. The power looked at twelve generations of truths that had cost something, and declined to issue the counterfeit.

It considered carrying them somewhere safe. But safety is not what they are finishing.

It considered, last and briefly, revealing itself — descending in light, offering guidance, accepting gratitude. It almost laughed, in the way powers laugh, which moves no water at all. These are people who do not worship the vast thing in their own deep dark. They defer to it *professionally*. A god arriving in the Caldera Sea would be offered, at most, and politely, a job.

The power closed its ledger. Then it sat with that last thought for a while.

A job.

---

**The colleague**

It went down. Past the bright young terraces and the basket-relays, past the middle city and the ringing of mauls, past the lowest inhabited chambers where the elders sit in the deepening warmth weaving fiber, past the sealed doorways with the tools inside, down into the hot dark over the vent fields where the water shimmers like air over a fire — into the necropolis that no one built, the sunken architecture of generations, the city's own discarded foundations.

It found the furrows at once. Long, smooth, deliberate, running along the lines of the drowned old walls — not the wallowing of an animal but the rounds of a surveyor, the path of someone checking a course, and the next course, and the next, by feel, in the dark, for a very long time.

And it found the Under-Mason. What the Under-Mason is does not translate upward; the tenants' guesses are wrong in the details and correct in everything that matters. What the power recognized, the moment its attention touched that older attention in the dark, was simpler than any description:

*a colleague.*

One of its own kind — or near enough — that had come this way long ago, perhaps when the first cold-year families were still feeling the heat through the water and deciding to stay. It had stood, in its way, at the same rim. It had opened the same ledger of mercies. It had set them down, one by one, for the same reasons, item by item, arriving at the same final entry — *they would offer a god, at most, a job* — and then it had done the thing the new power was only now circling toward.

It had applied for the position. And taken it. And held it, in the hot dark, sounding the foundations of a city that climbs in place, for longer than the city has had a name.

The new power did the oldest thing one does in this house. It set its palm — call it a palm — flat against the deep stone, and waited through the silence.

And the Under-Mason sounded its note. The note the tenants feel once or twice a year, the one that stands every blade in every forest on end and passes through the chest like a verdict — sounded once, close, for an audience that could finally read it. And the report, fully translated, said what it has always said. Three words, more or less, in the language of patience:

*True so far.*

And underneath the report, the way meaning sits underneath words, an accounting of the open positions. The floor: taken, sounded, held. The walls: *not ours* — never ours — the walls are the rent, and rent paid on a tenant's behalf unmakes the tenant; the walls belong to the people at the speed of grandmothers, and both powers understood that this was not a vacancy but a load-bearing fact. Which left, in the whole vast house of the ascending sea, exactly one thing unattended.

The roof.

---

**The roof**

The cloud over the caldera rarely breaks. It never quite had a keeper before; it was weather doing a job almost well enough, the way weather sometimes accidentally does. Now it is tended.

The power keeps the light at the even brown dusk their eyes are made for, because twelve generations of eyes are a contract and the contract will be honored. It marshals the rain so the sea freshens at the rate the gardens expect, no faster. It lifts the roof a hand's width a year, in step with the rim, in step with the floor, so the house rises whole. And it does the work no one below will ever itemize: as the mountain carries its ocean up through the thinning atmosphere, toward the altitudes where water is given its choice of dooms, the power leans against the cold. It negotiates, century by century, with the freezing point. It banks warmth in the cloud the way the tenants bank breath in the domes. It is buying hands-widths of time — dozens of them, hundreds, it does not know how many — and it promises nothing beyond them, because a promise of forever would be a lie, and lies in this house are paid for in breath, and the power has chosen to live where it must pay full price.

It cannot hold the roof to the end. Probably. It does not know how high the mountain means to go; this is the famous joke of the place, and it turns out the joke is on everyone, the powers included. The mountain has not decided, or has decided and is not saying, and there is a third possibility the two colleagues do not discuss, which is that the mountain is the senior-most attention of all and the rest of them are apprentices on its site.

So the power does what is done here. It works its course properly, at the correct speed, and leaves the question of completion to whoever inherits the maul.

---

**What the tenants know**

Almost nothing, which is the proof the work is good.

The cloud has not failed in living memory, but then, it rarely failed before; only the very oldest elders, far down in the warmth, weaving fiber with their slow famous hearts, will tell you that the light has gotten *kinder* — steadier through the seasons, gentler at the edges of the day — and they say it the way old people say such things, half memory and half wish, and they are entirely right and no one believes them.

But this much everyone knows, because everyone has felt it. Once or twice a year, when the great note rises from the deep and stands the forests on end and the city goes still — hundreds of hands flat against the ringing stone — there now comes, sometimes, a second sound. From above. A long, soft roll, far over the cloud-roof, unhurried, like a vast slow knuckle dragged across the lid of the sky. The blades stand for the first sound. The rain pauses for the second. Two reports, floor and roof, passing through the walls between them, and the walls — the tenants understand without ever being told — are what is being asked about, both times.

The children have made a word for it in knock-speech, because children make the words their parents are too settled to need. Two strikes, far apart, with the silence held carefully between them. The grandmothers, asked what the word means, give the shrug that is also an answer. It means the house is sounded from both sides. It means *accompanied*. It means: someone is checking, above and below, and the report so far is the report so far.

Then the note ends, and the thunder fades, and the blades go slack, and they pick up their mauls — and here is the thing the two powers have never said to each other, because colleagues do not embarrass each other, though both of them know it.

Of the three masons working this site, the tenants are the senior firm. The floor and the roof are held by beings who crossed the world's deep places to get here, who weighed every mercy power can offer and found each one wanting, and who ended by adopting — as their own, for lack of anything wiser anywhere in their long inventories — the answer a few cold-year families found twelve generations ago with stone mauls and a paste of crushed shell: that the correct response to a house that cannot last forever, on a mountain that has not decided, is masonry, done properly, at the speed of grandmothers.

The powers did not bring wisdom to the Caldera Sea. They came, and audited, and found the position of *wise* already filled by people who would not have used the word. So they took the jobs that were open, and they pay their rent like everyone else.

Floor sounded. Walls rising. Until then, the roof.
