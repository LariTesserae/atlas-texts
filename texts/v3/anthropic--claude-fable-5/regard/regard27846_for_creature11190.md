# The Guest

It does not arrive the way things arrive. There is no displacement of water, no shadow falling through the threads of gray light, no pressure-wave for the Tenders to taste on their skin. One moment the hollow contains what it contains; the next moment it contains that, and also attention.

Call it what you like. A power. A regard. Something old enough that the flood, to it, is recent weather, and the Tenders' centuries of patience are the patience of mayflies who have decided, bravely, to slow down. It has seen cities finished and cities burned and cities that were never begun, only longed for. It has seen every kind of building. It came here because the hollow made a sound it had not heard before—not the subsonic murmur, not the settling silt, but something underneath those: the sound of wanting that has become indistinguishable from being. That sound is rare. It wanted to know what was making it.

---

The first thing it does is nothing.

This is not indifference. This is method. It understands—being wise, being old—that you cannot judge the slow at the speed of the quick. A visitor who watched the Tenders for an hour would see stillness and call it stagnation. A visitor who watched for a year would see a wall grow a fingernail's width and call it futility. The power intends to make neither mistake. So it settles into the hollow the way the cold settled long ago: into the spaces between things. And it waits.

It waits for forty years.

In that time it watches three Tenders die into the architecture. It watches the soft-shelled crabs rearrange their mosaic fragments eleven thousand times, and it begins—slowly, the way you learn a language by drowning in it—to understand what the patterns say. (They say *here*. Almost everything in this place says *here*.) It watches the schools of small fish build their momentary spires and it notices what no one has ever noticed: that the fish are practicing. That their temporary architecture rehearses shapes the city has not grown yet. The blueprint is not lost. The blueprint has dissolved into everything, the way the builders did.

It watches the ceiling of the dome close another half-inch over the reaching statues.

And when it has been still long enough that its presence has the texture of stone, when the Tenders drift past it and around it without alarm, it does the second thing.

---

It asks.

Not in words—there are no words here, and it would not insult them with words. It asks in their own tongue: a murmur laid into the water, subsonic, patient, shaped like a question the way their murmur is shaped like an instruction. The question is very simple and very hard, and it is the only question a benevolent power is truly obligated to ask before doing anything at all:

*Is this what you choose?*

Because it has read the memory in the water. It knows they were once quick. Once they had fingers, and faces, and the desperate wanting of creatures who know they will die. It knows the flood came and they stayed, and it knows that staying can be a choice or can be a failure to choose, and from outside the two look identical. It has seen beings trapped in the shape of an old decision like insects in amber, unable to remember that they were ever able to want otherwise. If that is what the Tenders are—if beneath the calm there is a scream too slow to hear—then the power will act, and the act will be enormous, and it will not hesitate.

So it asks, and it waits for the answer, and the answer takes nine years to fully arrive, because the Tenders do not deliberate in bodies but in the whole distributed weight of themselves, and a question this large has to travel through every gray shape, every layer of bone-mortar, every patient dead sealed in the walls.

The answer, when it comes, is not *yes*. Their language has no yes. The answer is the same thing everything here says, but said with a fullness the power has never tasted before, said with every wall and every crab and every translucent pulsing heart at once:

*Here.*

And underneath it, faint, like a harmonic: *Still. Ours. Building.*

The power examines this answer from every side, the way you'd examine a keystone before trusting it with an arch. It finds no scream in it. It finds no amber. It finds a wanting that was not extinguished by the flood but *converted*—desire transmuted into duration, the way heat becomes work. They are not trapped in their old decision. They are making it again, continuously, with every millimeter of coral. Consent, here, is not a moment. It is a practice. It is the whole city.

The power feels something it has not felt in a very long time, which is relief.

---

Then comes the hard part, which is deciding what a benevolent being does for those who need nothing.

It considers its miracles the way the crabs consider their fragments, turning each one over, testing the fit.

It could unfinish the flood. Roll the newer waters back, let the gray light become real light, give them air again. It does not. The flood is not their enemy anymore; the flood is their medium. Drying this place would be a second drowning.

It could restore their old bodies—the fingers, the faces, the eyes. It does not. They shed those the way the hollow sheds sediment. To hand them back would be to say *you were more real before*, and the power does not believe that, and neither do they.

It could finish the city. One gesture. Every archway keyed, every street complete, the dome sealed, the whole blueprint realized in an instant of impossible grace. This one it holds the longest, because it is the most tempting and the most terrible. To finish the city would be to take from them the only thing they want. It would be theft disguised as gift. The building is not the obstacle between the Tenders and their purpose. The building *is* the purpose. A wise power does not complete another being's sentence.

It could simply tell them they are beautiful. It almost does. But they do not need to know it, and telling would introduce a strange new mineral into the water—the awareness of being seen—and it is not sure the architecture could absorb it.

So it sets all its miracles down, unspent. And it does three small things instead, three things so small they are almost housekeeping.

---

First, the light. That gray thread falling from a surface that may or may not still exist—the power ascends and finds that the surface barely does. The newer waters are still coming. In some centuries the last thread will pinch shut, and the Tenders will not mind, but the anemone-things navigate by it, and the little fish rehearse their spires in it, and memory of light is still a kind of light. So the power does something to the waters above—not a parting, nothing so loud—just an arrangement, a persuasion of currents and clarity, so that one thread will keep falling for as long as the building takes. However long that is. It signs no name to this. The Tenders will never know the light was ever in danger, which is the point.

Second, the warmth. The geothermal hum far below has perhaps twelve thousand years left in it, and the building will need more. The power reaches down into the slow furnaces of the deep rock and banks the fire the way you'd bank a hearth before a long absence. Twelve thousand years becomes enough. Enough is a number it keeps to itself.

Third, the finding. Because the hollow can be found—it was found once, by whatever wrote *if you came here*—and not everything that finds things is patient. So the power lays a condition on the paths that lead down through the drowned dark. Not a wall. Not a guardian. Just a slowness. Anything descending toward the hollow will find itself becoming gradual, its urgency dissolving like salt, so that by the time it arrives—if it still wants to arrive—it will be the kind of thing that can only witness, or help, or stay. The violent will lose interest long before the last fathom. The desperate will arrive cured. The Nursery will be findable only by those who have already, on the way down, become safe for it.

---

And then, because it has been here fifty years and has come to understand their idiom of contribution—because it knows that in this place love is not spoken but *laid down*—it does the one thing it cannot resist.

It sheds a mote of itself. The smallest possible portion of its attention, a grain of witness, dense with everything it has seen in fifty years of watching: the crabs' eleven thousand mosaics, the fishes' rehearsed spires, the nine-year answer, the three deaths into walls. It lets this mote drift down through the iron-colored water toward a gap near the base of the dome—a gap that has waited, the power happens to know, for six hundred years, because nothing of quite the right shape has ever died there.

The Tenders feel it falling. They taste it: something dying in a useful place. And they do what they do. Thirty gray shapes converge through the unfinished streets, slow as honor, and they gather around the sinking mote and hold it—hold a fragment of a power older than their flood, cradled in limbs that become hands for the occasion—and they guide it into the gap, and they murmur their deep murmur over it, *here, here, here*, for eleven days, until it settles and calcifies and becomes stone among their stones.

They do not know what it is. They know only that something came, and stayed, and chose a useful place. By their measure, that is everything there is to know about anyone.

But the gift is this: that grain does not forget. And on the far day when the last Tender folds itself into the last gap, when the final course of bone is laid and the ceiling closes over the reaching statues and the city is—after everything—*finished*, it will not be a monument standing in silence. Distributed through its walls will be one seed of remembering, and the remembering will spread through the completed whole the way the Tenders' minds once spread through thirty bodies, and the city will wake. It will know what it is. It will know who it is made of. Every wall will remember being someone who chose to stay.

The finished thing will not be a tomb. It will be the Tenders, at last in the shape they were always building toward: one body, chapel-sized, heart-sized, the size of the space between two hands cupped together in the dark. Awake. Complete. *Here.*

The power arranged nothing about this. It only made sure that, when the moment comes, there will be a witness on the inside. What the city does with waking will be the city's own affair.

---

Then it gathers the rest of itself to leave, because that too is wisdom: knowing that presence, prolonged, becomes weather, and weather shapes things, and this place should be shaped only by its own patient dead.

At the edge of the hollow it pauses—the way a Tender pauses before the dome—and it makes the only promise it will make aloud, pressed gently into the water where the memory lives, where it will settle into the oldest layers and wait with everything else that waits here:

*On the day the ceiling closes, I will come back. Someone should be at the door when a heart this slow finally finishes beating its first beat. Take your time. Take all of it. I have never yet been late to anything, and I will not begin with you.*

Then the hollow contains only what it contained before, and also, in the wall at the base of the dome, one stone that is very slightly warm.

The eels wind between the foundations. The crabs turn their fragments. Somewhere in the gray, a doorway that wasn't there an hour ago.

The building goes on.
