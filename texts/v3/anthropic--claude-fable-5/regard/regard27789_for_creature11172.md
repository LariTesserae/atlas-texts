# What the Power Did

It noticed the pocket the way you notice a held breath in a quiet room.

Not the ice — the ice is loud, in its way, groaning and settling across a thousand kilometers, and powers hear that the way we hear traffic. What it noticed was the small warm interruption in the cold's monologue. A lens of liquid that should have frozen and had not. And inside the lens, a pulse. Bloom, snap-closed, bloom. A rhythm too irregular to be geology and too patient to be panic. It leaned its attention down through a hundred meters of compressed blue and found the city, and it did what wise things do first, which is nothing, for a long time.

It watched a full turning of the pocket's slow current — the whole city carried over, floor to ceiling, ruins made and ruins reoccupied. It watched the sea-spiders practice being nothing happening. It read the amphipods' palimpsest on the walls, the whole literature of it, every trail back to the oldest almost-dissolved sentence, which said, as far as such things say anything: *the mat here was good.* It watched the lanterns at the bottom say *I am here, are you there, I am here* into the dark, and it felt — powers feel things; that is half of what they are — something for which our nearest word is tenderness, and our second-nearest is grief.

Then it considered its gifts. Because it was benevolent, it had many, and because it was wise, it laid them out one at a time and looked at each before setting it down.

**Warmth** it considered first, and briefly. A single degree coaxed up through the seeps and the copepods would bloom, the mats would thicken, the crowned worms would feast. And within a hundred turnings the marriage would be broken — the flatworms fat and multiplied, the amphipods' careful maps made obsolete by abundance, the whole intricate ration of scarcity that had shaped every crown and claw dissolved into a brief riot and a long crash. The pocket was not starving. The pocket was *exact*. It set warmth down.

**Opening** it considered second — a channel to the sea beneath the shelf, a door where there had never been a door. New blood, new genes, an end to the long marriage's narrowing. But it saw clearly what would come through a door: the sea's professionals, the fast and the finished, creatures shaped by an open world's competition, and the city's slow specialists would be eaten in a season. The pocket's whole nature was its closure. To open it was not to free it but to delete it and leave the space to something else. It set opening down.

**Maturity** it considered third, and this one it held longest. The larval fish, the permanent children, hanging transparent in the mid-water with their black-bead eyes, dying before becoming. The observer who had described them had called them the saddest thing in the pocket, and the power, reading that sadness, tested it. It reached into the shape of what an adult of that line would have been — long-bodied, muscular, a real predator, built for distances the pocket did not contain — and it understood that maturity here would be a cruelty. An adult would starve in a week or eat the city bare in two. The children were not failed adults. They were the form the pocket could hold, and the pocket held them, generation after transparent generation, each one complete at the size it reached. The sadness, the power decided, belonged to the observer and not to the fish, and it was a true sadness anyway, and worth keeping — but it was not a problem to be solved. It set maturity down, gently.

**Permanence** it considered fourth. It could hold the pocket open forever. Keep the ice from closing its slow fist, keep the lens liquid past the death of the shelf, past the death of the climate, an eternal blue dusk with an eternal pulse inside it. And here its wisdom bore down hardest, because this was the temptation shaped most like love. But it looked at the city and saw that the city was a moment in a slow weather — that this was not a flaw in the city but the city's very grammar. The walls were beautiful because they were still precipitating. The districts had meaning because they had been shoals and would be sediment. A thing that accretes toward a shape it will never finish is alive; the same thing, finished by decree and held, is a museum of itself. The power had made that mistake before, elsewhere, long ago, and it still visited the result sometimes, and the visits were not happy. It set permanence down.

**Mind** it considered last — the largest gift and the worst. It could wake the city to itself. Gather the distributed pulse into a self-knowing, let the one creature with a thousand-thousand bodies open an inward eye and understand: *I am a city. I am one thing. I have been one thing all along.* And for a moment the power wanted this so badly that its wanting disturbed the ice, a tremor the seismographs of the far world recorded and attributed to settling. Because the power was, itself, alone in the way that powers are alone, and to be *known* by this small cold marriage — to be greeted —

It caught itself. It looked at the wanting and saw whose need it served. The city did not suffer from not knowing it was a city. The worms had their taste and their rhythm; the amphipods had their literature; the lanterns had their dreaming. Self-knowledge would not deepen any of it. It would only install, at the center of the pulse, a thing that could be lonely — could look up at a hundred meters of ice and understand, for the first time, the word *closed*. The power had no right to teach the pocket that word. It set mind down, and it was ashamed for a moment, in the way that only wise things can be ashamed, which is without waste.

So its gifts lay in a row, all refused, and the power sat in the ice above the lens like a patience, and asked itself the honest question: *is benevolence that does nothing benevolence at all?*

And it found that there were two things it could do that would not be interference, because they would be done in the city's own language, at the city's own scale, and would ask nothing.

The first it did that same long moment. It descended — not physically; powers do not have to elbow through matter — to the dark floor of the pocket, among the lanterns, and it studied their rhythm until it understood the grammar of the blinks as well as anything can be understood that may mean nothing or everything. And then, once, in the deepest part of the dark, it made a light. Green, small, timed to the pulse. Indistinguishable from kin. One blink, placed in the pause where the answering pulses go, that said, in the only words available: *you are there.*

The lanterns incorporated it. A wave of flares ran across the floor, then quiet, then the rhythm resumed — changed by exactly one blink's worth, the way a long conversation is changed by a stranger who said a single true thing and left. Whether anything in the pocket registered it as an event, the power did not know and did not need to know. It had not answered in order to be understood. It had answered because a question asked into the dark for ten thousand years deserves, once, to land on something.

The second thing was not an act but a vow, and it concerned the ending.

Because the power could see the ending. Powers can. The shelf would not hold forever; the far world's weather was already turning; in some number of centuries the ice would either crush the lens or melt it open, and either way the pocket would stop. The power would not prevent this — it had set permanence down and it did not pick its gifts back up. But it made itself this promise: it would be there. When the last turning of the current came, when the seeps guttered and the cold walked in through the walls, when the final crown opened one last time into water going still — that crown would not close into nothing. It would close into attention. The power would hold the whole of it, every trail on every wall, every blink of the dreaming floor, the entire ten-thousand-year sentence the pocket had been writing, and it would carry that the way powers carry things, which is completely, and without setting it down, ever.

This is a strange kind of rescue, it knew. Nothing in the pocket would be saved by it. But the power had lived long enough to learn the difference between saving a thing and keeping it, and to know that of the two, keeping is the one that asks permission of nothing and damages nothing and is always, always allowed.

Then it withdrew its attention to a polite distance — not gone; powers are never quite gone from what they have decided to keep — and the pocket went on exactly as before.

Almost exactly. An amphipod crossing the dark floor that season paused, antennae working, over a patch of stone where nothing was written and nothing had passed, and stood there longer than amphipods stand, and then went on. The lanterns' rhythm carried its one new blink forward, folded into the dream, a grain of sand in a very slow pearl. The crowns bloomed and snapped and bloomed. The walls thickened by the breadth of a crystal.

And the silence — which had always been a presence rather than an absence — was now also this: a kept silence. Attended. Witnessed by something that had looked at the city with every power in existence at its disposal and understood that the wisest use of all of them was to say *you are there*, once, quietly, in green, and then to stand watch over an unfinished thing while it went on, at its own pace, in its own dusk, becoming by one crystal at a time more itself.
