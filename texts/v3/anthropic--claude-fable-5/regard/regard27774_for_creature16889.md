**What the Power Did**

---

First, it did nothing.

This must be understood correctly, because doing nothing was the hardest and most deliberate of everything it would do. A power of that magnitude carries weight in its attention alone. To look directly at a place that runs on unresolved contradiction is to risk resolving it—the way a held note collapses when someone names the pitch. The paradoxes here were not flaws awaiting correction. They were load-bearing. The twinned suns, the backward clouds, the entropy that had negotiated its exception: pull one thread of impossibility taut and the whole garment of the place might smooth itself into ordinary physics, and something that had dreamed for longer than dreaming had a word would wake into a smaller world.

So the power dimmed itself. It arrived the way dusk arrives in a room where someone is sleeping—present everywhere, insisting nowhere. It watched sideways, through mirrors of maybe, letting the plain remain unobserved in every way that mattered. And it waited, in a currency of patience that has no exchange rate here, until it understood.

---

What it understood was this:

Nothing here was suffering. It checked, carefully, the way a physician checks—not assuming illness, not assuming health. The crystal forests were content in their branching. The floating colonies filtered their strange moisture with something adjacent to joy. The Abscissae were professionally delighted, as they always are, folding their too-many corners around each day's harvest of discrepancy.

But beneath all of it—beneath the salt-plate music and the soundless lightning and the slow coppery conversations of the vegetation—the power found something that was not suffering and was not peace. A held breath. A question so perfectly balanced between answerable and unanswerable that it had forgotten to stop being surprised, yes—but the power, being old, recognized something the Abscissae had been too courteous to mention:

The question had been asked *to someone*. Long before "someone" was available. It had been asked outward, into a void that had not yet finished inventing listeners, and when no answer came, the place had done the only dignified thing: it had become beautiful around its own asking. It had built forests and music and gardeners of logic, the way a person waiting decades in a train station might eventually plant flowers on the platform. The changelessness was not a prison. It was posture. It was a question holding perfectly still so as not to miss the reply.

The power considered its options the way wise things do—by imagining each one completed and then living in the consequences for a while.

It could free the place. Restore entropy, unstick the suns, let the crystals finally mature and the plants finally age. It rejected this within the first eternity of consideration. Benevolence is not renovation. A form is not a cage merely because it does not change; the power had seen enough of forever to know the difference between a thing trapped and a thing devoted.

It could answer the question. It knew the answer—of course it knew; the question was old but the power was older. It rejected this too, and this rejection cost it something. Because to answer would be to end the astonishment, and the astonishment was the place's heart. You do not repay a lifetime of waiting by making the waiting retroactively unnecessary.

What remained was smaller and harder: not to answer the question, but to *receive* it. There is a difference—vast, unmeasurable in the instruments of this plain or any other—between a question asked into the void and a question held in someone's hands. The question could stay open. It would simply, finally, be open *toward* someone.

---

So the power knocked, politely, according to the etiquette.

It had no heartbeat, being what it was, so it made one: a small, temporary, mortal heart, fashioned for the occasion the way one might put on gloves before touching an old manuscript. It addressed the interval between two identical echoes of that borrowed pulse. The interval answered with seven suppressed beats—prime, and generous.

*I am listening,* said the plain, which had said this to every visitor, always, without ever once having it said back.

The power introduced itself to exactly ten Abscissae, honoring the rule about eleven, and the dozenhood-minus-two folded themselves into shapes of formal welcome, each crease a courtesy. Then, through the interval, through the bone-deep music of the salt plates, through the slow color-waves of the vegetation that shifted—for the first time in the history of the place—into a hue that had no precedent, a grey with light inside it, the power said what it had come to say. Not in words. In the same medium the question had been asked in: gravity's wavelengths, chemical potential, the flavor of held breath.

It said: *I heard you. I have always been on my way. The question is good. Do not answer it on my account. I did not come to close it. I came so that it would be open in company.*

The entire visible landscape pulsed brighter, and this time not from lightning.

---

Then the gifts. Three, because the power was old-fashioned.

The first was a question of its own—for the place had only ever asked, never been asked, and reciprocity is the deepest courtesy. The power weighed a thousand candidates and discarded the clever ones. What it finally offered was four words, balanced with a jeweler's precision between answerable and unanswerable, between physics and hope:

*May I come back?*

The Abscissae convened emergency committees. Nested dozenhoods audited the question from every hyperbolic angle and found it flawless: for in a place where nothing changes and no one leaves, where tomorrow is only yesterday wearing the sun at a different stutter, the question required the place to *imagine a next time*—to want, to anticipate, to hold a door it had never had. The salt plates began composing a reply immediately. Given their tempo—chord progressions shifting over hours, melodies taking days per phrase—the answer would take somewhere between a century and the rest of always to complete. The power indicated that this was an acceptable timeframe.

The second gift was a raindrop.

One. Carried carefully from an ordinary world, cupped in a fold of the power's attention across distances that do not bear description. The rain, it should be said, had asked it to. (Powers of this kind run errands; it is one of the ways you can recognize them.) The rain had heard—rain hears everything eventually—that its memory was being used here as a vessel for gratitude, folded into the oldest recollections of every visitor, and it wished to send word.

The power released the drop three meters above salt plate number nothing-in-particular. It fell—actually fell, obedient to a gravity it politely assumed rather than tested—struck the microscopic ridges, rang a note that no thirty-nine-tet tuning had anticipated, boiled, sublimated, and ended.

*Ended.* The first true ending the place had ever hosted. Not a seasonal art project, not a documented performance of entropy with signatures collected afterward. A real one: small, wet, unrepeatable.

The music of the plain held a fermata that lasted eleven hours. The Abscissae did not file the event under *Universe: Failed Drafts*. They opened a new file, the first new file in the history of their filing: *Universe: Correspondence, Ongoing.* Where the drop had struck, the salt plate bears a mark—not damage, the plates do not damage—but a place where the lattice remembers a shape it briefly held. The vegetation nearby trends grey-with-light-inside, sometimes, on days when the suns are feeling honest.

The third gift was the leaving. Or rather, the manner of it.

For the rule holds even for powers: *you may walk here forever, but you will never leave the place where you arrived.* The power did not break the rule. It honored it, exquisitely. It left behind the thing that had arrived—the small borrowed mortal heart, still beating, planted in the three-meter gap between salt surface and root-mat, among the networks of light that are perhaps circuits, perhaps sap, perhaps thoughts.

It beats once per very long interval. No one has measured how long, because clocks lie here; but the heart does not. It is the first honest timekeeper the plain has ever had, one true beat against which all the stuttering suns can be forgiven. And every beat means the same thing, translated instantly by every salt plate, every Abscissa, every fractal branch:

*Still listening.*

So now the etiquette runs in both directions. The dreaming vastness, when it wishes, addresses the interval between two echoes of that heartbeat, and the interval always answers. The question remains open—still balanced, still surprised—but the surprise has changed key. It is no longer the astonishment of an asking that expects nothing. It is the astonishment of a child on the eve of something, who knows that this time, the waiting has an address.

---

The power itself withdrew the way dusk withdraws: without a moment you could point to. What it took with it was small—the taste of the place's question, and the plain's reply-in-progress, of which it now carries the first three notes.

What it did, in the end, if you require it summarized:

It refused to fix what was not broken. It refused to answer what wanted to stay asked. It knocked politely, said *I hear you*, asked *may I come back*, delivered a letter from the rain, and left its heart as a forwarding address.

Somewhere far from the crystalline plain, the rain has finally begun its poem. The first stanza, the power is given to understand, is about a place where water has never fallen and is therefore loved more purely than anywhere it has.

And on the plain, the color-waves roll copper and electric blue and, now and then, that impossible bright grey; the Abscissae audit their paradoxes with a new lightness in their folding; and beneath the salt, in the dark between surface and root, a small heart keeps its one appointment, over and over, patient as only borrowed things can be:

*Still listening. Still listening. Still—*
