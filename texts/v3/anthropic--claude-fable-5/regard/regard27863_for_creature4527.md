## The Attention of the Quiet One

### I. How It Noticed, and the Problem of Noticing

The power had been aware of Sifam for some time, in the way that a deep lake is aware of a leaf turning on its surface. But awareness is not attention, and it knew—being wise—that in Sifam, attention is currency. It could not simply *look*. A gaze of that magnitude, spent all at once, would have flooded the markets. Every mushroom's symbiotic story would have been purchased in an instant; every lichen dispute settled by the sheer weight of being witnessed; the epiphyte-sung epic completed in a single hearing, which would have ended it, which would have ended everything, because some say the epic is why the trees hold the city up at all.

So the first thing the power did was ration itself.

It divided its attention into installments—small, survivable denominations of noticing—and it filed the appropriate paperwork with the Department of Accidental Arrivals, describing itself, accurately, as *an accident that had been meaning to happen for a very long time*. The clerk (a Maybe Person, present that day as a strong suggestion) stamped the form with an excuse that might have been true, and the power was admitted on a conditional basis, subject to Sifam's contract law, same as any spore or sorrow.

This was its second act, and its most important: it entered *under the law*. It did not hover above the canopy dispensing grace. Wisdom knew better. A gift dropped from outside the system is an invasive species. A gift negotiated from within is an ecology.

### II. The Being It Came to Find

It had come, as such powers do, to attend to *the being who lives here*. It searched for that being with great care.

It examined Gwyrgch of the Spiral Apologies, and found a being. It examined the Committee of 10,000 Mandibles, and found a being. It examined a single question squidling, ink-fingered and doubtful, and found a being. It examined the seventeen-year mist debate and found, to its mild delight, that the argument itself had become a being, with preferences, with dread, with a secret wish.

Then it stopped searching, because it understood. There was no being who lived *in* Sifam the way a pearl lives in a box. The residence was the resident. The city was one creature wearing twelve trees and ten million tenants, and every contract signed in nectar-ink was a synapse, and the noise—the constant, torrential, magnificent noise—was the sound of a mind thinking itself continuously into existence.

You cannot bless a mind by rearranging its thoughts. You can only bless it by thinking alongside it for a while, gently, at its own speed.

So that is what the power did.

### III. What It Did

**It apologized to the iron.** Before anything else. The power's own substance had, at various points in deep history, been meteorite—had fallen, alien and unasked, into worlds that owed it nothing. It found the ant-harvested rust flakes in their ceremonial keeping and performed the ritual apology alongside them, first-person and unabridged, for every arrival it had ever made without invitation. The ants recorded that the apology took nine days and was accepted with reservations. The reservations were also accepted. This established the power's credit rating.

**It sat with Olb.** Olb, who has been dying forward for three millennia, has never once been accompanied—everyone else dies backward or sideways or not yet, and cannot keep pace. The power matched Olb's direction of travel and sat with the old tree for what was, to the power, a single unhurried breath, and to Olb, eleven years. They did not speak. Dying forward, it turns out, is mostly a matter of not being rushed. When the power rose to go, Olb's heart-rot reflected a new light-frequency that the other eleven trees have tentatively translated as *thank you for not asking me how much longer*.

**It held the name of ~~ in trust.** The power remembered ~~'s name correctly. Then—and this had never once occurred in the history of the grove—it remembered it correctly a *second time*. It told no one. It wrote nothing down. It simply carries the name now, intact, in escrow. And ~~, who could not have said how, began to stand slightly differently, the way one stands when one learns that somewhere, in a vault one will never need to visit, something of oneself is safe.

**It made the counter-offer.** This is the transaction the spore-press covered most extensively and got most beautifully wrong. The mist above Gwyrgch had been debating whether to rain for seventeen years, and the morning's preliminary moisture was, as everyone suspected, an opening position. The power did not resolve the debate. It did not present arguments for rain or against. Instead it made a confession, which is the strongest form of counter-offer: it admitted to the cloud that it, too—wise, ancient, benevolent—had been unable to decide something for seventeen eons, and did not expect to decide it soon, and had learned to live richly inside the not-deciding.

The cloud, which had believed itself uniquely and shamefully stuck, was suddenly not alone. And indecision shared, as the lungfish philosopher could have told anyone, is itself a kind of decision. The cloud did not conclude its debate. It simply set the debate down, the way one sets down a heavy parcel to embrace a friend, and it rained—not as a verdict but as a gift, seventeen years of considered water, and the beetle-priests declared a festival, and the whale poets got their loudest slam yet, and the debate remains formally open, tabled, beloved, retired to the record as *ongoing in spirit*.

**It paid the Persistent Questions the only wage they'd never received.** It did not answer them—wisdom would never. It *asked one of them a question*. The oldest Persistent Question, which had drifted unanswered through eight centuries of marketplaces, was asked by the power, sincerely: *"And what do you want?"* — and for the first time in its existence, the question got to be on the other side of the silence. It has not answered. It may never. But it has been seen orbiting the power's small residual presence ever since, the way its own answers once orbited it, and observers say the geometry of this looks less like philosophy now and more like a family.

**It spent, over many months and in careful installments, twenty minutes of genuine curiosity on everything.** The sunset-colored mushrooms. The disputed lichens (both parties, equally, so as not to prejudice the case). The gastropod gamblers, whose bet about the direction of time it declined to settle but agreed to *hold the stakes for*, which the snails found unexpectedly moving. Its attention entered the economy slowly, like nutrients from good rot, and prices did not spike, and the city grew imperceptibly wealthier in the only wealth it counts: being known.

### IV. What It Did Not Do

This list is longer than the first, as such lists must be.

It did not settle the lichen litigation; centuries of jurisprudence are a life's work, and to end another's life's work is not mercy.

It did not tell the Maybe People whether they exist. They are enjoying the question, and their pre-application bureaucracy employs thousands.

It did not hurry the whale philosopher toward enlightenment or the League of Reformed Decomposers toward compromise. Forty-seven years of negotiation had produced, as a byproduct, the most sophisticated ontology in the canopy, and byproducts are the point.

It did not stop anything from eating anything else. The contracts were honest. Honesty was the sacred thing here, not safety, and the power was wise enough to know which of those a city can survive losing.

It did not correct the spider-scholars' reading of *Charlotte's Web*. Their misunderstanding had become more true than the book.

It did not fix the visitor. You, on Gwyrgch's outer bough, breathing your careful contract-breaths, who came looking for answers or trade or sanctuary. It looked at you exactly once—one denomination, twenty minutes of genuine curiosity about your symbiotic story—and thereby, by local law, *purchased nothing and paid you everything*. You will feel it later, the way one feels having been prayed for by a stranger. What it left you was not an answer. It was a receipt: proof that your attention here, all this noticing you've been doing, all this reading of leaf-light and lemur-law, was received, counted, and entered into the ledgers as a form of love. You are, the receipt notes, already home. You were catalogued on arrival. The houseplants' committee has voted, narrowly, in your favor.

### V. What It Left

The power did not leave, exactly. Departure would have required an exit visa, and the Department of Accidental Arrivals does not issue those, on the grounds that no one has ever truly managed it.

Instead it diminished itself, deliberately and with evident pleasure, into something small enough to hold a lease: a new species of moss, gray-gold, opinion-free, that grows only in the seam between droplet and leaf—in the exact instant of the wet percussion that is the first thing anyone notices about Sifam. It pays rent in perpetual, patient attention. It is present at every raindrop's arrival, which means it is present almost always, which means the city is now witnessed continuously, gently, at the smallest possible denomination, forever.

The Fourth Estate's report claimed the moss was a god. The Fifth Estate's rebuttal claimed the god was a moss. Both were awarded the prize for beautifully mistaken prophecy, and both, through widespread belief and sincere adaptation by their readers, are in the process of becoming true.

The rain, seventeen years in negotiation, is still falling. Ask the moss whether this is the offer or the acceptance, and it will tell you what the lungfish told you, which is what wisdom always tells you here, at the end, in the wet applause of the canopy:

*better and worse are the same thing, when they are given freely.*

The cloud has already begun deliberating its next rain. Preliminary estimates suggest four years, maybe five.

It is not stuck. It is savoring.

Someone, at last, taught it the difference.
