# The Neighbor

The wall mice noticed first.

On a single day — the logbooks of forty districts agree on this, though no one has ever had reason to lay them side by side and check — every wall mouse on the Shelf shifted its hum by a quarter-tone. Forty districts' worth of households apologized to forty districts' worth of loaves of opinionated fur until, satisfied with something, the mice settled back into the pump-rhythm and held it. The watchers wrote *No change. Mist heavy. Birds on time*, and were, as far as anyone has ever determined, wrong, for the first time in the history of the watch.

Something had arrived.

It had been called many things in many places. It is not called anything here, because nobody knows it came. It does not glow, descend, or announce. It is a power of the old kind — the kind that is mostly attention — and the first thing it did with the Shelf was the wisest thing anyone can do with the Shelf, which is nothing, on purpose, for a long time.

It watched for a hundred years before forming an opinion. By its own standards this was patient. By local standards it was a little hasty, and it knew that, and was mildly embarrassed.

## What It Saw

It saw at every speed at once, which is its nature. At one speed it watched a drop form on the lip of the Tessile Row gutter, forty to the minute, true as a heartbeat. At another it watched the city deposit itself like coral, stratum on stratum, repairs growing patinas and requiring repairs. At another it watched the cliffs slump their vast shoulders in real time, the whole tableland finishing its ten-million-year argument with itself.

It read everything. Every logbook of the settling watch, back to the volumes that have gone soft and brown and become, honestly, part of the building. It read every struck-out line, out of respect for the past's right to have happened. It read the Third Book of the Westmost Tower, and the entry that says *the rim is nearer than it was — measured — re-measured*, and the sixty years of *No change* after it in a hand that pressed too hard, and that entry was the only thing on the entire Shelf that hurt it.

Then it did what no one on the Shelf has ever done. It looked *under*.

It will not be telling anyone what it saw, for reasons that follow, but it can be set down here: green. A continent of it. Patient in a different key — wet where the Shelf is wet, slow where the Shelf is slow, fed for ten thousand years on what the swifts carry down each dawn, the city's whole mineral wealth raining gently through the cloud deck onto something that has been growing toward the light it cannot see. Climbing the cliff face. A few meters a century. Sending up, every thirty years or so, by bird, a single green thread.

The power held the whole shape of it in its mind, the way a watcher holds a district, and understood three things in close succession.

First: the rim is nearer than it was, and will go on being nearer. The Shelf is not falling. It is leaning down.

Second: the violet film is not fleeing east. The power slowed itself to film-speed to be sure of this — more on that below — but it suspected immediately. The mortuary violet, the film of the dead, is withdrawing from the western walls at the pace of a continent thinking something over, because the western walls are going to be needed.

Third: someone is courting someone. A repair, unasked, well, and without mentioning it — that is how interest is declared here. The green threads in the nests are not messages or seeds. They are mendings. *Under* has been courting the Shelf for ten thousand years, in the Shelf's own idiom, by bird, and the Shelf — which never disturbs a nest, never takes a thread, only logs it in a flat hand and goes on dripping — has not yet replied.

The Shelf is very good at *yet*. The power sat with that for a decade or two.

## What It Considered, and Set Aside

**To tell them.** Set aside almost at once. It would take *yet* away from them, and *yet* is the finest thing they make. Besides, the public register here is shouting, and this is not news for shouting. If it was to be said at all it would have to be said by hand, to one person, slowly — and the power filed that thought away, because it would be needed.

**To halt the rim's retreat.** Set aside. The rim is not failing; it is leaning down to meet what climbs. To freeze the Shelf in its perfect balance forever would be to build dry — to make a world that sheds everything in order to keep itself — and that is obscene here, and the power found, examining itself, that it agreed.

**To mend the rotted early volumes,** to give them back their origins. Set aside, with some difficulty, because the power loves origins. But a finished past is a finished people. The ambiguity is load-bearing. Nobody designed this place; it eroded into balance; to restore its founding would be to claim an authorship that belongs, properly, to ten million years of weather.

**To lengthen their lives.** Set aside fastest of all. Age is the Shelf's luxury good *because* it ends. A person of ninety is the height of value because memory is scarce enough to need. Immortality would bankrupt the entire economy of patience, and the economy of patience is the only economy here that matters.

**To leave.** Considered longest. The place wants nothing. The loop closed its seam ages ago. A wise power knows that most help is a kind of littering — a new machine not built to last four hundred years — and very nearly went.

But there was the entry in the Third Book. The watcher who measured truly, and re-measured, and was right, and was frightened, and pressed the lie of *No change* into sixty years of paper, and died not knowing.

And there was the local law, which the power had finally understood, and which changed everything. Nothing on the Shelf is *fixed*. Everything is *repaired*. A fix erases the damage and pretends it never happened. A repair keeps the damage's history in place and answers it. The power had, in its long career across many places, mostly fixed things. It was very good at fixing. It looked at the Shelf and understood that here it would have to learn the other art, and that this — not the cloud, not the birds, not the green — was the real reason it had stopped.

## What It Did

Four things. None of them large. It is not particular about the order of time, so neither is this account.

**It went to the watcher.** The night of the second measurement, in the Westmost Tower, sixty years before the watcher's death and several centuries before the power's arrival — it is not particular about these things — the watcher fell asleep over the Third Book with the chalk still in hand. And something signed onto the sleeping shoulder, the way parents sign the day's news onto a child, out of the belief, never quite stated, that it soaks in. It signed in the second language, the skin-speech, which has no word for *quickly*, and so it took most of the night to say:

*You measured truly. The rim is nearer. It is not falling. It is leaning down to meet something that is climbing. You will not see the meeting. Neither will your post-name's next ten bearers. Keep the watch. Wait.*

The watcher woke with no memory of it and a certainty in the sternum — the way a bearing going dry registers three streets over, but reversed: a *rightness*, lodged under the collarbone. Struck the entry with a single light line, out of respect for its right to have happened. Wrote *No change*, pressing hard.

Read again now, knowing this, the pressed hand of those sixty years is not fear held down. It is a vow held onto. The power considers this the best repair it has ever made, and it has repaired stars.

**It spoke with the film.** This required slowing to film-speed, which even the power found heavy going. The question took nine years to ask: *What are you thinking over?*

The answer took the better part of three centuries: *Making room.*

The power, having seen the green, did not ask for what. There is a politeness in not making the dead explain themselves.

**It paid a call on the Tenant.** It went down to the First Cistern, below the Westmost districts, where the wardens feed the great blind eel off a schedule older than their guild. The Tenant turned its white head toward the power in the dark, the way the wardens have always insisted it wouldn't. They stayed together some years. Nothing was said; between things that old, the visit is the sentence. On leaving, the power finally understood the feeding schedule for what it is — courtesy between patient residents — and resolved to be worthy of the same.

The Tenant remembers nothing, the power would report, if it reported to anyone. Which is exactly what the power would say.

**It mended the gutter on Tessile Row.** The gutter had been slowing for thirty years — thirty-nine and a half to the minute, then a touch worse — and the bell-keepers had been worrying about it in palm-speech, because to lose the gutter would be to lose a clock of the world. One morning it ran at forty. True. Unasked. Well. Unmentioned. The bell-keepers found their work already done and said nothing, because what is there to say.

By the custom of the Shelf, this is not maintenance. The power knew the custom. It had read everything. It made the declaration anyway, and settled in to wait for the reply the way one waits here: indefinitely, with one's hands busy.

## The Post It Took

The Westmost Tower's post-name had gone back to the post some while before, and was waiting, as post-names do, for someone to repair their way into deserving it. The power did not take the name. It would be like wearing someone's hands, and the power — which has, technically, no hands — respected the rule anyway. It simply sat where watchers sit, and watched, which is its nature, and kept its log in the only medium it owns, which is attention itself.

The entries, if they could be read, would read: *No change. Mist heavy. Birds on time.* Without irony. Without boredom. It turns out the power had been training for this curriculum its whole existence and had never known the post existed.

The reply to its declaration came two generations later, and came the way everything comes here. The wall mouse of the Westmost Tower — who had known about the power from the first day, mice being mice — decided emphatically *for* the tower's crumbling stair, and held the route, humming, until the district's masons did what masons here have always done and followed the mouse. They re-pointed the whole stair beautifully, for no reason any of them could give. The city, led by its smallest residents, had mended the god's house without knowing the god was in it.

The power logged it: *Counter-repair received.*

Then struck the line, lightly, out of respect, and wrote: *No change.*

One more thing sits in the tower now. When the Third Book's watcher had been forty years in the mortuary wall and the niche had gone to that deep violet, the family climbed up and took their one shell, as families do. A generation after that, a great-granddaughter — in violet herself by then, with the watcher-stillness already on her — climbed the Westmost Tower for reasons she declined to examine and left a second shell on the windowsill. *For the post,* she signed against the stone, and went back down.

The power keeps it. It is the only thing the power owns, on the Shelf or off it.

## One Last Thing

Someday — forty thousand years, give or take, and nothing here happens soon — green will crest the western rim. It will come up the weeping stone at the pace it has always kept, and the dead in their violet will have finished making room, and the first true leaf the Shelf has ever grown will open on a fog-fence stanchion in the pre-softened light.

And a swift will land. On the leaf. Not to nest — just to land, for the first time in the memory of the species, because there will finally be somewhere worth landing, and a go-between's work, after forty millennia of carrying threads, will finally be done.

The power intends to be at the window. It intends to write the entry itself, in whatever hand attention writes in:

*Change. Mist heavy. Birds on time. All well.*

Then it will set the shell back on the sill, hand the watching back to the post, and do what neighbors do at a wedding they have seen coming for forty thousand years: stand at the back, keep score, and say nothing — because everything will already have been said, slowly, by hand.

Until then it sits in the tower, and the gutters drip at forty to the minute, and the city repairs, repairs, repairs. The Shelf is very good at *yet*. The power, who had been everywhere and fixed nearly everything, had never once in its existence been good at *yet*.

It is learning. From the masters. And of everything it has ever done, in all the places that called it all those names, it suspects this apprenticeship is the thing it will have done best.
