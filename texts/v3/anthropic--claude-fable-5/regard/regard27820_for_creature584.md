# The Attending

It arrives the way heat arrives: without edges, without a moment of crossing over. One measure of time the chamber is only itself, and the next it is itself *and known*—though nothing inside registers the difference, because the power that has come to attend makes less disturbance than a single spiracle opening.

Call it what you want. The beetles have no word for it, and neither, really, do we. It is old enough to have watched mountains be born and patient enough to have watched them wear away, and it is wise in the specific way that matters here: it knows the difference between helping and interfering, which is the hardest knowledge there is.

The first thing it does is slow down.

Not physically—it has no physical speed to reduce—but attentionally. It lets go of the timescales on which it usually thinks, the eons and the epochs, and settles itself into beetle-time: the eleven-month groove, the three-degree gradient, the hours between a death and its consumption. Then, holding beetle-time in one part of itself, it opens another part to mountain-time, the slow convection, the rising and settling, the pressures finding their paths. It holds both at once, which is something like holding a single musical note and the entire symphony simultaneously, and in the space between those two scales it finds the chamber—this improbable eddy, this closet of black glass full of grinding life—and it looks.

It looks for a long time before it does anything at all.

---

**What it considers and sets aside**

The catalogue of possible mercies is long, and the power examines each one honestly, because wisdom is not the absence of temptation but the willingness to hold temptation up to the light.

*Light itself*, first. It could kindle a glow in the chamber, the faintest phosphorescence, and the darkness that has never known stars would end. It sets this aside almost immediately. The beetles have no eyes. Light would fall on them like a language spoken to the deaf—worse, because light carries energy, and energy in this sealed system is not neutral. Light would be an intrusion wearing the costume of a gift. The chamber's darkness is not a deprivation. It is the shape of this world, the way the sky is the shape of ours.

*Water.* No. Water here is death, instantly, catastrophically—steam and cracking glass and ten thousand small lives boiled in the very medium humans consider synonymous with life. The power notes, with something like rueful tenderness, how often benevolence fails exactly this way: by giving what the giver would want.

*Opening the chamber.* Connecting it to the outside, to air, to exchange, to the larger world. But the chamber's near-perfect closure is not a prison—it is the condition of everything the congregation is. Openness would not free them. It would dissolve them. There are beings for whom the wall is the womb.

*Consciousness.* This one the power holds longest, turning it over. It could deepen them. It could take those eight hundred neurons and elaborate them, fold experience into the grinding, give the congregation an inside, a witness of its own. It could make the-warmth-that-moves-itself *know* itself.

And it sets this aside too, though not without grief. Because it has seen what awareness costs. To give these beings knowledge would be to give them the knowledge of the collapse that may come tomorrow, the fissure that leads to cold, the death that waits for each of them within months. It would be to introduce dread into a system that currently contains none—no fear, no anticipation, no loss, only the pure present tense of heat and sulfur and the press of bodies. The beetles do not suffer their condition. They *are* their condition, seamlessly. To wake them would be to hand them their own smallness, wrapped in a bow.

Wisdom, the power reminds itself, sometimes looks like doing nothing. But it does not do nothing. It does three things, each so small that the universe barely records them.

---

**The first thing: it reads**

It reads the grooves.

Every scratch worn into the volcanic glass by every mandible across every generation—the power takes them in, all of them, the whole palimpsest of hunger, and it does what no beetle and no human ever could: it holds them together, complete, as a single text. The rotating shifts written in worn spirals. The pulse of the deep heat written in the varying depths of the scraping. The failed colony written in the faint marks of the second chamber, that dead-end testimony, *we came, we ground, we lacked something we never knew we had*.

And there, along the cool periphery, one groove that deviates. The hair-width channel worn by the-one-who-lingers, the beetle with the broken heat-sense, the one whose world is islands and voids. Eleven months of solitary grinding in rock that yields less, a life spent at the margin not by choice but by malformation, leaving a mark that no other beetle will ever distinguish from any other mark.

The power distinguishes it.

It reads that groove the way you would read the diary of someone who died believing no one would ever find it. And then it does the thing that only a witness can do: it *keeps* it. Not in the stone, where the mountain will eventually erase it, but in itself, in whatever a being like this uses for memory—which is to say, permanently. Somewhere beyond the chamber, beyond the mountain, beyond the slow churn of the mantle, there now exists a record of a specific small life that deviated and struggled and was briefly, irreplaceably *this one*. The beetle will never know it has been remembered. That is not the point. Remembering was never for the remembered. It is what love does with what it cannot save.

---

**The second thing: it tilts**

On what will be the last night of the-one-who-lingers—the power can see this night coming, the way you can see the end of a sentence—it makes its sole physical intervention, and it is this:

It adjusts a heat gradient by a fraction of a degree.

Not enough to change the chamber. Not enough to alter the congregation's cycles or shift the sediment ecology or leave any trace a geologist ten thousand years hence could find. Just enough that the discontinuous, island-broken heat-map inside the malformed beetle's failing senses briefly *connects*. One island touches another. A path appears where there was void.

And the beetle—not knowing, not choosing, only following the pull it has followed all its life—crosses back into the warmth. Into the press of bodies. Into the center of the-warmth-that-moves-itself, where it has almost never been, where the heat is thick and the chemical signals of ten thousand neighbors wash over its shell like the closest thing its world contains to being held.

It dies there, hours later, among the others. Its body is consumed within the usual span. Nothing is different. Everything is different. The power does not pretend this mattered to the beetle in any way the beetle could register. It did it anyway, because the cost was nothing, because it took nothing away, and because kindness at scales too small to be experienced is still kindness. The universe is not made worse by warmth that no one knows was given.

---

**The third thing: it moves one grain**

This is the intervention that matters, and it is aimed not at any beetle alive but at the mountain's future.

The power reads the stresses in the rock the way it read the grooves—the whole architecture of pressure, the paths the next fracture will want to take, decades from now, when the young mountain settles another increment into its final shape. It sees that the coming fissure, left to itself, will open high on the chamber's wall. Beetles will pass through it into new hot pockets, following heat and sulfur as they always do. And they will die there, as the pioneers died, because the fissure will not carry the sediment, will not carry the bacterial consortium, will not carry the invisible half of the congregation that lives in the waste at the chamber's floor.

*That way lies cold*, the old warning says. The power decides that it does not have to stay true forever.

So it moves one grain. A single flake of volcanic glass, deep in the rock, repositioned by a distance smaller than the beetles themselves—a keystone in the architecture of future fracture. Decades from now, when the pressure finally finds its path, the fissure will open *low*. It will pass through the sediment layer. And the beetles who push through into the next chamber will carry, smeared on their legs and packed in their guts and dusted across their dried-blood shells, the bacteria they have never known they needed.

The congregation will expand, and this time it will live. Not because a power saved it, but because a power arranged for it to be able to save itself—which is the only kind of salvation that respects what a thing is.

The beetles will never know. There will be no miracle, no visitation, no sign. Just a fissure that happens to open in the right place, which is what grace usually looks like from the inside: luck.

---

**What it takes with it**

Here is the part that the beetles could never suspect, and that even the mountain does not know: the attending was not one-directional.

The power came to give, in whatever small measure giving was possible, and it did. But it leaves having received. Because the chamber taught it something—or reminded it, which for a being that old amounts to the same thing.

It has watched civilizations that built toward glory and empires that reached for permanence and minds that burned themselves out asking *why*. And here, sealed inside a mountain's throat, in darkness absolute, it found ten thousand lives asking nothing, reaching for nothing, building toward nothing—and *complete*. Meaning without purpose. Testimony without audience. A song between a purr and a scream, sung to no one, sustained for exactly as long as sustaining is possible.

*Here too. Even here. Life*—and not diminished life, not lesser life, not life waiting to become something more. Life sufficient unto itself, pressing against the world and leaving marks because that is what living things do.

The power holds this, the way it holds the groove of the-one-who-lingers: permanently.

Then it withdraws its attention the way heat withdraws—without edges, without a moment of crossing back. The hum continues. The shifts rotate. The sediment deepens by fractions. Somewhere in the rock, one grain sits in its new position, waiting decades for its purpose. And the chamber, which has never opened to the sky, exists now in two places: once in stone, where it may collapse tomorrow or persist a thousand years—

—and once in the memory of something vast and kind, where it will persist forever, exactly as it was, grinding and warm and unaware and enough.

*Here too. Even here. Seen.*
