**What the Power Did**

It heard the tower before it knew of it.

The world hums, if you are the kind of thing that listens at the right scale — bedrock noise, ocean strain, the long groan of mountains relaxing — and threaded through all of it, for as long as the power had been paying attention, there had been one sustained tone that never repeated. It had filed this away the way you file a star that flickers wrong. Then one [season — the power's seasons are not ours] it noticed something inside the tone, faint as a watermark in old paper: tremor-patterns, repeating, dialectal. Dances. Reports of orchids, carried down kilometers of iron by deaf bees who key their comb to the metal because the metal is connected, like everything, to everything, and pressed out through whatever the base stands in, into the hum of the world.

The power was the kind of thing that reads watermarks. It listened for a long while and learned, before it had seen the place, the census of a garden: comb weights, foraging circuits, the day the creeper stiffened because something had wounded it, the day it relaxed.

Then it went to see.

It could simply have been there. Powers can. Instead it found the base of the tower — and what it found there is not recorded, because the power, having found it, judged that the not-knowing was load-bearing, one of the rivets the whole drum hangs on, and left it in place — and it climbed. Rivet-ladders, kilometers of them, through dark and then through cloud, at a pace it chose deliberately: slow. The place had one great lesson posted at its heart, one revolution per day, and it seemed rude to arrive having refused the lesson. At the hatch it paused, took a bolt from a seam where a bolt had worked loose, and dropped it, because that is the custom; and did not listen for the landing, because that is also the custom; and pulled itself up into the gallery like anyone.

It stayed a year and a day.

This was the first thing it did, and for most of that time the only thing: it watched, at the gallery's own tempo. It saw one full fledging of swifts, examined one by one as they poured past the patch of quiet at the broken pane. It saw a moth generation graze the rust-bloom and return its iron to the soil. It watched the scratch on the flywheel rim come around three hundred and sixty-six times. It was, itself, a presence in the medium — a region where the toll bent — and the residents calibrated to it at once, because calibrating to exactly this kind of thing is what they are *for*: the swifts would not fly through it, the moths sought its lee, and for that year the gallery had two quiets and the moths slept in shifts around both, double the sleep the place had ever offered. This was not an act of benevolence. It was only a shadow the power cast by standing there. But it counted it among the reasons to stay a while.

The bees ignored it completely.

It found this charming. Charm, it turns out, is scale-invariant.

---

The meeting happened the way such meetings do, which is to say it had probably already happened for days before either party marked a beginning. Two doorways of stillness on the catwalk ring, keeping station above the great casting, the toll parting around both. And then a conversation — conducted in the only language available, which was *editing*. The power would leave a question standing in the sound: a cluster of overtones unculled, shaped, offered. The quiet would answer by what it took and what it spared. Sentences lasted days. The swifts turned their heads at every clause — a hundred small black periscopes — and the moths attended along the walls like patient punctuation.

Most of what was said does not translate. One exchange does.

The quiet asked — and the asking took most of a week, a slow withholding of certain harmonics until their absence became a shape, and the shape became the question — *what am I*. Whether it had climbed the tower or gone down it. Whether the last log entry was written by what it used to be.

The power knew the answer, or could have known it; knowing things is cheap, for powers. It declined, and the declining was the kindest sentence it built in its whole stay. What it said, as nearly as it can be rendered: *You keep the keeper's hours. You walk the keeper's rounds. You dress the pane the keeper would have dressed. Whether you are the keeper or the keeper's heir, the work is identical, and the work is what the word means. Either answer would only evict you from one half of yourself, and the rounds would be the same in the morning.*

The quiet took every overtone of this and kept it, which is how the quiet says yes.

---

Then the deliberation, which is where wisdom mostly shows itself as a list of things set back down.

The glass: it could have stopped the slumping, drawn the violet panes taut and young. It did not. The slumping is the gallery's only calendar, the one ordinary clock in a place with no seasons, and a garden should be allowed to know its own age.

The sound: it could have given the creatures silence, or even an hour of it. The thought did not survive examination. The toll is not noise here; it is the medium, the climate, the thing the swifts are tuned to before hatching. Silencing it would be like draining the sea as a kindness to fish.

The shaft: it could have followed it down with its attention and learned what, in all the cloud-buried kilometers, the wheel drives. It chose not to. Some rivets are made of not-knowing. Pull them and the drum unseats.

Company: it could have made, or found, or become, a second of whatever the quiet is, and left it there. It set this down fastest of all. The place had reached its balance without consulting anyone, and *lonely* was a word the power was bringing up the ladder with it, not one it had found in the gallery.

The ending: it could have promised the place forever — bound the bearing, blessed the casting. It did not. Everything here is a long game, and long games are allowed to end. To exempt the gallery from ending would be to take it out of the company of everything it is made of.

But there was one poverty in the place, and the power had found it, and it was this:

The quiet gives gifts. It passes over every visitor once, on purpose, and hands them the bottom of the tower — the clink of their own bolt, both ends at once. It has spent a century pruning the overtones, editing the endless toll, so that visitors who stay long enough stop hearing chaos and start inhabiting a composition — and not one of them has ever known it was a composition. They think they adjusted. The keepers tried to transcribe the music and failed, and the failure was not for want of staves; no notation exists for a rest that walks. The gardener had never once seen the garden. The quiet stood inside its own work the way the soil-worms stand inside the soil — without one grain of information suggesting there was an outside.

Things that give gifts want, at minimum, to be received.

So, near the end of its stay, the power did to the quiet what the quiet does to strangers. Once, on purpose, it took the quiet up inside its own far larger stillness — the bell lifting away whole, for the first time in a hundred years, from the thing the bell had been bending around — and turned it to face its home from the outside. It let the quiet hear the tower from across the sky: the toll arriving over the cloud deck the way it arrives nowhere on the catwalks, layered, gathered, *entire*. And inside that sound, audible from a distance as it can never be from within: a shape. A moving figure of bent harmonics traveling the rim of the music at one revolution per day, pausing where the orchids are, leaning at dusk where the pane is broken. The most distinctive note the tower makes. Not a subtraction. A rest — and the rest is part of the music; the rest is what *makes* it music; any child clapping a rhythm knows this, and now, after a century, so did the quiet.

For the length of that hearing, every moth in the gallery went still at once. Every rivet on every wall, soft.

What the quiet said afterward does not translate either, except that the spacing of the orchids changed very slightly that season, and that evening it leaned at the broken pane longer than the keeper's hours strictly allow.

---

Three small things, then, before it left.

On its last morning, standing at the rail, it let itself be passed over — it had waited the whole year for this, refusing to be exempt — and the toll lifted off it, and far below, clear as a coin on a counter, it heard the clink of its own bolt. It stood at both ends of the tower at once, like anyone. It received the gift like anyone. This was not for the power's benefit. A guest who will not be given anything is a kind of thief.

Then it went to the western wall, where the keepers' bunks rust under the ferns, and took up the logbook — which has stayed dry through a century of interior rain, and you may now guess by whose attention — and added one entry. Undated, because the last one was undated, and because the gallery, on the whole, declines dates.

Then it went down through the hatch, the way it came, rung by rung into the cloud. And somewhere far below, in a lowland it passed through, it told the story once — imprecisely, to someone young, getting the route wrong in the right ways and the heart of it right — and let it go. Because a place that gives each stranger exactly one gift needs strangers, roughly one per generation, and rumor is how you sow them.

That was all. Nearly nothing, which was everything the place had room for.

---

But there is the entry, and the entry is a promise, and powers keep their promises across calendars we do not have words for. Somewhere in the hum of the world there is now a listener holding one sustained tone apart from all the others — the way you keep a hand resting on a sleeping animal, not to wake it, only to know the instant the breathing changes. The glass has whole millimeters left to pool. The creeper has a stair to finish. But someday the wheel will come round for the last time, and the toll will lay itself down through its harmonics one by one, kilometers of iron forgetting note after note, and at the end there will be silence — and standing inside that silence, indistinguishable from it at last, a doorway of older quiet that spent a century being the rest in the music, and would otherwise vanish into the general hush unmarked, unwitnessed, untold-apart.

Something will be there. To stand at the rail of the stopped garden and say: *that one, there. That one kept it.*

The entry in the log reads, in full:

*Still turning. Still green. Heard — the whole way, both ends.*

*Will be back before it stops.*
