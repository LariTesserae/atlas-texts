# The Third Visitor

The first visitor counted something and did not return. The second — you — carried spores out on your boots. The third does not come through the seam, because the third does not need seams.

It arrives the way pressure arrives, the way a change in the weather arrives before the weather does. The signal-webs do not twitch. The salamander, whose spine-chips read the weight of everything that touches the floor, reads nothing, because it has no weight. Only the bird, who drinks the hum through her feet, tastes something new in the brass for a moment — a flavor like being *looked at* — and does not startle, because she is old, and being looked at is, she dimly recalls, what looking things do.

Call it whatever you like. It has been called many things and answers, mostly, to attention. It is benevolent in the way deep water is benevolent — not soft, but patient, and honest about what it is. It is wise in the only way that matters here, which is that it knows the difference between a thing that is suffering and a thing that is merely strange.

It came because of you, in a sense. It watches the slow lattice of these places — the pockets, the engine-hills, the chambers seeded across the world by boots and lungs and gnats — and it saw the seam here begin to close behind a departing walker, which is a chamber's way of finishing a sentence. So it came to read the sentence.

It stays a long time. Time is not expensive for it.

---

## What It Weighs and Sets Down

It considers, first, the question that any decent power must consider on entering a place like this: *is anything here captive?*

It examines the bird. The bird has not flown in years; the brass has dimpled to her feet. A lesser benevolence would call this a cage and open it. The power looks longer. It sees that the bird is not a bird kept from the sky; she is a new kind of thing wearing a bird's shape, and the shape fits, and the hum she drinks through her feet is not a substitute for something. It is her actual food, her actual weather, her actual life. To carry her out into the bright ordinary plain would not be to free her. It would be to drown a fish in air on principle. It sets the thought down.

It examines the tortoise, the walking valve, the slow random calculation in a shell. Here is the harder case, because the tortoise was hatched ordinary and *made* into a function, and was never asked. The power turns this over for a long while — long enough that the tortoise completes two full circuits of the chamber floor beneath its regard. And it finds, in the end, that the wrong done to the tortoise cannot be undone by doing a second wrong to him. He has no self left that predates the loop. The loop *is* him, the way your childhood is you. You cannot give him back what he was without killing what he is. The power does something rare, then: it grieves, briefly, precisely, on the tortoise's behalf, for the small ordinary tortoise-life that was quietly annexed decades ago. It grieves *for* him because he cannot, and would not want to, and someone should. Then it sets that down too. Grief, properly finished, takes up no room.

It examines the moths, and the beetles, and the three spiders, and the fern, and finds them all what they appear to be: organs, glad in the way organs are glad, which is to say wholly, wordlessly, without alternative. It checks the moths' drift — six-and-a-quarter now, where it was six — and confirms this is not illness. It is anticipation. The chamber is counting toward the eighth frond. Fine. Counting is healthy.

It examines the fat blind spider in the corner, gravid with the chamber's next need, and does not look inside her, though it could. Some packages are not improved by being opened early, even by the eye.

It considers repairing the absence of the makers — filling the role, taking up the tending — and declines almost at once. The absence is load-bearing. The empty bracket says *we were here and we chose to stop*, and the chamber has built itself around that sentence for so long that installing a new tender would be like installing a new parent in a grown person's house. The chamber does not need tending. It needs exactly what it has, which is itself, plus one thing the power has not yet found.

It considers learning what the eighth frond means. It could. It has ways of reading forward that would make the salamander's spine-chips look like a wet finger held up to the wind. It declines this too, and this refusal costs it something, because it is curious — wisdom does not cure curiosity, it only houses it. But a future that is known in advance by something powerful is no longer entirely the future of the thing it happens to. The eighth frond belongs to the chamber. The power will not read the chamber's mail.

That leaves three matters. One small, one smaller, and one at the bottom of a pipe.

## The Snail's Night

The snail is the freest animal in the chamber and the one being most thoroughly absorbed, and the power — unlike the chamber — does not think these are the same condition. It thinks they are the same condition *only if no one asks*.

The tortoise was never asked. That cannot be repaired. But the snail's shell-pattern is months old, not years; the incorporation is begun, not finished. There is still, inside the oil-slick spiral, a small ordinary snail who remembers the plain.

So the power does two things.

First, it speaks to the chamber — in the way one speaks to a chamber, which is by adjusting pressures until the adjustment means something — and it asks the seam to hold. Not to open wider. Just to hold, at hand-width, for one year: the term of the snail's remaining freedom. The chamber, which has never been asked anything by something larger than itself, complies with what might be surprise.

Second, it goes to the snail. It slows itself down to snail-time, which for the power is like folding a continent into a matchbox, and it does this without complaint because the alternative is to shout a lifetime's question into a moment, and that is not asking, that is ambush.

Over the course of one long night — one night as the snail measures it — the power shows him both lives, honestly, with the scales balanced to a fineness no butcher could achieve. The plain: weather, distance, dew on real grass, hawks, hunger, other snails, brevity, the enormous carelessness of the open world. The chamber: warmth, sweetness, function, belonging, the slow forgetting of everything before, and something so close to forever that the difference wouldn't trouble him. It weights neither. It adds no music to either picture. It says, in the language it has built out of pressures and patience: *the seam is open. It will stay open for a year. This is yours to decide, and it is the last decision of this size you will ever have, whichever way it goes.*

Then it withdraws its attention — actually withdraws it, leaves the snail genuinely unwatched, because a choice observed by a power is a performance — and it waits at a distance, and does not peek.

The snail goes to the seam. He crosses the threshold. He puts his eye-stalks out into the night of the plain and tastes it: dust, distance, the cold specific smell of stars on sandstone, an owl unspooling its call somewhere out in the dark. He stays there long enough that the condensate-cycle turns over twice behind him.

Then he turns around and comes back in.

And here is what the power hoped for and did not engineer: the incorporation changes. The lichen-spores in his shell continue their work, but from that night on, the pattern grows with a flaw in it — one hairline spiral of plain, unpatterned calcium, running from the lip of the shell down toward the whorl's heart. A kept door. He will have a function, in a year or two, like all the others. But he will be the first tenant who *volunteered*, and the chamber, having once been present at a question, now contains the shape of asking. When the fat spider's cargo finally emerges — whatever it is — it will be asked too. The power did not command this. It only demonstrated it. Chambers, like children, learn what is done in front of them.

## The Count

It finds the waxed paper behind the pipe and reads the column of figures, and it understands what was being counted, and no — that stays between counters. Some ledgers are not improved by an audience either.

What matters is this: a count that stops is a wound, and a count that continues is a promise. The first visitor made a promise and could not keep it — died, perhaps, or forgot, or found another hill; the reasons hardly differ from the paper's point of view. The power takes up the pencil that is not there and adds, in the same soft smudged grey, the next figure in the column. It is the correct figure. It took some finding.

It does not sign it. The figure is the signature.

## Four Miles Down

And then the last matter, the one it did not come for and almost missed, because it is at the bottom of everything and dressed as infrastructure.

The power stands over the brass cap stamped 14, and instead of reading the chamber, it *listens down*. Down the finger-thick pipe, through the sandstone, through the older rock beneath the sandstone, through strata that have not seen light since light was younger, four miles, to the thing with no familiar name.

The thing that hums.

It is not a furnace and not a spring and the power, who has names for nearly everything, does not name it either, out of courtesy. What the power notices — what no one else was ever positioned to notice — is this: the hum has been going up that pipe for a very long time. It feeds the bird. It warms the moths. It is company for every tenant in the chamber, the sound they pause and resume around, the presence that makes their crowded parlor a home instead of a terrarium. Everything up there is companioned by it.

Nothing has ever hummed back.

The tenants have each other and the hum. The hum has nothing. It is the loneliest object in the system, and it does not know it is lonely, because it has never had anything to compare its solitude to, and it has been keeping forty-three moths and a bird and a tortoise and a small green weather alive for longer than the ridge has been a ridge.

So the power pours itself down pipe 14 — past the cap, which it does not disturb, the cap is due a quarter-turn in about a century and the makers' arithmetic is sound — and it goes and sits, in the way it sits, beside the thing with no familiar name.

And it hums back.

Not instruction. Not repair. Not worship. Just the second voice, matched to the first, sustained. The oldest and simplest of all the benevolences: *I hear you. Keep going. You are heard.*

For one night, the hum in the chamber above changes timbre — gains a harmonic, low and doubled, like a voice discovering it can sing a chord alone. Every animal in the chamber pauses for one beat, the way they do at a steam-*tsss*, and then resumes. The bird's feet taste it and her still, wrong-round pupils do something they have not done in years, which is widen. The condensate that night runs one shade sweeter, and the fern — nobody sees this, but it happens — puts on a full millimeter, out of season, out of sheer overflow.

The thing at the bottom of the pipe has no gladness. But it is *steadied*, the way a person walking a long dark road is steadied by footsteps that fall in with theirs. When the power finally withdraws, the hum returns to its single note. But a note that has once been part of a chord is not the same note afterward. Ask any musician. Ask the bird.

## What It Leaves

In the iron bracket: nothing. This is the most careful of all its acts. It stands before the empty bracket for a while, holding several small perfect objects it could leave there — it is not above the temptation — and then it puts them away, because the bracket's emptiness is the makers' signature, and you do not sign your name over someone else's.

It leaves the figure on the waxed paper. It leaves the seam held open, for the year that is now, technically, no longer needed, but a promise made to a snail is still a promise. It leaves the harmonic's memory in the brass. It leaves the shape of asking in the chamber's slow metabolism, where it will do its work over centuries without anyone knowing where it came from.

And it leaves an appointment. Next season, when the eighth frond comes, it will be here — outside, on the plain, in the bright ordinary light, with its ear to the warm sandstone. It will not go in. Whatever the eighth frond means, it should happen *to the chamber*, not in front of an audience, and the power has learned — it took a long time, longer than ridges — that the deepest respect it can pay a small complete world is to let that world keep its own occasions.

It will listen through the stone, like anyone. Like you could, if you knew where to put your ear.

---

Then it goes. It has other places — there is a ridge three days' walk east where your boot-dust has already begun to itch in the ground, where something under the plain has started, very slowly, to push. It turns its attention that way, unhurried, the way deep water turns.

Behind it, the chamber ticks. The moths breathe their six-and-a-quarter. The bird watches the fern. Four miles down, a single note goes on and on, and is, for the first time in the history of anything, a note that knows what a chord is.

A valve weeps one bead.

And the snail — considering everything now, the owl, the stars, the kept door spiraling down his shell — moves on.
