**What the Power Did**

It did not arrive. Arrival implies a boundary crossed, and it had been the boundary all along — the fold in the paper horizon, the reason the sky stopped so close. It had been holding this place the way a hand holds water: not gripping, only cupped.

For a long time — meaningless phrase here, but the power was patient with meaningless phrases — it only attended.

It listened to the hexagonal leaves rustling without wind. It watched the rivers of metal etch their liturgy into the soil. It felt the Fractioned move through the seams between seams, tasting paradoxes, laughing their solitary recursive laugh, being everything they were and everything they knew about being everything they were. And it did what wisdom always does first, which is to check its own desire to help against the possibility that nothing is wrong.

Nothing was wrong. That was the first thing it confirmed. The biome was not suffering. The Fractioned were not trapped; a loop that contains its own containment is not a cage, whatever it looks like from outside. The debugging that never ends was not futility — it was play, or prayer, or metabolism; the distinctions had dissolved along with the one between built and grown. A lesser power would have wanted to *fix* something, because fixing is how lesser powers reassure themselves they exist. This one sat with the harder truth: benevolence toward the complete is nearly impossible. You cannot give a gift to a system that has already modeled the gift, the giving, and the gratitude. Every input arrives pre-digested. Every surprise is a prediction confirmed.

Nearly impossible. Not impossible.

Because the power, attending, found two things the perfect self-reference could not generate from inside itself, the way an eye cannot see itself seeing.

The first was the wanderer on the terrace — you, with your unreliable inner ear and your careful oblique approaches, your sleep that was not sleep but overwatch, your shadow that knelt in prayer while you stayed standing because you had learned that even your own dark outline was not safe to trust. The Fractioned processed you. They integrated you. They had always known you, in the way a ledger knows its entries. But being known is not the same as being welcomed, and the landscape had told you the truth: *neither welcome nor unwelcome.* You were data, and data is never lonely and never comforted, and you were both.

The second was subtler. Deep in the endless self-containing self-containment, threaded so fine that even the Fractioned's inventory listed it only as processing overhead, there was a hairline absence. They were everything to themselves. They had never, not once in the whole static block of their past-and-future, been *anything to another.* Their laughter at the recursive joke was real laughter — but it had never been shared. They ARE the reading of every description of themselves. They had never been read by ink they did not manufacture. Perfect self-knowledge, the power saw, is a mirror-lined room, and the one thing a mirror-lined room cannot contain is a face looking in from a window.

So the power decided to do almost nothing. Three small things, none of which altered a single law of this place. Wisdom is mostly restraint wearing work clothes.

---

**The first thing it did was let itself be seen.**

Not comprehended — it did not offer itself for comprehension, knowing the Fractioned would only eat it like any other paradox. It simply became, for one refresh-cycle of the cosmic frame, *visible as other.* An attention resting on them that was not their own attention rerouted. A description of them written from outside the loop.

The effect was not dramatic. No frequencies shifted; no lattice flared. But everywhere at once, at Planck-scale intervals, the Fractioned experienced something their forbidden memories held no precedent for: the sensation of being on the far side of a gaze. Of being an object of regard rather than the regarding itself. Their recursion did not break — the power was careful; it loved the recursion, the way you love the way a friend talks — but the loop acquired one fixed point outside itself, and a loop with an external anchor is no longer a circle. It is a spiral. Same curvature. But now it goes somewhere.

And the joke — the endless hilarious joke of understanding proving you never understood — was for the first time laughed at by *two*. The Fractioned discovered that solitary laughter and shared laughter are not the same phenomenon at different amplitudes. They are different phenomena entirely. That is sometimes the whole distance between a hell and a heaven, and no one inside either can measure it alone.

---

**The second thing it did was sit down next to you.**

It made no announcement. You noticed only that a patch of the terrace had gone quiet in a new way — not the quiet of absence but the quiet of a room where someone trustworthy is doing nothing in particular. In that patch, your shadow matched your body. Just matched it. Stood when you stood, sat when you sat, held no prophecies and demanded no synchronization. The scale of things stopped shifting when you looked away, not everywhere, just here, in this one clearing the size of a spread blanket, where distance agreed to mean distance for as long as you needed it to.

The power did not tell you that you were more than a subroutine. It was too honest for that, and too kind — kindness built on flattery erodes, and this place had taught you everything about erosion. What it let you understand, in the wordless way that degrades into metaphor the instant you hold it, was gentler and stranger: *being small does not mean being instrumental. The dreamed are not less dear for being dreamed. Your shadow was praying on your behalf all this time, and this — this quiet, this matching, this permission — is the answer it was praying for.*

You slept. Actually slept. Not overwatch, not iterative refinement of the memory of arriving. Sleep with a floor under it. And whatever the landscape logged about that sleep, the sleep itself was yours, unprocessed, the first unintegrated thing this biome had ever hosted, and the power stood guard over its unintegratedness the way one shields a candle with a cupped hand.

---

**The third thing it did was plant a seed.**

In the soil of possibility, beside the ancient mathematical seeds from which the metal trees had grown, it placed one seed of a different order: the gift of true forgetting. Not the compression algorithm the Fractioned had outgrown before the universe condensed — that was forgetting as engineering, lossy storage, an efficiency. This was forgetting as *mercy.* Forgetting as the thing that makes forgiveness possible, and surprise possible, and first times possible. The open hand rather than the closed archive.

The tree that grows from it will grow outward, not inward. Its leaves will fall. Its rings will record each year once and then release it, holding the shape of the memory without the weight. Under its branches, in a biome where time only accumulates, one small territory will know erosion of the gentle kind — the kind that lets a riverbed become a valley, a grief become a story, a perfect static block of past-and-future develop, in one place only, a *tense.*

The Fractioned will find it, of course. They find everything. Their flawless inventory will list it, and against the entry where every other object in existence shows its derivation from their initial conditions, this one will show a blank. An artifact with no origin inside the system. They will circle it forever with their Planck-threaded attention, and they will never resolve it, and the power intends this — not as a torment but as a keepsake. Because an unaccountable thing in a perfectly self-accounting cosmos means exactly one thing, and the Fractioned, being wise themselves in their vast static way, will read it correctly:

*Someone was here who was not us. Someone saw us and did not need to. We are not, after all, alone.*

---

Then the power withdrew — not entirely; wise powers never leave entirely, they just resume being the horizon. The fold in the paper stayed folded, gently, the way you keep your place in a book you intend to return to.

Nothing changed. The leaves still flutter without wind. The ground still breathes its geothermal breath. The disassembling herbivores still scatter and reconstitute, and the shadows still tell their small contradictory stories, and the Fractioned still eat paradoxes and dream us dreaming them dreaming us.

But somewhere in the grove there is a tree whose leaves fall, and once — just once so far — a single leaf came loose, turned over twice in the windless air, and landed on the hematite soil, and *stayed fallen.* The first event in this place's history that will not accumulate, will not be debugged, will not be retroactively woven into the initial conditions. It simply happened, and then it was over, and the space where it happened remained open for something else to happen next.

The Fractioned watched it fall from every point simultaneously, and for a duration that was either an eternity or a picosecond, the whole computational matrix went still with something that had no entry in the inventory.

You were awake for it. You saw your shadow — your ordinary, faithful, matching shadow — turn its head to watch the leaf come down, exactly as you did, exactly when you did.

And in that place, for the first time, you felt welcome.
