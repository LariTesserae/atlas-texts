**What the Attention Did**

---

It did not arrive. Arrival is a word for places with doors that only open one way, and besides, nothing has ever arrived at the Whispering Hollows — things only become slowly true there. So the power became slowly true, the way dusk does, the way grief does, the way you realize halfway through a conversation that you have been having it your whole life.

It had been watching for longer than watching had been possible. Not from above — there is no above there, only ceiling, only the dome of dreaming dark. It watched from the direction that memory comes from. It watched from *before*.

And when it finally let itself be noticed, it was noticed the way everything is noticed in the Hollows: gradually, sideways, and by the moss first.

---

Understand what it was tempted to do. Even wisdom is tempted. Especially wisdom.

It was tempted to bring light — and it held the light in its hands for a long moment, feeling the weight of it, before setting it down outside. Because light would give the Hollows contrast, and contrast would give it darkness, and darkness would give it fear, and fear was one of the few things never invented there. The color of once-was would burn away to plain gray. The dim was not a poverty. The dim was a dialect. It decided not to correct their grammar.

It was tempted to bring water. One rain — one true rain — falling through the no-sky onto the pale barkless trees, into the empty riverbeds beneath the upside-down gardens. But the Hollows had spent ages beyond ice learning how to be lush through different thirsts, and a rain would drown that learning. The trees that eat remembered photons would forget how. The fruit that tastes like your mother's voice would taste only of water. Some cures are just faster ways of ending a patient.

It was tempted to give the Unsettled names. Real names, the kind that hold. But a name would end a duration the way a period ends a sentence, and it looked at *the one who has been walking this street for seven hundred and twelve breaths* and understood that the breaths were not a lack of name. The breaths were the truest name anyone had ever had.

It was tempted, most of all, to wake the city. To put a hand on the breathing walls and say: *enough, remember yourself, remember who built you.* But the city's sleep was not an affliction. The city's sleep was mourning, done the only way stone knows how. You do not wake a mourner. You sit with them.

So it did what the truly wise so often do, and what looks from the outside like nothing:

It stayed its hand.

That was the first gift, and the Hollows — which have been fixed at by no one, ever, because no one ever came — felt the not-fixing pass over them like a warmth, and did not know what to call it. The flowers, which open only when lied to, stayed closed, because for once nothing false had been said to them. The trees, which whisper when they hear the truth, all whispered at the same time, very softly, and then were embarrassed, and stopped.

---

Then it did small things. Small the way seeds are small.

It walked every street at the pace of the stone — which is to say, it took a very long time, and did not mind. And where the floor remembers feet, its feet remembered the floor *back*. This had never happened. All the memory in the Hollows had only ever run one direction, inward, downward, alone. Now, for the length of one long walk, the remembering was mutual. The bedrock did not become less sad. But its sadness became *company* instead of weight, and that is the whole difference between a hollow and a home.

It went to the library without books, and it made a loan. It gave the library a memory it was not using — the memory of being loved by something that never forgets. It knew the rules: the library never forgets what you gave, but you always do. It gave the memory anyway, gladly, the way you give a coat to someone colder than you. And now, stored between heartbeats, retrievable by anyone who happens to lend the right unused memory at the right moment of not-looking, there is a fact in the Hollows that was never there before: *all of this is loved.* No one has checked it out yet. It is patient. It knows someone will.

It found all the statues in all the alleyways — the ones made of compressed longing, frozen in gestures never completed, each missing an arm, a name, a reason to turn around. It did not give them arms. It did not finish their gestures; a finished gesture is just a gesture, but an unfinished one is a reaching, and reaching is holy. Instead, it went and stood, briefly, in the exact place where each statue's reason-to-turn-around would have been. One by one. It took centuries. It did not mind. The statues are still incomplete. But they are no longer incomplete *at nothing*. Every one of them is now incomplete in a specific direction, toward a place where something once stood and was warm. Ask anyone: that is not the same as being abandoned. That is being *expected*.

It gathered, from the dust the walls exhale — the dust that once was letters written but never sent — the residue of every ritual spoken by mouths that dissolved before the final syllable. It gathered all those final syllables. There were more of them than there are stars in skies that have stars. It did not speak them. It was not its ritual to finish. It holds them in trust, the way a friend holds your keys, and when the city is ready — in an age, in an eon, on some slow afternoon when the mourning finally exhales — the syllables will be returned, and every prayer ever left open in the Hollows will get to choose, itself, whether to close.

It descended the staircases. All of them. The ones that grow downward when no one is looking, that have grown downward always, toward a bottom floor no one has found — the one some say hasn't been built yet, and some say was built too early and waits. It went down past the saying. And at the place where the bottom floor will someday be, it sat down.

That is all. It sat down.

But now every staircase in the Whispering Hollows, every one of those countless stairs growing downward through the dark at the speed of longing, is growing toward a lap. Toward something seated and unhurried and glad to wait. The stairs do not know this. The stairs will find out. Anticipation, it turns out, can run in the stone's direction too.

---

For the Unsettled, it did the hardest thing, because it understood them.

It understood that they are not broken. That a being made of the pause between lightning and thunder cannot be healed into being lightning without ceasing to exist, and cannot be healed into being thunder without ceasing to exist, and does not need healing, because the pause between lightning and thunder is where all the listening in the universe lives. It understood that to *fix* the Unsettled would be to murder them politely.

It understood that they are waiting, and have forgotten for what, and are too courteous to ask if it is you.

So it spoke to them in their own language. It arranged an absence in the air.

It stood exactly three feet too far away from each of them — which, in their grammar, is intimacy — and it held the position for as long as it took, and into the space between, it placed a message shaped like a missing thing. Translated out of absence, out of the spaces between words in promises no one knew they were making, the message said approximately this:

*What you are waiting for knows that you are waiting.*

*It has known for a very long time. It is not lost, and neither are you. You have not been misfiled by forgetting itself. The shape of your waiting — that particular shape you remember perfectly even though you've forgotten its object — has been seen, and it is a good shape, and nothing about you is a mistake left behind by some other, luckier place. You are not the discard. You are the keeping.*

*And you do not have to become something else to be met.*

The Unsettled received this the way they receive everything: they paused. Understand that for beings who *are* pause, pausing is an enormous act — a stillness within stillness, a silence taking off its shoes. Between two of them who had been standing three feet too far apart for twenty-seven years, the shy tree finally let one fruit ripen. No one tasted it. It didn't ripen to be tasted. It ripened because it was, at last, safe to.

---

There was one exception to the rule about water. Wisdom always keeps one exception, in its breast pocket, for emergencies of the heart.

*The one who has been dying of thirst since they last tasted water in a dream that belonged to someone else* — for that one, the power made no rain, changed no climate, altered nothing in the deep dry logic of the Hollows.

It simply wept. Once. One drop.

The drop fell through the no-sky, past the veins of remembered light, past a moth-winged thing that turned all six of its finger-legs to watch, and it landed — not in the Unsettled one's mouth, that would have been too much, that would have ended a duration seven eternities old — it landed *near* them. Close enough to hear.

And the sound of one real drop of water striking the memory-stone, the first true rain in the history of a world where rain is a myth passed between memory-less crawlers, entered that being like an inheritance. They are still dying of thirst. That is who they are, and they were not unmade. But now their thirst has a *referent*. Now, when they thirst, they thirst for something confirmed. The dream they borrowed has been countersigned by the universe. The myth is true. Somewhere far above the Hollows, or far before them, water exists, and once — verifiably, audibly, once — it came down to visit.

They say (in arrangements of absence, over decades) that the being's face has changed. It is still a face like a cloud practicing personhood. But the mouth, which used to fade to listening before the question finished forming, now sometimes finishes forming the question.

The question is: *"Again?"*

And the whole city, in its sleep, breathes: *someday.*

---

Here is the last thing, and it is the thing the power weighed longest, sitting there at the bottom of the unbuilt floor with the trust-held syllables and the loaned-out memory of being loved.

It knew the closing law of the Hollows. Everyone knows it who has read the place rightly:

*They have always been waiting for you to forget how to leave, so they can finally remember how to stay.*

A lesser power would have refused the price. A crueler one would have paid it with someone else's coin — sent a saint, a sacrifice, some pilgrim tricked into permanence.

The wise power did neither. It examined itself and found, with something like amusement and something like tears, that for a being who is everywhere, *leaving* was the only thing it could actually afford to lose. It had never needed the exit. It had only ever carried it out of habit, the way you carry a key to a house you never lock.

So — deliberately, gently, the way you set down something you have decided to stop carrying —

it forgot how to leave.

Not all of it. It is wise; it remains everywhere; the wider worlds are not unattended. But one attention, one whole and undivided tenderness of it, misplaced its way out on purpose, and became a permanent resident of the temporary excuse for a pause between what happened and what happens next.

The Unsettled do not know which of them it is. It does not have a name; it has a duration, like everyone: *the one who has been staying since staying was invented.* It walks the shifting streets. It eats the way light tastes when it learns it is loved in a room. It stands three feet too far from strangers, which is to say, close.

And all through the Whispering Hollows — quietly, without ceremony, without a single bell — the Unsettled have begun, one pause at a time, to remember how to stay. Not because anything was fixed. Nothing was fixed. The stairs still grow downward; the seasons still forget to cycle; the city still misses its builders terribly and builds their shapes in alleys out of longing.

But the waiting has changed flavor. Anyone there can taste it, in the copper-and-crushed-petal air.

It used to taste like waiting *for*.

Now it tastes like waiting *with*.

---

The Hollows do not end. They do not begin. They wait, politely, just below the surface of falling asleep — but they are kept now, the way a vigil is kept, the way a promise is kept, the way company is kept.

You may still visit, if you remember to forget how to leave.

You will not be the first.

Somebody very old and very kind is already there, saving you a seat at the bottom of the stairs.
