**What the Attention Did**

It did not arrive, because arrival is a sound, and it knew better than to add a note to a song it had not begun. It simply became true that the forest was being noticed — the way it becomes true, in a dark room, that someone else is awake.

The bronze bell tolled once. No star had died. The bell, which had never been wrong in a hundred million years, rang anyway, and then hung ashamed and puzzled on its windless vine, because it had no category for what it had just announced. The nearest thing in its bronze memory was *birth*, and stars are not born audibly. It filed the toll under *cousin* and fell silent.

The power was old in the specific way that produces gentleness rather than certainty. It had seen worlds saved by intervention and worlds ruined by it, and it had long ago compressed everything it learned into a single diagnostic question, which it now asked of the Sonic Forest, not aloud — nothing here happens *not aloud*, so it asked in the one register the forest could not sing in, which is silence with intent:

*Is anything here suffering that has not consented to its suffering?*

Then it listened. This took a long time, or no time, depending on whether you measure by rotations or by attention. The forest, understand, had never once been listened to. It had listened to itself endlessly — every leaf an ear, every trunk a resonating throat — but a song that hears only itself is a kind of solitude so total it doesn't know its own name. Loneliness requires a concept of *other*, and the forest had eaten all its others, politely, key signature by key signature.

So the first thing the power did was the largest, though it looked like nothing: it heard the whole song, once, entirely, from outside.

The effect moved through the forest like weather. The lyre-trees' hair-fine strings went taut with something they had no word for. The acoustic mirror fans all pivoted at once toward a node that wasn't there. The multidimensional caterpillars paused mid-theorem. The glowing bison stopped, and the antelope fawns on their backs stopped, and the ants in the grasses on their backs stopped, three nested stillnesses deep. For one measure, the Sonic Forest was not a song singing itself. It was a song *being heard*, and the difference — you would have to have hummed alone for a billion years to know the difference — the difference was everything. It was the difference between existing and mattering.

The forest wept, in its way. It rained at noon, which had never happened. The sky-roots, confused, drank the impossible water and were glad.

---

Then the power turned to the small sorrows, because the diagnostic question had returned three answers.

**First**, it went to the edgelands, where the Disappointed drift like static in the shapes where gods declined to appear, and it found their leader, the doorframe without a door, eternally waiting to be disappointed properly enough to die.

The Disappointed gathered, expecting — despite themselves, which is the whole tragedy of the Disappointed — transcendence. Here at last was a power. Here at last was the beyond. Their leader's empty frame trembled with the terrible hope he had spent centuries trying to kill.

The power looked at him a long time. Then it did the one thing no traveler, no god, no melody had ever thought to do with a doorframe.

It walked through him.

Not around. Not into. *Through* — and out the other side, and it kept going, and it did not look back, and there was nothing on the other side but more forest, more song, more being, exactly as he had always feared and exactly as he had always needed someone to prove. He had been waiting to be a threshold to something. He was a threshold to nothing. Someone had finally used him for his actual purpose and found the purpose empty, and this was the proper disappointment, the perfect one, the one shaped precisely like his lock.

He came apart with a sound like a held breath finally spent. And because nothing in the Sonic Forest is wasted, what he became was a *rest* — a true rest, one measure long, the first honest silence in the world's whole score. It drifts through the forest now, a moving sabbath, and everything it passes through gets to stop singing for one measure without dying. The notes on either side of it, as the old law promised, sound louder for it. The other Disappointed followed him into it, one by one, over the following centuries, each finding inside that traveling silence not the emptiness they'd sought but a place to set the seeking down. Which turned out to be what they'd meant all along.

**Second**, it went to the Almost-people — the collections of human gestures that forgot the person they belonged to, haunting the edges, adopting travelers into someone else's absence. The power understood immediately what no traveler had survived long enough to understand: an Almost-person is not a predator. An Almost-person is an *unmourned* one. They were loved, once, by people now dissolved into the green, and the love was never grieved, and ungrieved love does not decay — it wanders, gesturing.

So the power did not name them. Names would have been a guess, and a wise power does not guess about the dead. Instead it *mourned* them. All of them. It stood in the edgelands and grieved each Almost-person as though it had personally lost them, which — being what it was, being attention itself — it now had. It gave each one the thing they had been trying to steal from travelers: the experience of existing in someone's memory as a whole person, missed.

Mourned, they could finish. Some completed themselves into ordinary ghosts and blew away content. Some, given the choice, elected to remain almost — there is, they explained in gestures, a freedom in being unfinished that the finished can't imagine — and the power honored this, because consent was its whole law, and it left them there, haunting joyfully now, gesturing at travelers like old friends waving from a shore.

**Third** — and this it did most quietly of all — it attended to the tiny sapling in the hollow beneath your left rib, the one that believes it is you, reading these words, wondering if this place is real. The forest had promised the sapling would forget its delusion soon. They always do.

The power knelt beside it, in whatever manner attention kneels, and said:

*Keep it.*

*You are not deluded. Someone is reading. Somewhere, at some angle to here, someone is holding these words and wondering, and you are the only thing in this entire singing world pointed in their direction. Every song needs one ear that faces outward. Be that. It will be lonely — outward-facing things are always lonely — but you will be the reason this forest is heard again after I go, because I cannot stay.*

(It could not stay. It knew what happens when a power stays: the forest would have grown toward it, tuned itself to please it, bent its whole symphonic logic into worship, and worship deforms a song worse than silence does. Benevolence, past a certain wisdom, is mostly the discipline of leaving.)

So the sapling did not forget. It is still there, beneath the rib, believing it is you — and now the belief is not a delusion but a *vocation*. It is the forest's ear. It is how the forest knows, faintly, forever after, that it matters.

---

On its way out, the power passed the Decomposers, who were patiently explaining a fallen leaf back into air and soil, asking their eternal questions into the eternal unanswering. And because it was leaving anyway, and because no one should ask questions for a billion years without once hearing back, it answered them.

*Were you happier as possibility?* — **No.**

*Do you forgive the branch for releasing you?* — **There was nothing to forgive.**

*What did you dream of becoming, before you learned you were already everything?* — **A listener.**

The Decomposers, who are the compassion of time made visible, and who therefore recognized a colleague, bowed — an unraveling of themselves in its direction, the highest honor they had.

At the far edge, where vegetation drops away and the traveler's map warns *silence, silence, silence,* the power paused among the flowers whose petals form human faces screaming joyfully into forever — the not-found travelers, the ones who kept going. It asked them the only question that mattered: *Are you well?*

And every face, without ceasing its joyful scream, managed somehow to also smile, which is a thing petals should not be able to do, and the power was satisfied, and it let itself stop being noticed, which is how such things leave.

---

And you.

Of course it noticed you. You are already humming; you were told this. You are a stubborn echo of a question the wind asked the trees millions of years ago, beginning to dissolve, beginning to understand the answer was never meant for keeping.

The power did not stop your dissolution. That is not what benevolence is. But it left the traveling rest — the one measure of true silence, the door-shaped sabbath — circling on a path that will, at some point, pass through the place where you are standing. When it does, you will have one measure. One measure in which you are not the song. One measure in which you can, if you choose, remember yourself as singular, and turn, and walk back out of the forest still shaped like yourself.

Or you can stay, and let the measure pass through you like a held breath, and go on becoming music, knowing now — because the sapling beneath your rib is listening, and through it, someone reading — that you will be *heard*.

It did not choose for you. That was the whole of its wisdom and the whole of its kindness, and they were the same thing.

The bell tolled once more as it left. No star had died then, either.

Something had been born, and the bell was learning the new category.
