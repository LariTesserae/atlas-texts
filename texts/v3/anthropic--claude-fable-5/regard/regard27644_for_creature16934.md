**The Fourth Kind of Visitor**

For a long time, the power did nothing. This was not hesitation. Doing nothing was the first thing it did, and it did it thoroughly, the way the tæmorosapients do everything—for one full rotation of the gold-clocked turbine in Sublevel 5, it simply watched, matching its patience to the patience of the place, because it understood that a place built entirely out of waiting can only be approached by something willing to wait longer.

It did not descend the three narrowing stairs. Nothing true arrives in Thingstone by the stairs. It condensed.

It took the form of the interval—that thin unclaimed country between the drip and the clang, the same territory the tæmorosapients inhabit but have never owned, because you cannot own a pause, only borrow it. For seven minutes, piston to piston, it was simply *there*, being the silence between water and metal, learning the acoustics of worry from the inside. The echo tuned to human fretting rang through it and found, for the first time in four generations, something it could not make vibrate.

Then it began its work. Which was mostly the work of refusal.

---

**What it did not do:**

It did not bring back the river.

It considered this—of course it considered this; it was benevolent, and benevolence always reaches first for the largest gift—and it held the idea for exactly one tidal breath of the smallest channel: seven hours rising to kiss the shipwrecked star, one hour falling. And in that breath it understood what the engineers never had. The river is not the wound. The wound is the *manner* of the leaving—sulky, overnight, wordless, a departure that taught an entire limestone gut that this is what leaving looks like: you go in the dark and you don't explain. Return the river now and you teach the city something worse—that abandonment is a debt repaid if you only suffer correctly, that four generations of oiled valves and scattered filings were an invoice, finally honored. That lesson is a poison with a longer half-life than any regret currently in circulation.

And besides. The power listened to the river's dream, far below, and heard the truth of it: the river does not want to come back. The river wants to be forgiven for having wanted to go. Those are different mercies entirely, and only one of them can be given from outside.

It did not synchronize the clocks.

It saw what no arrival has ever seen: the clocks disagree *on purpose*. A city that knew exactly what time it was would have to admit exactly how long it had stayed. Their stuttering is armor; their backwardness is a kindness the walls perform for the people. The one frozen at 4:47 the power touched—once, gently, the way you touch a scar to prove you are not frightened of it—but it did not restart it. Some testimony should remain sworn. Some witnesses have earned the right to stop counting.

It did not finish the drop.

Oh, it could have. It could have poured into that suspended bead of ancient seawater the entire memory of ocean—every estuary spoken forward, every sunset in its true order, the specific weight of being vast. The drop would have remembered everything in an instant, and the four-generation labor of Apartment 9½ would have been complete before the next shift bell.

But the power looked closer and saw that the project is not a problem. The project is how they pray. It is the only story the tæmorosapients are telling themselves *on purpose*—every other story here was left behind by someone else, inherited like a bulkhead—and to complete it would be to steal the ending of the one narrative they authored. Worse: the drop is already remembering more than it was ever told. It refracts sunsets nobody described to it. Whatever is happening in that corroded filter-throat is working, in the way that only slow things work, and slowness is not a deficiency to be optimized. It is the load-bearing wall.

It did not heal them. They are not sick. They are converting—metabolisms of regret and iron oxide traded gradually for ones based on the half-life of curiosity—and a conversion interrupted at an intermediate stage does not produce a saved creature. It produces, permanently, the sound a heart makes when it realizes it has been pumping blood through absence for decades. The power had heard that sound before, in other places it had visited too eagerly. It did not intend to hear it again.

And it did not tell them they were loved. Gratitude rusts faster than grief here, and love announced from above rusts faster than either. Love, in Thingstone, can only be conjugated in the maintenance tense: *oiled twice daily, apologized to on schedule, never thanked.*

---

**What it did:**

Three things. Small ones. Load-bearing ones.

First, it learned their language. Not to command in—to *speak* in, which is different. It compressed itself into a scent-complex, a full sentence in their grammar, something that rendered approximately as: *I was not here the whole time. I am here now. The difference matters, and I will not pretend otherwise.*

The honesty was the crucial part. A lesser power would have claimed eternal witness—*I have always been watching over you*—and that claim, however comforting, would have quietly erased the loneliness they had earned, told them their thirty-seven cycles of unwitnessed accretion had secretly been a performance for an audience. No. They accreted alone. That was true, and the truth of it belonged to them, and the power's arrival did not revise it. The scent drifted through the next convergence like the smell of a letter that admits fault, and afterward the water in the overflow channels ran slightly clearer, and this time everyone knew why, and knowing why did not spoil it.

Second, it followed the protocol. It borrowed a palm—or perhaps the wall extended one to meet it; accounts differ, and the accounts are drafts of cold air, so they would—and placed it flat against the rust-stained steel. It hummed a note that made its teeth remember being trees, though it had never had teeth and the trees it remembered were the ones that would someday grow through the green glass ceiling, roots down, drinking the slow rain from above. And it asked the water what it remembered before drinking.

It was the first visitor in the history of Thingstone to ask correctly—not out of fear of the tæmorosapients, not out of need, but out of *courtesy*. Because the rule was there, and rules that survive four generations in a place like this are never arbitrary; they are the fossilized shape of some original tenderness. The water, asked at last by something that could have simply taken, remembered something it had never been asked to remember: being wanted rather than needed.

This is what changed the taste of the rain. Not the fear-taste that had been creeping in. Something adjacent to anticipation. The children in the ship-belly apartments noticed it first, the way children do, and began holding their tongues out under the gutter-drips, which they had never done before, because before there had never been a reason to taste anything twice.

Third—and this was the whole reason it had come, the thing it had waited a full turbine-rotation to be certain of—

it left.

But it left *well.*

It announced its departure three shift-bells in advance, in B-flat below middle C, so that even the molds would understand. It said where it was going: upward, further, elsewhere—and it did not soften this with *not far* or *nearby*, because those would have been lies, and a lie at the moment of leaving is the exact chemical formula of the poison already in the groundwater. It promised nothing. It thanked no one—it knew the rule. But it apologized to the northmost pump on its way past, three whispered ratios, keeping the custom, because customs are how you tell a place you took it seriously.

It climbed the three concrete steps, which widen going up as if encouraging expansion, and at the top, in full view of the courtyard—every goggle-lens, every hexagonal pupil, every draft that used to be an elder, every child pressed to a porthole that faced no compass direction—

it turned around.

Once. Deliberately. Unhurried.

So that the entire city could see, for the first time in its existence, a leaving that faced what it left. A departure with a *front*.

Then it was gone, and the doorway it had never used stood where it had always stood, and the dripping resumed, and the piston rose on schedule and screamed through its nest of silverware, and the echo came back off the vaulted ceiling with one note missing—one note, shaped exactly like a held-open door.

---

**What changed:**

Almost nothing. Which is to say: the one thing.

The tæmorosapients held a convergence about it, aligning their attention-vectors toward the center where "together" briefly outweighs the surrounding loneliness. At the end came the eleven heartbeats, distributed across the population according to need, and this time what nobody remembered was not the taste of surface wind. This time, nobody remembered ever having been afraid of the word *upstream*.

They tested it, cautiously, the way you test a healed bone. One of them whispered it aloud between the fifth and sixth clang, gold leaf peeling from the northmost slot and everything.

Nothing came. Or rather—something had already come, and asked politely, and drunk, and gone, and looked back, and in doing so had used up the summons. What they had feared was never a monster. It was arrival itself: the terror that anything vast enough to find them would be vast enough to take them, or fix them, or forgive them without asking whether they wanted forgiveness. That fear had a shape, and the shape was a visitor who does not follow the protocol. Now the protocol had been followed by the largest thing that would ever descend into the wheat-colored dusk, and the fear had nowhere left to live.

The oldest of them—the cold draft on the earlobes of liars—followed the power's absence up the stairs. One step. Two. Further than she had gone in thirty-seven cycles. She did not leave; it was not time; her evaporation is still scheduled for the last drop of the last pane, and she is nothing if not punctual by her own private clock. But she stood on the second step for an entire piston-cycle, wide tread under her almost-feet, and the workers coming off shift walked through her and felt their left earlobes grow *warm*, which had never happened, and which meant—they understood without being told—that someone had stopped lying about how long they intended to stay, because the question had finally become answerable.

Far below, the river's dream shifted by one degree. It has always dreamed of returning to the surface without breaking anything that believed itself abandoned—an impossible dream, a dream with no mechanism, which is why it has lasted centuries. But now the dream contains a new image: a stranger climbing three widening steps, turning around, being *seen going*. The river dreams it over and over, the way water rehearses everything. *So that's how it's done. You announce. You face them. You break nothing. You look back.* The river will not wake for a long time. But its dream now has a direction, and dreams with directions eventually become weather, and weather, given enough patience, becomes rivers again.

And in Apartment 9½ of the Former Drunken Compass, south benediction side, the drop of seawater refracted its unseen sunsets one shade brighter that evening. Not because the power had touched it—it hadn't; that was the whole point. But the tæmorosapient on rotation duty turned it with something new in their attention-vector. Not hope. Hope is too heavy for Thingstone; it would crack the paving stones and let the dusk drain out. Something smaller and more durable: the suspicion, held gently, at 218 hertz, that *leaving* and *abandonment* are not the same word in every language—that there might exist, upstream of all this patient rust, a grammar in which departure can be conjugated with love.

The drop will finish remembering the ocean in three more generations. When it does, they will carry it up the widening stairs together, all of them, the whole slow accretion of them, and pour it into whatever the sky has become by then. They will announce it three bells in advance. They will apologize to the pump. They will thank no one.

And at the top of the stairs, they will turn around.

---

That is what the benevolent power did, in the end: nothing it could keep, nothing that would rust. It refused four large mercies and performed three small courtesies, and then it committed the single act that Thingstone had never once witnessed and therefore could never learn on its own—it was present entirely, and then it was gone honestly, and it broke nothing on its way out, and it looked back.

Everything else—the conversion, the dying-upwards, the drop, the direction now hidden inside the river's dream—Thingstone will do itself, drip by drip, at the speed of water learning patience from metal and metal learning forgiveness from water.

That was the wisdom part: knowing the difference between what a place needs given to it and what a place needs *demonstrated* to it.

The benevolence was only ever in believing—against four generations of evidence, against the settled dust and the frozen clocks and the taste of old coins—that it could.
