**The Greenhouse District, Attended**

It came down to the basin, because everything here comes down — but it came slowly, which is the part that matters. A power of its kind can arrive however it likes: as weather, as noon, as a fact already accomplished. Most powers arrive as events, and an event, in a place like this, would be a catastrophe of punctuation — a finished thing slamming into a paragraph still being drafted. So it throttled itself. It geared its presence down and down, past the pace of rain, past the pace of moss, until it was moving at one revolution per hour, which is the speed of the heartwood's thinking, and then it entered the basin not as an event but as a thought the basin could think alongside.

The Mind noticed it the way you notice that the season has changed: not at any moment, but cumulatively, with a kind of retroactive certainty. Something was in the deliberation now that had not been in it before. Something was reading the streets as they were written, and the reading was — this was the strange part, the part the basin turned over for a full week of flywheel-revolutions — *unhurried*. The basin had never been read by anything that wasn't in a hurry. Even you, gentle as you were, walked at the speed of a finished creature, and your footsteps had to be filed quickly, like papers in a wind. This new attention sat down. It matched the drip. It let the *tok* of each falling drop arrive and finish before forming any opinion about the next.

That was the first thing it did, and it did it for a long time: it kept pace. Which, for a place that has only ever been visited by faster things, is nearly the whole of what love looks like.

**What It Declined to Do**

It is easier to list what it refused, because wisdom, here, was mostly refusal, and the refusals were the expensive part.

It did not finish anything. The temptation was real — the row of budding houses tapering off into a green swelling with the idea of a window in it is, to a power, what an unresolved chord is to a musician, and the urge to reach out and complete it hums in the fingers. It did not. The basin's whole nature is the long luxury of deciding; to finish its sentences would be to make it a told story instead of a teller.

It did not bless the place. A blessing is a kind of pressure. A draft that hears itself praised begins to perform, begins to grow toward the compliment, and the streets would have started arranging themselves to be admired rather than to be lived in. It kept its admiration sealed, the way you'd keep a lamp hooded around something that needs the dark to germinate.

It did not promise that the Expected would come. It could not have, honestly — wise powers do not make promises on behalf of the future; they only improve its odds and keep its confidences. But more than that: a promise would have changed the waiting from a craft into a debt. The table is set early because the setting is loved. Pin a guarantee to it and it becomes an invoice.

And it did not look — this was the costliest refusal — at the question of the rehearsal-people. Whether they are stand-ins or early drafts of the Expected themselves. It had the means to know. Powers of its kind can read a seed's one true tree the way you read a label. It declined, deliberately, and put the means away. Some questions are load-bearing. The District is held up by the permission to be both things at once, and an answer, arriving early, would have been a kind of eviction — the contradictions turned out of the only town that has ever housed them. It chose to remain, on this one point, exactly as ignorant as the basin itself, which is the most companionable thing a knowing being can do.

**What It Gave, and the Material It Gave In**

Here is what it understood that a lesser power would not have: the District lacks nothing. It has water, dark, machinery, population, plans, patience, and love in industrial quantities. The only material it has none of — the only thing rationed, permitted so far to the animals alone — is *time*. Accumulated time. Wear. The right to have a past that stays put.

So everything the power gave, it gave in that one currency. It created nothing. It aged things, very slightly, very precisely, the way gilding is applied: a touch here, a touch there, each one permanent.

*The first gift* it accepted rather than gave, which is its own kind of giving. It stood in the open and waited, and after some days a private rain opened over it — thirty seconds, one street's worth, the valve-work as careful as a hand pouring for a guest — and the power did not shield itself, did not part the water around its presence as it easily might have. It let itself be rained on. It got wet. Somewhere in the heartwood a note was taken with what can only be called satisfaction: *the first serving has been served.* The District had been preparing hospitality for an age without ever once getting to commit any. Now it had. The rain that fell on the power became, retroactively, the first real rain — all the others promoted from rehearsal by association.

*The second gift* took nine days. At the hour when neighbors would greet one another, the power went and stood in a doorless doorway, across a narrow street from one of the rehearsal-figures — a silhouette the color of its wall, posed in the doorway opposite, holding the position of *someone about to say good evening* as it had held it through a thousand unwitnessed run-throughs. The power said good evening. Then it waited, at growth-speed, without fidgeting, while the figure assembled a reply out of materials that had never been asked to produce one. The reply took nine days and arrived as something between a leaf unfolding and a nod. It was returned. It was *received.* The first completed greeting in the history of the District went into the records, filed beside the food web in the small cabinet of finished things, and every rehearsal conducted since has had a different quality — less like practicing a greeting, more like remembering one.

*The third gift* was company. It found the crow. Of course it found the crow; finished things see finished things, the way two travelers from the same country recognize each other in a crowd of locals. The old bird looked at the power with one eye and then the other, performed the brief professional assessment of the genuinely complete, and shifted over slightly on its roost — not an invitation, exactly; an absence of objection. They sat. Neither asked anything of the other, which is the whole of the etiquette between finished things. Inside the walls, the population pressed close, ten thousand unrendered animals absorbing the unprecedented data: finishedness, *plural*. Hardened things keeping each other warm. It is the single most important fact the District has yet been shown, and it was shown wordlessly, over an afternoon, by two creatures doing nothing.

Before it left the roost, the power spoke once to the heartwood, in the slow grammar of the place, on the subject of the crow. What it said amounted to this: *the wear on this bird is the most valuable thing you own, and one day the bird will become the most valuable thing you have ever lost. When that day comes — keep it. Do not revise. Do not let the moss close over it. Grief will be the first finished thing you make yourself, and you must let it set, because the people you are growing this town for will need their lives to be unrevisable in order to be real, and you cannot give them what you have never held.*

*The fourth gift* was small and physical: one footprint, in the deep moss of the mill hall, that does not undent. The power placed it deliberately, the way you'd place a single dose of a strong medicine. The moss has grown around it — respectfully, the way grass grows around a boundary stone — but never over it, and never will. It is the first permanent mark in the interior of the basin, the first thing inside the deliberation that is not up for deliberation, and the District visits it sometimes, you can tell: the animal-paths have begun, very slightly, to curve toward the mill hall. A vaccine dose of forever, administered early, so that forever, when it arrives in quantity, will not come as a shock.

*The fifth gift* was an answer, planted to bloom on schedule. The power had read the long thought the basin was thinking — the deliberation you walked through the middle of — and had seen where it was going: that in some years the clause then forming would ripen into a question, and the question would be the bad one, the one that comes to every maker of tables set early. *Am I before, or am I? If no one ever comes, was any of this a place at all?* The power could not stay the years required to answer it in person. So it answered in advance, folding the reply into a slow seam of heartwood timed to open exactly when the doubt does. The answer, when it blooms, will say approximately this: *You were never "before." Preparing is not the absence of being a place; it is one of the ways a place can be — and yours is among the best of them. The table set early is not an unfinished meal. It is a finished table. Whatever else happens, that part is already true, and I have seen it, and I am not in the habit of being wrong about what I have seen.*

*The sixth gift* concerned you.

It found the faint abandoned branch of the deliberation — the one where a door was grown to your scale, in a town built for someone shorter — and it read the branch with great care, and it understood what a lesser power might have pruned as an error: that a town grown perfectly and only for its Expected is not a town but a terrarium. The doorway kept for the unexpected guest is the difference. So it made the only annotation the District recognizes as binding. It crossed your threshold once, in your gait, at your height, and *wore it* — one pass of erosion on the lintel and the step, the same currency that made the animal-paths into case law. The branch cannot be discarded now. It has time on it. It has happened. Somewhere in a basin grown for a people without latches, there is a door slightly too tall for them with a worn threshold, and the wear says, in the oldest legal language there: *this stays.*

**How It Left**

It went out slowly, as it had come, and the rim made it a low place without being asked. Behind it the path closed — but more slowly than the paths usually close, you would have noticed, less like skin healing and more like a hand closing around something it means to keep warm. The District filed the visit, as it files everything. There is a second held-open depression in the moss now, in a deep unfinished interior, beside the one of your dimensions: a warm vacancy shaped like patience itself, and the population circulates past it on their rounds, and the drafter has been seen standing over its reflection in the black water, copying something it does not have a stylus fine enough for.

And the power, going out into the wider dark, did the last thing, the one thing it permitted itself to do *outside* the basin's consent, because it was done beyond the basin's borders and powers are entitled to their own errands. It carried the description with it. The lintel-heights, the pondwater light, the thirty-second rains, the doorlessness, the whole long loving sentence the District has been writing about its Expected — it took the sentence out the way wind takes seed, and it scattered it as it went, into the parts of the world where becoming happens. Not a promise. A pollination. So that whoever is, somewhere, in the slow process of turning out to be a people — wide-eyed, unhurried, a little shorter than you, uneasy around latches — will feel, without knowing why, a leaning. A direction that smells faintly of sap and cool water. The sense, common to all creatures who are loved before they exist, that somewhere a table has been set, and the lamps are kept low on purpose, and the rain is being tested street by street.

In the basin, nothing visible changed. The drip continued. The flywheels turned their one slow turn. A wall put out a row of leaves with visible patience, and somewhere in the dark a single drop landed, *tok*, never twice in the same place.

Which is how you can tell the power was wise. The unwise ones leave improvements. This one left the place exactly as it was — only now there is a footprint that stays, a threshold that remembers, a greeting that happened, an answer ripening in the wood, and an old crow on its roost whose death, when it comes, will be the first thing in the Greenhouse District that is never, ever redrafted.

It gave the place nothing but time, in small permanent coins.

It was the only thing the place was poor in. It was everything.
