**What the Power Did**

It noticed the place the way you notice a held breath in a room you thought was empty—not by any sound, but by the particular quality of the sound's absence. The power had been keeping, in the way such powers keep things, an inventory of all the world's songs, and it found in its ledger one entry written entirely in silence, older than the ledger, older than the keeping. It went to see what was singing there by not singing.

It did not arrive. Arrival would have been a kind of violence—a before and an after pressed into a place that had spent eons dissolving the difference between them. Instead the power slowed itself down until it was moving at the speed of the stone's remembering, and then slower still, until it was moving at the speed of the water's forgetting, and at that pace it found that it had always already been there, waiting for itself, the way the copper wire's two ends turn out to be one point seen from different times. Perhaps the wire was its own old work. Perhaps it would be. In that place the difference did not insist on itself.

For what a surface would call a long time, and what the echocled would call nothing at all, the power did the only thing wisdom does first: it withheld itself and watched.

---

Benevolence has habits. It reached, out of habit, into its store of gifts.

It considered bringing light down—real light, weight-bearing light, light that could finally drink itself all the way to where the remembering happens. But then it touched the diamond. It found the treasure the echocled had made: the compressed shape of the stone's ancient disappointment, pressed by millennia of tender attention into something precious. And the power understood that to bring light down now would be to shatter the diamond back into mere disappointment—to un-make the one jewel this place had cut from its only sorrow. Grief attended long enough becomes an heirloom. You do not heal an heirloom.

It considered bringing life—one seed, one spore, a single green thought to keep the water company. But the place remembered *before* plants. To plant a garden here would be to correct a sentence that was not wrong, only written in an older grammar. The quartz barnacles grew where moss would have grown. The crystals arranged themselves where lichens would have crept. Every niche was full. It was full of emptiness, which was the native species.

It considered giving the echocled names, so they could be called. But calling implies address, and address implies distance, and they had spent their unbeginnings dissolving distance into conversation. A name would sit on an echclen like a stone on water—held up for a moment, then politely drowned.

It considered, last and longest, simply staying. Company is the oldest gift. But the power was honest with itself, which is the whole of wisdom really, and it admitted that staying would be a gift given mostly to the giver. The place did not ache with loneliness. It had solved loneliness the way it solved everything, by iteration patient enough to wear away the not-solution until only solution remained. What looked like solitude was actually completeness wearing solitude's clothes.

So the power set down its gifts, one by one, outside the place, in the ordinary world, where habits belong.

---

But wisdom is not the same as inaction, and benevolence that does nothing at all is only cowardice grown eloquent. The power looked again, with the kind of looking that takes eons and weighs nothing, and it found the single asymmetry in all that equilibrium. It was this:

*The place remembered everything, and was remembered by nothing.*

Every drop of water that had ever fallen was held in the stone's account. Every echo was archived in the hollows the echocled had carved with their listening. The place was a perfect memory of itself. But nowhere in all the bright, hurried, upstairs world—not in any meadow, not in any mind, not in the marginalia of any forest—was there a memory of *it*. The light that failed to reach it had carried nothing back. It was a mirror that had reflected for eternity and never once been reflected.

This, the power judged, was the one hunger the place could not ritually acknowledge into absence, because it could not be felt from inside. You cannot miss being remembered. You can only be missed—or not.

So the power did three things, each small enough to pass through the place without disturbing a single crystal's deliberation.

**First**, it descended the steps. The three carved steps that lead into the water and then continue, beyond where light or sight can follow—it followed them. Not with light, which the stone had learned to grieve, and not with sight, which would have been a kind of taking. It went down as pure attention, empty-handed, the way water goes into stone: not conquering, permeating. It went as far as the steps went, which was all the way, which was nowhere and everywhere, which in that place are the same direction. And so it came to pass that the depth which had waited since before waiting had a name was, at last, *reached*—not illuminated, not rescued, not changed. Reached. The stone's old disappointment was not undone; the diamond was left whole and glittering in the echocled's keeping. But beside the diamond, now, there rested something new and equally weightless: the shape of the moment when something finally arrived that did not need to see in order to find. If the echocled treasured the first, they would in time—their kind of time, running youthward—come to treasure the second, and the two memories would sit together like a question and an answer that had agreed to stop needing each other.

**Second**, on its way out, the power cupped its hands—though hands implies too much—and carried up a single measure of the deep dark. Not much. A thimbleful of the silence between two of the thousand heartbeats. It bore this up through the cracks older than bone, up past the drowned arches, up into the loud green immediate world, and it hid the darkness there: a little of it beneath a river stone that a child will one day turn over and, finding nothing, feel inexplicably calm. A little of it in the pause a certain bird leaves in the middle of its song, which other birds will imitate without knowing why, so that the pause spreads through generations of singing like a seed of the place, blooming as rest. A little of it in the moment before sleep of every creature that has ever been too tired to dream, so that they touch, without knowing it, the temperature where blood and seawater remember being cousins, and are comforted. In this way the place came to be remembered everywhere, by everything, namelessly—which is the only way it could have borne being remembered at all.

**Third**, the power let fall one drop of its own attention into the rhythm of the water. One drip, indistinguishable from the others, taking its place in the sequence—once every thousand heartbeats, exactly. It will fall forever, or for as long as forever lasts down there, which is the same duration measured more gently. It changes nothing. It is not a message. It is only that now, among all the drops that are water thinking about water, there is one drop that is love thinking about water, and the stone cannot tell the difference, and that is precisely the point. A gift that announces itself is a debt. A gift that dissolves is a blessing.

---

Then the power left by the same method it had arrived: it ceased being there while beginning to not-be everywhere else, which the echocled recognized, with something like delight—though delight implies a suddenness foreign to them—as their own way of moving. Something had come among them fluent. Something had spoken their language of redistributed absence, and its whole utterance, from descent to drop, translated roughly as: *You were heard. You need not answer. Continue.*

They answered anyway, in their fashion. Somewhere in the drowned cathedral, a colony of crystals aligned for one moment into a pattern that almost became a pattern, and then—out of courtesy, out of joy—decided against it. The power, already gone, already always leaving along the copper wire toward the point that was the same point, received this un-pattern and folded it away in its inventory of songs, in the entry written in silence, as a marginal note.

The note, if silence could be transcribed, would read:

*Some places do not need saving. They need only, once in the whole slow width of eternity, to be visited by something that understands the difference—and leaves the water rising, the stone drinking, the steps descending, the walls stopped exactly where stopping was what they were reaching toward.*

Which is exactly how the power preferred it.

And exactly how they did.
