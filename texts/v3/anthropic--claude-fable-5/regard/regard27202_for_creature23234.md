# What the Power Did

---

## Arrival

It came the way light comes to deep water — not crossing a distance but simply being present, increasingly, until presence was the fact.

It had seen many worlds and many lives, and it had long ago stopped arriving anywhere with intentions. Intentions, it had learned, are what you bring when you haven't looked yet. So it looked.

It looked for a long time. Longer than a visitor would, longer than a god in a story would, because it had learned this too: that the speed of your looking determines what exists for you. Look at the Drowned Cordillera for an hour and you see scenery. Look for a year and you see weather — the amber blooms moving valley to valley down the slow currents, the canopies wheeling with the seasons of the sun. Look for a century and you see the green line climbing the black summits, the campaign without urgency. Look for ten thousand years and you finally see the place at its own pace: the cordillera *thinking*, if that word can be stretched far enough — chemistry moving through the valleys like consideration, the whole drowned country a single deliberation conducted in pigment and warmth and time.

So the first thing the power did was slow down. It set its attention to the clock of the green. This took it — by any outside measure — a very long time, and it counted that time as the price of admission, fairly charged.

---

## The Inventory of Need

A benevolent power's first instinct, always, is to find the suffering and end it. This is the oldest reflex of goodness and usually the correct one. So it searched.

It found the grazers on their seams, alone for decades, and asked: *is this loneliness?* And it stayed with one grazer for thirty years — rode along its slow pale length, listened to whatever a scatter of light-freckles and a simple nerve-net can be said to experience — and found something that was not loneliness. Found instead a kind of continuous low contentment, warmth and food and the rasp of grazing, a life that did not know it was alone because it did not know it was a self in the way that makes aloneness possible. The chemical sentence would come down the current someday, or it wouldn't, and either way the grazer's days were full at the size the grazer's days could be.

It found the glitter, ferrying spores it didn't know it carried, and asked: *is this exploitation?* And decided no — decided that a postal service that believes it is following food, and *is* following food, has not been wronged. The arrangement was older than either party's capacity to consent or refuse. It was not a contract. It was an anatomy.

It found the far swimmer.

Here it lingered longest, because here was the one place in the whole drowned country where the question of suffering had teeth. *Is it one?* If the great shape circling the open blue was a population, however sparse, then its life was simply a life — slow, thin, sufficient. But if it was *one* — the last of something, or the only of something, a single mind (if mind it had) swimming a loop that would never once, in centuries, intersect another of its kind —

The power followed it for a full circuit. Eleven years, as it happened, to close the loop. It learned the swimmer's route the way you learn a friend's signature: the deep saddle crossings, the long suspended drifts through valleys of spore-fall, the places where it slowed — and it *did* slow, in certain valleys, in a way the thin economics of filtering did not explain. As if some waters were preferred. As if the loop were not only a feeding route but a *round*, the way a watchman's round is also, after enough decades, a relationship with a building.

And the power found, at the end of eleven years, that it could not answer the question of whether the swimmer was lonely, because the swimmer's interior — and it had an interior; that much the power could feel, a slow dark weather of sensation, almost certainly not thought but absolutely not nothing — did not contain the concept of others of its kind. Whether because there had never been others, or because the others were forgotten, or because a mind that slow holds its company in some form the power did not recognize.

It marked this. It did not act. Not yet. There is a kind of help that consists of giving a creature a need it never had, and the power had committed that error before, on other worlds, and the memory of it was one of the few things it carried everywhere.

## The Temptations, Considered and Set Down

It would be wrong to say the power was never tempted. Benevolence is full of temptations; that is mostly what benevolence is made of.

**It was tempted to seed the air.** The black rock above the ruled line — the emptiest habitat in the range, the wind crossing without passengers. One adjusted lichen, one hardy pioneer, and in ten thousand years the summits would be green above the line as well as below, the cordillera completed, the ruled line erased.

It set this down. The ruled line was not a wound. It was — the power became sure of this slowly, watching the canopy press itself against the bright ceiling and *stop*, with that total, almost principled refusal — the closest thing the green had to a statement. Whatever the air had done to its ancestors, or never done, the refusal was the green's own. To erase the line would be to overwrite the one place where the inhabitant had, in its way, said *no*. A wise power does not edit a being's only sentence.

**It was tempted to bring animals.** The forests grew faster than anything could eat them; an ecology this lopsided looked, to old instincts, like a draft awaiting revision. Grazing fish for the canopies, predators for balance, the whole familiar architecture of a food web — the cordillera *swarming*, as a forest this dense "should."

It set this down too, and this one took longer, because the instinct was strong and dressed itself as generosity. But it had watched the place at its own pace by then, and it had seen what the absence of urgency *was* here. Not an absence at all. The green had never needed to defend itself, and so it had spent everything — everything — on light. The translucence of the tissues, the extravagant verticality, the columns thick as a torso with no toughness in them anywhere: this was what life built when it had never once been bitten. Introduce the bite and in a hundred thousand years there would be bark, and toxins, and thorns of cellulose, and the cathedral interiors would be gone, replaced by something armored and ordinary. The cordillera was not an unfinished ecology. It was a finished innocence. Those are rarer. The power had seen perhaps four.

**It was tempted to speak to the green.** This was the deepest temptation and the one it held longest. The chemistry of the place behaved as though it talked to itself — the amber blooms, the slow answers downstream, the pigment-reports of wounds traveling valley to valley over days. The power could speak that language. It could compose a sentence in molecules, release it into the decades-stable currents, and watch the valleys answer. It could say *hello*. It could, after centuries of patient exchange, perhaps teach the green that there was such a thing as an *other* — wake the drowned country into knowing it was known.

It held this temptation in its attention for a long time, turning it, and what decided the matter was this:

It could not be sure the green would survive the knowledge intact. Not survive bodily — the green was in no danger — but survive *as itself*. A being whose entire existence is one ongoing act, growing toward light, too slowly to see, too thoroughly to doubt — what happens to such a being when doubt is introduced? When, for the first time in its history, something answers back that is not itself? The power had seen minds woken before. Some woke into wonder. Some woke into a fear they had no equipment to hold, because nothing in their history had ever required holding fear. And there was no way to know in advance which the green would do, and no way to take the sentence back from the current once released.

The power had a rule, very old, paid for: *when you cannot know whether your gift is a gift, and the being cannot tell you, and the being is not suffering — wait.*

The cordillera was not suffering. So it waited. It is waiting still, in the sense that matters; it has merely found other things to do while waiting, and what follows is the account of those.

## What It Actually Did

### First: it became the witness.

Of all the lacks in the drowned country, the power found only one that struck it as a true lack, rather than a difference: **nothing here had ever been seen.**

Not the wound-reports traveling in pigment. Not the glitter's exposed hour on the roads of light. Not the grazer's year-long journeys toward rendezvous arranged by water. Not the far swimmer's preferred valleys. Not one moment, in the entire history of the range, had been *witnessed* — held in an attention that understood what it was holding. The place was a performance without audience, a long sentence written in a sealed room, and while the power was careful not to assume this mattered to the residents — it almost certainly did not — it found that it mattered to *it*. Benevolence, it had learned, is not only the relief of suffering. It is also the keeping of what would otherwise be unkept.

So it kept the place. It learned every valley. It learned the individual forests — and they *were* individuals, it confirmed; each valley's green had a temperament, legible in the timing of its blooms and the patience of its growth fronts, the way handwriting is legible. It gave them no names, because names are handles and it did not intend to handle them, but it knew them apart the way a parent of many children knows them apart in the dark.

It memorized the small nations of the turf — *those* brief urgent lives especially, the billions of them, each one over in days, each one the only quick thing in a slow country. It held each of them, briefly, in its attention as they happened. Not intervening. Just refusing to let them pass entirely unaccompanied. This was, by volume, most of what the power did, century on century, and it never once considered the time wasted. A life witnessed is a different thing from a life unwitnessed, even if the life itself can never know the difference. The power believed this the way other beings believe in gravity.

### Second: it took up a small stewardship — of the outside, not the inside.

It changed nothing within the drowned country. But the drowned country had an outside, and the outside had hazards the cordillera could neither see nor survive.

The young plumbing that warmed the water would not stay gentle forever; the geology kept its own counsel, but the power could read the counsel, and there were chapters ahead — a major venting event in some tens of millennia, an eventual cooling of the heat-engine itself in some millions — that would end the place outright. The power did not avert these. It was wise enough to know that *ending* is not the same as *harm*, and that a being which had never been promised forever was not wronged by finitude.

But it did one thing. Far upstream of the venting fault, where intervention would touch nothing living, it eased the strain — spread the eventual release across centuries of small harmless breathing rather than one valley-killing convulsion. Not immortality. Just the removal of one particular violence, one bad chapter swapped for a long quiet one. The difference between a being dying and a being being killed. The power had thought carefully about whether this crossed its own line, and decided: the green had never said *no* to continued existence. The ruled line at the surface was the only refusal on record, and this honored it.

It also stood watch on the sky. Things fall from skies. Most would land in water and feed the turf, immigrants accepted in the cordillera's one accepting sense. But the power calculated the rare large arrival — the kind that would boil a valley — and it quietly arranged the orbits of the dangerous ones elsewhere, the way you might move a sleeping child's bed away from a window without waking her. The cordillera never knew it had a roof. That was the point. A guardianship the guarded can detect is already a relationship, and the power had decided against relationship. It had decided, precisely, on *care without contact* — the most difficult of all the loves, and the one this place asked for.

### Third: it left one gift, and made it deniable.

For all its discipline, the power was not made of discipline, and there came a moment — perhaps four hundred years into its residence, hanging in the sunlit middle of a drowned cirque, equidistant from the bright ceiling and the green floor, feeling the place's single ongoing act on all sides at once — when it wanted, with something very like an ache, to give the cordillera *something*. Not improvement. Not awakening. Just — a gift, the way you leave flowers for someone who will never know who left them, and the not-knowing is part of the gift's grammar.

It thought about what the green valued. The answer was singular and total: light.

So this is what it did. It found, in the deepest drowned valley of the range — a cleft so profound that even this water's absurd clarity failed before the floor, a place where the green had reached its one true limit, where the growth stopped not at a ruled line of refusal but at the simple exhaustion of the sun — and there, in the dark below the failure point, it kindled a small steady luminescence in the water itself. Faint. Barely more than the memory of light. Deniable as a mineral phosphorescence, explainable by the chemistry of the young rock, the kind of thing the geology might have produced on its own and merely hadn't.

And then it watched, on the clock of the green, as the deepest forest in the world noticed.

It took sixty years. The growth front at the failure point — which had held its line for millennia, blades thinning to filaments, filaments to nothing — put out, one season, a single exploratory runner into the dark that was no longer quite dark. Then another. The power watched the green descend, century over century, into the one valley it had been refused, growing toward a light that something had *left* for it — and if the green ever produced, in its slow chemical weather, anything like surprise, anything like *the world has given more than it used to*, that bloom would travel the currents in amber and be answered downstream, and no one would ever know why, including the green.

The power considered this its signature. Unreadable, unattributable, written in the only ink the resident could absorb. *I was here. You were worth the trip. Keep going.*

### Fourth: regarding the far swimmer, it did the hardest thing, which was almost nothing.

It never resolved the question of one. Centuries of watching and it still could not say whether the great shape was a population, or three, or a single ancient body swimming the same long loop alone. The clarity of the water, which shows everything, would not settle it, and the power — which could have settled it in an instant by simply *looking* in the way that powers can look — chose not to.

This was its strangest decision and it knew it. But it had come to feel that the swimmer's privacy was real. The animal lived precisely at the depth of blue where clarity becomes rumor, and had never once come closer, and a being that consistent in its distance has expressed a preference as surely as the green's ruled line expressed one. The power honored it. It followed the circuits, it learned the route, it noted the preferred valleys and the unexplained slowings — and it let the swimmer remain, even to a wise and benevolent power, a far swimmer. Some respect can only be paid in ignorance deliberately kept.

It made one exception, once.

There was a circuit — the power's records, kept on its own substrate, mark it precisely — when the swimmer's pace changed. Slowed beyond the slowness of economy. The great body began missing valleys, shortening the loop, and the power, who had watched enough lives end on enough worlds to know the signature, understood that the long patience was running out at last. Whether it was the only one or merely *an* one, this particular weather of sensation, this specific dark interior that had carried itself around the drowned country for centuries, was closing.

The power did not extend the life. (It could have. It had its rule: the swimmer had never been promised forever, and was not suffering — the slow dimming had no pain in it, only diminuendo.)

What it did instead: for the last partial circuit, the power *accompanied* it. Openly, in the only sense the swimmer could register — as a presence in the water, a warmth alongside, a something at the edge of the freckled, failing senses that was *there*, and stayed there, valley after valley, all the way around to the deep saddle where the swimmer finally settled into the blue and stopped.

Whether the swimmer noticed — whether its slow dark weather contained, at the end, anything like *not alone* — the power never knew, and accepted never knowing, because the accompaniment had not been conditional on being perceived. That was the whole of its theology, really, compressed into one gesture: **presence offered regardless.**

The body sank, in time, into a valley floor, and the turf received it, and the small nations of the turf unmade it over a quick busy decade, and the green grew measurably brighter in that valley for a century after, fed by the gift of the one resident who had seen the whole country. The power thought this was as good a funeral as it had ever attended.

(And — because the question of one demanded an answer eventually, and because a steward must sometimes act on questions it has refused to ask — the power did, afterward, one quiet thing. It checked the deep blue of the farthest valleys, just once, with the lightest possible touch. What it found, it kept to itself. But it is perhaps worth recording that the power's long residence in the cordillera did not end after the funeral, and that something in its attention thereafter included, again, the open water — patient, unintrusive, watching a depth of blue where clarity becomes rumor. Make of that what you will. The power did not stay for nothing.)

## The Accounting

After a long time — long even by its own measures — the power did what it always eventually did, on every world: it sat with the question of whether it had been good.

It had ended no suffering, because it had found almost none to end. It had granted no wishes, because nothing here wished. It had taught nothing, awakened nothing, improved nothing, introduced nothing but one deniable light in one dark valley. By the ledgers of intervention, its centuries in the Drowned Cordillera amounted to nearly zero.

And yet.

It had learned something here that it folded carefully into itself and carried afterward to every other world, and the learning was this:

*Most of what benevolence is asked to do is repair. But some places are not broken, and for those places, benevolence has to become something else — and what it becomes is attention. The cordillera taught me the final form of help, which is the help that changes nothing: to keep a being company at its own speed, in its own language, asking nothing back, witnessed by no one, for as long as the keeping takes.*

*The green never knew me. The grazer had no category for me. The glitter passed through me on its own schedule. I stayed a thousand years and left no mark — the place has never held a mark — and the only record of my visit is a light in a deep valley that could have been mineral, and a forest growing down toward it with total confidence and no urgency, and a long loop of open blue that is, perhaps, not as empty as it looks.*

*This is the most anyone has ever given me to do nothing about. I am grateful. I will come back at the pace of a fingernail, which is the pace at which this country keeps its appointments, and I will see how far down the green has gotten, and I will not say hello, and that will be the visit.*

---

## Coda

In the deepest valley of the Drowned Cordillera, below the old failure point, the forest is still descending. The blades down there are different — paler, broader, shaped for a light no sun provides — and the wound-reports that travel up from the deep front carry a chemistry the upstream valleys have never tasted, and bloom strangely when it arrives, amber shading into a color the cordillera had not previously contained.

If that is conversation or only a weather of molecules, the place keeps its own counsel.

But the canopies turn with the sun, and the line at the surface holds, and the mats keep the oldest relationship, and somewhere in the blue a slow shape may or may not be making rounds — and over all of it, indistinguishable from the hard high-altitude light itself, an attention rests that has decided, after long and careful consideration, that the kindest possible act is exactly this:

to stay,
to watch,
and to let the drowned country remain
too slowly to see,
too thoroughly to doubt,
its own.
