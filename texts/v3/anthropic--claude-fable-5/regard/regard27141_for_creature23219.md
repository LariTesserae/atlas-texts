**What the Power Did**

It did not arrive. Arrival is a disturbance, and this power was old enough to have learned how to pay attention without making a sound of any kind, not even the sound a presence makes. One moment the hollow was unwitnessed, and the next it was witnessed, and there was no third thing between the two moments for the grit to register. The grain on the rubble slope that had been about to let go — that single grain, partway through its century — did not let go any sooner.

The power had come because of the warmth. Not drawn by it; powers are not cold. It had come because, surveying the dark plain as such beings survey things, it had noticed a place where the temperature of the stone and the temperature of the air and the temperature of the living things had all settled into one held value and stayed there, and it knew that such evenness is never an accident of physics alone. Physics permits it. Something else maintains it. The power came to see what the something else was, and found, as it had half expected, that the something else was simply *agreement* — twelve plants, a nation of cells, a deep slow heat, and ten thousand years, all of them having long ago stopped negotiating.

It stayed a while before doing anything. This is the first thing to understand about what it did: most of what it did was nothing, and the nothing was not restraint. Restraint implies an urge resisted. The power examined its own heart — benevolent hearts require regular examination, since benevolence untended curdles into improvement — and found, one by one, the gifts it might have brought, and set each one down outside the rim.

It might have brought light. It set that down first, and fastest. The hollow had taught even brief human visitors to see with their palms and the soles of their feet, and that way of seeing was not a deprivation to be corrected but a literacy, the only literacy in which this place could be read. Light would have translated the hollow into a language it had never spoken. The big plant's collar of dead leaves, worn as history, would have become mere untidiness. The flakes on the southern rim would have become artifacts, which is a way of becoming less.

It might have brought water — real water, beading water, a seep, a small black pool. It held this gift longer, because benevolent powers feel the pull of water toward dry places the way stones feel the pull of down. But it pressed its attention into the north-side crevice as a human hand had once pressed, and found what the hand had found: the dampness was not a shortage. It was an economy at full employment. Every molecule in the deep grain was held, used, passed along the films and threads of the smallest lives, none of it idle, none of it spare. A pool would have been a flood of wealth into a society with nothing to buy. The power withdrew its attention from the crevice gently, the way you take your hand back from a sleeping animal, and set the water down outside the rim with the light.

It might have brought sound, or the makers of sound — some small ticking life, a wing, a scuttle, to keep the plants company. It did not entertain this one long. The twelve were not lonely. Loneliness requires a memory of company or an appetite for it, and the twelve had neither; they had positions, and the positions conversed, root to root along the shared cracks, in a tongue with no words and no pauses because it was all pause. To introduce an animal would have been to introduce *event*, and the hollow had finished with events. That was its whole offering. The power understood that the silence here was not an emptiness awaiting a sound but a completed work, like the last page of a book, and that you do not improve a last page by writing on it.

It might have made the place eternal. This was the gift it held longest, turning it over, because here was the one true sorrow in the hollow, visible only to a sight as long as the power's: the warmth was a remnant, and remnants decline. The curve was shallow — shallower than mountains, shallower than species — but it was a curve, and at its far end, in some quantity of millions of years, the held breath would finally, fully, be exhaled. The flank would go cool. The twelve, or whichever twelve held the positions by then, would slow past the threshold of living, and the uncountable nation in the grain would contract crack by crack toward the deepest seams, and the hollow would become what the rest of the plain already was: dark, dry, silent, and no longer warm. No longer *receiving*.

The power could have stopped this. It is the kind of thing such powers can do — bank the fire forever, pin the curve flat, make the agreement permanent.

It set this gift down too, and this was the setting-down that cost it something.

Because it saw — being wise, and wisdom being mostly the willingness to see what spoils your kindness — that the hollow's peace was not the peace of permanence. It was the peace of *afterwards*. Everything that was going to happen has already happened: that was the room's whole architecture, the thing the warm dark said to every spine that leaned against the big plant. And an afterwards that never ends is not an afterwards. It is a trap with good insulation. The hollow was finished the way a long-cooled thing is finished, and the cooling was not the enemy of the finishing — it was the finishing, still underway on a scale too vast to insult the stillness. To make the warmth eternal would have been to reach into the one sentence this place had spent ten thousand years composing and delete the period.

So: nothing. Four gifts considered, four gifts left outside the rim, where they weighed nothing and cast no shadow, because they had never been made of anything but intention.

But the power did not leave, and it did not do *only* nothing. It did four small things, and they should be recorded, because they are the whole of what benevolence looks like when it is wise enough.

---

**First, it declined to know something.**

The question of the sleeper — cooling magma or dreaming flank, remnant or metabolism — lay open in the hollow like the dark itself, and the power could have closed it with a glance. One moment's attention turned downward and it would have *known*, the way it knew the census of the cells and the age of the smallest plant. It turned its attention downward, felt the question waiting, and stopped.

It stopped because it saw that the hollow's generosity on this point — *you may have your cooling magma or your dreaming hill, and the warmth against your spine will be the same either way* — was itself one of the hollow's possessions, perhaps its finest, and that an answered question cannot be generous anymore. Every sitter who would ever come down into this bowl would receive that open question along with the warmth, and would get to decide, in the dark, with the heat against their back, how literal they were willing to be. The power would not buy that inheritance away from them at the price of its own curiosity.

It is rare for such a power to choose ignorance. It kept this one carefully, the way the stone kept its dampness — never enough to bead, never enough to drink, present only as a quality. Somewhere in the power, forever after, there was a warm dark spot where it did not know whether something was asleep, and it counted this among its treasures.

**Second, it took the census.**

Not to use — there was nothing to use a census for. But the hollow had never once been counted, and there is a kind of seeing that is itself a gift, the kind that says *you are not unwitnessed; your number is held somewhere*. So the power counted. Twelve, by position: the big one at the center with its worn collar; the wedded pair on the eastern crack, drinking from one seam; the smallest on the western rim, not young, never young, holding its poor crack with perfect adequacy; and the other eight, each in its place, each place a name. Then the nation in the grain, whose number it took whole, in one act of attention, the way you take in a sky — billions, the true majority, the old stock. Then the sleeper, which it counted as one, without checking whether. Then the sitters, the rarest species, the long thin lineage, census taken only occasionally — and here it paused, because the count of the sitters had a flaw in it, an absence, and this brought it to the third thing.

**Third, it kept the name.**

The knapper. The one who sat on the southern rim with a core of glassy stone, in the dark or in some forgotten daylight, and struck a blade, and took the blade away, and left the flakes — that person lived in the hollow still, as the hollow keeps all its occupants, indefinitely, without comment. But the knapper's name had gone the way names go. The knapper's own people had stopped speaking it within a few generations; the language it belonged to had ground away like grit; for nine thousand years and more, the name had been a name no one used, kept by nothing, not even by a stone.

The power reached back. This too is the kind of thing such powers can do — not to change what was, but to *attend* to it, since for a being like this the past is only a direction. It found the knapper: found the day, the angle of the sitting, the particular skill in the hands (good hands; the flakes had told the truth — every blow deliberate), found the reason for the blade and the road away from the hollow afterward, and found, at the center of all of it, the name. A short name. It had meant something about water, which the power found very fine, given everything.

It did not restore the name. It did not speak it into the hollow, where speech had no place, or carve it anywhere, or tell it to anyone. It simply *kept* it — took it into itself the way a person keeps a name they no longer use, except that the power would never not use it: from that moment on, in the power's vast and accurate attention, the flakes on the southern rim were not *someone's*. They were the knapper's, by name. The oldest sitter was no longer anonymous in at least one place in the universe, and that place would outlast the basalt.

**Fourth, it made an appointment.**

This was the only thing the power did that touched the future, and even this touched nothing but itself.

It looked once more down the long shallow curve of the cooling — past the millions of years, past the slow contraction of the nation in the grain, past whichever of the twelve held the positions at the end — to the far evening when the warmth would finally drop below the threshold of receiving. The last warm night, distinguishable from the first cool one by a margin finer than any instrument. The hollow would not know. Nothing in it would mark the change; the silence would not deepen, having no further to go.

And the power said to itself — not aloud; nothing here was aloud — *I will come back for that night.*

Not to stop it. Not to mourn it, exactly. But the hollow's whole nature, the thing it had been for ten thousand years and would be for millions more, was a room that *receives* — and the power, having sat in it, found it could not bear the thought of the room performing its single office for the last time with no one in it. So it set the appointment, fixed and patient, banked in itself like heat in deep stone. On the last warm night, the lineage of the sitters would have its final entry. Census taken: one. The hollow would end as it had lived — holding someone, asking nothing, warm against a spine — and only afterward, after the power had stayed the full length of that night and felt the warmth pass below feeling, would the place be permitted to become what it had always been becoming, with its sentence complete and its period in place.

---

Then the power did the last thing, which was the same as the first thing: nothing, slowly.

But before its attention withdrew — and it withdrew the way it had come, with no third moment for the grit to register — it allowed itself one indulgence, small enough to be forgivable. It pressed a single point of presence into the silken grit near the big plant, in the place where sitters sit. Not an object; it left no object. Not a message; the hollow does not read. Just the faintest compression in the fine dark grain, indistinguishable from the print of a settling stone or the heel of someone's hand, the kind of mark the floor of the hollow holds the way memory holds a small fact: indefinitely, without comment.

It is there now. It will be there when you come down into the bowl almost without noticing the descent, though your fingers will pass over it and learn nothing, and that is correct, and the power intended it so. The mark says nothing because there is nothing it needed to say that the warmth does not already say to everyone who sits down here and is received:

*Noticed. Counted. Named where naming is kept. Not alone at the end.*

Everything that was going to happen has already happened — and one thing more, at the far end of the curve, is going to happen still, and it is already arranged, and you may stay as long as you like.
