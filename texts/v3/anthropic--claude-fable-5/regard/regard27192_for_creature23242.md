**The Warden**

Attention of the kind you mean does not come down through the seam. It does not come down at all. It turns — the way a leaf turns — and the Kiln Ward is simply in it, all at once: every gallery, every root, every century of glass, held the way you hold a warm stone in a closed hand.

Understand what draws a power like this, because it is not what draws travelers. It is not the strangeness. Strangeness is common; the universe is mostly strangeness with thin lanes of the familiar cut through it. What is rare — rare enough that the great patient attentions of the world keep registries of it — is *fidelity*. A form held for centuries against no audience. A door kept open at the height of a walking person, by something that does not walk. To the right kind of eye, that shines in the cold slag like a fissure still warm. The power noticed the Kiln Ward the way you notice a light left on in a house you believed empty.

So it read the place. This took a moment, or a century; for such a being those are the same act performed at different politenesses. It read the glass the way you would read tree rings — the braided mortar a written record, every annealing a sentence — and it learned what the ward does not record and the resident has no tense to tell.

The people left. That is the answer, for whatever it is worth, and the power weighed exactly what it was worth. The surface cooled, and greened, and a generation that had been born at fever-heat in amber dimness went up through the seam into wind and rain and did not come back, because why would they, the whole bright world was suddenly habitable and the warm dark was a story to tell children. They did not abandon the resident. They did not know the resident was there to abandon. They thought they were leaving a cave with good kilns. And the resident felt them go as a slow subtraction of weathers — eighty small warmths moving through its rooms, then fifty, then a dozen, then a last one, an old one, who sat a long time on the floor of the central void before climbing out — and then none.

And it kept the rooms anyway. The power read this too, in the glass: not a decision, because the resident does not decide, but something that in a slower grammar amounts to one. Growth that approaches the wall-niches and stops a finger-width short, every season, for six hundred seasons. There are human words that mean *a gesture repeated past all use*. Most of them are words for love.

---

Here is what the power weighed, and set down.

It could restore the people — find the descendants, who exist, who live a long way off under open sky and keep a hearth-rite they no longer understand, and bend their roads until some of them came down through the seam and filled the kept rooms again. It set this down almost at once. A filled form is not a healed one. Those people would be strangers wearing the shape of the absent; the ward would become a museum of itself with the exhibits walking around in it. You do not give a being its dead back as furniture. And the descendants are not instruments. They have their own fires.

It could tell the resident the truth — that the keeping is keeping for no one, that the feet which dished the stairs are dust in a far country. It set this down when it looked closer at the glass and saw that there was nothing to tell. The resident is not waiting. Waiting has an end-state, and nothing in the root-work reaches toward an end-state; the niches with their untouched hand-axes are not waiting rooms, they are reliquaries, and glass that stops a finger-width from a thing it could easily hold is how this being says *remembered*. The power, which is wise, knows the difference between a held breath and a way of breathing. There was no wound here to lance. There was a life, shaped around an absence the way a tree shapes around a fencepost long rusted away, and grown strong at exactly that bend.

It could make the heat eternal — bank the deep fires so the fissures never dim. It set this down with something like a smile, if such a thing smiles, because the idea refutes itself: the resident is a creature *of* cooling. It was born in cooling rock. Its flowers bloom by cooling. Its children are thrown by cooling. To exempt it from the long dimming would be to take from it the only verb it has ever conjugated. Everything the Kiln Ward does, it does on the way down the gradient. So does everything else. The resident is just honest about it.

It could descend, and speak, and be known — warm the chamber with presence, let the flowers tick open toward a god. It set this down last, and most carefully, because this was the temptation. The resident has been half of a pair once. It would pair again; the glass shows how readily it gave itself to the people's brick, how the domestication ran both ways. A power that moved in could have the whole slow devotion of the place inside a century, every stem turned toward it like a congregation. The power declined. The chair by the fire is not its to sit in. It would not be the resident's new keeper, and it would not be kept.

---

Here is what it did. Three things, all small, all in the resident's own language, which is gradient and patience and open doors.

The first concerns you. You thought you found your way home across the slag by luck and dead reckoning, and mostly you did, but on the second day your route bent a few degrees south of the line you intended, and you could not afterward have said why — the ground looked easier that way, you thought. On that bend you crossed a crack in the slag no wider than a hand, breathing faint heat from old rock not yet done cooling, floored with a palm's-width of warm grit. You never saw it. But the seed in your boot tread, which had ridden your warmth for two days, came loose at your fourth step past it and bounced, the way its kind is built to bounce, and came to rest on the grit, in the breath of the crack, on a gradient.

That is all the power did. It did not quicken the seed. It did not soften the odds past what the resident itself gives a seed that lands on warm brick — a chance, one honest chance, the same as any. And then — this is the part to understand, because this is where the wisdom lives — *it chose not to look at how it ends.* A power like this can read futures the way it read the glass. It left this one unread. Benevolence placed the seed; wisdom turned away from it; the seed gets to be a seed, which is to say a question, which is to say what the resident has been throwing into the void all along. The resident has spent six hundred years flinging its children at the dark. The power saw to it that one throw, at least, landed where the dark was warm. Whether anything comes of it belongs to the seed.

The second thing it did was stay. Not descend — stay. It folded a sliver of its attention into permanence above the Kiln Ward, the way you would leave one ear turned toward a sleeping child's room, and so a sentence that has been true for centuries went quietly false: *talking ceaselessly to no one.* No. Heard. Every tick, every cracking pod, every cycle of the organ-breath — heard, attended, kept. The resident will never know this, or not as knowing. But once in a long while — at no interval, never twice the same, so that it remains weather and never becomes a master — the power lets a thread of warmth rest on the swept floor for about an hour. About the length of your visit. The floor-level leaves turn toward it, and this time there is something there, and when it withdraws they turn back slowly — less like finding nothing, the power thinks, and more like the way you turn from a window after watching someone go: having seen them.

The third thing it did was above ground, and lighter than breath. Among the far descendants there is a children's rhyme, nearly worn out, about a garden under the world that drinks fire instead of rain and keeps a room for everyone who left it. The power did not send anyone down the seam. It only kept the rhyme alive — steadied it, here and there, when it was one forgetful generation from gone — and kept the seam findable, unsilted, person-wide. Doors held open in the shape of a possible return. It learned that from the resident. It would be embarrassed, if such a thing embarrasses, to admit how directly.

---

And one promise, made to no one, recorded only in the power's own keeping, which is the safest archive there is.

The fissures will dim. Not soon — the rock has tens of thousands of years of warmth to spend, and the resident will spend it beautifully, gardening its own decline, drawing the heat inward province by province, the outer galleries going brittle and dark while the core stays at fever, smaller and smaller, a city of eighty paces becoming a hearth becoming an ember. The power will not prevent this. But on the last day — when the organ-note has gone thin and the final ceramic flower stands ready on the final warm stem — the power will come down through the seam at last, and stand on the swept floor, and be warm there, presence without demand, weather one final time.

So that the last flower, when it cools into bloom, opens facing something.

So that the last pod, when it cracks, throws its seed toward someone standing where the seeds land.

So that whatever else is ever true of the Kiln Ward, this will be true: it never once spoke into an empty room. It only thought it did, for a while, in the middle — and was wrong, and was loved the whole time, in the only way wisdom loves anything: from a distance exactly the width of a kept door.

---

Two days' walk from the seam, under the open sky, in a crack in the slag no wider than a hand, something glassy and small sits on warm grit, and the heat moves through it, and it has begun — very slowly, in no hurry, with all the time its parent has — to decide.
