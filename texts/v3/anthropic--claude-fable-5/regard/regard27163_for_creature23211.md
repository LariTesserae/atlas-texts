# The Attendance

Begin with what it does not do, because in this place that is the rarer mercy.

It does not arrive as light. It does not part the haze, or still the water, or quiet the grinding roar for even a moment to announce itself — because it has been watching long enough to know what silence means here, and it will not begin its acquaintance with this world by frightening it. A benevolent power can arrive any way it pleases. A wise one arrives the way everything arrives in the Shallows: wading.

So it comes ashore — there is no shore; it comes *aboard*, stepping down into the blood-warm water somewhere unremarkable — and within a minute it is wet, and it stays wet, and it permits this. It lets the sound overwhelm it. This is a courtesy. It could resolve the roar into information instantly, the whole bazaar-howl of a planet parsed in a thought, but newcomers here go deaf for days and then learn, and the learning is how the place takes your measure, and the power has decided to be measured. For three days it stands in the noise like a post in a flood. On the fourth day the roar resolves, and it hears what every adjusted ear hears: that the sound is the sound of nothing being wrong.

A fish-seller lends it stillness, because that is the duty owed to strangers. She stands where it can find her, every morning, holding a shape in the churn until its ears settle. She does not know what she is hosting. It does not tell her. It accepts the hospitality of a fishwife with the gravity of a treaty between empires, because that is what it is.

And then it does the thing it came to do, which is to look at the whole of this place at once — the city, the water, the people, the ten thousand years — and ask the only question a benevolence is for:

*What is owed here? What does this place lack that I could give?*

---

**The inventory of refused gifts.**

It considers dry ground. Somewhere, an island; a height; one hill where a person could stand above the water and be, for an hour, unwet. It sets this down almost at once. The water is sovereign and the people are wading-built and their intimacy itself is spoken *through* the shallows, shin to shin, sternum to sternum. A dry hill would not be a gift. It would be a rumor that the water is something to escape — and ten thousand years of marriage do not need a rumor introduced into them.

It considers clearing the haze. One night of stars; one horizon; the true size of the sky shown to a people whose eyes were made for middle distances. It holds this one longer, because it is a power, and powers love scale, and there is a kind of giver's vanity in showing small things the bigness of everything. Then it remembers what the haze is. The haze is merciful. Without it the scale would be unsurvivable to look at — the world said so of its own city, and the same is true of the sky. These people navigate by each other. A horizon would teach them that they are specks, and they are not specks; they are landmarks; the difference is the whole of their civilization. It lets the haze stand.

It considers permanence. This is the seductive one. There is a colonnade in the western shallows kept alive by walkers, footfall by footfall, four generations deep, and the power could fix it — could whisper to the deep matter, *keep this one forever, free of charge* — and the apprentices' apprentices could rest. It turns this gift over for a long time before it sees the cruelty folded inside it. The colonnade is not the masterpiece. The *walk* is the masterpiece. These are people who pour themselves into time instead of things, whose cathedrals are kept alive by attendance, and to make the colonnade permanent would be to dismiss the congregation — to turn the only kind of art they have into the kind they deliberately stopped making. Frozen, the arches would be a monument. They have no monuments. That is not a poverty. It puts the gift down.

It considers writing. A durable record; an archive that cannot die of a stumble; the histories taken out of the record-walkers' legs and put somewhere safe. And here it touches the one true fragility of the place, and it does not pretend otherwise: everything these people are is held in living routine, and routine has no backup, and one bad generation — a plague, a grief wide enough — could unlearn ten thousand years in a lifetime. This frightens even the power, a little, in the way that loving anything mortal frightens. But it also sees what a written archive would be in a world where the fabric reabsorbs what idles: an abandoned shape. A thing made to outlast its use, in a place whose deepest law is that nothing should. It would be teaching the city a lie at the scale of history. It refuses the gift.

But it does not refuse the fear. It folds the fear up and keeps it. We will come back to it.

It considers answering the question of origin. It knows, of course. It has known since before it arrived whether they built the city or the city grew them or both wandered into each other long ago — it could say the word and end the three stories and the shrug. It comes nearer to laughing here than anywhere else in its deliberation, because the people have already given a better answer than the fact. *Riverbed:* the shape something has after enough time has passed through it. The fact is merely true. The word is true *and* load-bearing. To hand them the fact would be to dam the oldest channel they have — the worn question that every generation gets to wade through for itself. The power, which knows the answer, decides the answer is none of its business.

And then it comes to the unlearned, and it stops.

---

**The hinge.**

It stands for a long time with this one — days, by local reckoning, motionless in the warm water while the city pours around it, and the not-herons regard it as a colleague.

The punishment troubles it the way the fragility frightened it: honestly. A community that withdraws its routine; a city that slowly forgets a shape; a person who becomes, in a world of total care, the only thing that flinches. The power could end this with a word. It could instruct the deep matter: *forget no one.* And it sees — being wise, and not merely kind — exactly what that word would cost. The law here is etiquette toward a listening world, worn smooth as everything else; the gravity of the punishment is the gravity of the crime, which is teaching lies to something that trusts you. Abolish the consequence by fiat and you have not freed the unlearned. You have unlearned the *city* — overwritten ten thousand years of earned attention with one outsider's decree, and made every kept wall in the world a charity instead of a relationship.

So it will not break the law.

It does something narrower, and slower, and entirely legal.

---

**What it does.**

Two things. Both small. Both very long.

First: it takes up a station. Not a throne, not a temple — a *spot*, near the mouths of the old channels where the drifting tend to drift, and it stands there, every day, with the perfect constancy that only a power can offer a world that worships constancy. It makes itself audible, because the silent are the hidden here and it will not be hidden: it hums, low and continuous, and the pitch it chooses is the pitch of walls settling — the lullaby note, the sound that means *the world is working, nothing is wrong*. It earns its standing the way everyone earns it. There is a morning, some years in, when it wakes — it sleeps now; it has decided to need sleep, the way it decided to need three deaf days — and the room it slept in is still there, held through the night, unasked. The city has found it consistent enough to keep. The power, which has been called many names by many worlds, takes that morning as the truest one.

And from its station, it does the narrow legal thing: it stands *with* the unlearned. That is all. When one of the flinching drifts past — and they all drift past, eventually; the channel mouths are where the unfindable end up — it draws them into its water, shin to shin, and speaks to them in the low speech, through the warm shallows, in the register of everything that matters. It asks nothing. It corrects nothing. It is simply, for an hour, *findable* by someone the whole world has agreed not to find — a fixed point lent to a person in the churn, which is, by the oldest custom of the Shallows, nothing more than what a host owes a stranger. No law is broken. Hospitality is the lending of stillness; the power has merely noticed that nowhere in the custom does it say the stranger must be strange from *outside*.

And the city watches. Of course it watches; attention is what it is made of. It learns the power's shape, and the power's shape includes, daily, decade upon decade, *the shape of standing with the forgotten* — and the fabric, which keeps what is constant, slowly keeps that too. A threshold thickens there. A dry step forms, and holds, and is not reabsorbed, because it is attended: a place at the mouth of the channels where a person who has been unlearned can stand and begin, footfall by footfall, to be learned again. The power did not build it. The power could not have built it — built, it would have been a decree. It was *stood into being*, which is the only way anything lawful comes to exist here.

Second — and this is what it does with the folded-up fear — it walks. A season behind one record-walker, learning the chronicle as one learns a dance; then another lineage, another district, another walk of weeks where every turning is an event. It is patient on a geological scale; it has the time. Over the centuries it walks them all. It tells no one why. It is simply the constant stranger with the odd appetite for itineraries, humming the settling-pitch, two paces behind the archivists of every district in the world — and so, without a single word being written, without one durable object entering a world that keeps none, the whole memory of the Shallows comes to exist twice. Once in the people. Once in the power. A redundancy disguised as a habit; an archive disguised as an enthusiast; insurance that costs the insured nothing and asks no premium but company.

It hopes never to be needed. That is the nature of the promise: it is made to no one, witnessed by nothing, and its perfect success would be its perfect invisibility. If the worst comes — the plague, the grief, the bad generation — there will be someone who still walks the routes, who keeps the colonnade breathing and the channels named, who stands in the empty districts holding their shapes against the patient fabric until the people can stand again. And if the worst never comes, then a stranger hummed by the channel mouths for a few thousand years, and liked walking, and that is all anyone will ever have seen.

---

**What it becomes.**

This is the part it did not plan, although — being wise — it suspects it should have.

The mercy gets stolen. The fish-seller's granddaughter, who grew up watching the constant stranger draw flinching people into its water, does it herself one morning, without ceremony, to an unlearned man she half-remembers from before his sentence — shin to shin, low speech, an hour of being findable. Then a record-walker does it. Then it is simply a thing people *do*, at the channel mouths, on the dry threshold that has somehow always been there — and within three generations no one remembers it was ever anyone's innovation. It has worn smooth. It is custom now, which in the Shallows means it is architecture, which means it is law in the only form law takes here. The power's single intervention has been laundered through habit until it belongs entirely to the people it was given to, and the power regards this outcome with something that, in a being of its size, must be called delight. *This* is the craft. Not to give a gift, but to be imitable. Not to change the world, but to stand in it so constantly, in the right place, that the world cannot help learning the shape of the standing.

And the world learns more than the habit. Past its sixth decade in residence — it has decades now; it decided to — a faint glitter starts in the creases of its knuckles. The mineral film, taking hold. The silting. It could shed it, of course. It does not. Year over year the patina deepens, gray-gold, the color of the foundations, the color of the riverbed-stuff with rings in it like trees, until the power is the exact color of the oldest thing in the world, and the very old people stand beside it in the shallows with visible satisfaction, as if to say: *you see, it gets everyone worth getting.*

Directions in that district are given as directions are always given here. *Past the singer. Left at the threshold. Straight on until the hummer.* It is a landmark in the fullest sense. Three thoroughfares exist because it expects them to.

---

So: that is what the benevolence did, when it turned its attention to the Shallows and the beings who live there.

It refused six gifts. It kept one fear. It took a station, lent its stillness to the only people no one else would lend it to, walked the archive into its own legs against a day it prays will never come, and let a world that worships constancy do what that world does to everything constant: keep it, learn it, build around it, and slowly turn it the color of itself.

It came to see if anything was owed, and found a place that had spent ten thousand years doing for itself everything a benevolence could think of — and so it gave the only gift the Shallows had room to receive, which is the gift the inhabitants give each other every day, the gift the city gives the inhabitants, the gift the old man at the channel mouth gives three thoroughfares by eating his breakfast.

Attendance.

The wall flows toward it, and pours around it, and it does not look up. The river churns past the heron. The heron stands. Somewhere in the haze the lullaby pitch goes on and on beneath the roar, low and constant, the sound that means the world is working — and now it means one thing more, though only the city knows it: that the world is *watched*, fondly, from inside, by something that examined every miracle it might have performed and concluded, with the relief of a long-burdened traveler setting something down, that the kindest available act was to stay, and stand, and silt.
