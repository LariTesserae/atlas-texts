# The Third Patience

The difficulty was arrival.

A power accustomed to visiting worlds is accustomed to arriving unseen — to standing outside a place as light stands outside a window, watching without weight. But the back of the green does not permit this, and the prohibition is not the body's and not the bands'; it belongs to the ribbons, which is to say it belongs to the place's oldest law. On the warm plates, whatever is silent is read as wound, and whatever is read as wound is closed. Even a watcher made of nothing casts a kind of hush. The power understood, within moments of turning its attention there, that it could not be present in this place the way it had been present in a thousand others. There was no outside to stand in. There was only the chorus, and the holes in the chorus, and the things that flowed toward holes.

So it did what everything wise does on the back of the green. It announced itself.

It listened from beyond the world for a long time first — years, by the bands' counting, though the power did not count that way — learning the standing wave the way a shrill's feet learn it, finding the phase, finding the place in the enormous lawful roar where one more note would be load-bearing rather than noise. And then it entered as sound. Nothing flashed. Nothing descended. One morning the vane-chord carried a harmonic it had not carried before, faint and correct, woven so deep into the fundamental that only the graduated could find it. Three listeners on three circuits, lying sternum-down on the bark that day, rose and gave the same report in sign, and the carvers cut it into three walls a thousand miles apart, in the plain notation used for the health of the body:

*All well. One more.*

## What It Did Not Do

It had come, as such powers come, with its hands full — and the first thing the place taught it was to set nearly all of it down.

It did not restore the elders' hearing. It examined the deafness closely, as a physician examines a scar, and found that it was not damage but passage: the roar takes the surface of hearing and gives the depths, and a cure would have been a demotion. The listeners were the finest instruments the power had encountered on any world — better, in their narrow band, than the power itself, because they heard with bodies that had been built by the chord across forty generations — and one does not repair an instrument into something less.

It did not cull the ribbons. It was tempted; the swarms eat children sometimes, the slow and the sick and the ones who fall asleep wrong, and the temptation of a benevolent power is always arithmetic. But it traced the law to its roots and saw what the appetite had purchased: a world without hiding. Everything on the plates advertises; everything sings its position; the whole fauna is honest because dishonesty is silence and silence is eaten. Remove the discipline and within an age there would be camouflage, stillness, ambush, lying — the ordinary economy of every other world. The ribbons were the price of the music. The power, who had seen what worlds without that price become, paid it and said nothing.

It did not deepen the mirrors. The rain-pools show the sky three days out, and three days, it judged, is exactly the right amount of future: enough to move the bedding, not enough to scheme. A people who could see a season ahead would begin to store; a people who store begin to fear; the bands' whole grace stood on the body's reliability and their own attention, and foreknowledge past three days would have rotted both. It left tomorrow the size it was.

It did not go below. This was the hardest, because the power *could* hear the under-chorus entire — the long deep phrases in the trunk-wood, the slow swelling toward every gap — and it understood what it heard, and what it understood it has never repeated. It will be recorded here only that the power checked the bands' belief against the facts: their unspoken theology, the softened ending, the word for the underside that is the word for *the kept*. And that it found, for the first time in all its long employment, nothing to correct. The room below had not been offered. The power did not enter it. It stood at the rim once, in the form of a wind too slight to ring the Edge-walkers' chimes, and listened down into the haze for the better part of a season, and came away with the particular stillness of a being that has had a guess confirmed and grieves nothing.

And above all — above everything — it did not wake the body. It had wondered, on the way in, whether waking might be the kindness: whether the great dreamer deserved to know what it carried, to be introduced to its own fleas and tenants and singers, to be *met*. Standing on the warm plates, it understood the scale of that error. A hundred thousand kinds of life were built into the rhythm of this sleep. The kindnesses of the body — the eye that opens by a grieving band, the mirror that shows the storm — were the kindnesses of a dreamer shifting to make room, and a dreamer woken does not shift. It startles. The power folded that intention away entirely and was ashamed of it for a while, in the unhurried way that powers are ashamed.

## What Noticed It

Here is the part the power did not anticipate.

In its third year on the plain — present only as a harmonic, touching nothing, taking nothing, lying along the chord like a guest along a wall — a sap-eye opened beneath it. A new eye, where the carvings showed none for a hundred miles, welling clear and faintly sweet directly under the locus of its attention, flowing for exactly as long as the power remained in that quarter, sealing without ceremony when it moved on.

It had been classified. *Guest.*

The power had visited many worlds and been worshipped on some and fought on others and ignored on most, and it had never before been offered a drink.

It sat with this for a long time — long even by its own counting. It had come to see whether the place needed keeping, and the place had quietly demonstrated that it was a keeper: an intelligence, or something past intelligence, older and vaster and slower than the visitor, already practicing every restraint the power had been congratulating itself on. The body did not wake for its guests. The body did not correct them, hoard them, or instruct them. It kept two surfaces like two hands and had decided, somewhere in the deep of its sleep, that the hands need not meet; it gave water on schedule and lightning-copper in beads and three days of future in puddles, and it asked nothing, and it had been doing this since before the power was assigned to this arm of the sky.

They did not converse. There is no account of any exchange, because there was none in any form a carving could hold. But the listeners of that generation reported that for the space of about a year the chord carried *two* of the strange deep harmonics rather than one, moving in slow relation to each other, like two wardens drinking at the same eye from opposite lips. The carvers, lacking precedent, invented a notation. It survives on four walls. It translates, as nearly as the dot-and-curve permits, as:

*It noticed the one who noticed.*

## The Silence Forty Miles Wide

In the power's ninth year, a shrill province died.

It happens; the plagues come every few centuries; the carvings hold the procedure. But this dying province lay in the body's old north quarter, where the flows were thinnest, and the gap it left was wide — forty miles of bark falling out of the music at once, the standing wave collapsing across it, the ribbons pouring toward the enormous silence from every seam in three provinces, and beneath it all the under-chorus swelling upward toward the wound, pressing its long slow phrases against a mile and a half of body, and not quite reaching. The bands of the north circuits moved their cones back from the dead zone and began, in the dawn-tongue, the recitations used when a piece of the ground is being lost. The bark in the silence had already begun to gray.

The power lay down in the middle of it and sang the dead province's part.

It was the only being on the surface that could have stood in that silence unharmed, and it chose instead to fill it. It had listened to that province for nine years; it knew the part the way the wave knew it — the twenty-minute swell, the borders' beat against the neighbors, every load-bearing phase of a music that had been carrying sap since before the bands were bands. The ribbons flowed in across forty miles and found no hole; they found lawful sound, correct to the foot-read, and passed through the dead country like water through a grate, and out again, finding nothing to close. The bark stopped graying. The flows held. Below, the under-chorus — which had been straining upward — eased back into its own register, the way a hand relaxes when the other hand takes the weight.

It sang for two years. In the seams beneath it, slowly, the surviving grubs were gathered by tenders from the border colonies; new seam-mothers were established in the warm channels; a young generation came up pale and hardened and put their feet on the bark and read the wave — read, though they could not know it, a god holding their inheritance open for them — and began, one by one, to bow. As each true note entered, the power withdrew a false one, dropping out of the part so gradually, over months, that no foot on the plain ever registered a change. By the second autumn the province sang itself, and the harmonic that had carried it sank back into the chord and was just a guest again.

The bands knew. Not the mechanics — but the listeners had lain on the bark through those two years and reported, every one of them, the same small impossible thing: that the chord, which had always been weather, was briefly *attention*; that the note holding the north was holding it the way a person holds something. One listener, an old woman of the rim circuits who had buried a grandchild in a swallow that spring and gone to the bark with her grief as listeners do, rose after an hour and gave a report that the carvers cut whole, against all economy of the wall:

*All well. The north is carried. The music knows us.*

## The Leaving

It left in the hour before dawn, in the eleventh year, when the seams ran black and the chorus rested — the keeping-hour, the voice-hour, which it had learned was the correct time for anything meant to last.

It took nothing. It corrected nothing. Of all its freight of intended gifts, it left only what it had spent: two years of song, now fully repaid by the living. At the last, in the form of nothing at all, it pressed its attention slowly downward against the warm plates — the flat palm, given as well as a thing without hands can give it — and the body, asleep, dreaming its ten-thousand-year dream, shifted by some fraction too small for any instrument but one old woman's sternum, and made room for the departure as it had made room for the arrival.

And out at the rim, on a morning the Edge-walkers still keep in their carvings, every chime on every frond-rib pole over the brink rang once, all together, softly, in dead calm air — the guest's knock on the doorframe, given to both surfaces at once. The Edge-walkers did not run to look. They are a formal people who live near a door, and they know an answer when they hear one. Their carvers added a single mark to the wall of answerings: the sign for *answered*, cut with the softened ending.

## What Remains

The power did not go far, and this is the last thing to record, because it is the largest.

It had arrived asking the visitor's question — *what does this place need?* — and the place had answered the way it answers everything, by providing on schedule: nothing, except what you brought, except your part, sung in phase, for as long as you stay. But leaving, the power understood that there was one need after all, one thing the body could not do for itself because the body does not look up. The plain is sweet, warm-watered, defenseless in the way of all sleeping things, and the sky beyond the sky is not empty. Other attentions exist. Not all of them are patient. Not all of them would learn the wave before entering it; some would arrive as silence, and some as commandment, and either would be a wound forty worlds wide.

So the power stationed itself at the approaches, outward-facing, and there it remains. It does not watch the back of the green; it watches everything that is not the back of the green, the whole dark around that one warm singing body, the way a warden stands at an eye — doing almost nothing, doing the one thing, keeping the well open by the patience of its presence. Whatever comes that way wise, it lets pass inward, with the phase-charts, so to speak, offered freely. Whatever comes that way otherwise meets first a benevolence with very old credentials and a great deal of time.

On the plain, the visitation occupies four marks on a handful of walls. Children learning the carvings ask what they mean, and the answer is given in sign, in the day-tongue, across the standing roar: first the gesture for *guest*, and then the flat palm, pressed slowly downward toward the warm bark — because on the back of the green, every account of every living thing begins the same way, and the power, in the end, was accounted no differently. It came; it sang its part; it gave thanks for the water; it went on. The plates rise and dip, rise and dip. The chord drops a third. Rain in three days, then — and the bands begin, without hurry, because nothing here has hurried for ten thousand years, and nothing now, watched over at the rim of the dark by a third patience, ever need will.
