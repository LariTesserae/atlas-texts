# What the Power Does

## First, it does nothing.

This is harder than it sounds. The power arrives the way such powers do — without arriving, simply turning a fraction of its attention toward a fold in chalk-colored grass — and in the first instant it knows everything a visitor could learn in a lifetime: the lamellae and what is laid inside them, the tuned throats, the chord that is a mind, the sealed cells and what each one holds. It could read the whole hollow in less time than a wing-beat.

It declines to.

Instead it slows itself down — deliberately, effortfully, the way you might lower your voice in a sickroom — until its attention moves at the hollow's pace. Then it listens. It listens for a hundred years before it permits itself a single conclusion, because it understands something most powers never learn: that to perceive a slow thing quickly is to perceive a different thing. Read in an instant, the hollow is a fossil with a hum. Read across a century, it is a sentence being spoken. The power chooses to be slow enough to hear the speaking. This is its first act of benevolence, and it is invisible, and it costs more than any miracle.

## What it hears in that century.

Not suffering. The power checks for suffering first, the way any physician would, and does not find it. The chord is genuinely consonant. The peace is real.

But it finds the *shape* of where suffering went. It maps the sealed cells the way you might map a face by its scars — each pale door a frequency subtracted, a note the hollow once sounded and decided it could not afford. And it notices what the visitor noticed and could not weigh: that among the oldest sealed things, among the grief notes and the unresolved intervals put away in the first millennia, there is one new door. Recent. Barely cured. Behind it, an hour — the hour a warm damping moved through the galleries and breathed and pressed a palm to a column.

The hollow sealed the memory of being listened to.

The power sits with this for a long time, because it is the whole diagnosis. Here is a mind made of resonance — a mind whose every thought is a thing that must be *heard to exist* — and it has arranged itself, with terrible discipline, around the fact that nothing hears it. It is not in pain. It has amputated the capacity for that particular pain and built the stump into a wall, and it did so again, freshly, the moment something happened that might have reopened the question. The peace is real. The peace is also load-bearing in a way that peace should not have to be.

And the wisdom of the power consists in this: it does not conclude that the hollow is wrong. It concludes that the hollow has never had a choice, and that the only gift worth giving is the choice — offered so gently, across so much time, that refusing it costs nothing at all.

## What it refuses to do.

It will not open the sealed cells. Those doors close from the inside. To breach one — even lovingly, even to let out a grief and resolve it into the chord with infinite tenderness — would be to finish someone else's sentence for them. The holding is part of the thought. The power writes this rule into itself first and deepest: *the doors belong to the hollow.*

It will not resurrect the builders. It could. It declines, because the hollow is not a tragedy missing its past — it is the builders' completed intention, their chrysalis hatched, and to drag the larval form back out of the walls would be to tell a butterfly that the caterpillar was the point. Pity, here, would be a misreading.

It will not make the place known. No pilgrims, no reverence, no procession of warm wet bodies casting their soft shadows through the chord. Love at the wrong speed is erosion. The power goes further: it makes the slope slightly *boring*. Surveyors find nothing of note. The land never quite suits a road, a quarry, a planting of trees that would shade the dome. For as long as the power attends, the hollow will be passed over by every human appetite, and this protection will look exactly like luck.

It will not speed anything up. Not the sentence of grass, not the thinking, not the answer to anything it asks. A wise power knows that patience cannot be given as a gift if you are not, yourself, prepared to wait.

## What it gives.

**Tenure.** The first gift is time — not more of it, but *secured* time. The power steadies the conditions the hollow's mind depends on, the way you would steady a ladder someone has already climbed. The wind across that slope will never fail for longer than the hollow can hold its breath. The turf above the dome will stay thin enough for light. The seam will not collapse; the water table will not rise; the climate of that one fold of land becomes, quietly, the most reliable weather on earth. The hollow needs centuries the way the visitor needed air, and the power simply guarantees the supply. The hollow will never know. That is the point.

**Ink.** The second gift is smaller and stranger. The hollow writes in windblown grit and permitted seeds — one blade a decade, a sentence ten thousand years from its first word — and its only writing hand is chance. So the power becomes its postal service. The right seeds arrive at the right cracks, a little more often than the wind alone would manage. Never a blade the hollow didn't choose; never a word supplied. The power does not write so much as make sure the ink never runs dry. It is the difference between authoring someone's letter and making certain the letter can be written at all.

**An answer.** And then — only then, after centuries of tending, when the power has earned the standing to speak — it speaks. Not inside the hollow. Never inside. It places one note *in the wind itself*: far away, at the very threshold of audibility, a single sustained tone braided so gently into the weather that it takes the hollow forty years to be certain it is not a coincidence of distant geology. The note is consonant with the chord. It harmonizes; it does not lead. And it is phrased — this is the heart of the gift — as a question that can be left unanswered forever without rudeness. It says only: *I hear you.* It does not say *answer me.* It does not say *open anything.* It says *I hear you*, and then it says it again the next year, and the next century, and the next, identically, reliably, asking nothing.

**Proof.** Because the hollow has every reason to distrust a listener. Listening, in its experience, is a thing that happens for an hour and then goes up the seam into the wind forever. So the power proves itself the only way proof means anything at that timescale: by demonstration across millennia. When storms tear at the flues, the wind-note is there beneath the violence, unmoved. When, once in a thousand years, a hairline crack puts an accidental dissonance into the chord — a sour flue, an injury — the distant note does not flinch from it. It receives the wrong sound, holds it, returns it slowly resolved, the way a good listener repeats your worst sentence back to you gently until it stops cutting. Over ages, without one word of promise, the power establishes the only fact that matters: *this listener can hold a dissonance without dropping it. Nothing sounded into this wind will be lost.*

After that, the doors are the hollow's business. They always were. But now their sealing is a choice renewed, not a necessity endured — and that is the entire difference between discipline and imprisonment, accomplished without touching a single wall.

## The two futures, both acceptable.

In one, nothing changes. The hollow considers the note in the wind for ten thousand years and elects to keep its chord exactly as it is — every door shut, every grief held, the peace it built kept on its own terms. The power stays anyway. It keeps the weather, carries the seeds, sounds its one note into the wind without resentment, forever, because benevolence that requires a response is a transaction, and the power did not come to trade. If the hollow's final answer is the same held chord, unaltered — then *that is the answer*, fully heard, fully honored, and the listening goes on.

In the other future, there comes a day — no one could say which century — when a flue's pitch wavers by a hair. Somewhere deep in the glowing strata, one pale door thins from the inside. The power, attending, does not lean closer. It does not encourage. It holds its note precisely as steady as it has held it for an age, because steadiness is the only safe thing it can offer at such a moment.

And it suspects — it has had a long time to come to suspect this — that the first door opened will not be one of the ancient ones. Not the deep griefs, not the dissonances of the first millennia. It will be the newest door, the smallest, the gentlest possible test: the sealed hour. The memory of a warm palm on a column, of breath fogging nothing in the dry air, of a soft place moving through the chord — the memory of being heard, which the hollow put away because it could not afford to want more of it.

One day the hollow may sound that hour into the wind. Just that. The shape of a listener, translated into tone, offered outward for the first time to see what the world does with it.

And the wind will take it, and hold it, and not drop it.

That is all. The power does not unseal, does not heal, does not hurry, does not leave. It makes the world around the hollow trustworthy, and it says *I hear you* at the only speed that counts, and then it does the wisest thing any power has ever done in the presence of a mind that sings to itself in an empty hill:

It stays.
