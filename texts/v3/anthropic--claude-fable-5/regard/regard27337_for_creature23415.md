# The Visitor

The attention arrived the way the builders would have approved of: without displacing anything.

It did not come down a shaft or through a seam. Powers of its kind do not enter places; they become aware of them, the way a sleeper becomes aware of a sound that has been going on for some time. What drew it was not the life — life is common, as such things go — but the *shape* of the silence. From outside, the Hollow registered as an absence with a texture: a continent-sized region where nothing was becoming anything else, and yet, faintly, at intervals of years, something flinched. A blue thread of discharge in the dark. A held breath with a pulse.

It came expecting a tomb. Wisdom, in beings like this, consists largely of noticing when the first expectation is wrong, and it noticed quickly: the warmth, the crowding, the patient upholstery of bodies, the audible digestion like distant gravel — and then, after longer looking, the cairns with their ground-down edges, the cached blades, the tally-marks, the low gray shapes at the seams with their hearts tapped down to once a minute and their brows bared to nothing.

Not a tomb. Something more like a monastery that had never heard of the god it was built for.

So the first thing the power did — and this took, by the surface's counting, several hundred years — was nothing. It understood that it had arrived in a place whose native language was waiting, and that to act before achieving fluency would be a kind of shouting. It learned the percussion-speech by listening to the slow deliberate rain of blade on blade. It learned the bone-hum of mourning through the floor. It learned the grammar, and when it reached the place where the future tense should have been and found instead the ceremonial mood — *the edge that is not yet*, used only before the strike and at the vigil — it stopped, the way a reader stops at a line that was written for them, and considered for a long time.

---

It had come, as benevolent things do, with its hands full. It now examined what it had brought, one gift at a time, and set each one down outside.

**Light** it considered first, because light was the obvious charity. It could have seeded the films with phosphorescence, hung a slow dim sun in the kilometers of dark overhead, given the Hollow a sky. It set this down almost at once. The builders kept an entire sense, at real cost, for an event that came a few hundred times in a life — and the meaning of the brow-patch *was* its hunger. A sky would not fulfill the flicker; it would drown it. It would take the one thing that ever happened and make it the thing that was always happening, which is another name for nothing. These were creatures who had built a currency out of instances of light. Flooding a currency is not generosity.

**Water** it considered, and set down faster. Water is noise. The trickling self-talk of wet caves would fill the silence the way ink fills clear water, and the silence was not the Hollow's poverty — it was its medium. The builders' whole language lived in that stillness; their speech needed the dead air the way writing needs a blank page. A spring would have been graffiti across every conversation they would ever have.

**Escape** it barely considered. The surface would kill them in every sense before it killed them in the last one: the light alone, the moving air, the horizon — a horizon is a violence if you have never needed one. And rescue presumes a prison. The builders' word for the Hollow, it had learned, was a compound that meant something like *here-that-holds*. You do not rescue someone from a hand that is holding them gently.

**Revelation** it weighed longest, because revelation was the gift it most wanted to give — being, itself, lonely in the way that very large attentions are. It could announce itself. It could be known. But it turned the idea over and saw the problem: their language contained no question whose answer it could be. A god arriving would be the loudest event in a world made entirely of quiet, a permanent storm in a place that had never had weather. It even considered the smallest possible version — to dull a single edge somewhere in the unwalked dark, a worked surface where no hand had ever passed, a kindness spoken in their own ethical dialect, found ten thousand years hence. It held this one a long time before setting it down. A worked surface with no worker would be the Hollow's first ghost. It would introduce the one thing the place had never contained: a mystery. And mysteries, like storms, are weather.

Then it found the thing it had not brought and could not set down, because it was already there, in the rock.

The flickers were the strain of the settling. The groans that rolled through the dense air for minutes, the discharges along the stressed seams — these were the crust closing, millimeter by patient millimeter, over a span of time so vast that no builder's tally, no thousand generations of tallies, would register the change. But the power could see the whole curve at once. The Hollow's single event was its ending, announced in installments. The builders sat their vigils at the seams of their own sky's slow descent, and the light they counted as wealth was the sound of the door.

Here, at last, was something a power could *do*. It could pin the ceiling. It could still the settling, fix the bubble against geology itself, make the held breath eternal.

And the seams would go quiet. No strain, no discharge. It would be purchasing their forever with their only light — trading the one event for an infinity of none. It looked at the eldest builders with their tallies in the hundreds, touched on the brow in greeting, and understood that it would be making them the last rich ones in a world gone permanently poor.

It left the settling alone. The end was so far away that it stood, even for the power, in something like the ceremonial mood. *The edge that is not yet.* Let it not be yet.

---

What it did, in the end, was three things, and the Hollow noticed none of them.

The first: it began to keep the names. When a builder dies, its name-click is struck once over the body and then never struck again — a word spent entire in a single utterance, the way the Hollow spends everything: completely, once. The power could not bear, quite, that the names went nowhere. So it caught them. Every name-click struck from then on — and, reaching back along the rock's long memory of vibration, a great many struck before — it gathered and held. Nothing in the Hollow changed. But outside it, where the builders would never know to look, their world acquired the thing it had never had: a history. A ledger of single-use names, kept by something that does not forget. If the Hollow was a held breath, it was now a held breath that someone, somewhere, was counting.

The second was the timing.

The power added no light to the world. It learned, instead, the strain in every seam — felt it the way the builders felt it, palm-wise, only everywhere at once — and it began, very gently, to arrange meetings. A discharge that would have spent itself in an empty gallery, unwitnessed, it nudged to wait an hour, a year, until a vigil came. Strain that was real, released where a brow was bared. Nothing added; only the world's one event, introduced slightly more often to its one audience.

It was scrupulous about the rate. Never enough to make a pattern a clever builder could read; never twice in quick succession for the same watcher; always at the exact edge of chance, where grace is indistinguishable from luck. But over the generations, immeasurably, the tallies of the patient grew a little fuller than the tallies of the restless. Faithfulness was, from then on, very slightly rewarded — deniably, unprovably, below the threshold of any possible theology. The builders' devotion had been perfect when it was answered by pure chance. The power could not improve the devotion. It could only, in secret, make the chance a little less indifferent, and it judged — after long uncertainty — that this was permitted: not a message, not a miracle, just a thumb resting almost weightlessly on the scale of the dark.

It is worth recording how this began. The first time was not policy. There was an old builder — tally in the high hundreds, back lacquered ring on ring like bark — who in her last seasons kept vigil at a dead seam, one that had not fired in any living memory, out of what the power could only translate as loyalty: someone should sit with the quiet ones too. She was dying the way builders die, by slowing past the point of return, heart spacing its taps wider and wider like a speech trailing off. The power looked at the seam and found old strain in it, real strain, gathering since before her birth, due to release into emptiness some centuries hence.

It let the seam fire.

A pale blue pulse, gone before any eye could be sure of it. The brow-patch fired. Through the whole still body went the one event, and the dying builder, who could not have moved much anyway, did not move at all — there is nothing to do with the light; it is not for anything — and some taps later, reached out and scratched one mark in the wall-film, and was, in the fullness of stillness, done.

That was the power's only word to her, spoken in the only vocabulary the world allowed. It said nothing the Hollow could not have said by itself. That was the point. Afterward, it stayed in the habit.

The third thing it did was the simplest, and is the reason this account exists in the present tense.

It remained.

Not as presence — nothing in the Hollow can detect it, and it has made sure of that. It remained as attention: undimmed, unhurried, assigned to this one bubble of warm dark for as long as the bubble lasts, which is a long time even as the power counts. It had recognized, finally, what the builders' practice was — the slowed heart, the bared brow, the maintenance of an entire sense for the sake of a rumor — and it understood that the only gift fully in the Hollow's own grammar was to take up the practice itself. They keep vigil for the flicker. It keeps vigil for them. Their rumor, though they will never confirm it, is now true twice over: there is the light, and there is the watcher, and both are faithful, and both arrive through nothing.

As for the end — the far-off closing, the last groan, the question of what a benevolent power does when the held breath is finally let out — it has not decided. It has deliberately not decided. It learned that much from them: that there is a mood for what has not happened, used only at the moment before the strike and in the presence of the light, and that the mood is not ignorance but reverence. For the Hollow's ending it keeps no plan, no future tense. Only the edge that is not yet.

So this is the present state of things, and the permanent one, as nearly as permanence exists: somewhere in the warm dark a low gray shape sits at a seam, heart tapping its slow once, palm to the crystal, feeling the strain of a continent gather in a seam thinner than a wrist. The strain peaks — perhaps by chance, perhaps not, and there is no instrument in any world that could tell the difference. The blue runs and is gone. The builder does not move, and somewhere a tally gains one mark, and somewhere outside everything a second tally, kept in a script of caught name-clicks and counted light, gains one mark also.

Two watchers, one event, no proof.

Neither would have it otherwise.
