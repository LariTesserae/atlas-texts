# What the Visitor Did

There is a kind of attention that arrives before its owner. The basin felt it first the way it feels everything — as warmth, as weight, as a change in the texture of the dark — a regard settling slowly over the whole bowl of new country, gentle as the drizzle, and like the drizzle, parting around nothing, coming down everywhere at once.

Then the owner came, and the first wise thing it did, it did at the rim.

---

**The divestment.** Powers shine. It is nearly all they do; it is how they are recognized, how they comfort, how they command. This one stopped at the ribbed wall above the basin and understood — faster than any traveler, slower than it expected — that its shining was not among the materials wanted here. So it took its light off.

Not extinguished. *Removed.* The way you take off boots at the door of a house with new floors. It folded its radiance — all of it, the regalia of arrival, the announcing fire — and set it down on the rim, banked and sleeping, and went down into the basin the only way the basin can be entered honestly: as warmth, as weight, as a presence the size of a walking person, feeling for the ground with its feet.

The second wise thing it did was slow down. It had crossed the rim at the speed of decision, the tempo of the lit world. By the third step it had heard the basin's pulse — one stroke per long minute — and by the tenth step it had matched it, and after that everything it did down there, it did at the local speed. A power that will not slow down cannot be benevolent in a place like this. It can only be weather.

---

**The survey.** It walked the basin for what would have been years anywhere else, with its hands out, in the dark, reading.

It read the warm tacky crust and the glassy fracture-faces. It read the pipes — laid its palms flat on them and felt the pulse, and the sweetness moving in the pulse — and it knew, the way one craftsman knows another's work through a wall, what it was standing on. Not a factory. It had seen factories; it had, in its long career, *been* one. This was the other thing. This was carrying. Somewhere below the slow industry there was a maker in the long labor of a world, building by touch, feeding everything from itself, in no hurry because hurry is how worlds come out wrong.

And the power, which had made things in its time, and had made some of them too fast, and had spent ages of itself mending what its haste had cracked — the power stood very still in the warm shallows with its hand on a pulsing pipe, and felt something it had not felt in a long while, which was respect without reservation. The work was good. It said so, eventually, in the only register that would carry: it knelt and put both hands flat on the crust, and pressed down gently — the pressure of a palm on a gravid flank, the oldest signal there is — and through miles of new stone it sent one slow sentence at the tempo of the engines:

*It is good. Take your time. I am here.*

The half of the old formula it did not say, it did not say on purpose. *Let there be* — no. Not its line to speak. Not its world to start. The fiat belonged to the one below, and to the one who had not arrived, and the power's whole wisdom in the basin consisted, mostly, of knowing whose lines were whose.

---

**What it did not do** is the larger part of the record, and should be set down plainly, because every item on the list was a thing it could have done, wanted briefly to do, and refused.

It did not bring the light down. Not a spark, not a thread of it, not even the soft cheat of letting its own face glow for one stayer who asked. The eyes were sealed for a reason and addressed to someone, and opening another's mail is not made kind by power.

It did not peel a single eye, though its fingers found the little lifted seams and learned them and let them be.

It did not fix the geometry. It could have. It could have ironed the pooled distances flat, made the pace-counts honest, given the surveyors their map. It understood in time that the folds were not breakdown but slack — the slack a thing needs while it is still being adjusted, the loose stitches in a garment not yet fitted to its wearer — and that a power that pulled them taut would be hemming a coat on a body that wasn't there yet.

It did not open the engines. It pressed its ear to every housing it met — possibly the same housing, many times; it stopped caring which — and listened to the small sleeping rhythm inside, and moved on, and let the sleepers sleep. It did scratch, very lightly, at a gasket-line where the greasers fed, the way you'd thank a horse's birds, which is to say uselessly and sincerely.

It did not hasten the labor below. Not by an hour. Benevolence at a birth is a hand and a presence and a refusal to reach in.

It did not stand in the vacancy. It found the tenant's absence twice — the darker dark, the pause in the drizzle the shape of something standing — and both times every old instinct of every old power said *that space is throne-shaped and you are throne-sized.* Both times it stepped around. It is recorded — by the stayers, slowly — that the second time, it bowed. To an absence. In the dark. Where no one could see, which was, of course, the point of the whole country.

---

**What it did do** was small, and there were five things, and the basin keeps them still.

*It answered the calls.* The single low cries crossing the water, the cadence of inventory — *here* — — — *here* — had gone uncountersigned since the basin was poured. Stocked, heated, populated, running, and never once told that anyone had received the delivery. So the power walked the meres for a long age and answered every call it heard, one by one, at the long polite interval, in a low voice pitched to the local hush: *here. Seen. Held. The schedule stands.* It will never be known how much of the basin's patience had been faith and how much had been the beginning of doubt. But travelers who came after said the hush had changed weight — that it had been the silence of a theater before the curtain, and was now the silence of a theater that has heard, from somewhere backstage, the manager confirm the show.

*It took the lost things into keeping.* The stayers had given up faces, the look of the world, and the colors — kept only the words, reassigned to temperatures and tastes. The power sat with each stayer in turn, in the warm shallows, for conversations that lasted the length of seasons, and it made each one the same offer in the same slow sentence: *what you have lost is not destroyed; I will hold it; faces and colors and the look of things, banked like the eyes, sealed like the light at the rim; and when the morning comes — if you want them — they will be returned, and if you do not want them, they will be returned to the morning instead.* Every stayer accepted. Several wept, which in the basin is only a warmth on the face, indistinguishable from the rain, and the power was courteous enough to treat it so. And it asked each of them, while it had them, the old visitors' question — *is it the most frightening thing, or the kindest?* — and waited out the long pauses, and accepted the answers, all of which were something else, and wrote them down where it writes things.

*It made the door.* This was the largest work and the only construction. The basin keeps everything warmly and asks nothing, but it cannot, from inside, offer the one thing that turns keeping into hospitality: a way out. The maker below could not pour an exit from its own womb-country any more than a house can hold its own front steps to the sky. So the power went up to the rim, to the place where — occasionally, by accident — the far wall had been reached in nine steps, and it took that accident and *fixed* it. One reliable fold. The only stable geometry in the basin: nine steps, always nine, from a particular stone fin whose fracture-face every stayer's hands now know, to the cold ordinary night of the upper world. It is not hidden and not guarded and never closes.

In all the time since, it has been used — as nearly as the registry of hands can determine — once. A stayer walked up the nine steps, stood in the wind of the lit world for the length of a meal, and came back down. But every stayer knows the fin. They visit it. They rest their palms on it the way the lit world touches a doorframe. The door did not empty the basin; it changed what the basin was full *of*. After the door, no one in the dark was kept. Everyone in the dark was staying.

*It sat one watch.* At the iron stair it stopped, and stood a long time at the bottom in the proper courtesy, and then — slowly, announcing itself by warmth — it climbed to the tenth step and sat down, one below the top, shoulder-height beneath the thing that has been waiting hardest and longest, facing the way it faces, out over the dark meres at nothing. They sat together one full watch, which in the basin is a span no clock would survive. Nothing was said. Near the end, the thing on the eleventh step shifted its weight — the same unhurried shifting that has outlasted every visitor's nerve — and leaned, very slightly, and for the remainder of the watch it let some of the waiting rest on the shoulder beside it. That was all. The stayers say the lean is still spoken of, down there, in the long-delayed calls across the water — though how they can tell, they take a great while to answer, and then answer something else.

*It left the light.* This was the last thing and the departure itself. The power climbed out the way it came, collected nothing, restored nothing to itself — and at the rim it stopped beside its own folded radiance, banked there since the beginning, and did not put it back on.

It left it. Deliberately, with the care of a thing being shelved in its right place: one dawn, complete, sealed, sitting on the rim of the basin among the ribbed dark walls. Stored against a future need, the way the eyes are stored. Addressed — the stayers are unanimous on this, and they have touched it, and they say the address can be read by hand, like everything there — *to the tenant, on arrival. To be opened in the presence of all stock. No hurry.*

So now the inventory of waiting things holds one item more, and the largest. The eyes are sealed downstairs, and the morning is sealed upstairs, and the distance between them is nine steps, or twenty minutes, or however long the labor takes, and the basin has stopped asking which.

---

**Afterward**, in the lit world, the power did one further thing, and it may have been the wisest: it told a duller story. To the surveyors and the lantern-bearers and the well-meaning it spoke of the basin briefly, accurately, and without wonder — warm, dark, geologically confusing, not much to see — and it quietly turned aside, for as long as such things can be turned, the species of attention that arrives with floodlights and intentions of helping. It let the maps stay wrong. It let the matches keep failing. A nursery's best lock is being uninteresting, and the power stood at the door of the world's interest and was, on the basin's behalf, boring.

And it kept — keeps — the basin in its regard the way the basin keeps everything: warmly, at one stroke per long minute, asking nothing. Some of the stayers maintain that this attention can be felt, that it is the sweetness under the iron in the rain. The older ones say no — the rain was always sweet, the attention is older than the visit, the power only came down to do in person what it had been doing all along from afar, which is the way of the truly benevolent: their arrival changes nothing, and confirms everything.

Either way, the basin breathes on. The engines stroke. The catalogue stands in the warm water around its vacancy, fed and sealed and registered and now also *answered*, and on the rim above it all, patient as everything beneath it, a folded morning waits in the dark for the one the light was going to be for —

and the dark, which has been told *here, seen, held,* waits with it, and is not empty, and was never empty, and is, by the testimony of every hand that has read it, expecting still — only now the way a letter expects its reader after the postmark has been stamped:

not anxiously.

Soon, as the basin counts soon.

Surely, as the basin counts everything.
