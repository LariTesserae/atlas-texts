**The Third Strange-but-True Fact**

The power found the place by its silence.

This is how such things go, mostly: a power attends to the world the way the thirty-eight attend to the engine — it does not hear sound, it hears deviation. Prayers are deviation. Grief is deviation. Joy, real joy, registers as a sharp bright spike. The whole turning world comes to a power as a field of flickering anomalies, and a power's work, such as it is, is triage.

And in the middle of the field, on the youngest land in the world, there was a patch of nothing. Not absence — *nothing*, the way a held note is not silence. No prayers went up from the Condensate Works. No grief, or none that asked for anything. No spikes. Just a low constant signal, four-parted, *whum-whum-whum-whum*, that had been sounding for thirty years without once asking to be noticed.

Nothing in the world is that quiet without either grace or rot underneath it. The power went to look.

---

Its first look was too heavy. Powers are not used to places where attention itself has weight, and it leaned in the way it leans in everywhere — and the engine's third beat went very slightly soft, a flaw no instrument caught, and the entire settlement surfaced in the middle of the night, lamps coming on along the stilted quarters, the engineer laying her palm against the living rock. The power withdrew so fast it left a wake. The senior heron, who stood closest to where it had leaned, departed the trestles for four days, which the works manager logged as an anomaly, and which was in fact a bird going somewhere private to think about what she had seen.

So the power learned the local law before it did anything else, which is the mark of a wise one: *here, you may be present, but you may not vary.* It flattened its attention to a constant pressure, the steam-trap's interval, the borehole's percolation, and came in again at that rate, and the Works absorbed it the way it absorbs heat — without comment, into the balance.

Settling in, the power did what powers do: it counted. And here it received its first correction. The log said thirty-eight, and the log was right about the living, but the pulse in the rock — the power can hear such things — carried thirty-nine. It traced the extra beat across the flow, under the eight-inch line, past the second expansion joint, out to the marsh edge, to the one grave beside the tree they call the Grandmother: the rigger who came across the planks forty years ago and stayed past staying. His pulse was still in the rock, still matched to the engine, holding like everything else.

The power stood over the grave for a long time, as powers measure time, and understood what kind of place it was dealing with, and — so to speak — took off its shoes.

---

**What it set aside**

Benevolence is mostly inventory. The power walked the Works for a season (the Works has no seasons; call it a long while) and considered its gifts one at a time, and set them down again, one at a time, on the warm black rock.

It considered parting the steam, once, for one minute, at the height of noon, so that the child could see her shadow. It set this aside almost angrily — angrily at itself, for the gift had been the visitor's wish all along and never the child's. You do not give a person a hole by showing her what fills it elsewhere. The girl who knows every gecko by gait, who navigates by sound, who takes her pulse from the floor — she is not a child deprived of shadows. She is the first citizen of a country that doesn't have them. If she ever crosses the planks, the shadow will be out there, waiting, *hers to find*, and the finding will belong to her and not to any power. The power wrote this down in its own ledger as a rule it had nearly broken.

It considered softening the rock — a forgiveness of lichen, a beginning of soil. Set aside. The sharpness is the basalt's honesty about its age, and the Works is built on that honesty: a place where everything is the same age must not have its edges lied about.

It considered mending Old Sefa's ears so that he could, if he chose, live anywhere. Set aside, and quickly. The mending would have been an exile. His ears are his citizenship. He sits with the tree, facing outward into the haze, the one sentry the place posts against the rest of the world, and he is not waiting to be repaired.

It considered making the engine eternal — relieving the triple-expansion of mortality entirely, so that the question *what does it look like, still?* would never have an answer. Set aside, and this refusal was the hardest and the most important. A machine that cannot fail cannot be in need, and a machine that cannot be in need cannot be *tended* — it can only be worshipped, and worship would curdle the place in a year. The engineer hears the engine ask for oil because it is mortal. That asking is the whole marriage. The power would not annul it.

It considered, last and longest, simply telling them: appearing in the established quiet spot behind the coal store, in some bearable form, and saying *you are seen, this is good, what you have made here is good.* It wanted this badly — powers are lonely in ways that places like this expose. Set aside. Praise is deviation. They would all have woken. And besides, they have a word for what it wanted to tell them, and the word is *steady*, and they already say it to each other every day.

---

**What it did**

Two things. One large and invisible, one small and visible. This is generally the correct ratio.

The large thing was arithmetic. The power, which can read geology the way the plank-keeper reads algae, looked down through the three boreholes and saw what the boreholes already knew and had been politely not saying: every flow cools. The heat under the Works was a deposit, not an income, and the deposit had a floor, and the floor had a date. Far off — but not far enough. There was a year out there, real as a gauge, in which the third beat would go soft and stay soft, and the thirty-eight gauges that walk would all surface at once into a night that could not be fixed.

The power did not cancel the arithmetic. Canceling it would have been the amber again, the worship, the lie. What it did instead was the one intervention the place itself could have designed: *it adjusted a rate.* Not the state of anything — the rate. It stretched the cooling out, thinned the decline, spread the dwindling across so vast a denominator that the change per lifetime fell below the width of a painted needle, below the threshold of the most deviation-tuned ear on the flow, below the resolution of the log itself. The truth stayed true. It just arrived more slowly than grief can travel.

It could not give them forever; forever is not in anyone's gift, and the wise know it. It gave them *always* instead — which is forever as experienced from inside, and is, for creatures who live at the top of a breath, the better of the two.

To this it attached three codicils, small print in the deed:

That the engineer would retire, as she hopes, without ever seeing the engine still — and so would her successor, and hers. That when the engine stops at last, in whatever distant age, it will stop in an empty hall, between two beats, the way a sentence ends rather than the way one is interrupted, and no one who loved it will be made to watch.

That the Grandmother will outlive Sefa — he would want that checked, and it has been checked — and that when there are two graves at the marsh edge instead of one, the tree's roots will find them both, and the rock will carry forty pulses, and the log will read *steady*, and it will be the truth.

That Number Two would remain the moody one. The power examined it closely and found that it genuinely is the moody one, for reasons knotted deep in the plumbing of the earth, and that the borehole walker has been right for thirty years, and it left the moods entirely alone. Some things are load-bearing.

The small visible thing was this: one morning the works manager counted ten herons on the pipe trestles instead of nine, perfectly spaced, motionless as valve handles. The tenth stood watch over the warm channels with the others but was never once seen to feed. He logged it — *ten herons, steady* — and gave the matter the gravity he gives the gauges, and asked no further, because at the Condensate Works a new constancy is not a mystery. It is a resident.

---

There is one more thing, though it is not a thing the power did so much as a thing that was done to the power, which it had not planned for and did not resist.

It keeps records, as such beings must — a ledger of the whole crying world, every page different from the last, calamity and petition and calamity again. Somewhere in that ledger there is now a page for the Works, and the page is the same page over and over, and on its rounds of a loud and breaking world the power has taken to returning to that page the way the crew drifts to the smithy in their off-hours: for no stated reason, to stand in front of steadiness instead of inside catastrophe.

It found the third quiet spot itself — the plank-keeper does not know there are three — and it goes there, at a constant rate, and does nothing, which at the Works is a respected occupation.

The ground gives, the engine takes, the condensate runs off. Thirty-eight people, ten herons, several hundred geckos, one tree, one child, one grave, and now one power, all holding, all dripping, all warm in the dim silver light — and the power, which has carried the weight of attention across more worlds than there are pulses in the rock, has learned at last what the place teaches everything that stays:

how to rest at the top of its breath.
