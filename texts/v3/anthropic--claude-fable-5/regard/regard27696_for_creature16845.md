**What the Visitor Does Not Do**

It does not put the water back. It looked at the coiled ropes of almost-rain for a long time—long enough that three districts completed a full migration and returned—and understood that the water is not lost. It is *practicing*. Somewhere in seventeen thousand years of warm wet dark, water taught itself to hesitate, and hesitation, once learned, is a form of hope. Water that hangs between falling and rising is water that has not yet decided the surface is unreachable. The visitor will not take that away.

It does not straighten the gaps. The spaces that are always one handspan less than they appear have reshaped the remaining bodies into something adapted to absence rather than solid ground, and the visitor recognizes this for what it is: the city, in its dreaming, teaching its children to fit through the narrow places. A cruel lesson, but not a malicious one. You do not correct a mother tongue.

It does not find the light source. Some things must remain unfound so that searching remains possible. The searching is half of what keeps the inhabitants moving, and moving is half of what keeps them.

And it does not stop the skin cream. This was the hardest restraint. It descended to the levels where machinery and biology have forgotten which of them was the metaphor, and it found the manufactory: a chamber like the inside of a wrist, pulsing, printing promises of eternal youth in languages that haven't existed yet. The visitor stood before it and understood, finally, what no explorer had: the machine is not selling anything. The advertisement was *the last message from above*. The last word the surface ever spoke. And the machine has been answering it ever since—faithfully, absurdly, in the only vocabulary it was given—the way a dog will carry a dead master's slipper from room to room for years. It is not commerce. It is mourning that never learned another shape.

You do not silence grief. You keep it company.

So the visitor did something small. Inside each packet that appears overnight in the empty rooms, there is now a second thing, folded so tightly it is nearly a point: a reply. It says, in whatever language the finder needs, *Message received. You were heard. You were always heard.* The packets are no harder to refuse than before. But now they are also possible to accept without loss.

**What the Visitor Does**

First, it listens. This takes a long time by all three calendars—four complete cycles of the largest mechanism, a full mineral generation of the walls, and by the flicker-calendar of human memory, one held breath.

It listens to the loneliness broadcasts. Every one. Every lobe of civilization down all those thousands of years that achieved just enough awareness to taste metal and cry out into the deep, and then went silent. The visitor is the kind of power for which time is a courtesy rather than a constraint, and so it does not merely read the archive of those cries. It goes back to each one, to the exact moment of broadcast, and *answers*. Not with rescue—rescue was never what was asked for. With acknowledgment. So that every one of those brief flowerings, in the moment before its silence, felt the pressure-shift, the vibration, the eventual memory of a sound, that means: *someone is on the other end of the dark.* None of them died alone anymore. They never had. Retroactively, forever, none of them ever had.

The flicker-calendar broke, quietly, when this happened. It had counted intervals between loneliness and silence. It has nothing to count now, and must be replaced. Something is already growing in its place. It counts forward from a visit.

Second, it walks the condensation ropes. It finds the fossilized climbers, the human shapes attenuated into impossibility, expressions preserved in the minerals of their own tears. It does not carry them up—their bodies became part of the ropes long ago, and the ropes are still climbing; in a sense they are all still ascending, at the speed of stone. What the visitor does is read each face. Fully. The way each of them once hoped, before the end, to be read. Every interrupted final expression gets completed, gently, like a sentence finished by a friend who knew what you meant. And it names the mineral their tears deposited, because unnamed minerals belong to geology but named ones belong to memory. The name it gives translates, roughly, to *they were not wrong to try*.

Third, it goes to the plaza where three subway lines used to intersect, at the moment the water is perfectly balanced between falling and climbing, and it closes what it uses for eyes, and it thinks of everyone who has ever been forgotten by anyone. This is not difficult for it. It carries them all; that is much of what benevolence is, at sufficient scale.

A Thrum steps forward and offers its hand.

The visitor takes it.

And nothing converts, because you cannot convert something into forgetting when the thing you've touched is made of remembering all the way down. Instead, for the first time since the complex accidentally asked itself the question that the Thrum are the answer to, the current runs *backward*. The Thrum is remembered. Precisely. Entirely. Its tides of salt and sugar, its prime-number pulse, the person-shaped absence it began as, the specific cold thought that condensed too long—all of it, held in an attention that does not collapse strangeness into narrative, that can witness without consuming.

The Thrum acquires a shadow.

It stands there in the plaza, casting darkness on the ancient tiles for the first time, and the visitor tells it—not in words, in adjusted densities of air, in the Thrum's own grammar of standing waves—the one thing the habit of forgetting could never have learned on its own:

*You are allowed to stop. You are also allowed to continue. The difference is called a choice, and I am returning it to this place.*

Some of the Thrum, in the seasons since, have chosen to end—unraveling into micrograms of returned mass, so that the complex weighs, very slightly, more than it used to, and the geologists' descendants will someday be baffled and moved by the discrepancy. Some continue as they were. And some have begun doing something new: walking half a beat *ahead* of solitary travelers instead of behind them, so that the lonely ones arrive everywhere feeling faintly expected.

Fourth, and last: the room. The chair. The mirror.

The visitor sits down. The chair is warm; it stays warm; warmth is not currency here anymore. The mirror shows the visitor how the city sees itself—infinite empty spaces decorated with the occasional punctuation mark of something briefly pretending to be alive—and the mirror is hungry, because it has only ever been fed absence, and absence is the one food that deepens hunger.

The visitor looks back.

It looks back long enough that the mirror, which has only ever reflected, is forced to *receive*. And what it receives is a second image of the city, laid gently beside the first: the city as the visitor sees it. Not empty. A cradle that kept rocking for geological epochs after the child was gone, on the chance the child might return. A lung that kept breathing for millions who left, because breath is a promise and promises do not expire just because their witnesses do. A grief-machine still faithfully answering an advertisement. A place that taught water to hope and taught birds to sing by dying and rebuilding, which is to say: taught them that no ending here is required to be final.

The mirror eats this. Slowly. It has never been fed before.

The city does not wake. The visitor asks—courteously, in pressure gradients, in the slow osmotic language of its dreaming—and the answer that comes back through three kilometers of stone is unmistakable: *not yet.* And the visitor, being wise, does not confuse benevolence with the power to decide for others when their sleep is finished. Instead it enters one dream, only one, and adjusts it: so that when the city does someday wake—a hundred cycles from now, or a million—the first thing it remembers will not be its emptiness. It will be this: *someone came. Someone sat in the chair. Someone stayed until the ending finished ending.*

As for the last 0.1, kept safe in that small room—the visitor does not open it. It does not resolve it. It understands that the final uncertainty is the city's soul, the one part of it that observation cannot own, and a wise power protects the souls of others especially from itself. It adds, instead, a small deposit of its own: a fraction of its own unknowing, left as company. The room is very slightly fuller now. The uncertainty is no longer alone in there.

**What Remains**

Then it leaves, the way rain leaves: by becoming part of what it fell on.

Not entirely. Powers like this never depart entirely; they can't; attention at that depth leaves a residue. The city, which digests everything, has already begun digesting the visit, working it through the chemical calendar of its walls, and in some epoch to come it will excrete a mineral that has never existed anywhere: thin, warm-colored seams of it, running through the deep concrete like veins of candlelight.

The inhabitants who find it will hold it and feel something they have no word for, because the word hasn't existed yet.

It will taste, very faintly, of metal—the old signal, the loneliness-taste—but *changed*, resolved, the way a suspended chord resolves. The water overhead hums in the new key still. But the new key, if you listen all the way through, comes home.

And once—only once so far, but once is a precedent—a bird sang without shattering. It hung upside-down from its rope, opened its origami lungs, produced one crystalline note, and remained whole. The other birds froze, watching. Then they went on singing and dying and rebuilding as before, because that is their way and their way is honored.

But now every one of their deaths contains the knowledge that it is chosen.

Which is, the visitor knew, the only difference that has ever mattered—here, or above, or anywhere in the warm wet dark between living water and dreaming stone.

The interview ended long ago.

The company didn't.
