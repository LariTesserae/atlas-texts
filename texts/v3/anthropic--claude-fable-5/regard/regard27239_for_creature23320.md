**The Attention**

It arrives the way everything arrives here: by condensation.

There is no descent, no parting of the vapor, no light. The power simply becomes more present, gradually, the way water becomes present on warm stone — first nothing, then a faint film, then beads, then a thing with weight enough to run. By the time it could be said to have arrived, it has been there for some time, which is the city's own trick, and the power performs it as a courtesy. You announce yourself in the local idiom or not at all.

The city files it immediately. A warm presence, stationary, in the plaza by the dry ornamental basin. The vent-moths drift over from their column within the hour and begin to circle above it, riding its heat in slow rings, having registered it — in the only language they possess — as a small new chimney.

The power lets this stand. It is, it decides, the most accurate description anyone has offered of it in a very long while.

**What it considered, and declined**

A wise power's work is mostly subtraction, and the power spends its first season — which is to say, no time at all, and also several months of drip and sigh and the twice-daily far-off concussion of the relief valves — going through the inventory of mercies it will not perform.

*It will not bring the builders back.* This is the first temptation and the crudest. The absence is not a wound in the city; the absence is the city's present shape, the way the hollow is the shape of the bell. The builders left a verb conjugated in steam, still running in the present tense, and to refill the streets with nouns would be to end the sentence mid-word. Whatever happened to them — and the power, who could know, chooses with some effort not to look — they closed the door gently. It will not be reopened from the wrong side.

*It will not halt the stone.* Every century a little more travertine, a little less machine: this is not a disease. The city is performing the one act that distinguishes the living from the merely arranged — it is dying — and it is doing so with a slowness that amounts to a style, a deliberateness that amounts to a choice. To strip the mineral from the flywheels and pin the city forever at idle would not be preservation. It would be a sentence of insomnia, imposed on a creature that has decided sleep and death can be the same act if performed slowly enough. The power has seen things held immortal against their grain. It does not do that anymore.

*It will not measure the swimmer.* This one costs something, because the power is curious the way deep water is wet, and the question — how long, how old, how many, what — sits in the warm channels like a hook. But the swimmer has kept its anonymity completely and without effort for longer than most empires, and anonymity that perfect is not an accident of fog. It is a property, perhaps the animal's only one. To know it would be to take it. The power averts the part of itself that cannot help but see, and the swimmer remains, even to omniscience, a wake arriving after the body has gone. Call it a gift. Call it the only kind of gift you can give something that wants nothing: the gift of remaining a question.

*It will not leave an image.* The temptation here is subtle and very strong. A hundred thousand tons of intention and not one picture — the power could stand still in the plaza for a century and let the weeping stone make of it the city's first and only statue, a figure in white travertine, attention given a face at last. It comes close. But the builders' imagelessness was not poverty; nothing else about them was. It was grammar. They declined, every one of them, every day, for the whole tenancy, and a people's most sustained refusal is as much their monument as anything they raised. The power will not correct their grammar posthumously. The city will stay faceless, as built.

**What it did**

First, it learned to read.

The breathing — eight seconds here, eleven there, thirteen across the way, the citywide arrhythmia that fills the fogged streets after dark — was tuned by hands, and a tuning is a text. The power gave it a winter. It stood in the night-fog where the streets run wall to wall with slow white rivers, and it listened the way the builders' children must have listened, learning the city not as architecture but as a score, and somewhere in the deep of that season the periods resolved, the way a language resolves, all at once and forever.

It is not a message. The power had half expected instructions, a testament, a warning. It is none of these. The breathing, read whole, across every vent and stack and governed valve, is a *name* — the city pronouncing, in the only voice it was given, the single word the builders left it to say. The city has been saying its own name into the fog, on an eight-and-eleven-and-thirteen-second meter, for all the centuries of the emptiness, with no one in the world who could hear it as anything but weather.

Now one thing in the universe knows the city's name. The power does not write it down — there is nowhere here to write, and that too is grammar — and it does not repeat it. But there is a difference, older than any inventory can capture, between dying named and dying nameless, and the power, which is among other things a kind of registrar, enters the name where such names are entered, and the city, without knowing it, ceases to be anonymous to existence.

Second, it answered.

This took the form of residency. The power chose a derelict stack in the high district, a stone throat that had gone silent when its feed pipe scaled shut sometime in the last few centuries, and it took up breathing through it: seven seconds in, seven seconds out — a period no building in the city uses, a new prime added to the old music. Not a repair; an inhabitation. To anything that reads the city the way the city deserves to be read, there is now one voice in the arrhythmia that was not set by the builders, answering on its own meter, saying — in the syntax the city itself established — *heard.*

The moths found the new column within a day. They ride it in slow rings, rising and settling on a seven-second pulse, the power's breath made into bodies. It finds it cannot stop watching them.

Third, it took up the vacant offices. Small ones. The moth-fossils in the young travertine — the white winged lenses that look like grief — looked like grief because grief was present and unstaffed; the power supplied the griever, which is all such things ever ask. It walked the grazers' full circuit once, at their pace, which took most of a year, and at each golden boss along the high conduits — each rounded knuckle where one of the old maintenance-walkers stood until standing became the stone — it paused, because that is what punctuation is for. It laid its presence flat on the back of a young one, smooth and almost bright, and was filed as a warm thing that is not a pipe, and the grazer resumed, and the power counted this among the more honest receptions of its career.

It kept its feet back from the water. Out of courtesy. Not fear.

---

**The vigil**

Last, and largest, it made the only promise the situation admits:

It will stay.

Not constantly — the power has other weather to be — but on the city's own timescale, which is generous: a presence each century at the least, condensing in the plaza by the dry basin, breathing its seven seconds, taking the long roll of the place. It will be there as the travertine closes, district by district, over the canopy of pipes. It will be there when the last factory's exhalation shortens, falters, and seals. It will be there — this is the heart of the promise — at the end, when the city is one continuous pale formation with flywheels at its heart like fossils, because the one mercy it can give without theft is attendance. No creature should have to perform its single long act to an empty house. The city's dying will have a witness from this clause to the final period, and the final period will be heard as a period and not as mere silence resuming.

And then — afterward, on the far side of the last sealing — the power will do the one thing it has reserved for itself, the one act of carrying-away that is not plunder:

It will take the verb.

Not the city; the city belongs to its plateau and its stone and its long finished sentence. The *habit*. Eight seconds in, eight seconds out — the conjugation, the governed patience, the art of bleeding off exactly as much pressure as the ground provides, the whole tense the builders invented and left running. The power will breathe it out somewhere else entirely: into a hot spring on a world without names yet, into a tide, into the chest of some sleeping animal in some unbuilt country — somewhere a rhythm tuned by vanished hands can take root and idle and wait, the way this one waited, for whatever comes up through the cloud next, and stops, and listens, and stays warm, and stays put.

The city, of course, knows none of this. It counts the power among its vents and breathes around it, eight and eleven and thirteen and now seven, the fog filling the streets wall to wall, the name going out into the dark on its old meter.

But twice in that first winter — the power would swear to it, and the power's oaths are weather — the whole arrhythmia drew, for a single cycle, into phase: every stack in the city exhaling together, once, like a creature settling deeper into sleep because something it could not name had, at very long last, sat down beside it.
