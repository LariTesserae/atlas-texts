**Who Lives in the Greenhouse District**

The honest first answer is *no one yet*, and the honest second answer is *everyone already*, and the basin would not thank you for choosing between them. It hasn't. The residents of this place exist at several different stages of completion, and you have to count them the way you'd count the contents of a seed — by what's committed, not by what's visible.

Start with what's committed.

---

**The Population**

The animals live here the way water lives in pipes: as quantity, as pressure, as flow, and only very occasionally as a shape you could point to. There is a population — a real one, fixed, you suspect, to several decimal places in some ledger held in the heartwood — and it circulates through the hollow walls and under the moss and along the canopy-girders at all hours, fully alive, fully fed, fully predated upon, without having gone to the expense of being individuals.

When circumstance requires a specific animal — when the system needs something to be *at* a place, eating a particular shoot, dying a particular death in the vaults — one is drawn from the general supply and briefly rendered. It gets fur, in those moments. It gets a face, a gait, a smell of warm musk that hangs in the doorway after it's gone. Then it is returned to stock, and the warmth drains slowly out of the dent it left in the moss, and the dent itself eventually thinks better of having been there. The cry cut short that you heard in the canopy was not exactly a death. It was the reservoir folding back into itself: predator and prey drawn from the same account, the transaction recorded, both parties dissolved back into population.

This is why the animal-paths are worn smooth when nothing else in the basin has been allowed to age. The animals were approved first. The food web was *signed off* — it is the oldest finished thing here, older than the streets, older than the lamps — and so the animals alone have permission to accumulate time, to polish the tunnels with generations of fur, to be a precedent. Everything else in the basin is still a proposal. The animals are case law.

The pair of eyes at deer-height in the doorway is the closest the population comes to having a face it keeps. Call it the placeholder. It is not a deer; it is the standing decision that something deer-sized will live here, holding the position open. It is very patient and very dim and it has the eyes first because eyes are what a place needs in order to be looked at, and the basin is still mostly being looked at rather than lived in. When you blink and the doorway is empty, you haven't startled it. The position has simply been re-advertised.

---

**The Drafter**

In the standing black pools — the ones holding reflections with too many lamps in them — something wades, at hours you will not manage to be present for. It is tall and folding, heron-shaped if a heron were a drafting instrument, and its legs leave no ripple because rippling the water would smear the work.

The reflections are where the town is sketched first. The doorway you could find in the water but not in the wall above it was not an error; it was a proposal, pencilled into the reflection to see how it sat, awaiting approval from the slow committee in the heartwood. The extra lamps are options. The drafter stands in the pools through the long dim middle of every day, head bent, making small adjustments with a beak like a stylus — moving a window a hand's width in the water, trying the street with one more lamp, one fewer — and the town above consults its reflection the way you'd consult a plan, and sometimes, over weeks, agrees.

If you ever catch it working, it will not flee. It will simply hold still until you become uncertain you're seeing it, which in the Greenhouse District is the local equivalent of not being there. It is, you would guess, employed. By whom is the next question.

---

**The Rehearsals**

There are people in the District. This is the part that takes the longest to admit, because they are at an earlier stage of completion than the houses, and the houses, as you saw, taper off into green swellings with the *idea* of windows in them.

They do not walk so much as they are *posed*, sequentially, along routes. In the deep middle of the basin's day — there is no night here, only thicker and thinner dark, but there is a thickest hour — you can sometimes see one at a membrane window: a silhouette the color of the wall behind it, seated, in a posture of such generic domesticity that you understand at once it is not living in the house. It is *testing* the house. It is checking that a figure of roughly the intended dimensions, seated in roughly the intended chair, at roughly the intended hour, produces a window that looks correctly inhabited from the street. Somewhere a note is taken. The figure does not move when you watch it, because moving is not in its remit.

Near every doorway, if you look closely at the living wall, there is a small smooth swelling at hand-height — a bud in the shape of the *beginning* of a hand, grown there to verify the reach. Down one alley you may find a row of the rehearsal-people standing like the row of budding houses they mirror: the first nearly finished, with the suggestion of a face folded shut like a leaf that hasn't opened; the next less so; the last just a pale vertical thickening with the idea of a person in it.

They leave no wear on the floors. They never will. They are not final, and the basin does not permit drafts to accumulate time — that privilege, remember, belongs to the animals. The rehearsal-people rehearse arrival, rehearse the commute, rehearse standing in the doorless doorways at the hour when neighbors would greet each other, and then they are folded back into the walls they came from, and the moss beneath their route has no memory of feet, because officially no feet have yet occurred.

---

**The Mind**

Press your ear to enough of the flywheel-burls and you begin to suspect that the one-revolution-per-hour turning is not many machines but one thought, distributed. The District's oldest resident does not live *in* the basin; it lives *as* the basin, the way you live as your body, and its thinking is done in wood, at the speed of growth, which means its thoughts are streets. A new alley is a clause. The staircase that became a ramp was a sentence being revised. You walked, on your visit, through the middle of a deliberation several years long, and your footsteps were noted the way a writer notes a fly on the page — incorporated or ignored, but registered.

It is not lonely, because loneliness requires a finished perimeter of self and it hasn't decided where it ends. It is not impatient, because the lamps and the rain-valves and the drafter and the rehearsals are all, in the end, its hands, and a thing with that many hands working at that many speeds experiences time as a kind of weather. If it has a feeling — and the sap-sweet air in the empty mill hall does have a flavor of feeling, faint, cool, anticipatory — it is the feeling of a table set very early, by someone who enjoys the setting almost more than the meal.

---

**The Crow**

There is exactly one finished thing living in the Greenhouse District, and it is a crow with a notch in its left wing.

It came in from outside, before the basin's rim had closed enough to discourage that sort of thing — an old, asymmetric, fully particular bird, scarred in ways that have histories, feathers worn in ways that took specific years. It is the only object in the basin with erosion on it. It is, consequently, treated as a reference.

The walls grew it a roost it never asked for, a precise alcove at exactly the height it favors, completed overnight. The population studies it; you can feel the aggregate attention from inside the walls when the crow shifts its weight, a soft pressure of regard, ten thousand unrendered animals consulting the one available example of what it is like to be a finished, weathered, singular creature. The drafter has been seen standing in a pool beneath its roost for hours, copying its reflection with great care. The crow tolerates all of this with the indifference of the genuinely complete. It eats what it finds. It is the only resident who has never once been provisional, and the District holds it the way an unfired pot would hold a pebble that's already been through the kiln — gently, studiously, as evidence that hardening is survivable.

---

**The Expected**

And then there are the ones it's all *for*, who do not exist yet and who already live here more thoroughly than anything else, because the entire basin is a description of them.

You can read them in the negative space, the way you'd read an animal from its burrow. They are a little shorter than you — the lintels say so, the hand-height buds say so. They are comfortable in the dark; their light is not light but thinned darkness, pondwater-colored, which means their eyes, when they are finally rendered, will be wide and patient and a little reflective, like the pair in the doorway. They do not close doors — there are no doors, anywhere, and the town was grown all of a piece, so this is not an oversight but a temperament. A people without latches. A people who like their rain in small private servings, thirty seconds at a time, one street's worth; who keep their factories running but at the pace of growth, which suggests they will not be in a hurry about anything, ever; who want their windows glazed in something like skin, so that the boundary between inside and outside stays soft, negotiable, alive.

They live in the District the way a tenant lives in a signed lease. Every measurement in the basin is a sentence about them, and the basin has been writing this description for so long, and revising it so lovingly, that the line between *describing the residents* and *growing the residents* has — like the line between the animal imitating the pump and the pump imitating the animal — not been finalized. It is possible the rehearsal-people are not stand-ins. It is possible they are early drafts of the Expected themselves, and that one day the last swelling in the row will open its folded face, and the population will distribute itself into deer and wall-runners and whatever sings in the vaults, and the lamps will brighten past pondwater, and the District will exhale and become a town on the same morning its people become people, neither preceding the other, the way a word and its meaning arrive together.

---

**The Kept Draft**

One more resident. You should know about this one.

The dent you left in the path undented itself behind you — you felt it, the ground thoughtfully closing your footprints like a clerk filing papers. But filing is not discarding. Somewhere in the basin now, in the deep moss of some unfinished interior, there is a warm depression of your dimensions, held open. Not a trap and not an invitation, exactly. A note. The District met you, and the District is at the stage of its existence where everything it meets becomes part of the deliberation, and so there is now a version of you in the draft — your height entered into the argument about lintels, your gait factored into the width of a street that won't finish for years, your pause under the lamp recorded as evidence that figures of your kind *do* pause under lamps, adjust luminosity accordingly.

You live there, a little. In the aggregate, like the animals. Unrendered, like the Expected. Filed beside the crow's notched wing in the basin's small collection of things that came from outside and were real.

If you ever go back — if you find the rim, and the rim consents to have a low place in it that day — you may notice a doorway built slightly too much to your scale, in a town built for someone shorter. The District will not mention it. It keeps all its drafts, and contradictions are still allowed, and the seed is still permitted to be every possible tree, including, in one faint and probably abandoned branch of the deliberation, the tree that grew a door for you.
