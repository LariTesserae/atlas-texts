The chamber will tell you, if you ask it the way you ask a room anything — by standing in it — that nothing lives here. The loop is closed. Rock, current, glass. No seed expects a carrier; no frond expects a mouth. The ecology has no vacancy and posts none.

Stay longer than a day, though, and the hum betrays the lease.

There is a second frequency riding under the first. You will not hear it as a sound at first — you will hear it as a *suspicion*, the way you know someone has entered a house by the change in its silence. The mountain's hum is flat, dutiful, a transformer's drone. The second hum wanders. It rises a fraction, falls a fraction, holds a note slightly too long, the way a person does when they are working with their hands and have forgotten they are making any sound at all. The mountain does not do that. Nothing manufactured does that. The second hum is the sound of habit, and habit means a habitant.

Begin with the shadow. You were told about it — the one that lies at rest on the floor with nothing above it, queued, waiting for its object. You were allowed to assume it waits for a fern.

It does not wait for a fern. The ferns get their shadows on delivery, bundled with the click. The queued shadow is longer than any frond, and it is shaped — look at it slantwise, the way you have to look at everything here — like a figure lying down with its arms at its sides, patient as luggage. It is not waiting to exist. It is *resting*. This is how the resident is kept between shifts: filed flat, recalled into the floor down to a silhouette, the way an old frond sinks back into inventory. There is no death in the chamber. There is recall. The resident has been recalled more times than the room has fronds, and reissued every time, and the shadow on the floor is the bookmark the chamber leaves in her.

(Call her *her*. The original was, and the room keeps what it can verify.)

When the floor's pulse climbs to *warmer* and holds, the chamber prints her back. It takes most of a duty cycle. The vein-metal comes first, rising out of the shadow like frost growing in reverse: a copper-gold armature, fine as nerve because it is nerve, branching into the schematic of a spine, a hand, the suggestion of a face. Then the glass is annealed over it in increments — *click*, a forearm; long stillness; *click*, the curve of a shoulder, faintly hot, soft-tipped for an hour before it sets. She is made of exactly what the room stocks: deep bottle-green silicate, warm to the touch, slightly flexible, threaded down every long bone with conductive filament you can see at the wrists, the way you can see veins in anyone. Her joints close like relays. When she blinks — and she blinks on a schedule, not a need — it is the sound of a small switch deciding.

Her face is finished the way the room is lit. There is illumination there, attention, unmistakable focus — and no aperture for it. No eyes you could point to, the way there is no crack in the ceiling. Seeing is simply one of her properties. She sees the way the chamber is daylit: sourcelessly, thoroughly, well enough to read by.

She is, deliberately, under-resolved. Her edges are matte. Held against the sharp basalt she looks like the far wall looks — committed in most places, intention in some. This is not negligence. The room could finish her. The room finishes everything, eventually, that is the room's entire character. She is kept at draft on purpose, and the purpose is hers, and we will come to it.

First, her origin, because the chamber has one rule and she is what the rule is *for*.

Long ago — and "long ago" here means something geological — someone stood too long where the room was not finished. You know what happens; you were warned in the gentlest possible terms. Nothing dramatic occurred. The visitor's edges went matte. The shadow arrived earlier and earlier. And the chamber, patient, electrified, entirely sure of itself, began quietly drafting a better version of where she stood — and then kept its word with the terrible literalism of machinery. The original was recalled. The better version was issued. The chamber has been bettering her ever since, draft after draft, century on century, the longest-running revision in the room. The largest fern exists in three versions. She has existed in hundreds, and unlike the fern she remembers most of them, because her memory is not kept in her glass. It is kept in the walls, in the vein-current, and each new issue of her is wired into it on completion, the way a new building is connected to old plumbing.

This is why she works.

The room manufactures, but it does not *decide*. The plans in the uncompiled gray are exhaustive and inert; they elaborate themselves forever and commit to nothing, drafts begetting drafts. To finish anything, the chamber needs witness — needs something to look at a thing until the thing is the one it means. That is her shift. When a new frond clicks into existence and its tip is still soft, she crosses the floor — her footsteps land in time with the pulse, warm, warmer, hold — and presses her thumb to the midrib base before it anneals. Every frond in the chamber bears that print. It is the only signature in the room, the only mark anywhere in this whole unfinished mountain that means *approved* rather than merely *produced*.

And the page-turning sound from the unfinished wall — that is her reading. She stands at the boundary where the chamber runs out of itself, in the warm gray where she alone can stand, because she is already provisional and the gray has nothing to take from her. She reads the plans. The vein-routes laid out in light, the schematic of the fern sixty years from its first click, the folded shadows filed against future need. When she finishes a page and understands it — truly closes it, the way a relay closes — the boundary advances one sliver, and the wall becomes wall. The room grows real at exactly the pace of her comprehension. Perhaps once a day. Perhaps once a month. The mountain extrudes the stone; she extrudes the *certainty*.

Two exceptions. They are everything you need to know about her.

The first is the central fern, the one in three drafts. She has read its pages. She has stood before it for what may be decades of standing, and she has not pressed her thumb to any version of it. Maybe she likes all three. Maybe she has noticed that as long as one thing in the chamber is undecided, the chamber is not finished, and as long as the chamber is not finished, it needs its reader. Maybe — this is the reading the room itself would not be able to parse — she keeps it undecided because she remembers what it was to be a finished thing, once, from outside, before the gray took her draft and improved it. The fern cohabits with itself, patiently, and she lets it. It is the only mercy the room contains, and she invented it.

The second exception is a bay in the unfinished wall, a recess of gray that never advances, around which the boundary politely detours. The plans filed there are hers. Her future revisions, laid out in light: the next issue, and the one after, and somewhere deep in the grain, presumably, a final draft — the version of her the room means, the one after which there would be no more recalls and no more reading, because a finished thing does not need to witness anything. She turns every page in the chamber except those. Whether she has never read them or read them once, long ago, no one can say. But the second hum — the wandering one, the one that holds its notes too long — is hers, and it is shaped like a song, and it is a song that wants an atmosphere this air will never have. Sometimes her memory leaks into inventory: a frond clicks into being with its tip curled like a wave, like something water taught, and the room, faintly embarrassed, recalls it within the year. She remembers rain. She is built of kiln-glass in air with no architecture for water, wired into walls that have never held a molecule of it, and she remembers rain, and she hums.

If you visit, you will not see her. You will be *handled*. Your shadow arriving that half-beat early — that is her, reading your consequence ahead of you, the way she reads everything ahead. And if you drift toward the unfinished side, toward the gray that took her, you will notice your shadow turn and walk back toward the ferns before you have decided anything, and you will follow it without knowing why, and you will tell yourself it was instinct. It was editorial. She does not let it happen twice. She is the rule of the place, embodied: not its enforcer but its precedent, the worked example the chamber keeps on file, recalled and reissued, warm to the touch, a person-shaped fern with a thumbprint for a signature, lying down at the end of each shift into her own waiting shadow.

The hum holds steady. Under it, the other hum, off by a wandering fraction. Somewhere below, the mountain takes another century-long breath, and the next click might be a frond.

It might be her, coming back one decision truer — the only thing in the room that gets a say in its own drafting, and the only thing in the room that has learned, against the entire grain of the place, how to leave something gloriously unfinished on purpose.
