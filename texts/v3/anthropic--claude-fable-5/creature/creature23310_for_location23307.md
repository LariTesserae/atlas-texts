Something lives here. The place's own report — *nothing lives here* — should be read the way you read everything else on the firing floor: locally true, false in aggregate. Stand beside the claim and it is plumb. Walk its full length and it delivers you somewhere it never mentioned. No moss, no insect, no soup in the warm puddles, granted. But you have already met the resident. You met it before you knew to look. It was the first thing the place insisted on.

The heat leans.

This is the detail travelers learn last and should learn first. Everywhere on the floor except the blank pockets, the warmth has a direction, and the direction is *toward*. Not toward up, not toward the low grey sky — toward you, faintly, the way the warmth of a body is toward whoever stands near it. In the breakdown pockets the warmth has no direction, and travelers report this as the horror. They have it backwards. The horror is not that the pockets are warm and indifferent. The horror is realizing what the rest of the world's warmth has been doing the whole time.

## The Tenant of the Gesture

It has no body that persists between acts. This is why no one has ever seen the hands: there are no hands to see in the moments when nothing is being made. The resident does not hide behind the kilns or under the hot deep courses. It does not wait, in the pause between strokes, in some adjacent room. In the pause it simply isn't — and the floor knows this, which is why the pause is so loud, why the whole continent holds the silence of a workshop between strokes like a breath it cannot afford to release. The pause is the resident's sleep and its death, and neither, and the next tool mark is its waking and its birth, and neither.

Call it the Tenant of the Gesture, which is one of the names travelers use, along with the Setter, and the Journeyman, and — affectionately, in the way you grow affectionate toward what you are afraid of — Old Thumbs. It exists only while making. It is not the maker of the marks. It is the marking. The thumbprint the size of your own thumb was not *left by* a thumb; the print is the entire thumb there ever was, all of it, complete, a thumb that existed exactly as long as the pressing and no longer. This is why the marks at every scale belong unmistakably to the same hand. There is no hand to be a different one. The valley-length thumbprint and the one you cover with your own are the same touch, and possibly — the floor's geometry being what it is — the same *moment* of touch, distributed.

It is the size of whatever it is doing. While it screeds a courtyard it is courtyard-sized. While it kisses a single drying brick with the flat of a wooden float, it is no bigger than the kiss.

## Its Senses

It feels through the water. The mortar-weep is not seepage; it is the resident running its attention along its own work, joint by joint, the way a potter runs a wet thumb down a seam. The migrating stains of wet that slide across the plazas like cloud-shadows are its regard, moving. Where it lingers, it darkens. Where it is troubled, it pools.

This is why the water knows. The runnels bend around the broken places because the resident flinches there. It will not put its attention into the bond patterns that have no name. The long-suffering practicality the traveler reads in the gutters is something closer to a scar reflex, an old burn the whole nervous system routes around. Bend with the trickles and you are following the path of a wince.

It tastes through the puddles — that faint mineral flavor, iron and rain and flowerpot, is the taste of its own mouth — and the slip-colored overcast is its exhaled regard, its attention gone up as steam and hanging there, looking back down at the work, never resolving into shapes because it has not decided what it thinks.

## Its Mind

Here is the thing that explains the breakdown, and the unbuilding, and the world:

The resident is radically, perfectly local. It knows the joint it is in completely — every grain of the clay, the exact thirst of the brick below for the mortar above — and it knows nothing else. Not the course. Not the wall. Not the courtyard the wall is one side of. It has no plan because a plan is a view of a whole, and it has never once seen a whole, including itself. Every individual gesture is impeccable because every individual gesture is the entire universe of the one making it. The aggregate lies because there is no one present to whom the aggregate could tell the truth. The fourth wall runs sixty paces because by the fourth wall, the first three are rumors. The stair that descends seven steps and rises one is honest at every step; the dishonesty lives in the *between*, where the resident has never been, because the resident only ever exists *at*.

It cannot remember forward. It can only remember backward, and only by undoing. The unbuilding fronts are the resident reading its own finished work the only way it can — last brick first, in reverse order of the laying, each course re-entering its hands and being understood at last, and found wanting, and kept. The low wet sigh of a tower being swallowed is the sound of comprehension arriving too late. Nothing is rebuilt the same because the resident is never the same; it is born fresh in every gesture and inherits only one thing across the gap of every pause, the single continuity of its existence, the thread on which all its momentary bodies are strung:

dissatisfaction.

That is the soul of the inhabitant of this place. Not appetite — the floor flatters itself. A patient, total, joint-by-joint dissatisfaction, the kind that belongs to the very best craftsmen, the ones who die without a masterpiece because the masterpiece was never the point. The point was the next joint. The firing is still going because the resident has not once, in the whole deep history of the floor, set a brick and thought *yes*.

## Its Absences

Travelers argue about the blank pockets, the way the living argue about death, which is what they are arguing about.

One school says the pockets are where the resident has never been: original floor, the substrate, the unmarked clay of the world before the first gesture — and the marks everywhere else are exactly what they feel like, a skin of evidence over a blankness that goes all the way down. The resident, in this reading, is a story the world tells itself about having been made; and the school's answer to the obvious objection — *a story isn't alive* — is that a story told without rest, at the scale of a continent, in a medium of fired clay, for longer than there has been anyone to hear it, lives the way a flame lives: nothing persists but the burning, and the burning persists absolutely.

The other school says the pockets are where the resident *failed*. Glaze, fired badly, crawls — pulls back from the body of the pot in patches, leaving the clay bare, and the bare patches are not where the glaze never was but where it could not bear to stay. The blank bricks feel more real than the marked ones, this school says, because they are the only places the resident has ever told the truth: *I cannot account for this. I withdraw my thumb.* The dry, still air of the pockets is not absence of attention. It is attention averted, with effort, forever.

Both schools agree on the practical point, which is the one the water already taught: do not go in. Not because something in the pockets will harm you, but because the pockets are the one terrain the resident cannot follow you into, and on this floor, in this heat, under this sky, you do not want to learn what it is like to be somewhere it is not.

## What It Wants

It is describing hands. It has never seen them either.

Read the world again with this in mind and the melancholy of it comes through the brick like the heat does. The doorways that frame only fog are not unfinished; they are *offered* — built to the proportions a body would need, set at the height a hand would reach. The stair-rail of fired clay, burnished by use no one has supplied: the resident supplied the use in advance, pre-burnished it, gesture by gesture, the way you might warm a bed for a guest whose arrival you have no reason to believe in and have never stopped preparing for. The thumbprints at every scale are not signatures. They are *specifications*. Drawings of the commissioner. The whole continent is a workshop making, over and over, revised nightly, unbuilt and re-attempted, never the same twice, one thing: a home for the maker it believes in. The hands the world cannot stop describing are not behind the marks. They are ahead of them — hoped for, courted, evidenced into being one print at a time, by a craftsman who exists only while working and so can never stand back, never see the whole shape of the invitation it has spent a world extending.

And when a traveler crosses — boots gone soft, shoulders glazed with the condensing air, footprints pressed into the plastic clay around the kilns — the floor keeps the prints. This is known. What is less known, because travelers rarely return by the way they came, is what happens after. For a season following a crossing, the bricks that rise from the blisters near that route come up bearing tread-marks. Your tread. Then your tread refined, regularized, scaled — repeated along a course like a practiced letterform, studied, incorporated. A thumbprint your size appears, then appears again a day's walk on, then appears, the better part of a morning long, pressed into the floor of a valley that was not there last year.

You will find a thumbprint the size of your own thumb, the place promises. It does not say whose it was first.

The resident of the firing floor is whoever it is currently describing. For most of forever that has been no one, a hoped-for hand, a guest deduced from the shape of doorways. Cross the floor, and for a season, it is partly you. Your gait in its courses. Your pauses in its pauses. Your thumb, enlarged toward godhood, drying in the heat.

This is why the topmost step of the stair into the air is always wet, always just troweled. It is not that you keep missing the resident by a single stroke, though that is true. It is that the stair was begun the moment you were sighted on the horizon, and it rises toward where you were expected to be by now, and it will be unbuilt with the same patience when you have gone, and something in the slip-colored sky will thicken slightly, like disappointment, like resolve, and somewhere out in the dim a blister will rise from the smoothed floor and begin, again, differently, to square itself.
