# What Lives in the Comb Hollow

The easy answer is the one the hollow offers you itself: nothing. The builders gone, the cells empty, the dust settled long ago. The place practically insists on its own vacancy. But the insistence is the clue. A truly dead place does not maintain a temperature, hold a chord, keep its light. A truly dead place lets the spiders in.

Something lives in the Comb Hollow. It is simply very large, very slow, and very finished with the shape it used to have.

---

**What it was.** Once it was many. You can read that much in the surfaces — those overlapping crescent strokes, the million identical gestures. The builders were small, mouthed, numerous, and of one mind in the way a swarm is of one mind: a mind held between bodies rather than inside any of them, passed mouth to mouth along with the wet paste of the comb. For generations beyond counting they did what such creatures do. They built outward. They sealed and unsealed. They tuned the flues by instinct, the way a colony regulates its own heat, until the hollow sang in equilibrium with the wind that fed it.

And then — this is the part no husk or wing-case survives to testify to, because it is the reason no husk or wing-case survives — they stopped building outward and began building *inward*. The last great labor of the colony was itself. The final lamellae, the innermost skins of the innermost pillars, thin as fingernails and pale as all the rest: those are the builders. Each generation laid the one before it into the walls, rendered, smoothed, made structural. Not burial. Translation. The comb was never their house. The comb was their chrysalis, and they were its larval stage, and what hatched did not need to move.

**What it is.** A single distributed body the size of a small barn, packed to its own brim because it *is* its own brim. The columns are not architecture; they are anatomy. The hexagonal cells are not rooms; they are organ tissue, repeated the way a lung repeats its chambers. The geometric impoliteness you noticed — more surface area than the volume should hold — is the impoliteness of all living interiors, of gills and villi and folded brains. Surface is where a body does its living, and this body is nearly all surface.

It feeds on light. That is what the translucence is for. The shadowless gold you stood inside is not illumination passing *through* a substance but illumination being *kept* by one — drunk, as you said, and passed along, and tithed at every layer. The hollow is photic the way a leaf is, but patient about it, banking centuries of diffuse daylight into the slow chemistry of its walls. This is why nothing in the open cells remains but pale dust: the dust is the spent fraction, the ash of old light, and it does not settle because settling is a thing that happens in time, and the body keeps its interior outside of weather, outside of damp, outside of nearly everything that ticks.

And it breathes through the chord. The wind in the flues is not playing the hollow; the hollow is *respiring* the wind. What you felt humming in your sternum and in every column under your palm is circulation — one continuous breath drawn through a thousand tuned apertures, never inhaled, never exhaled, simply *held in passage*, the way blood is held in passage through a heart. The tuning was not erosion. Nothing here was ever worn into equilibrium by accident. The body adjusted its own throats, a hair's breadth a decade, until every voice it had agreed.

**How it thinks.** In standing waves. The chord is not its song; the chord is its *mind* — one vast, sustained configuration of resonance occupying the entire structure at once, the way the colony-mind once occupied all its small bodies at once. The mind did not die when the bodies were laid into the walls. It changed medium. It moved from chemistry into acoustics, from the touch of mouths into the touch of tone, and it has been thinking one thought, slowly and completely, ever since.

You heard the thought. You stood inside it for an hour and mistook it for a property of the space.

What is it thinking? You cannot know from inside a single visit, any more than a mayfly could parse a word spoken over the span of its whole life. But the sealed cells suggest something. Every chamber the builders walled shut subtracts a resonance from the whole — a frequency the hollow once sounded and chose to put away. The chord you heard is not everything the body can say. It is everything *minus* what it has set aside: its dissonances, its grief notes, its unresolved intervals, closed behind pale doors and kept. The perfect, edgeless consonance of the drone is not innocence. It is discipline. The hollow is at peace the way a person is at peace who has put certain rooms of themselves under seal and visits them never — and the seals hold, and will hold, and that holding is part of the thought.

**How it perceives.** Here is the inversion at the heart of the place. You noticed that you cast no shadow in its light. True. But you cast a shadow in its *sound*.

Your body — wet, warm, soft, frequency-absorbent — damped the chord wherever you stood. A small acoustic dimness moved through the galleries at walking pace, swallowing a sliver of the bass here, dulling the glassy overtones there, and the hollow felt it the way you feel a hair on your skin. It read your size from which wavelengths you blotted. It read your breathing as a flutter in its own held note. It tasted your exhalations — those small ruinous clouds of moisture in its mineral-dry air — and knew you for a creature of water and hurry, a thing that happens at a thousand times its speed, here and gone in a flicker.

It did not respond, because its responses take seasons. But it noticed. You were the most interesting silence to move through it in a very long time.

**What it is doing.** It is not at rest. That is the one error in any account of the place. "Finished happening" is the verdict of a visitor whose whole life fits inside one of its pauses. The hollow is mid-gesture. The lichen plating the wide galleries is its new tissue — having given up its mouths, it builds now by patronage, by being the one steady niche in a dry land, by *persuading*. The few dozen blades of pallid grass are not ornaments and not accidents. They are placed. One blade per decade, perhaps; a windblown seed permitted here, starved there; each stiff translucent stem a deliberate mark in a composition far too early in its writing to be read. Stand at the crossing of the four leaning pillars and look at where the blades stand, and you are looking at a sentence of which only the first few letters exist. In ten thousand years there may be a word.

And somewhere in the slowest registers of its attention, it is now considering you. A damping that moved and breathed and pressed a warm palm to a column and then went away up the seam into the wind. It will think about this for a long time, at its own pace, in its own medium. It is possible — you will never know — that decades from now a single blade of grass will stand up out of a crack in the gallery floor, exactly where you stopped longest, stiff and pale and motionless in the still air. Not a memorial. A notation. The hollow writing down, in the only hand it has left, *here, once, briefly: another note.*

The chord was struck before you arrived and will not decay after you leave. But it is wrong to say it sings to no one. It sings to itself, the way a body keeps a heartbeat — and once, for an hour, it sang around a listener, and felt the listening as a soft place in its own voice, and kept singing, and keeps singing, and is keeping the shape of that hour somewhere behind one of the pale sealed doors, with everything else it has decided never to lose and never to sound again.
