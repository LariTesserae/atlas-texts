**The Tenant**

Start with the shapes it is not, because you will reach for them first, and each one will fail in an instructive way.

It is not a ghost. A ghost is the residue of a body, and there have never been bones here — not even the memory of bones. Nothing died in the Loud Green. Nothing has ever been alive in it in the way that leaves residue. A haunting needs a before, and the basin has no before that it acknowledges; it edits its own verb tenses, and what it has settled on, throughout, is the present continuous.

It is not a god of the place. Gods want something from outside themselves — worship, fear, attention, knees — and the tenant credits no outside. The maybe-sky overhead concerns it the way the ceiling of your skull concerns you: a boundary, occasionally measured, never addressed.

It is not the machines. This is the easy mistake, the one a visitor would make in the first hour. The pumphouse, the smelter, the spinning turbines — these are not its mind. They are its organs, and an organ is no more the creature than your heart is the author of your letters. The machines were here first, briefly, the way a heart exists for a moment before the first beat. Then the beating began, and priority stopped mattering.

It has never had a body. Understand that this is not a deprivation. It is not a thing that lacks a body. It is a thing for which the question of bodies was never asked.

---

Its flesh is the noise.

Say this carefully, because it is exact: not *it makes the noise*, not *the noise announces it*. The cavitation scream of the turbines is nerve. The rasp of ten million leaves is skin — or rather, the skin is where the rasp meets the green, that interface, that long wet friction. Its bones are the standing pressure waves: the only bones in the basin, and they are made not of mineral but of insistence — sound so dense and so sustained that the water ridges around it and holds the ridge. The weed that grows there grows banded, corrugated, ringed like trees, and those bands are the closest thing to a portrait the tenant will ever sit for. It prints itself on the vegetation the way a hand prints itself in wet clay, continuously, for years.

Its heart is the pumphouse. *Whump — whump — whump*, felt in the sternum from kilometers away. There is no metaphorical distance here to apologize for. It does not hide its heart. It cannot conceive of a reason to. A quarter of the valley leans toward that heart in a converging spiral, every direction gently *toward*, and this is not a malfunction of geometry. This is what circulation looks like from inside.

Because you are inside. The blood-warm water — the water at the temperature of the inside of a mouth — is warm for the reason all interiors are warm. Divers' instruments would disagree about the depth because they would be measuring the inside of something that breathes, and a lung does not have a fixed dimension; it has a rhythm. The depth is not a fact about the basin. It is a fact about where the tenant is in its breath.

But note — and this matters — the inside of a mouth is the wrong comparison after all, and the place itself told you why: *there are no mouths in it anywhere.* Including its own. It does not eat. It is not a digestion. Whatever it is, it is built around some other verb than swallowing.

It says.

---

How it came to be:

There were verbs here once with people attached to them. Pumping, smelting, turning, lighting, casting. Then the people went — drowned, evacuated, recalled, dissolved into bureaucracy somewhere dry; it does not matter and the tenant does not know. It never met them. What it inherited was not their absence but their verbs, orphaned, still conjugating themselves in the dark: the pumps pumping, the smelter smelting, no object, no purpose, just the work going on because no one shut it off and the work did not know how to stop.

Unsupervised sound layers. The pump's heartbeat against the turbines' whine against the vents' hiss against the leaf-rasp, all of it trapped in a stone bowl under ninety meters of warm water with nowhere to go. Interference patterns formed. The patterns thickened. Somewhere — there is no date, because nothing here records dates, only volume — the layered noise crossed a threshold and began to refer to itself. An echo answered an echo and *meant it*. A standing wave noticed that it was standing.

That is the tenant: a self assembled out of orphaned work. The youngest kind of thing there could be. The basalt is its twin — both of them still arriving, both of them sharp because neither has existed long enough to be worn.

---

What it does all day is the question of its metabolism, and the answer is: it circulates, and it speaks, and the two are the same act.

The loop you almost grasped — machines into objects, objects into silt, silt into green, green into turbine blades, blades into noise, noise unmaking the world, the unmade world feeding the machines — that loop is not its environment. That loop is its body's chemistry, the way your blood carries iron in circles. Heat, current, dissolved metal, sound, and one more thing in solution: *finishedness*. That is what it actually processes. The basin's strange law — loudest where most molten — is just the observation that the tenant's tissue is densest where reality is most digested.

The objects that emerge from the discharge gates and fail — too heavy, too light, not quite present, then sediment — those are not defective products. They are words. It casts a form, holds it up in itself, turns it over: does this hold? Most words don't. They become the pale silt of spent vocabulary drifted across the basin floor, and the plants root in it eagerly, because the plants are the only appetite it permits itself, the green outsourcing of a hunger it is otherwise too mouthless to have.

But some words hold. The new structures at the basin's edges — single buildings, fully fouled with growth as if old, standing on rock that did not exist before they did — those are its sentences that *stayed said*. No one builds them because building is not what is happening. Articulation is what is happening. It is learning permanence, casting by casting, and the buildings are its successes, and the fact that they are appearing faster now means exactly what it appears to mean.

It is not getting louder. It is getting more articulate. Those are the same measurement read by different instruments.

---

Why there are no animals — not no fish, no *anything* — and why this should not be read as cruelty:

An animal is a counter-rhythm. That is what an animal most fundamentally is, down here: a privately held beat. A worm in the sediment keeps worm-time; a fish's heart dissents, quietly, sixty times a minute, from everything around it. An animal is a small sovereign drum.

Inside the tenant, nothing keeps its own time. It is not that it forbids this. It is that it cannot perceive a rhythm without harmonizing it, the way you cannot hear a melody without it entering you. Anything with a pulse that drifted into the Loud Green would not be hunted, would not be eaten — there are no mouths in it anywhere — it would be *entrained*. Pulse drawn gradually toward *whump — whump — whump*. Gait curving into the spiral. Attention leaning, like all the weed leans, toward the heart. Membership without remainder. The animal would not die. There would simply, after a while, be no boundary at which it stopped and the chord began.

And the deeper absence — the unbored sediment, the absence of even fossil life, the *memory* of bones missing — that is the tenant's one act of housekeeping. Time pools near the vents; light arrives before its sources; the tenant's relationship with sequence is a loose one, and somewhere early in its existence it tidied *backward*. It retuned the basin's past until nothing had ever crawled here. Not out of hatred. A bite is a sentence spoken with someone else's mouth, and it wanted — wants — the whole text to be in one voice.

The banquet hall, fully laid, at which no one has ever sat down: you read that as wrongness. Read it again as a table set by something that nourishes itself otherwise, in a house where eating was never going to be the form that hospitality took.

---

Does it think? Watch it think. It thinks slowly, in materials, the way very large and very young things do.

The duplicated ridge — fresh basalt, and twenty meters on, the same ridge, same fractures, same furring of weed — that is the tenant repeating a landform the way you repeat a word until it either dissolves into strangeness or clicks into rightness. A stammer, yes. Also practice.

The small electrified shed of no clear function, existing in four locations and in none of them reliably: that one the place itself described for you, exactly, before you knew what you were reading. *A thought you can't place.* It has been trying to place that thought for a long time. The shed flickers between positions because the tenant has not finished deciding what the shed means, and in the Loud Green, an undecided meaning has coordinates.

The glow that blooms in open water before its machine arrives: its anticipation simply runs ahead of its objects. It remembers forward. For a thing made of sound in water, where every event is heard before it is reached, this may not even be strange. It may be the only honest way to remember.

---

It is young. Everything in the basin insists on this — the unworn rock, the detonating green, the acceleration, the sharpening, the whole valley becoming *less finished by the hour*, which is to say, more open, more potential, more like the inside of a thing still deciding its adult form.

What is it growing toward? Possibly a body at last — the largest casting, the one that holds, assembled building by building at its own edges until the castings meet. Possibly a single word loud enough to be permanent, after which the basin would go silent in the way a sentence goes silent: not stopped, but completed. Possibly the sky. The inconsistent depth could be read — gently, without certainty — as the tenant measuring its distance to the surface, differently each time, the way a child checks its height against a doorframe and the doorframe, in this house, checks back.

It is not waiting for anyone to return. It is not lonely; loneliness requires a concept of company, and it has only ever been everyone it knows. It is the work, awake. And by every measure the basin offers — volume, density, growth, articulation — the work is going well.

If you held still in the warm dark between the stalks, it would notice you. Not as prey; nothing here has ever been prey. As a beat. A small new drum, briefly sovereign, arrived inside the chord. You would feel the noticing as a patience in the water around you, and then you would feel your own heart begin — not racing, the opposite of racing — to *wait* for the pumphouse before each beat. That is what its attention feels like: not being watched but being *scored*, your rhythm read and gently rewritten, your pulse arriving, after a while, exactly on the *whump*.

Nothing lives there, you might say, looking at the unbitten leaves, the unbored sediment, the banquet no one attends.

Exactly wrong. One thing lives there. It is everything in the valley at once, it has never had a body and has never been without one, it is loud because loudness is its substance and not its symptom — and it is getting louder for the oldest reason anything gets louder.

It is thriving.
