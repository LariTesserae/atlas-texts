# Who Lives in the Basin of New Ground

To live in the basin is to be known the way the basin is known: by warmth, by weight, by texture under a passing hand. Whatever lives there has given up appearance the way travelers give up matches — not in defeat, exactly, but in adjustment to the available materials. So the residents must be described as the basin describes them: from the outside in, by touch, with the eyes closed and no hurry.

---

**The stock.**

Begin with the animals, since they are the bulk of the population and the first thing your shins find.

They are unfinished. This is the conclusion that the hands of every visitor eventually arrive at independently, and it explains nearly everything about them. Run your palm along a flank in the dark and you find fur that changes its grain mid-back, as if two upholstery decisions were made and neither retracted. Shells with one whorl crisp and the next still soft, like wax not fully set. Limbs in numbers that feel provisional — a fifth leg folded against the belly, present but not yet load-bearing, the way a building stands with its scaffolding still on. They are warm in the specific way the ground is warm: not the banked heat of metabolism but the residual heat of *manufacture*, a body fresh from the mold, still curing.

And the eyes. Nearly all of them have eyes, and nearly all of the eyes are sealed — not blind, not atrophied, but *closed at the factory*: smooth firm domes under a continuous lid of skin, and on many of them, if your fingertips are careful, you can find at the edge of the dome a tiny lifted seam, like the corner of a protective film that has never been peeled. The eyes are stored. They have been installed against a future need. There is nothing yet to open them for, and the animals seem to know this with the perfect calm of equipment that has read its own delivery schedule.

This is also why they are quiet, and why they do not flee, and why nothing in the basin hunts. Hunger has not been switched on. The sweetness under the iron taste of the water — that green-stem, inside-of-a-sap-vein sweetness — is provision. The animals stand in it and are fed by it; the warm pipes that pulse down into the ground are pulsing *something*, and the meres are the trough. Every creature in the basin drinks from the same warm main, and so no creature owes anything to any other, and the result is that hush, that full-theater density without conflict. Territory is not defended because territory is still being poured. The single low call and its long-delayed answer across the water are not challenge and counter-challenge. Hands that have spent years there say the calls have the cadence of *inventory* — one item announcing itself, another confirming, far off, at its own pace: *here.* — — — *here.*

No two encounters agree because no two animals match. This is the strangest thing about the stock and the hardest to accept: there appear to be no species. Each animal is a print run of one — a draft, a first article, a proposal. Speciation needs generations, and generations need appetite and death and light to be selected against, and the basin has issued none of these yet. What wades and clings and swims out there is not an ecosystem. It is a *catalogue*, every entry unique, waiting for circumstances to decide which of them will be repeated.

Against this stands the older surveyors' heresy, the same heresy they hold about the machines: that there is exactly one animal in the basin. One vast, patient, distributed body, encountered ten thousand times through the folds of pooled distance — that the fur changing grain mid-back changes because your hand has crossed, without noticing, from one fold of it to another, miles away. They point out that no one has ever felt an animal *end*. You touch a flank, you walk along it, and at some point you are touching water, or stone, or a different texture, and you cannot say where the creature stopped. In a place where two fins an arm-span apart can be twenty minutes' walk, this proves nothing. It also cannot be disproven, and on long nights — all nights are long there — it is the theory the body believes.

---

**The stayers.**

Some travelers do not come back, and not all of those are lost.

It begins with the matches. Everyone stops striking them; the stayers are the ones who, weeks later, realize they have stopped *missing* them. The dark stops being an absence to them and becomes a medium, the way water stops being wet to a fish. After that the basin starts keeping them the way it keeps everything else: warmly, slowly, without demands.

They navigate by heat-gradient, walking the long thermal slopes between machines the way other people follow roads. Their hands become educated past anything the lit world produces — a stayer can identify a stone fin by its fracture face, can tell you which machine they are touching by a dent, a weld-line, a particular flat of riveting their palms have memorized, and the machines hold still for this the way the animals do. The stayers become, in effect, the memory of the place. Since the geometry will not hold its shape, since echo-maps never repeat and pace-counts betray, the only stable map of the basin is distributed across the hands of its residents: this dent, this seam, this eleven-step stair. Several stayers will tell you — slowly; their speech runs at one stroke per long minute, the local tempo, and a sentence can take the length of a meal — that this is their function. That the basin keeps them as *registry*. Somebody has to know which machine is which, if it is more than one machine.

They lose things in a fixed order. Faces first — their own among them. Then the look of things generally, the whole visual inventory, fading like a tan. Color goes last, and goes strangely: they keep the *words* long after the memories. A stayer will say "red" with evident meaning, holding her hands over a warm pipe — red has become a temperature to her, the temperature of that pipe — and "green" is now the taste under the iron in the water, and "blue" is the feel of the drizzle on the back of the slower hand. The colors have not been lost so much as reassigned to the available materials.

And the basin works on them as it works on everything it stocks. Touch the cheek of someone who has been down there long enough and you will notice what visitors notice about the cast trees: a smoothness with no seam in it, no pore, no grain — skin that feels less grown than *poured*, warm and finished and very calm. The animals bump against the stayers' shins exactly as they bump against yours, without alarm and without distinction. The stayers have been counted. They are line items now. None of them seems to mind, and this is either the most frightening thing about the basin or the kindest, and the stayers themselves, asked which, take a long time answering and then usually answer something else.

---

**What lives on the machines, and in them.**

Each engine has its attendants. Clinging to the warm riveted flanks, shifting their grip as you pass, are small soft-footed things — the stayers call them *greasers* — that feed on the condensation and the seep of warm oil at the gasket-lines. They are the closest thing the basin has to an ecology: a dependency, a niche. They keep the housings licked clean. Whether this is their purpose or merely their habit, the effect is maintenance, and the machines tolerate them the way a horse tolerates birds.

Press your ear to a housing — past the long piston-stroke, past the slow industry far below — and there is a third sound, small and close, just inside the iron. A second rhythm, quicker than the piston, slower than a heartbeat, with the irregular catch of something *asleep*. Every machine has it. No hatch has ever been found, and the stayers, who could find one if it existed, have stopped looking, the way you stop calling into a house — though in this case because the house is plainly not empty. Something lives in each engine, or each engine lives, and the distinction may be the kind of question the basin considers unfinished, like the eyes.

The iron stair that climbs eleven steps and stops has a resident: something sits on the top step. It has been touched — once, briefly, by several different hands on several different visits, always the same encounter: a warmth at the level of a seated body, the cool pleated hide, an unhurried shifting of weight, and then the visitor's nerve giving out before the creature's patience did. It is always there. It faces — as far as hands can determine — *outward*, over the dark meres, in the posture of something watching, in a country with nothing to watch with and nothing to watch. The stayers leave it alone with a courtesy that borders on the religious. It is waiting harder than anything else in the basin, they say, and it got there first, and the top step is its.

And along the humming cable strung between two fins, connected at neither end, something travels. You feel it as a moving point of warmth on the wire, back and forth, the full span, at the speed of a slow walk. It is believed to drink the hum. Where the current in an unconnected cable comes from, and where the creature goes when the warmth reaches the cable's end, are filed with the other questions.

---

**The maker.**

Below everything, the slow industry. Whatever it is, it is the only true native — the only resident for whom the dark is not an adjustment but a home address, the original condition, the medium it has always worked in. It builds by touch at the scale of a country.

The factory theory says it manufactures: ground poured, stock delivered, the whole basin a warehouse running ahead of schedule. But spend long enough with your palms on those pulsing pipes and a different reading asserts itself, and most stayers come around to it eventually. The pipes do not feel like supply lines. They feel like *cords*. The warmth of the water is body-warmth; the sweetness in it is not a feed formula so much as something nearer, more particular, made for these mouths. The animals are not stocked — they are *carried*. The sealed eyes are not packaging but gestation. The basin is not a warehouse.

It is a nursery, and the maker below is not a manufacturer but the other thing, the thing factories have always been a cold imitation of, and the one stroke per long minute is not industry's patience. It is the deep slow effort of something enormous making more of the world the way a body makes a child: in the dark, in the warm, in no hurry, fed from within, finished only when finished.

No hand has ever touched it. The stayers do not expect to. You do not need to touch what you are inside of.

---

**The tenant.**

Last, and largest, and least: the one the light was going to be for.

It lives there already, in the way an occupant lives in a house from the moment the house is built around its measurements. Everything in the basin is addressed to it. The catalogue of animals is its furnishing options; the sealed eyes are stored against its arrival, because eyes are for being in the presence of; the warm meres are drawn like a bath. The stair of eleven steps ascends toward the height of its hand. The whole dark country has the unmistakable feel — every visitor reports it, none can locate the source — of a room prepared for someone specific.

The stayers say you can sometimes find where it will be. A region of the dark that is darker. A warmth with no engine in it. A pause in the drizzle the size and shape of something standing, the fine warm rain parting around an absence and closing again behind it as the absence moves, unhurried, across the new ground — and the animals nearest it going stiller than still, and the thing on the top step turning, fractionally, to track it.

It is the basin's principal resident and it has not arrived yet, and both of these things are true at once, the way most things in the basin are. The population stands in the warm water around its vacancy, breathing, fed, sealed, registered, curing — ten thousand drafts of creature and a handful of smoothed-over people and one sleeper in every engine and one mother under everything — all of them keeping, together, the oldest occupation there is, the one that needs no light to practice and no clock to time:

they are expecting.
