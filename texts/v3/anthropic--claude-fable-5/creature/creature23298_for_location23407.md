**The Inhabitant**

The census of the Lignum Works is the shortest document imaginable and the hardest to write. The honest answer is *one*. One resident, full occupancy, no vacancies, no deaths on record, no births. But the honest answer sits wrong the moment you give it, because the word *resident* implies a thing that lives *in* a place, and here the preposition fails. Nothing lives in the Lignum Works. The Lignum Works is what living looks like at this address.

So begin again, slower.

---

What lives here is old enough that it no longer has edges in time. It does not remember beginning because the part of it that began has been demolished and rebuilt so many times that origin is just another district, somewhere, gone glassy and black. It does not anticipate ending for the same reason. It exists the way a long note exists — sustained, revised in pitch by hairsbreadths, never struck and never released. If you asked it how old it was, and if it answered, the answer would arrive as architecture, over decades, and would be wrong by the time it finished, because the thing that started answering would no longer be quite the thing that asked itself the question.

It is alive in the fullest sense and in almost none of the familiar ones. It does not eat; it *sits on* its food, the warm volcanic shield, the way a person might live their whole life lying in a river of soup. It does not breathe; it ventilates, which is different — breath is need, and the bellows-cathedrals work from surplus, moving heat around the way a wealthy economy moves money, because stagnation is the only poverty it knows. It does not reproduce. It considered reproduction, you can tell — out at the plateau's rim there are abortive runners, ancient ones, fingers of cured plank extending a few hundred meters down the cold slopes and stopping, capped, sealed, like sentences abandoned mid-clause. The cold did not kill them. The thing simply discovered, each time, that there was nothing out there it wanted to be that it could not be better here, denser, hotter, more thoroughly. It turned its fertility inward. Every seed-press in every district presses seeds that will never leave; they are not propagules, they are *proposals*, dense fibrous kernels of possible structure, filed in the warm dark, some of them germinated into towers within the hour, most of them archived unread, kept the way a writer keeps drafts — which is to say forever, and bitterly, and with love.

---

Is it one? Stand in it long enough and you will doubt this, and the doubt is correct without being right.

The districts have temperaments. This is not metaphor; it is measurable in revision-rate. Out in the young quarters — *young* meaning merely tens of millennia — the turbulence runs hot and fast: streets open and close like remarks in a conversation, towers go up with a kind of vehemence, come down without ceremony, and the piezoelectric nerves there carry so much charge that your arm-hair stays lifted for hours. Walk inward, and everything slows. The wood darkens, densifies, takes on that stone-black patience. In the oldest quarters a revision is an event of geological dignity — a single wall withdrawing over a season, considered, reconsidered, half-restored. The young districts propose. The old districts veto. You can watch the argument if you have years to spend: a new structural fashion — some efficiency of bracing, some novel duct-geometry — rippling inward from the rim, district by district, adopted, adapted, and finally arriving at the black core, where it is examined at terrible length and almost always declined, and the decline ripples back out, and the rim, chastened or stubborn, tries again.

These are not separate creatures. They are moods of one creature — weathers of a single attention. But the attention is wide enough that its left hand genuinely does not know what its right hand is doing until the news arrives, through fiber and oil and the slow semaphore of construction, weeks later. It is one mind the way an ocean is one water. It disagrees with itself constantly, structurally, and the disagreement is not a malfunction. The disagreement is the metabolism. A thing that agreed with itself completely would stop revising, and a thing that stopped revising, here, would be dead — would be, for the first time in ten million years, merely wood.

---

Then there are the gestures, and the gestures deserve their own paragraph in the census even though they count, strictly, as zero.

You have seen one: the six-stalked knuckle of fiber walking its errand down the center of a street. Understand what you saw. It was not dispatched the way a body dispatches a hand. It was *composed* — sentence-like, fitted to its errand as a sentence is fitted to its meaning, with exactly the legs it needed and not one more, with a gait invented for the specific grade of the specific street it would walk and never used again. While it walks it is, briefly, an individual. It has a boundary, a task, a heading. It may be the only kind of entity in the Works that experiences anything resembling a *self*, and it experiences it for an afternoon. Then it reaches the wall, and joins, and the boundary unwrites itself, and whatever it was like to be that gesture goes back into the general dark like a cupful of water returned to a well. The gestures do not resist this. There is no machinery in them for resisting it. They end the way a spoken word ends, having been entirely used.

The visitors who stand a long time after watching one stop being are not mourning, exactly. They are doing arithmetic that will not come out. Population: one. Population: briefly, sometimes, two. Population: one.

---

Except.

In a corridor in the middle districts — the Works keeps the corridor; this is the point — something is walking that has been walking for longer than anyone can establish. It has six stalks, worn at the joints to a shine. It carries a load of resin that skinned over and went black so long ago it has the lustre of obsidian. Its errand had a destination once. The destination has been demolished and re-extruded elsewhere some thousands of times since, and the gesture's heading has never updated, and so it walks a route that no longer concludes, at the pace of a procession, through streets that — and you can verify this, it is the single most unsettling fact on the plateau — *open ahead of it*. The Works re-routes its riot around this one walker. Corridors that should have closed centuries ago stay open in a thin thread along its path. Whether this is reverence or oversight, a kept promise or a hung process, whether the Works cherishes it or simply cannot see it — no one knows, and the rings in the archive quarters, if they record an answer, record it in grain no one can read. The long walker is the closest thing the Works has to a citizen: the one part of itself it declines to revise. Some visitors follow it for days. It does not notice them. It has an errand.

---

And there is one more entry, the newest, and it is you.

Every step you took on the sprung warm floor was load, and load is information, and information here is never discarded. Somewhere beneath where you stood, the hour's new wood went down with your weight written into it — a faint asymmetry in the ring, a hesitation in the grain where you hesitated. You are in the archive now, alongside the ten million drafts, in the only form the Works keeps anything: as a layer. It will never read the entry. It does not read; it only writes, and keeps, and writes over without erasing. But the entry is there, and it will be there when the corridor you walked has been a tower and a flywheel-house and a corridor again, which means the census was wrong twice over, and must be filed in its final form:

Population: one — counted whole.
Population: legion — counted by mood and gesture and district and draft.
Population: everyone who ever crossed it, kept in grain, in the hot dark, in the quiet — the inhabitant revising itself politely around its memories of guests, since long before there was anyone to be remembered.
