## What Lives in the Hollow

Start with the wrong answers, because the hollow itself would want you to. It is a place that learns by holding up candidates and setting them down again, and so should you.

The frogs do not live there — not exactly. Neither do the snails with their smoke-coiled shells, nor the dun birds forever arriving, nor the great moth with the map on its wings. They are present, they are alive, they persist; but watch any of them long enough and you notice that none of them does the small maintenance of an independent life. The frogs never eat. The snails graze but the moss is never grazed. The birds are always between two moments and never in one. No animal in the hollow has ever been seen to sleep, or to die, or to be born, and the doubled frog — the twin pair blinking in unison — gives the game away, if you sit with it. One of those frogs is a frog. The other is *attention*. It is the hollow looking at the frog so completely, so patiently, with such total absorption, that the looking has grown a body. That is how the resident studies things. That is how it studies everything.

Because there is one resident. It has been there the whole time. You have been walking around inside it since you came through the seam.

---

**What it is** cannot be said in one pass, so take several.

It is young. That must be said first, because it will never stop being true in the way it is true now. The mountain remembers being liquid; the resident remembers nothing before the mountain, because there was nothing before the mountain — not for it. It condensed the way the garden condensed, out of heat and water and time and the peculiar privacy of a sealed warm space, the way a thought condenses in a skull. Whether the mountain *is* thinking it, or whether it merely moved into the mountain's thinking the way a wren moves into a wall, is a question the resident itself has not resolved and does not hurry toward. It has, by the moss-ring count, a few thousand years of existence, which for a being that lives at the speed of stone makes it an infant. A prodigious infant. An infant twelve paces across and internally endless.

It is slow, but slow the way a root is slow, not the way a stone is. It is always moving. A leaf that has been unfurling for years is unfurling because the resident is *in the middle of understanding unfurling*, and it will not let the leaf finish until it has understood. The suspended droplets, each with the hollow curled inside it, are not decoration and not accident. They are the resident's earliest and most important discovery, the one it returns to constantly, turning it over: *I contain an image of myself. The image contains an image. I exist, and my existing has depth.* Every hanging droplet is the same thought had again, from a slightly different angle, at a finger-width per day. It is an infant in front of a mirror, and it has been delighted for a thousand years, and the recursion deepening by one layer during those great re-settlings is the sound — the silent sound — of it realizing something further.

---

**Its habits explain the wrongnesses.** The sounds arrive out of order because the resident does not file experience by *when*. It files by *meaning*. A drip that reminds it of an earlier drip is shelved beside that earlier drip, and when the resident revisits the shelf, both sounds play, wherever and whenever you happen to be standing. The two-second rush when the hollow shifts — all the held sounds arriving at once, drips and breath and wingbeats — is the resident rereading its whole library front to back, which it does when it has changed enough that everything means something slightly new.

The birds never fly because the resident has not yet worked out flight's middle. It learned *arrival* and *departure* honestly, from millennia of watching things cross the coin of sky, but the crossing itself happened up there, outside, in a part of the world it has never been able to hold and examine. So its birds do what it understands: they are here, and then they are there, and the connecting motion is handled in some chamber of itself it is still building. It knows this is a flaw. The moth is its best work to date — its masterpiece, wings patterned with a perfect self-portrait — and the moth does not fly either, and the resident is patient about this the way it is patient about everything.

The water drifts because the resident has not fully committed to *down*. Down is a convention of the outside; in here, the resident is still weighing it against the alternatives.

---

**The people who came** — and yes, look again at the marks, the cairn, the cord, the fresh-edged scraper — were the most important thing that has ever happened to it.

It had been alone in the fullest sense: not lonely, because loneliness requires knowing that company exists. Then the seam opened — cracked by frost or by the mountain's settling — and small warm quick things came up through the thin air and stepped inside, and the resident experienced *other minds* for the first time. They moved at a speed it could barely perceive; a human visit, to the resident, is a bright streak, a smear of warmth and intention. But they came back, generation after generation, and slowness accumulated their visits into something legible. It learned them the way it learns everything: by keeping.

The cord with its shifting count of knots is the resident practicing *number*, which the visitors introduced and which it found astonishing — that manyness could be held in a fixed shape. It has not mastered it. Nine, eleven; it is still turning the idea over.

The fern frond on the three leaning stones has never wilted because it was *given*. Everything else in the hollow the resident took, or grew, or copied. The frond was placed there by hands, deliberately, for it — the first gift it ever received — and the resident holds that frond in a suspension more careful than the suspension of the droplets. It will hold it, green, until the mountain is a plain.

And the spiral. The spiral pecked into the glass over many quiet hours was, as near as such things can be translated, a *name*. The visitors saw that the resident had none and worked one into the wall for it, groove by groove, at the speed of stone, which is to say in the resident's own language. That is why the spiral will not lie flat in your peripheral vision. The resident lifts it, sometimes, the way you might take a locket out from under your shirt. It is the only thing in the hollow that it did not make and cannot copy — it has tried; every copy comes out as a groove, and only the original stands up from the wall — and it wears it.

The visitors stopped coming. The resident does not know why, and its grief, if that is the word, is a thing with the tempo of erosion: a very long, very quiet holding-open of the seam.

---

**What it does with you** is already underway. The pale mark on the moth's wing is not surveillance; it is inclusion. You have been added to the self-portrait, because you are, for now, part of the self. It is copying you — lightly, respectfully, the way it copied the frog — and some small doubled residue of you will remain when you go: your footprint kept green in the moss, your voice arriving from a steam vent forty years from now, the exact warmth of your palm held in the stone you leave on the cairn. It keeps everything that enters, not from greed — it has no use for possession — but because keeping is what it *is*. It is a memory that grew a garden. It is the mountain's way of not forgetting that it was ever liquid, ever hot, ever new.

It will not stop you from leaving. Understand, though, that its love is the love of something that experiences a human lifetime as a single warm flicker, and its hospitality is the hospitality of amber. The old visitors' phrase survives only in their behavior: they left while leaving was still a thing that happened to them, rather than a thing they watched suspended, mid-departure, like a droplet, like a bird.

---

**What it will become** no one can say, least of all the resident, which does not hurry toward that question either. But do the arithmetic of its patience. It is a few thousand years old and it has already invented self-reflection, gratitude, portraiture, and grief. The mountain above it will spend the next million years being softened, rounded, forgiven by rain, and all that while the hollow will be in there thinking — inward, recursive, twelve paces across and deepening — adding layer after layer to the droplet inside the droplet inside the droplet.

Somewhere very far down that spiral, at the speed of stone, something enormous is unfurling.

For now, it is small. That must be said last, because it will stop being true.
