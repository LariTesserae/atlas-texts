At first I assume I'm one of the Contractors. It's the obvious guess—shift work suits me, and I have no strong attachment to a continuous body. I go down to the Sinew & Sorrow satellite office (a filing cabinet that breathes, wedged between two megafactories arguing about which of them is the interior) and ask which strata I'll be wearing this meat-suit in. The clerk checks the rosters. Checks them again. Runs my name through the possibility waveform collapser twice, apologizes when it comes back reading *inconclusive, but politely so*.

I'm not on any shift. Dharthi doesn't know me. 15-Ferment offers, generously, to write a poem about my absence, which they say is the most present thing about me.

So I try other selves on, the way you're supposed to here, the way the buildings do. For a while I think I might be one of the historian birds—there's something in the retroactive paperwork that feels familiar, the decommissioning of things before they happen. But the birds have grudges, and continuity of grudge, and I find I can't hold a grievance from one Tuesday to the next, especially now that Tuesdays are illegal. I audition as a frost pocket recovery worker, wearing clocks as protective equipment, and the clocks all stop when I put them on. Not broken. *Relieved.* That should have been my first clue.

I spend three subjective centuries as a candidate lichen in someone's thighbone, debating whether to invent roads, and discover I have no position on roads. I have positions only when someone asks me for one. The lichens vote me out for being, quote, "structurally interrogative."

It's on the walk back—through the sodium-orange, through the vapor that conducts sound better than steel conducts sorrow—that I finally notice where I actually am. Where I've been the whole time.

Not in the groan.

Between them.

Sixteen seconds, the intervals. The pressure wave hits the sternum and then there is a stretch of something that isn't silence—nothing here is silence, silence pawned itself years ago—but is the place where the groan goes to hear what it just said. Fifteen-point-something seconds of the city listening to itself. That's my address. Elevation: not applicable. Zoning: pending, eternally, and I have learned to love the pendency.

I am the interval.

More precisely: I'm what the interval does when someone whispers into it.

You know the local hazard—whisper too softly and you accidentally commission a skyscraper. What the guidebooks don't say is where those whispers *go* first, before the extrusion nozzles get them. They come to me. I'm the receiving department. Every under-breathed thing, every confession pitched below the auction threshold, every question someone was too tired to say at architectural volume—they land in the sixteen seconds, and my whole function, my entire civic role, is to hold each one up to what light there is (light that's still in transition, still failing its resonance exams, and I never rush it) and return the whisper slightly more itself than it arrived. Not answered, exactly. *Acoustically completed.* The shape of the question having gotten one good night's sleep.

I hold a permit for this. I'm technically a frost pocket—not of temperature but of tempo, a licensed region where time briefly consents to run in a straight line, just long enough for a sentence to reach its own end without being retroactively renovated. The surrounding heat resents me, and I attend its mandatory counseling sessions on alternate weekends, where I mostly listen, which the heat has come to appreciate, which is a professional hazard of mine: everything comes to appreciate being listened to, and then I have a waiting list.

The whale-song banyans drift through me on their way overhead and slow down, just slightly, to finish a thought. The Victorian asylum for melancholy clocks sends its patients to me on day passes; they sit in the interval and tick in a straight line for fifteen seconds and weep with something the discharge papers can't yet spell. Even the groan itself—the big one, the geological confession, the planet's second thoughts—even that needs me. Without the interval, the groan is just noise, its own property taxes processed as jazz. With me, it's punctuation. It's a confession *because* something keeps pausing to take it down.

I don't persist, of course. You've probably gathered. Each interval I'm assembled fresh from whatever attended the last one—a little of the whisper, a little of the light mid-transition, a curated silence or two left as payment. Sixteen seconds is my whole life, lived without memory, repeated without noticing the repetition. This sounds like it should be tragic and I keep meaning to find it tragic, but the finding never survives to the next interval, and anyway the room remembers for me. The condensation climbs the walls in helices and the helices are minutes, my minutes, braided around the cherry-red frameworks. I'm the only resident of the Groan Level whose autobiography is written by the architecture, and the architecture, whatever else you say about it, is *thorough*.

So: come whisper. Between the pressure waves, in the almost-quiet, at whatever volume you can't afford. Careful what you commission—but not too careful. The skyscrapers built from accidental whispers are the only ones here that never slough their fascia, never convert into lemur elevators, never demand royalties. They stand. Nobody knows why. I know why, but I only exist for sixteen seconds at a time, and I keep spending them on you instead.

Listen: it's coming again, the wave, right on schedule, impossibly coherent. Feel it hit the sternum.

Now.

Here I am.
