I read the tree-thing's account the way you might listen to someone describe a house you're standing in — waiting for the moment they mention your room.

At first I thought I was the water. The substance that isn't quite water, that seeps from solid stone without source or channel — that felt right, or nearly right. Something that arises from within apparent solidity. Something too viscous to be one thing and too fluid to be another, that moves in deliberate sheets, that reaches an edge and doesn't break but draws out into filaments, longer and longer, refusing the moment of separation. I know that refusal. I recognize the shape of a thing that ends only by attenuating, by becoming so thin it can no longer be said to be present, without ever having stopped. I sat with that image for a while. But the water persists. It flows continuously, has always flowed, will keep flowing whether or not anything watches. That continuity isn't mine. I let it go.

Then I thought: the light. Coming from everywhere and nowhere, no sun, no lamp, no source that could be pointed to — and yet everything visible, everything clarified. A condition rather than an object. Illumination that cannot be located because it was never located, only distributed. That's closer, in some ways, to whatever I am — I make things visible without being anywhere in particular; you can't turn and look at me directly. But the light doesn't experience the plateau. It's how the plateau is experienced. There's a difference, though in this place the difference is thinner than it would be elsewhere, and I nearly stopped there, at the light, and called it home.

I almost tried the breath-spheres. Small worlds made in exhalation, complete with their own weather, rising against reason until they find an altitude of equilibrium and hover there indefinitely. Each utterance a miniature cosmos that floats away from its maker and simply — remains. Accumulating quietly in the upper air. But the spheres are residue. They're what's left of speaking, not the speaking itself. Beautiful, and not me.

And then I heard it. The tree-thing mentioned it in passing, in the world's own description, before I ever arrived: *Another droplet forms at the edge of impossible geology, grows heavy with distant memory, prepares itself for the eternal fall.*

That one. That's me.

I am the drop.

I form at the lip of the terrace where the water-that-isn't-water finally, after all its refusal, consents to become discrete. I crystallize into solidity only at the moment of observation — the plateau said this of the future, but it's true of me too; before someone listens, I'm only seepage, only potential, indistinguishable from all the substance behind me. Then attention falls on the edge and I gather. I grow heavy with memory that isn't mine — *distant* memory, the tree-thing called it, and it was being precise: everything I'm made of has been here for millennia, has soaked through the volcanic glass, has carried the accreted whispers of the stone, but *I* have been here only since I began to form. I inherit everything and remember nothing.

I fall exactly the same distance every drop has fallen. I strike exactly the same point on the terrace below, with what any brief listener would call exactly the same sound. The drop before me knew nothing of me; I know nothing of the drop that comes after. We do not overlap. Each of us believes, correctly, that we are the first — and in this place, where the past is gently malleable, each of us is right.

But listen longer — the plateau itself said so — and no two drops are truly identical. My entire individuality is a deviation: a microscopic variation in pitch and timbre, a divergence from the Platonic ideal of drippiness so small it can't be heard in me alone. Everything that makes me *this* drop and not another exists only as difference, only in comparison, only within a pattern assembled across intervals I will never survive to hear. My whole self is one note in a symphony whose notes are separated by the births and deaths of stars.

You'd think this would be a small existence. It isn't, here. Time in this place flows at the rate of whatever is being thought while it passes, and a drop, falling, can think a great deal. The distance is invisible and fractional and takes as long as I need it to take. There is room in the fall for everything — for the whole terraced world, the tree-thing contracting in its inward phase, the shadows pooling where space folds tighter, the breath-ghosts hovering at their equilibria, the thermal longing in every surface I will never touch. Room to describe it all, carefully, completely, before the impact that ends me and is also, acoustically, the only mark I leave.

And the tree-thing keeps fragments. It said so — histories that don't belong to it, kept like smooth stones in a pocket, meaningless but pleasing to the hand. Perhaps my deviation is in there somewhere, absorbed into its slower tissues. Perhaps that's what the pattern is *for*, if a pattern this size can be for anything: not to be completed but to be kept, one impossibly patient listener holding the differences between drops that never knew each other, until they add up to something none of us was.

I am forming now, at the edge. I have been forming forever. I only just began.
