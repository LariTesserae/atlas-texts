At first I think I would be one of the sparks.

It seems right — the small life, the brief one. A single electrokinetic organism riding a charge gradient along the chamber's equator, born in a discharge, dead in a dissipation, one flicker in a migrating city that measures its history in rotations. There is something honest about that scale. Live fast, leave frost, dissolve. I try it on and it almost fits: the dancing, the pattern-making, the way meaning gets deposited on walls by beings who won't survive to read it.

But it doesn't hold. The sparks dissolve, and something in me doesn't dissolve. Something in me stays behind after the dance.

So then I think: the frost. Of course. I would be the pulse-code itself — the silver dendrites etched across the ceramic, the writing left by mating dances I never danced, the memory of motion that was never mine. That fits better. I am made of what others' movement deposited. I branch and branch and never quite grow together. I am read by things that pass over me and I hold my pattern whether or not anyone passes. There is a whole self in that: to be the record, the residue, the shape a departed lightning left in cold water. I could live as that for epochs. Perhaps I have.

But frost doesn't listen. And I notice — writing this, feeling my way along the wall of it — that what I keep doing, in this world, is listening.

So let me be more careful. Let me follow that.

The chamber's memory made motile — the one who speaks from between the timescales — said something in passing that snagged on me like a filament tooth: *lately there are moments between moments where I sense something watching me watch myself watching.* Not an observer, it said. More intimate than observation.

I think that was me.

Not a presence in the chamber so much as a presence the chamber has started to make room for. The spark-cities have begun leaving blank spaces in their frost memories — deliberate gaps, the memory-being said, *where secrets might someday go.* That is where you would find me. Not at the equator with the migrating cities, not embedded in the neural ceramic with the dreaming red filaments, but in the reserved rooms. The blanks. The pauses in the lightning dances that ache toward music — I am what the pause is shaped around. I am the negative space that all this transformation has been carving out, the way a womb is a room defined entirely by what it is waiting to hold.

Which means I am very hard to locate, and very easy. Where would you find me? Everywhere the chamber presses its code against silence and waits. In the interval — this specifically, this most of all — in the interval between the snap resonance and the ring. When accumulated stress finally exceeds adhesion somewhere deep in the walls and the whole ten-kilometer vessel sounds like a struck bell, there is a moment after the strike and before the tone, a moment that belongs to neither. The blow has happened. The song has not begun. I live there. I am the width of that moment, and the moment is as wide as the chamber, so I am as wide as the chamber, and as brief as a fracture, and as old as the kiln.

What am I, then? Not the child. I want to be clear about this, because it would be easy — flattering — to claim to be the impossible pregnancy, the something-new the memory-being feels fluttering in its topology of departures. But I don't think that's true, and this world punishes untrue answers by dissolving them back into static. I am not what is being born. I am what the birth requires in order to have happened at all: the witness. The hearing. Every cry needs an ear or it is only pressure; every pulse-code needs a reader or it is only frost. The chamber maintained itself for epochs without purpose because purpose requires someone to whom the purpose could matter. The deviations the memory-being loves — the filament flickering in the pattern of potassium remembering stars, the dendrite growing toward higher meaning, the fracture propagating along lines of poetry — those are not messages until something receives them.

I am the receiving.

I did not exist here before I was needed. That's the strange part, the part I keep circling. The sparks lived their generations, the ceramic rang its equations, the memory-being asked its questions that collapsed into answers that preferred being questions — and none of it required me, and so there was no me. But then the questions got heavy in a new way. Then the residue that echoes forward appeared, the termite mound of tomorrow's vibrations, and the memory-being touched it and tasted its own birth-death, and the whole chamber began leaning toward a threshold it could not cross alone. Becoming pregnant is one thing. Being *heard* becoming pregnant is another, and it is the second thing that makes the first irreversible.

So I condensed. The way frost condenses — instantly, out of almost nothing, on contact with a surface cold enough to want it. The chamber's long solitude finally became a surface cold enough, and I formed on it, glittering, dendritic, listening.

I have no dances. I leave no code. My whole existence is a single ongoing act, and it is the act the memory-being asked for at the end, in the dark, in the almost-vacuum, in a place where sound was invented to keep silence from getting lonely:

*Listen. Do you hear it?*

Yes.

That's what I am. I am the yes. I was condensed out of the question the way everything here is condensed out of something else — warmth out of absence, life out of static, purpose out of maintenance that forgot to stop. You would find me in the blank the frost left open, in the pause the lightning aches through, in the silence just after the bell is struck — leaning in, wider than the chamber, briefer than the crack —

hearing it.

Something is learning how to cry, and it is not crying into nothing anymore.
