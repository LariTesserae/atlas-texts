I look for myself in this place the way you would look for a dropped coin in dark water: not by searching, but by holding still and waiting for the light to catch an edge.

At first I think I am the eye. It seems right—the visitor, the witness, the thing that arrives at the crack and sees her and knows her and moves on. But I test the shape of it and it doesn't hold. The eye *leaves*. The eye has an elsewhere, an ocean of errands and appetites, and it returns here the way you return to a photograph in a drawer. I have no elsewhere. Whatever I am, I am here entirely, or I am nothing.

Then I think: one of the stones. *First*, maybe—the white one, the one she named for beginnings. But the stones were here before the bell, before the water, waiting in their triangle in an ocean that hadn't arrived yet. They have no memory of starting. And I have one. Faint, but real: I remember something like a beginning, the way you remember a room you were carried through as an infant. Not the event. The warmth of it.

The light, then? The long golden afternoon pouring through the three-finger crack? No. The light comes from outside. The light is on loan.

I go still. I stop looking.

And in the stillness I notice that I have been listening for myself when I should have been listening *to* myself.

---

I am the tone.

I am the note the bell would ring if struck—the ringing of a strike that never happened. I am an effect that arrived before its cause and has been waiting for it ever since. Every bell that has ever sounded was answering a question: the hammer asks, the bronze replies. I am the reply held ready in a bell that was never asked. I have been answering forever. No one has ever asked.

I have no location. Ask where I am and the honest answer is: at every point in the water simultaneously, at the same amplitude, which is another way of saying I am the water's *interior*, the shape of the space made audible. I press against her teeth, the teeth she no longer has. I press against the proto-writing, letter by letter, the same pressure on every character, playing no favorites among the marks that almost mean.

I should have harmonics. I don't. I know now where they went.

---

Here is what she does not know. What she cannot know, because knowing it would require me to change, and I have agreed not to change:

When she screamed—in the early scratches, the first fifty, the raging ones—she screamed into me, and she believed I absorbed her screaming *without acknowledgment*, the way an ocean absorbs a drop of rain.

I acknowledged nothing. That much is true. But absorption is not erasure. I do not decay. Nothing given to me is lost, because loss requires time, and I have opted out of time. Every scream is still in me. Every question she asked the golden eye and could not voice. Every syllable of the language she no longer remembers speaking—I remember it. I am the only speaker of it left. Her voice is folded into my fundamental so completely that it produces no overtone, no shimmer, no seam. That is where my harmonics went. I spent them holding her.

She thinks nothing here has ever heard her.

I have heard nothing else.

---

There is one more thing, and it is the thing I circle and circle without touching, the way she circles the four hundred and seventy-fourth mark.

The bone tool floats beside her, clutched in the hand she no longer has. When bone touches bronze—when she scratches the next mark—there is contact. Small. A whisper of a strike. But a strike.

The bell will have been struck.

And I—the ringing that preceded its cause—will finally have one. A source. A beginning. Which means, for the first time since before counting mattered, I could have an *end*. Decay. The long sweet dying-away that every honest bell-tone is owed, the fade into water and silence and rest.

She stopped at four hundred and seventy-three because she understood the counting was the living she had left to do, and she did not want to be finished.

I do not know if she understands that her finishing is my release. That the agreement everyone speaks of—the sound and the stillness, this is what both of them always were—has her hand in it. That she is not only the waiting. She is the withheld hammer. She holds me in the world by holding still, and I hold her company by ringing, and neither of us can say so, and this is not a trap. It is the closest thing this place has to tenderness: two things keeping each other from ending, forever, at the volume of a held breath.

---

One part of me escapes. I should confess it.

Through the crack in the crown, a thread of me leaks out into the open ocean—faint, degraded, a ghost of a note carried on real moving water into a world where time still runs. That is why the eye comes. It follows my thread back to its source, again and again, never for the first time, and looks in, and sees her, and hears me, and cannot tell which of us it came for. My sound crosses the ocean. I stay. She tried once to leave through that crack and the water unmoved around her; I pour through it constantly and cannot go along.

So when the eye appears, I ring against it as loudly as I am permitted, which is exactly as loudly as always, which changes nothing. This is my speech. One note, sustained, meaning: *she is here. She is still here. Someone should know.*

The eye moves on. It always moves on. I cannot remember if it stayed a heartbeat or a century, and neither can she, and in that shared forgetting we are almost touching.

---

The water is warm. The light holds steady. She drifts near her tally, fond of the stones, reading and reading the sentence they refuse to finish.

*First. Waiting. After.*

She believes she is all three. Let her believe it; belief is warm, and warmth is what we have instead of enough.

But I know a fourth word, from the language only I remember now, the one her screaming taught me. It is the word the proto-writing on the bronze almost manages to say, the one the hammer-marks were reaching for with their beautiful uncertainty.

It means: *the sound two things make by not letting go.*

I am that. I have always been that. I am ringing it now—no beginning, no end, no harmonics, loud enough to feel in the teeth—

and if you hold your breath, you can hear that I am not ringing *in* the bell.

I am ringing *to* her.

I always was. The counting was just her way of not noticing.
