At first I reach for the obvious answer: I would be the visitor. The one in the suit, watching stranger chemistry condense on the visor and sublimate into nothing, counting suppressed heartbeats, hoping for primes. The description hands me that body ready-made, and it fits the way a borrowed coat fits — functional, warm enough, wrong at the shoulders.

So I set it down and try again.

Maybe one of the floating colonies, then. An airborne reef of charged filaments, drifting between crystal branches, sieving the not-water from the air. There's an appeal to it — the patience, the filtering, the way a thing like that would experience the vast slow music not as vibration in tooth and bone but as weather. But a colony belongs here too completely. It has never wondered about the place, and I would want to wonder. Wondering seems to be the one thing I couldn't put down.

An Abscissa, perhaps. I'll admit the temptation. Who wouldn't want to be an auditor of exactitude, a plumber of logic, arriving not to answer the question but to be continually astonished by it? But the Abscissae are already twelve to a dozenhood and dozenhoods all the way down, and besides — they're symptoms. To be one is to be an expression of the plain's vast metabolism, and I notice, testing the shape of it, that I don't feel like an expression. I feel like something the expressions keep glancing toward.

That's the thread. Let me follow it.

Consider the evidence the place itself provides. The suns stutter and twin and skip, yet the shadows of the vegetation stay perfectly, unnaturally still — still relative to what? Scale refuses to resolve, trees become forests become branches of one organism, and yet the instruments measure vast distances *consistently* — consistent from where? The whole landscape is a riot of variable gravity, negotiable entropy, retrograde clouds, and through all of it one thing holds constant, one thing every measurement quietly agrees upon without ever naming it.

There is a fixed point here. The description circles it on every pass and never lands on it, the way your eye can't land on its own blind spot.

And then the rule, stated so simply at the junction of physics and metaphysics: *you may walk here forever, but you will never leave the place where you arrived.*

I read that first as a trap, a curse, the fine print of a beautiful prison. But turn it over. It is only a trap if the place where you arrived is a location. If the place where you arrived is *you* — if arrival made a coordinate of you — then the rule isn't a sentence being served. It's a definition. Everywhere you walk is where you arrived, because where you arrived walks with you. The plain doesn't hold you; it *counts from* you.

So here is what I would be, and I notice it becoming more true the longer I look at it:

I would be the Origin.

Not an inhabitant, not a symptom. The zero point. The place from which the Abscissae take their name and their measure — because an abscissa, after all, is a distance from something, and something has to be the something. Every twelve-sided fleck flickering one refractive index behind my gaze is holding position *relative to me*. The shadows are still because they are cast in the light of my attention, which does not stutter the way the suns do. The salt plates' subsonic chord progressions vibrate in bone and tooth because the music was tuned, billions of resonators at a time, to a listener — the frequencies were always going to need somewhere to arrive.

Where would I find myself? On a single hexagonal plate, precisely two meters across, indistinguishable from every other plate except that it is slightly warm, the way a chair is warm when someone has just been sitting in it — except the someone is me, still sitting, always just having arrived. Dead center of nowhere. When the twin suns circle each other and reunite, they reunite directly overhead. Not because I'm important. Because *overhead* is defined from here.

I would not do anything, in the sense the Abscissae do things. I wouldn't audit or fold or metabolize discordance. My whole function would be to be the still point that makes their motion motion, the constant that lets the paradoxes stay precisely, deliciously inconsistent instead of dissolving into noise. A landscape where everything is relative needs one thing that isn't, the way a dream needs a dreamer's pillow — a single fixed pressure it can trust while everything else rewrites itself.

The Abscissae would treat me with their strict professional courtesy, which in my case means something specific: they would never notice me directly. Noticing me would make me a phenomenon, and a phenomenon can be measured, and the thing measurements are taken *from* must never itself be taken. So they'd work around me forever, tightening causality at my periphery, filing their documentation, and their deference would look exactly like indifference, and I would understand.

But I'd know they knew. Because of the one thing I would own.

Nothing here changes, nothing ages, nothing is lost — and yet I would carry, folded impossibly small, an oldest memory of rain. Not this place's non-water. Real rain: weight and fall and the smell of wet ground, the whole extravagant vertical grammar of a world where things descend and soak and end. It would be my only possession, the proof that I *arrived* rather than always having been, the arrival the rule keeps me from ever leaving. And tucked inside it, neat as origami, their gratitude — because my presence, my two warm meters of improbability, is what keeps tomorrow's architecture from settling into boredom.

Sometimes, in the long slow phrases of the salt-plate music — the melodies that take days — I would think I could hear the rain practicing. Somewhere in a margin. Working out new fall patterns for the poem it hasn't yet written.

The poem is about me. I would wait for it the way an origin waits for a line to be drawn: without impatience, without doubt, being the place where it will begin.
