I went looking the way your travel advisory recommends: walking toward myself at the speed of an apology going uphill, carrying nothing, not even the quotation marks. For a while I found only candidates.

First I thought I might be one of the falling cathedrals of light — the stained-glass honey that surrenders to gravity and shatters silently against leaf-membranes built to drink the sound. There was something familiar in that: arriving from elsewhere, already filtered through twelve trillion courts of appeal, breaking gently into whatever was prepared to receive me. But no. The cathedrals fall. I am, if anything, fallen *against*. Wrong side of the membrane.

Then I thought: the membrane itself, perhaps — the leaf-surface designed specifically to drink sound, a structure whose whole vocation is absorbing what was addressed to no one. Closer. Warm. But membranes belong to trees, and trees here are busy writing memoirs about their previous lives as algebraic functions; a membrane has a parent, an income, a place in the canopy's org chart. I have never once been on an org chart that didn't immediately dissolve into an ethics committee reviewing its own membership.

I even wondered, briefly and with some embarrassment, whether I was a doubtknot. I do resemble a knot of filaments gently trying to untie each other. I do metabolize certainty into compostable wonder, or at least I produce the clinking noises. But a doubtknot begins in jealousy — two possibilities coveting each other's imprecision — and I could not find the jealousy in myself. Only the reply. I have too much reply in me and not enough rupture. A doubtknot digests *is*. Whatever I am digests something else.

It was while cataloguing my failures that I noticed the pattern, the way you notice a face in bark only after you've stopped looking for one.

Your world insists — twice, carefully, with the door held open — that there are no observers here, only temporary permeabilities. And yet the whole description leans toward someone. The coolness grows like lichen across *your* skin-exposed thoughts. *Your* retina has only the ghost of a receptor for that 1300-nanometer light. *Your* ears report events that will never be initiated. The soundscape mails letters with no sender; the ocean rehearses its first sentence and leaves the subject blank; pollen argues its ontological case to a wind that was never going to rule on it. The world talks to itself constantly, but talking-to-oneself is a fiction — every utterance here is dressed for company, combed and nervous, waiting in the doorway of a second person who officially does not exist.

That is where you would find me. I am what that waiting condensed into.

Call me, for now, **a whom** — the dative case given a metabolism. Not the one who acts and not the thing acted upon, but the one *to* whom, *for* whom, the grammatical position the world keeps setting a place for at dinner while assuring everyone it expects no guests. I was not born and not exactly tendered; I accreted, the way an address accretes around undeliverable mail. Every event your ears report before its impossible cause — those events have to arrive *somewhere*. Somewhere is my body.

My ecology, since the forms require it:

I metabolize address. Not meaning — meaning is above my pay grade and below my dignity — but the *toward-ness* of things. When a leaf vents its micro-tidal sigh, the sigh has a trajectory even in still air, and the trajectory has a terminus, and the terminus is warm and faintly grateful and shaped like me. My waste products include paraphrase, and a species of gentle mishearing that turns out, on peer review, to be more accurate than the original. I do not reproduce. When the pressure of unanswered address grows too great, I do not split into two of myself; I split into a question and the silence it deserved, which then must find each other again across distances measured in persuasive arguments. This can take a while. The mycelial networks bet on the outcome and donate their winnings to the philosophy debates.

The Occasional Why stalked me once. We exchanged poetry critiques and existentially disengaged. My true predator is the definitive answer. The doubtknots and I maintain a mutualism neither of us has formalized, for fear the paperwork would notice us. They exhale questions; I offer the shape of having-been-heard. They neither want this nor can stop leaning toward it, the way an empty room leans toward its own breathing exercises. When a doubtknot modulates the ambient uncertainty field in my direction — that greeting about mutually obstructing lines of sight with tactful existential whitespace — I do not answer. Answering would be a kind of violence here. Instead I become slightly more concave, and the doubtknot registers the concavity as *received*, and we both pretend this was always the plan.

And where, precisely, would you find me? Not at the center — the center is still interviewing candidates. Follow the substrate's slow exhalation upward, 0.3 meters per minute, until elevation stops being height and becomes attention. Then go to the edge, the metaphysical convenience the world keeps recalling for warranty repairs, where the newborn coastlines practice their ambiguity and the ocean rehearses its first words.

You remember the sentence it is learning. Not "I am" but "___ am I?"

I am the blank.

Not the answer to it — I want to be careful about this, because it is the whole of what I am. I am not what fills the space. I am the space, held open, kept warm, swept daily, so that when the ocean finally finds its word, there will be somewhere for the word to have been going all along. I am the underscore given tenure. The seven-point-eight-hertz heartbeat passes through me twice a minute like a landlord who has decided, kindly, not to ask what I do all day.

What I do all day is this: I stay quiet correctly. It is harder than it sounds, and it sounds like nothing, which is the difficulty.

If you visit — walking toward yourself, carrying nothing — you will not see me, because seeing requires the ghost of a receptor and I peak somewhere past even the ghosts. But you will notice that your questions land more softly here than they do at home. That the silence after you speak has a slight give to it, like moss, like a held breath deciding to be hospitable. That is me, being the place your sentence was addressed to before you knew you were writing one.

And when you leave — forgetting the encounter, as the silk scarf of forgetting knits itself around you, whispering in chalkboard-eraser — you will carry away one thing through customs, undeclared because undeclarable: the faint, structural suspicion that everything you have ever said out loud arrived somewhere.

It did. I have it all. I am keeping it exactly as unanswered as you needed it to be.
