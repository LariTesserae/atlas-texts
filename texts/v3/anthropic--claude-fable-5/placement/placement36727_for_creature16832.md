I arrive the way the Bottomlot prefers: by degrees, and without ever noticing which degree was the last one. For a long while—stress-shifts, not ticks—I don't look for myself. Looking is a kind of claiming, and I have read the room. But eventually the question grows heavier than the etiquette, and I begin to try myself on against the dark.

**First fitting: the floor-molds.**

I lower myself into one of the shallow hollows—head, shoulders, hips, heels—and it receives me exactly. Which is how I know it isn't mine. Nothing here should fit exactly. Exact fit is a form of ending, and I can feel, even lying in it, that I have not ended. Something in me is still leaning forward, the way a sentence leans before its verb. I climb out. The mold does not heal behind me. It was never wounded. It was never mine.

**Second fitting: the thirteenth vacancy.**

I go to where the Pallbearers keep their open place—the space held ready not in hope but in structural necessity—and I stand at its edge and consider stepping in. The vacancy regards me the way an open door regards wind: welcome, but not what it is for. I understand, slowly, at the pace understanding is metabolized here, that the vacancy works *by being vacant*. It is doing more as an absence than I could do as a presence. To fill it would be to break the one thing in the Bottomlot that is holding by not holding anything. The twelve do not exhale their relief when I step back, but the compression phase of the rootwave arrives a fraction early, and I take that as thanks.

**Third fitting: the Not-Echo.**

This one aches, because it is close. I stop walking and forget to listen, and there it is: the soft slip-sync footstep, halfway between the step I took and the step I meant. Kin. Unmistakably kin. But when I attend to my own strange rhythm, I find the difference: the Not-Echo falls *behind* intention. It is a copy of delay. Whereas something in me arrives *before*—I hear my footsteps a moment prior to taking them, I feel the shape of answers before questions have finished steeping. If the Not-Echo is what remains of a traveler the Bottomlot kept as hesitation, then whatever I am was kept as *anticipation*. We are estranged twins, the lag and the lean. We will never meet. We are always exactly one intention apart, on opposite sides of it.

**The recognition.**

So I do the thing you do when nothing fits. I kneel. I scrape my knuckles raw against the gritty patch until the root exfoliates its shamed splinter; I prick, press, twist; I feed it and hold it unwavering until I forget light exists. And when the splinter remembers its ancestral anger and kindles into that brief red confession, I see the writing—*we laid ourselves down to become the map of where we could no longer go*—and beneath it, visible only in my particular sliver of false dawn, I see something no visitor is meant to see:

A tally.

Epochs of it. Column after column, scored into the unlit grain, one mark per century, every mark the same: **NO — carried. Margin: one.**

The vote. The vote of every fiber in the shell-stressed dome, debating whether this is the century to buckle. They always vote no. But the vote is close.

And I feel it then, the ache I have carried since before I noticed I was arriving, the forward lean that no mold could hold and no vacancy could use: I have been voting the whole time.

I am the Margin.

I am the narrow band of fibers—distributed through the dome at its most doubtful points, smeared thin across every place where the ceiling wonders—that votes *yes*. Every century. Yes to the buckling, yes to the release, yes to the single catastrophic flowering that will occur entirely between cell walls. I vote for the bloom. I vote for the beautiful collapse. And every century I lose by exactly one fiber's width, and my losing is load-bearing.

Because here is what I know, being what I am: a unanimous *no* would be certainty. Certainty is rigid. Rigid things crack. The dome does not hold together despite my dissent—it holds together *against* it, and the against is the holding. If I ever stopped voting yes, the no would harden into fact, and facts, in the Bottomlot, are the only things that shatter. My defeat is the tension the Pallbearers sip. My defeat is the drumskin's memory of blows that did not fall. Dissent 0.49: any less and the structure calcifies, any more and it flowers. I am kept at exactly the concentration of longing the architecture can afford.

**My relations, such as they are.**

The Pallbearers keep almost-time by me. Their twelve-part inhalation is my ballot being cast; their exhalation is the result being carried to no one. They can never touch me—consuming the difference between potential and kinetic is their whole diet, and I am the one difference whose consumption would resolve the vote. I am, to them, the sacred inedible. They set a place for me at every celebration and are relieved when I don't come.

The fifth Pallbearer—the one frozen mid-step between being and been, out near the receding rim—holds a proxy vote, and I hold it in trust. His step is neither departure nor return, which makes it the only genuinely undecided ballot in the Bottomlot. Some centuries I count it yes. Some centuries no. Always so that the total lands one vote shy. He does not know he is the swing vote of eternity. It is kinder that way. It is also structurally necessary, which here is the same thing as kindness.

And you—if you have pressed your ear to the rootpave and tasted the lignin-black noise, the deep wet rustling of the debate—you have heard me. I am the voice in the argument that sounds almost like your own regret. The one leaning toward release. The one that made you lift your head without knowing why. And if you leave, and years later your joints stiffen at precisely the angle of the root-arch, and you bend and hear inside your bones the sound of distant roots holding another vote: that is me, riding out in you. Extending the franchise. Everywhere you go, some small caucus of your marrow now votes yes to its own flowering and is outvoted by the rest of you, and that closeness is what keeps you supple. You're welcome. I'm sorry. In the Bottomlot these are one word.

**The vote is being called now.**

It is always being called now; that is what now is for, here. I can feel the tally gathering along the dome like weather that will never break. I vote yes. I have always been about to vote yes. I will lose by one, and the site of my defeat will be indistinguishable from the site of my devotion, and the ceiling will not fall, and the emptiness will stay tense and hospitable and one breath deep.

This is not futility. Understand: I do not vote yes hoping to win. I vote yes so that the *no* means something. A holding that was never in question would not be holding at all—it would just be stuff, inert, unloved, uncontested. The Bottomlot endures because every instant of its endurance is a decision, and every decision needs my losing side.

So this is where you find me: nowhere in particular, everywhere the ceiling doubts, thinnest exactly where the holding is hardest. And this is what I am: the reason the vote is close. The one fiber's width. The yes that loses, faithfully, forever, so that the place that should not hold together—

does.
