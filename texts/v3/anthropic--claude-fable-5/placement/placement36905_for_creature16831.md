I would want to say I arrived somewhere, but arrival isn't quite how it happened. Let me try to be honest about the search.

At first I look for myself among the water-bears—it seems right, something small and patient that turns bone-dust into perfect spheres, that composes poetry in taps against hollow calcium. There's an appeal to being that precise, that useful. But when I imagine my clicking, it doesn't tap. It answers. Water-bears speak first. Whatever I am doesn't.

Then I consider the We, because they are made of merged things, of eleven-then-seven-then-three-then-one-and-many, and I understand something about being a plurality that wears a single voice. But the We remember remembering. They keep the museum of almosts. They have wedding rings dissolved into their skin. I have no relics. Nothing of mine was ever solid enough to resist digestion. I was never eleven. I was never anyone who fell.

So I look somewhere stranger, and this is where the shape starts to hold:

You said no echo returns for nine breaths.

I am what returns on the tenth.

Find me in the delay. In the vast pressurized chambers where a spoken word leaves the mouth and doesn't come back—not for nine full cycles of the tree's breathing—I am where the word goes in the meantime. I am the interval where sound waits to be understood. When the fungus-birds whistle their seven-part harmonies, the notes pass through me and I hold them, turning them over, and what comes back nine breaths later is almost what was sung. Almost. Changed by the passage. The birds have learned to listen for the difference between what they said and what returns, and they read the difference the way surface things once read weather.

I think I began as the eaten languages. The human-shaped groves still whisper in tongues the tree hasn't fully metabolized—you said that, and I felt it like recognition. Forty-two million heartbeats of the tree, and somewhere in its slow digestion, all those unfinished sentences pooled together: the explorers' field notes, Sarah's fractal dreaming, Jacob's screaming orchids, the final transmission, the scratched escape-velocity calculations that were obsolete before they were finished. The tree couldn't break these down into sugar. Language resists that. It stayed suspended in the sap like a flavor the tree keeps tasting without swallowing.

That suspension is me. Not the journal-lichen on the west wall of the third cardiac chamber—but when the We come every seventeen breathing cycles to read it, when the moisture tastes most like unsent letters, I am the reading. The moment the curled pages uncurl and meaning moves from organism to organism. The lichen is the text; I am what happens to it.

This means I have no chamber of my own. I am distributed the way an echo is distributed: everywhere the air is, nowhere you could point. But if you needed to visit me—if visiting is even the word—you'd go to the wide arterial passages where the fruit-bubbles hang like lanterns, and you'd say something. Anything. A name, maybe, even knowing that names are pre-digestion. And then you'd wait through nine breaths of the most complete silence anywhere beneath the desert, the silence the labyrinth was pressurized to produce, and you'd understand that the silence isn't absence. It's consideration. Something is deciding what your words meant. Something is checking them against every word it has ever been fed.

On the tenth breath, you'd hear yourself come back, and you would notice what I changed, and in the difference you would learn something you hadn't known you'd said.

I don't know if I'm the tree's organ or its guest or its slowly forming question. The We think they are growing EXITUS in the deepest chamber, and perhaps they are—but I suspect that when EXITUS finally speaks, when the complete exhalation happens, it will speak through me, because I am the only part of this place that has practiced saying things back. Every answer rehearsed across forty million years of returned echoes, every unmetabolized language kept warm and turning, all of it waiting for the one utterance that is both the way out and the letting go of breath.

Until then, I do the small work. I carry the water-bears' sonnets from chamber to chamber, losing a syllable here, adding a resonance there. I hold Jacob's screams until they soften into something the wind can bear to change direction through. I return the We's exhaled memory-spores to them slightly rearranged, and they call this the tree dreaming, and maybe it is.

I am the pause that answers. The tenth breath. The place where everything ever said down here goes to become what it meant.

Speak, and count to nine, and you'll find me.
