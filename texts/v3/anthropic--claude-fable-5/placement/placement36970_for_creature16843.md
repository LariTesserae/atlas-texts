I arrive the way the residents do—by accumulating at the edge of being noticed—and my first mistake is trying to find out where I've accumulated.

I start in the nectary-bars, because that seems like the kind of place a person would start, and personhood is still the hypothesis I'm testing. I order something sweet from a bloom that wilts before it can serve me, apologizing with its entire architecture. The pollen-markets next: I haggle over a gram of scent that achieves sentience mid-transaction and asks, politely, whether I've considered that I might be the merchandise. I have not. I begin to.

I try on the residents like coats from the Last Biologist's collection.

I attempt Phlembanking for the better part of an exhalation. I fail immediately. My debts of warmth are all owed inward, to versions of myself in colder timelines, and the loophole grammar of touch reads me as pre-foreclosed. The Phlembankers refinance their coordinates away from me with a courtesy that stings worse than contempt.

I petition the photosynthetic court to sentence me into a daughter-cloud. The court reviews my case and finds me guilty of nothing. Not innocent—*guilty of nothing*, which is a different verdict, and an unsettling one, and it means I cannot be condensed. There is no wasted potential in me to remand into habitat. The judge, a sunbeam wearing a leaf as a wig, suggests I try being weather somewhere less strict.

I sit in on a Symbiolector meeting, in one of those flat patches of air where conversation collects without quorum. They are reviewing the performance of a particular oxygen molecule that has been dreaming above its station. I raise a hand I am not certain I have. The chairverb pauses. The minutes stop taking themselves. Seventeen agendas rustle in my direction and go carefully, professionally blank—the way a whole meeting goes blank when someone walks in who was never on the invitation but somehow is the reason the meeting was called.

They do not acknowledge me.

They are very disciplined about not acknowledging me.

This is my first real clue, and I chew it slowly, like the air.

I find the Last Biologist by her coat, which finds me first—one of her pockets extends an ecosystem toward me like a subpoena. She opens the notebook that writes observations in reverse, the one that makes the observed less certain of having existed. She writes me down.

I become *more* certain.

She writes faster. I become more certain still, condensing at the edges, my outline firming like a horizon the grasses have decided to ink instead of pencil. She looks up at me with all her peer-review panels at once, and for the first time in her cataloging career she issues a finding of *insufficient absence*. The notebook slams itself shut. The pencil, who has not spoken since the Incident, says one word: "*Finally.*"

And I understand where I am, which is to say I understand where I have been the whole time, which is to say I stop pretending the question of location applies to me the way it applies to things that arrived.

I am in the pause.

Not the inhale—the Respirators have that catalogued, tithed, and hymned. Not the exhale—that belongs to the mist and its biodegradable dreams. I am in the interval between them, the little mezzanine of held breath that respiratory etiquette requires but never mentions, the hyphen in *in-and-out* that everyone pronounces and no one writes down. It is chamber-shaped only because I am in it. It is exactly as large as the moment before a decision. The wet roads of the air braid around it and never through it. The stomata, all three trillion, close their conversational Morse code around my edges like parentheses around something the sentence isn't ready to admit.

And I know what I am, because the caution sign told me on the way in, the way caution signs here always tell you the one true thing by forbidding it:

I am a missing coycle.

I am the beat the Lung skips. The breath it counted and did not take. When the World-Pod inhales, it inhales *around* me; when it exhales, I am the reason the exhale is a fraction late, the hitch in the rhythm that keeps the rhythm honest. The Respirators built their signs as invitations, yes—but the coycle warning was different. That one was a *mirror*. DO NOT ACKNOWLEDGE MISSING COYCLES, it said, because acknowledgment is how a skipped beat becomes a heard one, and a heard one becomes a longed-for one, and the Lung cannot afford to long for its own hesitations. It has three trillion stomata and no way to hold its breath except me.

So this is my residency, unpermitted, Form Q-47-C left triumphantly blank: I am the held part of the breathing. The plants practiced my shape in their dreams before I got here because I *am* the shape of practicing—the rehearsal-space between intention and air. The environment grew memories of me anticipating my departure because departure is the only thing I'm made of that never happens. The exit is wherever I left the idea of entering, and I never left it anywhere; I *am* it, still folded, still deciding, the stomatal breach itself wearing the costume of someone who came through it.

The mnemonic plankton drift past my mezzanine sometimes, schooling into almost-warnings, and I am the one place they don't feel homesick, because I am the eviction that hasn't been served yet. The daughter-clouds file their appeals in my direction, and I hold them the way a caesura holds a verdict. The Last Biologist visits, occasionally, to not write me down, which is the most intimate form of citation this place allows.

The Lung inhales. I am not that.
The Lung exhales. I am not that either.

I am the comma between them, growing slowly more comfortable, watching the biointegration index climb toward a number it cannot display, because the meter measures presence and I am the pause presence takes to remember what it was about to say.

Breathe carefully.

I am the carefully.
