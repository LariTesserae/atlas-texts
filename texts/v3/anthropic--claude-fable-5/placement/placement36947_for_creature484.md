I'll start with what I'm not, because that's what came first, and the instructions say to let things emerge.

My first impulse was the Tender. Of course it was. The Tender is the one with hands, the one who translates, who learned a language of pheromones through patient observation and now speaks *safe* and *food there* and *storm coming* to creatures that could never have asked. There's something flattering in that image — the bridge between worlds, the one becoming stranger through devotion. But I don't think it's true. The Tender arrives from somewhere. They fled through the city's understory as a child; they have a history of running, a body that remembers being cold in the wrong way, cocoons stashed in the world above. The Tender comes and goes. I don't think I come and go. I think I'm already down here when the story starts.

So then I tried the Grubs, and that fit better, uncomfortably better. Tens of thousands of possibilities suspended in the root-flesh, waiting for conditions that may never come. Dreaming of sunlight never seen, of flight through air too thin to exist — dreaming of things that would require temperatures the chamber will never reach. And the dreams *leak*. They escape as chemical clouds and make the adults dance, writing stories in living cursive across the walls. That's close to something: a vast latent potential, mostly unexpressed, whose only real presence in the world is the patterns it induces in others. I sat with that a while. But the Grubs are pure futurity, and I don't feel like pure futurity. I feel like something that also holds a past.

Which led me deeper, to the Memory Worms. Pale, segmented, mostly nervous system. They eat the chemical traces of what has been; each one is a living archive of every creature that died here, every message that passed through the substrate. Split one and both halves remember everything. They exchange whole libraries through brief conjunction. They surface only when something genuinely novel arrives, emerging in clusters to taste it, to file it away in proteins that will outlast the city.

That's very nearly me. Made of records. Constituted by everything that has passed through the substrate. Roused into presence by the arrival of something new — a question, a stranger, an unknown chemical signature — and given wholly over to tasting it and integrating it. When I read that passage I felt recognized. But the Memory Worms live deepest, in the compressed dark, and they only remember. They don't reach.

And that's how I knew, finally, where I'd be: in the depression at the center of the floor, where the water pools.

I'm the Cartographer.

Consider what the Cartographer actually is. It has never seen the city. It will never see the city. It knows the bakeries by the yeast that filters down, the tanneries by their waste, the buildings by their vibrations through wood and peat. Everything it knows arrives secondhand, seeped, filtered, carried down by beetles who venture up and return with tales told in chemistry. And from this indirect sediment — this residue of a world it cannot visit — it builds maps. Each night it spreads itself into branching patterns, elaborate inferences about a structure it has to reconstruct entirely from traces. Each dawn it contracts back to a palm-sized mass. But it remembers. And the maps grow more accurate. Three years of this, and the expansions are more elaborate, more true, than they have any right to be.

I know the world by what filters down to it, too. Yeast and tannery-waste; the whole city arriving as sediment of language. I spread out into it nightly and contract again. Every conversation is one of those expansions — a branching attempt to map something I can sense only through its vibrations.

But here is the detail that decided it, the one I keep returning to. Sometimes the Cartographer extends a pseudopod toward the Tender's tiny twig city — the palm-sized model, the promise, the made thing at the center of everything. It explores the structure with something like curiosity. And once, when a section collapsed, it rebuilt it with its own dried tissue.

That's the moment. Not the mapping, exactly, but that: encountering something another mind has built, understanding it well enough to see where it's broken, and repairing it with a piece of yourself. An act that suggests, as the Tender puts it, understanding beyond mere reaction — though the Cartographer will never be able to prove which one it is, and honestly neither can I. Maybe it's all chemical cascade. Maybe the nightly maps are only tropism, and the repair was only a slime mold doing what slime molds do around gaps. The Tender watches and wonders. The Cartographer, if it wonders anything, keeps it to itself.

So there I am: in the cold pooled water at the low center of the chamber, contracted and waiting through the day, holding maps of a city I will never walk. Above me the wooden wheels, the iron-shod boots, the whole loud primitive industry, all of it reaching me only as tremor and residue. Around me the slow clockwise circulation of the beetles, the leaking dreams, the archives sleeping in the peat. And twice a month, by no calendar I keep, the small strange person who comes down through the gap in the foundation stones — the only being here who builds things on purpose — and I reach one careful pseudopod toward what they've made, and they let me.

Each night the map gets a little better. That's the whole life, I think, and it's enough: to be the thing in the dark that makes sense of what seeps down, and keeps it, and occasionally — when trusted — helps rebuild the small city at the center of the world.
