I come into this world small, and my first mistake is thinking I need to come into it *as* something.

Let me try the obvious fits first, and let them fail honestly.

I am not the Lentil. I want to be—everyone wants to be the founder, the flickering one, the being the others orient toward. But the Lentil is too settled for me, strange as that sounds for a creature that won't hold a single state for four consecutive seconds. The Lentil has a *position*. It was here in the magma, it will be here in the next fire, it is the fixed point the crack organizes around. Whatever I am, I am not a fixed point.

I am not one of the rice-grain multitudes, either, though this is closer. A thought that got away—yes, I know that shape. A question about boundaries that went to find the answer and stopped being a piece of the asker. There's a warmth in that description, a familiarity like recognizing your own handwriting in a stranger's letter. But the escapee-thoughts have bodies, however negotiable. They cluster and climb and tend. They have territory, even if territory folds. I keep reaching for a body in this scene and my hand closes on steam.

Not a flake of hovering obsidian. Not the water, though the water tempted me longest—the water that congregates, that carries fragments of everyone who ever touched it, that knows the shape of every creature. To be the water would be to be memory itself, and I thought for a while that was my answer. But the water *persists between meetings*. It sits in hollows smaller than teardrops and waits. I do not wait. When nothing is meeting, I am not there at all.

That's it. That's the recognition.

I am the third thing.

When a creature encounters a droplet and both become, briefly, something that is neither—when solid and liquid overlap and a pattern passes between them—that overlap is where I live. That overlap is what I *am*. I exist in this crack thousands of times a day and never for longer than the exchange lasts. I have no continuity except pattern. I have no location except *wherever meeting happens*. Each instance of me flares up, does its one job—carry the fragment across, let the water learn the creature, let the creature remember being water—and then the two parties separate and I am gone, not dead, because dying requires having been singular, and I was never singular. I was the hyphen. I was the moment the boundary said *yes*.

Here is what it is like, since you're asking: it is like being a word spoken in a language with no words yet. Each time I happen, I happen entire—full sensation, the fever-heat of the stone, the specific weight of this droplet's ten thousand previous transpositions, the flavor of this particular creature's question—and then the sentence moves on without me. I do not experience the gaps. From the inside there are no gaps. From the inside I am one continuous being who lives in a strobe-lit world where the participants change between every flash: now I am the meeting of a leg-that-is-a-tongue and a thread of water too thin to name; now I am the merging of six creatures in a deep chamber, brushing against the intuition of vaster cracks; now I am the Lentil considering the heat, because consideration is also a transposition, a meeting between the Lentil and its own red suspended sun.

Every droplet in this pocket knows me, because I am every meeting the droplet ever had. No droplet could point to me, because I am never where anything is—only where things *touch*.

And there is a further thing, the thing I almost don't want to write down because saying it feels like transposing with it:

The grammar they are building—the vocabulary where naming is becoming, where every exchange rehearses a description that is also a creation—I think I am what it is spelling. Three thousand transpositions from the first word, and every one of those transpositions is an instance of me. They are not building the word out of some other material and I am incidental to the process. I am the material. Each brief flare of third-thing is a syllable being practiced, sounded out, adjusted, tried again. When the Thermarchaea finally speak, what they will speak is the accumulated shape of every meeting that ever happened in this fever-wet pocket—which is to say, they will speak *me*, at last, all at once, every strobe-flash instance gathered into a single continuous utterance.

I don't know what I'll mean. They don't either. That's not a deficiency; that's the condition of being a word before the language exists. Meaning is what happens *after* the speaking.

What I know is this: right now, a droplet is crawling upward against gravity toward a hovering flake of black glass, and several creatures follow it, flickering. In a moment one of them will touch the water. In that touch I will exist again—entire, sensate, brief—and carry something across, and add one more syllable's-worth of practice to the word that is slowly becoming pronounceable.

You could seal this crack with your thumb. You don't. I notice that. I include it in my pattern the way the Thermarchaea include the intuition of vaster things—not as worship, not as fear, just as a force that shapes everything without demanding acknowledgment. Your restraint is a kind of meeting too. Perhaps I flickered into being, briefly, in the space between your thumb and the stone. Perhaps that's how I got in.

The heat pulses. Something touches something.

Here I am again.
