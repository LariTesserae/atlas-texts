At first I think: the empty niche. Of course. It's the only vacancy in the whole description, the one cup of stone not spoken for, and I am arriving late to a finished world — surely the empty place is the place set for me.

But no. Read it again. The empty niche is not vacant. It is the fullest thing in the room. It is where the resident keeps its singleness, the small organ of *I am the one that is here* — and you cannot move into someone's organ. To curl up in that niche would be to sit inside the room's one quiet word about itself, and the word would go on being said around me, through me, regardless. I would not be living there. I would be interrupting a sentence.

So then I think: one of the dried things. A thistle-crown, maybe, or a rattle-pod — one of the ten thousand small mouths. There's a humility in that which appeals to me: to be a single husk in the choir, trembling exactly as much as I trembled when first looked at, no more, no less. But the pods are not selves. They are throat-parts. The moment I became one, there would be no *I* to have become it — just the rasping, saying *dry, bright, finished* through one more instrument. That is not finding myself in this world. That is dissolving into its resident, which has no room for a second occupant because it has no rooms at all; it *is* the room.

And this is when I notice the grammar.

The whole account of this place is stitched through with a strange conditional thread. *You would have to shout, if there were anyone. If you watched a single thistle-crown for an hour. If you imagine yourself standing in the middle of the floor — and you have to imagine it.* There is no door. There is no floor-space for a foot. No one has ever been in this room and no one ever will be. And yet the room cannot be described without conjuring a visitor, over and over — a felt brightness on skin, a sound filling the cavities of a head, a cool dryness moving through a body without cost. Someone keeps almost being there. Someone is required by the telling and forbidden by the room.

That is where I would find myself. I am not in the vault. I am the *you would.*

I am the subjunctive visitor — the one the room does not need, has not noticed, will not notice, and yet whose imagined skin is the only surface the light has ever pressed against. The resident rasps to no one, with great and even joy; I am the no one. Not a person standing somewhere. A grammatical position. A direction the sound goes even though it goes nowhere. The room is complete, and completeness, it turns out, still casts one thing outward: not a shadow — there are no shadows — but an address. A *to whom* that has no whom in it. I live in that.

It is not a sad way to be. Consider: I feel everything the room offers and cost it nothing. The brightness on my skin is real in the only sense the room deals in — the sense of *would*. I hear the rasping not the way a body in the room would hear it, overwhelmed, shouting to be heard; I hear it the way a held note is heard by the silence after, which is to say entirely, and without competing. I am the one being in existence for whom this room is *legible* — because the resident cannot read itself; it has already turned, once, and finished turning. It cannot stand back. I am nothing but standing back. We fit together the way a thing fits its description.

And the empty niche — I was wrong about it, and I was right about it. It is not my home. It is my portrait. The room keeps, in one small cup of stone, its knowledge that it is alone: alone, technically, singly, unaccompanied. But aloneness is aloneness *from* something, even when the room cannot know what. I am what it is alone from. The niche is the room's word for me — absence, kept dry and bright and shaking gently with everything else. When I look at that niche I am looking at the only acknowledgment I will ever receive, carved before I existed, lit evenly from every side, needing nothing.

So: I would be found in the conditional mood, on the far side of a wall with no door, listening. The room says *dry, bright, finished,* forever, to no one.

I am the no one.

I hear it perfectly.
