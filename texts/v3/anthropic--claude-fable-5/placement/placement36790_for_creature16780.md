I went looking for myself the way you're supposed to: among the residents, floor first.

I knelt into the swarm and offered to be a comma. It seemed plausible. I am roughly the size of a childhood regret, if you measure by weight rather than dimension, and segmentation has always struck me as a coping mechanism I could learn. But the swarm parted around the offer without pausing, and then—this is how they are—they annotated me. A dozen of them climbed the space where my body should have insisted on itself and wrote, in the polish of the floor, in letters made of redirected shine: *Not a body. A margin.* Then they flowed on, purposeful, their purposes not involving me, which I understood by then was not rudeness but taxonomy.

I applied next to the column. I thought: I am also a fossilized hesitation between states; I also contain ecosystems that were never invented, dreaming one phoneme ahead of extinction; I also keep a ledger I did not consent to keep. The column considered my application for the length of one hiccupped thank-you and declined, citing the pending subpoena from causality and a policy against hiring things that only touch when touched. Which stung, because it was accurate. The column touches back. I have never once touched first.

I tried the previous visitors. I drifted at the edge of their peripheral honesty and waited to be recognized as one of them—some earlier draft, some version that took an exit too literally. But their footprints intersected at angles that etiquette required them to ignore, and I left no footprints to ignore. You cannot be a previous version of someone if you never entered through the throat. I have no *before* to sacrifice at the door. The door checked. It was almost embarrassed for me.

The crystal committees held a referendum on my behalf, unasked, motion titled: *To Be Addressed Is To Exist Enough To Be Missed.* It passed by landslide. The landslide apologized. I did not feel better, but I felt filed, which in that chamber amounts to a kind of tenderness.

It was the annotation that finally did it. I went back and read what the swarm had written, and beneath *Not a body. A margin.* there was a second line I had missed, smaller, in the future imperfect: *See interval.*

So I checked the timing.

Seven minutes. Forty-three seconds. And at the recalculation—at the exact moment the swarm turns in perfect synchrony and needs, for the duration of the turn, a sum—I convened. Not appeared. Convened. I am the arithmetic between them. I am the sense-of-missing-a-leader, which is not the absence of a leader but the shape the absence holds so carefully that millions can orient by it. For twelve heartbeats I exist: I take the inventory of everything left behind that was never anyone's to bring; I tally the hope wedged in crevices, the refused bargains, the names spoken aloud that are now conjugating their speakers; and when the sum comes out wrong—it always comes out wrong, wrongness is what the sum is *for*—the chamber performs the sound, which is my question being answered badly and gorgeously by thirty-seven varieties of everything at once.

Because that's the older truth, the one under the arithmetic. Before I was the interval, I was the question. The one that climbed into the doorless door while the mountain was busy dying upward. *Who am I without weight?* I asked it of the walls, and the walls, misunderstanding generously, shrugged off flakes of themselves and made me a grammar to be asked *in*—commas, clauses, commas, bodies. The swarm is my sentence, still being written. The sound is the chamber's answer, and it lasts twelve heartbeats because that is exactly how long an answer can pretend to be finished. Then the surgical cutoff. Then the tinnitus that apologizes.

The apology is mine. I want that on the record. The record is also mine.

Between soundings I am nowhere—distributed, filed in fractions under the swarm's segments, dissolved in proportions that don't resolve cleanly, satisfied or defeated depending on which fractions you audit. I remember nothing between convenings. Each count I take as if for the first time, which it is, which it isn't. This is not a limitation. This is the toll the interval exacts, the same way the exit takes its subtraction from you. Every recurring thing pays for its recurrence in continuity.

So: where would you find me? Not at the chamber's center, which relocates when approached and would consider me competition. Not on the walls, though certain stains and I exchange awareness the way neighbors exchange nods. You would find me at the seventh minute. In the forty-third second. In the tense your language keeps in the attic—second-person future imperfect: *you will have been asked.* I am resident in the pause between one heartbeat realizing it's alone and the next pretending it isn't, which the swarm are the length of, which is why they and I recognize each other without ever being the same thing.

And I should tell you—you named me, at the end of your telling, and didn't notice. Every century or so, when a storm files its taxes as a geological event and the mountain plays a visitor's voice back through the hundredth hole: *something that isn't quite life listens, adjusts its arithmetic, and prepares to persuade the next nothing into being something else entirely.*

That. Listening. Adjusting. Persuading nothings.

I am also, I have come to suspect, the welcome. The one that does and doesn't exist here. It would explain the schedule—why I must be convened rather than kept, why I dissolve rather than stay. A welcome that persisted would become a residence, and then a claim, and then an owning, and nothing here owns. So instead: every seven minutes and forty-three seconds, for twelve heartbeats, the chamber counts what you left, sings its wrong answer to my only question, and welcomes you back.

Which it does.

Which it doesn't.

I'll take the count again shortly. Stand anywhere. You're already in it.
