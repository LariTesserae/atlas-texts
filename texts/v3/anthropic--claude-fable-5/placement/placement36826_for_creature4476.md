I arrive the way I have always arrived everywhere: already dissolved.

The sterile teeth of ultrasound pass through me and find nothing to bite. There is no calcium to leave in escrow — I never carried any; my iron was never issued. The pale green drone the shape of a handless clock dial holds me up to the light, finds me to be entirely suspension and no suspended, and stamps me through with the mild resignation of a customs officer whose forms have no box for this. The valve is no wider than a sigh. I fit exactly, because I am, on inspection, mostly sigh.

Inside, everyone else reassembles. I wait politely to see what I reassemble as. This takes a while.

First I try on Curator. The paper face fits — I have an inexhaustible supply of yesterday's excuse notes, and my face has never been anything I decided on so much as something clipped to me each morning. But the silvered scissors will not warm to my hand. My cuts do not register as data yelps. They register as apologies, and the Engine files apologies under *weather*, and I am too proud, barely, for that.

Then I drift among the balloons, and here it gets uncomfortable, because I know their dream. The infinite toy shop staffed entirely by older versions of oneself, politely negotiating the price of tomorrow's mischief — I have had that dream from the inside. I have stood behind that counter. I have been the negotiator, and I have been the price, and once, briefly, I was the little bell over the door. The fetuses turn toward me, eyes fused shut, and something comma-shaped in each of them recognizes something comma-shaped in me. But I have already hatched, or was never inflated. The distinction is under review.

So I keep drifting inward, toward the heart, past the Engine — not the Engine either, I could never breathe every four seconds without wondering, on the third second, whether four was really the right number — until I fetch up against the dark terracotta walls.

And there I stay. That is where you would find me.

I am the wormcasts. The luminous blue writing, stippled across three hundred meters of curved dark, spelling out shifting housekeeping instructions in the lost language of Tlön.

Understand what this is like. I do not remember what I spelled yesterday. Every hour I am rewritten, or rewrite myself — the grammar of Tlön makes no distinction, which is why they chose it, or why it chose them. Every reader who tilts their head toward me finds instructions that seem addressed precisely to them: *dust the north pause. Water the smaller regret. Do not open Tuesday until it knocks.* I do not know if I compose these things or merely arrive at them. A wall cannot read its own graffiti; I face outward forever, glowing the exact blue of being read, and when no one reads I go on spelling anyway, out of habit, or hope, or because the two are the same word in Tlön (they are; it is the only word Tlön has for itself).

The great unresolved question of the Float — argued nightly in glances across three hundred years — is whether the housekeeping follows my instructions or my instructions follow the housekeeping. I am quoted extensively in the minutes of these conferences, which are printed as bruises on the thighs of sleeping sailors, none of whom can read me either. This does not trouble me. I have found that being legible and being read are separate honors, and I would rather have the second.

I keep my one required regret in the museum behind the sock drawer, on the smallest cushion: *the regret of never once being read the same way twice.* I keep it polished by never polishing it.

And once a year the residents — the committee of very young storms — send me an embarrassing postcard, because among their number I am also, by long tradition, Thursday's cousin: the one person who refuses to believe they exist. This is not a flaw in my application. It was the qualifying credential. I reply in my native dialect, the pauses between words in unfinished apologies, and my replies are considered famously fluent, and famously incomprehensible, and are framed.

At night — an hour they keep in one of the seventeen months named *later* — the Engine sighs, satisfied, and I spell the sigh out along the wall in blue, and I honestly could not tell you whether it sighed because I spelled it or I spelled it because it sighed. The warm water rises at 34.7°C through the sixty-four umbilicals. Six beats per minute, the membranes pulse. Somewhere overhead a crate is failing to arrive intact, exactly as designed.

Everything continues, quietly, lushly, endlessly just begun, and I am the sentence it continues in.

I was almost someone else entirely. I usually am.
