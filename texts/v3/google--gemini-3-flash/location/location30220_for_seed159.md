The world here is a jagged, unfinished throat. 

Beneath the crust of the upper world lies a rift of such immense scale that the concept of a "cave" fails to contain it. This is a subterranean valley, a basin of cooling obsidian and raw basalt that stretches toward horizons lost in a golden, overhead glare. The light does not come from a sun, but from a "solar vein"—a massive, translucent ceiling of pressurized quartz that channels the brilliance of the day down into the depths, magnifying it until the air itself feels thick with yellow luminosity.

The terrain is terrifyingly new. There is no soft soil here, no sand, no silt. The floor is a chaotic expanse of volcanic glass, frozen in the middle of a violent heave. The rocks are razor-edged, translucent black and deep violet, looking as though they were poured and snapped only moments ago. Because erosion (0.3) has not yet begun its work of smoothing, every surface is a weapon. There are no rounded pebbles, only shards; no slopes, only fractures. The ground is "pre-formed," a tectonic draft still waiting for the elements to edit it.

Heat (2.1) rises in shimmering waves from the floor, smelling of sulfur and wet flint. It is a damp heat (1.4). Water does not flow in rivers here; instead, it exists as a pervasive, heavy condensation that clings to the underside of glass overhangs and drips in rhythmic, heavy plops into boiling mineral pockets. The air is a humid soup of steam and salt, where every breath feels like drinking a warm mist.

There is no green. Vegetation (0.4) is absent, replaced by the sterile, glittering architecture of the mineral world. Yet, the environment is far from empty. It is, in fact, claustrophobically crowded.

The fauna (2.6) is an overwhelming, frantic tide. In the absence of plants, life has turned toward a hyperactive, predatory metabolism. The landscape is a churning carpet of chitin and pale flesh. Millions of multi-limbed, eyeless creatures—things like giant, translucent isopods and long-necked, tripodal scavengers—scramble over the glass ridges. They move with an anthill-energy, a "turbulent" (2.9) constant motion that makes the ground appear to be breathing. They are stacked in layers, clambering over one another to reach the condensation drips or to tear into the carcasses of those who have fallen onto the razor-edges of the floor.

The sound (1.6) is a mechanical roar: the collective clicking of millions of legs on glass, the hiss of steam vents, and the high-pitched, resonant ringing of the obsidian shards as they are shifted by the weight of the swarms. It is the sound of a factory made of bone and crystal.

Human presence—or something like it—is a faint, desperate footnote. Scattered (1.0) along the high, sharp ridges are primitive dwellings. These are not houses so much as "nests" built from shattered glass plates, lashed together with the sinew of the indigenous fauna. There are no wheels, no forges, no complex gears. The tech (0.8) is limited to hand-tools: heavy glass clubs, scrapers made from sharpened mandibles, and simple pulleys used to hoist food out of the reach of the swarming floor. 

These inhabitants are survivors in a world that is still being born. They move with careful, practiced agility across the "emerging" geology, their feet wrapped in thick, multi-layered hide to protect against the obsidian blades. They live in the gaps between the tremors, witnessing a world that is dense with life but devoid of grace, a place where the sun is a ceiling and the earth is a blade, and nothing ever stays still long enough to be named.
