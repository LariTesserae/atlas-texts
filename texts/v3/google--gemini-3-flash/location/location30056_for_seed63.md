This is the Interior of the Hollow Needle, a vertical rift situated near the summit of a jagged, airless peak. It is a space of suffocating intimacy, a cathedral of stone no larger than a village square, yet packed with a density of existence that feels infinite.

The air here is a physical weight. It is dry, bone-dry, devoid of even a hint of vapor. It tastes of pulverized mineral and ancient dust. The temperature is a constant, punishing radiance—roughly 140 degrees Fahrenheit—bleeding out from the deep tectonic throat of the mountain. It is a heat that does not shimmer because there is no moisture to catch the light; it simply bakes the silence into something brittle.

In the near-total darkness, the eye struggles to find a horizon. There is no sky, only the jagged, obsidian ceiling of the rift hundreds of feet above, and the walls that lean inward like the ribs of a dying giant. The stone is ancient, polished to a dull, greasy sheen by eons of friction rather than water. There are no rivers here, no pools, not even a damp patch of moss. The world is built of basalt, pumice, and the desiccated remains of what came before.

But the most striking feature of the Needle is the Floor.

You cannot see the ground. From wall to wall, the space is entirely occupied by the *Kith*—a teeming, multi-layered carpet of soft-bodied, eyeless fauna. They are the size of flatbreads, velvety to the touch, overlapping like shingles on a roof. There are millions of them. They do not fight for space; they simply exist in a state of total saturation. To move through this place is not to walk on earth, but to wade through a living, shifting sea of warm, dry flesh.

The Kith do not make noise. The sound of the Needle is a 0.9—a muffled, rhythmic thrumming, like a heartbeat heard through a thick mattress. It is the collective sound of a billion tiny legs scraping against obsidian and the soft, dry rustle of chitinous plates sliding over one another. There are no cries, no calls, no songs. Only the sound of the swarm breathing in unison.

Scattered through this living mass are the Built remains: obsidian pillars, perfectly hexagonal, rising like fingers from the shifting floor. They are not tech; they are primitive, labored-over monuments, perhaps carved by hands that vanished a million years ago. These pillars are covered in faint, shallow grooves—tally marks or maps of stars that no longer exist. The Kith flow around these pillars like slow-motion lava, their bodies polishing the ancient carvings until the stone feels like silk.

There is no vegetation. Not a blade of grass, not a speck of lichen. The energy of this world is purely thermal and biological. The Kith feed on the radiant heat of the mountain and the fine, mineral soot that falls from the ceiling. Their life cycle is the only dynamic force: they grow, they dry out, they crumble into a fine gray powder that the others then absorb or trample into the floor.

The movement here is constant. The Kith are always shifting, a slow-motion roil that keeps the density uniform. If a gap opens, it is filled within seconds. The "weather" of this place is a cycle of heat-pulses from below, causing the entire floor to rise and fall by several inches in a long, slow tide of living bodies.

It is a claustrophobic paradise. It is a place where the concept of "individual" has been eroded by the sheer pressure of "many." The heat is the only god, the darkness is the only veil, and the silence is the only language spoken by a world that has no room left for anything but itself.
