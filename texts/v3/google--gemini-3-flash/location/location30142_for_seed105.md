Here, the sky is not an absence, but a weight. You exist within a colossal, hanging ocean trapped against the jagged peaks of a mountain range that has never known the touch of air. The water is gelid, hovering just above the freezing point, dense with minerals and a preternatural saltiness that makes the darkness feel thick, like velvet pressed against the skin.

### The Terrain and Scale
The landscape is one of verticality and shadow. You are standing—or drifting—upon a high-altitude plateau, a massive lithic shelf that sits thousands of feet above the unseen valley floors. The ground is composed of dark, igneous basalt and obsidian, worn smooth by eons of slow, churning currents. It is an "established" world; the erosion has reached a state of equilibrium where the rocks no longer shatter, but pulse with the slow, rhythmic grinding of the deep. 

The scale is vast. Through the gloom, you can perceive the silhouettes of neighboring peaks, miles away, connected by invisible bridges of cold current. It is a regional expanse of drowned highlands, a continent of peaks held in a permanent, watery midnight.

### The Vegetation and Dynamics
Despite the darkness and the cold, the world is stiflingly lush. In the absence of animal life, the flora has claimed every ecological niche, evolving into forms that blur the line between mineral and biological. 

Massive, translucent forests of *Glass-Kelp* rise from the basalt, their stalks reaching hundreds of feet toward the crushing ceiling of the upper thermocline. These plants do not sway; they vibrate. The water is hyper-active, moved by deep-seated thermal vents and the gravitational pull of a heavy moon, creating a constant, roaring "wind" of liquid. The vegetation has adapted by becoming rigid and resonant. 

The *Chime-Oaks*—great, branching structures of calcified cellulose—clash their limbs together in the current. This is the source of the sound: a deafening, percussive symphony of wooden thunder and metallic clinking that echoes through the water. It is a loud world, a landscape of constant, structural noise where the plants sing the songs of the currents.

### The Light and Atmosphere
The light is a bruised, necrotic purple, hovering at the very edge of visibility. It does not come from above. Instead, it emanates from the "breathing" of the vegetation. Certain mosses, clinging to the leeward side of the rocks, pulse with a dim, bioluminescent rhythm, timed to the movement of the tides. There are no shadows, only gradients of blackness. The cold is a physical presence, a leaching force that demands constant movement or total stasis.

### The Built and The Tech
Scattered across the plateau are the remains of a primitive, inexplicable intent. These are not cities, but "markers." You find rings of standing stones, each twelve feet tall, lashed together with braided tendons of dried kelp. They are anchored to the sea floor by crude, hand-chipped stone weights.

There are small, beehive-shaped huts made of stacked slate, their interiors empty and scoured clean by the silt. There is no evidence of fire—how could there be?—but there are tools: scrapers made of obsidian, heavy mallets of basalt, and long, barbed spears made of sharpened bone-wood. They are "scattered" and "sparse," suggesting a population that was either very small or purely migratory, moving through these highlands like ghosts before vanishing entirely. 

The tech is lithic and manual. There are no gears, no wires, only the clever application of weight, tension, and friction. A "primitive" mastery of the dark.

### The Absence
The most striking feature is the silence of the soul. There are no fish here. No crustaceans scuttle across the rocks; no leviathans move in the heights. The "ecology" is a closed loop of plants consuming the minerals of the rock and the waste of their own decay. The "active" nature of the world is purely botanical and geophysical. The plants grow with a predatory speed, their tendrils lashing out to claim space, their seeds exploding in the dark with the sound of small cannons, but there is no heartbeat in the water. 

It is a world of vigorous, violent growth, entirely indifferent to the concept of the animal. It is a garden that has forgotten the gardener and never known the pest, thriving in a cold, loud, and beautiful dark.
