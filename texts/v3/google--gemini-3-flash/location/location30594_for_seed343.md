This is the world of the Vertical Hallows, a place where the concept of "ground" has been forgotten for eons.

Here, the landscape is composed of colossal, calcified spires of lignin and cellulose—ancient, petrified trunks the size of mountain ranges that rise from a lightless abyss into the blinding clarity of the upper atmosphere. These are not trees in the modern sense; they are the skeletal remains of a primordial flora that has settled into its final, stable state. The erosion of ages has smoothed their surfaces into vertical deserts of grey-green bark, carved with deep, wind-hollowed flutes that stretch for miles.

### The Terrain and Scale
The scale is terrifyingly open. You stand on a "shelf"—a protruding bracket fungus the size of a village—and look out across a gulf of five miles of empty air to the next spire. Because the density of the world is so low (0.5), the visual field is dominated by the void. It is a landscape of massive presences separated by vast clarifies of nothingness. The elevation is extreme; the air is thin but kept warm by a strange, constant thermal updraft that rises from the unseen depths, smelling of fermented sap and ancient mulch.

### The Substrate and Moisture
Everything you touch is biological. There is no stone here, no silt, no sand. The "geology" is layered bark, hardened resins, and tangled mats of air-roots. Water is a precious, intermittent passenger. It does not fall as rain so much as it manifests as "cloud-drifts"—dense, slow-moving banks of fog that snag on the spires, drenching everything in a sudden, heavy condensation. Mossy depressions in the bark act as riverine pockets, holding small, tea-colored pools of water for a few days before they evaporate or are sucked dry by the spire’s own thirst.

### The Sound and Fauna
The silence of the void is an illusion. The spires are screaming. The fauna is so overwhelming (2.9) that the very air vibrates with the friction of life. Trillions of iridescent, membrane-winged insects and hollow-boned, featherless flyers inhabit the furrows of the bark. Their collective calls, mating hums, and territorial shrieks create a deafening, constant roar—a biological white noise that makes speech difficult. 

Large, slow-moving mega-fauna—things resembling six-legged crustaceans with the grace of lemurs—clamber across the vertical faces, their hooked limbs clicking rhythmically against the petrified wood. You see them in the distance, tiny specks against the grey spires, moving in vast, migratory ribbons that never end. The ecosystem is in high-speed motion; predators the size of kites dive into the voids, snatching prey from the air and retreating into the deep cracks of the trunk-mountains.

### The Built and The Tech
Human presence—or what passes for it—is scattered and precarious. Small, woven hamlets are tucked into the rain-shadows of massive bark-flaps. These structures are built from scavenged chitin, cured leather, and braided vine. There is no fire here; the air is too rich with spores, and the wood too precious to burn. 

The technology is strictly manual and primitive. Bone-needles, stone-scrapers (traded from the lightless depths far below), and sophisticated rope-work define their existence. There are no wheels, for there is nowhere to roll them. Life is lived in a series of harnesses and hand-holds. The inhabitants use long, hollowed reeds to harvest condensation from the air and craft tools from the discarded carapaces of the great sky-flyers.

### The Atmosphere
The light is steady and normal—a bright, unfiltered sun that illuminates the dust motes and drifting seeds in the great gaps between the spires. But the dynamic of the place is one of constant, rhythmic movement. The wind whistles through the eroded flutes of the wood, the clouds snag and release, and the animal population swells and ebbs in frantic cycles of birth and predation.

It is a world that feels finished, as if the great biological experiment of the universe has already reached its climax and is now simply vibrating in place. You are a guest in a cathedral of ancient wood, standing on the edge of a five-mile drop, surrounded by the deafening song of a billion creatures that do not know you exist.
