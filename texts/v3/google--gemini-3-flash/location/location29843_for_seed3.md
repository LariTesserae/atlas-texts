In this place, the horizon does not exist. There is only the Vertical. 

We are at the ceiling of a world still in the process of deciding its own geometry. The peaks here are not mountains in the traditional sense; they are raw, un-weathered obsidian teeth, jagged and black, rising miles into a bruised, perma-twilight sky. Because the erosion is so young—embryonic, really—the stone has no softness. There are no scree slopes, no rounded boulders, only razor-edged facets that look as though they were snapped into existence seconds ago. Yet, they rise with an agonizing slowness, a geological pulse so rhythmic and deep that it manifests not as movement, but as a constant, low-frequency thrum that vibrates in the marrow of one’s bones.

The air is cool and heavy with the scent of wet iron and crushed mint. It is not raining, but the atmosphere is perpetually saturated. Mist clings to the underside of every overhang, eventually gathering into fat, heavy droplets that fall with a deliberate, rhythmic *plink* into the abyss. This intermittent moisture feeds a lush, surreal flora that defies the darkness. Iridescent mosses, thick as carpets, bridge the gaps between the raw stone, glowing with a faint, internal bioluminescence that provides the only real light. Fern-like structures with translucent fronds reach out from the vertical faces, shivering whenever the industrial engines of the city below vent their heat.

The built environment is an impossible feat of industrial desperation. This is a city of "Bolt-ons"—a massive, urban sprawl of iron and brass that has been literally screwed, riveted, and chained into the un-eroded rock. Great tiered districts hang over the void like rusted honeycombs. Massive, soot-stained factories are suspended by tectonic-grade cables, their chimneys belching out thick, pale steam that mixes with the mountain fog. This is a place of heavy industry: the sound of massive pistons, the groan of turning gears the size of cathedrals, and the hiss of high-pressure hydraulics echo perpetually off the stark rock faces. 

Despite the industrial density of these urban pockets, the scale of the world makes them feel sparse. You can stand on a balcony made of corrugated steel and look across a three-mile chasm to see another cluster of glowing windows and smokestacks, a lonely archipelago of fire and metal in a sea of silent, rising stone.

The fauna here has adapted to the "Reality Breakdown" that plagues these altitudes. Great, six-winged scavengers with oily plumage drift on the thermals created by the factories. They move with a stuttering grace, their bodies occasionally flickering in and out of visual phase, appearing in two places at once before snapping back into a single form. Small, multi-limbed amphibians crawl along the wet rivets of the city’s underside, their skin mimicking the texture of rusted iron. There is an abundance of life, but it is a strange, quiet life that understands the instability of the ground it walks upon.

In this realm, the laws of physics are suggestions undergoing a slow revision. Gravity is directional but inconsistent; a dropped wrench might fall straight down for a hundred feet, then suddenly curve upward to orbit a specific jagged peak. The "Slow" dynamic of the world means that while a bridge might take a hundred years to settle into its final shape, the process of its settling is accompanied by a deafening, metallic shriek that lasts for decades.

It is a world of dim, beautiful gloom, where the roar of industry competes with the groaning of newborn mountains, and where the damp, cool air carries the promise of a world that is still, very patiently, inventing itself.
