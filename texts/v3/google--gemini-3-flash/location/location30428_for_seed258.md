The world does not have a floor; it has a loom.

Imagine a vast, regional expanse, miles across, where the concept of "earth" or "rock" has never existed. Instead, the substrate is a dense, high-altitude mat of interconnected cellulose—a woven continent of moss, thick vascular vines, and aerated pith that spans the horizon. This is the *Aethel-Mat*. It is warm, soft, and perpetually saturated. Because the elevation is high, the air is thin but heavy with moisture, creating a paradoxical sensation of breathing in a warm, wet cloud that tastes of sugar and chlorophyll.

The landscape is "built," but not by hands. Every feature of the terrain—the soaring arches that bridge twenty-mile gaps, the hollowed, bulbous cathedrals that serve as landmarks—has been grown with the deliberate intent of architecture. There are no tools here, no metal, no fire. The "buildings" are gargantuan, translucent gourds the size of hills, their walls made of amber-colored resin and living bark. They are spaced far apart; the density is low, leaving massive, breathtaking voids of open air between these organic monoliths. You can stand on a spongy ridge and look out over a hundred miles of empty, green-tinted space, seeing only three or four colossal, living towers rising from the mat like the ribs of a buried giant.

Water is the primary inhabitant. It does not fall as rain in the traditional sense; rather, the atmosphere is so heavily saturated that water simply "emerges" onto surfaces. The rocks—which are actually nodules of hyper-dense, calcified wood—are constantly weeping. Crystal-clear fluid seeps from every vertical surface, running in silent, shallow sheets over the mossy plains. Because the world is "emerging" and lacks erosion, there are no carved riverbeds or jagged canyons. The water flows where it likes, forming wide, still lagoons that reflect the constant, unwavering light of a sun that never seems to move.

The sound is the most overwhelming characteristic. It is a loud, steady, biological roar. It is not the sound of wind or machines, but the collective sound of trillions of gallons of sap being pumped through the vascular systems of the world-mat. It is a deep, thrumming vibration that resonates in the marrow of your bones—the sound of a planet-sized heart beating at a low, frantic frequency. It never wavers, never rises or falls in pitch. It is a permanent, static wall of noise that anchors the soul to the present moment.

Despite the abundance of life-sustaining water and warmth, fauna is nearly non-existent. Occasionally, one might see a "drifter"—a translucent, gelatinous organism the size of a house, floating aimlessly in the heavy air, tethered to the towers by thin, silken threads. They do not hunt; they filter the wet air. They are as stable and unchanging as the terrain itself.

The world feels embryonic. The surfaces are smooth, unscarred by time or weather. There is no decay here, only growth that has reached a state of perfect, frozen equilibrium. The light is a steady 2.1—a bright, diffused radiance that comes from everywhere at once, casting almost no shadows. It illuminates the veins in the giant leaves and the pulsing glow of the sap-reservoirs deep within the towers.

It is a place of immense, quiet loneliness, despite the deafening roar of the sap. The scale is so large, and the features so sparse, that a traveler feels like a microbe walking across the skin of a ripening fruit. Nothing changes. The weeping rocks continue to weep; the sap continues to roar; the great green arches continue to reach toward the static light. It is a masterpiece of biological construction that has just been finished, and now, it simply exists, waiting for a history that may never arrive.
