The horizon does not exist here. Instead, there is only the **Megabasin**, a continental depression of such immense scale that the atmosphere within it has its own tides. 

At a low elevation, where the planetary crust has been excavated or worn down into a flat, endless plain, the world is a solid, packed mass of hyper-dense infrastructure. There is no soil. There is no green. The vegetation parameter is near zero, replaced by a mineral and metallic metabolism. The ground is a complex, weathered crust of layered circuitry, obsidian-slick conduits, and throbbing heat-sinks that have been eroded by eons of heavy condensation into smooth, river-carved canyons of glass and alloy.

It is perpetually warm—a humid, heavy 2.0 heat that radiates from the "ground" itself. Because the technology here is beyond modern comprehension, the city (if it can be called that) functions like a biological organ. It sweats. Water (1.7) is everywhere, though not as oceans or lakes. It exists as a constant, weeping moisture that clings to every vertical surface. Heavy, warm mists roll through the narrow gaps between structures, and the atmosphere is so saturated that it feels like walking through a damp lung. Runoff trickles down the sides of monumental pylons in rhythmic pulses, fueling the "fauna" that thrives in the dark.

The sound (2.7) is a physical weight. It is a deafening, low-frequency thrumming—the vibration of a trillion processors, the roar of massive coolant vents, and the tectonic grinding of the city shifting its own weight. It is a sound so loud it becomes a silence you feel in your marrow, a constant acoustic pressure that never relents.

This is a world of maximum density (3.0). There is no "empty" space. Every cubic meter is occupied by functioning matter. The architecture isn't built of discrete buildings, but is a single, continuous, porous mountain of technology. It is "urban" in the way a coral reef is urban—a hyper-organized, suffocatingly crowded sprawl where every surface is a substrate for something else.

In the absence of plants, the ecology is driven by a frantic, turbulent fauna (1.5). These are not animals as we know them; they are lithovores and electro-parasites. Great, multi-segmented carapaces, the color of bruised oil, scuttle across the weeping walls, drinking the mineral-rich condensate. Swarms of humming, translucent flyers—more like kinetic energy patterns than birds—dart through the steam, their wings vibrating at frequencies that harmonize with the city’s roar. They feed on the leakage of the beyond-modern tech, clustering around glowing nodes of "post-physics" energy that defy gravity, suspending spheres of heavy water in mid-air.

The dynamics (2.9) are hyperactive. The landscape is in a state of constant, turbulent flux. A sector that is a marketplace of shifting geometric plates one hour may be a churning vat of bio-mechanical sludge the next. The city reconfigures itself in real-time to manage the heat and the data-flow, creating a "Solaris-like" instability. You might see a bridge begin to grow across a chasm, only to be recycled into a fountain of sparks before it reaches the other side. 

Light (2.1) is oddly normal—a bright, diffuse glow that seems to come from the mist itself, rather than a sun. It reveals the "established" nature of the place; despite the chaos, this world is old. The edges of the great conduits are rounded by age; the metallic surfaces are pitted by centuries of chemical rain. It is a settled-form machine, an ancient, roaring, wet, and crowded equilibrium.

To stand here is to be a microbe on a circuit board during a thunderstorm. The scale is so vast that you cannot see the walls of the basin, only the receding layers of construction, disappearing into the warm, white haze of the weeping atmosphere, while the deafening heartbeat of the world shakes the mist from the air.
