This is a world of unrelenting brilliance and stagnant, heavy heat. There is no horizon of soil or rock; the entirety of the terrain—every valley, every low-lying basin, every sharp-edged ridge—is a constructed artifice of fired clay and hand-hewn stone. It is a vast, regional-scale monument, a landscape-sized floor of interlocking terracotta tiles and obsidian slabs that stretch as far as the eye can see, yet the craftsmanship is raw, displaying the tactile, uneven marks of hand-tools and thumb-pressed joints.

The light is a constant, vertical noon. It does not move. Shadows are clipped short, dark puddles beneath the structures that never lengthen or retreat. The air is thick and liquid, smelling of baked mud and wet ferns.

### The Materiality of the Basin
The ground is a geometric marsh. Water does not soak into this world; it sits in shallow, perfectly square cisterns and flows through narrow, hand-carved runnels that intersect at right angles. Because the world is "young" in its erosion, the edges of the bricks are sharp enough to draw blood, and the decorative grooves in the stone are crisp, as if the mason finished them moments ago. Yet, despite the heat and the constant moisture, there is no sign of wear, no rounding of corners, no crumbling of the mortar. 

Water seeps upward from the joints between the tiles, a rhythmic weeping that keeps the surface slick and shimmering. It pools in wide, flat plazas, creating mirrors that reflect the blinding sky. The atmosphere is a perpetual steam-bath; the moisture hangs in the air as a visible, golden haze that never coalesces into rain, nor does it ever evaporate.

### The Lush Intervals
The vegetation is not wild, yet it is overwhelming in its vitality. It grows from "growth-wells"—perfectly circular apertures in the masonry floor, spaced far apart. Within these wells, the density of life is terrifying. Giant, emerald-hued ferns and broad-leafed vines burst upward, climbing nothing but the thick air itself, or draping over the low, sharp walls of the terracotta landscape. 

Because the density of these groves is sparse, the landscape feels like a series of vibrant green islands separated by vast, white-hot oceans of masonry. The plants are succulent and heavy with sap, their leaves always beaded with water that never falls. There is no wind to rustle them; they stand in a frozen, lush equilibrium.

### The Inhabitants and the Quiet
Fauna is abundant but strangely muted. Iridescent, heavy-bodied insects the size of a human palm cling to the undersides of the stone eaves, their wings vibrating with a low hum that is felt rather than heard. Pale, slow-moving lizards with skin like wet porcelain crawl over the sharp edges of the bricks, their movements synchronized and rhythmic. Occasionally, large, flightless birds with plumage the color of bruised fruit wade through the shallow water-plazas, their steps making no splash. 

The soundscape is a heavy, pressurized silence. The usual cacophony of a lush environment is absent, replaced by a dull, subsonic thrum—the sound of the world’s own internal pressure.

### The Breakdown of Reality
As one moves across the plazas, the rules of space begin to fray. The "total built" nature of the world means there is no basement, no sub-strata; if you were to pry up a tile, you would find another tile beneath it, and another, an infinite stack of masonry reaching down into the heat. 

Perspective does not behave. A cluster of ferns that appears to be a mile away may be reached in a dozen steps, while a low wall that seems within arm's reach remains eternally distant. The water in the runnels sometimes flows uphill, or flows in two directions at once without a ripple. Time has no purchase here; the world is a snapshot of a finished creation that refuses to age. The hand-tools—simple stone hammers and wooden trowels—lie scattered on the tiles, looking brand new, as if the builders simply stepped into the golden haze a second ago and will return in the next breath. 

It is a hot, wet, beautiful cage of clay, where the "natural" has been entirely replaced by a primitive, eternal architecture that refuses to decay.
