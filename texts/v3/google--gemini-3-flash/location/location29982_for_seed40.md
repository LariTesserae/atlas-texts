The horizon does not curve; it terminates in a sharp, geometric fracture. 

This is a world of total construction, a regional sprawl of stacked, interlocking industrial modules that extend as far as the eye can see, elevated into a thin, cool atmosphere where the light is a constant, piercing white. There is no soil here, only the "new" substrate—fresh-cast alloys, unoxidized brass, and polished slabs of synthetic stone. The edges are razor-sharp, untouched by the softening hands of time or weather. It is a landscape that has just been finished, or perhaps one that is constantly being overwritten by its own internal logic.

The scale is vast, yet the density is claustrophobic. Massive, cathedral-sized pistons and cooling towers are packed tight against one another, separated only by narrow, light-filled chasms. These structures are not stagnant; they are part of a slow, rhythmic mechanism. A gear the size of a city block might rotate only a single degree every hour, its movement detected not by sound, but by the gradual shift of the shadows it casts.

Despite the industrial magnitude, the world is remarkably quiet. The air is thick with a muffled, expectant silence, broken only by the occasional rhythmic *clack* of a relay switch or the distant, subterranean groan of shifting plates.

Water exists here as a persistent, incidental presence. It is the condensation that clings to the undersides of brass overhangs, dripping in slow, measured beads onto the plates below. It is the dampness found in the narrow ventilation alleys where the air is coolest. Occasionally, a fine, mist-like rain falls—not from clouds, but from the overflow of massive, elevated cisterns hidden somewhere in the upper tiers of the sprawl. This moisture pools in shallow, geometric depressions, creating miniature, crystalline lakes that reflect the unrelenting brightness of the sky.

Vegetation is a rare, stubborn interloper. It does not grow in groves, but in singular, isolated instances: a clump of wire-thick, silver-leaved ferns sprouting from a seam in a pressure vessel, or a patch of neon-green moss coating a damp cooling fin. These plants seem less like biological accidents and more like a different form of circuitry, slow-growing and patient.

The fauna, however, is abundant and strangely suited to this metallic ecology. Swarms of glass-winged insects, transparent as water, hover over the condensation pools, their wings vibrating with a high-frequency hum that falls just below the threshold of human hearing. Small, multi-limbed creatures with shells like hammered copper skitter across the vertical faces of the machines, their movements jerky and precise. They move in vast, coordinated migrations across the regional expanse, thousands of them flowing like a river of metal over the motionless gears.

It is in the "rules" of this place that the reality breakdown becomes apparent. The architecture obeys laws that are not entirely Euclidean. A staircase might ascend for fifty steps only to arrive at the floor it started on. A massive cooling pipe might pass through a solid wall without a hole, the materials simply overlapping as if they were ghosts to one another. Gravity is high-elevation and thin, but it is also inconsistent; in certain pockets of the sprawl, the condensation drips upward, or the fauna walks effortlessly on the underside of a platform, oblivious to the void below.

The light is the most dominant feature. It is a shadowless, omnidirectional glare that seems to emanate from the very atmosphere itself. It catches on the sharp, uneroded edges of the world, creating a shimmering, high-contrast environment where every bolt, every seam, and every microscopic scratch on the metal is rendered with agonizing clarity. 

Everything is new. Everything is made. The world is a fresh, bright machine, vast and dense, ticking toward a purpose that unfolds with the agonizing, beautiful slowness of a glacier. It is a masterpiece of industry, populated by millions of living things, all breathing in the cool, damp silence of a reality that has forgotten how to be natural.
