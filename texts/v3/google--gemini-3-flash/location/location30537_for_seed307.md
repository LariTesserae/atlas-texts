This is the interior of a **Glass-Lung**: a vertical, razor-sharp fissure no wider than a cupboard, hidden deep within the geothermal vents of a nameless, sky-piercing crag. 

### The Terrain and Scale
The environment is a vertical pocket, an intimate and claustrophobic chimney that rises hundreds of feet upward but offers barely three feet of width at its most spacious point. It is a "hidden" world, a micro-ecosystem tucked into the marrow of a massive, geologically young spire. Because it has only recently emerged from the tectonic pressure below, the walls are not smoothed by age; they are jagged, crystalline obsidian and translucent quartz, honed to a molecular sharpness. To touch the wall is to be cut. There is no soil, no sediment, no soft landing. Everything is an edge.

### The Atmosphere and Water
The heat is a physical weight—2.9 on the scale, a stifling, humid 140 degrees Fahrenheit. The air is not gas so much as a suspended liquid state. Water does not rain here; it "weeps" from the facets of the stone and then, due to the fraying laws of physics in this pocket, it crawls. You might see a globule of boiling mineral water detach from a crystal point and drift horizontally across the gap, or slide upward against gravity to merge with a shimmering pool on the ceiling. 

The humidity is so high that the air feels viscous. It carries the scent of sulfur and scalded salt. Light enters from some impossible source—perhaps refracted through miles of translucent rock—filling the fissure with a "normal," steady, amber glow that reveals every glistening detail.

### The Absence of Flora
There is nothing green. No moss clings to the obsidian, no lichen crusts the quartz. The world is entirely mineral and animal. In place of vines, there are long, pulsing translucent cords of nerve-tissue; in place of flowers, there are sessile, filter-feeding polyps that bloom with iridescent flesh when they sense a heat-gradient shift. It is a post-biological or perhaps pre-biological landscape where the "plant" niche has been entirely colonised by soft, wet, animal life.

### The Overwhelming Fauna
The density of life here is staggering. Every square inch of the razor-sharp rock is occupied. Soft-bodied, multi-limbed creatures the size of human fingers—resembling a cross between a nudibranch and a tarsier—cling to the vertical faces with suction-cup digits that are impervious to the glass-edges. They are silent. Thousands of them shift, ripple, and pulse in a collective, slow-motion dance. 

They do not make a sound. The only noise is the faint, rhythmic *thrum* of the earth’s heat and the wet *slap* of a water droplet hitting a membrane. 

### The Tech and the Weirdness
Despite the lack of "civilization," there is a primitive industry here. The creatures use tools. You might see a cluster of them using sharp obsidian flakes, bound together with their own hardened silk, to scrape mineral deposits from the walls. They use these scrapers to "harvest" the crystalline sweat of the mountain, piling the translucent shards into tiny, precariously balanced cairns. There are no buildings, but there is a sense of *arrangement*—a manual craft dedicated to some inscrutable, non-human purpose.

Reality here is thin. At this elevation and heat, the "rules" of the world have begun to dissolve. You might look up the chimney and see the top of the fissure looping back to become the floor you are standing on. You might see your own shadow detached from your body, flickering a second behind your movements, or see the water droplets crystallizing into solid ice for a fraction of a second before exploding back into boiling steam. Space is folded here; the three-foot width of the crack feels, if you stretch your arms out, like it contains miles of depth that your eyes cannot quite resolve.

### The Dynamic
The world is in constant, quiet motion. It is a predatory ecosystem, but a slow one. A creature might spend four hours inching toward a mineral pocket, only to be enveloped by a larger, gelatinous mass that descends from the ceiling like a falling shroud. Everything is eating, everything is breathing the heavy, wet heat, and everything is working with those tiny, primitive stone scrapers, carving at the very walls that house them, as if trying to excavate their way out of reality itself.
