The space is a hollowed-out sphere, no larger than a cathedral, yet it feels terrifyingly vast because there is almost nothing in the center. It is a geode of living velvet. The "ground" is not stone or soil, but a pressurized, interwoven floor of dark moss and thick, fibrous mycelium that yields slightly underfoot like a firm mattress. This is the **Hollow-Grown Foundry**.

The air is cool and smells of crushed mint and wet iron. It is damp, but not rainy; the moisture manifests as a persistent, clinging condensation that beads on the surfaces of the "machines." These machines are the primary feature of the environment—huge, biological engines grown from the floor and walls. They are industrial in their function but purely botanical in their anatomy. You see massive, pulsating galls the size of houses, connected by translucent, vein-like pipes that throb with the passage of thick, bioluminescent sap. Gear-shaped flowers, rigid and serrated like obsidian, grind against one another with a low, wet friction, powered by the hydraulic pressure of deep-rooted pumps.

Though the scale is that of a small town, the density is incredibly low. The industrial structures are clustered around the inner shell of the sphere, leaving a massive, yawning void in the middle where gravity seems to lose its grip. In this central emptiness, rogue filaments of vine drift like seaweed in a current, occasionally sparking with static electricity.

The "urban" layout is organized but alien. There are tiered walkways made of hardened resin and flat, shelf-like leaves that form a spiral city around the perimeter. Small, hive-like dwellings are budded directly into the walls, their entrances dilated like pupils. Everything is brand new; the textures are slick, embryonic, and free of the scars of time. There is no dust here, only the glistening sheen of something recently born.

Reality, however, is fraying at the edges. In certain pockets of the Foundry, the botanical machines don't just process sap—they process the dimensions. You might see a gear-flower turning in two directions at once, or a translucent pipe that contains a river of water flowing toward a point that doesn't exist. Occasionally, a section of the mossy floor will simply cease to be solid, becoming a localized patch of two-dimensional shadow that you must leap over, or the temperature will drop to a freezing point in a single, inch-wide pillar of air.

The silence is the most dominant sound. It is a "mostly quiet" that makes the industrial activity feel secretive. There is the occasional *thwump-hiss* of a sap-piston, the rhythmic clicking of beetle-like laborers, and the distant, muffled sound of rushing liquid. 

The fauna is everywhere but remains largely unobtrusive. Thousands of spindly, multi-jointed harvesters—creatures that look like a cross between an orchid and an opilione spider—skitter along the vein-pipes, tending to the botanical engines. Heavy, slow-moving moths with wings like damp parchment hang from the ceiling-growths, their eyes glowing with a faint, dying ember light, providing the only illumination in the near-total darkness.

The landscape is deeply dynamic. The Foundry is constantly growing, reconfiguring its own architecture. A walkway might be retracted into the wall over the course of an hour, digested and regrown elsewhere to suit a new industrial need. It is a city that is also a single, shivering organism, working toward an unknown production goal in the cool, damp dark of its own interior. As you watch, a new engine-bud begins to swell from the floor, its translucent skin stretching tight to reveal the churning, leafy gears forming inside—a fresh piece of infrastructure emerging from the void.
