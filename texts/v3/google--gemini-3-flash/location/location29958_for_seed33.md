In this place, the horizon does not exist. Instead, the world is a vertical delirium of parched, white-grey pillars, each the size of a mountain range but no wider than a cathedral. They rise out of a lightless abyss and ascend into a dim, violet-tinged haze that never clarifies into a sun. 

The air is cool, dry enough to crack skin in minutes, and carries the faint, metallic scent of cold flint. There is no water here—not as a liquid, not as a vapor. The "clouds" that drift between the pillars are fine, particulate veils of pulverized mica, shimmering weakly in the twilight.

This is a landscape of impossible architecture. Though there are no tools, no gears, and no electricity, the pillars are densely "built." They are honeycombed with millions of rectangular apertures, tiered balconies of smooth, fused mineral, and winding external stairs that spiral for miles without a single railing. This infrastructure is not placed *upon* the rock; it *is* the rock. The pillars have grown into the shape of a city, an urban density achieved through a slow, geological mimicry. It is a settlement of ghosts or intentions, vast and organized, yet utterly hollow. 

Because the density of matter is so low, the gaps between these vertical cities are terrifyingly wide. You might stand on a balcony and look across five miles of empty air to the next pillar, which is perfectly aligned with your own, its dark windows staring back like empty sockets. The scale is regional, yet the world feels starved of substance.

The reality here is frayed at the edges. Gravity is a local suggestion rather than a law. As you walk the winding stairs, the pull of the pillar might shift, allowing you to walk on the underside of a cantilevered plaza as easily as the top, the void always yawning beside you. Space-time is compressed in the shadows; a step into a darkened doorway might exit onto a balcony three miles higher in the same instant, accompanied only by a soft, percussive *thrum* that vibrates in the teeth.

Movement is the only constant. Despite the lack of wind, the pillars are active. They do not stay still. They sway with the slow, rhythmic grace of kelp in a deep current, a motion so massive it is felt in the inner ear rather than seen. Occasionally, the distant grinding of mineral against mineral echoes through the void—a low, tectonic groan that constitutes the world’s only music.

Vegetation is absent, replaced by a non-biological ecology of "glass-growths." In the crevices of the urban pillars, fragile, translucent filaments sprout like frozen hair. They are not alive in any sense we recognize; they are crystal structures that pulse with a dim, internal rhythm, absorbing the stray kinetic energy of the swaying pillars. They are the primary producers here, turning vibration into a faint, violet luminescence.

Fauna are rare and unsettling. They are creatures of geometry and shadow—spindly, multi-jointed things made of the same grey mineral as the world. They move with a stuttering, frame-by-frame motion, as if they are only partially present in this dimension. They do not hunt; they seem to "graze" on the reality breakdowns, congregating in areas where the gravity is most unstable, their long, needle-like limbs clicking softly against the stone. 

It is a world of settled erosion. Every edge has been smoothed by eons of mica-dust abrasion. The grand stairs are worn into gentle troughs; the balconies are polished to a dull sheen. It is a finished place, a masterpiece of emptiness that continues to perform the functions of a city—shifting, growing, and echoing—long after the concept of a resident has been forgotten. There is no sound but your own breath, and even that feels like a violation of the profound, ancient silence that fills the gaps between the towers.
