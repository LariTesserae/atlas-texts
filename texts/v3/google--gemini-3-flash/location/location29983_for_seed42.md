In the high, cold basins of the summit-seas, where the air of the world ends and the thick, pressurized water begins, lies the Aethel-Root District. It is a place where the concept of "ground" has been forgotten, replaced entirely by a colossal, singular organism—a species of macro-kelp known as the Great Weave.

Here, the water is a heavy, translucent jade, kept at a constant, bracing chill by the altitude. Light filters down from the surface far above in long, steady shafts of pale emerald, illuminating a space that feels simultaneously like a vast cathedral and a claustrophobic thicket. Though the vegetation is total—every square inch of solid matter is either a living root, a petrified stem, or a woven mat of moss—the density of the landscape is deceptively sparse. The massive trunks of the kelp-trees, some as wide as merchant ships, are separated by wide, silent voids of open water, creating a landscape of monumental pillars and yawning gulfs.

The Aethel-Root is an urban space, though not one of stone or steel. It is a settlement of concentrated, primitive ingenuity. Thousands of dwellings are lashed to the swaying trunks: spheres of woven sea-grass, hollowed-out seed pods the size of carriages, and platforms made of sun-bleached bone and dark, water-logged wood. These structures are clustered in tight, vertical neighborhoods that spiral around the stalks, connected by dizzying networks of vine-bridges and pulley-elevators. It is a city of layers, where "up-trunk" and "down-trunk" are the only directions that matter.

The tech is humble, born of hand and grit. There is no fire here, and no forge. Tools are knapped from volcanic glass found in the root-beds or carved from the serrated mandibles of deep-trench leviathans. Textiles are thick, oily weaves of hair-moss that repel the chill. The engineering is one of tension and knots; the entire city groans and flexes with the slow, rhythmic pulse of the high-altitude currents.

The atmosphere is loud—a constant, thrumming percussion. The water carries the sound of the Great Weave’s stalks rubbing against one another, a deep, cello-like wood-creak that vibrates in the chest of every inhabitant. Overlaying this is the frenetic activity of the fauna: the clicking of millions of armored shrimp that clean the moss-mats, the low, mournful lowing of the filter-whales that drift through the voids between trunks, and the sharp, rhythmic snapping of predatory eels darting through the shadow-zones.

Dynamics here move at a glacial pace. A single "building" may take a generation to grow as the inhabitants guide the curve of a living branch. Erosion is not a force of destruction, but of polishing; the currents have spent centuries smoothing the edges of the bone-platforms and rounding the corners of the pod-homes until the city looks less built and more birthed.

It is a world of balance and patience. A resident might sit on a woven porch, watching a single bubble of gas rise from the lightless depths below, taking minutes to pass through their field of vision. The light never flickers, the temperature never shifts, and the great green pillars stand as they have for millennia, anchored to the mountain peak beneath the waves, holding their small, crowded civilization in the quiet, freezing palm of the sea.
