The ground is not stone, nor soil, nor silt. It is a planetary scab, a trillion-mile weave of desiccated, fibrous matter that has long since replaced the crust. This is the **Ossified Expanse**, a world where the vegetation did not merely grow upon the land, but consumed it, digested it, and became the only remaining substrate. 

Here, at the high-altitude ceiling of the world, the air is a thin, biting chill—a steady 0.6 on a scale of warmth, dry enough to crack bone. There is no water. The concept of a liquid state is a chemical myth here. Instead, the "plants"—colossal, grey-silver filaments and petrified polyps—subsist on the slow, thermal radiation of the planet’s core and the static electricity generated by the relentless wind.

The landscape is ancient, eroded not by rain, but by the slow grinding of time itself. The horizon is a flat, bruised line where the purple-black sky meets the grey-white mat of the world-flesh. It is an elevated plateau that spans continents, a high-gravity shelf where the mountains were leveled eons ago by the weight of the living mass they supported. Now, the topography is subtle: gentle, sweeping swells of calcified vine-forests and shallow basins filled with fine, non-reactive mineral dust.

Dotted across this sparse, open wasteland are the Anchors. These are the urban centers, though they bear no resemblance to human cities. They are towering, geometric monoliths of a technology that bypassed the industrial and the digital entirely. They are psionic-kinetic structures, anchored to the substrate by filaments of dark matter. These buildings do not sit on the ground; they are quantum-locked inches above it, vibrating with a low-frequency hum that suggests a power source derived from the curvature of space-time itself. The architecture is sparse—huge, solitary spires separated by miles of empty, whistling heath—but within each spire, the density of information and engineered life is staggering.

The scale is vast. To stand on a ridge of petrified lichen is to look out over a thousand miles of unchanging, silvered plains, broken only by the occasional shimmering needle of a city-spire. 

The silence one might expect from such a void is nonexistent. The world is incredibly loud. The "plants," despite their rigid, mineralized appearance, are hollow and resonant. The wind, passing through billions of microscopic apertures in the fibrous ground, turns the entire planet into a flute. It is a constant, discordant roar—a harmonic screaming that ranges from a sub-bass thrum that rattles the teeth to a piercing, crystalline whistle. 

In this dry, noisy dimness, the fauna thrives. They are creatures of glass and chitin, translucent and multi-legged, skittering across the fibrous plains. They do not drink; they absorb the violent sound waves, converting mechanical vibration into metabolic energy. They are abundant but solitary, like sparks of light dancing across a dark carpet. Large, kite-like organisms drift in the high, thin air, their wings made of gossamer-thin layers of carbon nanotubes, catching the static discharge of the upper atmosphere.

The light is a permanent twilight. The sun is a distant, pale eye, filtered through a permanent haze of dry, suspended spores that have never known the weight of a raindrop. This dimness gives the landscape a ghostly, monochromatic quality, where shadows are long and sharp, stretching for miles across the flattened world.

Everything is in motion, despite the antiquity of the place. The ground itself pulses with a slow, biological rhythm—a centennial contraction and expansion of the global root system. The cities hum, their surfaces shimmering with the movement of nanoscopic maintenance swarms. The fauna dart and weave, driven by the frantic need to harvest the sound. 

It is a world that has reached its final form: a dry, screaming, high-tech garden of stone-flesh, suspended in a permanent evening at the edge of the sky. There is no room for anything new here; the system is closed, perfect, and deafeningly alive.
