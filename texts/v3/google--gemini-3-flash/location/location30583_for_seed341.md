The air in the Sump is a thick, visible soup, hovering at a constant, stifling 98 degrees. It is a place of low elevation, a geographic bowl where the world’s runoff has gathered and settled into a permanent, steaming state of saturation. Here, the boundary between liquid and gas is porous; every breath feels like a drink, and every surface is slick with a perpetual, oily dew.

The landscape is a "grove" of industry, a single, cathedral-sized courtyard where the scale is intimate but the verticality is imposing. It is an urban ruin that has reached an equilibrium with its environment. The erosion is not chaotic; it is established. The brickwork has been smoothed by centuries of condensation into something resembling river-stone, and the iron girders have oxidized into a deep, stable violet-black, no longer flaking but standing as solid as basalt.

Everything here is loud. The silence is buried under a rhythmic, mechanical cacophony that seems to emerge from the earth itself. Deep in the flooded sub-levels, massive, ancient pistons—the size of oak trees—continue to churn. They don't seem to be powering anything anymore; they simply *are*. Their movement creates a low-frequency thrum that vibrates in the marrow of your bones, punctuated by the high-pressure hiss of steam venting from brass nozzles hidden deep within the foliage. The sound of water is constant: the splash of heavy overflows, the rhythmic *thwack-slap* of water hitting broad leaves, and the gurgle of clogged drains.

The vegetation is aggressive and lush, yet it possesses a strange, translucent quality. Giant, waxy ferns with fronds the size of sails wrap themselves around rusted steam pipes, drawing warmth directly from the metal. Pale, bioluminescent moss blankets the brick walls, glowing with a dim, sickly verdance that provides the only light in this permanent twilight. There is no sun here; the sky is a ceiling of thick, churning grey mist that never breaks. 

The density is sparse in terms of "objects," but each object is massive and purposeful. A single, towering cooling tower might stand thirty paces from a cluster of ornate, copper-domed kiosks, with nothing but a shallow, bubbling pool of black water between them. There is a sense of "room" here—the space is organized, with clear corridors of water and stone, yet it is utterly empty of people or animals. 

There is a profound absence of fauna. No birds cry in the heights; no insects buzz in the stagnant pools. The heat and the rhythmic mechanical violence have driven away anything with a nervous system. The only "life" is the marriage of the botanical and the mechanical. The plants don't just grow on the machines; they have begun to mimic them. Long, vine-like tendrils pulse with a rhythmic internal pressure, timing their contractions to the beat of the underground engines. 

The world is intensely active. It is not a place of decay, but a place of high-energy stasis. The water is sucked up by the heat, condenses on the cold iron of the overhead struts, and rains back down in a closed, frantic cycle. The machines pump water to no destination, the plants grow to no harvest, and the steam vents into an atmosphere already heavy with it. It is a beautiful, suffocating machine-garden, humming with a purposeless, eternal energy, locked in a hot, dim, and thunderous balance.
