This is the **Lichen-Works**, a low-lying basin where the distinction between a forest and a factory has long since eroded into a singular, weathered metabolism.

### The Terrain and Substrate
The ground is not earth, but a vast, undulating floor of compressed, petrified cellulose. It is grey-green and cool to the touch, feeling more like polished bone or dense cork than soil. This is a "total vegetation" environment; every square inch of the landscape is a living or once-living biological structure. There are no stones, only nodules of hardened resin; no sand, only fine, desiccated spores that gather in the lee of the great trunks. 

The elevation is low—a shallow bowl in the world where the air settles and stays still. Because the ground is a continuous mat of interwoven roots and fungal mycelium, the terrain is stable, showing the "established" erosion of a place that has occupied this state for centuries. There are no jagged edges, only smooth, sweeping curves where the landscape has grown to accommodate its own weight.

### The Architecture of Industry
Rising from this floor are the "Urban Nodes." These are not buildings in the traditional sense, but massive, hollowed-out arboreal monoliths, hundreds of feet wide but spaced far apart, leaving great stretches of open, misty air between them. 

Within these trunks, the "industrial" character reveals itself. The trees have been guided by ancient intent to grow around heavy iron gears and brass pistons. Copper filigree is embedded directly into the bark, acting as a nervous system that carries low-voltage electrical currents between the nodes. You can see giant, slow-moving wooden cams—smooth as sea glass—turning against leather belts harvested from giant leaves. 

This is a mechanized grove. In the center of the basin, a "factory-tree" rhythmically pulses. Its bark is reinforced with iron bands, and it breathes with the heavy, sighing sound of a bellows. It isn't producing smoke, but a fine, cool mist of processed spores and oxygen.

### Atmosphere and Water
The air is perpetually damp (0.9), but it does not rain. Instead, moisture exists as a constant state of condensation. Droplets bead on the copper wires and the cool metal valves, eventually rolling down the trunks to collect in shallow, tea-colored basins at the base of the trees. These pockets of water are still and dark, acting as the "batteries" for the hydraulic systems that power the gears.

The temperature is a steady, bracing cool. It feels like the moment just before a frost that never arrives. The light is "normal"—a filtered, pale silver that suggests a sky obscured by a high, translucent canopy far above, though the sun’s position can be tracked by the way the shadows of the copper-wrapped branches crawl across the cork floor.

### The Dynamics of the Grove
Though the landscape is mostly quiet, it is incredibly active. The "dynamic" quality comes from the constant, low-frequency hum of the mechanical biology. 
- **The Sap-Cycle:** Every twenty minutes, a heavy *thrum-clunk* echoes through the basin as a massive wooden valve releases, sending a surge of pressurized sap through the pipes to turn the overhead leaf-turbines.
- **The Ventilation:** The "urban" nodes sway slightly, not from wind, but from the internal shifting of their gears. 
- **The Light-Play:** Bioluminescent mosses, triggered by the electrical current in the copper veins, pulse with a dim, rhythmic amber glow, timed to the "breathing" of the central factory-tree.

### Ecology and Fauna
Life here is specialized and rare. There are no birds or large mammals. The "fauna" consists of small, chitinous maintenance-creatures—insects the size of house cats with scissor-like mandibles that prune the excess growth from the gears and oil the leather belts with secreted waxes. They move with a clicking, mechanical efficiency, indifferent to anything that does not interfere with the machinery.

### The Rules of the World
In the Lichen-Works, the primary law is **Homeostasis**. The technology does not consume the environment; it *is* the environment. The machines do not rust; they scar over. If a gear breaks, the tree is grafted with a new wooden prosthetic that grows into the shape of a cog over a decade.

Everything is spaced out (sparse density). There is a profound sense of "room" between the technological presences. Standing in the middle of the basin, you are surrounded by the infrastructure of a city, but it is a city that breathes, photosynthesizes, and waits. It is an industrial park at the end of time, where the engines are made of wood and the power is drawn from the slow, cool pressure of rising sap.
