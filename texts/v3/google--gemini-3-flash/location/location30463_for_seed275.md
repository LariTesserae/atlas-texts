This is a world of parched, architectural biology, a subterranean cathedral where the air is cool and the concept of a "drop of liquid" is entirely alien. We are deep beneath the crust of a planetary body, in a hollowed-out regional expanse that stretches for hundreds of miles, yet there is no rock to be seen. The "ground," the "walls," and the "sky" are all composed of a single, continuous, and dry botanical substrate.

**The Substrate and Terrain**
The terrain is not dirt or stone, but a vast, interconnected network of silver-grey cork and petrified cellulose. It feels like walking on high-density foam or tightly woven, sun-bleached rope. Everything is dry. If you were to snap a twig, it would not bleed sap; it would puff out a fine, electrostatic dust. The topography is a series of rolling, fibrous dunes and steep, terraced ridges formed by the overlapping of colossal, plate-like leaves that have hardened into permanent shelf-fungi the size of city plazas.

Because the erosion is so low (0.3), the landscape is impossibly sharp. The serrated edges of the "grasses"—which are actually stiff, three-meter-tall shards of crystalline lignin—are as keen as glass. There is no rot here, no decay to soften the edges, and no wind-driven sand to smooth the surfaces. The world feels embryonic, as if it were 3D-printed an hour ago from a blueprint of perfect geometry.

**The Urban Forest**
The "vegetation" and the "built environment" are indistinguishable. You are standing in a city that grew itself. Immense, hollowed-out trunks rise from the floor like skyscrapers, their barks spiraling in mathematical Fibonacci sequences to create natural staircases and balconies. These are not mere trees; they are infrastructure. 

Between these massive pillars, industrial activity hums in the cool, bright air. Broad, leathery ribbons—living conveyor belts—stretch across the chasms, transporting dry seed-pods the size of train cars. The technology is mechanical and rhythmic. You see massive, piston-like stalks that rise and fall, powered by the slow, pressurized expansion of gases within their stems. These "engines" drive gears made of hardened, interlocking seed-cases, grinding mineral dust into a fine powder that is then distributed through a network of hollow vines. It is a factory-city of wood and fiber, organized with the terrifying precision of a beehive but scaled to the size of a metropolis.

**Atmosphere and Light**
The light is constant and clear, emanating from the "ceiling"—a dense canopy of bioluminescent moss that mimics a bright, overcast sky. The light doesn't flicker; it provides a steady, clinical illumination that catches the silver glint of the fibrous air. Despite the industrial motion, it is remarkably quiet. The botanical substrate is a perfect acoustic dampener. The heavy thud of a biological piston or the scuttle of a multi-legged creature is swallowed instantly by the porous walls, leaving only a faint, low-frequency vibration that you feel in your marrow rather than hear with your ears.

**Fauna and Motion**
The world is active. Though the density of structures is sparse—leaving vast, echoing voids of open air between the industrial hubs—life moves through these spaces with purpose. Chitinous, dry-skinned creatures, resembling a cross between an orchid mantis and a clockwork mechanism, navigate the vine-cables. They don't drink; they harvest the static electricity generated by the rubbing of the giant leaves. They move with a clicking, jerky grace, tending to the industrial plants, pruning the sharp edges of the city to keep the pathways clear.

**The Logic of the Place**
This is a non-aqueous civilization. In the absence of water, life has turned to tension, gas-pressure, and structural integrity. The "urban" layout is not for people, but for the optimization of the forest’s growth. The buildings are the organisms; the machines are the organs. Everything is cool to the touch, smelling faintly of dried tea and ozone. It is a regional-scale engine of dry growth, a quiet, bright, and crystalline wilderness that is currently, and forever, under construction.
