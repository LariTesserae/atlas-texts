In a low-lying basin tucked between the roots of a colossal, unseen mountain range, the world is still in the process of arriving. This is a pocket-scale environment, a hidden grotto no larger than a cathedral’s nave, where the ground is a raw, jagged obsidian-black. There is no sand here, no soil, no rounded river stones. The geology is embryonic—sharp, fresh-cooled mineral plates that have never felt the abrasive touch of a wind or the grinding weight of a glacier. The edges of the rocks are like broken glass, yet they are strangely warm to the touch, radiating a gentle, subterranean heat that keeps the air at a constant, balmy temperature.

In this sheltered hollow, a miniature city has taken root. It is not a human settlement, nor is it a machine-made grid; it is an organized, layered construction of biological intent. Wasp-waist spires made of hardened, translucent secretions rise from the black floor, connected by arched bridges of the same amber-like material. These structures are arranged with the precise logic of a metropolis—distinct districts of tall, honeycombed towers separated by wide, flat plazas of bare rock. There is an urban rhythm to the layout, a sense of infrastructure and tiered living, yet not a single tool was used to build it. It is a city of secretion and instinct.

Despite the "urban" density of these spires, the grotto feels sparse. There is a vast amount of empty space between the clusters of buildings, giving the landscape a sense of lonely grandeur. The black, unweathered floor acts as a dark mirror for the life above it.

The vegetation is lush but selective. Enormous, broad-leaved ferns, their fronds the color of bruised plums, lean over the spires. Thick, velvet-textured mosses do not simply grow; they drape over the sharp mineral edges like heavy tapestries, softening the raw geology. The plants are engorged with water, though there is no standing pool or flowing stream. Instead, the moisture is a matter of breath and beads. Intermittent mists roll through the grotto, leaving fat globes of condensation on every surface. These droplets cling to the amber towers and the fern tips, trembling but rarely falling, caught in the humid, still air.

Movement is constant but hushed. The fauna is abundant, yet remarkably quiet. Soft-bodied creatures, resembling terrestrial octopuses or giant, shell-less snails, glide along the amber bridges of the city. Their movements are purposeful, following the "streets" with the steady pace of commuters. In the air, heavy-bodied insects with wings like stained glass hover between the ferns, their flight making only the faintest thrum, a sound so low it is felt in the chest rather than heard in the ear.

The light is dim, filtered through a thick canopy of leaves far above the grotto's lip. It is a green-gold twilight that never changes. There is no sun here, only the pervasive, sourceless glow of a long afternoon.

The world here operates under a rule of patient construction. Because there is no erosion, nothing is ever lost or worn away; the city only grows, layer by biological layer, atop the sharp, new earth. It is an active ecosystem in a state of perpetual expansion, a silent, warm, and busy cradle hidden in the dark folds of a young world. The silence is not an absence, but a presence—a heavy, damp velvet that holds the city together, ensuring that the only sound is the occasional, rhythmic *tink* of a single water drop finally losing its grip and shattering against the glass-hard floor.
