The air here is not air; it is a pressurized, tea-colored suspension, a medium halfway between a warm tropical sea and a saturated gas. To move through it is to push against the weight of a world that has forgotten the concept of a horizon. Everything is submerged in a thick, amniotic gloaming, a dimness that feels less like the absence of light and more like light has been ground into a fine, muddy powder and scattered through the depths.

The sound is the first thing that breaks you. It is a monolithic, tectonic roar—the sound of a thousand waterfalls colliding with the rhythmic, metallic shriek of a factory that never sleeps. It is a deafening, white-noise vibration that shakes the marrow in your bones. It comes from the "engines"—titanic, rusted industrial cylinders the size of cathedrals that hang suspended in the murk, their massive pistons churning the thick water with a violence that creates localized whirlpools. These machines are not built into the ground; they are tethered by mile-long chains to jagged, obsidian-sharp peaks that have only recently pierced the crust of this world.

The terrain below is a nightmare of "young" geology. The rocks are not rounded by the water but are terrifyingly sharp, like freshly broken glass or the teeth of a saw. There are no plains, only sudden, violent upthrusts of basalt and obsidian, shimmering with an oily sheen. 

Clinging to these razored edges is a vegetation so aggressive and lush it seems to be actively colonizing the sound itself. These are not plants as we know them; they are translucent, vascular structures, deep violet and bruised gold, that pulse in time with the industrial thrum. They grow in dense, suffocating mats, layering over one another until they form living rafts that drift through the turbulence. Some resemble giant, dripping lung-worts; others are like bundles of copper wire that have sprouted soft, velvet polyps. They have no leaves, only receptors—gills that filter the metallic richness of the pressurized soup.

There is no animal life. Not a single fish, crustacean, or insect breaks the emerald-dark density. The world is sterile of spirit but hyper-active in form. In the absence of fauna, the environment itself has become predatory and sentient.

Reality here is an unstable draft. The physics of the space are prone to sudden, violent "rewrites." You might see a cluster of iron girders—remnants of some scattered, forgotten infrastructure—suddenly begin to bloom like a rose, the metal softening into a liquid state before snapping back into a jagged, frozen explosion of rebar. Gravity is a suggestion; a heavy industrial turbine might drift upward like a bubble, only to be crushed flat by a sudden localized increase in pressure. The water itself sometimes forgets its state, turning into a wall of solid, crystalline heat for a few seconds before collapsing back into a churning torrent.

Small, scattered outposts—remnants of an industrial effort that seems both prehistoric and futuristic—clutch the sides of the new-born cliffs. These are skeletal things: gantries made of brass and salt-pitted steel, lonely observation decks with thick, cracked glass, and pipes that vent scalding, iridescent steam into the already warm abyss. They serve no apparent purpose now, their gears jammed by the fast-growing, wire-like vines that thread through every valve and piston.

The light is a dull, rhythmic throb. Every few minutes, a massive electrical discharge ripples through the overhead murk—not lightning, but a slow, expanding dome of bioluminescent green that reveals the terrifying scale of the place. In these flashes, you see the "forest" extending for miles: a vertical jungle of pulsing stalks and grinding gears, all suspended in a turbulent, boiling medium that feels like the inside of a dying god’s throat. 

It is a place of frantic, meaningless production. The machines scream, the plants grow with a feverish, cancer-like speed, and the very landscape shatters and reforms, all in a dark, warm wetness that never finds peace. It is a world that is being born and being ground to dust at the exact same moment.
