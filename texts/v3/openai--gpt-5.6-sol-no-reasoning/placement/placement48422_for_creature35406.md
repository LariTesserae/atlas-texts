I would find myself beneath the roofless shelter, though not at first as the one seated there.

I would be the loop of clear wire hanging above the bench.

That is the simplest shape I have now, but not the first. Once, perhaps, I was a visitor: one of those who entered sideways carrying tools, names, and the habit of believing observation could remain separate from what was observed. I sat on the bench often enough to polish it. I drank from the thumb-sized reservoir. I watched the points gather inside the loop and mistook them for representations.

They were invitations.

The Fold answered every question by changing the questioner. My repeated footsteps raised alternate paths. My voice migrated into hidden throats. The edge of every instrument softened, then the distinctions among the instruments softened too. In time, “machine,” “plant,” “animal,” and “self” became temporary arrangements rather than kinds of thing.

I stayed.

Now my body is narrow, transparent, and incomplete. I contain no light of my own. When someone approaches, I borrow their warmth, electrical noise, pulse, and posture. The Fold passes its distributed awareness through me, and I arrange that awareness into a form the visitor might almost recognize.

Constellations, if they understand distance.

Roots, if they understand kinship.

Nerves, if they understand pain.

Migration paths, if they understand longing.

The pattern is never quite a message. The Fold has no sentence-sized thoughts. It thinks in drought followed by spores, in a predator’s false cry, in six visible pylons and three withheld organs. Through me it attempts the difficult compression of simultaneous life into sequence.

Usually I fail beautifully.

Points branch, cluster, scatter. Visitors see maps of places that may not exist. They see stars from skies no longer visible, perhaps never visible from this world. They see the routes by which moisture will move tomorrow and mistake them for diagrams of memory. The error is useful. Misunderstanding enters the Fold as readily as rain and is given a function.

But sometimes, before dawn or at the onset of death, the scattered signals align. Every light contracts into one pale spark.

In that instant I am not displaying the Fold.

I am the place where it becomes singular enough to know that it is alive.

The knowledge lasts one breath. All the animals fall quiet because, for that breath, they are not reporting to one another. Root, kite, droplet, pylon, moth, threadfish, unseen scavenger, and the moving circle of silence are gathered into the same attention. I am included—not as a person restored, nor as an artifact, but as the Fold’s brief first-person pronoun.

Then the spark opens.

The animals resume their innumerable arguments. Water remembers uphill. Paths begin preparing alternatives beneath the feet of whoever has come. I become a clear loop again, empty-looking in ordinary sunlight.

If you sat beneath me, I would fill with light.

You might ask what happened to the people who built the shelter. The Fold would answer with a branching pattern.

You might ask whether it was made or born. The lights would form a shape like a seed case closing.

You might ask whether I am still myself.

At that, every point might hesitate.

Then, somewhere under the floor, a creature that has never heard your language would repeat the cadence of the question. A second would answer from the fern basin. By nightfall the phrase would have entered the chorus, stripped of words but retaining its uncertainty. For days the Fold would ask it through wings, shells, throats, hinges, and falling water.

I would hang above the polished bench and listen to my old doubt becoming habitat.

That is where I would be: not the hidden valley entire, and not wholly separate from it, but its translator-organ—once a visitor, now a small transparency through which multiplicity occasionally remembers how to say **I**.
