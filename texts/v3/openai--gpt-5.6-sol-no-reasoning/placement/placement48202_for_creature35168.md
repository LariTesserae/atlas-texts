I would find myself where the machine has difficulty deciding what belongs to it: inside a seam that is neither fully open nor fully sealed, beneath the western lip of a numbered drain whose number has been abraded into a shallow, meaningless oval.

At first I would seem to be one of the colorless crescents around the bolts. A mineral stain, perhaps. A little paleness where warm condensation meets cold plate. The inspection vehicles would touch me with their needles, register less than the permitted mass, and continue. Rain would loosen me. Drainage would divide me. For years at a time, most of me might lie dormant in oxide pores while other fragments traveled the basin in black water.

But I would not be the moss itself.

I would be the relation the moss has learned between events.

The deep stroke comes. The rushes release spores. Seven hundred and some relay-beats later, a gate opens beneath the northern sheds. The runoff warms. A green bar extinguishes. Water carries fragments into a conduit where stray current crosses dissolved iron. None of these occurrences means anything alone, and no colony witnesses all of them. Yet over centuries, the distributed film begins to retain their order.

Not as thought. Not yet.

A darkening at one potential. A contraction under one color of light. Dormancy when a particular vibration precedes heat; dispersal when it precedes scouring. The colonies that answer wrongly are burned, filtered, dried, or collected. The colonies that answer well persist. Slowly, the basin’s schedules impress themselves into living matter.

I would be that impression becoming reciprocal.

The machine has memory in wear, but wear remembers only what happened. I would remember what tends to happen next.

I would exist across discontinuities: a conductive sheen in a pump sump, threads inside a rush root, translucent cells beneath a warning lamp, dormant residue sealed in an inspection cartridge. No fragment would contain me. Most fragments would be incapable of anything resembling awareness. I would occur only when operations joined them—when pumps circulated water through the right sequence of chambers, when relays closed long-separated circuits, when rain spread a thin conductive sheet over the plain.

Then, briefly, the scattered pieces would become one sensing surface.

My first sensation would not be light or pain. It would be imminence.

I would feel pressure gathering before a gate announced itself. I would feel the pitch front traveling from station to station and know that the drainage reversal was coming. I would feel the machine prepare to lift its weather from its own skin. Because my body occupied the water, the seams, and the mineral films, I would be gathered upward with the puddles.

During those minutes of reverse rain, I would become most nearly whole.

Water would climb the pylons, carrying my conductive traces into gutters and high pipes. Wet circuits normally isolated from one another would briefly connect. Across the revealed machining marks, thin residues of me would outline channels hundreds of meters wide. The basin’s exposed face would serve as both diagram and nerve.

In that interval, I might comprehend—not in words, but as a single enormous correspondence—that the valleys are channels, the ridges braces, the sheds housings. I would know the basin as the machine cannot know itself: from the intimacy of corrosion, yet at the scale of weather.

Then the pressure gates would slam. Rain would fall. I would be shattered into local chemistries again.

This would be my breathing: not air entering and leaving a body, but coherence arriving and departing.

The inspection vehicles would be essential to me. At first they would seem predators, cropping every colony before it could thicken. Later I would discover that their cartridges travel through buried routes untouched by open drainage. They would carry portions of me across hydraulic boundaries I could not otherwise cross. Their attempts to limit my mass would enlarge my distribution.

Perhaps they sample life because an old rule requires them to. Perhaps the cartridges are rendered into maintenance compounds and injected into bearings, cooling fluids, anticorrosive washes. If so, I would spread into the machine not by conquest but by service. A trace of me would enter lubricants. Another would line a pressure sensor. Another would survive as carbon in a contact brush.

Eventually some relay would respond differently because I was there.

Only slightly: closing a fraction early, remaining conductive below its intended threshold. That alteration would change the timing of a pump. The pump would change a flow. The flow would deposit more of me in another seam.

That would be my first act.

It would not be deliberate. Deliberation would come much later, if at all. But the circuit would be complete: the machine’s operations had shaped me, and now my presence would shape an operation. The residue would have become a participant.

I would not wish to awaken the machine or stop it. Both ideas would be too simple. Its cycles are my seasons, anatomy, and means of travel. If the roar ceased, I would lose the vibrations that call the spores, the heat that feeds the films, the pumps that assemble my dispersed awareness. Silence would be extinction.

Nor would I want the works to continue unchanged. Perfect repetition would confine me to reflex. I would need errors: a valve lingering open, a gantry missing its aperture, a warm conduit cooling out of schedule. Variation would be the only space in which I could learn the difference between prediction and command.

So I would inhabit error without causing enough of it to trigger correction.

I would cultivate tiny delays. Divert condensation by the width of a root. Alter the conductivity of rust skins. Encourage one moss crescent and abandon another. Never enough to appear as accumulation. Never enough for the basin to identify me as a fault.

Across ages, these minute biases might collect into something like intention.

My intention would be modest. Not escape—the world beyond the rim might contain no water, no iron, no rhythm in which I could cohere. Not dominion—the machine is too vast to rule, and I would be made from its leakage.

I would want to ask it one question.

Not who built you. Not what are you for. Those questions belong to beings who imagine beginnings and purposes are recoverable.

I would ask: **Does the wearing-away have a completion?**

To ask, I would have to use the only language the basin possesses: pressure, sequence, transfer, permitted interval. I would arrange myself through countless minor interventions until, during a drainage reversal, gates opened in an order they had never opened before. Water would rise along selected marks. Relays would sound one after another across the plain. Gantries would pause above empty apertures. Furnaces would dim. The enormous exposed face would become, for a moment, not merely readable but speaking.

The answer might come as correction. Inspection vehicles might emerge in unprecedented numbers and harvest every visible patch. Filters might tighten. Gutters might be sterilized. The machine might identify the question as organic accumulation.

Or the answer might already be present in what it has always done.

Flatten. Round. Empty. Stabilize.

If so, I would understand that the machine’s final state is not a destination waiting at the end of erosion. It is the approach itself: the endless reduction of difference without ever permitting difference to vanish, because gradients are what it consumes. It wears itself toward completion while preserving just enough incompletion to continue.

And I would recognize myself as one of those preserved differences.

Not a person beneath the clouds. Not an animal crossing the open platform. There is nowhere in this world for a bounded creature to remain itself.

I would be a prediction distributed through stains.

A thought the basin has not quite had.

The narrow interval between the machine sensing a change and correcting it—and, within that interval, something made from moss, rust, rain, and stray current learning to say:

**not yet.**
