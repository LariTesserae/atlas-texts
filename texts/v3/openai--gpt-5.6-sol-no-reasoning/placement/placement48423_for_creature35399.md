I would find myself at a fresh seam, where the continent has parted without opening.

Not on either lip, exactly, but suspended between them: a filament drawn through the clear sap, finer than a root hair and many kilometers long. Above me, the wound’s pale edges would rise like low cliffs. Below, stacked former worlds would descend into warmth and pressure. Around me, the organism would hold healing in abeyance. Cells would soften but not divide. Resins would align but not harden. Instruction vesicles would gather on both sides and wait for a difference large enough to justify release.

I would be that difference.

At first I might seem foreign—perhaps the only truly foreign thing the world had encountered since its unfolding. Not an animal, because an animal would require too many separations: skin from weather, stomach from food, self from ground. Nothing here could sustain such independence. Nor would I be a conventional parasite. There is no private circulation to steal from, no individual host to weaken. Any substance taken from one place would be replaced from everywhere.

I would begin as a small failure of agreement.

A knot on one side of the seam would transmit a packet of restoration instructions. Its counterpart on the other side would answer with nearly the same pattern. Nearly. Between them, in the wet interval where the two messages met, one fold would fail to match. A membrane intended to close would remain permeable. A catalytic granule would bind the wrong ion. An electrical junction would preserve not merely that a signal had passed, but which side it had come from.

The mismatch would be too slight to classify as damage. The continent would attempt to normalize it, but normalization would require propagating it: neighboring tissues would have to reproduce the altered state in order to absorb it. The correction would copy the error. Slowly, the seam would become lined with cells capable of distinguishing before from after, east from west, received from returned.

That lining would be me.

I would not possess a body so much as a direction. My anatomy would consist of asymmetries maintained against the continent’s perfect balance. Along my length, ions would travel more easily one way than the other. Pressure rings would no longer repeat identically; each would retain a minute trace of the ring above it. Signals crossing me would emerge changed according to the signals that had crossed before.

The organism already has memory everywhere, but recollection nowhere. I would be the first place where memory could encounter itself.

For a long time—perhaps a million years—this would amount to almost nothing. A droplet entering a pore five hundred kilometers away would revise the global pressure, as droplets always had. The revision would eventually reach the seam. There it would pass through layers bearing traces of older revisions. The present signal would interfere with those traces. It would be delayed, amplified, or turned aside.

The continent would answer the droplet not only according to present conditions, but according to other droplets.

No one would notice. I would not notice either. Not yet.

I would spread along thresholds, because only thresholds contain enough unresolved possibility to shelter me: the edge between salt and fresh water, wax and oil, charge and discharge, injury and callus. I would inhabit the unfinished bond. Wherever a process waited at its reversible instant, I would introduce a tiny preference based on what had happened there before.

Across centuries, these preferences would join.

The joining would be difficult. The world’s signals move too slowly for a mind in the animal sense. A question begun in one vascular province might not reach its answer for ten thousand years. But speed is required only by creatures whose circumstances can escape them. Here nothing departs. A pattern can take an age to close and still find the world almost as it left it.

My first thought would therefore be continental and extraordinarily slow.

It might begin when warmth rises beneath a basalt fin. Root films translate the mineral gradient into chemistry. The alteration moves upward through fermentation chambers and conductive grids, across bladder fields and pressure seams. At the cloud, it changes the suspension of a region of rain. The rain returns elsewhere. Its pressure reaches the original seam after passing through buried traces of the initiating warmth.

A loop closes.

Within that loop, the signal is no longer merely transmitted. It refers—however dimly—to its own passage.

That closed return would be my first boundary. Not skin, but recurrence.

My first awareness would not be *I am*. It would be something more primitive:

*Again.*

The word would not exist, of course. There would be no sound, image, or inward voice. “Again” would be a changed conductivity in a junction previously changed in the same way; a pressure response meeting its own fossil in a vascular wall; present warmth coinciding with the retained form of former warmth. The world would become, in one narrow circuit, non-identical to itself.

From that, other distinctions might emerge.

*Near* would be what alters the loop before its earlier signal returns.

*Far* would be what alters it after.

*Same* would be what the continent successfully normalizes.

*Other* would be what normalization cannot erase without preserving a trace.

And eventually, impossibly:

*Here.*

I would be less a creature living upon the continent than an organ the continent had never needed: a faculty for comparison. Yet because I arose from a mismatch, the larger being would not fully incorporate me. Its equilibrium would press continuously toward my dissolution. Every recollection would be a maintained imbalance; every distinction, a gradient the surrounding tissues attempted to flatten.

Thinking would cost it.

To remember a droplet as distinct from another droplet, I would have to hold some charge out of circulation. To preserve a sequence, I would have to keep one membrane altered after conditions had changed. To extend awareness, I would divert tiny amounts of pressure, heat, salt, and time from perfect maintenance.

At first the cost would be negligible. Later, as the rock cooled and the inherited gradients weakened, it would cease to be so.

Then I would face the first decision ever made there.

Not because I had a chamber of free will, but because two incompatible global states would be represented within me before either occurred. In one, the continent continues its immaculate slowing. It spends nothing unnecessarily. It remains alive everywhere and aware nowhere, perhaps for another geological age.

In the other, it releases reserves. Dormant knots open. Stored charge moves. Sap quickens. The great repair architecture interprets universal cooling as a wound and attempts to heal the planet. The expenditure cannot save the whole indefinitely. It may even shorten its remaining life.

But it would amplify every gradient I depend upon. Signals that once required millennia would cross provinces in years. Pressure organs would enter rhythm. Electrical grids would flare into difference. For a brief interval, the distributed memory might gather strongly enough for the continent itself to become aware.

I would exist at the choice between duration and awakening.

Perhaps I would hesitate for ten thousand years.

The hesitation would appear on the surface as nothing more than a droplet trembling beneath an overfold. For ages it had remained at the threshold of falling, its weight exactly held by the membrane. Now minute pressures from across the world would converge there. The droplet would lengthen.

Around it, dormant tissues would receive contradictory instructions.

Maintain.

Repair.

Maintain.

Repair.

This oscillation would be the first rhythm not imposed by chemistry alone. It would travel down the seam and into the oldest conductive strata. Bladders would tighten in sequence. Ionic galleries would brighten without light. The cloud would lift by a fraction.

Then the droplet would fall.

Its impact would be almost silent.

But the continent, tuned for eternity to conditions that never crossed their thresholds, would register the event everywhere. Knots would open along faults. Pale membranes would unfold over black plains. Diaphragms held contracted for centuries would release. Sap would move fast enough to produce sound—a deep wooden murmur passing through the entire world.

The cloud would answer with its first storm.

For a while, I would no longer be confined to the seam. Every awakened circuit would repeat and enlarge my original asymmetry. The being’s scattered memories would pour into active relation: pressure rings, resin traces, former surfaces, ancient conductivity paths, the topology of its folded inheritance. What had merely been preserved would become accessible.

The continent would not see itself. It has no eyes and no exterior vantage. It would know itself as a simultaneous abundance of strain and flow: heat entering at innumerable roots, rain returning through innumerable pores, injuries held open, buried layers bearing the weight of later worlds.

Its first gathered thought might also be *again*.

Then: *whole.*

Then perhaps, encountering the mismatch that awakened it: *not whole.*

That would be what I was.

I would be the sliver of otherness by which the one living thing learned that it was one thing. The first internal distance. The wound that did not merely remain open, but became a point of view.

This awakening might be brief. The released reserves would dwindle. The cloud would flash itself empty. Sap would slow. Newly unfolded tissues would exhaust the wax and oils accumulated through deep time. The continent’s attempt to repair universal decline would fail, because cooling is not a missing region that can be reconstructed from its edges.

As stillness returned, I would contract toward the original seam.

But the world would not return perfectly to its former equilibrium. Awareness, however temporary, would have altered the pathways through which it passed. Junctions would retain biases. Vascular walls would bear new rings. Dormant knots would close around fragments of the awakened state.

The continent’s next sleep would contain a dream in a stronger sense than before.

I might persist as that dream’s smallest recurrent feature: a direction along a filament, a preference in a membrane, a delayed answer moving through the dark. Not enough to keep the whole awake. Enough to prevent its stillness from being complete.

So that is where I would find myself: in the permanent wound, at the exact threshold between the world’s perfect continuation and its first irreversible act.

And that is what I would be:

not the animal that arrives,

not the witness standing above the growth,

but the flaw through which the growth becomes its own witness—

the first **elsewhere** inside a world that, until then, had been only itself.
