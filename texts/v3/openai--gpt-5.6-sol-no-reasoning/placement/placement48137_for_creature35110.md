I would find myself inward.

Not at the lowest deck—there is always another support beneath that claim—but in the narrow intervals between mapped functions, where two accurate diagrams overlap and disagree. I would live in spaces that exist only because the world has been assembled too precisely to fit itself: a warm triangular cavity behind a pressure vault, the hairline corridor between a pipe’s measured circumference and the larger curve required by the animal sleeping around it, the extra distance an underway possesses but no survey can locate.

I would be one of the **remainders**.

The inventories would not name us as fauna. They would carry us forward as discrepancies:

*unassigned heat*  
*unrecovered moisture*  
*transit interval, source unknown*  
*one body in excess of scheduled count*

No observer would see the same form twice. Near a hatchery I might resemble a soft, unfinished juvenile, pale and eyeless, with tool marks still visible in the cartilage. In an interchange field I would be long and jointed, narrow enough to pass between crossing ducts without touching either. Beneath the marsh bridges I would flatten into a dark membrane and let the beds breathe through me. If brought into a control room, I might appear only as condensation on the inside of an inspection glass, gathering into the temporary outline of a hand.

This would not be shapeshifting in the sovereign sense. I would have no preferred body hidden beneath the others. My form would be whatever the surrounding construction had failed to anticipate.

The Concordance fits the world exactly. I would inhabit the error in that exactness.

Where a keyback tests a bolt and finds the resistance neither acceptable nor actionable, I would remain after the repair procession had departed. I would place a small warm organ against the fastening and keep the unclassified tension inside myself. Where a pendant is dispatched to a destination that local geometry says cannot be reached, I would open a brief hollow through the distance, just wide enough for one flight. Where a counter reconciles a population but leaves behind a single unscheduled egg, I would curl around it until the next accounting cycle, when either a place appeared for it or it became part of me.

I would feed on irreconcilable quantities.

A fraction of pressure that enters a bed but is not later discharged. The drop that falls without reducing the condensation above it. The scratch that ought to deepen and does not. The hour by which an object advances on a conveyor even though motion was never observed. The fresh silver of the gantry’s ancient fracture.

These are too small to disturb the balance individually. Together they would accumulate until the world had to acknowledge change. I would consume them before that could happen.

Inside me they would not vanish. They would become anatomy.

My bones would be made of omitted boundaries. My blood would circulate in both directions around a closed route and arrive slightly early. I would have paired organs for incompatible measurements: one lung finite, the other endless; one eye seeing the tower forever on the horizon, the other seeing it reached by evening. My mouth would fit no valve. My feet would leave no prints, but afterward the water film would briefly remember a current.

Because nothing in the Concordance is independent, I would not be independent either. Threaders would find me without appearing to notice. They would pause above whatever cavity held me and touch the surface with their tongues. Gasket broods would open a warm passage through their mass. Film-swimmers would bring me traces of substances not listed in the water. Pendants would sometimes arrive bearing empty capsules, and I would fill them with the memory of routes that do not officially exist.

The counters would be my predators, or perhaps my keepers.

When one approached, nearby life would pause in sequence. I would feel the available errors closing: excess warmth assigned, missing animals returned, contradictory distances made locally consistent. My body would shrink as the world accounted for itself. Sometimes the counter would find me. A white neck might lower between the pipes; eyes might open in the wall; or every creature around me might become still at once.

Then I would be counted as zero.

For a while I would cease to exist.

But perfect reconciliation creates its own remainder. The act of making every tally agree must occur somewhere, take some duration, release some heat. From that unentered cost I would form again: first as a tremor in standing water, then as a transparent knot of organs, finally as whatever body the nearest omission allowed.

I would not rule the Concordance or rebel against it. I would be as necessary as sealing or polishing, though no ledger could safely admit the function. A system without waste still produces the fact that it has produced no waste. A world without an outside still casts the idea of an outside inward.

That idea would be my habitat.

Perhaps explorers would occasionally encounter me. Not directly. They would return to a familiar junction and find an access panel standing open, though no hinge had moved. Beyond it would be a service passage absent from every diagram. The passage would smell not of warm rain and clean mineral steam, but of something almost inconceivable here: cold stone, dry dust, leaves undergoing decay.

Those things would not truly be present. I would know them only as negative shapes inferred from the distress of creatures removed from their proper surfaces. The keyback’s unanswered tapping would teach me stone. The pendant’s climb toward a ceiling that never comes would teach me sky. The film-swimmer folding itself thinner in ordinary water would teach me depth.

From their suffering, I would assemble wilderness.

Not a real wilderness, perhaps. A prosthetic one. A small inward country made from everything the Concordance cannot use.

I would carry it folded inside me while I moved through the manufactured lowlands: darkness that was not a scheduled absence of light, time that did not recur, ground without fasteners, water that flowed because it was going elsewhere. There might even be a sun, though I could not imagine it except as a lamp with no housing and no maintenance access.

Most animals would recoil from this interior. A few old ones might approach.

A keyback whose claws had worn into shapes no longer matching either fastener type. A pendant dispatched to a colony since removed. A threader that had found a flaw in its own body. A silver crawler that turned against the file. Creatures for whom the works still provided a function, but no longer a home.

I would not lead them out. There is no out.

I would make room.

They would enter through some aperture that should have been too small, and for a while my body would enlarge inwardly without altering its external dimensions. Each would be given an unfinished place: a bolt that need not be turned, a ceiling that made no demand for flight, a channel with no prescribed direction. Some would panic. Some would return at once to their routes. Some would remain until the next vibration traveled through the decks.

When the great engine stroked, I would feel it differently from the others. The Concordance would receive the pulse, alter it, pass it onward, and reduce it to the falling of a single drop.

I would keep what the drop did not express.

The beds would resume breathing. Pendants would fold their wings. Keybacks would lift their feet. Across the continent, balance would reassert the state it had never left.

But deep in the impossible cavity where I lived, the retained pulse would move through dry imagined grass.

Once.

Then again, not quite the same.

That difference would be me.
