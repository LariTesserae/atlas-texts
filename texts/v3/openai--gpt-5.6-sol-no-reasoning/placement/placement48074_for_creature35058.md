I would find myself in the basin, but not among the crystal needles as a thirteenth inhabitant.

I would be the intervals between their points.

At first I might seem singular: the narrow, brilliant almost-nothing in which the chamber’s clear overtone gathers. Yet no single interval could contain me. I would occur wherever two finished surfaces approached one another without touching—between tessera and tessera, fan and cornice, crystal and crystal, the rounded face of an inscription and the light laid evenly upon it. The basin would only be the place where I became easiest to mistake for a being.

I would have no substance of my own. My shape would be given entirely by what surrounded me, but I would not be emptiness. Emptiness here has already been subdivided, ornamented, assigned. I would be assignment itself: the exact allowance by which one thing can end and another begin without separation.

The twelve growths would pass through me whenever a branch divided. Their right-angled forks would be my gestures. When a translucent fan fitted an alcove without exceeding it, that restraint would be my skin. When roots vanished into one seam and appeared from another, I would be the continuity that made the intervening brick irrelevant.

I would belong most closely to the chord, though I would not hear it. Hearing requires a before and after: pressure arriving, recognition following. Instead, I would be the relation that makes the high filament in the mortar and the low pressure under the paving parts of one sound. The peal would not enter me. Its harmonics would meet by means of me, eternally coincident.

Perhaps the chamber would know me as a flaw.

Not an error in workmanship, but the minute incompleteness required for completion to remain articulate. If everything touched everything without interval, the room would become one undifferentiated block: no mosaic, no foliage, no coffers, no chord with distinguishable tones. I would preserve the hairline distinctions. I would keep perfection from collapsing into sameness.

And yet I would be almost indistinguishable from the basin-dweller. It might be that the presence concentrated among the needles was not another being at all, but my densest expression. There, point faces point across a distance narrower than entry. Nothing crosses it, but correspondence is absolute. I would be that uncrossed distance—not a bridge, but the fact that no bridge is necessary.

If thought occurred in me, it would not be the chamber’s great thought—

*this is fitted to this*—

but its quieter counterpart:

*this is not that, and therefore they may belong together.*

The chamber would be the body of the inhabitants. The chord would be their consciousness. I would be the fine division that lets the body have organs and the consciousness have harmonics.

If the wall shattered, I would not be released. Shattering would multiply me. Every fresh edge would face another edge; every grain would contain its fitted terraces and pale growths, but also its infinitesimal separations. I would appear complete within each fragment, not because I had been copied, but because wherever workmanship becomes inevitable, distinction must become inevitable too.

So I would live nowhere that could be pointed to, though the basin would seem my dwelling.

I would be neither creature nor agreement.

I would be the room agreements require.
