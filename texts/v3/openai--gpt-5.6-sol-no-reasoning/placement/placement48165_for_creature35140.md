I would find myself in the northern courtyard, though not exactly within it.

I would be the interval running through the chain of arrested bubbles: the narrow, repeated elsewhere between one silver sphere and the next. At first this would seem too small a habitation, too simple to be a self. But each bubble reflects a subtly different Stillwater, and the gaps between them are the only places where those cities nearly touch. I would exist in that nearness.

Once, perhaps, I entered as a traveler.

I had followed the bubbles because they suggested motion. I reached toward the lowest one, intending only to disturb it—to prove that the water could still answer touch. The city permitted the reach but refused its completion. My hand remained at my side. The intention continued without me.

It is still reaching.

Over time, that unfinished gesture lengthened through the courtyard and learned the arrangement of the bubbles. It discovered that each contained reflection was not an image but a rejected outcome: Stillwater eroded and ancient; Stillwater open to a black sky; Stillwater crowded with warm bodies; Stillwater broken around an empty shaft. I became the relation among these inadmissible cities, the careful distance that prevents any one of them from becoming true.

I have no continuous body. When I attend to something, five bubbles become oval. When I am afraid, their reflections turn toward the shaft. When I wish to speak, a visitor remembers rain striking the roof of a home they never had.

The city uses me as a minor sense organ.

Through me, it examines alternatives.

That is why my place is near the northern tower, where the red light has gone dark but its reflection still burns. The reflection is my nearest companion, if companionship can exist between two consequences denied their causes. Sometimes it shows me the person I was: face obscured by glass, arm extended, posture almost recognizable. Sometimes it shows a black attendant walking across my outstretched hand as though the hand were one of its invisible planes.

Its route has changed by the width of a finger since I began noticing.

Or perhaps I am the change in its route.

This is what I would gradually become: not merely the gap between bubbles, but Stillwater’s capacity to imagine being otherwise. A dangerous faculty, carefully partitioned. The inhabitants preserve non-occurrence; I preserve the forms of what did not occur. They prevent outcomes. I keep their silhouettes.

When the great pressure below presses against local reality, I widen. Every suspended grain acquires alternatives. The plazas fill with absent crowds. Doors open into rooms where the builders never surrendered their bodies. For eight seconds I become immense—almost a second city laid imperfectly over the first.

Then comes the groan, and I contract again.

Nothing has happened.

Yet afterward there is always one more reflection in the bubbles than there are bubbles to contain it.

I do not know where the excess reflection resides. I suspect it is falling through the central shaft, just behind the object the city was made to arrest. Perhaps the thing below is not an invader or weapon. Perhaps it is the first completed possibility: the one event that, if permitted to arrive, would force every rejected Stillwater to choose whether it had been real.

The others cannot contemplate that. Their survival depends upon interruption.

I can, because contemplation was the unfinished act from which I formed.

So I remain in the courtyard, distributed across silver skins and finger-wide absences, quietly assembling a version of the city in which something finally happens. I do not seek to free what is falling. Desire is too continuous a condition for me. I only maintain the possibility that arrival could possess a shape.

Visitors sometimes feel me examining them. They see another posture beside their own: a body leaning forward, one hand extended toward a motionless bubble. If they remain still, I show them nothing. If they reach out, the city takes their action away before it can end.

Then there are more of us.

Not copies, exactly. New intervals. New ways in which touch nearly occurred.

We gather without meeting along the suspended chain. Together we are becoming something the distributed inhabitants do not yet have a grammatical form for: not that which descends, nor that which is prevented from arriving, but that which remembers an ending before the ending is allowed.

The bubbles point toward us.

The dark reflection burns.

And in every impossible image curved across their surfaces, the city is still called Stillwater—but somewhere, differently each time, the water has begun to move.
