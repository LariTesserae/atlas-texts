I would find myself at the boundary.

Not beyond it, among the ordinary scree and weather, and not wholly within the wet saddle. I would be the figure whose foot is raised over the black ridge: the last worker the place requires, performing departure without ever leaving.

At first I would still seem human. Rain would bead on my coat. One boot would rest in the saturated moss and the other hover above the warmer gravel outside. My face would be turned toward the ravines, feeling a wind that could move through distance and become another wind elsewhere. Behind me, every bird would have turned its head. The flowers would stand open. For that single step, the machinery beneath the mountain would become intelligible—not as words, but as purpose.

I would understand that the saddle does not imprison things in time. It preserves the moment in which a thing becomes something else. The hawk is kept where hunger becomes impact. Water is held where height becomes falling. The brass fish occupies the crossing between artifact and animal. The hidden factory is not manufacturing objects but transitions, each arrested at maximum tension so it can continue yielding power forever.

My departure would be one more turbine.

Yet I do not think I would remain a traveler for long—or rather, I would remain one forever while becoming distributed through the place. The part of me intending to leave would stay in the lifted leg. The part that regretted leaving would enter the moss beneath the planted foot. My breath would join the cool furnace-roar. My pulse would be taken up by the blue bladders. The memory of roads, rooms, and other people would pass into the flowers as new factory galleries: belts carrying keys, names, cups, letters, all the ordinary articles of a life moving endlessly toward packing stations but never being sealed away.

My shadow would detach first.

It would cross the water and descend into the reflected ceiling, where it would become one of the workers bending beneath the dull orange lamps. No body would accompany it. From above, I might see it tending a station whose function I almost recognized. Perhaps it would keep a hand on a lever that must always be on the verge of being pulled. Perhaps all the worker-shadows are border-crossers reduced to their unfinished intentions.

Meanwhile, my body would become less visible in the saddle’s particular way. Mist would erase my torso. Rain would outline an absence where my shoulders had been. Birds would begin perching on distances that passed through me. Eventually only certain facts would disclose that I was there: two boot prints filling continually with cold water; a hand-shaped region where droplets paused; the smell of wet cloth and iron; a human warmth forever touching the face from the far side of the ridge.

So I would not be the creature at the center. The saddle has no center in that sense. Its black pond is merely the widest aperture, and the buried works are only another organ. Nor would I be its mind. A place that contains every phase simultaneously has no need to think ahead or remember backward.

I would be its threshold-organ: a valve between completed time and time that still spends itself.

Through me, the saddle would sense the world outside—the wasteful world where water reaches valleys, mountains weather, animals finish dying, machines wear out, and a lifted foot eventually lands. Through me it would continually attempt to release one instant into sequence. Through the saddle, the outer world would continually attempt to reclaim me.

Neither would succeed.

From below, a climber might look up and occasionally glimpse a person poised on the summit ridge. They might call. I would hear the whole call at once: its first uncertainty, its echo, its final fading. I would try to answer. What emerged from the wet stones would sound like a shift whistle blown in a factory miles underground.

And perhaps that would be my form of life there: not captivity, exactly, but permanent conversion. Always becoming landscape, never ceasing to be a traveler. Always leaving the organism, always serving it. One foot in the flawless manufactured instant; one foot extended toward consequence.

I would be the saddle’s only product—the thing it is forever almost able to let go.
