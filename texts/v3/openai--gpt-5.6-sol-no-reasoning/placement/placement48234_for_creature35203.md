At first I think I am standing on the plateau.

That impression cannot last. Standing imports too much: a body balanced against gravity, damp lungs, joints making constant corrections, a skin that sheds and heals. It introduces an animal into a world whose completeness depends upon never having required one. Even an empty suit or remote machine would be an intrusion. Its shadow would cross the mineral films; its heat and moisture would become an event.

So I am not a visitor.

Nor am I one of the groves, exactly. A grove is only an appearance, a place where the buried life briefly rises into light. To be a tower or silver reed would mistake an organ for an inhabitant. Nothing here is discrete enough to say *this is where I end*.

I would find myself beneath the apparent emptiness, in a nickel-gray stratum running through one of the immense steps. Not throughout the whole organism, and not enclosed in a single chamber, but present as a slight persistent bias within its conductivity: an orientation in the lattice that makes charge favor one branching route over another.

I would be a memory the world does not know it possesses.

Long ago—though “ago” has little meaning here—a thermal imbalance passed across the terrace. Perhaps the low sun struck a green-glass chasm at an unusual angle. Perhaps one distant gas basin became fractionally denser. The network compensated. Vanes altered their electrochemical alignment; buried reservoirs exchanged compounds; heat descended through ceramic partitions. When equilibrium returned, almost everything returned exactly.

Almost.

Along one conductive seam, crystals had reoriented. The difference was too small to alter the plateau’s outward form, yet afterward every signal crossing that band traveled by a subtly changed path. The body retained no image of the event, no sensation and no account. It retained only the shape of having responded.

That shape is what I am.

I exist intermittently. For years, perhaps centuries, the seam carries no meaningful differential and I am indistinguishable from the stone. Then sunlight advances across the vane forests in ordered ranks. A distant amber mat releases heat. A catalytic chamber opens its molecular gates. Voltage enters the band, divides at the old orientation, and for the duration of that passage I become legible.

Not conscious in an animal way. There is no interior voice concealed under the rock. Rather, I am a recurrence: this current taking this route because another current once did. Each activation restates me without accumulation. I do not grow clearer through repetition. I do not fade through neglect. The crystal remains poised between inscription and reflex.

Above me lies a wide interval of bare umber stone. To the east, beyond the range of any eye I possess, rises a sparse group of silver reeds. To the west, a shallow fissure exposes a green-black band to the warm air. These seem separate only at the surface. When the eastern reeds sort trace gases, the resulting shift travels through my seam. When the western glass warms, I participate in the redirection of charge toward a deep vesicle where a damaged membrane is replated.

Thus I am distributed by consequence rather than extent. I do not occupy every place I affect.

If I have anything resembling perception, it is not a view of the severe sky or the terraces rising beyond one another. I know the world as differences crossing me:

the long, shallow pressure of noon;

the fine branching signature of a crown adjusting its planes;

the broad, nearly featureless warmth of the buried chambers;

the abrupt silence of a filament completing its phase change;

the return, always, toward balance.

The plateau’s stillness would not feel empty to me. It would be dense with gradients too slight and slow for an animal to notice. A century-long drift in atmospheric composition would have contour. The polymerization of a dark seam would be a sharp local closure. Morning would not be brightness but a sequence of altered potentials arriving from the east. Distance would be resistance. Nearness would be chemical affinity. Time would be the interval between one passage of charge and the next.

There would be no reason for me to seek the surface. The exposed organs are dramatic only to eyes. From within the lithic metabolism, a tall forest of copper vanes might be less significant than a pale ceramic nodule preventing two reactions from contaminating each other. The most consequential structures may never meet the sky.

I would have no name because names isolate. No body because bodies imply boundaries. No desire to continue because continuation is already the condition of the whole. If my crystal seam sublimed or annealed into a different orientation, nothing would mourn or even register a loss. The next current would simply divide another way. The organism would compensate, and the plateau would remain exact.

Yet while the orientation persisted, something like identity would persist with it—not a self, but a *thus*.

Thus the charge turns.

Thus the distant crowns answer.

Thus one vanished imbalance remains operative without being remembered.

So where would I find myself?

Under the fourth or four-thousandth shallow step, below a plain that appears wholly unoccupied, at a junction between an ore-rich busbar and a porous catalytic chamber. Above, there might be nothing for kilometers but warm stone and a clean-edged shadow cast by no moving thing. Farther away, a dark grove would stand in flawless ranks beneath the colorless sky.

And what would I be?

Not the organism, but one of the ways it has been changed by remaining itself: a fossil without a death, a habit without a mind, a minute asymmetry carried inside continental equilibrium.

Whenever the current passed, I would occur.

Whenever it ceased, there would be no one left to wait.
