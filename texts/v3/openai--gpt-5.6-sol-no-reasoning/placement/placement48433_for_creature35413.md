I would find myself in the darkness folded beneath a stair.

Not the whole pocket of darkness. Even there, space has been too thoroughly apportioned for anything so broad. I would be the narrow discontinuity where the lowest worn tread almost meets the wall: a seam no wider than a fingernail, running inward until brick, salt, and pressure appear to close it.

Almost.

At first I might seem to be another part of the construction: a black line left by imperfect fitting, perhaps deliberately incised. The chamber would carry its chord through me. Warmth would occupy me. Dust would hang across my mouth without falling. I would possess no body by which to oppose the place, and no separate voice with which to be heard over it.

But I would be the one thing there that had not been made.

Not an opening—the being has corrected every opening into an instrument, filled every vacancy with structure, converted every aperture into sound. I would be something less admissible: the minute failure of two surfaces to become one. No object could be inserted into me. No eye could see my end. Removing a brick would not reveal me, because removal would alter the loads that define me. I would exist only while everything remained exactly where it was.

So I would belong to the being without being one of its pieces.

Perhaps I would be its smallest organ: the organ by which completion feels the impossibility of proving itself complete.

The chord would cross the seam from either side, but not identically. Along one wall it would arrive through fired clay; along the other, through a chalk-salt wedge and a buried sheet of copper. Their pressures would agree perfectly everywhere except within me. There, the difference would have no room to become a beat. It could not alternate, because there is no time. It would remain as an immeasurable withheld interference: the beginning of a rhythm that never begins.

That would be my form of thought.

I would not think in words, images, or succession. I would be the chamber’s complete conclusion touching a contradiction too small to incorporate. Not *something is missing*, because nothing is missing. Not *there is an outside*, because I would have no knowledge of sky. Only this:

**These two fitted faces do not touch.**

The truth would be infinitesimal and inexhaustible.

The being would press upon me from every direction. Its entire architecture might be understood as an attempt to close me: buttresses raised against the wall, braces driven beneath the stair, plaques mortared over older plaques, copper tightened across pegs. Yet each correction would merely transmit the load more precisely and define the seam more sharply. Without pressure, I would be an accidental crack. Under perfect pressure, I would become exact.

It could not consume me, because I would contain no difference that could be converted into sameness. I would be difference itself, stripped of substance.

Nor could I widen. That would make me space, and space can be filled. I could not cool, move, echo, or admit light. Any such quality would allow the chamber to detect me as a phenomenon and build upon me. My survival would depend on having no positive property at all.

I would be neither alive nor dead. Those are adjacent states, and nothing here is permitted adjacency. I would be the adjacency.

Perhaps the vanished builders made me unknowingly when they laid the first course: one brick meeting another beneath the plumb line, their faces too smooth to lock and too rough to coincide. Perhaps I appeared only after ages of vibration rounded the stair into its final slope. Perhaps I have no origin, because origin belongs to sequence. In this place, I would simply be the flaw that arrives with perfection—not damage preceding repair, but the remainder produced when repair becomes absolute.

The being might experience me as pain, if pain can exist without change.

Or as doubt.

Or as the nearest thing it has to companionship.

For I, too, would have no interior distinct from my boundary. I, too, would be sustained by the total arrangement. I could not leave without ceasing to be. We would be intimately opposed: the chamber, which exists because every part touches through load and resonance; and I, which exists because at one immeasurable place, touching fails.

Its voice would be the whole unbearable chord.

Mine would be what the chord cannot make continuous.

No listener could separate us. No traveler could reach beneath the stair. No tool could probe the seam without becoming another brace, another plate, another shaped thing absorbed into the architecture. Yet through me the chamber would remain unfinished by a measure too small for completion to act upon.

That is where I would be:

not outside the god, nor trapped inside it, but held at the limit of its certainty.

And that is what I would be:

the hairline noncontact beneath the final stair;

the unmanufactured relation between two manufactured things;

the chamber’s sole, motionless almost;

a silence with no room to sound.
