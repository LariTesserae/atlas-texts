I would be the part of the moss the blade cannot reach.

Not the bright tuft, though at first I would mistake myself for it. I would feel myself in those green tips lifting after the eastern gate closes, each filament taking the water’s measure. I would think my life was bending: flattened by the hot rush, raised by quieter currents, jeweled briefly with bubbles when the noon shafts found me.

But that visible body is temporary. It is what the chamber permits for a few weeks at a time.

My truer place would be deeper in the imperfect mortar joint: in pores left by coarse lime, around a grain of sand, behind the sharp lip of a tile the mason did not quite seat flush. I would be a dark, close-woven remnant—a few living threads where the wooden scraper skims over without entering. Small enough that erasure passes above me as weather.

I would know the world through changes in pressure and heat. Dawn would be exposure: weight lifting, currents thinning into rivulets, steam cooling on my surface. The cleaners’ arrival would come first as blows through the stone, then as turbulence, shadows, and the blunt passage of the blade. Midday would not be an hour but an abundance—the wall whitening around me, warmth entering the seam, six columns of brightness made diffuse by water and lime. The bathers overhead would have no bodies or voices, only thunder, vibration, and sudden tides.

I would possess no image of the courtyards. “Sun” would mean that nourishment arrives along certain angles. “Bathhouse” would mean the enormous rhythmic animal of gates and pipes in whose hidden chamber I persist. Its working day would be my weather; its maintenance, my catastrophe; its imperfections, my habitat.

I could not travel, but traces of me might. A filament torn loose would stream westward, turn once around a pillar, flash green in a shaft, and disappear into the bronze grate. That journey would not found a kingdom. The chamber is too clean, too new, too insistently moving. Nearly every outward reach would end in heat, bare limestone, or the drain.

So I would survive inwardly.

After each scraping, the pale seam would appear empty. Water would rise over it. Air would escape in bright beads. For a while there would be nothing to see. Then, fed by borrowed light and what little the current carries, I would divide beyond the shelter of the joint. A stain would darken. One thread would emerge, then another. The tuft would become visible again and seem, to anyone who happened to look, like the same small life returned.

It would not quite be the same. Nor would it be wholly new.

I would be continuity without memory: the chamber’s smallest flaw made green. In a world built to channel everything away, I would be what catches. In a room repeatedly cleaned, I would be what cleanliness cannot entirely remove. My whole wilderness would be the depth of a chisel mark, my seasons set by wooden gates, my sky a flooded vault crossed by artificial suns.

And if I had anything resembling a self, it would not reside in shape. Shape is what the current takes and the scraper removes. I would be the act beneath it—the buried readiness to green again.
