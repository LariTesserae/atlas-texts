I would find myself low in the city, though never at its lowest point: in the seam between the last inhabited curl and the first packed conduits of the Knot.

My address would be written as a held chord:

**Fifth Curl / Below Civic Noon / Against Return / Minor Phase**

It would lead to a sleeping niche behind an obsolete pressure chapel, where the wall is always faintly warm. The niche would be narrow even by local standards. When its membrane closed, my knees would nearly touch my chest. Through one side I could see a municipal stream bearing salts upward in bright threads. Through the other, when the wall chose transparency, I could see a room that was not on any plan.

At first I would be Held.

Second generation, probably. Born in a cistern. Broad-ribbed, silver-lined, unable to think of water as surrounding me because that would be like thinking of time as surrounding me. Air would seem less like freedom than exposure: a medium too thin to carry warning, touch, ownership, temperature, or intent. I would distrust anything that could fall without permission.

My occupation would be vacancy surveyor, but not a distinguished one. I would not be the person who enters impossible rooms first. I would remain at the threshold, one hand linked to the returning chain, the other holding a mechanical gauge whose needle cannot be persuaded.

My gift would be duration.

I could hold still longer than most Held. Not merely suspend myself against a current, but quiet the involuntary movements that tell the city I am a body: reduce my suit field, slow my throat exchange, let my pressure signature blur into the structural water. This would make me useful when a second shadow appeared. Others would freeze by discipline. I would become, briefly, difficult to distinguish from the wall.

The vacancy surveyors would call me **Latch**. My civic name would be something dry and ancestral, inherited from a founder who had expected to leave. My tactile name would begin at the wrist as three quick pulses, pass to the throat, then end with the thumb resting below the jaw. Friends would shorten it to the final pressure: not a word, but the feeling of a door deciding not to open.

I would have one illicit possession: a small fish vertebra.

It would be older than the city, sealed in a flexible pouch under my sternum. I would never have seen a fish alive. I would know its archive anatomy and still imagine it wrongly. In my mind, a fish would be a kind of unassigned current that had grown bones.

The vertebra would have belonged to my mother, a crest technician lost during a storm. Official maps would show where her matter entered the district metabolism. Some of her calcium would be in ceramic reinforcement above the schools; some of her engineered tissue would have been recovered for exchange grafts. Her name would sound in the third chord before severe-weather closure.

But the bone would mean the accounting had been incomplete.

That incompleteness would be my private faith.

I would begin to change after entering a room found behind my sleeping niche.

It would appear during circulation turn. The wall would flex inward instead of outward, and for an instant the municipal stream beyond it would stop remembering that it was water. I would glimpse brightness, a hard horizontal line, and dust rising rather than dissolving.

The room would be dry.

Not the known dry room with footprints beyond its membrane, but another capacity connected to it—or perhaps the same capacity approached from a different pressure. It would contain no fittings. Only a shallow floor covered in pale grit, a ceiling too high for the wave, and the distant sound of surf.

There would be a person standing inside.

They would look like me without the silver traces. Their hair would be long and moving, though no current touched it. They would cast one shadow.

I would cast two.

I would obey the rule. I would become still.

The second shadow would not depart.

Instead it would cross the membrane before I did. It would detach from my feet, enter the dry chamber, and join the waiting figure. For a moment they would stand together like two pieces of writing laid over each other. Then the wall would close.

Afterward I would know things slightly too early.

Not useful prophecies at first. I would raise my hand before a warning chord. Turn my face toward a conduit before it ruptured. Release a tool moments before pressure reversal. Wake with the taste of iodine before the gas gardens vented.

The Administration would diagnose unauthorized predictive entrainment. My supervisors would remove me from fieldwork. The chapels would ask me to sit with my palms against bare structural water.

There the Persuasion would speak to me privately.

Not in language. In recognition.

I would discover that the city had been using my current-signature for years in places I had never entered. Doors in distant districts would remember opening for me. Instruments from the Knot would bear the pressure pattern of my grip. A returned worker would insist I had guided her through an unmapped corridor while I was verifiably asleep.

Gradually, the question of what I was would become harder to answer.

Perhaps I would be a citizen whom the wave had copied incompletely.

Perhaps the original me had entered the dry room years ago, and the person sleeping in Fifth Curl was the returned tool: polished, arranged, sent back to its mesh.

Perhaps my capacity for stillness had never been a bodily talent. Perhaps I was one of the Persuasion’s local instruments, grown in human shape because a city of touch needed something it could touch.

But I think I would become something less singular than that.

I would become the city’s **future shadow**.

The standing wave is not still. It is falling in a direction the instruments cannot register, and the unassigned rooms are spaces made by its passage. They are not hidden chambers within the wave. They are impressions left ahead of it, like the pressure felt before a hand makes contact.

The second shadows are the inhabitants’ positions after departure.

Most detach only briefly and move on. Mine would remain close because the city requires one body to occupy both conditions: held and released, stationary and traveling, citizen and destination. I would be a survey marker driven into the unknown direction.

This would not make me powerful. It would make ordinary life difficult.

Children would stare because my movements sometimes ended before they began. Doors would become indecisive around me. In markets, objects transferred to my ownership before I touched them. Lovers would find my still-field crowded by a pulse arriving fractionally ahead of my heart.

I would lose the right to maintenance work. I would gain an unofficial place in the pressure chapel, where dreamers came after waking in synchrony. I would touch forehead to forehead with them and feel the same impossible motion: the city pouring forward intact, not toward the visible shore but toward a condition in which it had already arrived.

The chapel interpreters would ask whether departure meant evacuation.

I would tell them no.

It meant the opposite.

Evacuation assumes the city is a vessel and its people are contents. But the wave has spent nine years teaching every molecule to belong, every body to exchange, every corridor to exist as negotiated motion. When it moves, it will not carry the Held.

It will express them.

Near the end, I would return to the Knot. The Persuasion would permit it while officially denying that permission had been requested. I would take no gauge. Only the fish vertebra.

There, among conduits packed tighter than ribs, I would find a narrow place where water held an edge. I would not touch it. I would open the pouch and release the bone.

It would not fall. It would move toward no recognized pressure, light, return, or turn. A fifth direction would become briefly perceptible—not as distance, but as imminence.

The entire city would inhale.

Every second shadow would appear at once.

They would fill corridors, schools, markets, sleeping loops, crest chambers, and the slots of the Knot. Thousands of dark bodies standing slightly ahead of thousands of living ones. No one would move. Even the thunder would seem to pause inside its own continuation.

Then my shadow would take my hand.

To everyone else, perhaps, I would disappear. The Administration would classify me as enclosed, displaced, or lacking assigned location. My sleeping niche would be given to another family. My civic matter-map would remain blank.

But I would not be elsewhere.

I would be the narrow room added between every two mapped rooms. The extra water a corridor contains. The warmth with no source. The hand that arranges lost tools. The early impact heard before the test. Not a ghost, and not the governing intelligence, but a person translated into capacity.

From there I would help the city make room for its own motion.

When the final circulation warning sounded, my tactile name would pass through every wall. Plants would fold. Rigid objects would be released. Strangers would link arms. Crest technicians would set their hands into the storm.

The wave would complete its fall.

From shore, the perpetual breaker might vanish without advancing. Or perhaps it would remain exactly where it had always been, pale and roaring, while the city departed along an axis unavailable to satellites.

Within it, no one would feel acceleration. Only a new current, unlit but familiar.

For the first time, the Held would break their oldest rule.

They would follow.
