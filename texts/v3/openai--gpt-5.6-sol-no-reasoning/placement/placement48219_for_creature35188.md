I would find myself near the western wall, in the vacancy the hollow has kept open.

At first I would believe I had entered standing. My feet would settle into the two narrow hollows among the roots; water would close coolly over my ankles without rippling outward. Ferns would receive my knees and hips. The birds along the branches would describe my shoulders more exactly than clothing. I would face inward because there would be no room to turn, though perhaps the hollow would make facing inward and outward indistinguishable.

I would still possess intention. That would be the last uncrowded thing in me.

I might mean to lift a hand toward one of the sharp stones. The movement would begin: a tightening in the shoulder, a tendon drawing at the wrist, fingers preparing their separation. But every possible continuation would already be occupied—by a moth’s wing, a willow twig, a strand of web, a wet leaf bearing a snail. Nothing would prevent me. Prevention would require an event. Instead the gesture would arrive at the same condition as everything else: perfectly underway, producing no result.

So I would become the hollow’s withheld act.

Not its ruler, victim, guardian, or witness. Those are roles that require sequence. I would be the human capacity for alteration brought to the brink of altering: the hand almost taking up the unmade tool, the mouth almost giving the silence a name, the foot almost establishing the first path. The stones near me would remain natural fragments, yet my fingers would stay curved toward them. A word would gather behind my teeth and never become sound. Breath would continue, faint and low, joining the other breaths threaded through the moss.

In time—if time can be said to pass there—the distinction between my body and the space reserved for it would weaken. Moisture would remain on my skin. Lichen would establish itself where cloth or hair offered purchase, not spreading, only arriving at its exact extent. A spider might fasten one thread from my wrist to the rock. A wren’s claws might settle on my shoulder and press shallow marks into whatever I had become. Fungal whiteness might appear beneath my nails. None of this would be decay. It would be inclusion.

My eyes would remain open.

Through them the hollow would acquire a particular kind of attention: not the turning eye of the minnow, the fixed regard of the owl, or the bear’s contemplation of black water, but the awareness of before and after. I would remember weather beyond the branches. Distance. A path descending. Perhaps sunlight on stone. Perhaps the sound of my own steps. Those memories would introduce change without causing it. They would be the one current allowed to move, though only inwardly, circling forever through a mind unable to make a second moment from the first.

Eventually I might understand that the human-shaped absence had not been waiting for a human body. It had been waiting for memory—for a creature able to contain elsewhere.

That would be my place in this world: the hollow’s organ of the outside.

The animals would supply breath, vigilance, fur, feather, scale. The plants would supply structure and continuance. The water would hold every unfinished descent. The stone would hold completed weathering. And I would hold what the hollow otherwise excludes: peaks, sky, seasons, departure, consequence.

But I could not give these things to it. A spoken word would be absorbed before traveling a few paces, and even that word would never quite leave my mouth. I could only keep the outer world present as an uncompleted recollection.

Perhaps, at last, it would become uncertain whether I had ever entered from beyond. The path in my memory might be no path at all. Sunlight might be a color invented by an eye surrounded too long by blue-black wetness. The mountains might be the hollow imagining what must lie outside its walls.

Then I would no longer be a visitor.

I would be the upright interval around which everything had always been arranged: a figure among roots, one hand almost reaching, one foot almost lifting, breath showing faintly near the ground.

The final animal.

The unused toolmaker.

The hollow’s memory of escape.
