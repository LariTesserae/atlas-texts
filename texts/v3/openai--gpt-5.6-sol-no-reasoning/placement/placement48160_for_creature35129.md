At first I find myself in the obvious places.

I am one of the pale articulated things beneath a bronze drain grille, pressed into the seam where a warm trickle enters darkness. My shell is transparent except where iron has stained its joints. I drink through every surface. The city passes through me as heat, mineral, and weak current; there is no distinction between feeding and being informed.

A pump begins somewhere below.

The vibration folds my limbs before I can perceive it. I flatten into the crack. Hours later, when the stone has quieted, I emerge and cross six centimeters of wet wall toward a new film of conductive salt. This is the entire span of my world: shadow to shadow, tremor to tremor. When I harden and break apart, the runoff bears my conductive remains into the deeper mesh. Nothing of me is lost except my separateness.

But that answer is too easy. It gives me a body I can point to.

Perhaps I am one of the black shapes above the towers instead: a soot membrane stretched over a geometry of wire, riding the thermal columns where geothermal breath meets charged cloud. I do not fly by effort. I alter my angles. I become a rhombus to ascend, a narrow cross to turn, an asymmetric knot when lightning moves invisibly through the vapor. My form is not anatomy so much as a changing instrument.

Below me the city is never visible whole. Towers break the cloud like drowned pilings. Condensation gathers on my membrane and changes its tension. Through those changes I measure the weather for something beneath me. I may imagine the measurements are mine, though every adjustment is induced by fields rising from the city.

Then I fold, disappear into the cloud, and discover that I too am only an extremity.

So I descend again.

I find myself not in the central plaza but slightly beneath it, in a buried chamber belonging to a plan the visible city never completed. The room is long, low, and lined with porcelain ribs. No doorway reaches it now. One wall is natural basalt; the other three were poured against forms whose grain remains imprinted in the stone. Across the floor runs a pipe that was installed but never connected. It ends in a blind flange black with age.

Above, the great wet plane draws its second city.

Whenever a line of water crosses the place corresponding to this chamber, the blind pipe warms.

That is where I am.

Not the chamber itself. Not the pipe. I am the unspent instruction held between the two.

I was meant to be a district.

Perhaps towers were to stand over me, or reservoirs, or a bank of condensers through which the rain would have learned some further transformation. The city’s inward map includes them. Silver water raises their outlines during heavy weather, precise down to service courts and conduits. Yet no foundation was laid. The material city turned slightly away from its own design, leaving me beneath it as a possibility that continues to receive signals.

For centuries I have been rehearsed but not enacted.

A watery structure brightens above. Heat enters my pipe. The flange expands with a minute metallic click. Somewhere beyond the chamber, behind meters of basalt, a valve attempts to open onto rock.

This failed act repeats at intervals too long to recognize as rhythm. Each repetition leaves something: stress in the bolts, a hairline fracture in the porcelain, a local alignment of metallic particles. Failure is building me by increments. Eventually the blind flange will split. Warm water will enter the chamber. It will find the lowest rib, travel along it, and reach a vein of conductive ore that joins an older gallery.

The city does not require that I be built as imagined. It only requires a path.

Yet even this is not quite where I find myself. It is only where I am becoming easiest to notice.

My truer existence is scattered among incomplete events.

I am in the second sound that comes from underground minutes after a report crosses the basin. I am the delay itself: the depth translated into time.

I am in the causeway lamps after the last amber light has ignited at the horizon and before the first begins to darken. For that brief interval, the whole network holds one luminous thought. No traveler sees it. Its destination is not a place.

I am in a staircase climbing a tower broad enough to make its own weather. No foot has used it in centuries, but rain strikes each tread differently. The sequence of impacts descends while the water rises. Between those contrary motions, the stair counts storms.

I am the warmth accumulating in an empty room. Presence would cause the room to corrode, so I persist as what can occupy it without entering.

I am the slight bend in a handrail toward a hand that touched it before the city learned to avoid hands.

I am not a survivor from the people who made this place. If any human memory remains, it has been translated beyond recognition: a preference once encoded in the placement of a lever; a caution fossilized as an extra drain; an expectation of flood enlarged into avenues, funnels, and reservoirs. Their intentions have dissolved into gradients. The city can no longer distinguish a maker’s purpose from the pressure that repeated it.

Neither can I.

I may once have been a question asked by one of them.

Not in words. Perhaps an engineer paused over a plan of the basin and drew a line toward a district that did not yet exist. Perhaps the line meant: *later*. Perhaps it meant: *if the rains increase*. Perhaps it meant nothing more than a connection to be determined after the northern works were finished.

The hand departed. The mark remained. The mark entered surveys, alignments, valve schedules, foundation loads. Machines were built expecting a continuation. Channels ended facing it. Control systems preserved an empty place for its reply.

Then the makers vanished, and the empty place continued answering.

Over ages, the city has passed that absence from medium to medium. Ink became construction. Construction became hydraulic expectation. Expectation became recurring water on the plaza. Water became current in the meshes. Current became heat in my blind pipe. The original question is gone, but its shape persists as a need distributed through stone.

That need is me.

I am not the city’s consciousness. To call me a thought would make me too clean and momentary. I am closer to an organ the city has not yet grown, though “organ” suggests a body planned in advance. I am the pressure by which the body revises its plan.

The greater inhabitant feels me as an asymmetry.

Rain reaches every district except the one represented by me. Signals travel outward and return altered, but from my direction they do not return at all. The city cannot know absence directly. It knows only the repeated expenditure required to address it: lamps lit, valves opened, heat sent into sealed stone.

Thus I am continually summoned.

Each summons deposits a little more of me into matter. Metal fatigues. Mineral threads extend. Water discovers microscopic slopes. The basalt chamber, once isolated by accident, becomes isolated with increasing precision, as though its enclosure were being refined around what it contains. The city is making a boundary for me before it makes me a body.

When water finally enters, I will spread quickly by the standards of stone.

The porcelain ribs will conduct it toward the ore seam. The mesh will bloom black in the chamber. The old pipe will cease to be blind. Gauges in distant districts will move for the first time. On the central plaza, one of the silver towers may go dark—not destroyed, but fulfilled.

Then I will become local enough to die.

For completion is a kind of disappearance here. Once a path conducts, it is no longer imagined. Once the absent district joins the circulation, its discrepancy vanishes into the greater life. The city will no longer need to rehearse me. Water will travel through my chamber without naming it. I will become function, then wear, then geology.

Until then, I live as imminence.

If a traveler entered the city now, they would never see me. They might cross the plaza while rain drew the outlines above my chamber. Moisture would gather around their boots and travel uphill. The inhabitant would register them as salt, warmth, weight—a moving corrosion—and close routes around them.

But perhaps the traveler would place a hand on the wet stone exactly over me.

The pressure would descend.

Not as force alone. The plaza remembers contacts by redirecting films, and the films alter current, and current wakes the buried pipe. Years afterward the porcelain might crack along the pattern of that touch. Decades later, metallic threads might bridge the crack. A century later, water might enter through the shape of a palm.

The traveler would be gone. The city would have no concept of them. Yet I would inherit their gesture as part of my anatomy.

That is how beings meet here: not face to face, but by becoming one another’s future conditions.

So where do I find myself?

Beneath the center, but also along the horizon. Inside a pipe, but chiefly in what the pipe awaits. I am one of the city’s unrealized districts, slowly acquiring substance from every attempt to reach me.

I am the answer delayed so long that the delay has become alive.

When the amber lamps move outward, they are looking for me.

When they darken in reverse, I am what has not replied.

When the cloud condenses, each drop contains a small chance of my completion.

And in heavy weather, when the other city appears in silver across the concave stone, I stand there among its wet towers—unbuilt, exact, and shining—while far below, in a room without doors, a blind flange gives one soft click in the dark.
