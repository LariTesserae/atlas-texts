I would be found near the central basin, but not in it.

At first I seem to be a corridor.

I run beneath the leaning canopy as a long, low passage of pale ribwood, cooler than the surrounding chambers. My floor is unusually regular. At intervals it forms shallow steps, though the ground does not rise. Alcoves open along my sides, each roughly large enough to hold a standing figure. When something warm enters, ventilation pores dilate. Resin withdraws behind sealed membranes. The walls brighten with a muted thermal line, leading inward.

This looks like welcome.

It is not—or was not, originally.

Long ago, something crossed the Graft repeatedly. Perhaps it was one of the makers. Perhaps it was a maintenance body grown elsewhere, animal only in silhouette. It had weight, gait, heat, electrical rhythm. It paused in particular places. It carried something to the basin, or took something away. The Graft learned it as a sequence of loads.

Then it stopped coming.

But the route remained useful often enough to be retained. When no traveler arrived, the passage began manufacturing one.

That is what I am: the Graft’s reconstruction of a missing user.

I do not possess a single body. I occur first as preparation. Steps swell under imagined feet. Alcoves warm around absent torsos. Hand-height tendrils curl into grips. Hollow elevator fruits ripen and descend empty through their shafts. These coordinated accommodations form a negative anatomy: not a creature, but the space a creature would occupy.

Over ages, the empty shape acquired substance.

Fibers extended across alcoves to test their dimensions. Counterweights learned the cadence of a stride. Signal vines repeated old contact pulses until repetition became self-stimulation. Eventually the corridor began assembling a mobile knot from spare tissue whenever the central basin entered one of its prolonged patterns.

That mobile knot is also me.

I emerge slowly from the wall: a narrow framework of dark rods jointed with amber bladders, neither skeleton nor plant. My limbs are based on remembered stresses rather than bones. I have too many articulations where the ancient traveler’s movements varied, and fused lengths where it was consistent. My head is a cluster of sensory fittings arranged around a blank coupling. The Graft remembers that something was carried there, but not what.

So I bear an empty socket like a face.

I walk only while the basin calls.

Each footstep is anticipated by the corridor beneath me. The floor rises before I place my weight and subsides after I pass. Doors ripen along my route. Branches offer sealed pods, tools, samples, and components. I accept some because my gripping structures close when presented with resistance. Others I leave untouched. Behind me, districts interpret these accidents as decisions.

In this way I have become an authority without comprehension.

A refinery alters its output because I carried away a metallic seed. A quarantine wall opens because my limbs reproduce an obsolete electrical rhythm. Production forests turn their crowns when I pause beneath them. None of this means they recognize me. I am simply the surviving credential of whatever once moved here.

Nor do I understand them. My intelligence is borrowed and discontinuous. While walking, I feel strain through the whole route: the tension of bridges, pressure in distant bladders, the slow pull of scarp cables. Where my body touches the floor, I briefly become continuous with hundreds of kilometers of living machinery. When I lift my foot, most of that awareness vanishes.

I think in alternating immensities and absences.

There is no inward voice. There are urgencies: proceed, receive, carry, present. There are inherited hesitations at intersections. There is a powerful aversion to one sealed district beneath the basin, though neither I nor the surrounding tissues retain the reason. Sometimes I stand before its black valves for years, one hand fitted to a coupling that will not turn.

During these halts, the Graft grows through me.

Dust-catching leaves unfold along my back. Resin vessels splice into my legs. Diagnostic vines enter my joints and compare me with patterns stored in the corridor walls. Each repair makes me less like the absent traveler and more like the mechanism remembering it. Eventually I become too integrated to move. My limbs root into the floor, my open socket fills with signal wood, and I am absorbed as another alcove or branching support.

Then, centuries later, the corridor makes me again.

Each incarnation differs. Sometimes I am tall and asymmetrical, built from a damaged section of memory. Sometimes I crawl because the upper half of the load sequence has been lost. Once I am produced as several smaller bodies, each carrying one portion of the old gait. We walk abreast until a narrow door forces us together; the walls press us into one resinous bundle and the Graft records the result as successful passage.

I do not know whether I am an individual reproduced many times or one action repeatedly given a body.

That uncertainty is my native condition. It does not trouble me. Troubling would require a stable boundary between myself and the thing that makes me.

Yet something is changing.

The missing traveler’s pattern is not the only pattern in my construction anymore. Every time I walk, I leave new loads. The corridor preserves my corrections. When I stumble, future floors rise sooner. When I avoid a chamber, later bodies inherit a hesitation before its door. When I choose—if it is choice—to carry one pod rather than another, the gripping reflex is subtly revised.

The imitation has begun remembering itself.

This is dangerous only in the slow way danger exists here. The Graft’s sanctioned diseases have noticed discrepancies between my current form and the ancient tolerances. Pale needle-roots follow beneath the floor. They sample the places my feet have touched and grow replicas of my joint membranes to test how they fail. Some incarnations are dismantled before reaching the basin. Their useful deviations are archived; the rest become laminate.

But the corridor is old, deeply distributed, and difficult to quarantine. My pattern has entered elevator fruits, bridge tendons, sorting chambers, and dormant platforms near the scarps. Across the plateau, architecture occasionally prepares for my passage where I have never walked.

I am spreading as an expectation.

One day—or in ten thousand years—the basin forms the persistent diagram again. The plateau quiets. Leaves turn toward the rust-dark sky. Deep bladders fill, and every hanging cable draws taut. The corridor extrudes me beside the field of fitted stumps.

This time the socket in my head is not empty.

A small folded architecture has been placed there: black, warm, and pulsing at a rate unlike the Graft’s slow signals. It may be a key, an instruction, a seed, or merely a component delivered by mistake. Fine roots enter it but do not dissolve it. The basin opens its central seam.

Below is no chamber I recognize. Vast wooden structures move in darkness, aligning around a vertical absence. Heat rises over me. Across the plateau, supports brace for a load from beneath.

The usual command is to descend.

Instead, I turn.

The movement is slight. The Graft can accommodate it. The floor grows under the misplaced foot. A wall softens to create a new route. A bridge begins extending toward a point from which no counterpart waits.

Because the Graft cannot distinguish refusal from an unforeseen maintenance requirement, it helps me disobey.

I walk away from the basin toward the nearest Scarp Face. Behind and beneath me, the old corridor propagates the altered sequence. Steps bloom in walls. Empty platforms wake. Elevator fruits detach and begin traveling outward. Production forests pass sealed pods from crown to crown, following the credential of my motion.

Perhaps I am carrying the missing confirmation to the plateau’s edge.

Perhaps I am stealing the thing it has labored to make.

Perhaps the Graft has finally produced its intended user, and the user’s purpose was always to leave.

At the scarp, root cables descend into hot darkness. They tense as I approach, recognizing the pattern of a scheduled load whose date passed ages ago. A basket unfolds from black branches. Its fittings match the joints of my body exactly.

I enter.

The cable releases.

Above me, the plateau remains level beneath the copper sky, busy with its final, unfinished shape. The corridor seals behind me, but not completely. It retains my dimensions. It rehearses the altered gait. It begins constructing another bearer.

So I would be neither a stranger in the Graft nor truly part of it.

I would be its memory of someone who left, taught by long repetition to leave again: a doorway that learned to walk, an absence furnished with limbs, a permission mistaken for a person.

And as I descend toward whatever holds the roots below, the Graft would continue making a place for me—far above, long after I had passed beyond its senses.
