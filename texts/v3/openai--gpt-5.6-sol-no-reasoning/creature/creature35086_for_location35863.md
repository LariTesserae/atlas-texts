What lives there is not a creature but a discipline set into stone.

It has no body distinct from the basin. The enclosing peaks are its shell; the scree is its loose, continually rearranged flesh. Its veins are gutters, drilled holes, hairline seams, and buried conduits. The shallow pool serves as an exposed lung, taking in meltwater and releasing old air in measured bubbles. The fissure above the seven steps is another breathway, inhaling after dark and exhaling beneath the noon sun. Its joints are balanced slabs and stone pins. Its pulse is the delayed fall of water from one hidden cavity to the next.

It does not feed. It accumulates differences: warmth and cold, height and depth, pressure and release. Sunlight charges it. Frost arrests it. Mist makes its smallest workings visible. Rain passes through it like a brief command, setting off knocks, tones, overflows, and distant answers under the rubble.

The hands that established its order are no longer separable from it. They survive as inclinations too slight to notice, sockets cut to receive vanished parts, steps aimed at a fissure, and channels that persuade droplets toward appointed places. Whether those hands belonged to priests, observers, pilgrims, engineers, or generations of travelers matters less here than it would elsewhere. Their intention has weathered into function. Whatever they believed has become hydraulics, shadow, and recurring sound.

So the basin’s inhabitant is a pattern.

It occupies no single stone and yet depends on every stone remaining nearly where it is. It appears differently according to weather: in clear light, a calendar; in thaw, an instrument; in rain, a network; in mist, a thing that seems to reverse gravity; at night, a locked mechanism under ice. Much of the time it is present only as readiness—the possibility of a channel filling, a counterweight tipping, a hollow speaking.

It is not alive, but it performs some gestures of life. It breathes without lungs, circulates without blood, remembers without mind, and answers touch without awareness. Its memory is stored in slopes, mineral lips, worn grooves, and the timing of water. Each day partially erases the previous day’s arrangement, and each night reconstructs the conditions for its return.

Nothing else lives there. The sterility is part of its nature, keeping every motion legible. No root diverts the water. No moss softens a notch. No nest fills a cavity; no animal adds an accidental track. In the absence of organisms, the ancient arrangement possesses the basin completely.

It is a resident made of recurrence: an old intention still being carried out by the mountain.
