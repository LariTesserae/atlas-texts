The valley stands high above any visible lowland, though it has no mountains in the usual sense. Its elevation is expressed by distance: a pale horizon below the horizon, cloudbanks seen from above, and warm air so thin that breath comes slowly. The land extends for many leagues in shallow shelves. Each shelf is broad enough to hold its own weather, its own groves and still waters, before ending at a green escarpment and descending to the next.

Everything here has been made, but there are no builders and no tools.

The ground is woven rather than deposited. Beneath the feet lies a close-set mat of woody ribs, root cable, compressed pith, and pale membranes laminated into terraces. What first appears to be clay is pollen worked with sap into a smooth ocher plaster. What resembles stone is timber mineralized while still alive, hard at the surface but moist and green within. The cliffs are vast retaining walls of interlocked trunks. Their vertical faces weep through thousands of tiny stomata, every drop emerging at the same rate, hanging for a moment, then falling.

There is no bare geology. If some primordial rock exists, it lies far below the reach of roots and knowledge. Here, plants are not inhabitants of terrain. Plants are the terrain.

Yet the valley does not feel crowded. Its construction is immense and nearly empty.

Across the terraces rise isolated structures: a tower of hollow reeds a mile away; farther on, three vaulted trees whose crowns meet to enclose a chamber of amber shade; beyond them, a long aqueduct grown from the linked backs of thornless brambles. Large intervals of low, taut groundcover separate these works. The eye travels freely. Nothing grows in thickets. Every stem appears to have been allotted its place, as if an absent gardener designed the whole region and then removed every path by which it might be reached.

Water is everywhere, though seldom deep. It lies just beneath the living floor and shows itself through round openings in the woven surface. Some are no wider than bowls. Others form shallow lakes, perfectly circular, with rims of white root tissue. Rain has recently fallen—or appears to have. The upper leaves carry clear droplets, the ocher pollen-plaster is darkened, and water threads down the green walls. Warm vapor gathers beneath arches and inside empty chambers.

But no rain is ever seen to begin or end.

The droplets do not shrink. The streams do not fill the lakes. Water falls from leaf tips without changing the beads from which it falls. Each descending drop vanishes into a dark pore in the floor, and at the same instant another occupies its former place. The valley possesses all the signs of aftermath, with no event before them.

Its loudness comes from this unending water. The escarpments hiss. Root conduits boom at low frequencies beneath the terraces. Broad leaves suspended high overhead shed curtains of drops onto resonant membranes, producing a continuous applause. Hollow trunks answer one another across the open distances with fixed notes: deep wooden vowels, unwavering and without rhythm. Near the circular lakes, the air hums from thousands of taut green filaments stretched across the water. They do not visibly move.

At first the sound seems full of activity. After a while, its perfect repetition becomes apparent. There is no gust, surge, mistake, echo out of place, or momentary quiet. The same distant splash follows the same close patter. The same three booming tones remain layered under the hiss. The valley is loud in the manner of an image of loudness, held forever at full resonance.

The structures possess an architecture without joints. Columns widen into roofs; roofs divide into gutters; gutters curl down into drinking basins. Walls have pores instead of windows. Doorways are grown as smooth ellipses, often too tall and narrow for any familiar body. Some buildings consist only of façades: elaborate screens of leaf-veined wood standing alone on empty terraces. Others descend into the substrate through warm, moist corridors lined with dormant buds. The chambers below are clean and bare. Their floors flex almost imperceptibly under weight, and clear fluid can be seen moving through channels beneath translucent skin.

There are bridges where no ravine has formed, stairs leading up gentle slopes, buttresses supporting walls that could stand without them. This is not ruin, and none of it is unfinished. Rather, it seems made in anticipation of pressures that have never arrived.

Erosion is present only as intention. Along the terrace edges, roots have formed gullies in advance: branching grooves lined with moss, precise as veins, awaiting runoff that perpetually passes over them without taking anything away. Green cliffs display shallow notches like the first instant of weathering, but they never deepen. Fallen-looking fragments lie at their feet, still attached by hair-fine rootlets and still receiving sap. The place holds itself at the threshold of becoming worn.

The ecology is similarly suspended.

Photosynthesis occurs, or something with its appearance does. Leaves turn their faces toward the ordinary warm daylight. Pale flowers open on the eastern side of towers, while darker blossoms hang on the western side. Yet the sun does not perceptibly cross the sky. It rests at a moderate angle, bright enough to clarify every rib and droplet but too gentle to cast severe shadows. There is no dawn color, no noon glare, no evening. The warmth remains constant against the skin.

The vegetation is extraordinarily varied in form but restrained in color: waxy greens, celadon, yellow sapwood, tea-brown fibers, occasional white blooms. Red appears only in the deep interior of cut-looking stems, though nothing is ever cut. Mosses cover plazas in geometric fields. Ferns make railings. Lichen-like rosettes tile domes. Water lilies lock together to create floating floors across the larger pools, each pad fitted to its neighbors without overlap or gap.

There is no decay. Nothing sprouts, grows, wilts, or falls.

Rare animals can be found, but they do not make the valley feel inhabited. A long-legged white grazer stands on one terrace, visible from several miles away. Its narrow head is lowered toward a bed of silver leaves. It neither bites nor raises its head, though its flank is warm and its dark eye remains wet. Under one arch hangs a pair of winged creatures with bark-patterned bodies. Their wings are open, as though drying after rain. Far out on a circular lake, a black animal breaks the surface with a smooth curve of back; the same portion remains exposed, without sinking or breathing.

Closer inspection does not reveal whether these creatures are alive, sculpted, or a category for which the distinction has no use. Tiny rootlets enter their feet, claws, and hanging limbs. Veins in their bodies branch with the same grammar as veins in the leaves. They may be animals sustained by plants, plants remembering animals, or ornaments grown by the valley to indicate that fauna is possible.

No insects pass through the warm air. No birds interrupt the open sky.

The rules of the place become clearest after walking. Distance is real, and effort is real. One may cross a terrace, descend a green stair, pass beneath a dripping vault, and emerge beside another lake. But nothing crossed is altered. Footprints rise out of the soft pollen floor behind the foot. Bent grasses are already upright before one turns to look. A cupped handful of water leaves no deficit in the basin, and the water in the hand stays warm and clear until released. Broken things cannot be made: stems yield, flatten, separate along inviting seams, and then are whole without any visible act of repair.

The world permits passage but not consequence.

Even memory begins to feel like a kind of illicit erosion. Landmarks are distinct—the reed tower, the three joined trees, the white-rimmed lake—yet the order among them loosens. A structure approached from the south may later appear eastward, though the light has not moved and the traveler has not turned. This is not change in the valley. It is the valley’s refusal to contain a route. Its spaces are stable individually, but their arrangement is architectural rather than geographic, connected according to purpose no body can fulfill.

On the highest shelf stands the largest construction: a vast open hall grown from a single organism. Its columns are spaced so widely that clouds can be seen between them. A canopy of translucent leaves spans the top, high enough to contain its own warm mist. Water descends from the canopy in straight, unwavering threads and enters aligned holes in the floor. Root balconies run around the interior at several levels. There are thousands of openings and almost no enclosure.

At the center is an empty depression shaped like the bed of a vanished inland sea. The basin is lined with pale, folded tissue, soft as the inside of a fruit. Channels converge upon it from every terrace in the region. Aqueducts point toward it. Prepared gullies branch down to it. Gutters turn inward. Every water-bearing feature of the valley appears designed to fill this one immense receptacle.

Yet the basin is dry.

The converging water stops at its rim. Droplets hang from the final lips of leaves. Streams enter porous roots and do not emerge. The thunder of the valley is loudest here, but the center receives nothing. It is not a drought. It is a held breath built on a regional scale.

Perhaps the whole country is a seed.

Its halls, bridges, pools, cliffs, animals, rain, and embryonic erosion may be organs folded inside a structure waiting for conditions unlike any that currently exist. Perhaps what seems open sky is a thin blue membrane. Perhaps the warm, unwavering sun is light passing through the wall of a fruit. The low white horizon may not be cloud at all, but the curve of an interior chamber too vast to recognize.

Nothing confirms this. Nothing develops.

The plants remain architecture. The architecture remains alive. Water continues its fall without arriving. Across the spacious terraces, the rare white grazer keeps its head lowered. The resonant trunks sustain their impossible notes.

And the immense made valley waits without waiting, complete in every detail except the one event for which everything was built.
