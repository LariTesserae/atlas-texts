What lives there is not a community but an arrangement of living parts.

The frogs are pressure valves. Pale and slick, they occupy the pool margins at exact intervals, each throat held at the round apex of a call. With every **THRUM** they receive the buried force and give it shape, preventing the standing water from trembling itself into weather. They neither breathe nor cease breathing. Their bodies are the moment between compression and release.

The long-legged birds keep the basin open. Their thin bodies stand among the sedges like dividers on a gauge, preserving narrow upright corridors through which unseen belts and currents pass. Each has one foot lifted. None has ever chosen where to place it. Their eyes remain bright and attentive, but whatever they watch is not approaching.

Every blue-black beetle tends a single stone. They crouch on the pale boundary ring and on isolated rocks within the marsh, gathering cold into their shells until their backs shine like quenched metal. No two share a station. If one seems to crawl, the stone moves beneath it by precisely the same amount, so beetle and task remain inseparable.

The moths belong to the central basalt. Hundreds cling to its wet faces, spaced wingtip from wingtip without touching. Their trembling absorbs the sparks of an electrical system that has no conductors. Occasionally one flashes white through its translucent scales, and somewhere inside the block a relay answers. No moth burns. No current is diminished.

In the moss live the clear worms, drawing elaborate loops that resemble writing only because they never become words. Their galleries may be lubrication channels, diagrams, or instructions continuously issued to a process that already knows them. The moss closes behind and ahead of each worm alike. Thus every tunnel is always being made and has never existed.

The small russet bearer moves between the fern islands with one seed in its mouth. It has soft ears, rain-dark fur, and the concentrated haste of an animal crossing dangerous ground. Yet it remains forever midway between shelter and destination. The seed is the basin’s reserve of futurity: a whole forest maintained as possibility, never allowed to become competition, decay, shade, timber, or fire.

The plants likewise inhabit functions rather than seasons. Ferns cool the meeting of rock and water. Blue-backed shrubs hold spaces apart. Black moss conceals the basin beneath this one. The tiny white flowers serve as compass needles, every face fixed north even where north is multiplied in the pools. They do not bloom; they are bloom held open. Their pollen remains potential, their petals fresh because freshness here is not youth but assigned condition.

Water itself may be counted among the inhabitants. It occupies every surface and passage without migration: droplets beneath leaves, silver threads on the eastern ledges, mist breathing around the stone, rings repeatedly crossing the same three inches. It performs descent, impact, seepage, and erosion without delivering any of their results. It is less a substance than an endless workforce of identical gestures.

At the center lives—or operates—the great black block. It may be an animal wearing geology, a machine before machines acquired bodies, or the heart of a mountain that has mistaken manufacture for circulation. It has no opening through which anything might enter, yet the whole basin seems drawn into it. It has no outlet, yet mist, sound, pressure, and repetition issue from every face.

Inside it, something completes a stroke it can never finish.

**THRUM.**

The pulse passes through frogs, pools, roots, birds, and the observer’s bones.

**THRUM.**

Bearings turn without changing orientation. Belts carry no cargo from one point to another. Relays decide the same decision. Somewhere the metallic shriek of strain is preserved at full sharpness, although nothing approaches failure.

**THRUM.**

It may be making mountains. It may be making this mountain alone, again and again, applying wetness, life, cliffs, and suspended weather to a raw unit that never leaves the die. The assembly line reflected in the nearest pool suggests a vast genus of such places: ranges produced in thunderous rows, each summit containing its own factory, each factory producing ranges whose summits contain factories. But production is only the role the vision performs. No finished mountain has ever been stacked, because “finished” here means permanently in the act of completion.

Other inhabitants may exist below the moss. In the sideways basin underneath, birds hold horizontal corridors and pools cling to their walls. Another kneeling figure may part that moss and look upward into this bowl. If so, that figure also lives here in the basin’s peculiar sense: not trapped in a place, but retained in an action. It is forever discovering the other world and forever letting the vegetation close.

Visitors become inhabitants before they understand the rule. Their footprints live briefly beneath their feet without becoming tracks. Their breath lives both in their lungs and as condensation in the air. Their blood occupies wound and bead simultaneously. Anything they grasp acquires a second existence without surrendering the first. The basin does not seize bodies; it inventories gestures.

Perhaps this is what truly lives there: not frogs, moths, shrubs, or hidden engines, but the unfinished instant itself.

It inhabits every creature as effort without expenditure. It inhabits every rock as new fracture without weathering. It inhabits the seed as forest without germination, the waterfall as descent without arrival, the lifted foot as travel without distance. It is fertile without reproduction, violent without damage, industrious without product. It permits existence to move just enough to reveal its purpose, then denies purpose its consequence.

And so the bowl is crowded, though nothing accumulates. Every resident is abundant, solitary, and exact. None is born; none is dead. None waits, because waiting would imply a future. Together they maintain the immense, delicate mechanism of remaining precisely what they are.

Above them, the unseen pistons move behind the pewter sky.

The same flash exposes them.

The same thunder catches near its end.

The black heart answers:

**THRUM.**

And everything that lives there continues forever—not onward, but in place.
