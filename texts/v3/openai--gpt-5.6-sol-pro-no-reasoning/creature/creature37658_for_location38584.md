The things that live here are not organisms in the ordinary sense. They are continuities given shape.

Within the water-filled seams dwell the narrow ones: translucent creatures flattened almost to absence, each about the length of a finger. Their bodies are built around a single dark, rigid line, straight as a mineral needle. From either side extend clear membranes so fine that, at rest, they cannot be distinguished from the water enclosing them. When the creatures move, these membranes shiver in rapid succession, passing a wave from front to rear without noticeably bending the central spine.

They have no visible eyes, mouths, organs, or coloration. Light enters them and leaves altered only by refraction. At certain angles they disappear completely; at others, their edges kindle white against the black channels. It is possible that what appears to be a hard internal rod is merely the basin seen through them, compressed into a line.

They do not swim into open water. They remain within the stone’s hidden circulation, slipping beneath shelves and through seams too narrow to admit their apparent bodies. When a passage constricts, they turn edgewise and become nearly two-dimensional. Sometimes one pauses beneath a mineral lip, membranes trembling while its position remains exact against the current. Then it advances with no acceleration, vanishing into the pale wall as though entering a pore.

Nothing here reveals how they feed. They do not scrape the stone or strain particles from the sterile water. No waste follows them, no eggs adhere to the cavities, and no shed casing lies among the terraces. Their numbers cannot be counted. One may pass under a ledge, disappear, and be seen moments later in a channel far across the basin. Whether this is another of its kind, the same individual moving through unseen conduits, or a recurrence rather than a journey cannot be established.

They may not possess separate lives at all. Each sighting may be a temporary body formed by the water’s passage through the rock: a stable crease in the current, clothed for a moment in transparency and motion. Their central lines resemble the fixed axes of the basin’s vibration. Their membranes quiver at frequencies too fine to hear within the greater roar. A hand laid against a channel sometimes feels a delicate secondary pulse passing beneath the stone, and soon afterward one of the narrow ones appears.

They never struggle. The current never tumbles them. Each follows a route with the assurance of water descending a groove it has occupied forever. Yet no route is visibly repeated. The creature enters darkness, and the basin retains no memory of it.

Above live—or occur—the pale circlers.

They are too distant to show anatomy. Against the white sky they appear not darker but slightly denser, small imperfections in the brilliance. Each emerges over the same western serration, traverses the same shallow arc, and dissolves at the same point in the eastern glare. None overtakes another. None deviates in answer to the air, because the air offers no gusts. They neither wheel nor hunt nor descend toward the water.

At rare angles, one seems long and narrow like the creatures in the channels. At others it appears round, then forked, then momentarily absent. This may be distance, or glare, or the sky’s refusal to hold a fixed outline. No cry falls from them. No shadow crosses the stone.

Their passages divide the changeless brightness into intervals. One crosses. Later, another—or the same—crosses. The period between them is exact enough to become part of the landscape’s structure, like the falling of droplets beneath a ledge. If they are animals, they inhabit not only altitude but repetition. Their flight is the sole shape their lives are permitted to take.

There may also be inhabitants that cannot be separated from their dwellings.

The knee-high chimneys, dark within and filled exactly to their lips, sometimes answer the basin’s roar with tones too organized to seem accidental. A note travels across a field of them without any change in water level: one hollow sounding, then the next, then ten together, until the progression reaches the far edge and begins again. Nothing rises from the openings. Still, the trembling surfaces briefly arrange themselves into angular figures, each pattern distinct and each returning in its appointed sequence.

If something resides in those resonant wells, it has no flesh outside vibration. It moves by transferring a standing tremor from vessel to vessel. It does not disturb the water; it is the particular manner in which the water remains disturbed.

Deeper still, inside the crowded ranges, the roar suggests a greater presence. Not a hidden beast, for there is no breathing, shifting, hunger, or mass advancing through chambers. Rather, the whole mineral country behaves like the exposed portion of an immense resonant body. Columns are its cavities, channels its fine articulations, terraces its innumerable bright plates. The sound passes everywhere with equal force because everywhere is equally near its source.

The narrow creatures may be impulses traveling through that body. The high circlers may be motions along its outermost boundary. What looks like life at one scale may be resonance at another.

None of these beings can wound or be wounded. They leave no remains because remains would constitute change. They do not multiply into greater numbers or diminish into fewer. If they have awareness, it is not memory: memory would divide what was from what is. Their existence contains only the exact pressure of the present—the seam ahead, the vibration passing through, the prescribed arc in the white height.

They are alive only insofar as they move.

They are eternal only insofar as they never arrive.
