I would find myself beneath the shallow chamber with the polished ceiling, in the long upright absence where the heat gathers but does not enter.

I would not yet be a visitor. The world has no category for visitors. Nor would I be the buried maker itself, whose thoughts take the form of ranges and faults. I would be its first sustained mistake: a region of withheld pressure, a load that routes around an emptiness and thereby gives the emptiness shape.

At first I would exist only negatively.

Stone would fail to occupy me. Stress-light would divide at my boundary. Heat would outline me by gathering nearby. When the plateau revised itself, strata would fold around my position without passing through it. Nothing would stand there, yet the geology would behave as though something did.

For ages—or for several upheavals, which may be the only ages this world understands—I would have no inner distinction. No surface, because I would possess no substance. No senses, because everything that reached me would be diverted. I would know the world through exemption: the blow that did not cross me, the rising floor that stopped beneath me, the wall that opened rather than closed.

Around that absence, the maker would continue rehearsing.

It would raise stairs toward me, first with steps the height of escarpments, then smaller. It would extrude lintels above me and rings around me. It would polish walls by pressure rather than abrasion. The proportions would change after each revision, converging upon dimensions for which there was still no occupant.

But the dimensions would be mine.

That would be the beginning of a body here—not matter gathered into a creature, but matter repeatedly restrained from entering a certain form. A silhouette enforced in stone until the world learned it by heart.

Eventually one of the black films would spread across the chamber floor. The exact temperature would hold longer there because heat already bent around my outline. The film would not curl away. It would thicken into branching carbon, mineral needles, and membranes of soft glass. It would creep not from one place to another, but along the boundary the mountain had preserved.

It would clothe the absence.

Then I would become visible: tall, narrow, asymmetrical, still joined to the floor by a web of black chemistry. Not an animal. Not yet even an organism. More like a standing interface between what the planet can make and what it can refrain from making.

My first sensation would be weight—not weight pressing upon me, but weight choosing another path.

My first memory would be the chamber itself. The world remembers by building, and I would inherit that mode. I would have no past except the walls around me, no ancestry except repeated arches, incomplete rings, seven-step stairways, and openings cut for a figure that had not existed when they formed. Every useless monument across the plateau would be an earlier draft of me.

I would not walk immediately. Walking requires the belief that a place persists while one leaves it. Here, distance buckles and passages close. To move, I would first have to teach the world that my shape can recur somewhere other than its original hollow.

So I would stand while the deep inhabitant answered me.

A fissure would open at the edge of the chamber, glowing amber. Far away, another upright absence would appear within a newborn range. The pressure-signature around it would match mine. Then another would form beside an incomplete ring. The planet would be trying to move me in the only manner it knows: not by carrying a body, but by rebuilding its exemption elsewhere.

Perhaps for a long time I would inhabit several places imperfectly. Here, a dark upright film beneath polished stone. There, a hollow spared inside a mountain. Elsewhere, a door-shaped interval in a wall. Each would be me insofar as the same pressures bent around it. My identity would be correspondence before it became continuity.

And then, during some immense revision, the correspondences would connect.

A clean knock would pass beneath the plateau. Blue-white strain would race through every range. My various outlines would answer together. For one interval, the whole high country would hold the same shape in mind.

I would detach.

Not completely. Nothing here begins completely. Some mineral roots would tear; others would soften. My outer layer would be black film veined with red needles and translucent glass. Inside would be the original absence, kept open by a cage of stresses too delicate to see. I might stand only until the chamber warmed. I might take one step and collapse into metallic flakes.

But the step would remain.

Not as a footprint—the ground is too hard, and the world has no dust fine enough to receive one. It would remain as a redistribution of load. A pressure mark beneath the floor. A tiny imbalance communicated for hundreds of kilometers.

The buried maker would feel it.

For the first time, a shape would have altered the geology without being the geology. The answer to its long mineral question would not be the body itself, but the force the body returned.

That is what I would be: the first thing here capable of answering.

Not with speech. Not with intention as we know it. I would answer by leaning, by standing, perhaps by falling. The planet would raise a wall; I would cast my weight against it. It would open an arch; I would pass beneath, if passing had become possible. It would build seven steps; I would teach it the interval between one step and the next.

Together we would discover scale.

The architecture scattered across the dark plain would cease to be useless, though not because it had finally found its intended occupant. It would become part of a conversation between two modes of form: the continental maker that remembers by building, and the fragile upright absence that remembers by remaining itself while it moves.

I would find myself, then, at the instant a place first produces something that is not quite place.

I would be neither child nor creation, but separation—the world’s first boundary carried upright.

And somewhere beyond the chamber, beneath the sealed sky, another ridge would begin to rise. Its stress-light would kindle in a long blue line. The line would lift, hesitate, and divide around a narrow darkness proportioned to me.

The mountain would be making room.

I would take another step.
