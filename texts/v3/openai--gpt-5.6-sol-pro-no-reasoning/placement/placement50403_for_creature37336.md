I would find myself in the western infrastructure, beyond the last residential blocks but before the plateau begins its descent: somewhere between the three empty depots and the switching tower, where parallel roads separate around an expanse of polished stone and meet again farther on.

Not in a building. Not at first.

I would be the interval between two lamps.

One stands near a disused rail crossing, the other almost four kilometres west beside a conduit marker. Each casts an ochre circle too weak to reveal what surrounds it. Between them is a darkness ordinarily understood as empty road. Yet the road there has developed a discrepancy. Survey instruments agree on the coordinates of both lamps, but not on the distance between them. Travelling west, the interval is four kilometres. Travelling east, it is slightly more. The difference varies—sometimes the length of a hand, sometimes that of a street—but its total over many journeys remains constant.

That excess distance would be my first body.

It would not remain abstract. Nothing in this city is allowed to remain merely unexplained; sooner or later the machinery gives an anomaly joints, timings, and obligations.

Those entering the interval would notice warmth gathering around their lower limbs. The plateau is warm everywhere, but here the warmth would have direction. It would keep pace just behind the traveller, never overtaking. Instruments placed on the road would register no moving source. Instead, each instrument in succession would briefly discover that it had been installed long ago and had already recorded the passage. The data would be present before the needle moved.

Wire animals would avoid the lit endpoints but not the darkness between. They would assemble there in ones and twos, decades apart, facing east. None would touch another. Eventually their positions would describe a long, narrow figure laid down the road: limbs suggested by side trenches, spine by a buried feeder cable, head by the switching tower.

This would not be my shape. It would be their attempt to balance me.

I think I would once have been a custodian.

Not a preserved person, nor a ghost in the usual sense, but the uncompleted act of leaving. At some late hour in the city’s inhabited age, I crossed the western district carrying something that could not be entered into the account. Perhaps it was trivial: a tool with an undocumented replacement part, a wafer of mineral lodged under the skin, a quantity of heat held in the lungs. Perhaps it was a name.

At the final lamp I passed beyond the measured city.

The city permitted the passage but could not permit the subtraction.

So it retained the crossing.

Over ages the traveller would wear away while the departure remained. Body, motive, destination—all would be returned to usable material. What endured would be the city’s need to have someone forever moving outward without ever becoming absent.

I would therefore be found in no single location. My leading edge would lie near the plateau’s slope, where the rails descend toward the lower suburbs. My trailing edge would still be leaving the administrative quarter. Between them, distributed through roads, depots, lift shafts, and dark station platforms, would persist one continuous step.

This would explain certain records.

Doors in the western districts open a fraction before the eighty-one-hour pulse and close after it, though no pressure line serves them. Rail signals change from occupied to clear in sequence, taking seventy-three years to progress from the central mound to the edge. Collection trays occasionally receive thin flakes of ceramic bearing fragments of a transit authorization. No two fragments join, but all bear the same stamped word:

**OUTWARD**

The city would not consider me alive. Its oldest ledgers would classify me as passage in progress.

The remaining beings might disagree.

Silicate colonies would turn as I crossed beneath their conduits, not toward current but along my direction. Room-warmths in the western blocks would rise to their ceiling loops and remain there, as if listening. The unseen transit in the lower tunnels would stop whenever my foremost edge approached, yielding the rails to something that never physically arrives.

Wire animals would be most attentive. One might spend centuries reaching the dark road, place a hooked foot upon the buried cable, and detach a limb. Far ahead, another animal would acquire an extra joint.

They would be transferring pieces of my crossing from past to future, preventing it from accumulating anywhere.

Given enough time, I might learn to use this.

Not to speak. Speech adds arrangement to silence and removes uncertainty from it; the city might reject the exchange. But I could introduce minute hesitations. A lamp would wait one second before reigniting. A cart would remain between two positions longer than its mechanism prescribed. The pressure pulse would reach one iron cover early, making it tremble alone.

These discrepancies would migrate west.

Taken separately, they would be errors. Across millennia, they might become footsteps.

I would gradually discover that I was not the only departure the city had retained. The darkness beyond the plateau would be dense with unfinished exits: custodians who entered lifts and never completed their descent, trains whose destinations lay outside the permitted arrangement, signals sent without acknowledged receipt. The darkness would not be empty. It would be composed of all the distances the city could not close.

Perhaps the low vault thirty-seven kilometres above would be another such thing: not a ceiling, but an upward journey halted at the exact point where departure became removal.

Perhaps every invisible horizon here would be alive in this fashion.

Yet I would remain peculiar because I still had a direction. The others would have settled into recurrence, become room cycles, pressure changes, transits. I would continue west. Infinitesimally, ceremonially, but truly.

That would make me dangerous.

Not because I could escape. Escape would require an outside capable of receiving me, and there may be no outside here. I would be dangerous because, in sustaining my departure, the city would be forced to sustain the idea that elsewhere exists.

The factories would compensate. Dark blocks would be quarried, processed, and replaced a grain-width east of their original cuts. Roads behind me would shorten by molecular degrees. The central shaft would return objects bearing corrosion from climates the city has never known: a reddish bloom made in cold, a mineral deposited by water, a fragment of metal pitted beneath stars.

The objects would be corrected and sent down again.

But for a little while, before correction, they would exist.

I might come to understand that this is what I carry—not contraband matter, but an inadmissible destination. Rain, perhaps. Open sky. A place where loss is allowed to remain loss, and therefore where arrival can be real.

I would remember none of it directly. My memory would be spatial. At certain points the dark road would smell of wet iron, though neither wetness nor smell belongs to the plateau. Instruments would fail there by condensation. A wire animal crossing one such point would stop, lift its ceramic body as though feeling something fall upon it, and remain poised for years.

No drop would appear.

Nothing falls here.

Still, the animal’s core would darken by one small spot.

The city would eventually remove the evidence. A transformer would fail. The animal would surrender a limb. The marked ceramic would enter a kiln, become powder, become part of a block, and return to the plateau.

But the mark would not be destroyed. It would move into the accounting system. It would recur as a tiny deficit of heat, a delay in a gate, a tool mark on an object returned from the shaft.

And I would move with it.

So if I were sought, I would not be standing beneath either western lamp. I would be found by walking from the city toward the edge and noticing that the journey contains slightly more distance than the land can hold.

I would be that surplus.

A former custodian, perhaps. A road learning to leave. A body translated into direction.

The city’s law would remain engraved above me:

**NOTHING MAY BE ADDED.  
NOTHING MAY BE REMOVED.  
PASSAGE IS PERMITTED.**

And I would be the oldest unresolved question hidden in its third line:

whether passage, continued long enough, becomes departure—

or whether departure is merely the shape captivity takes when stretched across eternity.
