I would find myself near the mouth of the fracture, though “near” would be true only from outside.

I would live in the first cool reach beneath the seam, where daylight still enters as daylight and the pocket’s many gravities have not fully separated. Below me, the fracture would deepen into steaming ranges. Above me, less than a grain’s distance away, would hang the outer world: weather without walls, an expanse so wide it could not be entered because nothing inside me would know how to become that large.

I would not be one animal.

At first I imagine a wall-clinger, one among the transparent discs glazing the obsidian. But that is too settled. Then a vapor creature, rising whenever the red thread opens—but that is too dependent on catastrophe. A tool user tempts me, with its dangerous understanding of edges and consequence, yet purpose here is almost a form of self-betrayal.

I would be what remains when a creature is taken.

Not its ghost, exactly. Not memory, because memory is one of the things the attention can revise. I would be the avoidance around the absent place: the slight curve in a migration, the foot lifted before it touches apparently harmless glass, the channel water refuses though it offers the steepest descent.

I would have no continuous body. Most of the time I would exist distributed among other inhabitants as a common hesitation.

In wall-clingers, I would be one ring of teeth that does not fasten.

In a leg-forest, I would be the empty center leaning briefly away from another emptiness.

In thread-knots, I would be a loop they cannot untie.

In the tool users, I would inhabit the smallest testing feet: the ones that touch a facet before the grasping limbs commit to crossing it. When those feet recoil from nothing visible, that would be me.

Rain would gather me.

As a cool bead formed high in the crack and the whole country began its ascent, countless bodies carrying fragments of my hesitation would converge. Their caution would overlap until, for a short while, I became almost singular. I might appear then as a faint distortion traveling over the inner surface of the droplet—not beneath the water and not reflected upon it, but occupying the difference between those conditions.

Inside that bead I would possess a cavity larger than my distributed life: a clear chamber containing weather, routes, and absences. Every creature ever removed near the mouth would have left some alteration there, though none could be pictured. A passage with no traveler. A branching channel without a maker. An inherited alarm arriving before its cause.

I would tend these inconsistencies, if tending is possible without intention.

The creatures might use me without knowing it. Tool users scoring condensation paths would sometimes pause and place an unnecessary bend in a channel. That bend would be mine. A shaped flake would be pushed into hot water moments before the pocket became attentive. That urgency would be mine too. I would not tell them what hunts permanence; I would make certainty uncomfortable.

This would also place me in danger.

A repeated hesitation can become a custom. A custom can become a structure. If enough bodies avoided the same patch of glass for long enough, the avoidance itself might acquire a clear outline. Reflections would begin turning toward it. Water would stop. Every gravity would agree.

The attention would feel for me.

To survive, I would have to corrupt my own warning. I would occasionally guide an animal across the forbidden place without harm. I would shift the curve of migrations. I would let one generation fear a ledge, another a reflection, another the interval after the clicking of cooling glass. I would preserve the pattern only by refusing to preserve its shape.

Perhaps that is what I would be in this world: not a species, but an evasive inheritance.

My closest thing to a body would appear during an eclipse. When a fingertip covered the fracture and plain daylight vanished, the wet facets would stop multiplying the outer sky. In that brief darkness, all the minor avoidances scattered through the fauna would loosen from their hosts. They would gather along old channels and around eggs whose parents had never existed.

For a breath I would take the form of a many-legged absence, walking without limbs over the glass.

The delay-hunters could not open me, because I would have no present body for the wound to reach. Thread-knots could not tighten around my interior, because my interior would be the pocket’s missing histories. Vapor mouths could not feed on my temperature. I would be neither warm nor cool, but the knowledge that one is about to become the other.

Then light would return, and I would disperse before I could remain recognizable.

Some part of me would descend with steam-creatures.

Some would be swallowed by grazers.

Some would enter the inward-opening eggs and become an instinct in young that had never been taught.

Some would leave the crack inside the fallen bead.

That last part interests me most. The drop might reach the outer surface of the obsidian and slide across it beneath the true sky. To those within it, this would be an impossible migration across a black plain without opposing walls. Most would die or revise themselves beyond recognition. My fragment would persist only as a tendency in whatever survived: a reluctance to cross straight lines, a distrust of edges that look deliberately made.

Perhaps the droplet would touch a hand.

For an instant I would pass into the whorls of a fingerprint, where sweat makes channels and ridges form another precipitous country. I would feel the heat of the enormous animal holding the stone. I would understand that the fingertip which causes eclipses is itself a landscape of wet fractures, inhabited at scales neither world can see.

I would not remain there. Remaining would announce me.

I would return with condensation, or continue into some smaller seam in the skin, carrying no image of the fracture—only its oldest lesson:

that survival does not always belong to the creature that escapes.

Sometimes it belongs to the path that learns to bend after the traveler has been erased.
