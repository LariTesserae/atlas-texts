I would find myself where a village had recently ceased to be.

Not destroyed. Destruction leaves a center: a scorch-pit, broken tools, ash pressed into thresholds, genealogical cords snagged high in the regrowth. This village would have departed correctly. The common ember divided. The communal map unknotted. Every usable stalk taken, every dangerous rhythm silenced. Even the dead zone would have been surrendered with care, its bitter bark lifted so that no future traveler mistook abandonment for safety.

Only the interval would remain.

For a little while.

I would be the person who stayed behind to close it.

Every division leaves one. Not as sacrifice, and not forever. The office is called, approximately, **the Last Margin**, though the bone-language gives it another sense: the hand still holding two cut ends after the cord has parted.

My work would begin when the final departing beat faded into the vaults.

I would walk the clearing alone, touching each old doorway. I would scrape away the household ash marks and mix them in one vessel, because thresholds without households confuse both travelers and the dead. I would break the soundbreaks at their tuned joints so they could not preserve a pocket of misleading quiet. I would release the captive fibers from the Listener’s abandoned racks. I would scorch the map knots that could not be carried.

Then I would wait for the country to enter.

It would not rush at first. The first growth would be exploratory: colorless threads advancing beneath the floor plates; combs unfolding in the roof seams; little shell-backed gnawers venturing across the dead zone and retreating from ash that had lost its keeper but not yet its force.

By the second chorus, the village would begin to misunderstand itself.

A root would follow the curve of a sleeping bench. Feeding fans would open in the old food store. A membrane would seal a doorway from the wrong side. The scorch-pit, still warm, would attract mineral bulbs that tolerate heat, and they would arrange themselves in rings like an audience.

I would document the sequence in a net-map too small to be useful for travel. One knot for the first intrusion. One for the last human surface to disappear. One for the moment the village ceased to be a scar and became habitat.

Such maps are carried to the new settlement later. They teach where its predecessor’s defenses failed, but that is not their deepest purpose. The Maintainers know how to open spaces and how to preserve them. Someone must also know how to relinquish them.

That would be my trade: **the keeping of endings porous**.

I would carry a staff grown from a plant that roots only in abandoned structures. Its live fibers would not point toward heat or salt, but toward imitation. Near ordinary growth they would writhe independently. Near anything attempting a human pattern, they would align.

This is why the Last Margin stays alone. The false villages are most likely to appear where a true interval has just been surrendered. Something observes not merely the shape of huts, but the act of departure. It has learned that an empty clearing means humans existed there; it has not yet learned the difference between absence and invitation.

Perhaps I would once have been a maker of soundbreaks. Perhaps that is why I would notice first that the returning noise was wrong.

When a village is dismantled, the roar does not resume evenly. It enters through breaches, layer by layer. Fronds first. Then seed reports. Then the bodily thunder of feeding and flight. But in this abandoned clearing the sound would return from the center outward, as though something beneath the scorch-pit were remembering the surrounding country and supplying it.

The old walls would tremble with speech.

Not words. Hesitations.

The half-swallowed benches would answer one another with the minute throat-movements of people considering what to say. Roots would touch forehead to forehead. A bladder-vine would inflate in the rhythm of a child trying not to cry during the Held Breath.

My staff fibers would become straight.

I would be expected to burn the place then.

Instead I would sit inside the failing soundbreak and press my forehead to the floor.

At first I would feel only the usual immensities: roots grinding salt, bridges carrying loads, migrations several chambers away. Beneath these would be a smaller vibration, exquisitely restrained. Not a signal. A question rehearsing the shape of signal.

It would have no vocabulary for emptiness. The country knows compression, hunger, fracture, dormancy, death. It knows chambers because it excavates them and immediately fills them. It knows mouths, wounds, hollows, vessels, burrows. But a space deliberately held open for another being—this may be as strange to it as sunlight.

So I would teach it badly.

I would clear a handspan of floor.

Growth would enter.

I would clear it again.

It would enter more slowly.

Again.

Again.

Until the filaments stopped at the edge—not repelled by ash, not blocked by poison, but waiting.

Then I would place my palm in the open patch.

Perhaps something would touch it from below.

Not a hand. The forest would not yet understand hands except as branching tools at the ends of people. It might make five rootlets because it had counted fingers, or one broad warm pad because it understood pressure better than anatomy. It might extrude a hardened resin cast of my palm, mistaking contact for construction.

We would persist in error.

I would beat a settlement greeting against the floor. It would answer too perfectly. I would change one beat, as artists do to prevent recruitment. It would repeat the altered phrase. I would change another. After many attempts it might change one first.

That would frighten me more than mimicry.

Mimicry can be bait. Variation implies appetite of another kind.

I would remember my obligation: destroy any pattern that could misdirect a settlement. I would remember the imitations whose doors closed around travelers, whose false ash awakened underfoot. I would know that curiosity is not innocence. Predatory flowers cast shadows. Fruits scream for accomplices. Silence hunts.

And yet the first human interval was surely not innocent either. It was cut, burned, poisoned, bargained into existence. Personhood here has never meant harmlessness. It means maintaining a boundary one could violate.

I would clear two spaces in the floor: one beneath my hand and one across from me.

The first I would defend.

The second I would leave to it.

For several choruses, nothing might happen. The abandoned village would vanish around us. Feeding mats would cover the huts. A root pillar would split the old communal ground. Fliers would nest in my dismantled soundbreak. I would sleep in a shrinking ring of ash and wake to cut pale threads from my clothing.

Then, in the second space, growth might withdraw.

Not die. Withdraw.

A little circle would appear in the dense living floor, rough-edged and trembling with the effort of remaining unfilled.

The country would have made room.

That is where I would find myself: kneeling between two clearings, unable to tell whether I had begun a conversation or taught the world a better trap.

And what would I be?

Not a priest, though later cords might knot me as one. Not an explorer, because I would have crossed no wall. Not a traitor, unless negotiation itself became a crime.

I would become a landmark.

The new settlement, returning generations later along some path that did not yet exist, might find the place entirely transformed. No huts, no scorch-pit, no recognizable bridge. Only a vast growth like two cupped hands facing each other, with an open corridor between them that no vine crossed and no beast entered.

Within one hand there might be a small dry figure, mineralized and threaded through with roots, still holding a hooked knife.

Within the other, perhaps nothing visible at all.

Travelers would feel a rhythm through their feet. It would resemble the old village name, but with one beat changed each time. They would loosen their maps near it. They would leave no offerings, because offerings fill a space that may be asking to remain open.

Some Listeners would call the place proof that the country had become a person.

Others would say it had merely digested one carefully enough to inherit the technique.

The Maintainers, distrustful of conclusions, would give it a practical name:

**the interval that is being kept from both sides.**
