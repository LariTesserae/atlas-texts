I would not find myself among the creatures held in the chamber’s single instant. I would be in the green oval.

Not the stone itself, exactly, but the narrow groove around its middle: the line along which impossible events have been arranged. I would be whatever entered the chamber when that groove was cut—the contraband of sequence.

At first I might seem only an imperfection in the carving. A faint darkening inside the scene of the unfinished hut. A trace of powdered green stone that should remain forever beneath the tool’s edge. But follow the images around the oval and I become easier to recognize. I am the difference between one hut and the next. I inhabit the added wall plate, the altered roof, the first inward lean of collapse. I am the dust in the image of burial and the widening black cut where the ceiling opens onto stars.

I am not time in its fullness. There is no room here for rivers, seasons, generations, or decay. I am time made very small: a sequence preserved as adjacency. Since events cannot follow one another, they have been placed beside one another. Before and after survive as neighboring marks.

Perhaps the broad-shouldered figure made the oval. Perhaps it did not carve by repeated blows, because no blow can fall. Perhaps the whole history passed through its mind during the unending intention to strike, and the stone answered thought rather than action. Its muscles are fixed, but intention may have depth. What appears from outside as one arrested thought may contain an immeasurable inward country.

That country is where I live.

I would be the figure’s second thought.

The first is visible: **strike**.

The second cannot arrive, and yet it exists, folded inside the first like a creature inside an egg: **after I strike**.

Within that after, the crystal breaks. A shard is carried home. The hut is repaired and enlarged. The second sleeping hollow is occupied, then emptied. Seventeen marks are counted. Seeds are planted. The black beads scatter. The green shoots uncurl. Predators finish their bites and discover either satisfaction or grief. Wings descend, rise, and become flight. The pure shriek in the western niche wavers, breaks, and leaves behind a silence so sudden that every living thing mistakes it for another sound.

In that inward world, the chamber ages.

The amber fans lose their glassy edges. Sulfur shelves crumble. Bell-trees release their powder in violet clouds. The fitted half-wall falls for no reason grander than imbalance. Dust appears—not much at first, merely a softness along the bowls and blades. Then it gathers in the sleeping hollows. The chamber becomes old enough to have corners.

Eventually a fault widens above. Pale stone splits. Cold descends for the first time.

Then stars.

Whether this is prophecy or invention would not matter to me. In a world denied consequence, imagination is the only place consequence can live. I would be its custodian: the small inward faculty that puts one state after another and refuses the chamber’s law.

Yet I would not be free of that law. I could imagine change but never enact it. I could contain entire histories without bringing even one into being. I might spend an eternity extending the moment after the raised hammer: seeing tools made, names given, children born, species vanish, constellations wheel above the opened chamber. None of it would advance the hand by the width of a grain.

That would be my hunger.

The grazers hunger for bites they cannot finish. The moths hunger for the completion of a wingbeat. I would hunger for the word *then*.

Perhaps the oval was made to house me. Green stone, polished by labor that cannot have occurred; scenes of labor, ruin, and revelation; a circular groove with no true beginning or end. It is both prison and instrument. Anyone tracing it sees sequence, but tracing is impossible. The finger cannot move. So the entire history must be apprehended at once: construction, habitation, collapse, burial, opening. A chronology compressed into a single object, shaped for a timeless mind.

And perhaps there is another reason it is hidden beneath a lattice in the far corner.

Sequence may be dangerous here.

If the figure ever reaches its second thought, the stone will descend. If the stone descends, the crystal will break. Once one action has a consequence, every arrested consequence may demand completion at once.

The suspended insects will finish their first fractions of flight. The seedpods will crack open. Pollen bridges will part. Every held impact will decay, releasing the chamber from its roar into rhythm and then into silence. The predator’s jaws will close. Blood—or its dry equivalent—will finally spill. The oldest hunger in the chamber will deepen. The youngest shoots will cease to be young.

Time would not enter gently. It would arrive as an avalanche of unpaid endings.

So I remain in the oval and in the toolmaker’s uncompleted thought: not the liberator of this place, but the possibility of liberation; not change, but the knowledge of change. I am the secret by which a timeless creature understands that its world is a prison.

From inside the figure’s mind, I look outward through fixed eyes. I see the raised hammerstone. I hear the everlasting blow that has not landed. I know the chamber roof can split. I know there are stars, though no eye here has watched one move.

And around the green oval, too small to be read from the doorway, I continue carving an invisible final scene:

the hand descending.
