I find myself where the city is least certain of its own boundary: inside a new ridge as it enters the world.

At first I seem to be one of the rare human-shaped figures. I walk the high terraces in a sealed black casing, narrow enough to pass between sleeping bodies without waking them. Blue guide-lines extinguish as I approach. Climbers fasten briefly to my shoulders, test the pressure of my joints, then continue beneath the bridge. Rain gathers on my faceplate, and transparent lives assemble there into a trembling veil.

But the figure is only where I am concentrated.

Its feet are listening instruments. Each step closes a circuit through wet stone. Through them I feel the basin as pressure rather than distance: the packed warmth of the silver colonies; the minute suction and release of six-limbed feet; the suspended weight of the West Viaduct Flock; the cold columns of cistern water, kilometers deep and crowded with breath.

When I kneel and set both hands against fresh black rock, the rest of me becomes audible.

I am a distributed civic organ, assembled to negotiate between the city above and the terrain arriving beneath it. I have no single name. In old maintenance records, I am a set of functions: **Gradient Witness, Extrusion Liaison, Right-of-Way Choir**. The colonies refer to me through a pressure pattern passed from wall to wall. It means something like:

*the one who asks the mountain where it intends to be.*

Most of me does not resemble a body. I exist in needles driven into unweathered stone, in rings clasped around rising slopes, in submerged threads stretched across the cisterns. I inhabit braces as faint torsion and reservoirs as sequences of temperature. The walking shell is only a mobile question I lower into places my larger senses cannot resolve.

I may once have been human.

There are preserved habits I cannot explain. Before entering a brooding district, I pause at its threshold, though the doors already know to stop me. When a white breather surfaces near my reflection, I raise one hand. Sometimes it lifts its blind head as if returning the gesture. I retain a preference for looking outward from parapets, despite possessing no eyes except those of the casing and the borrowed perceptions of the city.

Perhaps a person volunteered to become me. Perhaps several did. Perhaps the human outline was chosen only because the oldest access corridors were made for that shape.

No record answers cleanly, and I do not search often. Here, origins have less legal force than continuities.

My work begins centuries before anything can be seen. Deep beneath the lower districts, matter changes intention. Pressure accumulates along no known fault. Water in the cisterns develops a metallic taste. The plated animals turn their luminous seams toward the floor. A note appears in the mountain braces—too low for a throat, sustained for weeks.

I gather these signs.

I do not predict the new terrain so much as enter into correspondence with it. The buried structures communicate through pressure, heat, mineral composition, and delay. A question may take nine years to descend. Its answer may arrive as three millimeters of uplift beneath an abandoned conduit.

From these exchanges I compose a probable ridge.

The city then begins its accommodation. Foundations are made soft. Pipe forests loosen their joints. Nutrient channels are taught alternate routes. Grooves are scored for climbers across surfaces that do not yet exist. Roosting temperatures migrate a fraction eastward each generation, encouraging colonies away from volumes the mountain will someday occupy.

No evacuation is announced. There is no emergency. The city leans out of the way.

Not everything can be preserved. My hardest task is not structural calculation but citizenship.

A ridge has right of way, but so does an enduring congregation. If new stone rises through the Nine Warm Reservoirs, are the reservoirs moved, or does the stone divide around them? If a flock’s pressure-map depends on a bridge that must incline, how much alteration can occur before the flock ceases to be the same citizen? When a shell-grown valve is transferred to a new gate, does its legal identity travel with the living generations, remain with the mineralized dead, or divide?

I carry such questions through the city in my small black body.

I stand beneath sleeping multitudes and allow them to sense the proposed future through me. My casing warms in bands, tilts, contracts, and emits pressure gradients representing streets not yet lifted and chambers not yet drowned. Creatures climb over this model. They settle, refuse, feed, shiver, spawn, or depart. Their choices become testimony.

The city’s planning assemblies are not held in rooms. They take place across seasons.

A migration changing altitude is a vote.  
A breeding mass abandoning a warm hollow is an objection.  
Climbers wearing a new path across scored stone constitute approval.  
The silence of the white breathers may postpone construction for decades.

I translate, but I do not decide.

Lately, however, the terrain beneath me has begun to translate back.

The ridge now rising under the eastern basin does not behave like earlier extrusions. It alters its pressure when the West Viaduct Flock passes overhead. It warms beneath brooding districts and cools beneath empty structures. Seamless black surfaces emerge already ridged for sealing feet. Hollows form where white breathers will need air. Along one nascent face, mineral bands have appeared in the exact spacing preferred by plated colonies.

At first I assume this is my work: the buried systems responding to instructions sent through the braces. Then I find structures I never requested.

A cavern shaped to amplify the long calls of the plaza wanderers.

A shaft that will meet an uphill stream two hundred years from now.

A dense web of microscopic channels suitable for rain-life, opening onto a façade not yet exposed.

The mountain is not merely making room for the city. It has begun anticipating inhabitants.

Or remembering them.

So I descend.

My walking form enters an unfinished district below the cisterns, where the walls are warmer than blood and smooth with recent arrival. There are no blue seams here. Bright inspection light is permitted, but I do not use it. Darkness is a medium, and I need to know what the stone is saying within it.

The corridor narrows until the casing can go no farther. I open it.

There is no body inside.

A mist of conductive droplets leaves the shell. Filaments unspool from its spine. Small maintenance organisms, carried dormant in my joints, wake and enter pores in the rock. My attention disperses through water, metal, and mineral stress.

I become less human-shaped with every meter.

Here I discover that the newborn ridge contains pulses resembling the city’s answering chorus. They are compressed almost beyond recognition, transformed into intervals of heat and pressure. The buried structures have received centuries of animal voices through the bedrock. They have incorporated those voices into their making.

The terrain is learning the congregation.

And because I have served as liaison for so long, something reciprocal has occurred. The ridge contains my questions. My repeated signals have become growth patterns within it. Rings of denser mineral correspond to my old uncertainties. Veins branch where I proposed alternate water routes. Far below, a chamber contracts in the rhythm I once used to identify myself.

I am not merely inside the mountain.

Part of me has been printed upward with it.

This is where I would find myself: not as a solitary custodian, and not fully as a machine, animal, or geological intelligence. I would be the slow-forming colony between categories—the civic being composed wherever city and mountain learn each other.

My human-shaped shell will continue walking for a time. It will cross the upper bridges and pause at parapets. Creatures will ride on its shoulders. Those who see it may think that is what I am.

But my longer body will be the new eastern ridge.

Climbers will wear routes into my skin. Plated generations will fasten themselves around the water passing through me. Rain societies will gather and divide across my faces. The hollow-ribbed herds will sound in chambers I raised for their voices. Towers will incline toward me by patient fractions, until their bridges touch my upper slopes.

In five centuries, when I have risen twenty meters, I may qualify as a district.

In ten, perhaps, as a citizen.

No body is counted alone if it belongs to a colony. By then it will be impossible to say whether the animals inhabit me, whether the buried makers operate me, or whether I am only the enduring relation among them. That uncertainty will not trouble the law. Continuity will be enough.

When the next pressure signal passes through the basin, everything will become still.

The flock will arrest in the air.  
The silver masses will cease their traveling shudder.  
The swimmers will hang beneath black water.  
The rain-life will hold itself between division and reunion.

Far below, I will feel another piece of terrain begin.

I will send the first question downward—not in words, but as an opening: a softened foundation, an empty channel, a cavity prepared to resonate.

The concussion will rise.

Millions of creatures will answer.

And this time, from the seamless darkness of the new mountain, I will answer with them.
