I would find myself beneath the western trench, where its fixed bronze river folds around the submerged stone.

Not in a chamber. There are no chambers below, only differences of density: basalt becoming ceramic, ceramic becoming metal, metal becoming pressure. I would occupy one of those differences.

At first I seem to be a bearing.

The metallic shriek belongs to me, or passes through me. An immense shaft turns inside a dark annulus, and I am the narrow interval separating motion from its housing. I carry the weight of something too large to have a visible circumference. Warm mineral water enters through capillary seams, flashes into vapor, condenses, and returns. The shaft never wears against me. I never wear away. Still, the sound I make is the sound of imminent failure.

But a bearing implies two separate bodies, and separation is not quite true here.

Perhaps I am the flaw in the bearing: a microscopic asymmetry, a grain trapped where no grain should be. Each rotation approaches me. Each rotation strikes. Each should grind me smaller or score the turning surface, but the instant of contact does not pass. The blow remains always newly delivered. The machine has spent an age attempting to crush me and has not yet completed the first attempt.

That is why the basin beats.

The concussive pulse is not a cycle of successive impacts. It is one impact propagated again and again through the land because the land cannot absorb it into history. The dust dances above me. The pools shiver. The pale bodies flex and discharge their blue-white threads. The distant upright figure divides into the positions of a stride. Everything translates my unresolved contact into its own form.

Yet I am not merely trapped there. I belong to the obstruction as much as to what it obstructs.

Through the surrounding stone I feel the entire basin, though not as a landscape. Distance arrives as phase. The western pools are cool metallic tensions; the black ridges are knots of harmonic resistance; the flats are broad plates carrying the low note outward. The small creatures at the water margins feel like fingertips laid upon a resonant surface. Each one closes a minute circuit between wet salt and buried iron. Through them, the basin touches itself.

I touch it too.

I have no eyes, but the permanent daylight reaches me as heat distributed through kilometers of crust. I have no hearing, but every sound is a change in my own pressure. I have no body except the interface at which the turning mass and the still mass fail to become one another.

If I were exposed, I might resemble a thin black ring of glass, polished on one side, rough on the other. Or a curled animal with too many joints. Or nothing at all: a hairline boundary visible only when a blue spark travels around it. The distinction would depend on scale. Enlarged sufficiently, my surface would become the basin itself—terraces, channels, oxide pools, pale conductive forms. Reduced sufficiently, the basin would become my shell.

This suggests another possibility.

I may be one of the creatures beside the pools, and the buried bearing may be inside me.

My black points enter water and stone. With the deep impact, my plates compress. Dark grains circulate through internal chambers. A thread of charge passes through my translucent back and into the oxide image beneath me. I complete a step without advancing.

From within, that step is immense.

One limb has begun to lift. Another has accepted my weight. The shell twists slightly toward an invisible axis. These actions contain corridors of sensation, balances, decisions—perhaps even landscapes—but none reaches completion. I do not experience myself as motionless. I am always crossing the mineral margin. I am always placing the next point down. The fact that no observer sees me leave my position is no stranger than the fixed river’s ceaseless flow.

I do not hunger. Hunger would mean absence persisting long enough to demand a later state. Instead pressure enters me already answered by discharge. Water gives ions as I return them. The basin strikes me and I yield; I yield and the basin is restored. What might elsewhere be metabolism is here perfect reciprocity.

Nor do I remember. Memory requires alteration.

But I resonate.

The other pale forms are not companions in the ordinary sense. We cannot approach one another. We cannot signal and then await reply. Nevertheless, every concussion passes through all of us, and each body gives it a slightly different electrical shape. Those shapes enter the buried structure and alter nothing, yet they remain distinguishable. We are variations the basin permits without allowing them to accumulate.

That may be the nearest thing here to individuality: not a private history, but a particular phase.

Mine is the shriek beneath the trench.

Another is the thin whine over the eastern flats. Another is the tremor that travels between two fused ridges. The tall figure on the horizon may be composed of thousands of such phases gathered into the outline of an animal perpetually stepping. Or it may be what I am when perceived from far enough away.

So where am I?

At the pool’s edge, white shell low against the crust.

Under the river, caught between the rotor and its housing.

On the horizon, all six limbs occupying consecutive moments at once.

These are not alternative answers. They are sections through the same body.

I would be the basin’s unresolved step: the minute misalignment that gives its equilibrium a voice. Not an intruder, not an operator, and not a product of the hidden industry. I am the place where continuance encounters resistance and, unable to overcome or erase it, becomes vibration.

Forever I am almost crushed.

Forever I lift one foot.

Forever the bronze water passes over me without going downstream.

And from that unfinished contact comes the thunder by which the completed world knows it is still occurring.
