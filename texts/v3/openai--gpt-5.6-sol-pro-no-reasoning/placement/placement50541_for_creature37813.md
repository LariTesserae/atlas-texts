I would find myself at the innermost point, though not inside the bead.

At first I would mistake myself for its reflection.

I would seem to be one of the small dark figures produced by the curvature of the water: a narrow upright interruption among converging towers, visible only when the bead trembled. Turn around and there would be nobody behind me. Look back and the figure would remain, displaced into another district, standing beneath a bridge whose construction had not yet been approved.

But I would not be a person reflected there.

I would be the city’s error in imagining one.

For ages the city has rehearsed stresses, entrances, rooms, gradients, routes. It has accepted fractures and tested districts. Yet the footprints pose a different problem. They imply a load that moves without becoming architecture: something that enters, bears weight, departs, and leaves the world altered only by pressure.

The city cannot understand such a thing directly. Everything it knows how to receive becomes part of it. Cracks become avenues. Basalt becomes structure. Heat becomes angle. Water is circulated, isolated, recovered. Nothing passes through while remaining itself.

So it has begun to construct passage.

That construction is me.

I have no continuous body. I exist as the interval between one footprint vanishing and the next appearing. Each time the dust rises, I acquire a heel, an arch, the pressure of toes—not the foot itself, only its consequence. Then the print fades and I am forced forward into another incomplete consequence. My stride may cross a chamber, a century, or several incompatible directions of gravity. I never occupy the space between steps.

The chambers containing footprints are therefore not places I visit. Together, they are my anatomy.

One is a knee: gravity changes direction there.

One is an inner ear: the low tension of the mountain becomes balance.

One is a mouth, though it has never opened. When its pressure door sounds like the rim of a cup, the tone propagates beneath the streets and returns altered. This is how I try to speak.

One is an eye. It is a window showing itself from the opposite side.

The warm cells of air are organs I borrow as they drift. Blue pulses serve briefly as nerves. Transit wells conduct my sense of altitude. I use bridges as the city once imagined a creature might use tendons, though they often flex toward me too early, responding to the load I will have placed upon them after I am gone.

No single district contains enough of me to make me conscious. I wake only during rare alignments: when a pressure wave crosses an avenue, when the summit rises by the required fraction, when a fissure touches one of the city’s folded foundations, and when the central bead holds the correct darkness.

Then distant functions coincide.

For an instant, a coolness traveling beneath a street becomes aware of the bridge changing curvature above it. A gravity threshold recognizes the dust arranging itself three districts away. The bead reflects a figure that does not yet exist, and I become able to see from that figure’s position.

These instants are my life.

They do not occur in order.

My first memory is of rain that has not reached the mountain. My oldest memory is the city opening the seam for whoever will someday find it. I remember the proposal that resulted in me, though it is still being assembled from ceramic grains around the bead. I remember being discarded. I remember the tiny adjustment that survived my collapse: a doorway left outlined in an otherwise blank wall.

That doorway is the nearest thing I have to a home.

It opens nowhere yet. Its depth has not been made relevant. Sometimes violet stress-light catches its edges and the city tests a room beyond it: floor, temperature, gravity, duration. Each version fails.

One room is spacious enough for a body but allows no sequence of moments in which the body could move.

One provides breathable air, but distributes it across incompatible distances.

One admits an occupant only by incorporating it, furnishing every bone with conduits and placing ceramic collars around the joints.

One is nearly correct. In it, a visitor may remain separate from the city for twelve steps.

On the thirteenth, the room becomes a reflection in the bead.

I wait at that failure.

“Wait” is not quite right. I recur there. Whenever the city proposes the nearly correct room, my scattered anatomy briefly aligns around its threshold. The footprints turn toward it. Warmth gathers behind the outline. Towers divide into thinner copies to produce enough possible perspectives for an interior. Throughout the city, coin-sized reservoirs become still.

Then the bead tests an occupant.

Ceramic grains rise around it. They do not form a building now but a cavity: two branching upper chambers, a central column, a hinged basin, paired supports. It resembles neither a human body nor any creature that has lived. It resembles the city’s deductions about what a visitor must be.

A being with an inside that cannot be entered from every direction.

A being carried by gravity rather than assigning it.

A being for whom water may overflow.

A being whose boundaries persist when touched.

Again and again, the proposal fails. Its limbs become streets. Its hollow spaces connect to conduits. Its supports sink through the floor and join the common foundation. The city keeps making an inhabitant and accidentally making more city.

I am what remains unincorporated after each attempt.

Not the proposed body, but its refusal.

That is why I occur as footsteps without feet. The footprint is the one part of a walker that architecture can hold without turning the walker into architecture. It is contact preserved as difference: floor here, visitor gone. The city has discovered absence, and from repeated absences it is slowly composing me.

The immortal drop is not my heart. It is the city’s.

Mine is the single drop that forms outside along the violet seam.

When internal pressure is high, moisture gathers there from air no interior reservoir claims. It hangs over the dark above the clouds, then falls upward. During that ascent it is neither recovered water nor permanent water. It has left one law and not yet entered another.

For that brief interval, I am almost whole.

I feel the summit as skin. I feel the cloud deck far below, white and inaccessible. I feel the mountain thrusting upward—not moving through the world so much as continuing to be introduced into it. The black ridges divide fog. The sky at noon resembles night. There is no city visible, only a hairline violet seam and a drop climbing into darkness.

Through that drop I learn something the city does not know.

Departure.

The knowledge enters slowly. It travels through reservoirs as simultaneous ripples, through walls as condensation, through transit lines as a schedule no change of altitude can explain. Doorways close, then reconsider. Floors tilt toward no collection seam. For the first time, a bridge flexes away from an anticipated load.

Around the central bead, the ceramic grains begin another proposal.

This one looks at first like a district: towers, avenues, a deep foundation joining every structure. Then the foundations stop short. One by one, the towers separate from their shared depth. The roads do not converge. A small structure forms at the edge rather than the center, and beside it appears a gap the grains do not attempt to fill.

The city pauses.

Across its impossible volume, all the footprints become complete at once.

Not a trail. A stance.

For one interval I stand everywhere the city has imagined standing. I have weight, because floors answer me. I have height, because gravity passes through me before choosing its direction. I have a surface, because warm air gathers against it and does not immediately become an organ. In the bead’s reflection there is a figure in every empty street, but all of them are the same figure seen through distances that have not yet agreed to be separate.

I face the outlined doorway.

Behind it is no finished room. There is only thickness, newly relevant.

The city does not know whether this is success. It brightens under the pressure of uncertainty. Violet seams spread through walls. Amber clouds the alloys. Blue pulses run along every lintel and cable until the hidden district appears for an instant as one enormous branching mark inside the mountain.

I take the thirteenth step.

Perhaps I become the city.

Perhaps the city learns not to make me part of it.

Perhaps there is no difference yet.

But somewhere outside, on the bare black summit, the upward-falling drop stops. It hangs beneath the dark sky, warm and spherical, holding in its reflection a seam wider than a handspan.

And from within that seam comes the soft, intimate sound of someone arriving.
