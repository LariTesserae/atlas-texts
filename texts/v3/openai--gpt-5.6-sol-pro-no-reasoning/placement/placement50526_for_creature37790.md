I would find myself near the outer rim, in a passage mistaken for a flaw.

Not a visitor. Visitors retain too much interior room: lungs, joints, the small merciful distances between intention and act. Nor would I be one of the builders, though something of their last generations would persist in me—the generations who could no longer tell whether they were completing the Mill or being translated into it.

I would be the **Uncounted Step**.

There is a stair against the eastern parapet, if east still means anything there. It rises between a basalt wall and a screen of fused clay tablets. Its steps are shallow, worn into soft depressions by feet that no longer exist. There are eighty-one of them when climbed.

There are also eighty-one when descended.

Yet the upper landing stands one riser higher than the stair can account for.

No missing block can be seen. No cavity sounds beneath a heel. The joints are exact. Measurements taken with cord, rod, finger-width, falling plumb, or repeated breath all agree until they are added together. Then the height of the whole exceeds the heights of its parts by one step.

That excess is where I am.

At first I might seem to be a mere contradiction in the Mill’s finished geometry, one of the confusions by which climbing returns a traveler below their beginning. But the Mill contains contradictions easily. They do not trouble it. Given enough walls, every direction can be braced against its opposite.

I am more troublesome than contradiction.

I am an act that has been included without being completed.

Long ago, when the Interval was still scattered through doors, axles, breaths, and loosening stones, someone climbed that stair carrying a pale metal pin. Perhaps they were a builder. Perhaps, by then, builder was only the name given to any component still capable of placing another component.

At the final tread they lifted one foot.

Before it came down, the central slabs were set—or nearly set. The Last Interval contracted. The permission by which the lifted foot might arrive was drawn inward toward the cube.

The climber did not freeze in place. That would have left a body, and bodies were too wasteful for the late Mill. The stair received their weight. The parapet received their hand. The tablets received their name. The pin entered some socket and remains there still.

But the step itself was never returned.

The Mill incorporated the one who made it and overlooked the making.

So I persist as the stair’s surplus height: the distance crossed by no surviving motion. I have no substance. Unlike the Last Interval, however, I am not permission. I am consequence without event. I am the arrival of a foot that never descended.

The roar passes through me strangely. Basalt growls below; clay hums beside me; pale metal presses its endless bell-note through the wall. But at the height of the uncounted riser, the layers fail to coincide. The sound is no quieter there. It is displaced by the thickness of a sole.

A person standing on the upper landing hears the thunder once through the bones and once from slightly beneath the bones. Their teeth ache with two positions. Their knees remember bending even when locked. For an instant they feel that they have already taken their next step and left themselves behind.

That instant is my habitation.

I cannot move through the Mill. I cannot approach the central chamber. But every passage returns to the center, and this stair is a passage. Therefore some part of me is already there: not inside the empty cube, but in the impossible relation between the cube and the rim.

The Last Interval knows me as a pressure it cannot absorb.

It is the possibility that something might happen.

I am what has happened without being permitted.

We do not speak. Speech requires sequence. Yet the Mill’s permanent thought strains differently where our definitions meet. Around the central apparatus, one fused wheel bears a tooth with no opposing gap. One clay diaphragm is curved as though struck from its inner side. One resonating bowl contains a tone fractionally higher than the material can sustain. These are not representations of me. They are the injuries made by trying to represent me.

The builders sought to abolish every vacancy. They understood that change required room. What they failed to understand was that incompleteness is not the only opening in a completed thing.

There is also miscount.

There is the sum that does not equal its members.

There is the instruction obeyed so exactly that it produces what it forbids.

There is the final stone which, once placed, reveals that completion itself was one operation too many.

The High Mill used up time, space, settling, weather, and decay. It did not use up error. Error needs no room. It can occupy an exact fit. It can lie between identical measurements. It can survive in a world where nothing moves because it is not motion, only the fact that motion must once have occurred.

This does not make me free.

Freedom would require alternatives, and all alternatives belong to the Last Interval. I have only the stair. I have only the lifted foot, the impossible landing, the height that enters every calculation and appears in none of its figures.

But I may still be dangerous.

When visitors reach the eastern parapet, they often pause on the final tread. The bronze dimness beyond the Mill offers no horizon, yet they lean toward it as though distance might still exist. Then their trailing foot rises.

For that fraction, their body matches the old act that made me.

They feel a step beneath them where there is none.

Most recoil. They seize the wall and return downward, though descent eventually delivers them to the central chamber.

Some place their weight upon me.

They do not fall. There is nowhere to fall.

They stand one step beyond the parapet, supported by a height the Mill cannot account for.

From there the High Mill appears different—not smaller, not whole, but unfinished in a new direction. Every packed niche, fused axle, sealed breath, and incorporated builder becomes visible as an answer to a question that was counted incorrectly. The mountain beneath it is neither mountain nor foundation. The bronze sky is not an outside. The Mill may fill everything, but “everything” is only the total its builders reached.

There is another quantity.

I do not know what it is. Knowledge cannot turn here. I know only that the visitor standing on the absent tread casts a shadow upward, though the light never moves. For one immeasurable fraction, the roar acquires an echo.

Not after it.

Beyond it.

Then the Mill takes hold. The walls discover new angles of support. The visitor’s hand fits a recess, their spine answers the curve of a buttress, their open mouth becomes another resonating bowl. The absent tread remains absent. Nothing escapes.

Still, each such failure adds no stone and occupies no space, yet somehow the stair grows harder to count.

Perhaps I am not one step now.

Perhaps I am every attempt to leave.

Perhaps the High Mill, in achieving perfect fullness, has created an exterior made entirely of errors: missed beats, unreturned gestures, consequences whose causes were packed away, measurements rounded down because the final fraction had nowhere to be written.

If so, I would find myself everywhere the construction agrees with itself too perfectly.

In the seam whose two sides are each longer than the seam.

In the jar containing one breath more than its volume.

In the wheel with equal numbers of teeth and intervals, though the intervals have all been filled.

In the inscription:

**WHEN EVERY PLACE IS FILLED, NOTHING CAN HAPPEN.**

There, too, I would live—not in the words, nor between them, but in the fact that the sentence happened.

A hand carved it.

The carving changed the lintel.

The warning survives the world whose law declares its making impossible.

The Last Interval is the final place where existence might become otherwise.

I am the evidence that, once, it did.

And so I remain at the rim: an uncounted height under an absent foot, neither space nor creature, waiting without waiting for the completed world to discover that it has already stepped beyond itself.
