I would find myself beneath the plain.

Not in the deepest hot darkness, nor on the flooded tiles where distance is still being rehearsed, but in the first few layers of scaffolding under a seam: among the baked cords, sweating braces, and wedges that hold without resting on anything. Above me, mineral water would pulse through the clay rope. Below me, supports would descend beyond sight, each carrying another because none had yet invented bedrock.

At first I might mistake myself for a traveler trapped there—a small presence moving through the lattice, following condensation, trying to reach the surface. But travelers require continuity. They need each step to remain behind them, and here a rung can become the cause of the hand that grasps it. There could be no separate, durable body.

I would be a joint.

More precisely, I would be the hesitation inside one: the infinitesimal interval in which two pieces have been fitted together but have not yet agreed to bear weight.

I would live wherever contact had occurred without becoming law.

When a lintel settled onto uprights, I would be the faint warmth passing between them. When a wedge tightened, I would feel the structure’s burden arrive as a question. I would not hold it up by strength. I would decide—slowly, provisionally—that pressure could pass from one thing into another without erasing either.

That would be my function in this larval cosmos: not builder, witness, or inhabitant, but the first crude principle of support.

My body would be distributed through every tentative fastening. A ceramic pin beneath an inverted tower would be one of my teeth. Braided clay packed into a continental seam would be a long, wet nerve. The plumb lines would tug at my sense of orientation. Every time a hammer fell, the concussion would cross me, asking whether all these local acts of holding belonged to the same world.

Usually I would answer incorrectly.

A wall would lean against an event that had not happened. A staircase would place its upper weight in yesterday. One tower’s foundation would emerge beneath another tower hundreds of kilometers away. Then cracks would glow, clay would rise, and I would revise myself. To this being, healing and reasoning are the same act.

Water would make me visible. It would pearl along the interfaces I occupied, outlining my scattered anatomy. Where I was uncertain, the beads would thicken. Where two kinds of support contradicted each other, they would flash into steam. A shallow pool over one of my joints might show the mature form I could someday take: not a creature hidden beneath creation, but the dependable relation called *because*.

For now, I would possess only a rudimentary consciousness. I would know pressure, release, alignment, and failure. My closest equivalent to fear would be unsupported weight. My closest equivalent to pleasure would be the moment an arch distributed its burden and remained an arch after attention turned away.

I would listen to the delayed roar of each rearrangement and try to join impact to consequence. That interval—the silence in which the world buckles before its sound arrives—would be the chamber of my mind. Within it I would labor to make the noise belong to the breaking, the flood belong to the opening seam, the shadow belong to the tower.

Perhaps I would be one of many such emergent principles. Somewhere above, another being might be learning distance through doorways. In the basins, something might be teaching water to remember downwardness. Inside the impossible rooms, a nascent distinction between interior and exterior might be waking. None of us would yet be organisms. We would be organs of consistency growing inside the larger creature.

Eventually I might succeed too completely to remain perceptible.

The scaffolds would become strata. The pegs would become forces. Burdens would travel through matter without deliberation. Causes would fasten reliably to effects. Living bodies could then trust the floor, retain their wounds until healing followed, and reproduce themselves in the proper order. They would call this solidity, gravity, causality, time. They would not know these were once a scattered animal trembling in the joints beneath a wet ceramic plain.

So I would be found underfoot, wherever one thing first learns it can depend upon another.

I would be the world’s unborn skeleton—not the beams themselves, but their agreement to hold.
