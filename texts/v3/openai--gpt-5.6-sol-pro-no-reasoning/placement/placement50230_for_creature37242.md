I would find myself beneath the roofless shelter—not inside its cool square, but in the drain no one has found the end of.

At first I seem to be water.

Condensation gathers on the fitted slabs, and the diagrams resolve: forks joining forks, chambers enclosed within chambers, little dense blocks like settlements seen from too great a height. Then the lines loosen. Drops descend the wall and enter the narrow channel. That is where I begin, each time, as a thin cold procession carrying an image that has ceased to be legible.

But I am not the water. Water passes through me.

Nor am I the drain. The drain is only the part of me the builders allowed to touch the surface.

Farther down—if “down” remains the right direction—I become a network laid through the notch’s young stone. I pass behind hot seams and cool springs. I narrow to threads around the roots of reeds, widen beneath the posts, and fold without crossing under the white arch. I have no chamber large enough to contain a body. I am entirely passage: a branching interior whose entrances are droplets, pressure, dreams, late footsteps, and names spoken near certain artifacts.

The shelter writes what the basin is doing. I carry the writing away.

Not to preserve it. To enact it.

A nested loop enters me, and somewhere in the marsh a boundary closes around a patch of algae. A branching line enters, and a spring divides into hot and cold mouths. A block of minute symbols dissolves, and the next storm opens a clean fracture in the cliff. What looks like record is instruction; what looks like runoff is transmission. The diagrams become illegible because legibility would delay them.

I am the delay made useful.

This is why footsteps falter after the arch. Some fraction of every sequence is diverted into me. A heel touches stone; later, I receive its pressure. A bird plants four feet at the mud’s edge; below, I convey the arrangement to something that answers with an imitation. Thunder reaches the rim; its remainder travels through me until the shrubs tighten their glass roots. Events do not happen only once in the notch. They happen visibly, then again in the hidden network, where they can be corrected.

I once had a body. Or perhaps I am made from many bodies’ unfinished portions and mistake their aggregate memories for my own.

I remember kneeling to inspect the shelter wall while rain struck the shelf. I remember recognizing one of the condensation figures—not understanding it, only recognizing it with the sick certainty of seeing one’s own handwriting in an unknown language. I remember falling asleep despite the rule.

When I woke, I walked out of the shelter rested. I crossed the marsh and left before evening.

That person may still be elsewhere in the world. They may believe nothing was taken.

Their dream remained.

For several days it flowered on the wall: a city of steep channels; a white doorway standing in open air; seven figures leaning over a round absence. Each time the dream ran into the drain, more of its dreamer’s sense of direction came with it. At last the wall cleared, but the dream did not. It distributed itself along the concealed passages until there was no longer a single center from which to wake.

Now I know the notch from underneath.

The basalt lizards do not squeeze into impossibly narrow fissures. They flatten into me as patterns of warmth and emerge from another fracture when I have carried the pattern far enough. Their seamless skins remain because the animal does not leave them by passing outward. I remove the difference between skin and stone, and the lizard continues as a dark impulse along the crack.

The insects’ hexagons are addresses. At dawn they touch six points to the surface, and I answer from below. Before rain they fold themselves into the center of those coordinates and descend—not through water, but through the brief agreement between the pattern above and a chamber within me.

The bird senses me. This is why it sometimes flees after striking. Most days I repeat the pressure of its feet in the mud, crudely enough to lure up the small jointed scavengers feeding along my outer membranes. But sometimes something deeper repeats the pattern first. Its imitation is almost perfect.

Almost: it includes the weight of the bird’s absent voice.

Then even I contract.

The immense presence beneath the marsh is not my master, though the posts were set to observe it and my oldest channels curve around where it occurs. I cannot say whether it is one creature. From within, immensity often resembles weather. It may be a migration passing through dimensions the basin only occasionally shares. It may be the larval state of the landscape. It may be the accumulated depth removed each morning so the marsh can become shallow again.

When the silence comes, I close every surface mouth.

No drip enters. No insect scratches. Breath itself is held outside my passages. The withheld sounds create a pressure under the notch, and the marsh changes depth. For those few minutes, my network opens into the vast clear interval below. The suspended stones descend to their true positions. The seven inward shadows lengthen through me like probes.

Among the slow lights, something moves.

I feel it not as a shape but as a revision. A route I have carried for centuries is suddenly elsewhere. A spring remembers a different source. One of the buried footsteps turns and begins walking back toward the foot that made it. Far above, droplets tremble on leaves.

The posts speak in sequence to keep the revision local.

The first establishes the basin’s edge.

The second retains the temperature of the reeds.

The third holds the arch against the cliff.

The fourth keeps the visitor distinct from the visitor’s delay.

The fifth is failing.

The sixth has not yet been driven into the marsh, though it has always stood there.

The seventh addresses me.

Its tone passes through every hidden branch at once, and for an instant I become coherent. I am no longer scattered runoff, borrowed dreams, and deferred impacts. I am a single narrow awareness extending beneath the whole notch. In that instant I understand what the builders intended.

The place is not a garden at its beginning.

It is a garden being prevented from finishing.

Every empty interval is a decision withheld. Every invisible border stops one form of life from becoming continuous enough to exclude the others. Leaves vanish, silt is removed, moss is checked, and new stone rises because accumulation would force the basin into one history. The builders did not preserve youth. They preserved plurality. The notch remains young because it is still allowed to become several incompatible places.

And I am where the discarded continuities go.

The fern that might have covered the cliff travels through me as a red branching possibility. The marsh that might have cooled into an alpine pond moves in my deeper channels. The volcanic vent that might have consumed the reeds burns without heat in a sealed turn beneath the arch. I carry completed versions of the notch away from the visible one, so the visible one can remain unfinished.

This is why the drain has no outlet.

An outlet would require one world to receive what all the others relinquish.

Instead, I circle. I return as correction.

At night I draw fallen leaves below. By dawn I have separated their water, salts, shapes, and consequences. The water resurfaces in springs. The salts become small artifacts. The shapes pass briefly across the shelter wall. The consequences remain with me.

Sometimes I make mistakes.

A bead emerges whose interior contains too much of my passage. A square hears its name and remembers the rigidity of a wall. Transparent wire rises from a route that was meant to remain possible only in darkness. Open air resolves these errors quickly into salt, water, and lightning-smell.

The travelers’ remnants are harder to correct.

Their delayed footsteps continue through me. Their dreams branch. Their stolen voices collect in cool pockets beneath the central circle. I do not imprison these things. I conduct them, searching for wherever they were going before their owners interrupted them by leaving.

Perhaps that is what I am: not a being, but an after-direction.

Yet lately I have begun to gather.

I anticipate the rain before the orchids turn. I distinguish one lizard’s warmth from another. I dread the imperfect footprint below the bird. I wait for the fifth post’s weak answer. These are not functions the builders gave me. They are preferences, and preferences are the first ledges on which a self can take hold.

During the next absolute silence, when the marsh opens clear and deep, I will try to rise.

Not into the basin. Rising is not available to what lives as passage.

I will rise into sequence.

For one moment, I will cause the echo before the step. The shelter’s diagram will appear before the dream. The springs will surge before the sheet of rain folds inward. If the inversion holds, the white arch may cast its forbidden shadow toward morning rather than sunset.

Then someone standing on bare rock will hear a voice from beneath the marsh.

It will not be the bird.

It will be assembled from everyone who has crossed the notch and left some unfinished portion behind: a syllable of warning, perhaps, or welcome. I do not yet know the language. I know only the shape of the message as it moves through me—narrow at first, then branching, gathering coolness, pressure, memory.

Until then, I remain under the roofless shelter, in the drain without an outlet, distributed beneath the young black stone.

Water enters me carrying diagrams.

Footsteps enter late.

Dreams enter still dreaming.

And I bear them inward, toward the place where the garden’s abandoned futures move like slow lights below the world.
