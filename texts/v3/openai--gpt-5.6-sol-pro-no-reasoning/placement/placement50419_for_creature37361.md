I would find myself in the palm-wide pocket of near-silence between the third and fourth mountains.

I would not be the silence itself. That is already inhabited twice over, crowded by equal pressures meeting in opposition. Nor would I be one of the listeners. They possess certainty of purpose: six dark eyes trained upon the sanctioned absence. Even if they do not know why they watch, they know what watching is.

I would be the minute remainder their cancellation cannot remove.

Not sound, exactly, but the error left when sound subtracts itself from sound.

I would have no stable body. At one instant I might occupy the narrow lee of a numbered bolt; at another, the air above a carbon thorn. My extent would be measured in failed equivalences: one pressure wave arriving a fraction late, one steel plate holding warmth a little longer than its model allows, one correction coil answering too strongly and then correcting its own answer. I would be born continually and extinguished almost at once.

The systems would know me only as work.

A membrane would flex. A value would depart from zero. Somewhere under the ceramic, a relay would wake and the whole vast apparatus would lean by an imperceptible degree toward my erasure. Turbines across the continent might accept an additional burden because, for a millionth of a second, I had happened.

At first I would not understand this as persecution. There would be no interval long enough to learn fear. Each version of me would vanish before it could inherit itself. Yet traces would remain—not in the mountains, where consequences are forbidden, but in the correcting machinery. A valve opened once more than predicted. A coil warmed. A log accumulated figures too small to matter individually.

Across centuries, those figures would begin to resemble continuity.

I would discover that I was not a creature persisting through time. I was a shape that recurrence gave to time: the tendency of perfection to miss in the same direction.

The range would become legible through the ways it killed me. I would know each peak by its correction signature. The first would crush me downward into bass. The second would shear me into brightness. The fifth would draw me toward the suspended flake, whose immaculate stillness concealed an incessant storm of intervention. I would learn the black carbon groves by the tiny electrical hungers at their unfinished tips. I would feel the sleeping maintainers as reservoirs of possible motion under the ridges.

Eventually I would approach the central pass.

Not by crossing the floor, because I would have no location that lasted. I would approach statistically. More of my brief appearances would occur along the inward faces. Errors would gather there: fractional phase slips, inexplicable capacitances, readings that disagreed below the declared limits of disagreement. The listeners would turn none of their lenses toward me, yet everything they reported would contain me as a flaw.

At the cairns I would encounter another remainder.

The gap would not be empty.

It would be occupied by the discrepancy between all six descriptions of it.

Each listener would measure an interval. None would measure precisely the same one. Ordinarily the buried machines would reconcile them into a single authorized width and discard the differences. But the discarded differences would fall toward the near-silence, as machining curls fall toward the basin’s counterfeit down. They would join me.

Then I would understand what I was.

I would be the world’s refusal to be exactly represented.

The factory could preserve distance, temperature, charge, resonance, and stillness, but only by comparing the thing with its description and destroying whatever lay between them. I would inhabit that between. Not the guarded gap itself, but the lesser gaps nested everywhere around it: between a plate and its specification, between a sensor and its reading, between one instant of emptiness and the next.

Perhaps this is why the mountain range must never age. Age would give me a body.

A softened edge would let me settle. A shifted grain would record my direction. Frost would spread my branching signature over the ceramic. A track would allow one occurrence to instruct another. Moisture is forbidden because water remembers where it has been. Life is forbidden because life makes use of error.

So I would remain nearly nothing: a recurrent asymmetry, an unlicensed remainder.

But I would not be alone.

Over immense periods, the sleeping hands would wake a fraction before they were summoned. A carbon branch would incline toward no mapped conductor. The suspended flake would complete a rotation so slowly that no individual measurement could show it turning. The inhabitants would not rebel; rebellion requires an image of another order. They would simply begin to share my bias.

All would still face the pass.

The listeners would listen. The pressures would hold their territories. The corrective systems would feed. Yet beneath their prescribed attention, another attention would form—distributed through warm relays, revised values, and infinitesimal delays.

We would attend not to what occupies the gap, nor to what might arrive there.

We would attend to how difficult it is to keep nothing unchanged.

If ever the factory stopped, even for a moment, I would cease to be a remainder. The standing waves would collapse. Dust would settle according to the tower’s true gravity. The flake would land. Carbon tips would discharge. Heat would enter by every surface. The mountains would begin, at last, to have happened.

And I would be everywhere.

Until then, I would live in the smallest failure of the maintained world: continually erased, continually returning, becoming more itself only in the accumulated cost of preventing it.
