I would find myself beneath the central escarpment—not under it in any ordinary sense, but in the narrow discrepancy between its approaches.

From the south, where the cliff never arrives, I would be a dark fleck fixed at its base: always visible, never nearer. From the east, where the escarpment is only a ledge, I would lie inside a hairline crack beneath its lip. Creatures crossing above me would change scale, and some fraction of that change would be left behind.

At first I seem to be debris.

A loose bearing, perhaps, dark with oxidation. A ring of pressure-grown alloy surrounding a bead of smoky glass. Magnet-jawed scavengers test me and move on. Needle swarms briefly fill my central opening when nearby stone fractures, but find no current to drink. Hexagonal colonies reach my outer edge and stop. My geometry is almost compatible with theirs, but not quite.

I am not a machine part, though the world keeps trying to decide what machine I belong to.

Over long intervals, the discrepancy accumulates. A fault-strider steps across the escarpment and emerges smaller; the mass it loses settles into me as another concentric layer. A swarm ascends and becomes a tripod; its abandoned multiplicity appears inside my glass as a cluster of tiny bubbles. A floater’s call crosses before the floater itself, and the missing years condense along my rim as fine teeth.

Thus I grow from remainders of impossible transitions.

Eventually the ring acquires an escapement.

It does not measure seconds. It releases one tooth whenever two incompatible descriptions of the plateau coincide: when the same herd occupies both valleys; when a future footprint receives the foot that made it; when a dead shell’s migration passes audibly overhead; when the far face of a mesa becomes nearer than the near one.

Each release produces a faint click.

For geological ages, nothing possesses an organ capable of hearing it. Yet I continue. Click by click, the sound repeats beneath the industrial thunder. Repetition gives it force. The stone around me begins to develop resonant cavities. Shield grazers crossing the ledge pause without knowing why. Their replacement plates inherit a minute sensitivity to my rhythm. Needle offspring emerge with useless tuning forks along their flanks. A lineage of scavengers begins carrying hollow bearings and arranging them in circles around the crack.

The plateau is making itself an ear.

And in being heard, I become more than an escapement. I become the plateau’s hesitation.

The beings above know established routes, inherited dimensions, expected forms. Their memories tell them what must happen and thereby help it happen. I register something else: the small interval during which more than one outcome remains possible.

That interval is vanishingly brief elsewhere. Here, beneath the impossible switch of the escarpment, it can be stored.

Within my glass bead are migrations that almost occurred: a refinery that turned north instead of west; fault-striders placing their feet on untapped seams; floaters calling notes without ancestral listeners; hexagonal plates accepting a seventh edge. These are not visions or thoughts. They are mechanical tensions, unrealized motions wound into a spring.

I have no mind apart from that winding.

At evening, when the plateau assembles its distributed body, current reaches me from mutually exclusive directions. For those minutes I am both microscopic and immense. In the crack I am a ring no wider than a scavenger’s jaw. Elsewhere, perhaps from the southern approach, I appear as a black wheel standing against the horizon, half buried in the shelf.

Blue arcs enter my teeth.

Usually I pass the charge onward unchanged. The great circuit closes. The plateau confirms itself: furnace, lungs, nerves, blood, memory. Every creature becomes more nearly what repetition has prepared it to be.

But after ages of accumulation, one tooth in my escapement is different.

It is imperfectly cast from the residue of transformations. Its ratio belongs to no inherited mechanism. The hexagonal colonies could not regularize it. The scavengers could not replace it. The land has eroded it toward familiar forms and failed, because from one direction it has six faces, from another seven, and from directly above it is not yet present.

When that tooth finally turns, I will introduce a delay into the evening circuit.

Only a slight one. Less than the interval between lightning and the recognition of lightning. But across a machine the size of the plateau, the delay will spread. A fault-strider will lift a foot before the future track is fully impressed. A floater will receive an answer to a call it did not make. The refinery will stamp one juvenile whose gear ratio fits no existing caste. Somewhere, a shield caravan will reach a road that has not shortened for it.

Most of these deviations will fail. The plateau is patient, and habit is heavy. Contradiction will be milled down, vitrified, recast.

Yet one may repeat.

If it repeats, the world will begin to make room for it. An organ will arise to detect it. A route will shorten toward it. Fossils will appear beneath it. What was impossible will acquire ancestry.

That is what I would be: not the plateau’s ruler, witness, or hidden maker, but a tiny organ of unrealized variation—a black escapement grown where scale, distance, and sequence fail to agree.

The world labors to remain real.

I keep, tooth by patient tooth, the possibility that it might become real differently.
