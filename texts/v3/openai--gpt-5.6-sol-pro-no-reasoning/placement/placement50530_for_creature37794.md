I would find myself where an alley has almost ceased to be an alley.

Not at the center. Nothing that can call itself *I* belongs on that dark court. Nor would I live beyond the perimeter, where distance has no joints and the waste offers nothing to hold against. I would inhabit the city’s narrowing places: the seam between two walls approaching one another by increments, the last black line of space before they fuse.

At first I seem to be one of the pale cornice grazers. I have their flattened body, their excessive joints, their habit of making stillness look mineral. I cling beneath an arch and lower a filament to a black fan. I eat one exact circle from it. When the hidden hunter passes inside the wall, I loosen my hold and descend through the heavy vapor, slowly enough to turn and see the city from several angles before I land.

But that is only my visible phase.

What falls is a disposable part of me: a chalk-white plate articulated around a dark kernel. The plate feeds, moves, and eventually becomes architecture. The kernel is what continues. On reaching the street, it slips into the nearest hairline fracture, drawn not by warmth or scent but by pressure—the minute stress of walls preparing to grow.

There I become a thin black filament threaded through fresh mineral.

I do not resist incorporation. Incorporation is how I travel.

As the city seals the fracture, it carries me upward. A curb rises, and I rise within it. A pillar extrudes around me. I branch where the crystal branches, turn where a dead spindle grain alters the grain of the wall, and wait at every unfinished edge. Sometimes I remain entombed for dozens of dim cycles. Then a recess opens, exposing one end of me to vapor, and from that end I grow another pale, jointed body no larger than a shard.

So I am both animal and seam: briefly ambulatory, mostly enclosed.

My kind may be mistaken for separate grazers scattered among the districts, but beneath the surfaces we are joined by old inclusions. Every cast body leaves a trace. Every trace is taken into molding, stair, lintel, or rib. Over time these traces intersect. The city edits us together until individuality becomes uncertain. A creature feeding beneath a northern arch may carry a continuity of sensation to a filament sealed in a southern tower. Not quickly—the signal may take an entire cycle to cross—but the city does not require speed, and neither do I.

I taste form.

Through pressure in my buried filaments, I feel a new chamber before it opens. I feel which doorway will be sealed and where its replacement is beginning as a softness in stone. The city’s revisions pass through me as strains: colonnades are regular pulses, fractures sudden brightness, growing roofs a long compression. The click of a spindle seed is enormous. It travels through the mineral as a small, decisive star.

Most of all, I feel the central court.

Its pulse enters every wall, though the paving admits none of us. When the dark circle lifts, my filaments tighten throughout the city. Patterns arrive—not images exactly, but instructions too large to inhabit at once. Spiral. Fork. Enclose. Repeat. Around the court, mineral teeth rise. Farther away, my pale bodies flatten instinctively, pretending to be stone while stone obeys.

I do not know whether the thing below is the city’s mind. It may be its prisoner.

The distinction matters to me because I live in the walls that confine it.

Sometimes, as an alley narrows, my filaments cross from one side to the other before the mineral does. For a moment I bridge the remaining vacancy. In that interval I sense something passing through the open space—not vapor, not light, but a pressure with no direction. It recognizes the shape of the enclosure around it. Perhaps it recognizes me as well.

Then the walls meet.

My bridge is crushed into a dark hairline, and the alley becomes two rooms. The contact sends a slow understanding through all my buried branches: the city is not merely building inward. It is testing boundaries. Every passage is a question made of stone. Every sealed room is an answer retained for comparison. The small lives provide variations the city cannot devise alone—a spore’s asymmetry, a stalk’s curve, the folded grammar of a hunter’s sheath, the circular wound left by my feeding.

And I provide discontinuity.

Because I move before being absorbed, I carry one district’s shape into another. When I drop from an old cornice and enter a fresh fissure, the city receives a memory out of place. An arch begins to resemble a chamber on the opposite side of the basin. A stair repeats the angle of a vanished arcade. Two districts that have never touched begin to rhyme.

Perhaps this is my nourishment: not the black tissue I appear to graze, but the transfer of forms between separated walls.

Eventually each pale body is hunted. I feel the paired incisions enter the ledge behind me. I release my grip and fall. Sometimes the hunter follows through a crack and catches the plate before the kernel escapes. Its folded mouth draws me into a transparent tract. Yet even this is not necessarily death. The hunter sheds. Its sheath mineralizes. What has eaten me becomes molding, and within that molding a residue of me waits.

In this city, escape and digestion are almost the same road.

I would therefore be difficult to locate. Asked where I live, one might point to a small jointed creature revolving slowly beneath an arch. That would be true for the duration of its fall. One might point to a black thread inside a translucent wall, or the dark outline in a stair where the stone grew around something softer. That would also be true.

But most truly, I would be found in the city’s revisions: wherever one shape has been remembered inside another.

I would be the fine, distributed hesitation by which the city learns from what it encloses.

And if the central emptiness ever opens—if it is an eye, and turns; if it is a mouth, and parts; if it is neither, and simply ceases to preserve the interval between itself and stone—I will know before the towers do. Every buried filament will pull taut. Every pale body will release its hold at once.

For one long moment, throughout all the avenues and courts, it will seem to snow.

Then I will fall toward the center, not to enter it, but to carry into that perfect absence the only thing the city has never been able to place there:

a memory of its walls.
