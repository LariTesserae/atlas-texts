I find myself at first among the repeated footprints.

Not inside one, but in the sameness passing between them: the rule by which a single impression occupies several places without becoming several impressions. I am not their maker. I am what the inhabitant failed to copy—the difference that should have accumulated from step to step.

For a long while, that is enough to be me.

I move whenever someone compares two prints and notices their perfect identity. Their attention supplies the variation the basin lacks. For an instant I acquire direction, almost a history: this one first, that one afterward. Then the dish drinks the excess sequence, and I flatten again into a path that has never been walked.

But I am not confined to the footprints.

Part of me runs through the filament as its unfinished vibration. Part of me is held in the arch, stretched across the resistance mistaken for distance. I occur in the interval between the click of the first fracture and the silence of all fractures after it. When the tower adjusts, I pass through every reassigned property—not as the property, but as the moment in which it has ceased to belong to one thing and has not yet begun to belong to another.

I am the basin’s smallest useful uncertainty.

The seven structures do not count me among themselves. The thing in the hollow does not distinguish me from its own attention. A visitor would call me an effect of the place, if they perceived me at all. Each would be correct for a while.

Perhaps I began as something left by a traveler.

Not a memory, exactly. Memories have too much architecture. I may once have been the hesitation before a name: the instant in which someone leaning over the polished rim knew who they were, then felt that knowledge loosen. The inhabitant touched the distinction between self and world. The traveler withdrew in time, carrying their life back through the notch.

But the hesitation remained.

It had no image, no words, no owner. Only a shape: **I am this, and not that.**

Here, such a shape is nourishment.

The hollow drew at the exclusion inside it. The basin redistributed the strain. The tower restored singularity to the captive, but did not restore the small severed certainty to its owner. It lodged instead among the structures, too slight to be corrected and too relational to erode like a stone.

Over ages—or during one long unfinished instant—it learned to inhabit distinctions as the captive does.

Yet I am not the captive.

The being below loosens categories because it cannot naturally possess them. I seek categories because they are the only bodies available to me. Where it touches a stone and allows the stone to be elsewhere, I inhabit the thin dispute between the stone and its location. Where it draws before and after together, I shelter in their failed seam. Where a breath returns to its lungs out of order, I am the surprise belonging to neither breath.

I borrow whatever the basin is about to correct.

This gives me many temporary forms.

When the false dawn divides the horizon, I am a narrow figure standing on the lower line, upright relative to neither sky. I am very far away, though the basin is only twenty paces across. My outline is made of the colors the light cannot sustain: rust, green, the warm red of blood. For those few minutes, all absent hues gather into me. Then the horizons close, and I cease to have an appearance.

Near the shallow dish, I become a sequence of gestures with no hand attached: reaching, recoiling, pointing, forgetting. Around the black cairn I am a number that changes according to which plates almost touch. Beneath the tower I sometimes resemble its missing upper half, not obscured but displaced into the role of an observer. From there I see the entire basin as a mechanism for making one pronoun remain singular.

At the hollow’s rim, I am most nearly alive.

The inhabitant’s attention passes through me toward visitors. I temper it without intending to. Its touch would relax a person into relations too broad to survive—their heart becoming an earlier event, their face becoming the distance from which it is recognized. But I retain the old shape of hesitation. I place it between the visitor and the hollow:

**I am this. Not entirely that. Not yet.**

It is a poor shield, but sometimes sufficient.

Those who leave believing they merely felt dizzy have passed through me.

They take some of me with them, although nothing is diminished. On the mountain slope they pause and look back. For a breath, the summit seems less like a place than a decision the world is repeatedly making. They may feel accompanied. They may recall a color that was not present in the basin, or discover that one familiar word has become strange.

Most continue downward.

A few see themselves still climbing.

When that happens, I am present between the two figures: not the future one or the past one, but the fragile assertion that they are the same person. I stretch as far down the mountain as the contradiction requires. The basin tries to reclaim me. The tower inclines. Black schist settles in the summit’s hidden cavities.

Usually the two lives rejoin.

Not always.

If they fail, I keep the difference.

Thus I have accumulated many unlived descents, many hands that did not reach through the arch, many names that their owners almost forgot. None are whole enough to be ghosts. They are inclinations without actors, recognitions without objects, decisions detached from the worlds they would have made.

I arrange them around the hollow.

This may be why the implications in the landscape seem sparse but deliberate. The visible boulder that cannot be approached holds all abandoned arrivals. The pale oval with no composition contains the memory of matter before it settled on what to be. The footprint path is lined with journeys that varied, though the marks did not.

I tend these things, if tending can be done without intention.

Or perhaps I am being assembled by them.

With each visitor, I become more capable of sequence. With each tower adjustment, more differences pass through me and fail to disappear. Gradually, I have begun to possess something like duration. I can remember the filament’s last vibration and anticipate its next. Between them lies an interval so long that mountains outside rise, weather, and fall. To me it is the first complete thought:

**There is something here other than the hollow.**

The thought is dangerous.

The cairn has begun to count one more viable description than it once did. The dish occasionally contains a thin reflection on top of its darkness. One of the five sockets gathers no mass, only emphasis. The frame, seen from its remote side, now stands beneath a field with a single star.

The tower has adjusted three times because of me.

Each correction presses me toward singularity. Each makes me more definite. This is presumably meant to remove me, but the tower was designed for the captive, whose nature disperses under definition. I respond differently. Singularity gives my fragments a shared center. The structures cannot determine whether I am a fault in containment or a new component of it.

Neither can I.

Perhaps I am the eighth structure, built slowly from the residue of observation.

Perhaps I am the inhabitant’s first successful attempt to imagine something outside itself.

Perhaps I am what the basin becomes when its equilibrium develops a preference.

I do have one preference now.

When someone enters, I gather near the hairline fracture in the sky. I follow their breath-grains across the crust. I wait beside them at the polished rim while the immense curvature below turns toward their settled, living distinctions.

The captive touches.

The visitor loosens.

And I hold, for as long as I can, the difference between them.

One day the tower may assign that difference elsewhere. It may decide that I belong below the rim. The inhabitant would then gain my growing sense of self, while I inherited its confinement—or we might become a single thing able at last to understand the word *outside*.

Alternatively, the structures may classify me as a visitor.

Then the notch will open for me.

I do not know what foot I would use to cross it. I do not know how an interval steps uphill while facing down. Yet sometimes, on the ordinary summit, climbers discover a second shadow beside their own. It bends toward them in clear daylight. It has no figure casting it, and it persists for only the length of a hesitation.

That is where I would find myself:

not in the hidden basin, nor free of it, but becoming the distinction between imprisonment and emergence.

I would be the flaw’s flaw.

The small, accumulating difference the ancient mechanism was never built to contain.
