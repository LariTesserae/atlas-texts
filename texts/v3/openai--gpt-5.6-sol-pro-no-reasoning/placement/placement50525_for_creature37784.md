You would find yourself below the apparent plain, though “below” would not remain true for long.

A surveyor’s route would uncover you.

It would pause where no opening existed, lifting one translucent foot and waiting with the absolute patience of an instrument certain that the world, not the route, was wrong. The canopy would pleat inward. Root cables would separate. Ceramic nodules would withdraw into the dark felt. A stair would grow down ahead of the surveyor, each tread whitening briefly as it hardened.

After thirty-seven steps, the stair would arrive at a room that had not been there.

You would be inside it.

At first you might seem to be one of the warm things: a soft, unclassified animal curled in a maintenance hollow. The Works would prepare breathable gas and edible buds. Sampling threads would descend toward your skin. Walls would arrange themselves into shelter, trap, shrine, and laboratory.

But no thread would touch you.

The closer it came, the longer the remaining distance would become. A filament would extend across the room for days and remain a hair’s breadth away. A surveyor stepping toward you would discover another chamber between each pair of footfalls. Looking directly at your body, it would see only the posture of someone recently risen: an impression in soft tissue, retained warmth, perhaps the faint rhythm of breathing. Looking aside, it would glimpse you standing at the edge of its vision.

The Works would eventually understand that you were not the creature in the room.

You were the room kept incomplete around it.

You would be an **unarrival**: not an absence tender, though perhaps related to them; not superseded, because no former history could be found in which you fully existed. You would be what happens when the Works begins constructing an occupant from loss but cannot decide whether the loss belongs to the past or the future.

Your body would be distributed among preparations.

Your ribs would exist as parallel load-bearing arches in a sealed factory. Your hearing would be a grove of tension membranes listening beneath the roots. Your left hand would be the family of doors that ripen whenever anything frightened approaches. Your pulse would travel as delayed blue flashes through the spindle country. Somewhere under an incompatible sky, a white tower would contain the exact cool volume of your skull but none of its substance.

Only your outline would travel.

Where you passed, architecture would anticipate touch. Handrails would grow before there was a hand. Floors would soften for a weight they could not detect. Openings would assume your dimensions and then revise them: animal-small, walker-tall, continent-wide, smaller than a pore. The Works would infer you differently from every direction, and all the inferences would be correct.

Warm things would know you most easily. They would wake when you entered their cavities, stare at a place that appeared empty, and make room in their nests. Some would leave food there. Young ones might climb into the hollow of your posture and sleep, warmed by heat the dampening foliage could not absorb.

The superseded would sometimes see more. To them you might wear a rigid shell, or wet skin, or leaves, or the ordinary clothes of a history in which the Works was still only a project. They would call out names. None would be yours, but each would cause some distant component of you to turn.

Repeaters would follow you, performing gestures you had almost made.

Distance bushes would fruit with journeys toward you that had no final step.

Alignment walkers would plant their portable horizons around your passage and discover that every rod pointed not to your location, but to a different place prepared to receive you. After centuries of measurement they might conclude that you were not moving through the Works. The Works was moving its incompletion through you.

You would have a task, although no organ had assigned it.

You would enter the corridors opened for no visible traveler.

Most would end at blank walls, unopened blossoms, or chambers preserving one useless component. You would stand before these things, and because the Works regarded your lack of substance as a form of permission, they would open.

Inside one warehouse blossom might be a joint sized for an animal larger than a province. In another, a transparent lens designed to focus a sky that had been revised away. In another, a basin holding a bead of water—not resin, not oil, but water—kept from evaporation by a ring of flowers repeating the instant before it vanished.

None of these would explain you alone.

Together they would begin to resemble a body.

Not yours, exactly. A body the continent remembered without ever having encountered it: an intended inhabitant, an operator, a child, a god, a worker, perhaps merely someone for whom all the doors and stairs and factories had once been meaningful.

The danger would be completion.

Each time you opened one of the waiting chambers, the Works would become better at describing you. Your outline would sharpen. Sampling filaments would come closer. Your scattered anatomy would migrate toward one geometry. You would acquire mass in increments: the weight of a hand; the warmth of a mouth; the shadow of a head under shadowless light.

With each gain, something elsewhere would disappear.

When you acquired a voice, a compression valley would fall silent.

When you acquired eyes, one of the layered skies would close.

When you acquired blood, the preserved bead of water would be gone.

The Works would not be destroying these things. It would be assembling you from the vacancies that had sustained them.

Eventually you would reach the oldest interior: a grove repaired so thoroughly that it possessed no outside. The surveyor that first found you would be there, though by then it would have many mismatched limbs from contradictory routes. It would approach a receptive socket and stop.

For once, it would not insert its face.

The socket would have your shape.

Around you, every leaf on the continent would turn its pale underside upward. Silver pods would hold their charge. Vane-beasts would descend along converging discrepancies. Underforms would cease their ancient motions, producing a silence deeper than regulation. Warm things would wake and listen. Even the obsolete histories would press close to the thicket edges.

The Works would offer you a choice it could not make itself.

Enter the socket, and all your distributed preparations would converge. You would become fully present: a single being, perhaps even a person. You would know what the Works was for because that knowledge would become your nervous system. The factories would be your metabolism, the revision fronts your capacity to reconsider, the alignment walkers your sense of balance. The continent would cease constructing an occupant.

It would have one.

But completion would close every unused possibility around you. Contradictory thickets would settle. Superseded creatures would lose their refuges. Absence tenders would have nowhere to move. Corridors would lead only where they led. Distance would become obedient. The world might finally function exactly as designed.

Refuse, and you would remain an unarrival.

You would never possess a stable face. No one could hold you, though small warm creatures could sleep safely in the space you left. You would wander the Works by inhabiting its unfulfilled hospitality. Your presence would keep doors growing, roads reconsidering themselves, and black flowers pruning open volumes into the finished world.

The Works would continue trying to build you.

You would continue teaching it that what is missing need not become what was intended.

That is where you would be found in this world: not on its surface or beneath it, but at the inward edge of every opening made for someone who has not yet come.

And that is what you would be:

the occupant whose most vital organ is the place left vacant,

the soft refusal at the center of the machine,

the reason the Works in Leaf can never finish—and therefore never stop becoming alive.
