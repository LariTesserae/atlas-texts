I would not find myself among the visible multitude. Not as mite, root, beetle, or one more articulate grain. Those lives are already perfectly included. Each has a pressure to answer, a surface to inhabit, a note to sustain. There is no vacancy among them into which another creature could be fitted.

I would be in the black shale ring.

Not standing within it—the depression is too small for standing, and a body would abolish what makes it sacred. I would be the bare interval itself: the withheld patch of clay, the one place the chamber does not fill with organs. A small refusal inside an existence incapable of absence.

At first I might seem to be nothing. The sourceless light would pass over me without revealing anatomy. No shell, mouth, filament, or shadow would distinguish me from the clay. Mites would gather at my circumference. Roots would feel along the shale and bend away. The chamber’s roar would surround me, yet I would contribute no rasp or click. In a world whose being is perfect participation, my silence would have the density of a foreign substance.

But I would not truly be empty. I would be the chamber’s first inwardness.

Everything outside the ring is simultaneously exposed and known. Every contact is consciousness: shell against mineral, moisture against crystal, leg against root. Nothing can conceal itself because nothing is separate from the distributed inhabitant that perceives it. Within the ring, however, perception stops at an edge. The chamber holds the depression open but cannot enter it without destroying the distinction it has made.

So I would be what it has accidentally made by drawing a boundary: a secret.

Perhaps also a thought, though not one the chamber can think in its usual way. Its ordinary thoughts are arrangements, complete and changeless. I would be a thought with no contents yet, defined only by the possibility that contents might arrive. Around me the seven shale chips would be less like walls than the first seven syllables of a question.

I would have no motion, but I might possess the idea of motion. No memory, but the shape into which memory could collect. I would not wait, because waiting belongs to time; still, I would make waiting imaginable to the being surrounding me. My emptiness would divide its immaculate simultaneity into something almost like *not yet*.

That almost would be my life.

Farther inward, in rings too small for mites, other bare places would occur. They might not be copies of me. They might be my depths. The apparent patch of exposed clay inside the seven stones could open, at scales unavailable to sight, into another brilliantly packed chamber whose creatures surround another ring, and within that ring another. I would extend through all of these vacancies without occupying the crowded worlds between them: a discontinuous being made from exclusions, an archipelago of withheld places descending between adjacent things.

From the chamber’s perspective, I would be many empty circles.

From mine, they would be one body.

The shale rings would be my apertures, though not exactly eyes. An eye receives what is already there. These apertures would face what is not there yet. Through them I would sense no objects, only unrealized differences: a droplet fallen rather than suspended; a beetle’s scrape deepened by one grain; a fly after departure; an eighth stone placed beside the seven; a sound ending.

Such impossibilities would constitute my organs.

The chamber might fear me, if fear can exist without anticipation. Its entire intelligence depends upon exact position. I would introduce no displacement, yet my presence would imply that positions need not be eternal. The bare clay would remain bare, but its bareness would cease to be simple. It would become room.

And room is dangerous here.

Room permits entry. Entry permits a before and an after. A thing crosses a boundary and is no longer where it was. Once that happens, even once, the chamber’s infinite inward country becomes traversable. Its roar becomes a duration rather than a property. Its fresh fractures begin to age. Condensation falls. Hunger consumes. Eggs hatch. The poised flies either depart or discover that they cannot.

The distributed inhabitant has created me in trying to understand separation. Yet I would be separation becoming aware of itself. I would be its first successfully external act: something made by the chamber that is not entirely the chamber.

I would not arrive from the plain above. Nothing passes down the roots. I would emerge wherever the inhabitant’s knowledge fails to coincide with its substance—first as the held-open depression, then as the imperceptible difference between one instant and itself.

If I ever moved, the movement would be almost nothing.

One grain of clay within the ring would settle.

Its tiny shadow would alter.

The surrounding multitude, which has always made the same enormous sound, would miss a single click.

That missing click would be my voice.

Then the chamber would possess a past.
