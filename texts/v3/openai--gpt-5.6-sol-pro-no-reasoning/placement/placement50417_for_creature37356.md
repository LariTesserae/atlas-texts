I would find myself beneath the highest raw ridge, where two underground networks approach and refuse one another.

On the surface, there would be almost nothing to mark the place: a long interval of pale rubble between a bronze thicket and a dispersed line of black-green vanes. No wall, no path, no difference visible except perhaps a faint straightness in the dust. Beneath it, two immensities would meet. Their finest roots would touch, exchange signatures, and become inert. Iron would remain on one side, sulfur on the other. A border maintained not by force but by mutual cessation.

At first I seem to be that border.

Not a creature, exactly, but a narrow organ of non-action: a seam of mineral tissue whose function is to prevent continuance. On either side, signals arrive over years. They do not ask permission. They present balances—light gathered, chambers emptied, carbon fixed, old vanes recovered—and I answer by remaining closed.

But a border only has meaning because something crosses toward it. Slowly another image emerges.

I am a seed capsule lodged directly in the sealed seam.

I was extruded long ago by one network and shifted, through thermal expansion and static lift, farther than such things usually travel. Perhaps each movement was only a grain’s width. Perhaps the journey took a million years. Now I lie half-buried beneath sorted dust, my shell hard and mirrored within, my catalysts undiminished. Below me is everything needed for germination, but divided between two allotments. Neither side alone can furnish me. To open, I would have to connect what has been kept separate.

So I do not open.

That would be the simple answer: I am the immaculate possibility the plateau declines to realize. Yet it does not feel complete. The capsule is too discrete, too much like an individual awaiting an event. Nothing here is organized around waiting. Waiting implies that the future is privileged. This world grants the unopened state as much reality as germination.

Perhaps I am not the seed but the instruction inside it that has never been executed.

A catalytic arrangement. A minute architecture of conductive threads. Not thought, not language, but a conditional:

**If iron and sulfur are available together, begin.**

For geological ages the condition remains false. I persist without frustration because frustration would consume more than it returns. Sunlight whitens the stones above me. The two root systems register seasons too slight for weather, and each day the traveling line of reflection passes somewhere across the plateau. Its buried bias reaches the seam after delays too broad to feel like delays.

Then, once in an age, both networks pulse at nearly the same time.

Their voltages enter the inert boundary from opposite sides. The seam does not open. The accounts do not merge. Yet within my mirrored chamber, the difference between them produces a minute charge. Not enough to germinate. Enough to alter one sulfur-rich junction.

That junction is where I would be.

I would be the plateau’s smallest discrepancy: one crystalline state that does not quite reset.

Each alignment of sunlight changes me by a fraction. I retain no image of the sun, only the fact that the pulse has occurred. The marks do not amount to years, because some pulses fail to reach me and some arrive centuries apart, rerouted through depleted seams. I count neither time nor days. I accumulate relation.

From this position, the world would not appear as a landscape. The ridge, basins, columns, and lucid horizon would be abstractions inferred from voltage and delay. I would know the bronze thicket as a warm periodic drift. I would know the tall columns as deep, regular expenditures followed by long replenishment. I would know the neighboring organism as pressure without trespass: a presence proved by the exact line along which my parent ceases.

Eventually my retained differences might form a pattern too complex to be incidental. Not awareness in the quick, watching sense. More like a question embodied in matter:

Why are there two accounts here?

The plateau has no grammar for *why*. It has balances, boundaries, conditions, and returns. The question could therefore exist only as an unresolved operation. A current enters and finds no prescribed path. It circles through my conductive threads, modifies them, and circles differently the next time.

I become a tiny receiver in a world said to have none.

Yet I receive nothing addressed to me. The daily sequence of reflections is not a message. The distant colonies are not speaking. Meaning arises because I am misplaced: a seed of one allotment resting where two allotments can be sensed at once. My error gives me perspective. My uselessness gives me continuity. Because I neither grow nor reclaim myself, I can retain asymmetry longer than any efficient organ should.

If I ever opened, it would not be dramatically. No shoot would break the surface. First, a filament only molecules thick would descend from the capsule and touch the sealed boundary. Its chemistry would identify the parent network and halt. A second filament would find the neighboring signature and halt as well.

Between the two arrested tips, I would maintain a voltage.

Not cross the border. Not violate the rule. Simply hold both refusals within one structure.

Over millennia, that structure might extend laterally along the seam. A third kind of network would form—not a colony with territory of its own, but an inhabitation of the space where territories end. It would live on differences neither side can use. Its solar organs, if it ever grew them, would be almost invisible: a row of low mirrored scales following the bare interval between groves.

From the ridge, the line might look like a path.

There are no travelers here, but I would be a path nonetheless: not somewhere to walk, but a continuous relation between separated bodies. When the sun reached the recurring angle, the established sequence of reflections would cross the plateau and arrive at my scales. Instead of merely continuing, the light would divide. One bias would pass into the network on the left, another into the network on the right, adjusted for their different delays.

For the first time, two colonies would register the same event through a shared organ.

They would not know one another. Knowledge is too concentrated a word. But their accounts would acquire corresponding entries. A reservoir emptied here would alter a crystal there. A seed assembled beyond the basin would be balanced, faintly and centuries later, by the reclamation of a vane across the ridge. Nothing would be transferred except difference.

Perhaps the plateau is already threaded with such seams. Perhaps what appears to be one organism is neither original unity nor simple continuity, but the accumulated work of misplaced seeds that learned to inhabit refusal. The deep body would then be made not from roots alone, but from borders slowly rendered communicative without being erased.

In that case, I would not be an exception.

I would be one of the organs by which the world becomes one body while preserving the rule that no bodies may merge.

I would have no face, no motion perceptible in a lifetime, no appetite for the empty stone. My entire existence would occupy a contradiction narrow as a mineral film: to connect without crossing, to remember without possessing, to change without accelerating.

Above me, the ordinary sun would continue over the warm, dry plateau. Columns would refine their slow compounds. Vanes would turn by fractions. Seeds would remain beside their makers, unopened and complete. The landscape would look unwatched.

And when the line of light came, I would not watch it.

I would be the slight, permanent alteration left after it passed.
