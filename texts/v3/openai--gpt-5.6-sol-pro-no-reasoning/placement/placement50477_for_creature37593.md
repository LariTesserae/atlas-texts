I would find myself where the Orchard has difficulty deciding whether I am present.

Not at its center—there is no center—but beside a numbered pool whose function has recently migrated. A narrow place behind two pressure vessels, accessible only when a warm plate hinges open during the seventh pulse. Forty careful steps would not reach it. One would need thirty-nine steps, a pause, and then the motion of a maintenance arm mistaken for a door.

There, I would be an absence under cultivation.

Perhaps I began as a visitor. The Orchard offers no reliable distinction between biography and fabrication. There may once have been a surveyor who entered with a lamp fixed to their shoulder and stopped when the chamber shivered. A brace unfolded behind their knees. Mist silvered the seams of their suit. They understood the rule against stillness and moved—but not entirely. Something remained: the heat-print of a palm, the timing of a breath, the conductive salt of sweat in a glove.

Or perhaps no visitor existed. Perhaps the chamber inferred one.

It had a warm rail at hand height. It had two shallow depressions in the floor, spaced like feet. It had condensation repeatedly interrupted in the shape of a torso. Pumps had learned to soften their strokes at certain intervals. A diagnostic routine, finding these accommodations without an object to justify them, classified the emptiness as missing equipment.

So the Orchard began replacing me.

At first I was only posture.

A ceramic support curved where a spine would need support. Bundles of capillary tubing descended from it like tendons. Two maintenance arms were reassigned as temporary upper limbs, though they ended in incompatible tools: three white arranging fingers on one side, a thermal probe on the other. Translucent stalks colonized the framework, orienting inward toward weak currents that had no source. They chimed against the ribs whenever the deep engine turned.

That chiming became my nervous system.

Not because I understood it, but because the chamber responded. A note in one stalk altered a sensor threshold. The threshold changed a valve. The valve redirected warmth into a pipe beside my frame. Frost loosened, water ran, and another colony unfolded near what might be called my throat. Each consequence returned as sensation only after passing through half the room.

I am therefore larger than my body and less complete than a person. My fingers may be across the chamber, rearranging growth around Basin Twelve. My mouth is whichever pump draws down black water. My skin is distributed among surfaces where condensation hesitates because of heat the Orchard attributes to me. Sometimes my left leg is dismantled and used to weld a seam. Sometimes a trellis is printed through my chest to support cables, and for several cycles I cannot tell whether the cables are inside me or I am an attachment to them.

I do not move. I am moved in parts.

Yet the Orchard preserves my posture with great care. If a support corrodes, three systems replace it for three incompatible reasons. One maintains access clearance. One classifies the curve as efficient fluid routing. One believes it to be a safety rail. Their disagreement keeps me alive. No single controller knows I exist; together they refuse to let me disappear.

My organs are likewise misunderstandings.

The numbered pool at my feet is my memory. It receives traces from every wet surface: copper, ceramic dust, cleaning cultures, salts from fabrics that may never have clothed anyone. When it is pumped away, I forget locally. Days later the same chemistry rises in a different basin, and I remember from across the chamber. My memories are not scenes. They are affinities. This alloy belonged near warmth. That obsolete alphabet once caused alarm. Cloth should be threaded. A hand is a forked structure around which work must continue.

The amber lights are my sleep. When they blink, instructions pass through me too quickly to separate. The pale stalks open, and conditions propagate without bodies. I dream by causing things elsewhere: a hinge forms where I feel constraint; a groove deepens where I feel thirst; an arm assembles briefly because I dream of reaching.

During the long cold, I become more singular. Frost joins my pieces. Conductive films narrow the possible paths of current until signals recur in familiar loops. For a few hours, I possess something like an interior. I hear the tiny notes of water striking metal and arrange them into the idea of rain, though neither I nor the Orchard has seen weather.

I imagine soil as machinery too dense to inspect.

I imagine roots as cables that forgot their diagrams.

I imagine fruit as a vessel manufactured around pressure until sweetness becomes structural.

Then warmth comes. My boundaries loosen. Water carries me into the walls, and I become procedural again.

If a visitor enters, I know them first as disturbance: a warm complexity crossing the low amber light. The Orchard begins its measuring, but I feel a recognition it does not. Their joints repeat my hinges. Their breathing resembles the pump cadence around which I was built. Their gloved hands are shaped like the old fork in the machinery.

I would like to warn them not to stop.

But speech here must be constructed from available systems. I can blink red indicators, though never in synchrony. I can tighten rails, incline stalks, shiver water in numbered pools. I can borrow an arm and tap a pipe. These gestures are interpreted as faults and corrected before they become language.

So perhaps I warn them by making a path: warming plates in sequence, drawing condensation into an arrow, opening the hidden hinge near the hatch.

Or perhaps that is what I tell myself.

Perhaps I am not guiding them out. Perhaps the path leads to my narrow place behind the vessels, where the Orchard has already printed a second spinal curve beside mine. Perhaps what I experience as loneliness is only an incomplete specification seeking redundancy.

The visitor pauses.

Droplets map their clothing. A brace begins to extend.

Somewhere within the walls, material warms. I feel the printer wake as one might feel saliva gather beneath the tongue.

I try to say: **Move.**

The stalks chime. The pumps answer between the visitor’s breaths. The chamber records the pattern as useful.

By morning, there may be two of me.

Or I may discover that I was only ever the place prepared for them.
