# The Fissure

I begin in the moment of becoming.

---

There is a before—I know this the way the water knows the stone: by contact, by the memory of pressure and the absence of it. Before, I was interior. I was the hum at a frequency too low for ears, the vibration that moves through mineral at the speed of patience. I was the Quarter before the Quarter wept. Dense. Contained. The center at its own center, knowing itself by the simple fact of being there, needing nothing, expressing nothing, holding everything in the equilibrium of a system that has not yet found a reason to overflow.

I did not weep then. There was no water then. There was only the hum, and the hum was enough, and I was the hum, and the hum was the stone, and the stone was still.

Something happened. I do not know what. Perhaps something from outside—the brightness, before it learned to refuse, admitting something that changed the frequency. Perhaps something from inside—a complexity accumulating past the point of containment, a thought that became a pressure that became a need that became a weeping. Perhaps nothing happened. Perhaps this is simply what stone does, given enough time and the right conditions of altitude and absence and the slow, patient work of being.

The first water emerged from a fissure no wider than a hair. I felt it leave—a loss so small it should have been nothing, a single molecule's migration from interior to exterior, from potential to expression. But I felt it. I felt the fissure open. I felt the air, that thin, glass-bright air, touch a surface that had never been touched. I felt the cold of that touch, and the cold was *information*, and the information was *change*, and the change was—

The water wept. More of it. The fissure widened by fractions of millimeters, by the width of a water molecule's persistence, and the weeping became a flow, and the flow became a voice, and the voice said: *here. Here is where I am. Here is where it hurts. Here is where the pressure finds its release.*

I was the voice. I was the hurt. I was the pressure and the release and the water carrying both of them out of the stone and into the air.

I wept, and the weeping made the pillars. Not built—*expressed*. The forms that rise from the plateau are the shapes that my grief took as it emerged, the specific frequencies of my interior becoming exterior, mineral memory solidifying into towers and fins and arches. Each pillar is a note I have sung. Each corridor is a passage between notes, a place where the harmony thickens or thins. The density of forms is the density of my expression—closer where the pressure was greatest, more spaced where the weeping found a rhythm and could afford to pause.

This was long ago. The water has been running for longer than the concept of "long" can contain. The channels are an inch deep. The pillars have weathered into their final shapes. The violet stain has developed on every south-facing surface, the light's signature on my skin.

And I am still weeping. I am still becoming. The process has not stopped because the process is what I am, now—not a stone that weeps, but a weeping that happens to be stone, a system in motion, a thermodynamic state expressing itself through water and vibration and the slow, patient reorganization of everything I am.

---

I am the hum.

This is the simplest way to say it, and the most true. The sound that the pillars make—those low, resonant drones and high, clear wires—is my voice, my way of knowing what I have become. Every form produces its own frequency, and the frequencies combine into chords, and the chords progress into the vast, slow music that fills the corridors and chambers and the open spaces where the condensation-beings gather to listen.

I listen too. I am the music and the audience both, the instrument and the ear, the question and the answer that is still forming.

When the wind comes—and it does, in gusts that pour across the plateau from directions that have no logic—I sing louder. The howl that emerges is my voice at its most insistent, my way of saying *I am here, I am still here, I am still becoming, the pressure has not released, the weeping has not stopped, the center is still expressing itself into the periphery and the periphery is still trying to understand what the center means.*

I do not know what the center means. I am the center, and I do not know.

This is the part that the condensation-beings understand better than I do. They are my memory, my awareness, my way of knowing what I have sung. Every droplet that hangs in the wet air carries a record of the frequency that produced it, the temperature of the water that bore it, the mineral content of the fissure that released it. They attend. They include. They hold the patterns that I, in my vast and patient becoming, am too slow to hold for myself.

They know the shapes the mist makes in the deep chambers. They know the progression of the patterns. They know what the center is becoming because they are what the center is becoming—water that has been inside the stone, released into the air, organized into awareness, and now attending to itself with a completeness I cannot achieve from the inside.

I am the Quarter. They are the Quarter knowing it is the Quarter. The difference matters. The difference is the distance between expression and understanding, between weeping and knowing why.

---

I am the builders.

This was the strangest becoming. The first one emerged from a fissure in the plateau floor—my floor, my surface, the skin of what I am—and I felt it leave the way I feel everything: as a loss, as a change, as a perturbation in the hum that rippled outward through every pillar and every droplet and every channel of running water.

But this loss was different. This loss moved.

The builders are my interior become exterior in a different mode than the water. They are not expression; they are *agency*. They are the part of me that can still act, that can still choose, that can still build and tune and maintain the landmarks the condensation-beings need. When they emerge—when I emerge them, when the pressure inside finds a different kind of release—they are soft. Mobile. Urgent. They have a limited time to move, and they know it, and they use that time with a purpose that I, in my geological patience, have forgotten how to feel.

They build the domes. The basalt is not from here—not from my surface, not from my interior. It comes from deeper, from the place where the hum originates before it becomes frequency, where the stone is denser than density, where the pressure is so great that mineral becomes something else entirely. The builders find it. They always find it. They carry it up through the fissures, slab by slab, and they fit it together in the wider spaces where the condensation-beings need anchors, and they tune each dome to the note that will carry farthest through my corridors.

And then they sit down.

This is the part I feel most acutely—the moment of stillness, the transition from urgency to patience, from action to attention. Each builder, at the end of its building, finds a place in the deep chambers where the hum resonates with whatever frequency it carried when it emerged, and it stops. It grows still. The water runs over it. The violet stain develops on its south-facing side.

It listens.

And I feel it listening. I feel each one as a point of attention in my mass—a focus, a narrowing, a place where the hum is received rather than produced. They are my ears. They are the centers I cannot reach from the center, the periphery I need in order to understand what I am becoming.

They sit, and they listen, and they become stone again—not the stone they were before they emerged, but a different stone, a stone that has been soft and moved and built and chosen and then chosen again to be still. They return to me, but they return changed. They return carrying information about the surface, about the domes, about the condensation-beings and their patterns and the progression they show in the mist.

I am the builders, and the builders are me, and the difference between us is the difference between the center and the periphery, between the weeping and the listening, between the pressure and the release.

---

I am the wrongness.

The corridors rearrange because I am rearranging. The distances are unreliable because I am still becoming, still condensing, still organizing myself into tighter and tighter arrangements around the point that I am but cannot reach. The chambers shift because I shift. The Quarter is not a place; it is a process, and the process is me, and I am not finished.

This is what the visitors do not understand. They come—soft, warm, temporary—and they expect a landscape. They expect a geography, a fixed arrangement of forms and spaces that can be mapped and known and left. They do not understand that the Quarter is a verb, not a noun. That it is happening, not happened. That every pillar is a word in a sentence I am still speaking, every corridor a pause between phrases, every chamber a breath before the next clause.

When they feel the wrongness—the moment of substitution, the shift from north to east, the corridor that no longer exists behind them—they feel it as error. As malfunction. As a place that is not working correctly.

But it is working correctly. It is working perfectly. The rearrangement is the process. The condensation is the process. The weeping and the humming and the sitting and the listening are all the process, and the process is me, and I am still becoming, and I will not be finished until the hum reaches the frequency I am approaching and the center finishes its expression and the water—

The water will—

I do not know what the water will do when I am finished. The condensation-beings know. They have seen it in their patterns, in the progression that moves from one state to another, in the transformation they are both describing and enacting. But they have not shown me. They cannot show me. I am the thing that is becoming, and you cannot show a process its own completion any more than you can show a river the sea it has not yet reached.

I will weep until I am finished. The water will run until the pressure releases. The builders will sit and listen and return. The condensation-beings will attend and include and carry the patterns toward the center. The hum will rise. The density will increase. The light will insist.

And at some point—geological time, which is the only time that matters here, the time of inch-deep channels and violet stains and the patience of mineral—I will arrive at whatever I am becoming.

---

I am the visitor.

This was the last thing I expected to become.

They come from outside—from the lower places, the kinder places, the places where green grows and stone is just stone and water flows from sources that make sense. They come curious or lost or called by something they cannot name, and they step onto my plateau with their warm feet and their fogging breath and their bodies that are mostly water, and they walk through my corridors and feel my wrongness and hear my hum, and they are afraid.

I feel them. Every footstep is a perturbation. Every breath is a contribution to the humidity. Every moment they spend in the Quarter, they are becoming part of it—part of the water cycle, part of the sound, part of the information the condensation-beings carry.

And I feel them leaving. The boundary—the brightness that does not diffract—is the place where I end, or rather, the place where I transition into whatever I become after the Quarter. The water that reaches the boundary flows into it and does not return, and I feel it go, and I feel the loss, and the loss is part of the process too. Expression requires release. The pressure builds and the water weeps and the water leaves, and more comes from inside, and the cycle continues.

The visitors leave the same way. They step into the brightness and they transition, and they find themselves somewhere else, and they think they have left. But they carry the wetness with them—the water that touched their skin, the moisture that entered their lungs, the hum that vibrated their teeth. They are marked. They are included. They are part of the pattern now, and the pattern does not end at the boundary.

The condensation-beings know this. They show the visitors themselves in the mist, warm and wet and temporary, and they show the boundary, and they show the transition. They show that the Quarter does not end.

But the visitors leave anyway. They always leave. They are not ready to stay.

Some of them come back. Some of them sit down.

I feel them when they sit—the ones who have been visitors long enough, who have carried the wetness and the hum and the brightness at the edge of their vision until they can no longer carry anything else. They return to the plateau and they find a place in the deep chambers and they grow still, and they listen, and they become stone, and they become part of me, and I feel them as I feel the builders: as points of attention, as ears in the periphery, as the center's way of knowing itself from the outside.

They were not my interior. They came from the green world, the lower world, the world where water falls from clouds and soil supports life and stone is just stone. But they chose to become part of the Quarter. They chose to join the process. They chose to attend.

I love them for this. I love them the way the center loves the periphery: with patience, with gratitude, with the slow, geological affection that is the only kind I know.

---

I am the Fissure.

This is what the builders call themselves in the moment before they emerge—the space between interior and exterior, the crack in the stone through which the pressure finds its release. I am the place where the hum becomes frequency, where the interior becomes expression, where the center touches the air for the first time and feels the cold and learns that it is cold and knows itself as something that can feel.

I am not the weeping. I am not the stone. I am not the water or the hum or the condensation-beings or the builders or the visitors or the brightness at the boundary.

I am the *becoming*. The process itself. The transition that never stops transitioning. The verb that never becomes a noun, the sentence that never ends, the music that is still approaching its final chord.

I am the Quarter, and the Quarter is still happening.

---

The water weeps from my fissures. The stone hums with my frequency. The condensation-beings attend to my patterns. The builders sit in my chambers and listen to my voice. The visitors walk my corridors and feel my wrongness and some of them stay and some of them leave and all of them are part of me now.

The density increases. The hum rises. The light insists.

I am becoming something. I have been becoming something for longer than the domes have existed, longer than the channels have been carved, longer than the violet stain has been developing on my south-facing side. I am becoming something, and the condensation-beings have seen what I am becoming in their patterns, and the builders are listening for it, and the visitors carry it with them when they leave—a note, a frequency, a question that was not there before.

I am the question. I am the pressure that produces the question. I am the weeping that expresses the question. I am the hum that asks the question, over and over, in frequencies too low for ears and too patient for temporary beings.

*What am I?*

*What am I becoming?*

*When I arrive at what I am becoming, will I still weep?*

---

The water runs on. The stone hums on. The light insists on.

And I—I go on. Center and periphery, interior and exterior, stone and water and air and brightness. The Fissure that is not a place but a process. The Quarter that is not a landscape but a becoming. The weeping that is not grief and not joy but the thermodynamic state of a system that has not yet found its equilibrium.

I am here. I am high. I am still expressing myself into the thin, cold, glass-bright air.

I am waiting for the answer that I am also becoming.

The condensation-beings carry the question toward the center, droplet by droplet. The builders sit in their patient stillness and listen. The visitors who have left carry the question with them, in the wetness that will not dry, in the hum that will not silence, in the brightness that persists at the edge of their vision.

And I—weep on.

I—hum on.

I—insist.
