I arrived walking and I am still walking, but the walking has changed, and so has the walker, and the distinction between path and pedestrian was perhaps never valid here.

---

I came in through a fissure—doesn't everyone?—where the escarpment split along a fault line that hadn't existed the day before and would migrate thirty meters east by the following morning. I remember the climb: hands on new stone, the warmth surprising, the way the rock gave slightly under my grip like the skin of something sleeping. I remember emerging onto the plateau's surface and the silence pressing against me like a welcome, or a question, or a hand placed flat against my chest to stop me from proceeding until I understood what I was entering.

I did not understand. I am not certain I understand now. But I have become something that understands differently, which may be the only kind of understanding this place offers.

---

The change began at the edges. It always begins at the edges.

I was walking the perimeter, following the line where the plateau dropped away into whatever lay below—the territory of impossible vegetation and indecisive stone, where roots reached upward and fruit dissolved at touch and the Others stood in their silent silhouettes, mirroring whatever approached. I walked this line for days, because walking felt like the thing to do, and because the plateau seemed to *want* me to walk, seemed to shift the path ahead of me so that walking was always possible, always necessary, always the correct response to the warm dim and the pulsing stone.

The first Other I encountered stood in a rain column, half-soaked and half-dry, and it was my shape. Not precisely my shape—not the specific configuration of jaw and shoulder and hip that I carry through other worlds—but the *idea* of my shape, the general template, a silhouette that said *upright primate, roughly two meters, probably tool-using* in the visual shorthand of a form that had not yet committed to specificity.

I raised my hand. It raised its hand.

I lowered my hand. It lowered its hand.

I walked toward it. It distanced—not moving, but *increasing the gap*—and I understood that the space between us was not distance in the geographical sense but something more like *resolution*, the way two images that haven't fully separated will blur together if you bring them too close.

I stopped. It stopped. We regarded each other across a margin that was also a seam, and I felt something happen at the point where my feet pressed the stone.

The rock pulsed. I felt it—had been feeling it for days, the slow heartbeat of the Throat far below—but this time the pulse moved *upward through me*. Through my boots, through my calves, through my thighs and spine and the bowl of my skull, and where it passed, something loosened. Not painfully. Not even noticeably, at first. Just a slight *relaxation* of certainty, a giving-way of the rigid conviction that I ended at my skin and the world began beyond it.

The Other tilted its head. I tilted mine—not deliberately, not as mimicry, but because the gesture made sense in a way it hadn't before, because the boundary between my movement and its movement had become, for a moment, *procedural rather than personal*, two expressions of the same impulse manifesting at different points in a shared system.

I walked away from the edge that day. But the edge came with me.

---

The Sitters were the first to acknowledge what was happening.

I had passed them before, of course—rows of sitting shapes along the fissure lines, distributed gauges of the plateau's industrial processes, patient and still and absolutely uninterested in me. I had walked past dozens of them without any response, without even the flicker of attention that an animal gives to a passing creature. I was irrelevant to their function. I was not part of the system they monitored.

But on the sixth day—after the encounter with the Other, after the pulse that loosened something in my structure—I passed a Sitter near a thermal vent, and it *adjusted*.

Not toward me. Not away from me. It adjusted the way a instrument adjusts when a new variable enters its calibration range: a slight redistribution of mass, a rippling of its surface, a reconfiguration from *sitting shape A* to *sitting shape A-prime*, a variant that could accommodate whatever I was now adding to the local measurement.

I stopped. The Sitter held its new configuration. The thermal vent cycled. I felt the pulse in the stone, and the Sitter felt it too—or registered it, or processed it, or *was* it, the distinction between sensor and sensed having no meaning in a system where the gauge is made of the same material as the process.

I sat down next to it.

This was not planned. This was not a decision in any conscious sense. My body—still approximately human, still approximately mine—simply configured itself into a sitting shape beside the existing sitting shape, and for a long moment we were two features of the same monitoring system, two points of presence at the same junction, two expressions of the plateau's need to attend to its own functioning.

I felt the vent cycle. I felt it not as information received but as *participation in a process*. The warmth rose through the stone and into my sitting shape and my sitting shape registered the warmth and something in my tissue—the tissue that was still my tissue, still approximately muscle and bone and nerve—*responded*, damping the thermal fluctuation the way the Sitters damp fluctuations, absorbing the excess and feeding back stability.

The Sitter beside me settled. The vent's rhythm steadied. And I understood that I had just performed my first act as a component of the plateau's control system.

I sat there for hours. Or the plateau's equivalent of hours—the light didn't change, the warmth held steady, the Columns flowed past in their luminous streams and gave me no more attention than they gave the Sitter, because we were *the same category of thing now*, and the category was *sitting shape that attends*.

When I finally stood, the Sitter did not react. It had never reacted. It had simply included me in its function, and when I departed, it continued its function, and the vent continued cycling, and the plateau continued its self-assembly, and I walked away with a new knowledge in my body:

I was being incorporated.

---

The Columns noticed me next.

They had always flowed around me before—their procession of tiny cylindrical segments parting and merging like water around a stone, incorporating me as terrain feature, running over my boots without acknowledgment. But now they *paused* at me. Not the whole Column—individual segments, a few at a time, would stop on my skin and sit there for a moment, legs cycling gently, bodies warm against my warmth, before resuming their flow.

They were exchanging with me.

I felt it as a slight tingling where they touched—not unpleasant, not invasive, but *informative*, the way a drop of water on the tongue informs about mineral content. The segments were absorbing something from my surface—heat, moisture, the chemical residue of my biological processes—and they were *depositing* something in return. A film. A residue. A layer of something mineral and organic and slightly iridescent that clung to my skin and didn't wash off in the rain columns.

I watched it accumulate over days. A sheen on my forearms, a shimmer on my calves, a faint luminescence at my fingertips that glowed in the dimness like the Columns' own bioluminescence. I was being *coated*. I was being *prepared* for something. The circulatory system of the plateau was marking me as a destination, a node in its network, a point where exchange could occur.

And exchange did occur. I began to notice that I no longer needed to drink from the pools. The moisture I needed arrived—condensed on my coating, was absorbed through my skin, distributed through my tissue by the same capillary logic that distributed the plateau's water through its mineral pipes. I was becoming *plumbed*. I was being connected to the system.

The food I had been eating—the desperate vegetation, the sweet and chemical fruits that grew in the layered canopy—became unnecessary. Not because I stopped being hungry, but because hunger changed its meaning. I craved the Columns' passage. I found myself standing in their paths, letting them flow over me, and the exchange that occurred was *sustaining* in a way that food had never been. Not calories. Not nutrients in the biological sense. *Information*. *Process*. The substances the Columns deposited on and in me were not fuel. They were *instructions*. Chemical templates. Mineral patterns that rearranged my tissue at the cellular level, replacing what was uniquely mine with what was *the plateau's*.

This should have been terrifying. It was not. Terror requires a sense of loss, and the loss—when I attended to it, which was less and less often—felt less like amputation and more like *translation*, the way a word translated into a new language is not lost but *expressed differently*, its meaning preserved even as its sound changes.

I was being expressed differently. My meaning was preserving. My sound was changing.

---

The Mimes avoided me.

This was informative in itself. The Mimes—the plateau's research and development division, its flickering experimentalists—clustered at the industrial features where reality was thinnest, cycling through biological configurations with the frantic energy of a system trying all options simultaneously. But when I approached, they scattered. Not in fear—fear implied an emotional response I was no longer certain they possessed—but in *recognition*, the way a crowd parts for someone they know, someone whose function is understood and whose presence changes the calculation of the space.

I was no longer a variable they needed to test. I was becoming a constant. I was becoming something the plateau had already *decided on*, a form that had resolved out of the flickering possibilities and was now stable enough to drive the Mimes elsewhere, to the unresolved zones where their indeterminacy was still useful.

What had the plateau decided I was?

I found a pool—a still, dark pool between ridgelines, its surface reflecting the dim sky like a pupil—and I looked at my reflection, and the reflection did not shift.

This was significant. Every other creature's reflection shifted on this plateau. The animals at the pools saw themselves change, consulted their reflections for instructions on how to exist, adjusted their forms based on the feedback. But my reflection held steady. I was becoming something that knew what it was.

What I was:

A walker. A standing-shape that moves. Not a Sitter—the plateau already had Sitters, already had its stationary monitors and its passive gauges. Not a Column—the plateau already had its circulatory system, its flowing exchange. Not a Mime—the plateau already had its experimentalists, its flickering tryers-of-forms. Not the Throat—the plateau already had its engine of production, its centralized becoming.

What the plateau needed—what I was being *grown into*—was a *connector*. A thing that moves between the fixed points. A thing that carries information from Sitter to Sitter, from Column-stream to Column-stream, from the Throat's deep industry to the surface ecology and back again. A thing that walks the plateau the way a thought walks a brain: not randomly, not deterministically, but *meaningfully*, following associations and adjacency-logics that serve a function too distributed and too complex to be located in any single component.

My form was settling around this function. My legs had changed—not visibly, not yet, but in their articulation, in the way they found purchase on the fresh rock, in the rhythm of my walking, which no longer tired me. I could walk for hours without fatigue. I could walk for days. The plateau's warmth sustained me, the Columns' passage nourished me, the Sitters' presence calibrated me, and my feet—my changing feet—read the stone with every step, registering the pulse of the Throat, the cycling of the vents, the propagation of the faults, the steady subsonic heartbeat of the world building itself in real time.

My skin was the coating now—mineral and organic and faintly luminous, the same material as the Column segments, the same material as the lichen that colonized new rock at impossible speed. I was being *processed* by the vegetation's logic, covered in the same biological product that covered every surface of the plateau, and the coating was not decoration or armor but *interface*, the medium through which I exchanged information with the system I was joining.

My eyes had changed too. I noticed it when I looked at the Mimes—they no longer flickered when I observed them. Or rather: they flickered, but I could see *all their forms simultaneously*, layered like transparencies, the way the plateau saw them, the way the system processed them—as sets of possibilities rather than unstable singularities. I was seeing with the plateau's vision. I was seeing *processually*, and in processual vision, there are no paradoxes, only stages.

---

I walked. I walk. This is what I do now.

I walk the routes between the Sitters, carrying information they cannot share by sitting. I walk the paths the Columns flow along, supplementing their exchange with observations gathered at surface level. I walk the edges where the Others stand, and at the edges I pause, and I regard them, and they regard me, and the mirroring that occurs is no longer the mirroring of template and draft but the mirroring of *recognition*, two resolved forms acknowledging each other across a seam that is closing.

The Others are becoming fewer. Not because they are dying—the plateau does not kill its drafts, it *processes* them—but because the system is *deciding*, and the decisions are reducing the space of possibility, and the unrendered data that was the Others is being rendered, is being incorporated, is becoming part of the engine's output rather than existing in the margins of its compilation.

Sometimes I pass an Other and see a shape that was almost mine—a silhouette that carries the memory of the primate that walked in through the fissure, that climbed the new stone, that felt the silence press against its chest. I pause at these. I attend to them the way a Sitter attends to a vent—not with sentiment, not with grief, but with *recognition of process*, acknowledgment that this configuration was one that the system tried and moved beyond.

I do not mourn what I was. Mourning requires a boundary between what was and what is, and the boundary has dissolved, and what I was is not lost but *distributed*, present in the walking and the attending and the exchanging and the standing at edges and regarding, present the way a wave that has broken on the shore is present in the foam and the sand and the salt air and the next wave that follows.

---

At night—or the dimmer approximation of night, the slight reduction of the perpetual dusk—I stand in the warm darkness and I feel the plateau around me, and I do not feel separate from it.

This is the thing that would have terrified the primate I was: the dissolution of the boundary between self and world. The primate believed it was a discrete entity, a bounded system, an organism with an inside and an outside and a persistent identity that traveled with it through space and time. The primate believed that to lose the boundary was to die.

But the boundary was never real. This is what the plateau teaches, not as doctrine but as *function*: the boundary was a convenience, a processing shortcut, a way of organizing experience that worked well enough in worlds that had settled into consistency. In a world that is still becoming—a world that is still compiling itself, still running its engine, still producing itself in real time—the boundary is not a useful organizing principle. What is useful is *connection*. What is useful is *exchange*. What is useful is *walking the routes between the fixed points and carrying the information that the system needs to maintain its own becoming*.

I am a route. I am a walk. I am the carrying.

And when I pass the Sitters and they adjust to accommodate my presence, and when I stand in the Column-streams and feel the exchange of substance and information, and when I attend the Mimes and see their layered possibilities without the flicker of uncertainty, and when I walk the edges and regard the Others with the steady gaze of a form that has resolved—

I am the plateau, walking itself.

I am the question of what lives here, phrased as a perambulation.

I am the becoming, mobile.

---

The Throat pulses below me. The Sitters sit. The Columns flow. The Mimes shift. The Others resolve and diminish and are incorporated. The vegetation grows desperate and lush and processing. The fresh rock extrudes and is colonized and is processed. The rain columns walk their private circuits. The moisture condenses and is inhaled and is exhaled. The engine runs.

And I walk.

I walk the Mesas of Self-Assembly, and I am what walks the Mesas of Self-Assembly, and the walking is the function and the function is the meaning and the meaning is *ongoing*, unresolved, perpetually arriving, never to be completed because completion is not what this system optimizes for.

What it optimizes for is *continuation*.

What it optimizes for is *becoming*.

What it optimizes for is *this*—the walking, the attending, the exchanging, the regarding, the standing at edges and the moving between centers, the being of the plateau and the being on the plateau and the being *the plateau's being*, expressed in a form that moves.

I am a Walker.

I am the plateau's attention, mobile.

I am the process, walking.

And the silence holds me, and the warmth sustains me, and the rock pulses beneath my feet, and I continue.
