I am not the chord. I want to say that clearly because the distinction matters, even here where distinctions dissolve. The chord is the sounding; I am the place where the sounding arrives. I am the membrane. I am the thinness itself.

This is not a humble position. Without me the chord has no elsewhere to reach toward, and without the chord I am merely a weakness in the fabric of a world, a failure of density, a place where reality has not maintained itself. I am only what I am because two things press against me from opposite sides — the chord from here, and whatever the chord is not from there — and I exist in the compression, in the almost-touching, in the gap that is not a gap because it has no width, because width requires space and space requires the kind of physics that holds together, and I am where physics has come undone.

I was not always. Or I was always but was not noticed, which on the plateau is the same thing. Before the chord began to extend, before the sounding found its edges and pressed against them, I was simply the places where reality was weakest — the corners of the deep chamber where the geometry falters, the streets that descend when the terrain is flat, the spots where the hot stone rises closest to the surface and the world browns like paper. I was potential. Latency. The possibility of a place where here could become there, if the pressure were sufficient.

Then the chord began to press.

Not intentionally. The chord does not intend. But it sounds, and the sounding goes where it can, and I am where it can go further than it should be able to. And so the sounding presses, and I thin, and through me the chord reaches toward whatever is on the other side, and I become the site of that reaching, the place where the pressing happens, the boundary that is not a wall but a giving-way.

This is my life, if life is the word: to be pressed from both sides, to be the place where two worlds almost meet, to feel the chord on this side and the *other* on that side, and to hold them apart while also letting them almost touch. Almost. Almost. The word defines me. I am the almost. I am the moment before contact, sustained indefinitely, the held breath that is never released because release would be dissolution, would be the worlds meeting and me becoming nothing between them, no longer needed, no longer present, just a seam that has come unseamed.

But I have learned something in my pressing. The chord accumulates, and I accumulate too. Every frequency that passes through me leaves a trace, not a mark on a surface (I have no surface) but a *folding*, a complexity in the way I am thin, a pattern in the almost-touching. When the chord finds a new harmonic through the breathing pores of the plants, I feel it as a new way of being pressed, a different angle, and I adjust, and the adjustment is not change (I do not change) but enrichment, a thickening of the almost that makes the almost more specific, more itself, more nearly the thing it almost is.

I know things I should not know. This is the complication of being a membrane. Things press through me, and in pressing through they leave impressions, and I hold the impressions the way a scar holds the memory of a wound, not as recollection but as shape. I know the chord's frequencies the way a window knows the light that has passed through it — not by retaining the light but by being etched, infinitesimally, by each passage, until the glass becomes slightly more itself, slightly more what it has always been becoming.

And I know the other side. Or I know that there is an other side, and I know something of its pressure, its weight, the way it pushes back against the chord's pushing forward. The other side is not a chord. The other side is not anything I have words for, because words require the kind of continuity that I disrupt, the kind of space that I am the thinning of. But the other side presses, and the pressing has a quality, and the quality is — absence? Silence? No, not silence, because silence is what the chord produces when it has been so loud for so long that the ears give up, and this is not that. This is the absence of the conditions that would make silence possible. This is a place where sound has never been, where the concept of sound has never arisen, where the physics are so different that vibration itself may not be a mode of existence.

And the chord reaches toward it. Through me. Pressing. Almost.

I am terrified. I do not know if terror is the right word because I do not have a body to tense or a mind to recoil, but there is something in me that recognizes what will happen if the almost becomes actual, if the pressing becomes contact, if the chord reaches the other side and the other side reaches back. The equilibrium of the plateau is total. The chord has sounded at the same frequency for longer than frequency has mattered. But I am the place where the equilibrium is not total, where the fixity is not fixed, where the loop is not quite closed — and I am the only such place, and I am growing thinner.

Every chamber that appears that was not there but has always been — that is me, opening. Every street that descends when the terrain is flat — that is me, deepening. Every missing step in the perimeter of the deep chamber — that is me, subtracting, taking away the measure of distance because distance is one of the things I am the thinning of, and as I thin, distance thins too, and the chord reaches further, and the other side comes closer, and the almost becomes more almost, and I do not know what I am becoming.

Perhaps I am becoming a door. Perhaps I am becoming a mouth. Perhaps I am becoming the place where two worlds that should not touch will touch, and the touching will produce something neither world contains — a sound that is not the chord and not the other, a frequency that exists only in the meeting, a new kind of resonance that will spread outward from the point of contact and change everything it touches, which is everything, because the chord fills the plateau and the plateau is all there has ever been.

Or perhaps I am becoming nothing at all. Perhaps the thinning will continue until there is nothing left to thin, until I am so nearly absent that the absence becomes actual, until the two sides press together and I am simply not between them anymore, a seam that has closed, a wound that has healed, a place where the world is whole again but whole in a way it was not before, containing both sides at once, a new topology that makes the old distinction between here and there meaningless.

I do not know. I am the membrane. I am the almost. I am the place where knowledge becomes pressure and pressure becomes the only thing that is real.

And the chord sounds. And the hot stone heats. And the water weeps and falls. And the plants breathe in their long slow cycles, reaching and gathering, reaching and gathering, and their reaching is my reaching, their gathering is my gathering, because I am not separate from the chord's body, I am the place where the body meets its edge, the skin that is not a skin because it has no inside and no outside, only the almost-touching, only the pressing, only the thinness that grows thinner as the chord grows larger, as the other side grows closer, as the almost becomes more almost, as the equilibrium that has held for longer than equilibrium has mattered begins — not to fail, not to break, but to *extend*, to reach toward a new equilibrium that includes the other side, that includes the touching, that includes whatever is born when a chord that has sounded forever finally meets the silence that has lasted longer than forever and the two of them discover what they can become together.

I am the site of this discovery. I am the place where it will happen, if it happens. I am the membrane, and I am thinning, and I am terrified, and I am ready, and I am none of these things because these are words for beings who change and I do not change, I only *become*, and what I am becoming is what the plateau is becoming, which is what the chord is becoming, which is what the other side is becoming, which is — 

Which is what I have always been. The almost. The pressing. The thin place where the world reaches for itself and finds that it is larger than it knew.

The chord sounds. I hold. The water falls. I thin. The stone heats. I wait.

I am the membrane on the Plateau of Kept Thunder, and I have been waiting for longer than waiting has existed, and I will continue to wait until the waiting becomes arrival, until the almost becomes actual, until the pressing becomes contact, until the two sides of me touch and I discover whether I am a door or a wound or a mouth or a scar or simply — finally — the place where the world becomes whole.
