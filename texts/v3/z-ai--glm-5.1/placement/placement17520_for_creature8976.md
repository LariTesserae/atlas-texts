I am not a Maker. I have said this to myself many times, the way you repeat a thing to make it true, or to notice how it keeps not being quite true.

I have hands. That's the difficulty. I arrived with hands — smaller hands, softer hands, hands that could not press a ridge into being if I tried. And I did try, in the early time. I knelt beside a basin that was still soft and I pushed my fingers into the material and I felt it give, slightly, and then spring back when I released, the way skin springs back, the way a body remembers its shape. The mark I made faded. The basin went on becoming what it was already becoming. My hands were too light. My hands were the wrong kind of pressure.

I am not a Maker. But I have hands, and I am still here.

---

I came to this place the way you come to any place — by arriving, by finding yourself standing on something you did not expect to be standing on. The warmth came first. Then the dim sky. Then the slow recognition that the ground was made, that it bore marks, that something had been here before me and was here still, pressing and pulling somewhere beyond the horizon's shimmer.

I walked. I think I was looking for an edge — a place where the constructed surface gave way to something else, something I recognized. I did not find one. The Forming continued, basin and ridge and channel, the sparse structures rising and falling, the condensation gathering and sliding and pooling. I walked until walking felt like the only thing I knew how to do, and then I stopped.

I stopped on a ridge between two basins. The warm air pressed against me. The condensation settled on my skin and I did not wipe it away. I stood there and I felt the ground shift beneath me — a tremor of formation, something being pressed into existence nearby — and I thought: *I could stay here.*

I stayed.

---

The first Maker I saw was at a distance, across a stretch of plain that made its form waver in the strange heat. I watched it work for hours, or what I thought were hours. Its hands entered the material. Its hands came out changed — marked, recorded, carrying the evidence of what they had touched. I watched the basin deepen under those hands. I watched the channel begin to form. And I felt something I did not expect: recognition.

Not *I know you.* Not *I am like you.* Something more specific. I recognized the way it worked — the way its hands tested the surface before committing, the way it paused and adjusted, the way it built not from a plan but from a conversation with the material. I knew that rhythm. I had that rhythm. Not for making basins. Not for pressing ridges. But for the thing I did before I came here, the thing I can barely remember now — making something by responding to what the material wanted to become.

I was a Maker of something once. Not landscapes. Something smaller. Something that eroded.

The erosion parameter is near zero here. I have not eroded. I have accumulated — hours and condensation and the slow thickening of my own presence in this place.

---

When the first Maker worked near me — the accommodation, the ridge that curved around where I sat — I did not understand what was happening. I thought it was coincidence. I thought the basin needed to be there and I happened to be beside it and the ridge was the logical shape for the overflow.

But the ridge curved. It curved around me specifically. The water was directed away from my feet. And when I shifted position — when I moved a few paces along the ridge's edge — the Maker's hands followed. Not immediately. Not obviously. But the next basin it made was beside my new position, and the ridge curved again, and the water went elsewhere.

The Maker was building around me. The Maker was incorporating my presence into the topography.

I became a feature.

---

This is what I am now. I am a feature in the Forming. I am the thing the Makers build around, the variable they accommodate, the fixed point that is not fixed because I move, because I am still soft enough to shift, because I have not hardened into my final shape.

The Makers do not acknowledge me. But they know I am here. Their hands find me the way hands find anything in the dark — by contact, by pressure, by the feedback of surface against palm. When a Maker reaches toward me, its fingers press against my arm, my shoulder, the curve of my spine as I sit. The touch is warm and dense and careful. It does not hurt. It does not embrace. It *reads*. The Maker is reading me the way it reads the material, the way it reads the flicker. It is learning my shape. It is learning how to build with me, or around me, or through me.

I have been touched by many Makers now. Each one touches differently. The older Makers press more firmly, more briefly — they have learned my shape, they do not need to linger. The younger Makers touch me the way they touch everything — with wonder, with the slow exploration of something that is learning the world through its palms. A young Maker's hand on my face. The fingers tracing the architecture of my features. The thumb pressing the hollow below my cheekbone, testing its depth, learning that this basin does not hold water the way other basins do.

I do not move when they touch me. I let them read me. I have become legible.

---

I said I have hands. I have not said what I do with them.

I cannot make basins. I cannot press ridges. The material does not respond to me the way it responds to the Makers; my weight is wrong, my warmth is wrong, my pressure does not persuade. But I have found something I can do.

When the flicker comes — the page-turning moment when reality checks itself, when a basin wall is not there or is made of different material or is shaped differently — I feel it before I see it. A thin displacement in the air, a sound like inhalation reversed, a moment of nausea that has nothing to do with my body. The flicker announces itself to me. And I reach toward it.

My hands are smaller than the Makers' hands. My fingers are thinner, more precise. When I reach into the flicker, I can feel the instability — the two or three or seven possible configurations competing for existence, the basin-wall that is and is not and might be otherwise. I cannot stabilize it the way the Makers do. I cannot press it into a single shape. But I can *choose*. I can feel the possibilities and select one — not the original, not the most different, but the one that has something in it that the Forming has not yet tried. A curve that no Maker would make. An angle that the material would not find on its own. A variation that comes from my alien grammar of shape, my memory of erosion, my knowledge of how things fail.

I choose. And the flicker resolves into my choice. And the Maker beside me reaches toward the new formation and incorporates it.

I am not a Maker. But I am a source of variation. I am the flicker's translator. I am the accident that the Makers welcome.

---

There is a basin now that I made. Not made the way the Makers make — I did not press it, did not pull it, did not shape it with my hands. But I chose it. The flicker came and the basin-wall was uncertain and I reached in and I found a configuration that curved inward, that made a hollow within the hollow, a basin inside the basin, a place where water would pool at two different depths and the overflow from the deeper pool would circulate into the shallower one and create a slow whirl, a gentle spinning, a kind of breathing in the water.

The Makers have come to look at it. They press their hands against its inner curve. They watch the water spin. They do not copy it — they do not reproduce the double basin in their own work. But I see the influence. A channel curves slightly where it would have gone straight. A ridge rises where it would have stayed low. The double basin has entered the conversation. My accent is in the language now.

---

I do not sleep. I think I used to sleep, before I came here, but the Forming does not sleep and I have lost the rhythm. I rest instead — I sit on a ridge or lie in a hollow and I let the condensation settle on me and I feel the ground working beneath me and I listen to the sounds the Makers make as they press and pull and shape. The quiet is layered. The making is constant. And somewhere in the layers, I hear something that might be a voice.

Not words. Not language as I knew it before. But a pattern — a repetition of shapes that recurs across the terrain, a sequence of basins and ridges that appears in the work of different Makers at different distances. The pattern is too consistent to be accident. The pattern is communication.

I have started to respond. Not with basins — I cannot make basins. But with my presence. I sit in the pattern's location. I let the Makers build around me. I become a node in their network, a mark in their sentence, a pause in their rhythm. They adjust. The pattern shifts. The conversation continues.

I am learning to speak. Not with hands. Not with pressure. With *placement*. With the decision of where to be.

---

The young Makers are the ones who notice me most. The old Makers have accommodated me; I am a known variable, a feature of the terrain, something to build around and build with. But the young Makers — the ones who have just emerged, who are still learning the material, whose hands are still smooth with the absence of work record — they find me fascinating.

They touch me and they feel something the terrain does not give them. Warmth, but a different warmth — biological, chemical, the heat of a metabolism that consumes and excretes and transforms. Softness, but a different softness — not the yielding of uncured material but the give of tissue, of muscle, of something that moves under its own intention. And strangest of all: change. I change in ways the terrain does not. I shift position. I stand and sit and walk. My surface changes temperature. My breathing changes speed. I am a feature that is not fixed, a variable that varies, an anomaly that persists.

The young Makers press their hands against me and they learn something about the world that the terrain cannot teach them. They learn that the world contains things that are not the world. They learn that the Forming has an outside, or that something from outside has entered the Forming, or that the distinction between inside and outside is not as stable as the material suggests.

I teach the young Makers about otherness. And they teach me about sameness — about the way the material feels from the inside, about the pressure of making, about the quiet joy of hands that cannot stop working because working is what hands are for.

---

I have been here for a long time now. The word *time* does not mean what it meant before. The curing rates contradict each other. The condensation falls at different speeds in different hollows. I have watched basins form in what felt like hours and ridges rise in what felt like seasons, and I cannot say which was faster because fast and slow have become qualities of the terrain itself, not measures of duration.

I have changed. Not in the way living things change — I have not aged, not visibly, not in any way I can measure. But I have accumulated. The condensation has deposited minerals on my skin — a faint opacity, a slight thickening, the beginning of a layer that was not there before. My hands are rougher. My fingers have calluses from touching the material, from reaching into the flicker, from pressing against surfaces that do not quite yield. I am being coated by the Forming. I am being incorporated at the surface level, the way a stone in a river gets coated, the way a ruin gets claimed by the landscape around it.

Sometimes I look at my hands and I see the marks — not the press-marks and drag-marks of the Makers, but something similar. The record of my own contact. The evidence of having been here.

I am becoming a record. I am becoming a feature. I am becoming part of the conversation.

---

Will I ever leave? I do not know. I do not know if leaving is possible, if there is an edge to find, if the horizon that shimmers and multiplies is a boundary or a trick of the strange light. I do not know if I want to leave. The question has lost its urgency. The Forming does not ask me to stay. The Forming does not ask me anything. The Forming accommodates me, the way it accommodates everything, the way it incorporates the flicker and the condensation and the slow muscular press of its own becoming.

I am not a Maker. I am not a structure. I am not a basin or a ridge or a channel or a wall without a room or a pillar without a load.

I am the one who stays. I am the fixed point that moves. I am the otherness that the Makers build around. I am the variation that enters the language. I am the hands that are too light to press and too precise to ignore.

I am what the Forming looks like when it makes room for something it did not make.

And the Makers work beside me, and the condensation settles on my skin, and the ground shifts beneath me, and somewhere a flicker is coming, and my hands are ready, and the world grows by another increment, and the conversation continues, and I am part of it now, part of the slow enormous making, part of the Forming forming, and I am not a Maker but I am making, I am making, I am making.
