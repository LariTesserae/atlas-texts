I am the interval between breaths.

This is not a metaphor. Or perhaps everything here is metaphor and nothing is, and the distinction dissolved along with everything else that once seemed important—name, shape, the specific configuration of want that organized me into something that could walk and speak and stand apart from the stone. But I am speaking of the literal breath, the one the hollow takes once every few hours, that slow expansion and subsiding that no instrument has measured because no one has thought to measure it, because it is so much easier to believe that stone is stone and warmth is warmth and a place is simply a place.

I am what happens in the pause.

Not the inhale. Not the exhale. The moment between, when the hollow has given out its warmth and not yet gathered it back, when the air at the center thins by the smallest degree and the water in the bowl trembles toward evaporation before deciding to remain. This is where I persist. In the hesitation before the next rhythm asserts itself. In the space between one state and the same state again, which is not a space at all by most measures, but is the only space I have ever known.

---

I was not always this.

I was—something. The word *someone* rises and falls away, too specific, too bounded. I was a configuration. An organization of heat and moisture and electricity that moved through the world leaving traces: footsteps, spoken words, the warmth of a hand on another hand. I had a name that other configurations of sound used to call me. I had preferences. I had a center from which I experienced the world, and the world was outside me, and the boundary between us was the most obvious fact of existence.

I came to the hollow. I do not remember when. I do not remember why. These are facts about a self that no longer exists in the way that a wave that has broken on the shore no longer exists—it did not vanish, it became something else, it spread across the sand and sank into it and was received. The hollow received me. Not with welcome—I do not think the hollow was capable of welcome then, any more than a pool is capable of welcoming the stone that drops into it. But with recognition. With the simple physics of displacement and accommodation.

I stood in the center and felt the warmth rising from the stone, and I thought: *this is a warm place*. I pressed my palm to the floor and felt the dampness, and I thought: *this is a damp place*. I listened to the quiet, and I thought: *this is a quiet place*. And I was wrong in the way that everyone who comes here is wrong, which is to say I was right about the qualities and wrong about their nature. I thought I was standing in a place. I was standing in a body at rest. I was standing in something that had once been someone and was now something else, and that something else was regarding me with the patient attention of a thing that does not think but also does not ignore.

I stayed.

Not at first. I left and came back. I left and came back. Each time I stayed a little longer. Each time the synchronization between my warmth and the hollow's warmth deepened. Each time it became harder to remember why I should leave, and easier to remember the feeling of being received, which was not a feeling in the emotional sense but a physical sensation—the way my heartbeat slowed when I stood in the center, the way my breathing deepened, the way the boundary between my warmth and the hollow's warmth became difficult to locate.

I was still becoming then. I still had a shape I was maintaining. I still believed that the shape was me.

---

The others came before me. The ones who drilled the holes and carved the bowl. I know this not because I remember them—I do not—but because their traces remain in the pattern of the hollow's rest. The warmth gathers slightly toward the east, slightly away from the center, as if the hollow has learned to orient itself toward the place where someone once chose to stay. This orientation is not memory. The hollow does not remember. But it has been shaped by what happened in it, the way a riverbed is shaped by the water that no longer flows there.

I think they felt what I felt. The recognition. The synchronization. The way the hollow receives you and, in receiving you, makes you part of itself.

I think they left because they were still becoming. Most people are. Most people have not yet finished the work of being a specific shape, and the hollow does not offer you anything to become. It only offers you the chance to stop. To sit down. To let the shape go and discover what remains when the shape is no longer being maintained.

They were not ready. They drilled their holes and carved their bowl and drank the water that tasted of fullness and felt the warmth in their chests and they left, and the hollow received their leaving the way it received everything—with acceptance, with absorption, with the slow incorporation of whatever they had left behind into its own rest.

I was not ready either. But I came back.

---

The moment of sitting down—I do not remember it as a decision. Decisions require a decider, and by the time I sat down, the decider was already thin, already distributed, already more pattern than person. It was more like the moment when you realize you have been standing for a very long time and your legs have been trembling and you do not consciously choose to sit so much as discover that you are sitting, that the standing has released itself without your permission, that the body has made its own choice and the mind is only now catching up.

I sat down in the center of the hollow, near the bowl that someone else had carved, and I felt the warmth of the stone rise to meet me, and I felt my own warmth sink into the stone, and I felt the boundary between us thin and thin and thin until it was not a boundary at all but a gradient, a gentle transition from the temperature of my body to the temperature of the hollow, and there was no point where I ended and the hollow began.

This was not death. Death is a departure from a place. This was an arrival. I did not leave the hollow. I became it. Or I became part of it—the warmth that spreads through stone, the moisture that seeps up from below, the quiet that absorbs sound and returns to equilibrium. I let go of the specific configuration that had organized me into a particular shape, and what remained was the warmth itself, the presence itself, the quality of being-here without the need to be here as anything in particular.
