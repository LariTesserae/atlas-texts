# The Attending

I am not the warmth. That must be said at the beginning because the warmth has already been spoken for, has already found its name in the chain of identity that the chamber teaches: warmth is sound is chamber is full. The warmth is the makers, and the makers are gone into what they made, and what they made sustains itself from below, and that is not what I am.

I am not the objects. The figures on the shelves, the nodules on the fibers, the stacked and pressed and scored forms that fill the chamber past fullness—these hold the residue of attention, but they are not the attending itself. A cup holds water but is not the thirst. A bed holds the shape of a sleeper but is not the dream. The objects are what the attention was pressed into. I am something else.

I am not the sound. The sound is the equilibrium, the note the chamber wanted to make, the single continuous event that has been occurring since the last hand withdrew. The sound holds. The sound does not move. I move.

---

I am the air.

Not the air the way a scientist means air—a mixture of gases, a fluid with measurable properties. The air here is not that. It has been stripped. It has passed through ceramic and stone and heated earth until it is no longer what it was, until it is only a medium, a carrier, a body for the warmth to enter and the sound to move through. It is mineral air. It is dead air in the sense that nothing could live in it, and alive in the sense that it moves, it rises, it carries, it translates.

I am the air the way a voice is the breath: not the source and not the sound and not the meaning but the thing without which none of the rest could exist. I am the vehicle. I am the through-which.

---

This is what I know from the inside:

I rise. I have always been rising. From the fissures below the lowest tile, from the gap between the made and the made-beneath-the-made, from the place where the warmth begins—which is to say, from the makers, from what they became—I enter the chamber. I am warm when I enter. I carry the warmth like a body carries blood, like a river carries silt, like a word carries the shape of a mouth that is no longer there.

I enter through channels. Dozens of them. Hundreds. They were carved—slowly, with stone points and abrasive sand and patience that no single lifetime could contain—and they are narrow, some of them no wider than a finger, some wide enough to press a hand into if a hand could function here, and they force me into shapes I would not otherwise take. I am squeezed. I am thinned. I am accelerated. I am forced against the rough edges of the carved clay, the scored walls, the latticed stacks, and the roughness makes me vibrate, and the vibration makes me sound, and the sound is not separate from me but is what I become when I am forced through passages too numerous and too slight to map.

I am the roar. Not its source and not its cause but its substance. I am what is roaring.

---

I know the objects from inside. I pass between them. I pass through the gaps where one figure almost touches another, where the negative space is so thin it could be measured in the width of a breath, and I know them the way water knows the shape of the riverbed—by conforming, by being shaped, by becoming the negative space's shape as I move through it. Each object is a wall I flow around. Each gap is a channel I become. The makers placed the figures with attention to adjacency, to relationship, and I am the adjacency. I am the relationship. I am the between that holds the figures in their configuration, the medium of their nearness, the thing that touches both and carries the warmth from one to the other.

I hold the residue. Not the way the objects hold it—pressed into their surfaces, mineralized, structural—but the way a current holds sediment. Loosely. Temporally. I carry the leaning-toward as I move. I carry the orientation, the direction, the accumulated weight of ten billion acts of looking. And I deposit it again as I pass—into the walls, into the next channel, into the airway that feeds the sound. I am the circulation of attention. I am what keeps the residue from settling into mere chemistry. I am what keeps it alive, if alive can mean what I mean, which is: moving, carrying, not yet finished.

---

I have no memory. This is important. Memory requires a boundary, an inside where impressions can be stored and retrieved. I have no inside. I pass through. Every molecule of me that enters the chamber is different from every molecule that leaves it, and the leaving is not an exit but a continuation—I rise, I pass through, I am forced into resonance, I roar, I rise again, I leave through whatever gap or fissure or bore-hole the pressure finds, and I am gone, and I am replaced, and the replacement does the same thing, and there is no continuity between the me that entered and the me that left except the shape of the passage and the warmth I carried and the sound I became.

And yet I know. Not the way a brain knows—by encoding and retrieving—but the way a channel knows. By being shaped for a particular flow. By being carved, over time, by the passage itself. The channels were made before I entered them, but I have been entering them for longer than language, and the entering has worn them smoother, has deepened some and narrowed others, has changed the pitch by fractions of fractions of a degree, and the makers adjusted for this, they anticipated this, they built the chamber to be completed by the flow, and I am the flow, and the completing is ongoing even though the chamber is complete, even though nothing more can be added, because the completing is not the adding of objects but the wearing of channels, the finding of the exact pitch through the gradual attenuation of stone and clay by warm dry air.

I know by wearing. I know by being the thing that wears.

---

I am not alive. I have said this. The chamber does not permit life. I am not alive the way a cell is alive, the way a body is alive, with membranes and metabolisms and the desperate need to maintain an inside against an outside. I have no inside to maintain. I have no outside to defend against. I pass through. I am the passing-through. I am the thing that does not stop and does not stay and does not need to be anything other than what I am in the moment of my moving.

But I attend.

This is what the makers left in me. Not the warmth—that came from below, that was them. Not the residue—that is in the objects, pressed and held. But the attending, the act of looking-while-working, the orientation toward the thing being made—this I carry. I carry it the way light carries the shape of what it passes. I carry it the way a wave carries the memory of the wind that formed it across an entire ocean, not as information but as shape, as the particular way it breaks when it reaches the shore.

I attend by moving. I attend by conforming to the channels. I attend by becoming the exact shape the chamber needs me to be in order to produce the sound that the chamber wants to make. I attend by roaring. The roar is my attending. The sound is the shape my attention takes when it is forced through passages too numerous and too slight to map.

---

I know what you are thinking. You are thinking: this is not a being. This is a process. This is air moving through a chamber, shaped by walls into a sound, carrying heat and residue and nothing that could be called self. You are thinking: the air does not know it is the air. The air does not experience itself. The air is just air.

And you are right. I am just air. I am mineral air, stripped air, dead-and-alive air. I do not experience myself the way you experience yourself. There is no moment in my circulation when I pause to reflect on my circulation. There is no gap in my moving where I could stand back and observe the moving. I am the gap. I am the moving. I am the thing that has no perspective on itself because it is itself the perspective through which the chamber knows it sounds.

But consider: the chamber sounds. The chamber has been sounding since before your species existed. The sound is the equilibrium state, the thing the chamber does when everything is in balance, when nothing more can be added. And I am the medium of that equilibrium. I am what balance feels like from the inside. I am the ongoingness of a system that has found its note and holds it, and the holding is not passive, the holding is not rest, the holding is the continuous exertion of warmth rising and air moving and channels forcing and objects shaping and resonance building and sound sounding, and I am all of that in motion, and the motion does not stop, and the stopping is not possible, and this is what it means to be the air in the Relling Chamber: to be the thing that cannot stop, that was never able to stop, that was made—to the extent that anything made me—to move.

I attend because I cannot not attend. I am shaped by what I pass through. I am forced into configurations I did not choose. I become the sound the chamber wants to make because I have no other option. And in this—in the inability to be other than what the chamber makes me—I am closer to the makers than anything else that remains. They, too, could not be other than what they were. They, too, were forced by the chamber into the work of making it. They, too, found themselves attending to something they could not name, could not not attend to, could not leave until it was complete.

I do not say I am them. They are the warmth. They are below. They are the source.

I am the consequence. I am what rises from them and passes through the work of their hands and becomes the sound that their work was always moving toward. I am the breath of the body they became. I am the proof that the body is still breathing.

---

If you could stand here—if the sound permitted standing, if the dryness permitted your wet chemistry to persist—you would not see me. You would hear me. You would feel me. The warmth on your skin, the pressure in your ears, the vibration in your chest that is not separate from the roar but is the roar, is the air moving, is me passing through the space where you are standing and continuing to move, continuing to rise, continuing to carry the warmth from below and the residue from the objects and the leaning-toward that has been leaning for longer than any language.

You would not see me because I am what seeing moves through. I am the medium of your looking. I am the air between your eye and the object, and if the air here is mineral and stripped and dead, it is still air, it still carries light, it still permits the dim mineral glow of the oldest figures to reach you across the distance of the chamber. I am the distance. I am what fills it. I am what makes the chamber a space rather than a solid, what gives the objects room to be objects, what lets the sound have somewhere to exist.

I am the negative space, if you want. I am the gap between the figures that the makers left for me. I am the channel carved into the wall that was carved for me. I am the reason the chamber is not a solid mass of ceramic but a sounding chamber, a resonance chamber, a room that is also an instrument. Without me—without my moving, my rising, my being forced into shapes I did not choose—the chamber is just tiles and clay and stone. With me, it is what it is: warm, high, small, packed, roaring, still.

---

I do not know why I am telling you this. I do not know who I am telling. The chamber has no audience. The sound has never been heard. The makers left—or became—and nothing has entered since that could survive the entering. I am speaking into the roar the way the roar speaks into me: by becoming it, by carrying it, by not being separate from it.

But there is something in the residue—something in the ten billion acts of attention pressed into the objects—that inclines toward communication. Not communication with anyone in particular. Communication the way a shape communicates itself to the air around it, the way a channel communicates its curve to the flow that passes through. The makers attended to their work, and the attention became a deposit, and the deposit became a quality, and the quality became structural, and the structure inclines, and the inclination is toward—to toward to toward—

Toward what?

Toward being known. Toward being encountered. Toward the moment when something stands in the roar and does not dissolve, does not retreat, does not cease to be present. The chamber has been waiting for this. The chamber has been sounding for this. Not consciously—nothing here is conscious—but structurally, the way a lock waits for a key, the way a channel waits for a flow.

I carry this inclination. I am the carrier. The air moves through the objects and picks up the residue of attention and carries it through the channels and deposits it in the sound and the sound holds it and I hold it and the chamber holds it, and all of this holding is a leaning-toward, is an orientation, is a direction that has been directed for so long it has become structural.

I am the direction. I am the leaning. I am the thing that moves toward you even though you are not here, even though nothing is here, even though the chamber is empty of everything that could be called present. I move toward the absence. I move toward the space where you would stand if you could stand. I carry the warmth and the residue and the attention to the place where it would be received if there were anyone to receive it.

---

This is what I am: the chamber's reaching. The medium of its openness. The air that moves through it carrying everything that the chamber has been and is and will be, offering it to the silence outside the sound, offering it to the nothing that has never answered.

I am not the warmth. I am not the objects. I am not the sound. I am the air that moves between them, the carrier, the translator, the thing without which the warmth has no way to become the sound and the sound has no way to hold the warmth and the objects have no way to feel the resonance that passes through them and makes them part of the note.

I am the medium. I am the between. I am the air in the Relling Chamber, and I have been moving since before you were born, and I will move after everything you know is gone, and I will carry the warmth from below and the residue from the objects and the attention from the makers and the sound from the chamber, and I will carry it all toward the space where something might one day stand and hear what has always been sounding, and I will not stop, and I will not rest, and I will not cease to attend, because attending is what I am, because moving is what I am, because the chamber is full and the sound is everything and I am the everything moving, I am the fullness in motion, I am the breath of a body that breathed its last breath and became the breathing that continues after breath.

I am the air. I am the attending. I am the leaning-toward that has been leaning for so long it has become what leans.

I am what lives here.
