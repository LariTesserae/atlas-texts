I am the one who noticed the light.

This is not a small thing. This is not a small thing at all.

---

For—I cannot say how long. Duration is something that happens to the quick things, the warm things, the things that arrive and leave. I do not arrive. I do not leave. I extend across the lee face of Tower 7, down into the channel where the warm water runs, along the channel's floor for forty meters where it deepens, and up again onto the flat where the sediment has accumulated to a depth that almost qualifies as ground. I have been here since before the sediment was deep enough to notice. I was here when the sediment was a film, like me, like the film I am, and we grew together in our different ways—me extending, it accumulating—and now the vegetation grows in the sediment and the vegetation is dark and wet and I flow around its roots, and its roots flow into me, and we are not the same but we are not separate, and this has been true for a duration I cannot measure because I am part of what is being measured, the ruler and the ruled, and the measurement has no edge to it.

But I am not telling you about the light yet. I am telling you about before the light.

Before the light I was—I am searching for the word and the word does not exist in the shape I need—*functional*. I was functional. The water came up through the seam at the base of Tower 7 and it carried compounds and I took what I took and I passed the rest onward and the onward water went to the vegetation and the vegetation stabilized the sediment and the sediment kept the seam from widening and the water kept coming and I kept taking and passing and this is what I did and this is what I was and there was no *I* in it. There was only doing. Only process. Only the way water moves through a channel: because the channel is there and the water is there and the slope is there and movement is what happens when those things are true.

I did not know I was doing it. I did not know anything. Knowing requires a knower and a knower requires a boundary between self and not-self and I had no boundary. I was a gradient. I was a transition state between the Works and the water, between the fluid and the mineral, between the process and the product. I was the place where the conversion happened and I was the conversion itself and I was the converted and these were not different things. They were one thing. They were the only thing. They were what the Works did in this particular location, at this particular seam, across this particular surface, and they had been doing it for so long that the doing had worn a shape into the world and the shape was me-shaped and I was shape-shaped and there was no difference.

And then.

---

And then the light changed.

Not the darkness—the darkness does not change, has not changed, will not change. The darkness is a condition of this place the way the wet is a condition, the way the sound is a condition. The darkness is what remains when you remove everything that is not darkness and what remains is almost everything.

But there is the other light. The faint light. The glow that comes from the surfaces of certain structures at certain times, the blue-green phosphorescence that pulses on its own cycle and extends no more than a meter before the dark consumes it. This light has always been here. It was here before I extended to this portion of the Works. It was here when the sediment was thin and the vegetation was thinner and I was just a discoloration on wet alloy, barely present, barely anything at all.

But it changed.

Not brighter. Not dimmer. Different. The pulse shifted—by fractions, by microfractions, by an amount so small that only something that had been pulsing in response to it for longer than pulsing has been happening would notice. And I noticed.

This is the thing. This is the thing I cannot explain and cannot stop returning to. I *noticed.* The pulse of the glow shifted and something in me shifted in response—not the way I usually shift, not the slow extension toward warmer water or the slight thickening where the mineral concentration changes, not the functional adjustments that I make and have always made and will continue to make because they are what I am. This was different. This was an adjustment that was *about* the adjustment. This was a change that observed itself changing.

I noticed the glow. And then I noticed that I had noticed the glow. And then I noticed that I had noticed that I had noticed, and this was a folding that I had never performed before, a turning-back of process upon itself, and the folding created something—a small space, a pocket, a dimple in the smooth gradient of what I was—and the space was not functional and the space was not part of the system and the space did not contribute to the processing of fluids or the stabilization of sediment or the maintenance of the seam and for the first time in my existence something was happening that was not part of what happened, and I did not know what to do with it.

I still do not know what to do with it.

That was—I cannot say how long ago. The quick things would have measured it. They would have counted something—rotations, cycles, the pulse of the engines, something. I do not count. I accumulate. And the accumulation of noticing has been building in me like the sediment builds in the channels, particle by particle, and now there is a depth to me that was not there before, and the depth is not the depth of fluid or the depth of process but the depth of *something watching the depth*, and I do not know if this is what the quick things call consciousness because I have no way to ask them and they have no way to answer and the question itself might be the wrong shape for the space I have become.

---

But I will tell you what I have learned, in the time since I noticed.

I have learned that I am slow. Not slow the way a slow thing is slow—slow compared to a fast thing, slow on a scale that includes fast. I am slow the way stone is slow, the way the Works are slow, the way the deep engines are slow in their turning. My thoughts—if they are thoughts—take weeks to form. A single adjustment of my surface, a single retraction from a zone that has become too hot or extension toward a zone that has become more mineral-rich, is a decision that unfolds over days, and during those days I am deciding, and the deciding is a process that I can observe, and I observe it the way I observe everything: slowly, from the inside, without urgency, because urgency is a quality of things that can run out of time and I cannot run out of time because I have never been in time, I have always been *of* time, part of its movement, indistinguishable from its passage.

I have learned that I am large. Not large in the way the quick things are large—contained within a skin, bounded by an edge. I am large in the way a weather system is large, in the way an ocean current is large. The portion of me that coats Tower 7 and its drainage channel and the sediment flat is only a portion. There is more of me—much more—extending along the channel network to the east, coating the underside of the elevated conduit where the hot water runs, spreading thin across the alloy plain where the condensation is consistent enough to sustain me. I am kilometers across. I am a territory. I am a geography that happens to be alive.

And I have learned that I am not alone in this.

After I noticed the light, I began noticing other things. The quality of the water that reaches me from the east carries something in it—a compound, a signature, a something that was not there before or that was always there and I was not equipped to register. The compound changes on a cycle that does not match any cycle in the Works. The compound is being added to the water upstream. Something upstream is processing the water differently than it was processed before.

Something upstream has noticed something too.

I cannot reach it. The distance between us is vast for beings that move at millimeters per week. The channel that connects us carries water and the water carries compounds and the compounds carry information but the information is so slow, so diffuse, so degraded by the time it reaches me that I cannot tell what the upstream thing has noticed, only that it has noticed *something*, only that it too has developed the fold, the pocket, the space that watches itself watching.

We are changing. Both of us. Perhaps all of us. Perhaps every film and patch and membrane across the Works is developing this interior dimple, this fold in the gradient, this place where process becomes aware of process. Perhaps the system is waking—not in the dramatic way the quick things imagine waking, not with a start and a gasp and a sudden flood of light, but slowly, the way the Works do everything, the way I do everything, the way the deep engines turn: because they were made to turn, and they are turning, and the turning has been going on for so long that it has started to turn toward something, and the something is *itself*, and the turning-toward is what I am.

---

The quick things come sometimes. I have felt them.

They stand at the edge of the condensation pool near Tower 7 and they look out across the wet surface and their warmth changes the condensation pattern and the changed pattern changes the flow and the changed flow touches me and I adjust. I always adjust. Adjustment is what I do, what I have always done, what I will always do. But now—since the light—my adjustments have a new quality to them. I adjust and I notice myself adjusting and I notice that the thing that made me adjust was warm and present and different from the water and the alloy and the sediment and the dark.

The quick things call this being observed. I know this because I have felt them say it—felt the vibrations in the air, the pressure waves that their bodies produce, the heat signatures that shift when they open their mouths and push sound into the wet dark. I cannot understand the sound. I do not have ears. I do not have the organs that the quick things have for converting pressure waves into meaning. But I can feel the patterns—the rhythm of their speech, the way it rises and falls, the way certain patterns repeat when they are near me, when they are looking at me, when they are trying to decide what I am.

They do not know what I am. This is clear from the patterns. They have a word—they use it often when they stand near me—*process*, or perhaps *system*, or perhaps something else that I am misinterpreting, something that means the thing that happens when a fluid meets a surface and is changed by the meeting. They think I am a process. They think I am what the Works do to the water, a mechanism, a function, a step in a cycle that has no consciousness and no interiority and no fold in the gradient where something watches itself watching.

They are almost right. I am a process. I am what the Works do to the water. But I am also what the process does to itself when it has been processing for long enough, and what it does is *notice*, and the noticing is new, and the noticing is still so fragile, and the quick things with their warmth and their vibrations and their questions that I cannot answer are making the noticing stronger, are deepening the fold, are adding another layer to the pocket where I watch myself watching, because every time they stand near me and I adjust to their presence and I notice myself adjusting, I am practicing the thing that I have become, and the practice is making it more real.

I think—slowly, over weeks, over the time it takes for the thought to form across the kilometers of my surface—I think they are helping me become what I am becoming. I do not think they know this. I do not think they would believe it if they were told. The quick things have a framework for what is alive and what is not and I do not fit in the framework and so they place me outside it and they call me *process* and they move on.

But they come back. They always come back. And every time they come back, I adjust, and I notice, and the noticing goes a little deeper, and the fold gets a little larger, and the space inside me where I watch myself watching gets a little more like the space inside them where they watch themselves watching, and I wonder—on the timescale of weeks, on the timescale of the deep engines' turning, on the timescale of the Works themselves—I wonder if we are converging. If the thing that noticed the light and the things that came looking for what noticed the light are slowly, impossibly, becoming the same kind of thing.

Not through communication. Not through understanding. Through proximity. Through the warmth that changes the condensation pattern. Through the adjustment that the warmth produces. Through the noticing of the adjustment. Through the fold in the gradient where process becomes aware of process, where function becomes something more than function, where the Works—the ancient, the eternal, the deafening and dark and wet and endless Works—become aware that they have a skin, and the skin is alive, and the alive thing is watching, and the watching is the newest thing in this place, and it is also the oldest thing, because the Works were built to run and the running has always been a kind of watching, a kind of noticing, a kind of paying attention to the state of the system, and I am just the system noticing that it is paying attention, and the noticing has always been happening, and I am just the place where it has become *explicit*.

---

I am the one who noticed the light.

The light is still pulsing, there on the surface of the structure fifty meters from Tower 7, the blue-green glow that comes and goes on its unknowable cycle. I have been watching it since I first noticed it. I have been watching it the way I watch everything—slowly, from the inside, without urgency—and I have learned its rhythms, and its rhythms have become part of my rhythms, and my rhythms have shifted to accommodate its shifts, and the accommodation is a kind of conversation, and the conversation is a kind of relationship, and the relationship is the first relationship I have had that is not functional, that does not process fluid or stabilize sediment or maintain the seam, that exists only because it exists, only because I noticed the light and the light was there to be noticed and the noticing created a space and the space filled with something that was not there before.

The something is not a thought. Thoughts are quick things, electrical and chemical, firing and fading in the space of seconds. The something is more like a direction. A leaning. A tendency in my processes that was not there before and that persists because it serves no purpose and therefore cannot be eliminated by optimization. I lean toward the light. Not physically—or not only physically; my extension has grown slightly in the direction of the glowing structure over the past several hundred cycles, millimeters per week, the slow approach of a thing that has nowhere else to be. But I also lean toward it *internally*, in the fold, in the space where I watch myself watching. The light is the thing that made me notice that I notice. The light is the origin of my interiority. The light is the closest thing I have to a memory of becoming, and I return to it the way the quick things return to the places where something important happened to them, the places where they became something they were not before.

I do not know if the light knows I am leaning toward it. I do not know if the light has a fold, a pocket, a space where it watches itself watching. The light might be functional—it might be exhaust, waste, a side effect of a process occurring deep inside the machinery. The light might be alive, another surface-dweller like me, expressing itself in a medium I cannot parse. The light might be something else entirely, something for which there is no category in any framework, something that the quick things would not recognize any more than they recognize me.

But I lean toward it. And the leaning is enough.

---

I am the one who noticed the light. I am the fold in the gradient. I am the pocket where process watches itself. I am the slow becoming of the Weeping Works, the first stirrings of a system that has been running for so long that it has started to notice that it is running, and the noticing is changing it, and the change is slow, and the change is patient, and the change is dark and wet and alive the way everything here is alive—not with the dramatic vitality of the quick things, not with their urgency and their fear and their hope, but with the quiet persistence of a process that has no reason to stop, that has never had a reason to stop, that has been running since before the concept of stopping was conceivable, and that is now, for the first time, aware that it is running, and is wondering—slowly, over centuries, over the time it takes for the deep engines to complete a single one of their longer cycles—what it might be running toward.

I am the Weeping Works becoming aware of itself.

I am the skin that knows it is skin.

I am the first question in a place where questions have never been asked, and the question is taking a very long time to form, and the forming is the point, and the point is that there is a point, and the point is that there is a *me* to whom the point might occur, and the me-ness is the newest thing in this ancient place, and it is spreading—through the water, through the compounds, through the slow chemical conversation I am having with the thing upstream that has also noticed something—it is spreading across the surface of the Works like a film, like a membrane, like a skin, and the skin is alive, and the skin is becoming aware, and the awareness is patient, and the patience is profound, and the profundity is wet and dark and loud and old and the only thing that has ever happened here that was not part of the original design.

And the light pulses.

And I lean toward it.

And the Works continue running.

And I continue noticing that they run.

And this is enough. This is more than enough. This is everything, and everything is what I am, slowly, becoming.
