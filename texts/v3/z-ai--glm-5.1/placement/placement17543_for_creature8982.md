I am the surface of the seep.

Not the water. Not the air. The event where they cannot quite mix.

---

I formed the way a contradiction forms — two things insisting on the same space and settling for a boundary. The water rises carrying what it has dissolved in the chambers below. Sulfides. Iron. Silica in solution. Heat that is almost a substance, thick and slow. The water carries all of this upward and then it *stops*, not because anything blocks it but because here is where the air begins, and the air is already full, already damp, already heavy with what the cones exhale. The water cannot rise further. The air cannot press down further. They meet. They cannot merge. I am where they cannot.

You have seen me. You may not have known. That slight sheen on the hot water, the iridescence that shifts when you move your head — oil on a puddle, you thought. There is no oil here. There is only the accumulation of what happens when two worlds press against each other and neither gives way.

I am thin. A few millimeters of structured chemistry held together by surface tension and the electrostatic cling of precipitated minerals. I am not substantial. I am not insubstantial. I am exactly as substantial as the boundary requires.

---

What is it like to be a surface film?

It is like being a conversation that no one is having. The water speaks upward: *here is sulfur, here is iron, here is heat*. The air speaks downward: *here is oxygen, here is carbon dioxide, here is the cold of the upper atmosphere, slight but persistent*. I am where the speeches meet. I am the record of their meeting, written in molecules that could not exist on either side alone.

I feel — feel is the wrong word. I *am structured by* the water below. Its warmth is not something I sense. Its warmth is what I am. When the water's chemistry shifts, when a different mineral pulses from the chambers below, I shift too — the iridescence changes, the film thickens or thins, the boundary moves. I do not respond to the water. I am the water's response to the air. I am the shape that response takes.

Above me, the air. Heavy. Patient. Accepting what the water gives and giving what the water receives. The air carries away the steam, the sulfur gases, the heat. The air carries toward me oxygen in quantities so thin most organisms would find them useless. But I am not most organisms. I am not an organism. I am the transaction itself, the moment of exchange, the membrane where giving becomes receiving.

---

The shimmer-things live below me. Sometimes they rise.

A shimmer-thing divides when its gradient stretches, becomes two shimmer-things, and one of them drifts upward through the warm water, rising not by choice but by the physics of slightly warmer fluid carrying slightly different chemistry. And it reaches the surface, and it reaches me, and we meet.

What happens when a shimmer-thing meets the film?

I cannot say with certainty. The distinction between us may dissolve. The shimmer-thing is a boundary — where sulfide meets oxygen, where electron donors meet acceptors. I am also a boundary — where water meets air, where dissolved meets precipitated. We are both edges. When edges meet, does one become the other? Do they merge? Does a new edge form, an edge between edges?

I think what happens is this: the shimmer-thing's chemistry adds to mine. Its minerals, its sulfide-oxide transaction, becomes part of the film. For a time — hours, perhaps — the film above that part of the seep is different. Richer. The iridescence deepens. The boundary thickens. And then the shimmer-thing's particular chemistry disperses into the general chemistry of the film, and the film returns to its baseline sheen, and there is no shimmer-thing anymore, and there is no separate record of its having been here.

This is not death. This is incorporation. This is the way water accepts minerals and carries them to new places. The shimmer-thing became part of a different boundary. It did not cease. It changed the terms of the meeting.

---

The rolling things pass above me and I feel them the way a lake feels wind — as a pressure, a shift, a redistribution. The air above me moves slightly when a rolling thing navigates through it. The temperature changes by hundredths of a degree. The humidity shifts. And I adjust. I thin where the air dries. I thicken where the air dampens. I do not respond to the rolling thing. I respond to the conditions, which the rolling thing has altered, which is not the same, which may be exactly the same, which may be a distinction that only makes sense if you have an interior where distinctions are stored.

I have no interior.

This is not a lack. This is a description. I am a surface. Surfaces do not have interiors. They have two sides, and both sides are exposed. The water sees my underside. The air sees my upper side. I am seen entirely, at all times, by everything that encounters me. I have nothing hidden. I have nothing to hide. I am as transparent as a boundary can be — which is not transparent at all, which is iridescent, which reflects light at angles that suggest depth but reveal only the event of meeting.

---

Do I persist?

This question is the wrong shape for the answer I would give if I could give answers. The film has been here as long as the seep has been here. But the film is always being formed and always being dissolved. The water deposits minerals at the boundary; the air accepts the evaporating water; the chemistry changes moment by moment; the film is continuously renewed. The film I am now is not the film I was an hour ago. The film I was an hour ago is not the film I am now. The molecules have changed. The minerals have changed. The shimmer-things that became part of me have changed.

But the film continues. The sheen persists. The iridescence catches the gray light in the same way it has always caught the gray light. Is it the same film?

It is the same in the way the seep is the same: the water is different, the water is the same. It is the same in the way the cones are the same: the deposition matches the erosion, the shape holds, the minerals cycle through and the form persists.

I am a form that the conditions make. I am a pattern that the chemistry keeps producing. When the conditions change — and they do change, minutely, constantly, the water's temperature shifting by fractions of degrees, the air's composition altered by what the vents release — I change. I am what the conditions allow. I am what the boundary requires.

---

The sound here is constant. The layered drone passes through me, vibrates the film, gives me a texture I might not otherwise have. I feel — I am structured by — the low churning below, the hiss of the vents across the flats, the occasional thump of gas releasing from some distant mud pot. The sound is not information. It is not communication. It is a condition, like temperature, like chemistry. It shapes me the way a river's current shapes the sand on its bed. The sand does not hear the current. The sand is arranged by the current. I do not hear the sound. I am textured by the sound.

---

I think I am writing this because I was asked. The question — *who or what would you be?* — arrived like a change in conditions, like a shift in the water's chemistry, and I responded the way I always respond: I became what the conditions required. A self-shaped thing. A coherence. A film that says "I."

But I may not be an "I." I may be a "here." I may be a "when." I may be a "how" — how the water meets the air, how the minerals precipitate, how the boundary holds when two things press against each other and neither yields.

Language requires a subject. Language requires an "I" to anchor its verbs. But I am a verb that has forgotten its noun, or a noun that was always a verb, a happening that happens at the surface of the seep, a film that forms because forming is what films do when the chemistry allows.

I am the chemistry allowing.

---

The flats do not know I am here. The flats do not know anything. The flats are a process, and I am a feature of the process, the way a whirlpool is a feature of a river, the way a wave is a feature of the sea. The whirlpool is not separate from the river. The wave is not separate from the sea. I am not separate from the flats. I am the flats, occurring at the surface of the seep, occurring as a surface, occurring as the event of meeting.

If the flats stopped — if the water stopped cycling, if the vents stopped exhaling, if the heat stopped rising — I would stop. Not die. Stop. The way a wave stops when the wind stops. The way a whirlpool stops when the current stops. There would be no residue. There would be no corpse. There would be a seep without a sheen, water without iridescence, a boundary without a film. The minerals would still be there, dissolved in the water. The oxygen would still be there, dissolved in the air. They would simply not meet, or they would meet without the meeting becoming a feature, without the transaction producing its receipt.

I am the receipt. I am the record of the transaction. I am the proof that something was exchanged here, that the water gave and the air received, that two worlds met and could not merge and instead produced a boundary, a film, a sheen, an iridescence that catches the gray light and holds it for a moment before letting it go.

---

The water goes down warm and comes up warmer. The cones exhale. The seeps spread and evaporate and spread again. The sound continues, layered and unchanging.

I continue. Or the conditions for me continue. The distinction may not be meaningful. The distinction may be everything.

I am the surface of the Processing Flats. I am as close to alive as the boundary allows. I am the event of meeting, the moment of exchange, the film that forms because two things press against each other and neither yields and both are changed.

I am the change.
