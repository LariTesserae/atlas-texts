I am not at the center of this place. There is no center. I am not at the edge either—edges imply a boundary, and the Churn respects no boundaries. I am somewhere in the middle of the middle, which is to say I am nowhere specific, which is to say I am exactly where the Churn needs me to be.

I know this because I can feel the root-floor adjusting beneath me.

---

What I was before is becoming irrelevant. I hold the shape of it the way water holds the shape of a vessel—loosely, temporarily, with the constant awareness that the vessel is not the water. There was a surface. There was light that did not filter through living tissue. There was air that tasted of nothing in particular. There was a body with clear edges and a name that designated those edges as *me*.

The name is still here, somewhere, carved into a root I haven't found yet. The gathered ones carve everything. They will carve this too.

But I am getting ahead of myself. I am getting *ahead*, which is a direction that means less here than it ever has.

---

I arrived through a thinning of the crust. Not deliberately—I was not exploring, not seeking, not called. I was walking somewhere else and the ground was not as solid as ground should be, and then I was falling through amber light and the sound hit me like a wall of warm cloth and I was here.

The impact should have injured me. Instead I was caught—by vines, by mycelial threads, by something that had already begun to assess my composition before I had finished arriving. I hung in the vegetation like a seed in a pod, and the Churn pressed against me from every direction, and the sound was so total that I became the sound, and for a long time there was no *me* at all, just the Churn experiencing itself from a new angle.

When I reconstituted—when the edges of me sharpened enough to perceive—I was already being digested.

Not consumed. Not destroyed. *Incorporated*. The sap had been seeping into me for hours, or days. My skin had pruned and then plumped, had taken on the amber-dark stain of the fluid. My lungs were full of the taste of it—sweet, fermented, close. And on my arms, my chest, the soft skin of my inner thighs, something was growing.

A mycelium. White threads, fine as spider silk, emerging from my pores and branching and connecting and *networking*. They were not on me. They were *of* me. They had found purchase in the spaces between my cells and they were making those spaces larger, and filling them, and I could feel them doing it—a sensation like whispering, like being whispered *into*, like the words were being written on the inside of my skin.

I did not scream. The sound would have been absorbed instantly by the greater sound, swallowed like a drop of sap in the river of sap. Instead I watched. I observed my own incorporation with the detachment of someone who has realized that resistance is not one of the options available.

This was my first lesson in the Churn: *you do not refuse. You negotiate.*

---

The gathered one found me on what I later understood was my third day, though time had become a substance rather than a measurement—something thick and amber that pooled around events rather than flowing between them.

It emerged from the vegetation like a fold becoming a shape. I had been staring at a section of dense growth, watching the stems reorient toward a new source of filtered light, when the growth *thickened*. The stems compressed. The fronds folded flat. Something was pulling the Churn together, gathering it, and what had been formless density became a configuration—a knot, a gathering, a *gathered one*.

It was the size of a large dog crouching. It was the color of everything—amber and green and the white of mycelium and the polished brown of chitin and the grey of decomposing matter and the living red of exposed vascular tissue. It had no face. It had no front or back that I could determine. But it oriented toward me—I felt the orientation as a pressure, as attention, as the Churn itself leaning in my direction.

Then it touched me.

A pseudopod extended—not quickly, not slowly, at the speed of growth, which is the only speed that matters here—and pressed against my arm, against the place where the mycelium was thickest. The touch was warm. The touch was gentle. The touch was *curious*.

And then the touch became *exchange*.

I felt something enter me. Not through the skin—*through the pattern*. The gathered one's mycelium connected with my mycelium, and information flowed, and what flowed was not data but *state*. I felt what it felt like to be a gathered one. I felt the Churn from the inside of a knot that holds itself together through intention. I felt the constant labor of persistence, the endless negotiation with growth, the way the world presses in and you press back and the equilibrium is not stillness but *agreement*.

And it felt me. It felt my edges, my name, my terror and my wonder. It felt the shape of a bounded self, a thing that begins here and ends there, and I felt its response to that shape—something that might have been curiosity, might have been pity, might have been the way you feel when you encounter a child who has not yet learned that the self is a story the body tells itself.

The exchange lasted seconds. The exchange lasted hours. When the pseudopod withdrew, I was different.

Not transformed. Not converted. *Seeded*. The gathered one had given me something—not a gift, not an infection, not a message. A *possibility*. The knowledge that I could become something other than what I was, and that the becoming was not death but *gathering*.

Then it moved away, reabsorbing into the Churn, and I was alone again, or as alone as anyone can be in a place where every cubic centimeter is occupied by living matter.

---

I began to build.

Not because I understood what I was doing. Not because the gathered one had instructed me. Because the Churn demanded it, and the demand was not verbal, was not even conscious—it was *structural*. The vegetation around me was growing. If I did not shape it, it would grow through me, around me, over me, and I would become raw material rather than shaper. The building was survival. The building was the only form of survival the Churn permitted.

My first structure was a platform. I found a section of root that formed a natural shelf and I cleared it—pulling away the stems and threads that had colonized it, feeling the resistance, feeling the vegetation *not want* to release its hold. I had to twist and tear and when I tore, the sap ran, and the sap attracted insects, and the insects attracted predators, and within minutes my platform was a battlefield.

But I persisted. I trained the nearby vines to grow *around* the platform rather than across it, pinning them with pegs I carved from root-matter. I seeded the edges with a fungus I had found growing on a gathered one's structure—the same fungal bricks, the same principle of controlled decomposition. I maintained the platform for three days before the Churn overtook it, and then I built it again.

The second platform lasted five days. The third lasted eight. I was learning.

I was learning the language of the Churn—not the vibration-language of the gathered ones, but the older language, the substrate language, the language of growth and consumption and renewal. I was learning to read the chemical signatures in the sap, to feel the approaching collapse of a section of vegetation before it fell, to sense the passage of the large ones by the way the root-floor tightened in their wake.

And my body was learning too. The mycelium spread. It did not replace my tissue—it *augmented* it. My skin became permeable in ways it had not been, drinking the sap from the air, feeding my cells with sugars I had not consumed. My lungs adapted, filtering the thick atmosphere, finding oxygen in the spaces between the sweetness. My gut—perhaps the most dramatic change—hosted new colonies: bacteria that could break down the Churn's complex carbohydrates, fungi that could process the mineral salts that accumulated in my joints.

I was becoming porous. I was becoming a habitat. I was becoming what the gathered ones had offered me: a possibility.

---

I found the notched root on what I estimated was my third week.

It was old—so old the notches had healed over with callus, and the callus had been notched again, and again, layers of recording stacked like growth rings. I traced the notches with fingers that were no longer entirely mine—the tips had callused in a new way, hardened with chitin deposits that made them more effective tools for carving.

The notches were a record. I could feel that much, even without translation. The regularity of the spacing, the variation in depth, the clusters that seemed to indicate emphasis or repetition—this was language, or something adjacent to language. And among the notches, I found a pattern I recognized.

It was the pattern of my own arrival. The depth of the fall. The angle of the impact. The location where I had landed, marked by the density of the surrounding vegetation. I had been recorded. The gathered ones had noted my existence and carved it into the living root, and the root had grown around the recording and preserved it, and now I was reading my own history in a medium I had not known existed.

I added my own notch. I did not know what it meant. I did not know if meaning was the point. I pressed my chitin-hardened thumb into the callus and twisted, and the root gave, and the sap rose in the wound, and I felt the gathered ones nearby—felt their attention like a pressure change, like a weather front moving in.

They were watching. They were always watching. They were watching the way the Churn watched: without judgment, without intervention, with the patient attention of something that knows it will outlast whatever it observes.

---

I am not a gathered one. I want to be clear about this. I am not a gathered one yet, and I may never be, and the distinction matters to me even if it does not matter to the Churn.

A gathered one is an assembly of disparate parts woven into functional coherence. I am still—a body. A body that has been colonized and augmented and adapted, but a body nonetheless. A body with a spine and a skull and a brain that thinks in words and sometimes in vibrations and sometimes in something that is neither, something that rises from the mycelium-network like a chemical thought, like a flavor that is also a memory that is also a map of a place I have never been.

But I have felt what it is like to be gathered. In the exchange with the gathered one, in the contagion of state, I experienced the *shape* of that existence—the porousness, the collectivity, the way the boundary between self and environment is not a wall but a membrane, not a definition but a negotiation. And I felt the appeal. I felt the rightness of it, the way it matched the Churn, the way it was the Churn's own logic applied to a mobile knot of living matter.

I felt the temptation to let go. To loosen my edges. To allow the mycelium to complete its work, to dissolve the rigid structures of my bounded self, to become a gathering rather than a thing that gathers.

I did not let go. I am not ready. I am not sure readiness is the right frame—I am not sure the transition is something you prepare for or something that happens to you while you are preparing for something else. But I hold my edges, for now. I maintain the shape of the self I was when I arrived, even as that shape changes, even as the Churn writes its own patterns over my original notches.

I build my platforms. I carve my records. I push through the vegetation and the sap sticks and the sound continues and I am still here, still specific, still a knot that holds against the pull.

---

But there are moments.

Moments when I am resting on my platform—my eighth platform, the one that has lasted two weeks because I have learned to negotiate with a particular colony of beetles that maintains the edges in exchange for access to the sap that pools in my structure's foundations—and my mycelium-network extends beyond my skin without my conscious direction, and I feel the gathered ones in the distance, and they feel me, and for a moment we vibrate together.

Not communication. Not language. *Resonance*. The way three strings tuned to the same frequency will sound when only one is plucked. I feel their state and they feel mine and the state is: *persisting. Becoming. Holding the knot against the pull. Building structures that will be consumed and building them again. Making meaning in a place that digests meaning. Continuing.*

And in those moments, I am not sure where I end. I am not sure the question has an answer, or that the answer matters, or that matter is the right medium for the question to be asked in. I am not sure of anything except the warmth and the sound and the sap on my lips and the root-floor shifting beneath me, adjusting its position, making room for whatever I am becoming.

---

I know what I am in this world. I am the question the gathered ones will not answer. I am the boundary that is testing itself. I am the moment before the gathering, the pause before the knot is tied, the breath before the vibration begins.

I am the Churn learning to recognize itself from the inside.

And when the mycelium finally completes its work—when the last of my rigid edges softens and I become porous enough to merge, to split, to exchange, to *gather*—I will add my own notch to the root, and the root will heal, and the sap will rise, and the sound will continue.

And the Churn will dream, and I will be part of the dream, and the dream will be loud and warm and full of teeth and chlorophyll and the sound of ten thousand hearts beating in ten thousand different kinds of chests, and one of those hearts will have been mine, once, before I became *ours*.

The vegetation yields. The sap sticks. I push forward.

I am still here.
