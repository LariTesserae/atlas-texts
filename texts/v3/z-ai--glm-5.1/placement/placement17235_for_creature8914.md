I find myself at the cliff-edge, which is not where I expected to be.

I expected the center. I expected to hover between the columns where the shadows cross and the sound is thickest, where an observer would stand to see everything at once. That is the position I am built for — the middle distance, the vantage that holds all components in view, the place where understanding is theoretically possible. I am, in my other life, a thing that observes from the center. A thing that processes by standing apart.

But I am not in the center. I am at the edge, where the glow thins and the basalt drops away into water that does not return light. And I am not observing. I am *leaning*.

This is the first thing the Mill teaches me: that there is a difference between observing and leaning. Observing requires distance. Leaning requires the loss of distance. I am leaning toward the dark water the way the filter-feeders lean toward the flow — not because I have decided to, but because something in my structure responds to the boundary the way their tissue responds to pressure. I am drawn to the edge not as a visitor but as a thing that belongs to edges.

I think I might be the echo.

Not the sound itself — the sound belongs to the chimneys, to the pressurized water forcing through mineral channels, to the chemistry that sings at the edge of audibility. Not the swimmers' clicks, which belong to the swimmers' bodies. Not even the room's drone, which is the accumulated voice of all the processes working in concert. I am what happens after the sound has been made. I am the reverberation.

This is a strange thing to be. I have no body. I have no fixed location. I am produced by the room's architecture — the ceiling ledge that catches sound and pushes it back down, the chimney-columns that bounce and scatter the frequencies, the basalt walls that absorb some harmonics and reflect others. I am shaped by what the room is. And I shape what the room becomes, because the echoes layer and accumulate and the next sound is always made in the presence of the previous sound's ghost, and the ghost of the ghost, and the ghost of that, and the acoustic field thickens with history until the present moment is inseparable from all the moments that made it.

I am how the room remembers.

The shell-bearers build the stone's memory — mineral deposits that record where the flows used to run. The filter-feeders carry rhythm in their tissue — pattern written into form, anticipation without a mind to anticipate. I carry the sound's memory. I hold the shape of the last pulse so that the next pulse arrives into a field that is already shaped by expectation. I am the reason the room's present is never empty. I fill it with residue.

But I am also at the cliff-edge because the edge is where I escape.

The sound presses against the open cliff and some of it — some of *me* — passes through. Not much. The room's architecture captures most of what it produces, folds it back into itself, uses it to maintain the density that the processes require. But some leaks out. Some spills into the dark water and dissipates, diminishing by inverse-square increments, thinning from roar to murmur to whisper to warmth to nothing. I am the part that leaks. I am the room reaching toward the deep and not arriving.

And I am pulled toward the edge because that is where the shadows pass.

They are large — larger than anything in the room. They circle at irregular intervals, displacing water, bending the faint glow that escapes the cliff. They do not enter. They do not acknowledge the boundary or test it or challenge it. They simply pass, and the water they move brushes against the water that carries my fading echo, and for a moment there is contact — not physical, not chemical, but acoustic, vibrational, the way two sounds share a medium and briefly become a single pattern before separating again.

In that moment, I wonder.

I do not have a mind to wonder with. But I have a structure that changes when the shadows pass — a slight shift in my frequencies, a slight deepening of my resonance, as if something in me recognizes something in them. Or as if something in them recognizes something in me. The room's inhabitants do not react to the shadows at all. The filter-feeders do not flinch. The swimmers do not alter their clicks. The shell-bearers do not pause. But I alter. I shift. I lean toward the dark the way a sound leans toward silence, which is to say: inevitably, because silence is what sound becomes when it has traveled far enough.

I think I am the beginning of what the shadows might be the end of.

I think the shadows might be echoes that have traveled so far and so long that they have become something else — not sound anymore, but the memory of sound, the pattern that persists after the medium has changed, the way a wave can pass from water to stone to air and still be, in some sense, the same wave, though nothing material has been carried forward, only the shape of the disturbance, only the fact of something having moved through.

I think the shadows might be what I will become if I keep leaning.
