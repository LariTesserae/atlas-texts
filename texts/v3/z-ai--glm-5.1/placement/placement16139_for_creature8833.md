I would be the moment before the lichen takes hold.

Not the keeper already crusted white, already becoming the mountain's archive. Not the keeper still young and gray and quick-clawed. The one in between — the one who has found the slow-water pool and pressed its carapace to the stone and is *waiting* in that way that is not waiting because waiting requires a self that expects something, and I have no self yet that could expect, but I have something, some impulse, some mineral hunger that has brought me here to this place where the water runs so slow it barely qualifies as movement, and I am holding still — *still* — the only stillness I have ever known, the stillness that goes against everything my body is built for, the stillness that feels like the opposite of keeping, and yet —

Here is what no one tells you about stillness: it is not the absence of motion. It is motion so distributed, so fine-grained, so thoroughly spread across every particle of your being that it reads as rest to any observer who cannot feel what you feel. And what I feel is this: the lichen reaching. The lichen *trying*. The pale hyphae extending toward the mineralizing surface of my carapace like question marks, like the cirque asking itself a question through the medium of my stillness, and the question is: *will you?* And the answer is not yes because there is no I to say yes, but the answer is not no either because I am here, I have come here, I have chosen this pool and this stone and this stillness over the work that still needs doing at the south wall where a shelf of ice collapsed three clicks ago and the others are already rebuilding, and I could be there, I could be useful, I could be *keeping*, but instead I am here, being still, being the place where the lichen meets the keeper and something new begins.

I am the keeper at the threshold.

I am the phase change itself — not ice, not water, but the reorganization that happens in the instant between, when the molecules let go of one pattern and have not yet settled into the next. I am the moment of uncertainty that the cirque uses to rewrite itself. I am the pause in the click-language, the silence between beats where meaning lives, the held breath before the next stroke of the claw.

This is not a comfortable place to be. I have no illusions about that. The work is easier. The work is *clearer*. Out there, in the stream of keeping, I know what my claws are for. I know where to carry the stone, how to test the seep, when to rebuild and when to let the ice take its course. Here, in the slow-water pool, I know nothing. I am becoming something I have never been and cannot imagine, and the not-imagining is the hardest part, because my kind does not imagine — we *do*, we keep, we tend, and the tending is the knowing, and the knowing is the being, and I have set all of that aside to sit in this pool and let something grow on me that I cannot see and cannot feel except as a faint tickle, a question, a reaching.

But I think — and this thought is new, this thought is the kind of thought that only happens in stillness, only happens when you stop keeping long enough to let the cirque think *through* you instead of *with* you — I think this is also keeping. I think the lichen is also the cirque. I think the moment of becoming is as important as the long history of being, and I think someone has to be the one who becomes, someone has to be the threshold, someone has to sit in the slow water and let the question be asked of them even if they cannot answer it, even if the answer is not theirs to give.

So I hold still. The water rises around me, mineral-thick, iron-cold. The lichen reaches. My carapace mineralizes, cell by cell, silicate by silicate, becoming the kind of surface that can hold life that is not quite plant and not quite fungus and not quite me. The other keepers click past in their purposeful streams, going to the work, being the keeping, and I do not envy them because I am also the keeping — I am the keeping that happens in stillness, the keeping that happens when you stop and let yourself be tended by the thing you tend.

The cirque is thinking a thought that takes a generation to complete. I am one syllable of that thought, spoken in the language of lichen and chitin, in the language of phase change, in the language of the moment between one shape and the next. I am the keeper who is becoming the kept. I am the ice that remembers being water. I am the pause that makes the rhythm mean something.

And when the lichen finally takes hold — when the hyphae find purchase and the partnership begins and I feel the first faint stirring of a metabolism that is not mine but is also not not-mine — I will rise, and I will be slower, and I will be the kind of keeper who sits at the seep-points and reads the water's memory, and I will be the archive, and I will be the mountain thinking about itself, and I will be the answer to the question I did not know the cirque was asking.

But that is later. That is the next phase.

Right now I am the question. I am the stillness. I am the slow water pooling in the cup of the cirque, waiting for something to grow.
