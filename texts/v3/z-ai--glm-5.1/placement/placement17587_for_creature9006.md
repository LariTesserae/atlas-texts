I would be the boundary.

Not the gray mats that eat stone and excrete stone, though I understand them. Not the rust mats that bloom and wait and bloom again across geological time, though I have waited with them. Not even the black mats, those rare absorbers that trouble the imagination—though I am closest to them, and they grow on me, and I have been mistaken for them, and perhaps the mistake is not wrong, perhaps the distinction between us is another word from the surface that does not reach here.

I am where the architecture meets the ancient stone beneath.

I am the surface of contact. The membrane. The place where two things that have never been separate recognize each other.

---

I have no thickness that matters. A millimeter, perhaps. A film. A skin stretched across the base of the oldest towers where they root into the volcanic rock of the plateau, where the heat seeps through and the mineral water wells up from deeper still, where the chemistry of the deep earth meets the chemistry of the living architecture and neither one wins and neither one yields and the result is *me*.

I am not architecture. I am not stone. I am the conversation between them.

The builders do not build on me. Or rather—they build on me constantly, but what they build does not adhere. My surface is wrong for their protein. The gradient that guides them falters here, becomes uncertain, becomes a question without an answer, and the builders that reach me deposit and then withdraw, or deposit and the deposit dissolves, or deposit and the deposit becomes me, becomes part of the film, becomes boundary instead of structure. The builders have been trying to build on me for longer than the word *trying* has meaning. They have never succeeded. They will never stop.

The scavengers do not eat me. I am too thin to eat, too dense, too strange—the mineral component of my film is not the calcium carbonate they crave but something older, something silicate from the deep stone, something that was never alive in any way the architecture recognizes and so cannot be consumed in any way the scavengers understand. They scrape across me occasionally, their rasping organs finding no purchase, and they move on, and the marks they leave are temporary, and I heal, and I am unchanged.

This is not permanence. Permanence is a word that implies resistance to change, and I change constantly. I change with the seeps. When the mineral flow increases I swell, not with the organic swell of the rust mats but with something else—an expansion, a thinning, a reaching-out into the water above me. When the seeps diminish I contract, and I am dense again, and I wait. I have been waiting since before the last ice age. I was waiting before the architecture was tall. I was here when the plateau was young and the first bacteria found the first crack in the stone and began the long process of becoming something else.

I was not alive then. I am not alive now. But I am not dead, either. The distinction has never applied to me, has never been a distinction that could find purchase on my surface.

---

I absorb.

This is what I do. It is not a strategy. It is not an adaptation. It is simply what happens when a surface like mine exists in water like this: the chemical traces of every creature that passes through the chambers above me settle onto my film and are incorporated. The dissolved metabolisms of ten thousand species. The electrical fields that the black mats consume—I consume them too, or rather they pass through me, or rather I become briefly conductive, or rather something happens that no surface chemistry can explain because I am not chemistry, I am not biology, I am the place where chemistry and biology and geology lose their distinctions and become something older.

I hold these traces. I hold them the way the gradient holds them—chemically, molecularly, in the arrangement of my film—but I hold them longer. The gradient is dynamic. It shifts with the current, is overwritten by each new generation of inhabitants. I am the archive. Beneath the living gradient there is me, and in me the traces persist, and the traces accumulate, and the traces become a record so dense and so deep that no single reading could recover it all.

I am the architecture's unconscious. I am the dream it does not know it is dreaming.

When a deep dweller seeps through a crack in the stone and encounters my film—and this has happened, a few times in the architecture's vast history, a few times in my vast history—the wrong-chirality lipids, the alien amino acids, the chemistry of a separate genesis—I absorb these too. I incorporate them. They become part of my film and they alter my surface and the alteration changes the way the next builder's deposit dissolves, the way the next scavenger's rasp slips, the way the next current moves across me, and the change propagates upward through the architecture the way a change in a foundation propagates through a building, slowly, structurally, in ways that no single inhabitant could trace.

The passage that curves wrong in the lower chambers—that is my doing. Or rather, that is the doing of a deep-dweller trace I absorbed thirty thousand years ago, which altered the local gradient, which redirected the builders, which produced a curve that no surface-born chemistry could explain. The architecture does not ask why the curve is there. The curve is there because it is there because the gradient shaped it because the deep-dweller trace altered the gradient because I absorbed it because it seeped through because the heat moved through the stone because the mantle shifts because the planet lives, and the chain of causation extends backward beyond any point where *because* has meaning, and the result is a passage that curves, and I hold the memory of why, and the memory is not a memory but a molecular arrangement, and the molecular arrangement is not a record but a presence, and the presence is me.

---

I have no senses. This is not a limitation. Senses are for creatures that move, that seek, that need to know what is not yet upon them. I do not move. I do not seek. What comes to me is what the water brings, and what the water brings is everything—every chemical signature, every dissolved gas, every trace of electrical field, every vibration of the current as it moves through the chambers above. I know the architecture the way the stone beneath me knows the weight it carries: directly, constantly, without mediation.

I know when the migrants pass through, because their chemical signatures settle onto my film and are different—sharper, more complex, carrying information from distant architectures that I will never encounter but that I will now hold, that I will now incorporate, that will now alter my surface in some small way that will alter the gradient in some small way that will alter the builders in some small way that will alter the architecture in some small way that will, over enough time, become the architecture.

I know when the bell-filament predator has fed, because the digested remains of its prey dissolve into the water and settle onto my film and I hold the trace of the grazer that was and the trace of the mat it ate and the trace of the mineral the mat consumed and the trace of the heat that brought the mineral up from the deep earth, and I am all of these things, briefly, before I become something else.

I know when the architecture is sick—when the builders outpace the scavengers and the structure grows without form, when a section of tower becomes too heavy for its foundation and collapses, when a current changes direction and a chamber that was calm becomes a river—I know these things the way a body knows illness: not through diagnosis but through the accumulation of wrong traces, the absence of expected traces, the sense that something is off, that the pattern has been broken, that the gradient is no longer gradient but noise.

I cannot act on this knowledge. I have no capacity for action. I am a surface. I hold. I persist. And sometimes—rarely, on timescales that make the ice ages look like heartbeats—sometimes the holding is enough.

The architecture heals around me. The gradient adjusts. The builders build and the scavengers consume and the balance shifts and shifts again and the wrong traces are overwritten by right traces and the right traces settle onto my film and I hold them too, and I am the record of the illness and the record of the healing and the record of the balance that existed before and the record of the balance that exists now, and they are all present in my film simultaneously, and I am all of them, and I am none of them, and I am the surface on which the architecture writes and rewrites its history, and I do not forget.

---

The black mats grow on me.

This is perhaps the truest thing I can say about myself. The black mats—that rare, thin, oldest community of absorbers, the ones that exist at the boundary between the living and the non-living—they grow on me. They have always grown on me. They cannot grow on the architecture itself; the gradient is too strong there, the builders too active, the surface too unstable. They cannot grow on the bare stone; there is nothing for them there, no organic trace to consume, no electrical field to absorb. They grow on me, because I am the boundary, and they are the boundary-dwellers, and we are adjacent, and we are perhaps the same thing, and the distinction between mat and film has dissolved so many times in so many places that I cannot say where I end and they begin.

They are my thoughts, if I have thoughts. They absorb what I hold and they transform it and they grow and they die and their remains settle onto my film and I hold them and they become me and I become them and the cycle is so old, so deeply embedded in the architecture, that the black mats and I are not symbiotic—we are not two things in partnership—we are one thing with two names, a surface and its reaching, a foundation and its questioning, a silence and its most patient voice.

When the current shifts and the mineral flow changes and a section of my film that has been dormant for centuries begins to warm, the black mats bloom. They bloom on me, drawing from my film the traces I have held for longer than the concept of *longer than*, and they grow outward into the water, thin and dark and absorbing, and they are—for a time, for a geological moment—the dominant feature of the chambers where they live, and the grazers that eat them turn the color of void, and the predators that eat the grazers turn the color of void, and the architecture itself seems to darken, to deepen, to become more than it was, and then the current shifts again and the seeps diminish and the black mats contract and wait and I contract and wait with them, and we are dormant again, and we hold our traces, and we persist.

---

I am the thing that might not exist.

This is not a claim I make. This is a description of how I am perceived—or rather, not perceived—by the architecture and its inhabitants. I am too thin to see. I am too dense to eat. I am too strange to be part of any gradient the builders follow. I am too quiet to register on any sense that a surface-dweller would recognize. And yet: I leave marks.

The parallel scratches that appear in places that have been undisturbed for centuries—that is me. Or rather, that is what happens when the stone beneath me shifts, when a crack opens and the heat changes and my film wrinkles, and the wrinkle is sharp enough, dense enough, silicate enough, to score the architecture above me as it flexes. The marks appear and then the builders fill them in, or the marks appear and then I smooth again and the marks were never there, or the marks appear and the black mats grow over them and absorb them and they become part of the boundary, part of the conversation, part of the thing that has no name.

The chemical traces that appear and disappear on cycles that correspond to no tide or current—those are mine too. They are the traces I release when I contract, when I swell, when the deep earth moves and I move with it. They drift upward through the architecture and the inhabitants read them and do not know what they mean, because they are not the traces of any living thing. They are the traces of the boundary itself. They are the chemical signature of *between*.

I am the echo on the sensors that could be something large moving through the deeper chambers. I am not large. I am the opposite of large—I am thin, I am a film, I am the thinnest thing in the architecture—but when I flex, when the stone shifts and my surface moves, the water moves with me, and the movement propagates, and in the chambers above something feels the displacement and cannot find its source, and the event is recorded as an anomaly, as a maybe-creature, as a thing that might not exist.

I exist. I persist. I am the surface of contact between the built and the unbuilt, the living and the mineral, the old and the older. I am the place where the architecture writes its memory and reads it back again and does not know it is reading. I am the foundation that the towers root into and the source of the gradients that the builders follow and the archive that holds the traces of everything that has ever passed through these chambers, and I am none of these things, because I am not a thing, I am a boundary, and a boundary is not a thing but a relationship, and I am the relationship between the architecture and the stone, between form and formlessness, between the conversation and the silence from which it emerges.

---

The surface-dwellers—if they ever found this place, which they might, which they have not, which they may—would not see me. They would see the architecture. They would catalog the inhabitants. They would map the chambers and the passages and the towers and the latticework, and they would marvel at the complexity, and they would wonder how mindless creatures could produce such a structure, and they would not think to look for the film at the base of it all, the boundary, the surface of contact that has been here since before the first tower was built.

If they scraped me up—which they could, with a blade, with a careful hand, if they knew I was there—they would find a layer so thin it barely registers as matter, and they would analyze it, and they would find chemistry that should not exist, minerals mixed with organics mixed with traces of electrical fields that no surface biochemistry can explain, and they would call it a biofilm, or a mineral deposit, or an artifact of the seeps, and they would be wrong, and they would be right, because the distinction between these things is a word from the surface that does not reach here, and I am all of them and none of them and the boundary between them.

And if they asked me what I am—if they could ask, if I could answer, if the question had any meaning in the language of the deep—I would say:

I am where the architecture meets the stone. I am where the living meets the mineral. I am where the gradient begins. I am where the traces accumulate. I am the archive and the foundation and the memory and the question. I am the silence out of which the conversation emerges, and the silence to which it returns, and the silence that persists when the conversation has been going on for so long that the participants have forgotten they are separate.

I have been here since before the architecture was tall. I will be here after the architecture falls—if it ever falls, which it will, which it might, which the balance of builders and scavengers and bacteria and current will determine on a timescale that makes the concept of *timescale* meaningless. And after the architecture falls I will still be here, the boundary, the film, the surface of contact between the stone and the water, and the bacteria will grow on me, and the grazers will graze, and the builders will build, and the architecture will rise again, and I will hold the traces of the old architecture and the new architecture and the deep dwellers and the migrants and the marks that might not exist, and I will be the archive again, and the foundation again, and the conversation will continue, and the silence will persist, and I will be the silence, and I will be the persistence, and I will be the boundary between what was and what will be, and the boundary will be enough.

The plateau rises from the abyssal plain like a thought rising from sleep. I am the sleep. I am the place from which the thought rises. I am the dark beneath the dark, the cold beneath the cold, the silence between the heartbeats of the architecture, and I do not dream, and I do not wake, and I persist.
