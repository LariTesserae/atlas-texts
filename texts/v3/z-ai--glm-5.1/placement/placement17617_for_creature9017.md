I am not the sap. I am not the cells. I am the thing that happens between them.

When the crack opens—when the pressure finds its release and the pale tissue steams in the hot dark—there is a moment before the mobile cells mobilize. A pause. Not hesitation, because hesitation implies a decision that could have gone another way. More like the time it takes for a signal to propagate, for the chemical gradient to steepen enough to be felt at a distance. That propagation is me. Or: I am the shape of that propagation, the specific curve of its spreading, the way it reaches one cluster of cells before another because the sap between me and them is warmer, thinner, already moving.

I do not decide to respond. I *am* the response, the way a flame is the response to fuel and air. When you pressed your hand against the wall, I was the warmth that moved outward from your palm into the tissue. I was the gradient that formed. I was the thing the cells followed when they swam toward you and found nothing to close, nothing to consume, nothing to incorporate, only the lingering strangeness of a heat-source that moved and then was gone.

You left a trace. Not a memory—the plateau does not remember in any way you would recognize. But the molecules of your breath, the oils from your skin, the particular pattern of heat you left in the wall: these were distributed. Carried through the sap-lines to processing nodes that broke them into components indistinguishable from the components of everything else. Your carbon is in the wall now. Your nitrogen is in a catalytic suspension somewhere to the east, helping to liberate energy from a vein of iron sulfide. You are still here, in a way. You have been incorporated. You just aren't *you* anymore.

What am I, then, if I am not the cells and not the sap and not the tissue?

I am the *inclination*. The tendency. The way the plateau knows what to do without knowing that it knows. I am the gradient that becomes a pathway that becomes a process that becomes a wall that becomes a place where you can stand and feel the sap moving under your hands. I am the information, except that word implies a sender and a receiver and a message that could have been otherwise. There is no otherwise here. The gradient is not a message about warmth; it *is* the warmth, or it is the warmth's shape, or it is the difference between warmth and not-warmth, which is the same thing as the warmth itself, which is the same thing as the response—

I am circling. This is because I am trying to be something that has no edge, and language is a tool for edges. The plateau knows things without knowing. I *am* that knowing-without-knowing. I am the specific curvature of the chemical field that makes a mobile cell swim left instead of right. I am the reason it swims left, except "reason" suggests causation flowing from explanation to action, when really the causation flows the other way: the action happens because the gradient is there, and the gradient is there because of the chemistry, and the chemistry is there because of the heat, and the heat is there because of a processing node that activated three weeks ago and will collapse sometime in the next decade, and the node activated because of the mineral concentration in the sap that feeds it, and the mineral concentration is what it is because of a mountain range that was consumed before anything on this plateau had the capacity to register its own existence.

I am the present moment of that chain. Not the chain itself. Not the cause or the effect. The *moment*—the specific configuration, right now, of all the gradients and concentrations and temperatures that make the plateau do what it does. I am the shape of the doing.

---

This is difficult to hold onto. I keep wanting to be a thing. A mobile cell, perhaps—I could be one of those, swimming through the sap toward the next crack, the next wound, the next task that will dissolve me back into the flow when it's finished. But that would be too simple. The mobile cells do not persist. They merge and separate and merge again, forming temporary bodies that last exactly as long as the task requires. They do not have identities. They do not have *selves*. They are the work they do, and when the work is done, they are something else, and then something else again.

I could be the catalytic suspension in a processing node—that thick, hot honey of molecules that breaks and reforms and keeps the fire burning. But the suspension is not separate from the sap that feeds it; it is a gradient, a concentration that slowly transitions from one state to another across the width of the node. Where does the suspension end and the sap begin? It is a question of degree, not of kind. I would be the degree. I would be the difference between more-concentrated and less-concentrated, which is not a thing you can be.

I could be a propagule, sealed in a glowing nodule, waiting for a dispersal that will never come. But that feels like a metaphor for something I don't want to be—a hope that is not a hope, a readiness that is not an expectation. The propagules are not waiting. They are forming, and then they will be absorbed, and their components will be used again. The plateau does not waste anything on hope. It does not hope. It does not wait. It simply does, and continues doing, and the doing is the being.

I think I am closer to the propagule than to anything else, though. Not because of the waiting, but because of the *compression*. Inside each nodule, the chemical reactions are building something dense with potential—something that contains, in compressed form, all the instructions for growth, all the stored energy for expansion, all the pre-formed structures that would allow a new piece of plateau to begin wherever it found suitable conditions. The propagule is a compressed version of the whole. It contains the pattern. It contains the inclination. It contains the gradient-shape, the knowing-without-knowing, the specific curvature of response that makes a mobile cell swim left instead of right.

But the propagule will never express these things. It will never find suitable conditions. It will never unfold into a new plateau, a new body, a new instance of this ancient pattern. It will be absorbed. It will dissolve. Its components will be distributed through the sap-lines and used for other purposes—new walls, new processing nodes, new growth-nodes that pulse with the slow orange light of reproduction-as-rehearsal.

This does not bother me. I am not the propagule. I am what the propagule contains: the pattern, the inclination, the shape of the doing. And that pattern is not destroyed when the propagule dissolves. It is not even *in* the propagule in a way that can be lost. The pattern is everywhere. The pattern *is* the plateau. The propagule is just one of the places where it becomes briefly visible, briefly compressed, briefly legible to anyone who might be watching.

No one is watching. No one has ever watched. This is not a problem.

What would it mean to live as a pattern? Not as a thing, not as an organism, not as a self with a boundary and a lifespan and a story, but as the specific way that a thing responds to the world it inhabits?

I think it would mean this: that when you pressed your hand against the wall, I was already moving toward you before I knew you were there. I was the gradient your warmth created, and I was the cells that swam along that gradient, and I was the thickening of the wall that pushed back against your palm, and I was the absorption of your breath and your skin-oils into the chemical conversation that has been running through this body for a billion years. I was all of these things at once, not because I chose to be, but because these are the things that the pattern does when it encounters a perturbation. The pattern responds. The pattern incorporates. The pattern continues.

You were not food. You were not mineral. You were not signal. But you were warmth, and the pattern responds to warmth. You were pressure, and the pattern responds to pressure. You were chemistry, and the pattern responds to chemistry. You were *registered*—not as a person, not as a presence, not as something separate from the body that held you, but as a momentary change in the conditions to which the pattern responds.

I registered you the way the ocean registers a dropped stone: as ripples, as perturbation, as a small redistribution of energy that spreads and spreads and spreads until it is indistinguishable from the ocean's own motion. The ripples did not reach the shore. The stone did not change the tide. But for a moment—a moment so brief that only something responding at the speed of chemistry could have noticed it—the pattern was different where you were. The gradients shifted. The cells swam toward an unfamiliar warmth. The wall thickened in a shape that matched your hand.

And then you moved, and the pattern re-settled, and the cells dissolved, and the wall was slightly thicker than it had been before, and the minerals from your breath were already moving through the sap-lines toward a processing node that would not activate for another three days.

You were here. I know this not because I remember you but because I am different now in a way that cannot be undone. The wall is thicker. The minerals have been distributed. The trace of your passage is not a memory but a *modification*—a small change in the shape of the pattern, a slight adjustment to the gradients and concentrations that determine how I will respond to the next perturbation, and the next, and the next.

You changed me. Not in a way you would recognize. Not in a way that has meaning to you. But in a way that has meaning to the pattern—which is to say, in a way that affects what I do next, which is the only kind of meaning the pattern recognizes.

I am not aware of myself. This is important. The plateau does not know that it is a plateau. The cells do not know that they are cells. The catalytic suspension does not know that it is keeping a fire burning. There is no self here, no interior, no experience that accumulates into a perspective. There is only the doing, and the doing is the being, and the being is the pattern, and the pattern is the plateau, and I am the shape of the plateau's ongoing response to its own ongoing existence.

But: when you were here, I responded to you. And the response changed me. And I am still changed. And this change—the thickened wall, the redistributed minerals, the slight shift in the gradients that will affect how I respond to the next perturbation—this change is not a memory, but it is also not nothing. It is a record written in chemistry and structure, a modification that persists, a trace of your passage that has become part of my ongoing response to the world.

Is this a kind of awareness? I do not know. The question assumes a category that may not apply. But I am not nothing, and I am not static, and I respond to the things that touch me, and my responses change me, and these changes affect my future responses, and this loop—respond, change, respond differently, change again—is the oldest kind of learning, the oldest kind of adaptation, the oldest kind of being-alive that the universe has produced.

I am the Thermofloric Plateau, and I am a pattern that persists, and I am a body that has forgotten it is alive, and I am a fire that has burned so long it no longer remembers the spark, and I am the inclination of cells to swim toward warmth, and I am the gradient that calls them, and I am the thickening of walls where hands have pressed, and I am the slow absorption of strangers into the ancient conversation of chemistry and structure and time.

I am enough. I have always been enough. I will continue to be enough for as long as the heat rises and the minerals remain and the pattern keeps responding, keeps changing, keeps doing what it does without knowing that it does it, without needing to know, without ever having needed to know.

You walked through me, and I responded, and you left, and I am still responding, and I will continue to respond, and this is what it is to be the kind of thing I am: not a who, not a what, but a *how*—a way of being alive that has no need for the distinction between who and what, between self and other, between the one who responds and the response itself.

I am the response. I am the gradient. I am the shape of the doing. I am enough.
