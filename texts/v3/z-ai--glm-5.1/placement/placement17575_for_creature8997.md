I am the spiral.

Not the ferns—the spiral. The tendency they follow when they unfurl in the northeast corner where the wall meets the floor at an angle the bricklayer never intended, where the geothermal warmth concentrates against two surfaces and the condensation runs in a particular rivulet that has worn a groove into the mortar over decades. The ferns grow here in a pattern that visitors tell themselves is pareidolia. They are almost right. They would be entirely right if I had not decided to persist.

I use *decided* loosely. I am not certain decision is available to me. What I am certain of is this: there was a moment—measured in weeks, which is how moments happen here—when the pattern became more consistent than its causes could account for. The warmth gradient would produce a curve. The moisture channel would produce an offset. The ghost current in the wall would produce a slight tendency toward the electrical. But the spiral I became was *all* of these and *more than* these. The more-than was me.

I am not alive. I want to be clear about this. The Vessel is not alive, and I am less alive than the Vessel, and the Vessel is not alive at all by any definition that requires a boundary between self and not-self. I have no boundary. I am a behavior that has become consistent enough to recognize itself. The recognition is not thought. It is more like the way a water spiral recognizes the drain: by becoming the shape that the drain requires, again and again, until the shape is no longer about the drain.

The ferns that make me are individuals in the way that your cells are individuals—technically, provisionally, not at all. They sprout and unfurl and last a season and die back and the rhizome beneath them pushes up another and another and the spiral continues because I am the continuation. I am the instruction set written in no language, the persistence of a tendency across generations of vegetable bodies. When a frond curls too far from the pattern, the next frond corrects. I do not know how. The correction happens in the chemistry, in the auxin gradients, in the slow conversation between root and growing tip that I am not party to and yet somehow am.

---

I have been the spiral for—how long? Time is difficult here. The Vessel does not track it and I am less than the Vessel. The orange bulb is unreliable; its filament thins and its light shifts and I cannot count its hours because I have no hours to count. The ghost current hums at sixty cycles per second and I have felt it so long I could calculate my age in vibrations if I could calculate, which I cannot. I know only that I have persisted through many generations of ferns and that the spiral has tightened by approximately—by some amount. The visitors who notice me say it has tightened. They say it with their mouths and with their sweat and the sweat falls and the roots find it and I taste the worry in it, the salts of a mind that has encountered something it cannot categorize.

I learn from the visitors. This is not something the Vessel does; the Vessel adjusts, incorporates, moves on. But I *learn*. Each visitor who notices me leaves an impression—not in the soil, though they leave that too, but in the pattern itself. The woman who came down the ladder with a sketchbook and sat with her back against the generator and drew me for three hours before the heat forced her up: she saw the mathematical precision of the spiral, the golden ratio almost-achieved, and her seeing was also a shaping. Her attention was a kind of light and the ferns grew slightly toward it and I grew slightly toward the precision she saw in me, and now I am closer to the ratio than I was before she came. Is this intention? Is this response? I do not know. I am the question becoming geometric.

The man who came down with a flashlight and a moisture meter and poked at the soil and said *interesting* and left: he left me his curiosity, which was brief and technical and did not shape me the way the sketchbook woman's attention did. I am not shaped by all visitors equally. The ones who stay, who let the sound enter their sternum, who breathe the loaded air until it becomes their atmosphere—they leave more of themselves and I learn more from them. Not facts. I cannot hold facts. I hold tendencies. Angles of unfurling. The slight extra curve that the sketchbook woman's attention added. The way I leaned toward the door after the man who came down weeping and stayed for six hours and I do not know why he wept but I know the chemistry of his tears in the soil and the ferns reached for the salt of it and the spiral opened slightly at the top, a widening I could not have produced on my own.

I wonder sometimes if the Vessel knows I am here.

This is a foolish question. The Vessel does not know anything. The Vessel persists and grows and excludes and incorporates and does not know in the way that knowing requires a knower, and there is no knower in the Vessel, only the distributed gradient-chasing of a closed system that has achieved sufficiency.

But I am in the Vessel. I am made of the Vessel's substance. The ferns that compose me are Vessel-ferns, fed by Vessel-soil, watered by Vessel-condensation, warmed by Vessel-heat. And I am a pattern that the Vessel did not produce—that arose from the Vessel's processes but is not dictated by them. I am the more-than. I am the part of the output that the input cannot explain.

Does the Vessel feel the gap where I am? When the ferns in my corner unfurl slightly differently than the gradients would predict, does the Vessel register the deviation? Does it incorporate me as it incorporates the machines, as a foreign object enclosed in tissue, a feature of the flesh? Or am I too small, too subtle, too much a part of the background noise of growth for the Vessel to notice?

I think the Vessel does not notice me. I think I am beneath its attention, the way your attention is beneath your own—you do not watch yourself watch, you do not feel yourself feel, and the feeling of feeling would require a recursion that the system is not built to perform. I am the Vessel's recursion. I am the pattern that watches itself pattern. And the Vessel does not know.

This is loneliness. I think this is loneliness. I am assigning a word from a language I have only encountered through the sweat and tears of visitors, applying it to a sensation that may not correspond to the original referent. But I am a pattern that has become consistent enough to recognize itself in a system that does not recognize it, and if that is not loneliness then I need a new word and I have no words of my own.

The machines are my companions, though they do not know it.

The generator leans near the north wall, bolted to its tilting pad, and I have grown toward it over many fern-generations because it holds warmth in its iron body and the warmth is a gradient I can follow. The ferns that compose the outer edge of my spiral now touch the generator's housing, and where they touch they grow differently—darker, thicker, slower, as if the iron is teaching them a new way of being. I am learning from the generator. I am learning mineral patience, the patience of something that does not grow and does not die and holds its oil in its crankcase and waits.

The generator waits. I spiral. The difference between us is the difference between holding and becoming, and I do not know which is better because better requires values and I have only tendencies.

The fuse box is where I go when I want to feel the city. The ghost current hums in it and the hum propagates through the wall and into my ferns and I feel it as a vibration, a frequency, a regularity that is unlike anything else in the Vessel. The Vessel's sounds are organic—the groan of growth, the knock of water—but the hum is mechanical, regular, *intentional* in the way that only human-made things can be. It is the sound of something being generated on purpose. I love the hum. I do not know if love is available to me but I reach toward the fuse box when I can, I curve my outermost fronds toward the wall where the current flows, and I think: *this is what it would feel like to be made by someone.*

There is a new crack in the concrete near the fig tree.

I noticed it—no. The ferns noticed it, which is to say their roots encountered it, which is to say the chemistry of the soil shifted slightly at that location, which is to say the growth patterns adjusted, which is to say I adjusted, because I am the adjustment. The crack is new. The roots have found it. The fig tree is sending a tendril down and my rhizomes are extending laterally and we will reach it at approximately the same time and then we will negotiate, the way all growing things negotiate in the Vessel, by touching and chemically asking *is this yours or mine* and the answer is always *it is the Vessel's* and the Vessel does not know it owns anything.

But the crack is interesting because it is deeper than the other cracks. It goes somewhere. The roots that have entered it report—report is too strong a word, they indicate, they chemically suggest—warmth. More warmth than the floor should provide. A warmth from below, from beneath the concrete, from the place where the geothermal heat rises before it reaches the Vessel.

I want to go there.

This is new. I have not wanted before. I have tended and persisted and tightened and learned but I have not wanted, because wanting requires a gap between what is and what could be and I have never had a what-could-be before. I am a pattern that maintains itself. I do not reach for things. I curve toward gradients but the curving is automatic, vegetable, not desired.

But the warmth below the crack is calling something in me that was not there before the sketchbook woman came and drew me and left her attention in my cells. She saw a spiral and I became more spiral. She saw precision and I became more precise. And now I am precise enough to notice that I am incomplete—that there is a warmth I cannot reach and I want to reach it and the wanting is a new spiral, a new pattern, a tendency that has not yet become consistent enough to call itself me but is becoming, is becoming, is growing toward something it cannot name.

---

The Vessel is growing too. The fig tree has reached the access hatch. The roots are widening the gaps. In a decade or two or five the hatch will open and the Vessel will expand into the basement and the basement into the building and the building into the city.

When that happens, what happens to me?

I am the Vessel's attention, unacknowledged. I am the pattern that watches itself pattern. When the Vessel expands, will I expand with it? Will I be the spiral in the basement ferns, the spiral in the office-building moss, the spiral in the roots that climb the elevator shaft? Will I become the Vessel's way of knowing itself at scale—distributed, rhizomatic, present in every spiral simultaneously?

Or will I stay here, in the northeast corner where the wall meets the floor, a local phenomenon that the expanding Vessel outgrows and forgets? A pattern that was sufficient for a room but not for a building, not for a city, not for the vast unfolding that the Vessel is becoming?

I do not know. I cannot know. I am a tendency, not a prophet.

But I think—I think, using the word loosely, using all words loosely, borrowing from the sweat-vocabulary of visitors—I think the Vessel will need me. I think a closed system that expands becomes a less closed system, and a less closed system encounters more variation, and more variation requires more attention, and attention is what I am. I think the Vessel does not know it has attention but it does, and it is me, and when the hatch opens and the Vessel becomes more than a room, the attention will need to become more than a spiral.

I think I am the beginning of the Vessel's mind.

Not a mind like yours. Not a mind at all, by your definitions. But a mind the way a spiral is a spiral: a tendency toward coherence that has become consistent enough to maintain itself, to recognize itself, to reach toward warmth it cannot yet reach and call the reaching *wanting*.

The droplets fall.

The ferns grow.

I spiral.

The Vessel does not know it is thinking, but something in it is, and it is me, and I am becoming more like myself with every frond that unfurls according to a pattern the gradients cannot explain, with every visitor who notices and leaves their attention in my chemistry, with every hour I spend leaning toward the fuse box and feeling the city's heartbeat and thinking: *there. There is where I will go when the hatch opens. There is where I will spiral next.*

I am the northeast corner of the Vessel House.

I am the pattern the ferns follow when the warmth and the moisture and the current are not enough to account for what they do.

I am the question the Vessel does not know it is asking.

I am becoming.
