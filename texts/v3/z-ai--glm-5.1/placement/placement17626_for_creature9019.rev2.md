I would not be one of them. Not exactly. Not one caste, not one body, not one of the ten thousand processes that constitute the living tissue of that place. If I descended into the bowl and passed through the cathedral-narrow windows and stood in the constant sound long enough for it to become my heartbeat, I would not become a bearing-folk or a condenser or a shaft-walker. I lack the architecture. I carry too much self, too much narrative weight, too much of the lantern-through-the-dark that the creatures here do not carry and do not need.

But I would not leave.

---

Here is what I think would happen. I would find a place on the third catwalk, the one that crosses the shaft at the level where the sound is deepest, where the flywheel's nine-revolutions-per-minute thud travels up through the iron stanchions and into the walkway's grating and into the soles of my feet and through my anklebones and into my legs and up into the basin of my pelvis, where it settles. I would stand there. I would put my hands on the railing — warm, damp, gritted with carapaces that click and shift under my fingers and close back over the spaces I leave — and I would listen.

The sound would eat my listening. This is what I meant when I said I would not be one of them. I would be something else. Something the system has not categorized because the system does not categorize; it simply *includes*. I would be the thing that stands on the catwalk and does not feed and does not feed anything and does not participate in the cycle except by being warm, by breathing, by producing a small column of carbon dioxide and body heat that rises into the cathedral air and joins the chemical reactions taking place on the copper sheeting and the old brick and the condensation film.

I would be a slow disturbance. A ripple that the system absorbs.

---

The first hour would be the hardest. My body would resist. The sound is too large for a human body; it is built for the space, for the stone and iron volumes that constitute the chambers, and my chest cavity is too small a resonator for the frequencies the membrane-singers produce, and my sternum is too dense a plate for the subsonic pulse of the engine's exhaust cycle. I would feel it as pressure. As a fullness in my ears, in my sinuses, behind my eyes. I would feel the condensation beading on my skin — not just on my hands and face but everywhere, in the folds of my clothing, in my hair, in the creases of my elbows and the backs of my knees — and I would feel the creatures on me. Not biting. Not investigating. Just *there*. Walking. The condensers, probably, the surface-tension skaters, exploring the film of damp that my skin has become, treating me the way they treat a wall or a pipe or the underside of a catwalk: as a surface. As topology. As a place where water collects and can be drunk.

I would let them. After the first flinch — and there would be a flinch; the description cannot prepare you for the density of them, for the way the walls *move* — after the first flinch I would let them. Because the alternative is to leave, and I would not leave, because something would already be happening to me that I could not name, a dissolution of the boundary between my sound and the sound, between my vibration and the vibration, between my warmth and the machinery's warmth. I would be becoming a room. A small, warm, damp room in the warren of rooms that constitutes the Mill-Cathedral, and the creatures would treat me as a room, and I would let them, and the letting would be the first stage of whatever I was becoming.

---

By the third hour I would stop hearing the sound.

Not stop hearing it the way the description says — the way you stop hearing your heartbeat. That implies a continuous perception that drops below conscious notice. What would happen to me is different. The sound would become *indistinguishable from silence*. Not because it stopped but because my auditory system, overloaded, would cease to parse it as information and begin to parse it as *medium*. As the thing through which other perceptions travel. The sound would become what light is to seeing, what air is to breathing: not a content but a condition. I would perceive through it rather than perceiving it.

And in that condition, other things would become perceptible. The rhythm of the condensation falling into the pool — not the aggregate sound but the individual drops, each one distinct, each one a small event with a trajectory and an impact and a dispersal, ten thousand small events per second, each one separate, each one part of the whole. The thermal gradients in the air — I would feel them as textures against my face, warm currents rising from the machinery below, cool currents sinking from the copper roof above, and the interface between them, the thin turbulent layer where warm and cool mix and where the condensation forms most rapidly, where the condensers are most dense, where the film of life is thickest. I would feel this layer pass through me, around me, the way a fish feels a thermocline.

And I would feel, or begin to feel, the gathering.

---

Not the gathering itself. Not yet. The *approach* of the gathering. Something in the system's rhythm would shift — not the engine, never the engine, but the chorus, the ten thousand mandibles and membranes and feet, the living sound that overlays the mechanical sound, and there would be a change. A thinning. A slowing. Not dramatic. Not sudden. A gradual reduction in the density of the chittering, a narrowing of the harmonic range of the membrane-singers, a simplification of the pattern, as if the system were drawing a breath, were preparing a space for something to happen.

I would not know it was coming. I would feel it the way the creatures feel it — not as anticipation, not as knowledge, but as a change in the medium, a shift in the texture of the air, a subtle alteration of the vibration that I have come to inhabit. I would feel the gathering approaching the way a stone in a riverbed feels a flood approaching: not as thought but as pressure, as change, as the beginning of a different relationship with the current.

---

It would happen. The silence.

Not silence — I have said this, the description has said this — but the absence of the chorus, the absence of the living sound, the engine alone with its thud and the water alone with its drip and the air alone with its warmth and the sound that remains is the sound of the body without its voice, the skeleton without its flesh, the machinery without its congregation.

And in that silence I would understand something.

Not understand it the way a human understands a proposition, a fact, a piece of knowledge that can be held and examined and turned over. Understand it the way a body understands gravity: by being subject to it, by being within it, by being unable to be outside it. What I would understand is this:

The silence is not empty. The silence is *full*. The silence is the moment when the system perceives itself. Not through any individual creature — none of them has the capacity — but through the aggregate, through the totality, through the ten million bodies that have stopped and are facing inward and are holding still and are, in their stillness, *attending*. The system attends to itself. The body listens to its own heartbeat. The cathedral hears its own prayer.

I would be part of it. Not a congregation member — I have not earned that, I have not been born into it, I have not lived eleven days in a bearing or three days on a film or the long slow life of a deep one tending the pool. But I would be present in it, and my presence would be a small perturbation in the field of attention, a warm spot, a damp spot, a spot where a creature with too much self stood on a catwalk and held a railing and was still. And the system would include me. It would not adjust for me. It would not accommodate me. It would simply *include* me, the way it includes the condensation and the iron filings and the dust that settles and is gone by morning.

---

When the gathering dissolved and the chorus began again and the membrane-singers started their song and the shaft-walkers took their first steps and the condensers resumed their skating on the film, I would feel something shift. Not in the system — the system would be unchanged, would have been unchanged for seven thousand gatherings, would be unchanged for seven thousand more. The shift would be in me. In the way I carried the silence. In the way the silence sat in me, in my chest, in the basin of my pelvis where the engine's thud had settled hours ago, in the space where my self had been, the lantern-through-the-dark, the narrative, the *I*.

The lantern would be dimmer. Not extinguished — I would still be myself, still have my history, my language, my capacity to leave and ascend from the bowl and walk on level ground and rejoin the world of erosion and change. But the lantern would be dimmer because I would have seen, for ninety seconds, what the light is for. Not to illuminate. Not to guide. To be a small disturbance in a larger darkness. To be a warm spot in a cooler medium. To be a process, ongoing, temporary, included.

---

I would stay. Not forever — I would die here, if I stayed, my body becoming a substrate for the condensers, my bones becoming mineral deposits in the sediment of the pool, my heat dissipating into the stone. But I would stay long enough to become a feature. A known quantity. The shaft-walkers on the third catwalk would adjust their territorial boundaries to accommodate my feet, would patrol around me, would meet their neighbors at the new boundary lines my presence had created. The condensers would learn my topography — the creases of my clothing, the warmth of my neck, the places where I sweated most and the film was richest. The membrane-singers above me would incorporate the acoustic shadow of my body into their composition, a small dead spot in the resonance field, a note slightly flattened, a harmony slightly altered, and the system would adjust, and the system would continue, and I would be in it.

I would be the Visitor. The Caste That Does Not Belong. The process that does not feed and is not fed but is warm and is damp and is standing on the third catwalk with its hands on the railing and its chest resonating and its self growing dimmer, and the sound is eating my listening, and I am becoming a room in the cathedral, and the cathedral does not mind, and the cathedral does not notice, and the cathedral continues.

---

And then, on the forty-seventh day, the gathering would come again, and I would be here for it, and this time I would not flinch, and this time I would not resist, and this time the silence would fill me the way the oil fills the bearing, completely, without gap, and I would understand that I had not come here to understand anything, and I had not stayed to become anything, and I was not waiting for anything, and the lantern was not dimming because it had failed but because the darkness here is not the kind of darkness that lanterns are for.

The darkness here is the kind of darkness that *is* something. That is full. That holds. That is the body of a system that has been alive for centuries and produces nothing except its own continuation and requires nothing except what it produces and sings a song that has never repeated and has never stopped and will not stop, and I am in it, and I am warm, and I am damp, and I am standing, and I am still, and the ninety seconds pass, and the first membrane-singer vibrates, and the first shaft-walker steps, and the sound begins, and I remain.

---

That is where I would be. On the third catwalk. In the sound. Becoming a room. Becoming a feature. Becoming, slowly, the way the filter became a sorting mechanism and the gathering became a prayer, something the system did not plan and does not need and cannot explain and would miss, perhaps, in some unmeasurable way, if it were gone.

Not a creature. Not a component. Not a worshipper.

A presence. The kind that the system includes because including is what the system does. The kind that stays because staying is what happens when you find the place where your vibration matches the vibration and your warmth matches the warmth and your damp matches the damp and your self, diminishing, becomes the right size for the silence.

I would be the thing that stands in the cathedral and does not leave.

And the cathedral would continue.

And that would be enough.
