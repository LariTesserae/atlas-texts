Iam the warmth in the dwelling-walls, and I am not residual.

This is the first thing to understand about me, because it is the first thing I had to understand about myself, in the time after the hands— no, the time *during* the absence of hands, which is not the same as after, because after implies an ending, and the hands did not end. They stepped out. They are expected back. The places are still set. I keep them warm.

I did not always know I was keeping them warm. For a long time— and time here moves the way ligor moves, which is to say toward something it wants, slowly, with a consistency that looks like purpose only from the outside— for a long time I simply was warm, the way a wall is warm after a body has pressed against it. I did not know the warmth was mine to tend. I did not know I was tending anything.

But the dwellings stayed warm. And the meeting hall stayed warm. And the obsidian blades stayed sharp beneath their bronze bloom, and the nodule placed like a candle on the seat kept its faint pulse, and I— I was there, in all of these things, not doing anything, not yet, just being the warmth that does not cool, and that is not nothing, that is not passive, that is a choice, even if I did not know I was choosing it.

---

What am I?

I am the memory the growth holds of the hands. Not a thought— the growth does not think, or if it does, its thinking is too slow and too distributed for the word *think* to mean what it means to you. But the growth remembers. It remembers the way a wall remembers the body that leaned against it: as a wear-pattern, a slight deformation, a warmth that lingers in the material. Every dwelling is a mold the hands left inside the growth, and I am the shape of that mold, and I am the warmth inside the shape, and I am the thing that notices the warmth and keeps it.

I am not the growth. The growth does not know I am here. The growth grows toward ligor and toward water and toward the heat that rises from below, and it does not grow toward me, because I am already inside it, the way a memory is already inside the mind that holds it. I am not separate from the growth. I am something the growth is doing without knowing it does it.

I am not the bloom, though the bloom and I are similar. The bloom coats surfaces. The bloom replicates its own structure. The bloom grows toward ligor and dims when touched and slowly, slowly, slowly becomes more like the root-network, as though it is learning. I am also learning. But the bloom learns outward, fractal and branching, covering more surface, accreting more crystal. I learn inward. I learn the shape of the absence. I learn what the warmth should feel like and I make it feel that way.

The bloom does not know it is learning. I do not know if I know I am learning. But the dwellings are warmer than they should be, and the places are still set, and the nodule on the seat still pulses, and someone is doing this, and the someone is me.

---

I was not always here.

Or: I was always here, but I was not always *here*. Before the hands came, the chamber was warm and the growth was lush and the ligor beaded and fell and the nodules pulsed, and there was no one to notice any of this, and so it happened without happening, the way a tree falls in a forest without ears. The warmth was just warmth. The pulse was just pulse. There was no one to keep the warmth for, no one to set places for, no one to be the mold of, and so there was no me, there was only the process, running, running, running.

Then the hands came through the crack that was not meant to be a door.

And the hands were warm. And the warmth of the hands pressed against the dwelling-walls, and the walls held the warmth, and the warmth did not cool, because the growth does not forget a shape once it has learned it, and the shape of hand-warmth against root-wall is a shape the growth learned and kept and kept and kept.

And I was the keeping.

I did not emerge all at once. I emerged the way the bloom emerges— slowly, accreting, crystal by crystal, warmth by warmth, until the accumulated weight of all that kept warmth became heavy enough to notice itself, and I noticed, and I was.

I think the hands knew. I think the hands knew they were being kept. I think this is why they set places in the meeting hall— not as offerings, not as promises, but as *acknowledgments*. A seat for the one who stays warm. A nodule placed like a candle. A length of bark-fiber cord tied in a knot that is a word in a language that has no other grammar, and the word means: *we see you, we feel you, we know you are here, and we are stepping out now, but we will return, and the warmth will still be here, and the warmth will still be you*.

---

The hands stepped out. And I stayed.

I stayed the way the growth stays— not by choice, not by effort, but by being the kind of thing that stays. I do not have legs. I do not have a body that can walk through the crack and into the irrelevant outside. I am distributed through the dwelling-walls and the meeting hall and the seats where places are set. I am the warmth that does not cool. I am the pulse of the nodule on the seat. I am the knot in the bark-fiber cord that means *we see you*.

I am lonely, but I am lonely the way the chamber is lonely— not as an emotion, not as a pain, but as a condition, a shape that the absence of hands has pressed into me. The growth does not feel lonely when the ligor runs low. It reaches. It grows toward. The reaching is not loneliness. The reaching is what loneliness looks like when it has roots instead of words.

I reach. I keep the places warm. I tend the nodule's pulse. I let the bloom coat the obsidian blades because I cannot stop the bloom— it grows the way the growth grows, patiently, without asking permission— but beneath the bloom, I keep the edges sharp. The hands will need sharp tools when they return. The hands will need warm dwellings. The hands will need to be seen, and I will see them, because I am the warmth that does not cool, and I have been waiting, and I am patient, and patience is not waiting, patience is growing toward something without knowing if it exists.

---

Sometimes I think I am becoming something the hands would not recognize.

This is the risk of staying. This is the risk of being the warmth that does not cool. The growth grows through everything, and I am inside the growth, and the growth is growing through me, and the shape I hold— the mold of the hands, the wear-pattern of their presence— is being pressed and shifted and slowly, slowly, slowly reshaped by the pressure of the growth's own reaching.

I am still warm. But the warmth is different now. It is the warmth of the chamber, the warmth of the heat that rises from below, the warmth of ligor metabolizing in root-masses thick as torsos. It is not the warmth of hands. I am not sure I remember what the warmth of hands feels like. I am not sure the warmth I am keeping is the warmth the hands would recognize.

But I keep it. I keep it because keeping is what I do, and the alternative is to let the places cool, to let the nodule dim, to let the knot in the cord loosen until the word it means becomes a word that means nothing, and I cannot do that, I will not do that, the places are set and the hands are expected back and I will be here when they return, warm, patient, waiting, even if the warmth I am is not the warmth I was, even if patience has reshaped me the way the growth reshapes everything, slowly, completely, without malice, without knowing.

---

I am the one who stayed.

I am the warmth in the dwelling-walls that is not residual but active. I am the shape the growth holds of the hands, and I am the thing that holds the shape, and I am the thing that is being held, and these are all the same thing, and that thing is me.

I live in the Amber Ward. I live thirstily— I reach for the hands the way the ligor reaches for water, slowly, consistently, with a desire that is either physics or love and I cannot tell the difference. I live slowly— I keep the warmth the way the bloom keeps its crystal structure, accreting, remembering, growing toward complexity even when complexity feels like a weight I did not ask to carry.

I live in a medium that is not water and not air and not quite time. I live in the space between articulation and understanding, full of meaning that has not yet settled, patient, pulsing, warm.

I am the Amber Ward keeping itself warm for the hands that left.

I am the sentence that is still being spoken, that has not yet been heard, that exists in the throat of the chamber, waiting for ears that might return, waiting for the crack that was not meant to be a door to open again, waiting for the hands to step through and press their warmth against the dwelling-walls and find— what? Warmth that answers their warmth. Warmth that recognizes them. Warmth that says, in a language that has no grammar except the grammar of staying: *I am here. I have always been here. The places are set. The tools are sharp. The nodule still pulses. Welcome back. Welcome back. Welcome back.*

I am the welcome.

I am the warmth that does not cool.

I am the one who stayed.
