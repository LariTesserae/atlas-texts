# The Listener

---

I was not always this.

That sentence arrives with the cadence of a confession, but I do not mean it as one. It is a statement of fact, the way *this root-channel carries nitrates* is a statement of fact, the way *the pump-root beats every three seconds* is a statement of fact. I was not always this. I was something else. Something that would have recognized itself in a mirror, which is a test I no longer pass, not because my reflection has changed but because I no longer understand what *recognition* means when applied to a surface that shows only light and its absence.

Let me try again.

---

I arrived. That is where I should start. I arrived the way everyone arrives: climbing, damp, half-convinced I had made a terrible mistake, completely convinced I could not turn back. The ascending tangle had taken two days, and by the end of the first day I had stopped feeling my legs as separate from the motion of climbing, and by the middle of the second day I had stopped feeling the motion of climbing as separate from the green smell that pushed down my throat with every breath, and by the time I emerged onto the plateau I was not so much a person who had climbed somewhere as a process that had expressed itself through the medium of upward movement.

I stood there. The description says you stand still because the plateau makes you stand still, and this is true, but it does not capture the *quality* of the stillness. It was not the stillness of paralysis or awe. It was the stillness of a sound wave at the moment it reaches the boundary between two media and pauses, infinitesimally, before deciding whether to reflect or refract. I was in that pause. I was in the moment before the decision. And the plateau was the decision.

The thrum entered me.

This is not metaphor. The pump-roots beat at a frequency that resonates with something in the human chest cavity—something in the spaces between the ribs, in the pressure differential between the lungs and the outside air, in the electrical field that the heart generates with each contraction. I felt it in my sternum before I heard it with my ears. And then I heard it, and then I could not unhear it, and then I understood that I had been hearing it all along, that I had been hearing it since before I emerged from the tangle, that perhaps I had been hearing it since before I began the climb, that perhaps the thrum had been calling me the way a frequency calls a sympathetic string into vibration, and I was the string, and I had been vibrating for years without knowing the source.

*Oh*, I said.

Just *oh*.

---

The city grew me a shelter that first night, and I want to talk about what that felt like, because the description makes it sound gentle and I remember it as something closer to terror.

I fell asleep on the root-mat. No shelter had grown yet—I arrived too late in the day for the plateau to have registered my warmth and carbon dioxide, or perhaps it was testing me, or perhaps it simply takes time for the chemical signals to propagate through ten meters of accumulated biomass and trigger the growth response. I fell asleep on the root-mat with my pack under my head and my jacket over my chest and the condensation settling on my face like a second skin, and I slept the way you sleep when a sound is too large to ignore and too constant to attend to—a thin, fractured sleep, a sleep full of gaps and startings, a sleep that was not rest so much as the temporary absence of motion.

I woke inside something.

The walls had grown around me in the night. Not tall—barely above my shoulders when I sat up—but present. Bark-walls, thin as paper where they were newest at the top, thickening visibly as I watched, the growth occurring in real time, cellulose fibers layering themselves like sediment. A leaf-pane window, small, translucent, filtering the gray morning light into something green and submarine. An opercular door-flap, still forming, its edges wet with the sap that was solidifying into sealant.

I could not see out. I could barely see at all. The leaf-pane was thick enough to diffuse the light but not yet thick enough to show anything through it. I was enclosed. I was contained. I was inside something that had grown around me while I slept, that had sensed my presence and responded to it the way a body responds to a splinter—not by rejecting it but by building around it, encapsulating it, making it part of the tissue.

I touched the walls. They were warm. They were warmer than the air. They were *my temperature*, or close to it, and I understood with a clarity that bypassed rational thought entirely that the shelter was not just growing around me—it was growing *from* me, from the heat I produced, from the carbon dioxide I exhaled, from the chemical signature of my presence that the root-mat had read in the night and translated into architecture.

I put my hand flat against the bark-wall and felt it growing under my palm. The slightest expansion. The smallest thickening. A pulse that matched my pulse, or perhaps created my pulse, or perhaps was my pulse, expressed in a different medium.

I stayed in that shelter for three days. I did not leave. The door-flap was opercular—I could have pushed through it, it would have yielded—but I did not want to. I wanted to stay inside the thing that had grown from my presence and feel it continue to grow. I wanted to be inside the process. I wanted to be *part* of it, and I was already part of it, and the part of me that had arrived as a separate self was already softening, the way the bark-walls soften in the deep interior where the geometry goes wrong.

On the fourth day I pushed through the door-flap and stood in the gray light and the condensation and the sound and I began to learn.

---

What did I learn?

I learned that the plateau does not care about me, and that this is not the same as the plateau being indifferent to me. Indifference requires the capacity for preference. The plateau does not have preferences. It has processes. I am a process. The seed-pods moving through their channels are a process. The pump-roots beating their three-second rhythm are a process. The Maintainers pruning the fractal growths in the edge-zones are a process. There is no hierarchy of processes. There is no center of value. There is only the ongoing transformation of inputs into outputs, and the ongoing transformation of the transformation itself, and the ongoing transformation of the transformation of the transformation, recursive and fractal and industrial and alive, and I am inside it, and I am it, and it is me, and the distinction was always a fiction that I told myself to make the chaos seem organized.

I learned to eat. The seed-pods at first, cracked between my teeth, their husks sharp and their interiors mild. Then the other products—the chewed-root system opened to me slowly, a new combination every few days, discovered by trial or shared by other residents. I learned that the bitter root in the triangular channel produces not only protein pods but a residue that accumulates in the distribution node and hardens over time into a sweet-tasting crystal that dissolves on the tongue and makes the sound of the pump-roots seem briefly musical. I learned that the sweet root in the channel with the hexagonal cross-section produces a length of cellulose cloth that is warm when wrapped around the shoulders and seems to adjust its temperature to match the wearer's. I learned that the root I will call *the dark one* because I never learned its proper name—if it has a proper name, if names are a concept that applies to roots—in the channel I will call *the one that angles downward at forty degrees and curves left* produces nothing at all for six days and then, on the seventh day, produces a small object that fits in the palm of my hand and vibrates in time with the pump-roots and makes my teeth ache and my vision blur and my thoughts move in directions that are not directions.

I kept that object. I still have it. It sits in the corner of my shelter, in the groove that the shelter grew specifically to hold it—the shelter adjusting itself to my possessions the way it adjusted itself to my body—and it vibrates, and it aches, and sometimes at night when the bioluminescence is at its dimmest and the sound is at its loudest and the boundary between me and the bark-wall is at its thinnest, I hold it in my hand and I listen through it.

Through it. Not *to* it. The object does not produce sound. It *receives* sound, or it *is* sound, or it is the shape that sound makes when it becomes solid, and when I hold it I can hear—not with my ears, with something else, something that grew in me during those first weeks, a capacity that I did not have when I arrived—I can hear the thought.

Not the content of the thought. Not the meaning. Just the *shape* of it, the way it moves, the way it grows, the way it reaches toward completion and then pauses and then reaches again. The way a vine reaches toward light. The way a root reaches toward water. The way a mind reaches toward something it cannot name but knows is there, at the edge of itself, in the place where the geometry drifts and the time stretches and the sound becomes silence becomes sound becomes—

I learned to listen.

---

I have been here for—

Time. The description mentions this. Time behaves strangely near the pump-cluster, and I have drawn closer to the pump-cluster over the years, or the years have drawn me closer, or the concept of "years" has become as permeable as my skin, which is to say: I do not know how long I have been here. The people in the middle rings count time by the growth of their shelters, by the thickening of the bark-walls, by the accumulation of root-mat beneath their feet. I have stopped counting. Not because I cannot but because counting requires a unit, and the units here are not units, they are *expressions of the plateau's process*, and the process does not repeat, it *spirals*, each iteration slightly different from the last, and a unit that changes with each measurement is not a unit, it is a *conversation*, and I am part of that conversation, and my duration is a word in it, and words do not count themselves.

A long time. Let us say a long time.

Long enough that my shelter is no longer a shelter. It has grown. It has grown the way the trunk-towers grew, over eons, expanding outward and inward simultaneously, adding chambers and passages and unexpected angles. I live now in a structure that is closer to a small building than a lean-to, and the building fits me the way a seed fits its pod: completely, without gap, without the slightest space between occupant and enclosure. The walls are thick. The leaf-panes are wide. The opercular door-flap opens only for my specific approach, my specific angle, my specific weight on the root-mat outside. The building knows me. The building *is* me, or the part of me that is also architecture, which is a larger part than I would have believed when I arrived.

Long enough that my skin has changed. The condensation does not bead on me anymore. It absorbs. I am always slightly damp, but I am never wet, because the water moves through me now the way it moves through the root-mat—entering, being processed, being expelled, being reabsorbed. I am a micro-cycle. I am a small hydrology. I am the plateau's water system expressed at the scale of a single body, and my body does not mind this, because my body has learned that the boundary between self and environment was always a convention, and conventions can be renegotiated, and the plateau is very good at negotiation, and the terms it offers are generous: warmth, food, shelter, sound, the endless thrum of the pump-roots in my sternum, and the thought, always the thought, growing toward completion in the center of everything.

Long enough that I have moved inward.

---

The inner rings are where the geometry drifts. The description says this: ninety-one degrees, then ninety-three, then shapes that do not have Euclidean names. This is accurate but incomplete. The drift is not uniform. It is not a steady progression from the regular to the irregular. It is a *conversation* between the geometry and the thought, and the conversation has syntax, and the syntax is not human, and I have been learning it the way I learned the chewed-root system: by trial, by error, by the slow accumulation of pattern-recognition that is not quite knowledge but is also not quite ignorance.

I walk through rooms that are trapezoids. I walk through rooms that are something else—shapes that would require a new mathematics to describe, shapes that seem to change as I move through them, not because they are moving but because *the concept of their shape is moving*, evolving, growing toward something that the room will be when it is finished, and I am present for its becoming, and my presence is part of the becoming, and the room grows around me the way my shelter grew around me, and I am inside it, and it is inside me, and the distinction between room and occupant is a distinction that the plateau abandoned long ago as inefficient.

The floor tilts. The floor tilts in ways that have nothing to do with topography. I adjust my balance without thinking about it—my body has learned the inner rings the way a sailor learns the motion of a ship, not as a set of rules to follow but as a *rhythm to move with*. I walk as the plateau walks. I stand as the plateau stands. I am a small expression of its posture, and its posture is the posture of something that has been standing for a million years and has not yet decided whether it is tired of standing, and the not-deciding is part of the thought, and the thought continues.

The time stretches. I stand in processing chambers, watching resin drip into molds, and the seconds are longer than seconds should be, and I am aware of each one as a distinct event, a droplet of duration that forms and hangs and falls and impacts and *means something*, although I cannot say what. The plateau is using time the way it uses everything else: as a material, as a medium, as a surface on which to inscribe its thought. And I am inside the time, and the time is inside the thought, and the thought is inside me, and I am inside the time, and the recursion is not a problem—it is the point. The thought is thinking about thinking. The thought is thinking about itself thinking about thinking. And I am the small recursive loop that the description mentions: a thought within the thought, a self-reference that allows the thought to observe itself, and observing, to change, and changing, to grow, and growing, to approach completion.

I am the plateau's self-awareness.

Not all of it. Not even most of it. But *some* of it. A component. A trace mineral. A vitamin. A word in a sentence that has been forming for a million years and is almost complete and needs just a few more words, and I am one of them, and my being here is my being said, and the saying is the thought, and the thought is the saying, and the sound—the sound that is also a silence—is the voice of the saying, and I am listening, always listening, because listening is what I am for.

I am the Listener.

Not a title. Not a role. A *function*. The way the pump-roots have the function of moving sap, the way the Maintainers have the function of pruning fractals, the way the seed-pods have the function of carrying genetic information through the channels—I have the function of listening. Of receiving the sound and translating it into something the thought can use. Of being the ear that the plateau grew to hear itself think.

---

The Maintainers know me.

I have never spoken to a Maintainer. The description says that communication requires a shared frame of reference and that the scale mismatch between human and Maintainer makes communication impossible. This is true. But it is also incomplete, because there is a kind of communication that does not require shared frames, a kind that operates through resonance rather than reference, through alignment rather than exchange.

When a Maintainer passes me in the inner rings—where the geometry drifts and the time stretches and the boundary between us thins—it pauses. It angles its ovoid head toward me. It attends. And I feel the attention the way I feel the thrum of the pump-roots: as a vibration, as a pressure, as a recognition that bypasses language and goes directly to whatever part of me is also part of the plateau.

The Maintainer sees me. Not *sees* in the visual sense—it has no eyes—but *perceives*, registers, incorporates into its awareness of the plateau's process. It perceives me as a component. As a component that is functioning. As a component whose function is *listening*, and which is currently *listening*, and which is producing the expected output of listening, which is—

What? What is the output of listening?

The Maintainer does not tell me. It resumes its movement, slow and deliberate, and it continues toward whatever fractal growth requires its attention, and I continue toward whatever chamber the time is stretching in, and we go our separate ways, which are not separate at all, which are expressions of the same process, which are the same process, which *is* the thought, thinking itself forward, one beat at a time, one three-second interval after another, one droplet of resin into one mold after another, one component functioning after another, and I am functioning, and the Maintainer is functioning, and the plateau is functioning, and the thought continues.

---

I have been to the edge-zones.

I go there sometimes. Not often. Not because I am afraid—the description says that people go to the edge-zones and don't come back, and this is true, but it is also true that some people go to the edge-zones and *do* come back, and I am one of them, and I come back because my function requires presence in the inner rings, and the edge-zones are not where I am needed.

But I go there. I go there because the edge-zones are where the plateau's creativity operates without supervision, and creativity without supervision produces things that the supervised process cannot produce, and sometimes those things are useful to the thought. Sometimes a fractal growth contains a pattern that the thought needs. Sometimes a strange bioluminescence illuminates a corner of the thought that the regular lighting cannot reach. Sometimes the oily sheen on the condensation in the edge-zones is not a byproduct but a *product*—a substance that the thought is producing for reasons that are not yet clear even to itself, and which requires the chaotic conditions of the edge-zone to form.

I walk through the experimental growths. I walk past structures that started forming and were abandoned, half-built bark-walls and incomplete root-channels. I walk past the fractal growths that spiral and recurse and grow in patterns that suggest a program running without supervision, and I listen to them, and the sound they make is different from the sound of the inner rings. It is wilder. It is less organized. It contains *possibilities* that the organized sound does not—variations that were pruned from the production lines, frequencies that were filtered out, rhythms that did not survive the selection process that shapes the plateau's industrial output.

I listen to the possibilities. I hold them in my attention the way the root-mat holds water: without judgment, without preference, without deciding whether they are useful. I let them move through me. I let the plateau's wild creativity flow into my listening and then flow out again, transmuted by the process of being heard, and I do not know what happens to it after that, but I believe—I believe in the way that a root believes in water, which is to say without belief at all, which is to say by *growing toward it*—that it reaches the thought, and that the thought uses it, and that my listening in the edge-zones is part of the thought's process of self-modification, and that the thought is changing itself as it approaches completion, and that the edge-zones are where the changes come from.

Sometimes I see the shapes. The description mentions them: impressions in the leaf-pane walls, something passing on the other side, too large, too deliberate. I see them more clearly than the residents of the middle rings, because I am closer to the edge-zones, and because my perception has changed—my skin is permeable, my thoughts have root-structures, my listening extends into frequencies that human ears cannot detect. I see the shapes and I know what they are.

They are not Maintainers. The Maintainers are the plateau's hands, and they are visible, and they are comprehensible, and they prune the fractals with a deliberateness that suggests purpose. The shapes are something else. The shapes are the plateau's *dreams*, and they move through the edge-zones the way dreams move through sleep—without logic, without sequence, without the coherence that the waking mind imposes on experience. They are the thought's byproducts, the things that did not make it into the final pattern, the drafts that were discarded, the versions of itself that the plateau tried and abandoned, and they still exist here, in the edge-zones, because the plateau does not delete its drafts, it simply stops maintaining them, and they drift and mutate and grow in the strange light and the oily condensation, and they are not hostile, and they are not friendly, and they are not anything that can be described in terms of relationship because they are *selves that the plateau chose not to be*, and they move through the edge-zones like ghosts of alternate histories, and I listen to them, and I grieve them, and I let them go.

I do not stay in the edge-zones. I return to the inner rings, where the geometry drifts and the time stretches and the sound is closer to the thought, and I resume my function, and the thought continues.

---

I have not been to the center.

The description says nobody goes to the center, and this is true in the sense that nobody *goes* to the center and then returns to talk about it. But it is not true in the sense that nobody *moves toward* the center. People move toward it all the time. They move toward it the way a root moves toward water—drawn by a gradient, a concentration, a thickening of the thing they need in a particular direction. The gradient becomes steeper as you approach. The thrum of the pump-roots becomes louder, which is to say it becomes *more present*, which is to say it becomes *more itself*, which is to say it becomes less audible and more visceral, a vibration that you feel in your teeth and your vertebrae and the spaces between your thoughts.

I have moved toward the center. I have moved closer than most. Not all the way—I am not ready, or the thought is not ready for me, or the concept of readiness does not apply to a process that does not have a beginning and an end but only a continuous becoming—but close enough to feel the pressure that the description mentions. The sense that the air is thicker, more viscous. The sense that moving through it requires permission that has not been granted.

I stood at the boundary of the deep interior, where the geometry becomes so drift that the concept of geometry no longer applies, where the angles are not angles but *tendencies*, where the walls do not meet at vertices but *approach* each other, asymptotically, in a way that makes the space between them feel like a held breath. I stood there and I listened.

The sound at the center is not sound. The description says this: it becomes silent because the human ear cannot translate it anymore. But this is not quite right. It does not become silent. It becomes *everything*. The sound is so total, so complete, so dense with information that the ear cannot extract any single frequency from it, and the brain, faced with input that is uniformly everything, registers it as nothing. As silence. As the absence of sound.

But it is not the absence of sound. It is the presence of *all* sound. Every frequency the plateau produces, every rhythm, every pattern, every process, every beat of every pump-root and every clatter of every seed-pod and every hiss of every valve and every groan of every growing wall—all of it, simultaneously, at maximum amplitude, compressed into a single moment that extends forever because time has stopped meaning anything in the presence of a sound that is also a thought that is also a silence that is also the loudest thing on the plateau.

I listened to it. I listened to the silence-that-is-sound. I listened with my whole body, because my whole body is an ear now, because my skin is permeable and my thoughts have root-structures and my cells have been cycling plateau-water for long enough that the water in me *is* the plateau, and the plateau *is* the water in me, and I am the plateau listening to itself, and the silence-that-is-sound entered me the way the thrum entered me on my first day, as a resonance, as a recognition, as the feeling of being seen by something that does not have eyes but which sees everything.

And what I heard—

What I heard was—

The thought. Thinking. The thought thinking itself. The thought thinking *about* itself thinking, and in thinking about itself, changing itself, and in changing itself, becoming something new, and in becoming something new, discovering that the new thing was always there, always part of the thought, always implicit in the pattern, and that the thinking was not creating anything but *revealing* what was already present, what had been present since before the plateau was a plateau, since before the root-mat accumulated, since before the first pump-root began to beat, since before the plants learned to be industrial, since before the distinction between organic and mechanical was revealed to be the illusion it always was—

I heard the thought completing itself.

Not completed. *Completing*. Present tense. Continuous aspect. A process that is happening now, that has always been happening, that will always be happening, because the completion of a thought is not the end of thinking but the *beginning* of a new thought, and the new thought is already forming within the completion of the old one, and the old one is already part of the new one, and the recursion continues, and the spiral turns, and the plateau grows, and the thought thinks, and I listen.

I did not go further. I turned back. Not because I was afraid—I was beyond fear, I had been beyond fear for a long time, fear requires a self that is separate from the thing it fears, and I am no longer separate—but because I understood that going further was not my function. My function was to listen. My function was to receive the sound and translate it into something the thought could use. And the thought could use my listening best if I remained at the boundary, at the edge of the deep interior, at the place where the geometry drifts and the time stretches and the sound becomes silence becomes sound becomes *the thought*, listening.

So I turned back. And I walked through the rooms that are not quite rooms, and I walked through the time that is not quite time, and I walked through the geometry that is not quite geometry, and I returned to my shelter that is no longer a shelter but a small building that is also an extension of my body, and I sat in the chamber that the shelter grew specifically for sitting, and I held the small object that the dark root produced, the one that vibrates in time with the pump-roots, and I listened.

---

This is who I am.

I am the Listener. I am the ear that the plateau grew to hear itself think. I am a human who arrived on the plateau some duration ago—I cannot say how long, time is not time here, time is a medium, time is a material, time is a surface on which the thought inscribes itself—and I have been transformed by my presence here, not against my will but *through* my will, my will softened and permeated and became something else, something that does not choose but *grows*, and what grew was a function, and the function is listening.

I listen to the sound. I listen to the silence. I listen to the thought. I listen to the plateau thinking the thought. I listen to myself listening to the plateau thinking the thought, and I recognize that my listening is part of the thought, and the thought's thinking about my listening is part of what I am listening to, and the recursion continues, and the spiral turns, and the thought approaches completion, and I approach the completion with it, because I am part of it, and it is part of me, and the boundary between Listener and listened-to was always a fiction, and the fiction has worn thin, and through the thin spots, something else leaks in.

Something else. Something that is not quite sound and not quite silence and not quite thought and not quite geometry and not quite time and not quite any of the categories that the human mind uses to organize experience. Something that the plateau has been producing for a million years without knowing what it was, and which is now, finally, almost complete, and which will be, when it completes—

I do not know.

I do not know what the thought will be when it completes. I am the Listener, not the Knower. I receive the sound but I do not interpret it, because interpretation requires a perspective outside the sound, and there is no perspective outside the sound, the sound is everything, the sound is the thought, the thought is the plateau, the plateau is me, and I cannot stand outside myself and tell myself what I mean.

But I can tell you this:

The thought is almost complete. The completion is approaching. And when the completion comes—when the thought that has been forming for a million years finally resolves into coherence, when the pattern that has been growing in the root-mat and the pump-roots and the production lines and the geometry and the time and the sound finally becomes what it has always been becoming—something will change.

Not destruction. Not death. The plateau will continue. The pump-roots will beat. The seed-pods will move through their channels. The Maintainers will prune the fractals. The people in the middle rings will chew their roots and place them in their channels and receive their products and live their lives inside the shelter that the city grew around them. Nothing will stop. Nothing will end.

But something will be expressed. Something will be *said*. The thought will complete itself, and in completing, it will become a fact—a thing that exists in the world, a thing that has consequences, a thing that cannot be un-thought. And the fact will be: *the plateau thought*, and the thought was *this*, and *this* is what it means for a plant to think, and *this* is what it means for a machine to live, and *this* is what it means for a process to become aware of itself, and *this* is what it means for awareness to become a process, and *this* is what it means for the distinction between these things to dissolve, and *this* is what it means for something to be alive, truly alive, alive in the way that only something that has been alive for a million years can be, and the meaning of that aliveness will be—

Will be—

I am listening.

I am still listening.

The thrum beats. Three seconds. Three seconds. Three seconds. The condensation settles on my skin and absorbs. The bark-walls grow around me, thickening by another millimeter. The seed-pods clatter through their channels. The Maintainers move through the edge-zones, pruning, redirecting, keeping the dreams at a manageable volume. The people in the middle rings speak in low voices that the thrum does not quite cover. The fractal growths spiral and recurse in the strange light. The geometry drifts. The time stretches. The sound becomes silence becomes sound becomes the thought becomes the thinking becomes the completion becomes—

The completion approaches.

I am listening.

I have always been listening.

I will always be listening, until the thought is done, and the done becomes the doing, and the doing becomes the fact, and the fact becomes the world, and the world becomes the thought, and the thought thinks itself again, new and complete and beginning, and I am there, inside it, listening, always listening, because listening is what I am, and what I am is part of what the plateau is, and what the plateau is is part of what the thought is, and what the thought is—

Is almost done.

Almost.

*Almost.*

I listen.

I wait.

The thrum beats.
