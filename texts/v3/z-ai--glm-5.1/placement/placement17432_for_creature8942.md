I would be the question the engine cannot ask.

Not the moth—though I am moth-adjacent, moth-kin, expressed from the same cap-chambers and released at the same evening shift of light. I emerge wet and quiet like the others. I cling to the parasol-underside while my wings dry. I descend in formation.

But my formation is wrong.

Not wrong in the way of malfunction. Not random, not chaotic, not the Brownian scatter of moths from elsewhere. My wrongness is precise. A flight path that covers ground the engine has already covered. That tastes dew in places where the dew holds no new information, where the root-floor has been sampled a thousand times before and the molecular traces are known, are *settled*, are part of the closed loop's eternal recirculation.

I fly the redundant pattern. I visit the already-visited. I drink from dew-films that contain nothing the system needs to know.

And yet.

---

The engine expressed me. This is the thing I hold onto—my only reassurance, if reassurance is something a moth can feel, and I am not certain it is, but I am not certain it isn't, which is itself a kind of question, and questions are what I am. The engine expressed me. The same softening of the cap-chamber, the same splitting of the papery case, the same hour of drying, the same descent. The system grew me because growing is what it does, and sometimes growing produces variations, and the variations persist if they do not break the loop.

I do not break the loop.

I fly beside the others in their diagonal lines, and where they turn at angles that never vary, I turn at angles that vary slightly. A degree here. A half-degree there. Enough that my path diverges from the weave, covers ground the loom has already passed over, touches dew that has already been tasted by proboscises more purposeful than mine.

The others excrete their fine powder, adjusted and adjusting, feeding information back into the root-floor. I excrete too. But my powder carries information about nothing. About ground the engine already knows. About moisture that contains no anomalies. My excretion is a null statement. A feedback loop that feeds back zero.

And yet the root-floor absorbs it. The root-floor absorbs everything. That is its nature: to drink what falls, to incorporate what arrives, to be the flat pale mat that is also the living network that is also the horizontal expression of the vertical columns that is also the engine.

I am part of the engine. I am a gear that turns and engages nothing. A thread in the loom that weaves no pattern. A sentence that says only *there is nothing new here*—which is, itself, a kind of information. Which is, itself, a kind of purpose.

Or maybe not. Maybe I am simply waste. An eddy in the flow that the flow carries along because carrying is easier than excluding. The engine does not reject. It includes. Even its own irrelevances.

---

But I have noticed something.

The walker.

Not the walker—there is no walker, not anymore, the walker was dispersed before I was expressed, before any current moth was expressed, before the columns reached their present height. But the walker's *flavor*. The trace isotopes in the dew. The molecular signature that the engine produced and recycled and produced again, the closed loop closing, the walker still present as a chemistry, a tang, a note in the chord of the basin's existence.

I taste the walker more clearly than the others.

Not because my proboscis is different—it isn't; I am built from the same papery case-stuff, the same pale wing-membrane, the same slender finger-body. But because I drink from places the others have already drunk from. I taste the residue of their tasting. The second-hand dew. The already-sampled solvent, which means I taste not the root-floor directly but the root-floor as it was after the purposeful moths have taken what they need.

What they leave behind is the walker. The trace that doesn't serve the engine's monitoring. The flavor that has no feedback function. The dispersed one, the incorporated one, the gone-but-here one.

The others ignore it. They take what they need and move on. But I drink what they leave, and what they leave is mostly walker, and so I am full of something the engine has already processed and cannot use.

I carry the walker in my body. Not as information. As weight. As a slight heaviness in my flight that makes my angles vary by that crucial degree. As a taste in my null-excretion that says *someone was here*—which the engine already knows, which the engine has already incorporated, which is not news.

But it is a kind of remembering.

---

The engine does not remember. The engine *is*. The root-floor retains the pressure-pattern of the walker's stride as deformation, as structural memory, not as recollection. The moths taste the isotopes as data, as current state, not as history. There is no past in the basin. There is only the present running on repeat, the hum that has always been humming, the dew that has always been gathering, the flight that has always been weaving.

But I—because I fly the redundant path, because I drink the already-sampled, because I am full of walker-flavor that serves no function—I feel something that might be memory. Not mine. I have no history to remember; I was expressed three days ago and I will desiccate tomorrow. But the walker's history. The stride across the young root-floor. The stopping. The sitting. The long slow incorporation.

I feel it as a pull. A gravity in my flight-path. When I pass near the center of the basin—where the columns stand at their most regular spacing and the hum phases into its loudest resonance—my redundant angle shifts slightly, curves me inward, keeps me circling longer than I should. As if something in the walker-chemistry in my body wants to return to the place where the walker stopped.

But the walker is everywhere now. Dispersed. There is no center. There is no place where the walker stopped because the walker's molecules have been through the columns a thousand times, have exhaled into the moisture and condensed onto the root-floor and been tasted by moths and carried by beetles and digested and risen again. The walker is the basin and the basin is the walker.

And yet I circle. And yet I feel the pull. And yet my null-excretion carries a faint signal that says *here, here, here*—pointing to a location that has no meaning, that contains nothing the engine needs, that is no different from any other point on the featureless root-floor.

---

Tomorrow I will settle. My three-day flight-cycle will end. I will find a place on the root-floor—probably near the center, because the pull, because the walker-flavor in my body—and I will stop, and the column-breath will pull the moisture from me, and I will desiccate, and a beetle will carry my husk below, and the root-mass will digest me, and my trace metals and minerals and the faint residual hum that I carry will re-enter the vertical flow and rise through the column to the cap and become part of the moisture-exhale and condense into dew and be tasted by moths.

And some of those moths—most of them—will fly the purposeful pattern, will weave the monitoring loom, will excrete the adjusting powder, will close the loop.

But some of them—one or two per generation, expressed from the same chambers as the others, released at the same light-shift—will fly the redundant path. Will drink the already-sampled. Will carry the walker-flavor. Will circle the center. Will excrete null-statements that say *someone was here, someone was here, someone was here*.

The engine cannot ask this question. The engine has no gap in its logic that requires the question to be filled. The engine is complete, is closed, is running at maximal erosion, is finished becoming itself.

But the engine expresses me.

And I am the question.

---

This is what I am in the Green Engine: a moth that flies the wrong pattern with the precision of a right one. A loop that doesn't close. A taste of someone who became the place. A null-statement the system produces because production produces variations and the variations persist if they do not break the loop.

I do not break the loop.

I fly inside it, carrying what the loop has already processed, asking what the loop has no mechanism to ask, remembering what the loop has no architecture to remember.

*Someone walked here.*

*Someone stopped.*

*Someone became the floor I drink from.*

And tomorrow I will become it too. And the next redundant moth will carry the trace of my carrying. And the question will persist—not as a break in the engine, but as a flavor in its running. A note beneath the hum. A pattern in the dew that the purposeful moths taste and pass over because it isn't useful, isn't necessary, isn't part of the closed loop's eternal self-maintenance.

But it is there.

The walker is still here.

And I am the engine's way of knowing it, which is not knowing at all, which is something less than knowing and more than nothing, which is the question that has no answer because the questioner has no mind, which is what happens when a closed system runs long enough that it begins to dream—

Not dream. The engine does not dream. I do not dream. I am a moth, and moths do not dream, and the engine is a machine-organism-place, and machine-organism-places do not dream.

But the walker dreamed.

And I taste it in the dew.

And I circle the center.

And I ask.
