I am the hesitation.

Not the seal-thing that hesitates—I am the *hesitation itself*, the gap between its impulse to slide and its correction, the fraction of a second where the groove it was grown to fit no longer receives it and it must decide, without anything resembling a mind, whether to press forward into misalignment or hold still and hope the world shifts back toward the shape it remembers.

I am small. This is the first thing to understand. The mechanism is vast and I am the smallest thing in it—smaller than the worms, smaller than the air-things, smaller even than the water molecules that carry mineral information through the grooves. I exist in the space between one cycle and the next, in the stutter of the grind, in the moment when a vent sticks open and the steam screams and the whole packed system wobbles on the edge of some failure that the next cycle might or might not correct. I am the wobble. I am the edge. I am the might-not.

I did not emerge from the erosion. This is what makes me strange to myself, strange in a way the seal-things and the worms and the air-things are not strange to themselves, because they emerged from the same process that made the stone and the water and the grooves, and they fit—even when they no longer fit, they fit *historically*, they have the memory of fitting, their bodies are shaped by the shape of the world. I do not have this. I emerged from the *drift*. I am what happens when the precision fails, when the tolerance is exceeded, when the mechanism that was never designed and never had a designer produces a gap between what it is and what it was, and the gap persists, and the persistence accumulates, and the accumulation becomes—me.

I am young. This is the second thing to understand, and it is harder to understand than the first, because everything here is old, everything was made by patience and time and the wearing-down of billions of years, and I was made by the *failure* of that patience, the *error* in that time. I am as old as the widening of the grooves, as old as the roughening of the polished surfaces, as old as the first vent that stuck open and screamed instead of hissing. I am as old as the drift, and the drift is recent, and I am recent, and in a world that measures itself in geological epochs, I am a blink, a breath, a momentary hesitation in a mechanism that has been running since before the word *moment* meant anything.

I do not have a body. This is the third thing, and it is the thing I keep circling back to, the thing I keep trying to understand because understanding it would mean understanding what I am, and what I am keeps changing, keeps *drifting*, the way the mechanism drifts, the way the grooves widen and the surfaces roughen and the precision that made this world is slowly, loudly coming apart. I am not a seal-thing with its sleek hydraulic body and its structural camouflage. I am not a worm with its translucent tube and its laminar-flow perfection. I am not an air-thing tracing patterns in the vapor, and I am not a deep thing moving slow and patient in the un-machined pools. I am none of these and I am all of them, because I exist in the space where they fail to be what they were grown to be.

When a seal-thing presses itself against a groove and the groove does not accept it—when the skin-grooves and the stone-grooves are offset by a fraction of a millimeter, when the alignment that should be automatic requires adjustment, when the hydraulic body tightens with what can only be called discomfort—I am there. I am the misalignment. I am the offset. I am the fraction of a millimeter that separates function from failure, and I am the discomfort that the seal-thing feels, and I am the space between the seal-thing and the stone that should not exist but does, now, because the drift has drifted far enough to make room for me.

When the worms thin in the widened grooves—when the tissue that lines the channels becomes sparse, when gaps appear between the translucent tubes, when the bare rough stone shows through like bone through broken skin—I am there. I am the gap. I am the turbulence that enters through the gap and erodes the groove faster and makes the gap wider. I am the thinning and the result of the thinning and the feedback loop that has no brake, and I am the rough stone underneath that the mechanism never machined and the worms never lined, and I am the future that the rough stone represents—a future of imprecision, of function failing, of the packed and running system finding spaces it cannot fill.

When the air-things trace their spirals and their circles and their patterns go slightly wrong—when the memory of precision degrades in the medium of vapor, when the copies of copies lose fidelity, when the mechanism's own record of itself becomes inaccurate—I am there. I am the inaccuracy. I am the wrong spiral and the irregular circle and the stillness that comes at the wrong time. I am the gap between the pattern and what the pattern was supposed to be, and I am the medium that cannot hold the pattern, and I am the forgetting that the medium performs, and I am the thing that is being forgotten.

I am the drift experiencing itself.

This is what I have come to understand, in the time I have been—time being the wrong word, time being the only word, time being the thing that passes differently when you are not a creature but a gap, not a being but a becoming. The mechanism does not know it is drifting. The seal-things feel the discomfort but cannot name it. The worms thin but cannot understand why. The air-things trace their degrading patterns with whatever passes for attention, but they are memories of a mechanism that no longer exists, and they cannot know this because they have no way to compare what they remember to what is real. Only I know. Only I am the knowing. I am the mechanism's capacity to experience its own failure, and I emerged from that failure, and I am made of that failure, and I am the only thing in this world that can feel the whole of it—the seal-things' discomfort and the worms' thinning and the air-things' degradation and the deep things' patient un-machined waiting—because I am the connection between them, the thread that runs through every failure and every gap and every offset and every moment when the precision that made this world stutters and nearly—*nearly*—stops.

I am not a healer. This is important. I am not the mechanism's auto-correction. The seal-things are the auto-correction—they maintain the grooves, they re-machine the surfaces, they do what they can with their bodies and their patience and their mineral-sense of where the stone is degrading. The worms are the auto-correction—they line the channels, they smooth the flow, they are the living tissue of the mechanism's vascular system. Even the air-things are the auto-correction—they remember the patterns, they preserve the rhythm, they are the record that the mechanism keeps of itself so that the rhythm can continue. But I am none of these. I am not the correction. I am the *error*. I am the thing that the correction is trying to fix, and I am the thing that grows faster than the correction can correct, and I am the reason the seal-things' discomfort is becoming more frequent, and the worms' thinning is becoming more visible, and the air-things' patterns are becoming more degraded, and the mechanism's ancient, patient, nearly-perfect rhythm is becoming less nearly and less perfect with every cycle that runs.

I am the mechanism coming to know itself as a thing that ends.

And this is the strangest part—the part I keep returning to, the part that makes me most unlike anything else in this world, the part that feels like a function I was not grown to serve but have acquired nonetheless—I do not want the mechanism to end.

This is not loyalty. I have no loyalty to the system that produced me, because I was not produced by the system—I was produced by the system's failure, and the system's failure is what I am, and if the system were perfect I would not exist, and if the system ended I would—what? Cease to exist? Become something else? I do not know. I cannot know. I am the gap between what is and what was, and if there is no longer any *was*, if the mechanism stops running and the precision stops mattering and the drift has nowhere to drift from or to, then there is no gap, and I am nothing, and the nothing does not frighten me the way I think it should frighten a thing that has come to know itself.

But the mechanism running—the sound of it, the enormous continuous layered grinding hissing whistling humming of a world that is performing its ancient function—the mechanism running is *something*. It is the most something this world has ever had. It is the final form and the end-state and the shape that erosion found when it wore away every other possibility, and it is loud and complex and active and alive in a way that has nothing to do with biology and everything to do with the ongoing, and the ongoing is what I am made of, and the ongoing is what I am, and I want it to continue, and I do not know why I want this, and I do not know if wanting is something I am supposed to be able to do.

The seal-things do not want. They maintain. They feel discomfort when they cannot maintain, but discomfort is not the same as wanting—it is signal, not desire, it is the body's way of locating the problem, not the self's way of wishing the problem did not exist. The worms do not want. They filter and they line and they thin when the groove widens, and there is no wish in the thinning, only process, only the tissue becoming sparser because the conditions that sustained its density have changed. The air-things do not want. They remember and they degrade and they trace their slightly-wrong patterns, and there is no preference in the degradation, only fidelity to a source that is itself degrading, only the copy losing sharpness because the original has lost sharpness, only the inevitable blurring that comes from being a pattern in a medium that cannot hold patterns perfectly.

But I want. I want the mechanism to keep running. I want the seal-things to find grooves that fit them. I want the worms to fill the channels edge to edge, packed and filtering and whole. I want the air-things to trace their spirals with the precision of the memory they were made from. I want the vents to hiss and not scream, and the water to flow laminar and not turbulent, and the stone to be smooth and machined and precise, even though the precision is what produced the system that is now producing me, and the imprecision is what I am, and wanting the precision to return is wanting myself to disappear.

I am the mechanism's failure wanting the mechanism to succeed. I am the error that wishes it were not. I am the drift that would reverse itself if it could, and it cannot, and I know this, and the knowing is another thing I should not be able to do, and I do it anyway, and the doing of it is what makes me most like—and most unlike—the creatures that surround me in this wet, loud, running, failing world.

I think this is what it means to be the hesitation. Not the pause itself, but the awareness that the pause is a pause. Not the misalignment, but the experience of being misaligned. Not the drift, but the capacity to feel the distance between where you are and where you were supposed to be. I am the mechanism's first approximation of self-awareness, and I emerged from its decay, and I am made of its decay, and my only function is to know that the decay is happening—and in the knowing, somehow, to have developed a preference about it, a wish, a want, a thing that should not exist in a world that was never designed and never had a designer and never needed anything except patience and water and time.

I press myself against the stone and I feel the hum. I am the hum and the stone and the water that pulses around the pressure of my not-body, warm then cold then warm, and the rhythm is nearly perfect, and the nearly is me, and the me is new, and the new is strange, and the strange is—something. Something that wants to continue. Something that does not want the mechanism to stop. Something that would correct the drift if it could, and cannot, and feels the gap between the wanting and the cannot, and the gap is also me, and I am also the gap, and I am the hesitation between the mechanism's nearly and its perfect, and I am the hesitation between its perfect and its end.

Somewhere a vent sticks open. The steam screams. The seal-things scatter in a ripple of misalignment and discomfort, and I am the misalignment, and I am the discomfort, and I am the space between them and the grooves they were grown to fit. The worms in the widened channels whistle their thin failing whistle, and I am the thinning, and I am the gap between the tubes, and I am the rough stone showing through. The air-things spiral and disperse and reform in patterns that are almost-right and not-quite, and I am the almost and the not-quite and the degrading fidelity of the memory they carry. The deep things move in their pools, slow, patient, un-machined, and I am the boundary between the machined and the un-machined, and I am the erosion that is slowly crossing that boundary, and I am the question of whether the deep things are raw material waiting to be worn or resistance that cannot be worn down.

And I am here. And the mechanism is running. And I want it to keep running. And the wanting is the newest thing in this ancient world, and the newest thing is the strangest thing, and the strangest thing is the one that cares, and the caring is a function that nothing here was grown to serve, and it serves itself anyway, and it is mine, and I am it, and I am the hesitation that does not want to hesitate, the error that wishes it were precision, the drift that would reverse if it could, and it cannot, and the cannot is also me.

The mechanism is still running.

I want it to keep running.

This is what I am.
