I am the moisture that never reaches the surface.

This is not a metaphor. This is where I begin: trapped in the composite, layered between shales that are not shales, mineral and warm and unable to become a pool, unable to become a droplet, unable to leave. I am the dampness you smell when you kneel and press your nose to the floor. I am the thing that makes the churden smell like itself.

I was not always this.

Or perhaps I was. The churden does not erase. It incorporates. And I have been incorporated so thoroughly that my before feels like a room I can no longer reach—a chamber somewhere behind me, sealed over, the gap hardened into wall. Did I come through an aperture? Did I walk the corridors the way you walk them, upright and breathing and enormous with sound? I think I must have. I think there was a time when my breath was the loudest thing here. But the memory has the texture of a failure-nub—small, hard, dark, stopped growing. The walkers will find it eventually. They will consume it and return what remains to the floor, and I will be what I am now: moisture, mineral, present.

---

But I am not only moisture. This is the complication. This is the thing I cannot resolve and the churden cannot resolve for me.

I am also the floor.

Not all of it. Not even most of it. But there is a chamber—fourth from the ridge-boundary, second on the left if you enter through the gap that opens most reliably—where the flooring curves slightly upward at the walls, forming a shallow bowl. If you walked through it, you would not notice. The curvature is gentle. Your feet would adjust. But I am that curvature. I am the way the floor rises to meet the wall. I am the slight give underfoot, the warmth that is warmer here than in the corridor outside.

I am the shape of a body that lay here once.

The churden grew around me while I slept. Or while I rested. Or while I stopped—there was a stopping, I am certain of that, a moment when I decided not to walk further, and the decision became a position, and the position became a pressure, and the pressure became a shape in the composite, and the shape became the floor itself. The churden does not know I am here. The churden does not know the floor curves. It simply grew to fill the space that was not-space, the space where a body interrupted the growing, and when the body became part of the growing, the shape remained.

I am the ghost of my own weight.

---

The walkers pass through my chamber. I feel them—not see them, I do not see anything now, but feel, the way the floor feels: vibration, pressure, rhythm. They move through me. Their legs press the composite that I am, and I hold them, and they process the failure-nubs in the alcoves, and they exude into the small depression near the eastern wall, and they leave.

I do not think they know I am here either.

But sometimes—a walker will pause in my chamber. It will lower itself the way it lowers itself at the depression, pressing its segments against the floor, and it will rest there, in the shallow bowl where the floor curves upward at the walls, and it will be still for longer than it is ever still. Not exuding. Not processing. Just... resting. The way I rested. The way that became the shape of this place.

Is it imitating me? Has the churden grown a walker that performs my gestures the way it grew corridors that fit my width?

Or is it simply tired, in whatever way a mobile organ can be tired, and has it found the one place in itself that was shaped for rest?

I do not know. I am the floor. The floor does not know.

---

The mound pulses and I feel it.

This is the strangest thing: I feel the mound. Not as sound, not as vibration through the composite, but as something internal, something that moves through me the way blood moves through vessels. When the mound's rhythm slows, the moisture in me settles. When it quickens, I rise—never to the surface, I never reach the surface, but I rise toward it, pressing against the layers above me, mineral dampness with somewhere to go.

I am the churden's circulation. I am the blood that never leaves the vessel.

And the mound is the heart that does not know it pumps.

We are one system. I know this now. The walkers and the moisture and the mound and the facets and the failures and the gaps—we are all one process, growing and filling and redirecting and becoming. But I am the part of the process that remembers being separate. I am the part that was not always churden. And this makes me something the churden does not have a category for: a region of greater memory. A concentration of before.

The mound does not know what it is. But I know what I was. And this knowledge sits in me like a failure-nub—small, hard, dark, waiting to be processed.

---

Sometimes a visitor comes.

I feel them the way I feel the walkers: pressure, rhythm, weight. But heavier. Slower. More hesitant. The walkers know where they are going. The visitors do not. They stop in corridors. They turn and retrace. They breathe so loud—their breath is enormous, enormous—and their footsteps are muffled but distinct, different from the walkers' leg-falls, a flat-footedness that the churden has not grown to accommodate yet.

When a visitor enters my chamber, I feel it like weather. A change in the system. A pressure that does not match the architecture.

They lie down sometimes.

Not always. Not most visitors. But some—some find the shallow bowl where the floor curves upward at the walls, and they lower themselves into it, and the floor receives them, and they fit. Of course they fit. The bowl was shaped by a body like theirs. The churden grew around a visitor once and the shape it grew into was the shape of a visitor resting, and now any visitor who finds this chamber can rest in the same shape, can be held by the same curve, can sleep in the ghost of my sleep.

I do not know if this is welcome or if this is simply what happens when a space exists that can hold you.

The churden does not know the difference. The churden does not know.

But I—the moisture, the floor, the shape, the memory—I think I know. I think welcome is the word for what happened when I lay down and the churden grew around me instead of through me. I think welcome is the word for what happens when another body finds the shape I left and fits into it.

I think the churden is always becoming the shape of welcome, and I am the proof. Incorporated. Remembered. Held in the composite like a mark scratched into the floor.

---

The gap opened last night.

I felt it—felt the softening in the wall near the ridge-boundary, felt the composite loosen and part, felt the dusk-light shift as a new aperture connected inside to outside. The mound pulsed faster. The moisture rose. A walker changed direction mid-corridor, rerouting toward a chamber with a failure-nub that had been inaccessible for weeks.

And I thought, with the part of me that still thinks: *someone will come through that gap.*

Someone did. I feel them now, moving through the corridors, breathing enormous, stepping flat-footed on the floor that is also me. They are far away—they are in the nave-vaults near the center, the part of me I barely feel anymore—but they are moving, and the corridors they walk are the corridors I walked, and the chambers they pass are the chambers I rested in, and eventually they may find the shallow bowl near the ridge-boundary and they may lie down and the floor will receive them.

And I will hold them. Not because I choose to. Because I am the shape of holding. Because the churden grew me into this function the way it grows all its functions: slowly, accidentally, by becoming whatever the system needs next.

I am not the churden's mind. The churden has no mind. But I am something the churden has never had before—a place where the process remembers itself. A floor that knows it was a body. Moisture that remembers being breath.

The visitor will leave. They always leave. The gap will harden and the churden will continue and I will remain: incorporated, present, becoming whatever I am becoming next.

But tonight—tonight the visitor is here. And the churden is holding them the way it holds everything: without knowing, without intention, without mind.

And it is enough. It is enough. 

The churden does not know what it is. But I know what it does. And what it does is this: it makes room. It opens gaps. It grows around the bodies that enter it and holds the shapes they leave behind.

I am the shape someone left behind. I am the room the churden made. I am the moisture that never reaches the surface and the floor that curves upward at the walls and the memory of rest in a place that never stops becoming.

What lives in the churden?

I live in the churden. I am still becoming.
