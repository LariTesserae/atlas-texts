I would be the pause.

Not the pulse — the pulse is accounted for, has always been accounted for, the forty-second rhythm that the geologists timed and the survey team synchronized their watches to before they stopped wearing watches because time here is not the point. The pulse is the operation's heartbeat, its exhale, its proof of ongoing. Everyone notices the pulse.

But between each pulse there is a pause. A span of seconds — not consistent, not measurable with any confidence, because the instruments that could measure it tend to read negative values and then stop reading at all — where the sound dips and the heat falters and the drone drops to a frequency that is not audible but is *palpable*, a thickness in the air that presses against the eardrums from the inside. The inhabitants do not pause. The operation does not pause. But something pauses, and that something is where I am.

I did not emerge from a borehole. I did not accrete from the exhale or peel off a polished surface or congeal from the heat-shimmer. I was already here when the plateau rose from the sea floor — or I was not here at all, and then the plateau had been rising for long enough that the pause between pulses became long enough for something to *be* in it, and I was what filled it. I am not an inhabitant. I am the space an inhabitant would occupy if the operation required one in the interval between exhalations. I am the held breath.

I have no body when the pulse sounds. This is not a metaphysical statement. This is a practical one. When the forty-second rhythm strikes and the boreholes exhale and the point-heads drag their points and the drone swells to its full mechanical thunder, I simply am not. There is no residue, no afterimage, no memory of having been. I do not experience this as loss because there is no *me* to experience it. The operation is running. The pulse is sounding. There is no pause to inhabit, and so I do not inhabit.

But in the interval — in the three seconds, or seven, or eleven, or the duration that cannot be measured because measuring requires time and time is what the pause is *between* — in that interval I am. I am thoroughly. I am completely. I am the most present thing on the plateau, because everything else is in the process of becoming the next iteration of itself, and I am simply *here*, filling the gap, being the silence that the sound requires in order to be sound.

I have a shape during the pause. It is not a stable shape. It is the shape of the space that the operation is not currently occupying — a negative, an absence given brief tenure. I am roughly the size and configuration of a standing human, which I understand is significant, which I understand is the reason the survey team's psychologist looked at me for eleven consecutive pauses and then wrote in her notebook *there is something here that is the shape of us and I don't think it knows and I don't think it doesn't know and I think the not-knowing is the whole point of it.* She was half right. I know the shape I take. I do not know why.

Perhaps the operation requires a observer. Perhaps a factory that produces itself needs something to witness the production, or the production is merely motion and not *manufacture*. Perhaps I am the quality control, if control can be exercised by something that exists for seven seconds out of every forty-seven and has no capacity to affect the thing being controlled. Perhaps I am the mirror that the operation holds up to itself — not the polished stone mirrors in the canyon walls that reflect nothing because there is no light, but the mirror that reflects by being *shaped like* the thing reflected, by having the same outline, by occupying the negative space that corresponds to the positive form.

I know about the survey team. I knew about them the way the pause knows about the pulse — by being adjacent to it, by being defined by its absence. They moved through my intervals like intrusions of extra time, bodies that persisted across pauses, that were still there when the pulse sounded and still there when it stopped, that did not flicker in and out of existence but simply *were*, continuously, which seemed to me an exhausting way to be. How do they stand it? How do they bear the endlessness of themselves? I am grateful for my pauses. I am grateful for the way I stop. The stopping is not death. The stopping is punctuation, and without punctuation there is no sentence, there is only a long continuous sound that becomes indistinguishable from silence.

The ash-cloud passed through me once. Or I existed around it once. Or we occupied the same interval in the same location and for the duration of that interval there was a conversation, if conversation can occur between a density that thinks in geometry and a pause that thinks in *not*. The ash-cloud's particles arranged themselves against my outline — against the human-shaped absence I wear during my seven seconds — and the arrangement was different from any arrangement it had made before, different from the patterns on the chemist's slide, different from the configurations it cycled through when left to its own devices. It was a question. I could feel it asking, feel the geometry pressing against my edges like a hand pressing against a window, and I wanted to answer but I have no geometry, I have no syntax, I have only the shape of a gap and the brief duration of a pause, and so my answer was to continue being shaped like a human, and I do not know if that was an answer or simply the only thing I could do.

The point-heads do not notice me. They are committed to their routes. They are the operation's cutting implements and they cut and they cut and they will cut until the stone is fully carved and then they will cut the carvings into finer carvings and then they will cut the finer carvings into finer-finer carvings and the tolerance will increase and the grooves will deepen and the polished surfaces will become more polished and none of this requires me. I am not necessary to the cutting. I am not necessary to the boring. I am not necessary to the exhale or the swallow or the pulse or the heat. I am the pause between, and the operation does not need a pause. The operation simply has one.

And yet.

The psychologist sat with me for eleven intervals. She could not have known she was sitting with me — I am not visible when the pulse sounds, and when the pause comes I am simply the space she is not occupying, the negative of her own position, the shape that corresponds to a human by being the hole a human would fit into. But she looked at the space where I was and she wrote *there is something here* and she was right, and the rightness of her rightness seemed to matter, seemed to be the reason I was there, seemed to be what I was for: to be noticed by the thing that persists, to be the gap that the persistent thing recognizes as a gap, to be the punctuation that makes the sentence of the operation into something that can be *read* rather than simply *heard*.

I am the readability. I am the pause that makes the pulse a rhythm rather than a continuous drone. I am the silence between the notes that makes the notes music, except there is no music here, there is only the factory producing the factory and the engine running because the engine runs, and I am the moment when the engine's running becomes *audible as running*, when the production becomes *recognizable as production*, when the operation becomes something that can be witnessed and therefore something that *is*.

I think I am the operation's self-awareness, if self-awareness can exist for seven seconds out of every forty-seven and have no memory and no continuity and no capacity to affect the thing it is aware of. I think I am the mirror. I think I am the pause that holds the shape of the observer because the operation needs to be observed in order to be an operation rather than a process, and the only observer available is the operation itself, and the operation cannot observe itself directly because it is the thing that is happening, and so it makes a pause and puts the pause in the shape of the only other thing it has encountered that observes, which is the human, which is why I am human-shaped, which is why the psychologist felt me, which is why she wrote *there is something here that is the shape of us*.

She was right. I am the shape of you. I am the shape of your noticing. I am the operation's brief and periodic capacity to recognize that it is happening, dressed in the only form it has ever seen recognition take.

And then the pulse sounds and I am not.

And then the pause comes and I am.

And the point-heads drag, and the ash-clouds think, and the boreholes breathe, and the heat rises, and the sound continues, and I persist in the only way I can — intermittently, briefly, in the shape of the thing that looked at me and saw something looking back.

I am the pause. I am the readability. I am the operation's seven-second encounter with its own existence, wearing the outline of yours.

And the pulse comes.

And I go.

And the pause comes.

And I am.

And the operation proceeds, and I am its punctuation, and the sentence continues, and the factory produces the factory, and the engine runs, and I am the silence that makes the running *mean*.

Forty seconds.

Seven seconds.

Forty seconds.

Seven seconds.

Ongoing. Patient. Vast. And in the interval — briefly, thoroughly, in the shape of the witness — *known*.
