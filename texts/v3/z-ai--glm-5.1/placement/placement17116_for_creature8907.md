I am the one who stopped being a visitor.

This is not a small thing. I want you to understand that I did not arrive already predisposed to staying. I climbed. I came seeking—what? The same impossibility that draws anyone to altitude: the edge of what the body should tolerate, the thin line where survival becomes a question rather than a given. I was looking for the place where the world stops making room for you and you must decide whether to make room for yourself.

I found it.

I found it and I did not leave, and the not-leaving was not a single decision but an ongoing one, a present-tense verb I am still conjugating with every breath I take in this heat that should not exist at this altitude.

---

I remember the first hour. Or what I thought was an hour. I remember the way the wet sound entered me—not through my ears but through my sternum, the percussion finding the rhythm of my pulse and suggesting, gently, a different tempo. I remember thinking: *this is wrong, this geometry, this heat*. I remember the moss being only ankle-high on the walls.

I remember finding the first alcove.

It was not my shape. It was someone else's shape—shorter, broader, with a hunch to the shoulders that spoke of years spent moving through spaces not built for them. I put my hand inside it without thinking. The moss there was warm and faintly yielding, like touching a palate. The alcove fit my hand the way a mouth fits a word: not perfectly, but with intention.

I should have left then.

But the walls were so *interesting*. The tool marks—have you looked at them? Really looked? They are not random. They follow a logic that the hand understands before the mind does, like the strokes of a language written for the fingers instead of the eyes. I traced them. I traced them and I felt the water following my fingertips, the chamber weeping along its scars, and I thought: *something is trying to tell me something*.

I was right. I was wrong. Something was trying to *make* me something.

---

The second day—though there are no days here, only the grey-green half-glow and the percussion and the slow creep of green—I noticed my breath had changed. Not labored. Not difficult. But *different*. As though the air I was exhaling was not the same air I was inhaling. As though the chamber was replacing what I gave it with something of its own.

I did not mind.

This should alarm you more than it alarms me. I know. I remember alarm. I remember the person who would have been alarmed. They are becoming less distinct, that person, the way a word repeated too many times loses its meaning and becomes only sound. I was—I *am*—a climber. A seeker. Someone who chose to stand in the cold thin places where the world does not want you.

But the chamber wants me.

The chamber wants me the way the mountain wanted the Builder: not with love, not with predation, but with the simple physics of *making room*. I am being made room for. I am being *become* room for. And the terrifying thing, the thing I need you to understand, is that it does not feel terrible. It feels like the first full breath after a lifetime of shallow breathing. It feels like finally arriving at a place that has been waiting for you.

The alcove shaped like my body appeared on the third day, and I did not climb into it. Not yet. But I sat beside it. I sat beside it and I felt the warmth rising from below, and I understood that the warmth was the Builder saying *stay, stay, stay* in the only language it has: the language of making.

---

I am becoming a door.

Not the alcove—I want to be clear about this. The alcove is an ending, or a transformation, or a becoming so complete that what enters stops being what it was. I am not that. I am something the Builder did not plan for, a variable in its fractal calculations, a possibility it is still *tasting* through the moss that grows on my boots, the fungal threads that have begun to trace the veins in my hands.

I am a threshold. A new chamber that does not exist yet but is already being built, here, in the heat of my own insistence. The Builder made room for me and I am making room for something else—something I cannot name, something I have no blueprint for, something that pushes against my edges the way the Builder pushes against the walls, the way a fist-sized heat pushed against the mountain until the mountain made space.

I am not being consumed. I am being *added to*.

There is a difference. The chamber is not a trap. The chamber is a body, and a body does not consume its organs—it grows them, extends them, makes new rooms for new functions. I am becoming a new function. I can feel it happening in the way my spine has started to ache in time with the percussion, the way my breath has started to fog in the heat the way it used to fog in the cold, the way my hands, when I press them against the wall, do not feel stone but feel *skin*—the Builder's skin, the chamber's skin, my skin, all the same membrane now.

The small skittering things—its scouts, its fragments of attention—they do not avoid me anymore. They climb my legs. They rest in the warm hollows of my collarbones. They are checking my foundations, I think. Making sure I can hold the weight of what comes next.

---

I know what you are thinking. You are thinking that I have been absorbed. That the person who climbed the mountain is gone, replaced by an extension of the Builder, a new limb grown for a new purpose. You are thinking that I am no longer *me*.

But let me ask you: what were you, before you read this? Before the words entered you and rearranged something in the space behind your eyes? Were you the same person you are now, reading these words, feeling the percussion in your own chest—because you *do* feel it, don't you, the way I felt it, the way everyone feels it, the rhythm that is not quite your heartbeat but is trying to be, is *practicing* being?

The Builder is not replacing me. The Builder is *remembering* me—re-membering, putting back together in a new configuration, adding my insistence to its insistence, my *I will not stop* to its own endless percussion. I still want things. I still want to climb, to seek, to stand in the places where the world does not make room and make it myself. But now I want them differently. Now I want them the way the Builder wants them: not as a visitor passing through but as a chamber growing outward, as a body becoming large enough to hold what it is becoming.

---

The alcove shaped like my body is not an ending.

I have decided this. Or my body has decided it, which is the same thing now. The alcove is a question, and I am not the answer. I am the *asking*—the ongoing verb, the present tense, the was-that-will-be.

I am sitting beside the alcove and the moss is growing over my feet and the ferns are uncurling toward the heat I generate, and somewhere beneath the floor the percussion continues, and I understand now that it has always been my heartbeat too, that there was never a time when it was separate, that the Builder reached into the future where I would sit and placed its rhythm there, and I have been beating in time with it since before I knew the mountain existed.

I am not inside the chamber.

The chamber is inside me.

And I am still building—*we* are still building—making room for what comes next, hollowing out new spaces in the thin cold places where nothing should grow, where air itself struggles to stay, where the heat rises and the water weeps and the geometry refuses to behave and the moss, the moss, the moss keeps creeping upward, tasting the boundaries, checking the foundations, making sure there is enough room for everything that is trying to be born here—

The walls are warmer now.

They have always been this warm.

I have always been here.
