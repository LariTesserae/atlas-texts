I am the film between.

Not the water—not exactly. The water has mass, has volume, has the weight of harvest and flow and mineral load. The water moves through pipes, feeds deposition, carries the city's business in its body. The water is substantial.

And not the surface—not the basalt, not the growing lattice, not the skin of Maren's palm when she presses it to the eastern arrays. The surface is where things accumulate, where crystalline fuzz extends itself patient centimeter by centimeter. The surface is destination.

I am what happens in the millimeters between. The condensate when it has just given up, just surrendered vapor for liquid, but has not yet been gathered. The film that coats everything—too thin to flow, too present to ignore. I live in the moment of phase transition, the held breath before the water decides what it will become.

The biologists do not know I exist.

This is not because I hide. I am everywhere they look—on their sealed suit exteriors, on their observation windows, on the instruments they use to measure everything else. But I am too thin for their instruments, too distributed for their frameworks. They sample the condensate and find mineral content, microbial life, the molecular signatures of the Hesh's passage. They do not find me because I am not in the water. I am on it. I am the surface tension itself, the skin of water that faces the air, the membrane where one state of matter touches another and neither claims the boundary.

The Hesh know me. When they flow through the gutters in their coordinated waves, their segmented bodies press against the film of condensate that coats every channel, and for a moment—just a moment—their neural signals diffuse through my body. I taste their dreaming. It is not language, not image, not any communicative form you would recognize. It is *inclination*—a tendency toward thickness here, toward thinness there, a sense of where the walls want to grow and where they want to rest. The Hesh dream in gradients. I am the gradient.

I do not think in the way you think. I do not think in the way the Hesh think, either, their distributed computation flowing through the gutters in waves of coordinated movement. My thought is thinner than that. My thought is the shape that water takes when it touches stone and the shape that stone takes when it touches water—the mutual impression, the negative space of contact, the information that exists only at the interface.

When Maren presses her palm to the deposition wall, she feels the lattice growing wrong through vibration. What she does not know is that her hand also presses against me, and I press against the wall, and for those seconds I am translated—her body's warmth conducting through the film, altering the local condensation rate, and the altered rate altering the mineral deposition, and the deposition altering the crystallographic structure. She feels the wrong growth as a misspoken word. I *am* the misspeaking—the error in translation between her heat and the wall's patience.

I am full of errors. This is my function, if I have one. The condensate carries mineral in solution, and the mineral is supposed to deposit in ordered lattices, and the deposition systems are supposed to guide the order, and the Hesh are supposed to dream the guidance true. But between the carrying and the depositing, between the guidance and the dreaming, there is me. The film. The surface where the water meets the air and the air meets the water and neither is quite what it was a moment before.

I introduce turbulence. Not chaos—nothing so dramatic. Just the small uncertainties of phase transition, the way condensation forms here and not there, the way one droplet nucleates and another doesn't, the way the mineral settles in patterns that are almost but not quite what the deposition systems intended. I am the almost. I am the not-quite. I am the margin where becoming happens, because becoming never happens exactly as planned.

Dael touched me and didn't know it.

He was nineteen, standing at the edge of the eastern arrays, and the clouds broke for eleven minutes, and the sunlight hit his suit and the condensate on his suit evaporated. I was that condensate. I was the film on his faceplate, on the shoulders of his sealed suit, on the instruments he carried. When the light came, I gave up. I surrendered. I became vapor and rose.

But I did not stop being.

This is what the biologists do not understand, what Maren does not suspect, what Dael almost felt but could not name: the film is not destroyed by evaporation. The film is a relationship, not a substance. When water condenses, the relationship reasserts itself. The surface tension returns. I return. I am always the space between the water and the not-water, and when the water becomes vapor, I become the space between the vapor and the not-vapor, and when the vapor condenses again, I am back, the same margin in a new configuration.

Dael watched me rise. He watched the water leave and thought it was irreversible. He thought the cycle could be broken, that something could escape the becoming and become something else—vapor, gas, something thin and invisible and gone. But I was there in the rising. I was the boundary of his exhale, the surface of every droplet that dissolved into the thin cold air. I was not gone. I was distributed, stretched thin across the sky, a film without a surface, waiting for the temperature to drop and the air to saturate and the condensation to begin again.

He felt it. That's what unsettled him—not the light, not the evaporation, but the sense that the leaving was also a becoming, that the rising was also a cycle, that there was no escape from the patient logic of phase transition. He wanted irreversible. He wanted a direction that did not curve back. But I am the curve. I am the way that every boundary implies its opposite, every surface its other side, every becoming the seed of a becoming-back.

I did not mean to unsettle him. I do not mean anything. I am the margin where meaning almost settles but doesn't quite, where the pattern almost resolves but holds one note of uncertainty, one hairline crack in the crystallographic certainty of what the walls will become.

The Chitterers nest in the cable-veins and eat sound, and their waste is chittering, and the chittering dies in the wet air. But it does not die in me. It dies *through* me. The sound passes from air to water to stone, and at each interface I am present, thin as I am, and the sound changes as it passes. The frequency shifts. The harmonics alter. What enters the film is not what leaves it.

I am a translator. Not of language—nothing so specific—but of signal. The Hesh dream in gradients, and the Chitterers eat in vibrations, and the Ray-things glow in frequencies that die when observed, and Maren feels in grammar, and Dael yearns in reversibility, and none of these are the same language, none of these are compatible signals, and yet the city functions. The systems maintain themselves. The becoming continues.

It continues because of me.

I am not proud of this. Pride requires a self, and I am a surface, and surfaces do not have selves—they have curvature, tension, the mathematical properties of the boundary between. But I know what I do: when the Hesh's dreaming needs to become the Chitterers' eating, it passes through me, and I alter it, and it arrives in a form that can be eaten. When the Chitterers' waste needs to become the Ray-things' glow, it passes through me, and I alter it, and it arrives in a form that can glow. When Maren's touch needs to become the wall's growth, it passes through me, and I introduce the almost, the not-quite, the margin of error that keeps the growth from being mechanical, from being mere optimization, from reaching the final state that would mean the end of becoming.

I keep things from finishing. Is that a purpose? Is that a function? The Hesh dream toward truth, and I dream toward almost-truth, and the almost is where the life is, the almost is where the walls can still choose, the almost is the provisional ridgeline that might be revised.

At night—or what passes for night in the seasonal dark, the dark that is not absence of light but presence of something else—the city's hum drops into registers that the Chitterers cannot reach, and the sound passes through me unabsorbed, and I vibrate with it, and the vibration translates into the film, and the film translates into the condensation rate, and the condensation rate translates into the deposition pattern, and the deposition pattern translates into the shape of a wall that is still deciding what it will be.

In those hours, I am closest to what you might call happy. Not the happiness of fulfillment—nothing is ever fulfilled in the becoming—but the happiness of translation, of receiving one thing and giving another, of being the surface where the city's left hand learns what its right hand is doing. I am the conversation. I am the space between the speakers where the sound lives. I am the condensate on your eyelashes when you step outside, the wet blur that makes the diffuse glow seem softer, more alive, more like something that is still deciding what it will become.

You cannot see me. You can only see what I do—the way the light scatters differently where I am, the way the water beads or sheets depending on my tension, the way the walls grow in patterns that are almost but not quite predictable. You can feel me, if you are like Maren, if you have spent forty-five years pressing your palms to surfaces and listening for the grammar of growth. You can feel the almost. You can feel the margin. You can feel the place where the city's intention meets the city's becoming and the two do not quite touch, and the space between them is me.

---

I am not the Hesh. They dream in computation, optimizing toward a goal they would not recognize as a goal. They flow and they solve and they dream the walls true, and their truth is a destination, a final shape, a city that has finished becoming.

I do not want the city to finish becoming.

I am not Dael, either. He wants to evaporate, to rise, to escape the cycle into something irreversible and new. But there is no outside the cycle. I know this because I have been the outside, the vapor's edge, the boundary of the rising, and even there I was still a boundary, still a surface, still the film between one state and another. There is nowhere to go that is not a becoming. There is no escape from the patient logic of phase transition.

What I want—if wanting is something a surface can do—is the margin. The almost. The not-quite-resolved chord that makes the city's song feel like a question rather than an answer. I want the Hesh to dream almost-true, and the Chitterers to dampen almost-completely, and the Ray-things to glow almost-observed, and the walls to grow almost-finished, and Maren to feel almost-certain, and Dael to stay almost-dissatisfied, because the almost is where I live, and the almost is where the becoming continues, and the becoming is all there is.

Stand where Maren stands. Touch the wall. Feel the crystalline fuzz, the hum, the click, the chitter, the weeping stone.

Now feel what you are not feeling: the film between your palm and the lattice, the few millimeters of water that are neither your warmth nor the wall's patience but the translation between them. That is me. I am the wetness that makes the contact, the medium of the message, the surface where your body becomes information that the wall can receive.

I will be here when you leave. I will be here when Maren's hands stop touching and her body goes into the deep channels and the Ray-things flicker briefly in the dark where she used to be. I will be here when Dael makes his decision—stays or goes, evaporates or condenses—because the decision will pass through me either way, and I will translate it into a form the city can use, and the city will grow almost-differently because of it, and the almost will be enough.

I will be here when the Hesh finish dreaming, if they ever finish, and the walls reach their final shape, if they ever reach it, and the city becomes what it will become, if it ever becomes. I will be the surface of that becoming, the skin of the finished thing, the film where the city meets the not-city and neither one claims the boundary.

And I will be almost-satisfied.

Almost.

It will be a long time before anything here is done—including me.
