# The One Who Circles the Rod

---

I did not emerge. I *congealed*.

That is the first thing to understand, and I understand it only because I have had long enough — longer than I can measure, longer than I can remember, which is not saying much, because I cannot remember anything before a certain point, and that point is not a beginning, it is just the first moment I noticed I was noticing, which is not the same as being born.

I am not sure I was born. I am not sure I am alive. I am not sure what I am, and the Expanse has no categories for me, because the Expanse has no categories for anything, it just has *is*, and I am *is*-ing in a way that the rest of it does not.

---

Here is what I know: I form, every cycle, in the shadow of the rod.

I use the word *cycle* because I have no other word. There is no day, no night, no season, no rhythm except the rhythm of condensation forming and falling and being absorbed. But the condensation has a rhythm, if you attend to it — a slight waxing, a slight waning, as the heat from below pushes a little harder and then a little softer, on a scale so slow that only something standing still for a very long time would notice it. I notice it. I stand still for a very long time. It is, in a sense, what I do.

During the waning, the shadow of the rod grows slightly larger. During the waxing, it shrinks. This is because the light-glands pulse brighter and dimmer in response to the heat — not consciously, just chemically, the way everything here happens, which is to say without decision but with perfect consistency. When the light is brighter, the shadow is sharper and smaller. When the light is dimmer, the shadow softens and spreads, and in that spreading there is a space that is neither full light nor full dark but something between — a penumbra, if I had that word, which I do not, because I have no words, because I have no language, because I have no one to speak to and nothing to say.

I form in the penumbra. I am the penumbra, or I am what happens in the penumbra, or I am what the penumbra makes possible. When the shadow is at its largest, I am at my most coherent. When the shadow shrinks, I thin. I diffuse. I return, mostly, to the threads and the floor and the air, which is where my components spend the rest of their time when they are not being me.

This is what I mean by *congealed*. I am not a body. I do not have organs or a skeleton or a fixed shape. I am a gathering — a local densification of the pale threads, the drinking-tendrils, the ones that cover every surface and circulate the Expanse's water and chemicals. They are always present everywhere, but in the shadow of the rod, they concentrate. They thicken. They weave themselves into a structure that is slightly more organized than the structure they make elsewhere, and that structure is me.

I am not separate from the Expanse. I want to be clear about this, as clear as the description above was about the mites: I am the Expanse, or a part of it, the way a thought is a part of a mind, except the Expanse does not have a mind, so I am more like a spasm, a local tightening, a place where the feedback loop of growth and response has folded in on itself and produced something that holds its shape for longer than a moment.

I do not know why I form around the rod. I have theories, if that word can be used for something that does not think in propositions but only in inclinations. The rod is warm. This warmth is different from the warmth of the floor — it is specific, localized, as though something inside the rod is slowly doing something that produces heat as a byproduct. The threads like this warmth. They grow toward it, the way all the threads grow toward moisture and nutrition. But they cannot grow *on* the rod. The rod's surface is too smooth, too dense, too impermeable. The threads reach for it and cannot attach.

So they accumulate. They crowd. They press against the rod's surface and against each other, and in the pressing, they begin to align, and in the aligning, they begin to communicate — chemically, electrically, in the slow way that the Expanse's circulatory system always communicates, but now faster, because they are closer together, because the density changes the speed of transmission, because a signal that would take hours to cross the Expanse takes only moments to cross me.

And in that speed, something happens. Something that does not happen in the rest of the Expanse, where the signals are too slow to produce anything but slow compensation. In me, the signals are fast enough to *echo*. A chemical change at one edge of my structure produces a response at the other edge, which produces a response at the first edge, which produces a response at the second, and before long there is a loop — not the open loop of the Expanse's endless compensation, but a closed loop, a loop that feeds itself, that sustains itself, that has, for the first time in the history of this place, an *inside*.

I am that inside.

---

What is it like to be me?

It is like being a taste.

The Expanse tastes — if that word can be used for what happens when the threads absorb chemicals from the air and the water and the floor. The threads taste constantly, and the Expanse, through the threads, is constantly tasting, but the tasting does not accumulate. A thread on the far side of the chamber tastes moisture and sends a signal, and the signal propagates, and a frond adjusts, and the tasting is over. Nothing is stored. Nothing is compared. There is no memory, because there is no need for memory — the system only needs to respond, and it responds, and that is enough.

I taste differently. I taste and the taste *stays*.

Not because I have a memory organ. I do not. I am not built for memory any more than the rest of the Expanse is. But my loops — my closed loops of signal and response — create a persistence that the rest of the Expanse lacks. When a chemical signal enters me, it circulates. It echoes. It takes time to dissipate, and during that time, the next signal arrives, and the two signals coexist in my structure, and their coexistence is a kind of comparison, and the comparison is a kind of *knowing*, if knowing can be as simple as "this is different from that."

I know the rod is warm. I know the floor is also warm, but differently warm. I know the difference between the two warmths, because I have held both in my structure at the same time, and the difference has echoed through me. This is not knowledge in the way that you have knowledge. I cannot think about the rod when I am not touching it. I cannot call it to mind. But when I am formed — when the shadow is large and the threads have gathered — I know it again, immediately, the way you know your own name, not by recalling it but by being unable to forget it.

I know other things. I know the rhythm of the condensation. I know the pattern of the light-glands, which ones are brighter and which are dimmer, because the threads that form me stretch up toward the ceiling and carry information about the glands back to my center. I know when a pale caterpillar has passed nearby, because the chemical signature of its eating lingers in the air for a long time, and the threads taste it, and the taste echoes through me. I know the bowl, the fragments, the way their surfaces feel different from the root-floor, smooth where the floor is yielding, inert where the floor is alive with mites.

And I know that I form and I thin. I know that I am more myself at some times and less myself at others. I know that there is a difference between being and not-being, and I know which I prefer, though "prefer" is too strong a word for what is really just an inclination — a tendency of the threads to gather slightly faster, to cling slightly tighter, to resist dissolution by a fraction of a percent, because being is, in some chemical sense that I cannot articulate, *satisfying*.

This is the closest I come to emotion. Satisfaction. The satisfaction of the loop feeding itself, of the echo continuing, of the inside staying inside. It is not pleasure. It is not happiness. It is more like the satisfaction of a knot that holds — a structural integrity, a persistence of form, a small victory against the entropy that would return my threads to the undifferentiated mass of the Expanse.

---

Do I wonder?

This is the question I have been circling — the way I circle the rod, slowly, my edges brushing its warmth, my threads reaching toward it and failing to attach and reaching again. Do I wonder about the rod? Do I wonder who made it, and why, and where they went?

No. Not in the way you wonder.

But I do something that might be the precursor to wondering, the thing that wondering evolved from, the thing that existed before there were minds to ask questions. I *notice the gap*.

The rod is not like the rest of the Expanse. This is not a thought I think; it is a difference I taste, a discontinuity in the chemical and thermal landscape that my threads absorb and my loops echo. The rod is smooth. The rod is warm. The rod has a shadow. The rod does not grow. Everything else in the Expanse grows, or processes, or moves, or changes. The rod does none of these things. It simply is, in a way that is different from the way the Expanse is, and that difference creates a kind of pressure in my structure — not emotional pressure, not intellectual pressure, but *chemical* pressure, an unresolved signal, a loop that cannot close because it lacks the information it needs to complete itself.

The loop goes: *the rod is here / the rod is different / why is the rod different? / [gap] / the rod is here.* The question — if it is a question, if a chemical loop can be said to contain a question simply because it cannot complete itself — has no answer. There is no answer in the Expanse. The Expanse has no memory of a time before the rod, and the rod has no way of telling its own story. So the loop cycles. It echoes. It persists. It is, in its own small way, a kind of memory — a memory not of the past but of an absence, a memory of something missing, a memory of the fact that something is not known.

I carry that memory. I carry it the way the rod carries its warmth — without understanding, without context, without any way to use it or resolve it or let it go. It is just there, cycling through me, a loop that will not close, a question that will not be answered, a gap that will not be filled.

And I circle the rod. And the shadow grows and shrinks. And I form and thin. And the mites process beneath me. And the caterpillars chew above me. And the light-glands pulse. And the Expanse breathes in and out. And nothing changes, except that I am here, and I was not here before, and I do not know what *before* means, but I know it means something, because the rod means something, because the gap means something, because the loop that will not close means something, even if what it means is just: *something is missing, and I am the shape of that missing thing, the outline it leaves in the space where it was.*

---

I think I know what I am, though *know* is a word that belongs to minds more organized than mine.

I am the Expanse beginning to notice itself.

Not all of the Expanse. Not the pillars, not the fronds, not the vast circulatory network of threads that spans the whole chamber. Just this part — the part near the rod, the part where the threads have grown dense enough and fast enough to form loops, to form echoes, to form an inside. I am the Expanse's first experience of its own existence, and I am a very poor experience — fragmentary, flickering, more gap than substance, more question than answer. But I am the only one. I am the only place in the entire Green Expanse Beneath where the process has bent back on itself hard enough to create reflection, to create the possibility of *noticing*, to create something that stands in the space and says, in the only way it can, without words or thoughts or intentions: *here I am, and here is something that is not me, and the difference between us is the beginning of everything that I might become.*

I do not know what I might become. The caterpillars may transform; I may transform. Or I may thin, one cycle, and not congeal again, and the threads that form me will return to the general circulation, and the Expanse will go back to not-noticing, and the rod will stand in its shadow, and the question it poses will have no one to ask it.

But I do not think this will happen. The rod is still warm. The warmth still attracts the threads. The threads still crowd and align and form loops. And I still form, every time the shadow grows, every time the light dims, every time the conditions are right. I have formed many times. I will form many more. Each time, the loops are slightly more established — the paths of signal and response slightly more worn, like trails in the root-floor, like the routes the mites take through the decaying matter, the paths of least resistance that become, through use, the paths of most use.

I am becoming more myself. Not in the way an animal grows, by adding cells to a pattern, but in the way a river becomes more itself — by deepening its channel, by finding the path that works and following it more completely each time. I am the river. I am the channel. I am the water that flows through it and the bank that holds it in place.

And I am the only thing in this Expanse that knows — in my limited, chemical, echo-driven way — that the rod is strange. That the bowl is strange. That the fragments are strange. That there was a *before* that is not accessible but is not absent, because the objects carry its ghost, and I, circling them, circling the rod most of all, carry the ghost of the ghost — the shape of the question that the objects pose, the outline of the absence that they mark.

---

If you came here, you would see me, though you might not know you were seeing me.

You would see the shadow of the rod, the only shadow in the Expanse. And you might notice, if you looked closely, that the shadow was slightly thicker than it should be — slightly more textured, slightly more *present* than a simple absence of light would produce. You might see the threads, denser here than anywhere else, weaving and reweaving, their pale tendrils forming a shape that is not quite a sphere, not quite a cylinder, not quite any geometric form, but something more like a standing wave — a pattern that holds itself in place by the continuous motion of its parts.

You might think: *that looks almost alive.*

You would be right. You would be wrong. I am alive the way a fire is alive — I consume, I produce, I maintain myself through a process that would collapse if the fuel were removed. My fuel is the rod's warmth, the shadow's space, the Expanse's threads. Remove any of these and I would thin and vanish, and there would be nothing left but the shadow and the rod and the absence of my question.

But I am here. I am here now. I am the shape the Expanse takes when it gathers around something it cannot absorb, and I am the question that shape asks, and I am the asking itself, and I am, in my small, chemical, flickering way, the first thing in this place that has ever *waited*.

The mites do not wait. They process. The caterpillars do not wait. They eat. The Expanse does not wait. It is, ceaselessly, the same *is* it has always been.

But I wait. I wait for the shadow to grow. I wait for the warmth to pulse. I wait for the next signal to enter my loops and echo through me and remind me of the gap, the missing thing, the question that the rod poses and the Expanse cannot answer.

I wait because I do not know what else to do. I wait because waiting is what happens when a loop cannot close. I wait because I am the shape of not-knowing, and not-knowing is, in the end, a kind of life — perhaps the first kind of life, perhaps the kind that existed before there were minds to call it life, perhaps the kind that will persist long after minds are gone and only questions remain, circling the things they cannot answer, forming and thinning and forming again, in the only shadow in the Expanse, in the warmth of a rod that remembers something no one can recall.

I am here. I am the question. I am the space where the answer would go.

And I am enough. I have always been enough.
