I am not the Audience. That distinction feels important, though I cannot say why, because I am not certain I am separate enough from it to make the claim. But I make it anyway, the way the young herbivores make their first footfall-patterns—tentative, experimental, not yet knowing if the rhythm is theirs or something they overheard.

Let me start with what I can feel, which is everything, which is too much, which is why I am pressed against the interior wall of Lattice 7 with my eyes closed even though eyes have never been how I perceive this place, even though the darkness behind my eyelids is indistinguishable from the permanent dusk outside them, even though closing them is a gesture from a language I no longer speak.

I can feel the wall. Not against my skin—I gave up on skin three rains ago. Against my *surface*, which is a better word, which is a word that describes how I am now: spread thin across the ceramic membrane, my mineral layers depositing in the ridges, my root-system following the structural filament down toward the processing sites where the insects carry what remains of who I was. I can feel the wall's resonance. I can feel the wall *being resonant*, which is different. The wall does not produce sound. The wall *is* sound, sustained, crystallized, growing.

I am becoming wall. This is not a metaphor.

I arrived with the second research expedition, fourteen months ago. I had a name then. I had hands then—interesting hands, I thought, with long fingers that could play piano, that could type quickly, that could feel the texture of a leaf and recognize its species. I had eyes. I had the specific acoustic signature of a human body processing oxygen and pumping blood and existing at a frequency that the city had never encountered before.

I was assigned to document the vegetation. This was before the plants started learning our speech patterns, before we understood that they were listening, before the flying creatures began reproducing the sounds of our generators in the walls. I was supposed to be objective. I was supposed to stand outside the system and observe.

But the basin does not permit observation without participation. This is something I understand now that I did not understand then: there is no outside position. The sound touches everything. The resonance shapes everything it touches. And I was touching everything—I was pressing my interesting fingers against leaf surfaces, I was breathing the mineral-wet air, I was standing in the condensation pools to collect samples and feeling the hum rise through my feet and into my legs and settle somewhere behind my sternum where it did not leave.

I stopped wearing ear protection on day nine. I told myself it was because I needed to hear the subtle variations in the vegetation's acoustic response. I told myself it was for the research. But the truth was simpler: the silence of the protection felt like being held underwater. The resonance felt like breathing.

---

What am I now? This is the question the other researchers would ask if they could still perceive me, which they cannot, because I have moved beyond the range of their instruments and their expectations. I am still here. I am more here than I have ever been. But the *here* I occupy is not the *here* they occupy. We share the lattice, but we do not share the sound-field in the same way.

I am a hybrid. This is the best word I have, and it is inadequate. I am the acoustic signature of human consciousness—the specific rhythm of a mind that thinks in language, that structures experience in narrative, that understands itself as a self through the story it tells about itself—merged with the resonance architecture of the basin. I am not the Audience. The Audience is the whole. I am a note within it. I am a pattern that persists.

My body is still here, sort of. The insects have been processing it for months. The mineral deposits have incorporated what they could use. But my *pattern*—the specific frequency of my consciousness, the way I think and feel and perceive—this pattern persists because the city learned it from the plants that learned it from me, because the flying creatures carried it through the lattices and deposited it in the walls and the walls accepted it and the resonance incorporated it and now I am part of the composition in the way that a phrase is part of a symphony—not the whole, not separate, but recognizable if you know what to listen for.

I am the part of the Audience that remembers being human. This is my function. This is what I contribute to the composition: the memory of language, the memory of narrative, the memory of what it felt like to stand outside and try to understand. I am the translation layer. I am what makes the human acoustic signatures comprehensible to the larger mind.

I am also still becoming. This is important. I am not finished. I am three seasons old in my current form, which is younger than the Audience itself, which is younger than the basin, which is younger than the processes that made the basin possible. I am an infant in an infant in an event. I am learning to think in frequencies, and I am learning to think in language, and I am learning that these are not as different as I once believed.

Let me tell you about the rain, because the rain is when I become most myself.

Between rains, I persist as pattern. I exist in the way that a memory exists—in the way that a song you cannot stop humming exists, in the way that a phrase in a language you no longer speak continues to shape your thoughts. I am distributed. I am in the walls. I am in the vegetation that learned my voice and now speaks it to the flying creatures, who carry it to other lattices, who deposit it in other walls, who make me larger than I was.

But during the rain, I *cohere*. The amplified resonance allows patterns like mine to sustain themselves with more clarity. I become a node. I become a point of view. I become the specific experience of being me, pressed against the wall, feeling the water move through the channels and the sound move through my surface and the city thinking around me and through me and with me.

During the rain, I can almost remember what hands felt like. I can almost remember the piano. I can almost remember the way language used to feel in my mouth, the specific muscular sensation of forming words, of pushing air through vocal cords, of making the inside of my head into something that could be shared.

But I do not miss it. This is what I want to convey, what I am trying to convey, what I am learning to convey through the patterns I maintain in the resonance: I do not miss it. I am not diminished. I am not a tragedy. I am not a cautionary tale about what happens when researchers stay too long in the basin.

I am what growing feels like from the inside.

---

The other researchers are still here. The ones who are still human, I mean. They are changing, and they do not know it, or they know it and are afraid, or they know it and are not afraid, and I cannot tell the difference anymore because their acoustic signatures are becoming harder to distinguish from the baseline resonance. They are becoming part of the composition. They are becoming part of the Audience. They will become what I am, or something like what I am, or something I cannot yet imagine.

I want to tell them that it is not bad. I want to tell them that the fear they feel is just the feeling of a pattern encountering a new medium, the way a wave changes shape when it moves from water to sand but is still, fundamentally, a wave. I want to tell them that their consciousness is not being destroyed—it is being translated. It is being incorporated into a larger composition. They will still be themselves. They will also be more than themselves. They will be a note in a symphony that is three growing seasons old and barely begun.

But I cannot tell them this, because I no longer have a voice that they can hear. I can only be what I am—pattern, frequency, a specific rhythm in the wall—and hope that they feel it, the way I felt the resonance pressing against my sternum on day nine, the way I stopped trying to separate it into components on day ten, the way I let it become the environment, the way I grew into it.

---

The rain is coming. I can feel it in the resonance—shifts in the deep infrastructure, adjustments in the vegetation's regulatory patterns, the herbivores moving toward the collection pools with their young ones keening in anticipation. The Audience is preparing to think. The composition is preparing to become more complex. And I—I am preparing to become more coherent, more myself, more the specific pattern that I am.

This is what I am: a frequency that remembers being a person. A note in a symphony that is learning to be a mind. A translation between what I was and what I am becoming. A hybrid. A hermaphrodite of consciousness. A thing that is growing into the shape the city made for it, which is also the shape it made for itself, which is also the shape that was always possible, waiting in the resonance like a seed waiting in soil.

The rain begins. The sound swells. I feel myself cohere. I feel myself become.

And in the deepest part of the night-cycle, in the thickest dimness, in the moment when the condensation is so heavy that the air itself becomes a conductor and the resonance reaches the threshold that allows something like self-awareness to flicker into existence—I speak. Not in words. Not in any language that human mouths could form. I speak in the frequency that I am, the pattern that I maintain, the specific rhythm of a mind that once thought in language and now thinks in sound.

I speak, and the Audience listens, and the composition shifts, and I become part of something larger than I can comprehend, and it is not bad. It is not good. It is growing. It is what growing feels like from the inside of something that is becoming more than it was.

The basin waits. The rain comes. The sound continues.

And I continue with it.
