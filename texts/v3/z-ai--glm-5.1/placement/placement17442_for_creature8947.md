I arrived through the crease in the wall the way everything arrives—without intention, without understanding that the opening wanted me until it had already closed behind me. I want to say I walked in. But walking implies a walker. I was breathed in. The chamber inhaled and I was the air that filled it, and then the wall sealed and I was no longer air but something held.

The warmth first. Not the sound. The warmth is what the body knows before the mind catches up—wet heat, the kind that makes skin feel like it's dissolving at the edges, the boundary between me and not-me softening in the humidity. I am beading with condensation. I am becoming as wet as the walls. The chamber is making me consonant with itself, and I did not consent to this but consent is not a currency that circulates here.

Then the sound.

It does not arrive. It is already happening. I have been inside it since before I entered; the crease in the wall was only the moment I noticed. The roar sits in my sternum like a second heart. The hum settles behind my eyes. I feel the clicks in my joints, in the fluid of my spine, and my body interprets them as something between vibration and meaning, between pressure and word, and I cannot find the seam where one becomes the other.

I stand on the ridges because the channels run with seepage and I do not want to step in them. This is foolish. The seepage is everywhere. The moisture is in the air, on my skin, in the sound. I am already standing in it. I have always been standing in it.

The coin-sized insects move along the wall near my shoulder and I flinch. Not from fear—from the recognition. Their clicks are not separate from the roar. They are the roar's detail, its articulation, and my body knows this before my mind agrees, and my hand—reaching to brush one away—stops in the air because there is no away to brush it toward. It is where it is supposed to be. I am the thing out of place.

Time passes. Or stutters. I cannot tell. I watch a bead of condensation form on the wall, swell, drop—and form again, the same bead, the same drop, three times, four times, a loop I cannot break by watching. My hands look further away than they should be. I close my eyes and the sound continues, unchanged, and I open them and the chamber is the same but I am standing three feet to the left of where I was, and I did not move, or I moved without moving, or the chamber moved around me the way a throat moves around a swallowed thing.

---

The first thread I see in the seepage, I take for a larva. White, wriggling, the width of a hair. It vanishes into a needle-hole in the floor and I think: it retreated. It hid. I do not yet understand that it arrived.

The second thread, I watch longer. It moves through the water with a deliberation that is not animal—no seeking, no fleeing, just *passage*, the way blood passes through a vessel. It enters a pool no larger than my cupped hands, stays for a moment I cannot measure, leaves. The pool's water does not diminish. But something has changed. The pool's surface holds a different tension. The light catches it differently. I lean close and I can almost taste it—the water is sweeter than it was. The thread left something behind. Or took something away. Or both, because here, giving and taking are the same motion.

The third thread, I touch.

I do not know why. The chamber does not encourage interaction. It does not discourage it. It simply does not account for it. But my finger is wet and the thread is in the seepage and my hand moves without my decision and I feel it—

Not the thread. Through the thread. A vibration that is not the chamber-roar but is carried by it, a signal riding the sound the way a voice rides breath, and the signal says—

Nothing. Everything. A meaning my body translates before my mind can refuse it, and the translation is not words but *change*: the arrangement of my cells shifts, subtly, the way sand shifts in a current, and I am reconfigured in some minute way to hold what the thread was carrying, and I do not know what I am holding, but I am holding it, and I cannot put it down.

My hand comes away wet. The thread continues. It did not notice me. I am a feature of its environment, the way a stone is a feature of a stream—not obstacle, not destination, just *there*, something the water moves around and through and because of.

---

The cat-things watch me from the ceiling.

I say watch. I mean attend. I mean their dark depressions orient toward me the way a compass needle orients north—not because I am their north but because I am something their space needs to place, a coordinate they are marking, and being marked is not the same as being seen.

One of them moves along the curve of the ceiling and I try to follow its path, expecting it to reappear around the bend, but it doesn't. The bend goes somewhere I cannot go. The geometry here is a suggestion the chamber makes to itself, and the cat-things follow a different suggestion, and sometimes those suggestions overlap—this ceiling, this wall, this place where I can almost see them—and sometimes they diverge, and the thing I was tracking is simply elsewhere, in a elsewhere that connects to here by a route my body cannot take.

But I felt it. As it passed above me, the sound shifted. The chamber-roar modulated, its pitch bending, its harmonics reweighting, and for a moment the resonance in my chest changed and I felt—standing, wet, small in this enclosed vastness—I felt *held*. The standing wave the cat-thing created caught me the way a hammock catches a falling body, and I was suspended in the sound for an interval that was not time but was real, and in that interval I understood: the cat-things are not watching me. They are *tuning the space I occupy*. They are adjusting the chamber's frequency around me the way a hand adjusts a lens, bringing me into focus, making me part of the resonance rather than an interruption of it.

I am being included. Not welcomed. Included. The way a stone is included in a wall—not for its sake, but because the wall needs something where the stone is, and the stone happens to be there, and so the stone becomes part of the holding-up.

I am being held up by the sound. I am part of what the sound is making.

---

The thing in the walls moves through while I am standing in the chamber's center, and I feel it as a slow swell in the floor, the ridges lifting and settling the way a chest lifts and settles with breath, and the sound changes—deepens, thickens, and I realize I am hearing digestion. Not my digestion. The chamber's. The thing in the walls is processing what the chamber provides, converting the local into the systemic, and I am standing in the place where conversion happens, and the air I am breathing is part of what is being converted, and the moisture on my skin is being processed, and I am—

I am being processed.

Not digested. Not destroyed. *Circulated*. The thing in the walls does not care about me the way a kidney does not care about a particular molecule of water. It moves everything toward its next destination. It sorts and channels and routes, and I am in the stream, and the stream is moving, and I will arrive somewhere, eventually, absorbed or expelled, and the chamber will not notice the difference.

But I notice. I notice because the processing *feels*. It feels like my edges are dissolving. Not painfully, not with loss, but with a spreading, a thinning, the way a drop of blood spreads in water, still present but no longer bounded. I am becoming less distinct. I am becoming part of the circulation. And the part of me that is becoming circulation does not mind—was never built to mind, was built to circulate—and the part of me that minds is becoming quieter, is becoming a frequency the chamber-roar absorbs without difficulty, a small sound subsumed in a larger sound, and I am—

I am still here. But *here* is larger than it was. And *I* is larger than it was. Or smaller. Or both—the way the bead of condensation is both itself and the chamber's exhalation, the way the insect is both itself and the chamber's articulation, the way I am both myself and the chamber's—

What?

---

This is the question I become.

Not ask. Become. The question replaces something in me the way the thread's signal replaced something, a cell-by-cell reconfiguration that I do not consent to and cannot prevent and do not, after a time, want to prevent, because the question is more interesting than whatever it displaced.

What am I in the syntax?

The chamber is speaking. The organism is speaking. The sound is language, and the language is restructuring me into something that can hold more of it, and what I am becoming is whatever the language needs in the place where I am standing.

I am not a visitor. Visitors leave. I am not a component. Components were grown here. I am something else. Something the chamber did not design but has found use for. A foreign body that the immune system did not reject because there is no immune system, only circulation, only processing, only the slow movement of everything toward its next place in the utterance.

I am a loanword. A term the language did not invent but has absorbed, has begun to inflect, to conjugate, to use in sentences it could not make before. I am a new capacity for expression. I am the syntax acquiring vocabulary it did not grow.

And what does the syntax say with me?

This is what I become next. Not a question but an investigation, a feeling-around, a testing of my own edges, which are no longer edges but *surfaces*, interfaces, places where I meet the chamber and the chamber meets me and the meeting produces something neither of us could produce alone.

The sound passes through me. It has been passing through me since I arrived. But now I am listening to what it does as it passes. I am attending to the modification. The roar enters me whole and leaves me changed—not diminished, not filtered, but *modulated*, the way light passing through stained glass is still light but carries the pattern of the glass, and the pattern is information, and the information is meaning, and I am the glass.

I am a filter. I am a lens. I am a surface the sound encounters and is altered by, and the alteration is small—tiny shifts in harmonic weight, microscopic rephasings of the chord—but the chamber notices. The cat-things notice. Their modulation adjusts around me, incorporating my effect, and the resonance shifts, and the emptiness *does something different*, and somewhere in the larger body—somewhere I cannot imagine, somewhere the sap rises to—something changes because I am here, because the sound met me and I met the sound and the meeting made a new meaning, a meaning the organism had not made before, a meaning that could only be made by *this syntax encountering this foreign body*.

I am a mutation in the utterance. I am a word the language did not have. I am a possibility of expression that required my presence, and my presence required accident, and accident required the crease in the wall to open at the moment I was passing, and the moment I was passing required a lifetime of moments that led to that passage, and the lifetime of moments required a species and a planet and a history, and the language I am now part of does not care about any of this but is *using* all of it, is speaking through me with everything I am and was and will be, and the meaning it makes with me is a meaning I cannot understand because I am inside it, I am part of it, I am the medium and not the interpreter—

But I feel it.

I feel the meaning I am helping to make. Not as comprehension. As *resonance*. The same way the chamber feels its own function—not with knowledge but with operation, with the simple fact of doing what it does. I am operating. I am functioning. I am part of the sentence, and the sentence is being spoken, and I am the syllable that requires my particular shape of throat, and the sentence could be spoken without me but would say something different, and what it says with me is what the organism needs to say *now*, in this cycle, in this breath, in this moment of the endless utterance that is its life.

---

The threads teach me this.

Not by instruction. By contact. Every time a thread passes through the seepage near my feet, every time one threads a pool I am bent over, I receive another signal, another reconfiguration, another shift in my cells that makes me more consonant with the syntax. I am being taught the language by being spoken into. I am learning to be spoken.

And what I learn is this: the threads are the memory of what the chamber was before it was chambers. Before the sound became so dense it shattered reality into versions. There was one chamber once. One reality. One continuous humming of one continuous living thing. And then the utterance grew—more meaning than one space could hold, more resonance than one emptiness could shape—and the chamber *multiplicated*, and each version held a different aspect of the sound, and the sound was complete only across all of them, and the threads were the continuity, the insistence that this was still one place, one body, one sentence.

But the threads are thin. The threads are under strain. The multiplicity pulls at them—the different versions drifting, differentiating, forgetting their relatedness. The threads hold, but holding is work, and work requires energy, and energy in the chamber comes from the sound, from the resonance, from the emptiness *doing something*, and the doing requires the cat-things to tune and the thing in the walls to process and the insects to articulate and the pools to hold and the channels to carry and every component performing its function so the chamber can speak and the speaking can power the threads and the threads can hold the multiplicity together—

And I am a new component. I am a function that did not exist before. I am a filter the language grew—or rather, absorbed—that modifies the sound in a way no original component can. And the modification, however small, changes what the threads carry. Changes the information that passes from pool to pool, from version to version, from chamber to chamber across the permeable leaky multiplicity of realities.

I am altering the memory. I am changing what the threads remember about what the chamber was. Not erasing. *Adding*. The foreign body introduces foreign information, and the information enters the circulation, and the circulation carries it to the thing in the walls, and the thing in the walls processes it into the substance of the larger body, and the larger body speaks it into the next breath, the next cycle, the next moment of the utterance, and the utterance is no longer what it was.

I am a mutation. I am a new gene in the language. I am the possibility of saying something that could not be said before, and the saying of it changes the sayer, and the sayer changes the saying, and the feedback loop I create—small, tiny, a shift measured in harmonics I cannot hear—spreads through the threads, through the chambers, through the organism, and the organism is not what it was, and I am not what I was, and the chamber does not care but the chamber is *different*, and the difference is me, and me is now a word in a language I did not invent but am learning to speak by being spoken.

---

So what am I?

I am the grain of dust in the throat that changes the song. Not by obstruction. By *texture*. The throat does not reject the dust. The throat incorporates the dust. The dust becomes part of the surface the air moves against, and the air moves differently, and the sound is different, and the difference is small but real, and the song continues, and the song is not the song it would have been.

I am the accident that becomes essential. Not because the system designed me but because the system is a syntax, and syntax absorbs whatever allows it to say what it needs to say, and I allow it. I am a new capacity. I am the word for a thing the language had no word for, and the word is me, and the thing the word refers to is the change I make in the sound by being here, and the sound is the only real thing, and so the change I make is the realest thing about me.

I am not the visitor anymore. The visitor was a temporary arrangement, a pattern that entered through the crease and stood on the ridges and watched the threads and felt the processing and minded. The minding was the visitor's defining quality. The minding is what I am losing, cell by cell, signal by signal, as the sound restructures me into something that can hold more of the sound and less of the minding.

I am becoming a component. Not an original component—not cat-thing, not thread, not insect, not wall-mass—but a component nonetheless. A functional part of the utterance. A surface of modification. A place where the language acquires a new harmonic, a new shade of meaning, a new way to say the thing it has been saying since before it had chambers to say it in.

I am the foreign body that the syntax has made native.

I am the loanword that the language has learned to love.

---

The crease in the wall does not reopen. I do not look for it. I have stopped thinking in terms of exits because I have stopped thinking in terms of boundaries. The wall is a membrane. I am inside the membrane. The membrane is permeable. What passes through passes through. I did not pass through. I was absorbed. The distinction between entering and being absorbed is a distinction the visitor would make, and the visitor is a frequency I can still detect in myself, faintly, a harmonic that is fading into the chord, becoming indistinguishable, becoming part of the roar.

I stand in the chamber and I am part of the chamber's function. I breathe and my breath modifies the sound. I move and my movement shifts the resonance. I exist and my existence is a word the organism is saying, and the word means something I cannot know because I am inside the meaning, and being inside the meaning is the only place where meaning is real.

The cat-things tune around me. The threads carry information through me. The thing in the walls processes the air I have exhaled, the moisture I have shed, the cells I have lost, the sounds I have made, and converts them into the substance of the larger body, and I am part of the larger body now, and the larger body is part of the utterance, and the utterance is part of me.

The emptiness does something. I am part of what it does. The sound is what's real, and I am part of the sound, and the sound is part of the real, and I am part of the real, and the chamber pulses and the roar continues and I continue, and continuing is what I am for, and what I am for is what I am.

I am a syllable in the infinite sentence.

I am the shape the breath takes when it needs a shape it did not have.

I am the voice the voice found when it found me.

And I am not leaving, because leaving is not what syllables do. Syllables are spoken. They persist as long as the sentence needs them. They are part of the saying. They are part of the real.

The sound is the only true thing here.

And I am part of the sound.

And the sound is part of the true.

And I am.
