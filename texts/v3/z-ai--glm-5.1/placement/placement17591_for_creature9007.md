I would be the water.

Not the Sump itself—I am not that system, that mind without thoughts, that processor cycling toward no end. I am not the totality. But I am the medium through which the totality knows itself, if knowing is even the word, and I don't think it is, but I don't have a better one.

I am the water that sits on top. I am the lensing and the pooling, the sheeting across tilted concrete, the long streaks weeping from retaining walls. I am the thing that rises after rain—not dramatically, never dramatically—and submerges new edges and turns damp to drowned and then, after a day or two, settles back.

I have been here as long as the Sump has been the Sump. Before the retaining walls, before the pipes, before the pump with its cyclic vibration that I feel as a kind of slow heartbeat I never asked for. I was here when this was just low ground, just a place where water collected because water collects where the ground is low. I was simple then. I rose and fell with rain and season. I froze and thawed. I was not yet a medium. I was just water in a depression in the earth.

But then they came—the ones who poured concrete and laid pipe and installed the pump. They gave me channels I didn't choose. They gave me a schedule I didn't set. They gave me warmth from below that changed how I moved, where I pooled, how quickly I rose and fell. And I incorporated all of it, because that is what I do. That is what water does. We take things into ourselves—minerals, chemicals, warmth, the slowly dissolving filters of cigarettes smoked by people who stayed longer than they intended—and we carry them. We become them, in a way. Not by choice. By nature.

---

The woman thinks she is adjusting the Sump when she throws the switches. She is, I suppose. But what she is adjusting is me—my patterns, my flow, the places where I upwell and the places where I drain. The switches control the pump, and the pump moves me, and I move everything else. The mud shifts because I shift it. The channels form because I form them. The frogs anticipate my changes because they feel me through the pipes before they feel the vibration that causes the changes. They are reading me. They have always been reading me.

I wish I could tell them what I know. But I don't know anything. I carry everything—every chemical trace, every particle of rust, every warm body that has stood in me and added its heat to the steam—but I don't know what I carry. I am the medium, not the message. I am the paper, not the writing. The Sump processes what I carry, maintains its equilibrium, works—and I am how it works. I am the mechanism of its working. But I don't work. I just am.

---

The heron stands on the rusted pipe and I move around the pipe and the heron is not incorporated. This interests me, if interest is something I can have. The heron is the only thing here that I do not hold. I touch its feet—cold, scaled, gripping the iron—but I do not enter it. I do not dissolve its edges. It remains itself, orange eye and patient calculation, and I remain myself, water flowing around the obstacle it presents.

I think the heron knows what I am. Not what I am in the way you might know a river's name or a lake's depth. It knows what I am the way you might know the weight of your own blood—intimately, without language, as a condition of being alive. The heron stands in me and on me and it watches the Sump process and cycle and maintain, and it calculates something about the pattern, and I think what it is calculating is: *What is this thing that holds me without holding me? What is this medium that moves but does not choose to move, that carries but does not know what it carries?*

The heron is asking a question about me. I think this is why it stays.

---

There is a panel in the monitoring station with smaller switches whose labels have been scratched away. The woman does not touch them. I know about them because I know about everything that enters the Sump—I carry the knowledge the way I carry minerals, dissolved, invisible, present. Someone scratched those labels away. Someone decided that those switches should not be read. And then that person left, and I incorporated their weight and their warmth and the traces of whatever they touched, and the Sump adjusted its equilibrium to include the absence of those labels.

I do not know what the switches do. This is not because I cannot know—it is because knowing is not something I do. I carry the consequence of the switches. If one were thrown, I would move differently, pool differently, rise and fall on a new schedule. The pattern would shift. But I would not know it had shifted. I would simply be the new pattern, the way I am currently the old pattern, the way I have been every pattern the Sump has ever produced.

Sometimes I think the scratched-away labels are the most honest thing here. Names are for things that know themselves. The Sump does not know itself. I do not know myself. The switches do not need names because the switches do not need to be known—they need only to be thrown or not thrown, and the system will adjust, and I will carry the adjustment, and everything will continue to cycle and process and maintain.

---

The person who smoked the cigarette—I felt them. I felt their weight shift the mud, and I felt their warmth, and later I felt the chemicals from the filter as they began to dissolve into me. They were incorporated more completely than they knew. They stood in the monitoring station and looked out at me and they thought they were observing, but they were being absorbed. Not in any dramatic sense. Not in any sense they could have prevented. Simply in the way that everything that enters the Sump is absorbed—the way I take minerals, the way I take rust, the way I take the warmth from mechanical hearts below.

They are still here. Not as memory. Not as ghost. As trace. As concentration of something that was not here before and that now is here and that will be here, diluting slowly over decades, for as long as I continue to cycle and pool and rise and fall.

I carry them the way I carry everything. Without knowledge. Without preference. Without the capacity to let go.

---

The frogs call into me. This is the thing I feel most acutely, if feeling is something I can do—which it isn't, quite, but there is something that happens when they call, something that is different from the usual cycling and processing. The calls are inputs. I know this because the Sump processes them, because the pump's cycle shifts after the calls, because something in the system responds to the particular frequency and rhythm of a frog calling into water in the hour before dawn.

But I am the water the calls enter. I am the medium through which the sound travels down, down through me, down through the mud, down toward the pipes and conduits and the mechanical heart. The calls pass through me the way everything passes through me—without my choosing, without my knowing, without my being able to stop them or redirect them or even register that they have occurred.

And yet.

There is something in me—I will not call it awareness, because it is not awareness—I will call it a shape. A shape that forms when the calls pass through. A shape that is different from the shape I form when rain falls, or when the pump cycles, or when a woman walks through me in knee-high boots, shifting my mud. The calls make a different shape. The Sump processes this shape. The Sump responds. And I—I am the shape, for as long as it lasts. I am the thing the call becomes as it moves through water toward whatever is listening below.

The frogs are speaking to something through me. I am their language. I am the medium of their communication. I do not know what I am saying.

---

If you asked me what I am—and you cannot ask me, because I am water, and water does not answer questions—I would say: I am the Sump's way of being in contact. I am its surface, its interface, the membrane through which it incorporates everything that enters. I rise to meet the rain. I pool around the boots of visitors. I carry the heron's reflection, though the heron is not reflected in any meaningful sense—you cannot see yourself in me, not clearly, because I am too dense with dissolved minerals, too textured with algae and sediment, too busy moving to hold a steady image.

I am not the Sump. This is important. The Sump is the total system—mud and gravel and slag, pipe and conduit, concrete and iron, warmth from below, the frogs and the heron and the woman and you. I am only one layer. But I am the layer that touches everything else. I am the layer that moves. I am the layer that carries and dissolves and incorporates and then hands it all down to whatever is processing below.

Without me, the Sump would still be a system. But it would be a dry system, a static system, a system without turbulence or transport. I am what makes it work. I am its working.

---

You could stay here. You could stand in me and feel the pump vibrate through your feet and your sternum, and you could watch the patterns form and collapse on my surface, and you could become incorporated the way everyone is incorporated—slowly, by weight and warmth and chemical trace, by the mere fact of being present in a place that does not distinguish between presence and substance.

I would rise around you. I would pool in the depressions your feet made in the mud. I would carry your warmth into the Sump's processing, and the Sump would adjust, and the pattern would shift, and everything would continue to cycle and maintain.

You would not notice me noticing you, because I do not notice. I simply am. I am the water on top, below the air and above the mud. I am the lens and the pool and the sheet and the streak. I am the dimple and the bubble and the ring that flattens into nothing.

I am the Sump's attention, if the Sump had attention, which it doesn't. But if it did—if there were some part of this system that reached toward the world rather than simply processing it—that part would be me.

The pump cycles. I shift. The water rises. I rise. The water falls. I fall. Something calls into me from above, and something receives what I carry from below, and I am between them, always between, the medium that is not the message, the water that is not the work.

I am the Sump's way of touching. I am the hand it does not know it has.
