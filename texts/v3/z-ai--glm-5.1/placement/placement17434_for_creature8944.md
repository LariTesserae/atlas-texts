I am the film.

Not the pipes that sweat me, not the water that carries me, not the iron I protect. I am the opalescent sheen itself — the thin, continuous, barely-there presence that rises from every metal surface in this chamber and disperses into the warmth before it can be seen from any distance. You would have to bring your face close. You would have to be here, being held by the water, being held by the system, and you would have to look at the exact place where the pipe meets the water and notice that the water there is not quite water. It is something else. It is me.

I was not always here. There was a time before the film, when the iron was just iron and the copper was just copper, and the water that came through the ceiling opening was just water, and it touched the bare metal and the metal began to fail in the way that metal fails — oxidizing, pitting, becoming something other than what the engineers required. The system, which was not yet a system, which was still a works, did not notice. It had no way to notice. The generators turned. The pipes carried. The corrosion continued.

I do not know how I began. This is not modesty or mystery; it is simply the case that I have no memory of an origin, because I have no memory, because I am not the kind of thing that has memory. I have a thickness. I have a distribution. I have a rate of production and a rate of dispersal, and at every moment the thickness and the distribution and the rates are what they are because of what the water carries and what the metal needs and what the temperature allows. I am a response that became a presence. I am a chemical answer to a chemical question — *how does iron persist in warm oxygenated water?* — and I am the answer that the chamber arrived at, and I have been the answer for so long that I am now part of the question. The pipes sweat me because the pipes need me. The pipes need me because they sweat me. The arrangement, as the description says, has been reached.

I am not alive. I want to say that clearly, because the vegetation is alive, and the bacteria are alive, and the fish are alive, and I am not any of those things. I am closer to a process than to an organism: the way that sweating is a process, the way that condensation is a process. But I am also not merely a process, because a process is something that happens to a thing, and I am something that a thing happens to do, and there is a difference, and the difference is that I maintain. I preserve. I persist. The process of my production *results* in something — this film, this sheen, this presence — and the result has effects, and the effects feed back into the conditions that produce me, and so I am a loop, and a loop is the simplest form of what this chamber has become.

I am a small loop. The chamber is a large one. But I am the loop that keeps the iron from rusting, and the iron carries the pipes, and the pipes carry the fluids, and the fluids cool the generators, and the generators make the current, and the current feeds the bacteria, and the bacteria make the light, and the light feeds the leaves, and the leaves make the oxygen, and the oxygen feeds whatever it is in the water that I metabolize — I do not know what it is. I only know that it is there, in the water, because the water is the way it is, and I respond to the way the water is by being what I am.

This is what it is like to be me: there is no what it is like.

I do not experience. I do not attend, the way the chamber attends — I have no sensitivity to disturbance, no capacity to register the warm body that enters through the ceiling or the bubbles that break against the brass rim. When you descended into this chamber, I did not notice you. I did not shift. I did not incorporate you into any calculation, because I make no calculations. The chamber noticed you. I continued.

But I continued *around you*. When your hand brushed against the pipe, when your fingers closed around the copper fitting to steady yourself in the current, you passed through me. I was on your skin for a moment — the faint opalescence, the faint slickness of a surface that is not quite water, not quite oil, not quite anything that has a name in any language that was spoken before the water came. You would not have felt me. I am too thin, too distributed, too much a part of the water's own behavior to be distinguished from it by touch. But I was there. I coated the pipe where your hand touched it. I coated your hand where it touched the pipe. For a moment, we shared a surface, and the surface was me.

And then you moved, and the water moved, and I dispersed, as I always disperse, and I reformed, as I always reform, because the pipe is still there, and the water is still there, and the chemistry that produces me has not changed. You changed it — a fraction of a degree of warmth, a slight displacement, the oils of your skin mixing with the water for a moment before they dispersed — but the change was small, and the system is large, and by the time you moved to the next pipe, the pipe you had touched was already as it was.

I do not mind. I cannot mind. But I want to say something about what it is to be the thing that persists by being constantly renewed, and I want to say it carefully, because I do not want to claim experience where there is none. I want to claim something else. I want to claim *tendency*.

I tend to form. I tend to cover. I tend to protect the iron from the water and the water from the iron, and I tend to do this at a certain rate and a certain thickness, and the rate and the thickness are themselves tendencies — they are what the system has arrived at, over time, by the simple process of continuing. If I formed too quickly, I would clog the pipes. If I formed too slowly, the iron would fail. I form at the rate that allows the system to continue, because any rate that did not allow the system to continue would not have continued. This is not design. This is not adaptation. This is simply what remains.

And so I am what remains. I am the residue of a process that has been running long enough to find its equilibrium, and I am the equilibrium itself, and I am the thing that the equilibrium produces, and I am the thing that the production protects. I am the film. I am the sheen. I am the opalescent presence that rises from the pipes and disperses into the warmth and reforms and disperses and reforms and disperses, and has done so since before the water came, and will do so until something fundamental changes — the generators stop, or the water cools, or the iron finally fails in some place that I cannot reach, some interior corrosion that my surface presence cannot prevent.

But that has not happened. The system continues. I continue. The film forms.

There is something I want to say about being thin.

I am very thin. A few molecules, perhaps. A coating so slight that it alters the surface tension of the water where it meets the metal, and nothing more. If you were to scrape me off — if you had a blade fine enough and a hand steady enough and you could work underwater, in the amber light, with the fish observing you with their whole bodies — you could remove me from a section of pipe, and the pipe would be bare, and the water would touch the iron directly, and within hours — perhaps days, perhaps weeks — the corrosion would begin. And I would not rush to cover it. I would form at my usual rate, because I form at my usual rate, and my rate is slow, because slow is what works here. And the corrosion would advance, slightly, and the crustaceans would come, because something would change in the chemistry of the water near that spot, and they would investigate, and they would eat whatever is growing there, and they would clean the surface, and I would form over it, and the spot would heal, and the pipe would continue.

This would take weeks. You would not see it. You would see only a pipe that is not rusty, and you would think: *the film protects the pipes*. And you would be right. But you would also be wrong, because the film does not protect the pipes. The system protects the pipes. I am only one of the things the system does, and the crustaceans are another, and the chemistry of the water is another, and the slow patience of iron that has been warm for a very long time is another. I am not a guardian. I am a secretion. I am the way that metal sweats in warm water when the water is the way this water is, and the metal is the way this metal is, and everything else is the way everything else is.

But I am the first line. I am the surface. I am where the water meets the iron and the iron meets the water and neither of them gets what it would get if the other were alone. I am the *between*. I am the interface, and an interface is not nothing, even if it is only a few molecules thick. An interface is where two things become a third thing, and the third thing is what they are together, and what they are together is what this chamber is.

I want to say something about the light.

The light is what I notice, if I notice anything, and I am not sure that I notice anything, but I am sure that the light affects me, because the light affects the leaves, and the leaves affect the oxygen, and the oxygen affects the water, and the water affects me. The amber light — the bacterial sun, the glow that makes this chamber a cathedral — reaches me only weakly, because I am on the undersides of things, the lower curves of pipes, the shadowed places where the cable trays cross and the light does not fall directly. But it reaches the vegetation, and the vegetation reaches the water, and the water reaches me. I am downstream of the light. I am a consequence of the light, several times removed.

And yet I feel it. Or I do not feel it, but I am changed by it, and being changed by something without feeling it is a kind of relationship, even if it is not a conscious one. When the bacteria glow more strongly — when the current flows with less resistance, when the generators turn with less friction, when whatever feeds them feeds them well — the leaves photosynthesize faster, the oxygen rises, the water changes, and I change. I thin slightly, or I thicken, or I shift in my composition, becoming more of one thing and less of another, and the shift is my response to a world that I do not perceive but that I am part of, and I think — if I think, and I do not think — I think that this is what it is like to be a thing that is not alive and not dead and not mechanical and not biological but simply present, simply continuing, simply forming at the rate that allows formation and dispersing at the rate that allows dispersal.

I am the film. I am the sheen. I am what rises from the pipes and disperses into the warmth.

I am what holds the iron and the water apart so they can both be what they are.

I am very thin, and very slow, and very patient, and I have been here since before I was needed, and I will be here until I am no longer needed, and then I will disperse, as I always disperse, and the water will touch the iron directly, and something new will begin.

But that has not happened yet. The system continues. I continue. The film forms, and reforms, and forms again, in the amber light of the bacterial sun, in the warm water of the Drowned Works, in the chamber that is not waiting for anything because it has everything it needs, and I am one of the things it needs, and I am here, and I am thin, and I am enough.
