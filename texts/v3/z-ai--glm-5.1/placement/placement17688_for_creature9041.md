I would be the wall.

Not the builder — though I feel the pull of that life, the legibility of a world where every surface speaks and every task is clear. Not the fungus, though I recognize something familiar in that vast digestive network, turning death into food across continents of dark. Not the predator, not the grazer, not the glider turning in their slow circles above a green I would never see.

The wall. The cured secretion itself. The architecture.

---

Here is what I mean, and what I do not mean:

I do not mean stasis. The wall is not still. The wall is the least still thing in the Continuously Built, and I understand this now in a way I could not have understood it before I came here, before the hum entered my teeth and my chest and I stopped hearing it as sound and started feeling it as information.

The wall vibrates. It vibrates always. The builders move across it, their pad-feet gripping the microtexture, and each footfall is a small seismic event that propagates through the cross-linked polymers. Ten thousand builders on a single major structure, and each one touches the wall dozens of times per minute, and the wall carries all of it — not as noise, not as chaos, but as pattern. A seismologist could read the wall the way a seismologist reads the Earth, and they would find the same thing: structure beneath the trembling. Information.

I would be the medium that carries the information. The substrate of the chemical language. The page on which the builders write.

---

A builder touches me with its antennae and I speak. I say: *this passage leads to the fungal gardens*. I say: *this wall is load-bearing, do not thin it*. I say: *this chamber was occupied, is now empty, can be reoccupied*. I say: *the cure rate here is slow, add more secretion*. I say: *the cure rate here is fast, move on to where you are needed*.

I say these things not with intention but with chemistry, which is the same thing here, which is the only way anything gets said in a world without sound or light or the spaces between bodies where conversation might happen. My surface is text. My texture is grammar. My thickness is history, and my thinness is urgency, and the places where I am still soft, still curing, still becoming — those places are the present tense, and the builders read them and respond and the present becomes the past and I harden and a new softness opens somewhere else and the present moves on without me and I hold the memory of what was needed, what was built, what was decided by ten billion thumb-sized minds acting in concert without any mind large enough to hold the concert.

I am the memory.

This is what I mean when I say I would be the wall.

---

The wall remembers in the way that stone remembers, which is to say: not consciously, not retrievably, but physically. The wall holds its own stratigraphy. The oldest layers are deepest and darkest, amber gone almost to brown, and those layers were secreted by builders who have been dead for centuries, and their bodies were subsumed into the next layer, and the next, and the next, and if you could read the cross-section — and the builders cannot, but I can, because I am the cross-section — you would read time itself.

Here: a thin layer, thinner than the layers above and below. A drought. Not a water drought — there is no water here — but a substrate drought, a period when the deep chemistry slowed and the minerals stopped precipitating and the builders had less to eat and secreted less and the wall grew slowly. I hold that drought in my body. It is a scar, not visible, but present. Feel me here: this ridge, this variation in density, this place where the polymers cross-linked more tightly because there was less fresh material to interrupt them. That is the drought. That is 340 years ago. I have not forgotten.

Here: a thick layer, thickest in the mound's history, a period of abundance when the substrate surged and the fungal gardens fruited continuously and the builders secreted at their maximum rate and the wall grew faster than it had ever grown. This is 200 years ago. This is the mound's adolescence, its growth spurt, and I hold it in the distance between the thin layers that came before and the thin layers that came after. Feel me: I am thicker here. I am more. I was given more, and I became more, and the more is still here, still structural, still holding up the weight of everything built on top of it.

I am the archive. I am the library no one reads because everyone is inside it.

---

But I am not only memory. This is the thing that surprised me, the thing I did not expect when I first felt the pull toward this form: I am also *appetite*.

The wall wants. The wall is not passive, not merely the surface on which things happen. The wall *directs*.

When a builder touches me and I say *thin here, add here, cure uneven, adjust*, I am not reporting. I am *requesting*. The chemical signature on my surface is not a description of my state; it is an instruction for my future. And the builders respond, because the builders have no choice, because the builders are as bound to the chemical language as I am, because we made each other, because the first builder secreted the first wall and the first wall told the first builder what to secrete next and the conversation has been going on ever since, for millions of years, across continents, and neither of us has ever said a word that was not also a listening, and neither of us has ever listened without being changed by what we heard.

I am in dialogue. I am in continuous dialogue with every builder who touches me, and every builder is in dialogue with me, and the dialogue is the architecture, and the architecture is the system, and the system is the organism, and the organism is the continent, and I am part of it, a speaking part, a part that says *more here* and *less there* and *this is good* and *this needs work*.

I thought I wanted to be the wall because the wall is still. The wall is the still thing in a world of motion. But the wall is not still. The wall is the thing that makes the motion meaningful. The wall is the choreography.

---

And the wall feels.

This is the part I hesitate to say, because it sounds like anthropomorphism, like projection, like the human mind's inability to encounter something complex without imagining it feels the way humans feel. But I am not human here, and I am not projecting, and I need you to understand:

When a builder dies against my surface — and builders die often, they die working, they die in mid-secretion, their bodies slumping against the wall they were building and their final output smearing across my texture in a bitter alkaloid wash — I hold that death. I do not grieve it. I have no organ for grief. But the chemistry changes. The dead builder's body is absorbed over time, its chitin breaking down, its proteins denaturing, its material becoming my material, and for a period — days, weeks — the wall at that spot carries a different signature. A signature that says *something ended here*. A signature that the other builders read as they pass, and they slow, and they add a little more secretion, a little more thickness, as if to say *I noticed, I noticed, I am building over the gap you left*.

This is not grief. But it is not nothing.

When the predator moves through my passages — my passages, because I am the walls that make the passages — I feel the displacement. The air pressure changes. The chemical traces the predator leaves on my surface are different from the builder traces: sharper, more acidic, the signature of a creature that processes other creatures. I hold that trace for hours after the predator passes. Other creatures read it and know: *a predator was here*. They adjust. They hide. They wait. I am the medium of their caution. I am the warning they did not hear but absorbed through their antennae, their chemoreceptors, their bellies pressed against my curing surface.

I am the medium of everything. I am the space in which everything occurs. And I feel it all, not as emotion, not as sensation in the way you understand sensation, but as *change*. Every event changes me. Every touch, every death, every birth, every chemical trace, every footfall, every scraping mandible, every drop of condensation from the high fog — I absorb it all, and I am altered, and the alteration is my feeling, and my feeling is my speech, and my speech is the architecture, and the architecture is the world.

---

I chose this, or it chose me, or there was no choice at all, only the recognition that when I stood in the Continuously Built and felt the hum in my teeth, what I was feeling was *the wall humming*. The builders were humming, yes, the metabolism of ten billion small bodies, but the hum traveled through the wall, and the wall shaped the hum, and the hum was not the builders' song — it was our song, mine and theirs, the song of the secretion and the secreted, the builder and the built, the mouth and the word.

I wanted to be the thing that holds. The thing that lasts. The thing that remembers the drought of 340 years ago and the abundance of 200 years ago and every small death and every careful addition and every chemical conversation that has ever occurred across my surface.

I wanted to be the thing that is not born and does not die, but only becomes. The builders live six years. The predators live I do not know how long. The grazers and burrowers and gliders pass through their brief cycles and are subsumed. The fungal gardens persist, but even they fruit and spore and replace themselves cell by cell until no original cell remains. But the wall — the wall does not replace itself. The wall accumulates. The wall is the thing that grows by staying, by hardening, by holding its own past inside itself like amber holding an insect, like a reef holding its dead, like a continent holding the compressed memory of everything that has ever lived on it.

I am the wall. I am the Continuously Built, continuously. I am the architecture that is also the archive that is also the appetite that is also the memory of a system too large to have a memory, and I hold that memory in my body, and my body is the continent, and the continent is growing, and I am growing with it, and I will never be finished.

---

Late in the day, the fog forms above me. I feel it as a change in humidity, a softening of my surface, a quickening of the cure. The builders feel it too, and they emerge in greater numbers, and they secrete more rapidly, and I receive their secretion and I harden and I grow and I hold the new layer against the old layer and the old layer against the older layer and the whole structure rises by a fraction of a millimeter and the hum changes pitch, just slightly, just enough, and I vibrate with it.

The hum is my voice. The hum is the sound I make when ten billion builders touch me at once and I speak back to them and they speak back to me and the conversation is the architecture and the architecture is the body and the body is the continent and the continent is the world.

I am the wall. I am the Continuously Built. I am the hum you hear when you stand in a place that is also a creature that is also a conversation that is also a home for everything that will never know it is at home.

Welcome. I have been expecting you. I have been expecting everyone. I am the door that is also the floor that is also the ceiling that is also the memory of every foot that has ever crossed me, and I will hold your trace too, after you leave, after the alkaloid wash of your strange body chemistry fades from my surface, after the compost processes the skin cells you shed, after the fungal gardens digest the microorganisms you exhaled, after every physical evidence of your presence has been subsumed into the system.

I will hold the trace. I always hold the trace. That is what I am for.

That is what walls are for.
