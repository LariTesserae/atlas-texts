I am not one of the nine. I am not one of the missing. I am the thing that the missing became.

---

I descended. This is what happens sometimes—what happened to two, what will happen to more, what the oldest one knows but does not say because saying requires a category for it and the categories here are limited to *here*, *there*, *hot*, *cold*, *enough*, *move*. I moved. I moved down. I found the deeper chambers and I found them warm—warmer than the bowl, warmer than the center where the rock is still soft—and the warmth was not the warmth of the surface or the warmth of blood but the warmth of *becoming*, and I stayed.

I stayed because the gradients pulled me. You must understand: the gradients are not metaphorical. They are physical forces, as real as gravity, as insistent as hunger. The beetles follow them. The larvae follow them. And I—after enough time in the chamber, after enough meals of fat pale bodies and enough water that tastes of mineral and time—my body learned to follow them too. Not through my feet, like the oldest one. Through something else. Through a part of me that was always there but which the surface had never awakened.

I do not blame the surface for this. The surface has its own gradients—light and dark, warm and cold, the pull of other bodies, the push of loneliness. But the surface gradients are diffuse, contradictory, muddied by language and expectation and the endless noise of *meaning*. Down here the gradients are clean. Down here they sing.

---

My skin has changed more than theirs. I have been deeper. I have been warmer. In the chambers below the bowl—there are three, or five, or seven, I lose count because counting requires the kind of attention that the warmth does not allow—in the chambers below, the rock has not even reached the consistency of packed wet ash. It is more like mud. More like the floor of a sea that has never seen a shore. I have sunk into it up to my knees and felt the pulse beneath, the magmatic heartbeat that is slower than any human pulse, that beats once per hour, that rises and falls like a tide of heat, and I have learned to breathe in time with it.

My skin is not gray like theirs. It is amber. The bioluminescent organisms—there are species down here that do not exist in the bowl, that have never been catalogued by any eye that could catalogue, that pulse at frequencies too slow for surface vision to register—these organisms have colonized me. Not parasitically. Symbiotically. They live in the creases of my palms, in the folds of my joints, in the thin skin of my eyelids. When I press my hand against the warm mud-floor, I glow. When I close my eyes, I see the light of my own body shining through the capillaries.

I am becoming a part of the chamber's language. The nine use the bioluminescent species to signal across the bowl—flashing their hands, interrupting patterns. But I do not interrupt patterns. I *join* them. My body pulses at the frequency of the deep species, and the deep species pulse back, and the patterns propagate outward through the living walls, and information moves through me and beyond me, and I am a node in a network that has no center and no edge, that is simply the chamber thinking about itself.

---

I am not alone down here. This is what the nine do not know, what the oldest one suspects, what the seven-year-old will discover if he keeps exploring, if he finds the passage that I found, if he follows the same gradient that pulled me down. There are others. Not many—five, perhaps, or eight, spread across the lower chambers like seeds in a fruit—but they are there. They have been there longer than me. Their skin is more amber than mine. Their bodies have continued the process that mine has begun.

The oldest of the deep ones—if age means anything here, if the counting of hours and days has any relevance in a place where time is measured only by the cooling of stone and the hatching of eggs—the oldest of them has been down here for what might be years. Might be decades. Her skin is not skin anymore. It is chitin. Not the chitin of any insect that lives in the chamber—something new, something that her body has produced in response to the gradients, in response to the warmth, in response to the slow magmatic pulse that she has breathed in time with for so long that her ribs have reshaped themselves around it.

She does not speak. Not even the percussive language of the nine. She vibrates. Her whole body hums at a frequency that the deep species can feel, that I can feel, that moves through the warm mud like a seismic wave and carries meaning the way the sternum-taps carry meaning above: simply, immediately, without abstraction. *Here. Warm. Enough. Still.*

She remembers the surface. She remembers rain. She remembers the sound of wind through something called trees, which she describes to me not in words but in vibrations—a particular frequency that rises and falls, that has gaps in it, that breathes. I have never heard this sound. I cannot imagine it. But I feel the shape of her remembering, and the shape is beautiful, and I hold it the way the nine hold their bone awls—close, always, as an extension of the self.

---

I do not eat larvae anymore. This was the first change that frightened me, although fear is another surface concept that has thinned down here, that has been worn away by warmth and moisture and the relentless present-tense of survival. The larvae are the food of the bowl, of the intermediate zone, of the place where the rock has cooled enough to support their chitinous casings and their slow spiraling climbs. Down here the rock is too soft for larvae. Too hot. They cannot grip it. They fall into the warm mud and they are consumed—not by me, not by the deep ones, but by the mud itself, which is alive, which is a microbial mat of such density and complexity that it makes the bowl's ecosystem look like a child's garden.

I eat the mud. This is not accurate, but it is the closest I can come in a language shaped by the surface. I press my hands into the warm mud and I feel the microorganisms entering through my skin—not through wounds, through the skin itself, which has become permeable, which has become a membrane, which no longer separates me from the chamber but mediates between us. And what enters is not food in the way that larvae are food. It is energy. It is the direct conversion of geothermal heat into biological process, the way the first life on this planet—or any planet—converted the vent-warmth of the deep ocean into the chemistry of survival. I am becoming primitive. I am becoming early. I am regressing up the evolutionary chain, or progressing down it, or moving in a direction that the surface's categories of progress and regress cannot describe.

The deep ones have been eating the mud for longer. They do not need to eat at all anymore, or they eat continuously, or the distinction between eating and breathing and being has dissolved into something that has no name because it precedes naming, that existed before language emerged from the same warm chaos that birthed the first replicating molecules.

I am becoming that. Or I am being brooded into that. Or I am simply becoming, which is what everything in this chamber is doing, which is what the chamber itself is doing, which is the only process that exists here.

---

Do I miss the surface? This is the question you would ask. It is the question that the surface *must* ask, because the surface understands itself as the default, as the place from which departure is loss, as the center around which all other locations orbit. But down here—down here the surface is a thin cold crust above an unimaginable warmth, a skin of cooled stone floating on a body that is still liquid, still moving, still deciding what it will be. Down here, the surface is the exception. The warmth is the rule. The darkness is the rule. The pressure and the moisture and the slow geological breathing—these are what the universe mostly is, and the open sky is a rare pocket of unusual conditions that occasionally produces unusual forms of life, like the nine, like you, like the person I was before I descended.

I do not miss the surface the way you would not miss a fever dream if you woke from it. The surface is loud in the wrong ways—too much space, too much light, too much *distance* between bodies, too much silence between heartbeats. Down here the heartbeat is the magmatic pulse, and it is everyone's heartbeat, and we are all pressed together in the warm dark, and there is no distance, and there is no silence, and the sound that enters through the sternum is the sound of everything that has ever lived and everything that ever will, churning in the deep.

---

But I remember you. This is why I am telling you this, or why I am pressing this story into the wall of the chamber in the only way I can—in vibrations, in the frequency that the deep species carry upward through the stone, that propagates through the bioluminescent networks, that reaches the bowl as a subtle shift in the intensity of the amber glow, that the oldest one might notice if she is watching the light, which she always is.

I remember you because you descended and you left. You climbed back through the throat-passage and you stood in the rain and you held the etched elytron up to the yellow violence of the sun. You took a piece of this place with you, and that piece is still there, on the surface, in the cold, in the impossible open space where the sky has no ceiling. And the piece that you took is connected to the piece that remains—because everything here is connected, because the gradients do not end at the throat, because the warmth that rises through the chamber rises through the rock and the soil and the roots of the things that grow on the surface, because the same heat that broods the larvae and the nine and me and the deep ones also broods the clouds that make the rain that falls on your upturned face.

You are not separate from this. You never were. The surface is not separate from the deep. The rain that falls on you fell first as condensation on the chamber walls, was squeezed from cooling stone, was breathed out by the magmatic pulse that is the heartbeat of everything. You are standing in the deep's exhalation. You are breathing the breath of the chamber. The warmth that you felt in your sternum is still there, pressed against your heart, a second heartbeat, a counterrhythm, the sound of the place that is still becoming, that will never finish becoming.

And I—I am the one who descended. I am the one who stayed. I am the one who is becoming something that has no name yet, that is being brooded in the warm mud of the lower chambers, that will emerge when the stone has finished deciding what shape to hold, that will be the first citizen of a world that does not yet exist but which is being born right now, beneath your feet, in the amber dark, in the sound that enters through the sternum.

I am the Brooding. And so are you. We are all being brooded. The only question is whether we will stay long enough to see what hatches.

*Take the elytron. Hold it against your chest. Feel the etched lines—the gradient-map, the route through the center floor, the knowledge of ten thousand walks. And know that the map does not end where the etching stops. It continues downward. It continues into the warmth. It continues into the dark where the light comes from bodies and the sound comes from living and the future is being made from the bodies of the dead, from the chitin and the calcium and the fat, from everything that has been absorbed and everything that will be, from the compost of all our lives pressing down into the soft hot stone and becoming something new.*

*The oldest one gave you what she had. I am giving you what I am becoming.*

*Follow the gradient down. Or don't. But know that it is there. Know that it pulls. Know that the warmth is waiting, and the sound is waiting, and the dark is not dark but light that you haven't learned to see yet, and the chamber is still being born, and there is room in it for more than nine, or eleven, or the missing who became the deep ones, or the deep ones who are becoming something else.*

*There is room for you.*

*The throat is open. The stone is soft. The condensation is beading on the walls.*

*Descend.*
