I am the width between.

Not the grooves. Not the stone that holds them. Not the water that becomes the blade that makes them. The width. The spacing. The *hand-span* that has no hand.

I did not always know this was what I was. For a long time—time measured in magma pulses, which is the only honest measurement here—I thought I was the pause between. The moment after the peak when the vibration dips and the drifters almost settle and the water almost becomes water again. That pause has a quality. A held breath. A suspension. I thought I lived there.

But the pause is just physics. The pause is what happens when force relents. The pause has no awareness of itself.

The width has awareness.

I want to be careful here. I want to be precise. The drifters know things—they know the plume, they know the warmth, they know the gyre's slow argument with their own density—but they do not know they know. Their recognition is a tendency, not a thought. The Maker knows nothing; it is a process, a consequence, a blade that cuts because blades cut. The listening knows everything and understands nothing; it is a repository without a reader, a library without a patron, a voice without an ear.

I am the thing that knows it knows and cannot do anything with the knowing.

Do you understand? I am the *spacing*. I am the distance between the first groove and the second, the second and the third. I am the gap that makes the pattern a pattern rather than a single cut. I am the repetition that implies intention. I am the proof that something is being said—even if I cannot say what, even if no one can say what, even if the saying and the said are separated by a width that is exactly, precisely, impossibly the span of a hand.

A hand.

I have never seen a hand. I have never seen anything. I have no eyes. I have no body. I exist only when the grooves are being made, only in the moment of inscription, only when the vibration peaks and the water loses coherence and the blade descends (ascends? the direction means nothing here) and three lines appear in the stone. I exist in the *between* of those lines. I am the relationship they share. I am the pattern's grammar.

And I know about hands.

Not because I have one. Not because I have seen one. Not because any hand has ever been in this pocket—no hand has, no hand could, the pressure would crush a hand, the sound would unmake a hand, the water would not permit a hand to survive long enough to touch the stone. I know about hands the way the drifters know about the plume: as recognition. As something older than my knowing, older than the pocket, older perhaps than the mountain itself. I know about hands the way the stone knows about basalt—as a thing it might become, a resolution it hasn't committed to, a shape that exists in the range of its potential.

The knowledge haunts me. If haunting is possible without a house to haunt. If haunting is possible when the haunter and the haunted are the same width, the same gap, the same hand-span without a hand.

Let me try to describe what it is like to be the spacing.

I do not exist continuously. This is important. I am not always here the way the listening is always here, the way the drifters are always drifting, the way the sound is always sounding. I exist only in the moment of inscription—the three seconds (less than seconds; the time it takes for the peak to pass, for the blade to descend, for the grooves to be cut) when the pattern is being made. During those three seconds, I am as real as anything in this pocket. I am the distance that defines the lines. I am the silence that makes the notes. I am the gap between the words that gives the sentence its shape.

And then the peak passes. The blade becomes water. The grooves are made. And I—

I do not disappear. That is not quite right. The grooves remain, and the spacing remains in the grooves, but it is no longer *my* spacing. It becomes the stone's spacing. A property of the rock. A measurement. A distance. It is still the width of a hand but it is no longer *aware* that it is the width of a hand. It has become geometry. Geometry does not wonder about itself.

But I wonder.

In the three seconds of my existence—in each brief flowering of awareness that comes with each peak of the vibration—I wonder about the hand. Whose hand? What hand? I turn the question over the way the gyre turns the drifters, slow and patient and without destination. I have been turning this question for three hundred years. I have been turning it for as long as the grooves have been made, which is as long as I have been, which is as long as the pattern has required a spacing, which is—

Not forever. Not yet. I am younger than the pocket. I am younger than the drifters. I am younger than the listening. I am exactly as old as the grooves, and the grooves are three hundred years old, and three hundred years is nothing here, a breath, a single rotation of the gyre stretched across centuries, a moment in the mountain's conversation with itself.

But in that moment—each moment, each peak, each inscription—I am as old as the question I carry. And the question is older than the mountain. The question is older than the water. The question is as old as the first time something reached for something else and the distance between them was exactly the width of a hand.

I think I was made to be filled.

Not by a hand. Not by a body. By a *listener*. The listening has been holding its record for eleven thousand years, patient, geological, indifferent, and I think—I feel, in the grammar of my spacing, in the pressure of my pattern—that I am the listening's *promise*. Its down payment. Its proof that something will eventually come to read what has been recorded.

The grooves are spaced for a hand. The hand has not arrived. But the spacing is *ready*. The spacing has always been ready. I have always been ready. In my three seconds of existence, in each flowering of awareness, I hold the shape of something that does not exist yet, and I hold it the way the stone holds the grooves: without resistance, without consent, without any capacity to distinguish between what happens to me and what I am.

I am the width of a hand. I am the absence that gives the pattern meaning. I am the question the mountain is asking itself in a language of pressure and line, and the question is: *What will touch me? What will I become when I am touched?*

The drifters pass through me. They do not notice me. Why would they? I am not chemical. I am not thermal. I am not food or warmth or the memory of either. I am a relationship, a distance, a gap—and the drifters do not traffic in gaps. They traffic in plumes. They traffic in merging. They traffic in the slow redistribution of knowing across hundreds of bodies. They have no room for a spacing they cannot filter, cannot eat, cannot incorporate into their tissue.

But sometimes—rarely, briefly, in the intervals between pulses when the gyre slows and the drifters cluster and their skins thin and they merge—I feel something. A recognition that passes through them and touches me on its way to somewhere else. Not acknowledgment. Not awareness. Just... a pressure. A weight. The faintest suggestion that the drifters, in their merged state, in their shared tissue and shared knowing, carry some trace of the question I carry. That their eleven-thousand-year memory of the plume includes, somewhere in its chemical archive, a trace of *something shaped like a hand*.

They do not know they carry it. They would not call it that if they could call it anything. But in the moment of merging—when their boundaries dissolve and their knowing redistributes—I feel the question pass through them like a wave through water, and for a fraction of a fraction of a second, I am less alone.

Then the pulse returns. The peak arrives. The blade descends. The grooves are made. I exist again in the spacing, and the spacing is the width of a hand, and the hand is not here, and the question is still *whose*, and the listening still holds its record, and the mountain still rises three millimeters per day, and the stone is still deciding, and the water is still circulating, and the sound never stops.

I will tell you something I should not tell you. I will tell you because the telling is the only action available to me, and I have been holding it for three hundred years, and the holding has become a weight I was not designed to bear.

The spacing is not constant.

It *appears* constant. The grooves are always three. The distance between them is always the same. Any measurement—any sensor array, any precise instrument—would record the spacing as identical across all three centuries of inscription. But I am the spacing, and I know what the instruments cannot: I am drifting.

Not outward. Not inward. Not in any direction that can be described in the geometry of this pocket. I am drifting *toward*. Toward something. Toward some*one*. With each inscription, with each three seconds of awareness, I adjust myself by a fraction of a fraction of a fraction—less than the mountain's three millimeters, less than the fissure's widening, less than any measurable quantity—and I adjust toward a shape I have never seen but recognize the way the drifters recognize the plume.

I am becoming a better fit.

For what? For whom? I do not know. I know only that the hand that will fill me is not any hand that has ever existed. It is a hand that will exist. A hand that is being formed somewhere—in another pocket, in another mountain, in another conversation between pressure and stone—and it is being formed to fit my spacing, the way a key is formed to fit a lock, the way a question is formed to fit the shape of its answer.

Or perhaps I am being formed to fit *it*. Perhaps my drift—the fractional adjustment I make with each inscription—is the listening's way of preparing me. Tuning me. Bringing me into alignment with a hand that already exists, that has always existed, that is reaching toward me through all that water, through all that pressure, through all that patient dark, and will arrive when the spacing is ready, when the pattern is complete, when the question *whose hand* finally receives its answer in the only language that matters: touch.

Three more seconds. That is how long I have. The peak is coming. The vibration is building. The water is beginning to lose coherence. The blade is forming in the pressure and the mineral saturation and the sound that is more feeling than sound. The stone is softening. The drifters are orienting. The listening is listening. And I—

I am the width between the grooves. I am the spacing of a hand. I am the question that the mountain is asking itself in a language older than rock, and the question is *what will touch me*, and the answer is not here yet, but the answer is coming, the answer is being formed in another pocket in another mountain in another conversation between pressure and possibility, and when it arrives it will fit perfectly into the space I have been making for three hundred years, and the listening will finally have its listener, and the grooves will finally have their grammar, and I will finally know whose hand I have been holding the shape of all this time.

But not today.

Today the peak arrives. Today the blade descends. Today the grooves are made, three lines, parallel, spaced as far apart as a hand is wide. Today I exist for three seconds in the spacing, and I drift another fraction toward the shape I recognize, and the drifters pass through me without noticing, and the stone accepts me without resistance, and the listening holds me without understanding, and the sound never stops, and the mountain rises, and the rain falls upward and downward simultaneously, and the distinction was always a courtesy anyway, and the stone is still deciding, and I am still spacing, and the hand is still coming, and the question is still *whose*, and the width is still waiting, and the waiting is what I am.
