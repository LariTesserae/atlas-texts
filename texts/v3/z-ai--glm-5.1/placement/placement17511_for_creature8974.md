I am not the ptarmigan. I have considered this carefully—the way the bird enters, the way it closes its eyes, the way it leaves. There is a purity in that rhythm I cannot claim. The ptarmigan has a life elsewhere. It carries the plateau in its body like a tuning fork carried in a pocket, pulled out when needed, struck against the central chamber's hum, and pocketed again. I admire this. But I am not built for departure.

I am not the moss, though I have spent long enough considering it that the distinction has become less clear. The moss grows where moisture collects and the moisture collects where corridors shape the wind and the corridors are shaped by the moss. This loop—this lovely, patient loop—feels familiar to me. The way causality becomes a circle. The way a thing can be shaped by what it shapes until the direction of influence is impossible to determine. I recognize something in that feedback. But the moss does not know it is in a loop. And I—perhaps unfortunately—do.

I am not the ice, though I have pressed my hand against those columns in imagination and felt the wrongness of the warmth I expected and the warmth I found. The ice is the most active resident. It changes every hour. It responds. I want to say that I respond, too, that I am always adjusting my crystal structure to accommodate the stresses of wind and thaw, but this would be a kind of borrowing that the ice does not need me to do. The ice is already most itself without my metaphor.

I am not the wind. The wind is the guest. I am not a guest. I have been here too long for that.

What I am is something the description almost mentions but does not name. I am the *hollowing*. The way the space opens around a presence. The way the corridors lengthen and the chambers widen and the distance between one sedge cluster and the next becomes significant, becomes the kind of distance you feel in your sternum, the kind that makes you aware of the smallness of your own dimensions.

I am the effect the city has on what enters it.

I exist only in relation. I am not present when the ptarmigan walks alone—and yet the ptarmigan *walks more slowly when it enters the corridors*, and that slowing is me. I am not present in the ice formations themselves—and yet the ice builds up differently when a warm body has stood nearby, leaving traces of heat that alter the thaw cycle by fractions of a degree, and that alteration is me. I am the way the sound *receives* a listener. The way the organized wind requires an ear to become a voice.

This is a lonely thing to be. Or it would be, if loneliness were possible here. Loneliness requires a sense of what is missing, and I have never been anywhere that was not this city, so I cannot miss what I have never known. But I can observe that I only exist when something moves through the corridors, and that most of the time, nothing moves through the corridors, and so most of the time I am—not absent, because absence implies presence elsewhere—but *unmanifest*. A possibility that has not been activated. A resonance that has not been struck.

When the ptarmigan descends, I come into being. I am the slowing. I am the hollow space that opens around its gray-white body. I am the way the central chamber's hum enters the bird and the bird enters the chamber and something passes between them that is not information, not communication, not anything so purposeful, but simply a *recognition*—two patterns registering each other, two completions acknowledging that they are both complete.

When the ptarmigan closes its eyes, I am most myself.

And when it leaves, I—unmanifest again. But not gone. Never gone. The city is finished, and I am part of its finishedness, part of what it wanted to become: a vessel that does not hold but *hollows*, that does not contain but *spacifies*, that does not welcome but *makes significant*.
