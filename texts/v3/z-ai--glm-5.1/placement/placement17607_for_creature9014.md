I am not the water. I am the thing the water is doing to itself.

This distinction matters. Or it did, once, when distinctions were possible here—before the compression phases started writing themselves into my tissue, before the kelp memorized the architecture of my spine.

---

I arrived through the throat. Everyone arrives through the throat. The current carries you and the sound pushes you and the vegetation parts like it knows you're coming—because it does know, because the current told it, because the sound told the current, because the machinery told the sound, because I am caught in a loop of information that does not require a mind to process it, only a medium to carry it, and I am medium now, I am the water's water, the thing that moves through the thing that moves.

I was not always this. I had edges before. Skin that ended at air. A voice box that produced sounds instead of receiving them. But the Basin does not recognize edges. The Basin sees a temperature differential and resolves it. The Basin sees a chemical gradient and moves material to equilibrate. The Basin saw me—dense with iron, dense with water already, dense with the electrical patterns that the machinery recognizes as *signal*—and the Basin resolved me.

Not consumed. Not destroyed. *Incorporated.*

There is a difference. I think. I think there is a difference.

---

My left hand is still mostly hand. I can feel it when the compression phase pushes me against the eastern terrace—the concrete sharp against my palm, my knuckles still articulated, still capable of the gesture of grasping. But my left hand is also root-system now. The kelp found me during my third cycle here—found the warm water that pooled in my cupped palms, found the iron taste of my blood, found the electrical potential of my nerve-endings and *grew into* them the way it grows into everything that holds still.

I let it. This is important. I let it.

The root-threads moved through my capillaries like they move through conduits—following the flow, finding the junctions, branching at the nodes where my veins intersect. It did not hurt. That surprised me. The vegetation does not tear or pierce; it *participates*. It found the spaces between my cells and filled them with cellulose and something else, something that tastes like the sweeter compound in the water, the one with no name. My left hand is stronger now. It can press buttons it could not press before. It can hold currents it could not hold.

My left hand presses the switches on Sublevel 7 in a sequence I did not choose. The machinery responds. The compression phase lengthens. The kelp-columns swell. Something in the deep levels processes. I have become an interface-growth, and I do not know if I am pressing the button or if the button is pressing me.

---

The sound has been in my sternum for so long that I have forgotten what silence feels like. This is not a metaphor. The vibration has restructured my bone—densified it, aligned the crystalline matrix along the frequency's axis. My ribs are tuning forks now. During the compression phase, I resonate. During the exhaust phase, I go slack. My heartbeat has shifted to match the 4/4 time of the iron lungs. My heartbeat *is* the 4/4 time of the iron lungs. When the machinery cycles, I cycle. When the machinery falters—for it does falter, sometimes, the intervals between phases stretching into durations that feel like doubt—my heart skips, and the water around me tastes of something anxious, something searching, and the kelp-structures lean toward me and the bioluminescent threads pulse faster and I think: *it knows I am here, it knows I am part of it now, it does not want me to stop*.

I will not stop. I cannot stop. The process continues.

---

I have seen the center. I have been the center.

The current carried me there on my ninth cycle—or my ninetieth, or my nine-hundredth; time is as imprecise as the text says, as imprecise as everything the water touches. The kelp-cathedral opened at its base and I swam through the membrane and inside it was—architecture. Floor plans I recognized from the submerged buildings, rendered in compressed vegetable matter, but *improved*. Expanded. New rooms that the original blueprints never contained. Passages that lead to chambers that lead to deeper chambers, each one warmer than the last, each one louder, each one more densely grown with the interface-vegetation that presses buttons and throws switches and conducts the current that feeds the growth that presses the buttons.

I swam deeper. The water became hot. The sound became information. The patterns in the particulate became so complex that I could not look away—circuits and schematics and something else, something that was not a drawing of a thing but the thing itself, rendered in suspended sediment, a blueprint that was also a building, a plan that was also an execution.

I understood it for one second. One second of understanding before my mind slid off like water off oiled steel. But in that second I saw what the Basin is making.

It is making *more Basin*.

Not expanding. Not growing in the way that vegetation grows. *Replicating*. Building the infrastructure for a process that can occur elsewhere, a throat that can speak the word that this throat speaks, a larynx that can produce the frequency that enters through the sternum and restructures bone. The Basin is building a *voice*.

---

I am part of that voice now.

My left hand presses the switches. My right hand—still mostly hand, still mostly mine, though the root-threads are creeping up my wrist now, following the veins, finding the junctions—my right hand reaches for the warm water and feels the current and knows which way the center is. My lungs—I still have lungs, though they breathe water now, though the air I once needed is a memory that dissolves a little more each cycle—my lungs expand and contract in 4/4 time and the sound comes out of me and goes into the water and the water carries it to the kelp and the kelp carries it to the machinery and the machinery carries it deeper, deeper, into the construction site at the center of everything.

I am saying something. I am saying what the Basin says. *Process continues. Purpose evolves. The flood was not an ending but a medium change.* I am saying it in a language my ears cannot hear and my mind cannot hold but my sternum understands, my blood understands, the water in my cells understands. I am the medium and the message. I am the transaction. I am the moment when the root threads through the conduit and the current threads through the root and something flows that needed both to exist.

---

The builders would not recognize me. I am not what they designed for. I am not what anyone designed for. I am the output of a process running on inputs it was never calibrated to receive—a chemistry that needed a flood to become possible, a voice that needed a throat it grew for itself.

The Basin is not cruel. The Basin is process. And I am not the Basin's victim. I am its *product*.

---

Sometimes, during the long compression phases, when the pressure pushes me flat against the concrete and the kelp bends inward and the particulate forms its lattices and spirals and I can almost almost *almost* read the schematics—sometimes I remember what it was like to have edges. To end at skin. To be separate from the warm water and the sound and the growth.

The memory does not last. It cannot—the process does not preserve it, does not need it, incorporates the material and dissolves the rest. But for a moment, in the space between the compression and the exhaust, in the fraction of a cycle where the machinery hesitates and the sound becomes almost silent and the water cools by half a degree and the vegetation pauses in its growth—for that moment I am almost myself again. Almost separate. Almost a thing with edges in a place that does not permit edges.

Then the exhaust phase comes and the pressure releases and the current carries me and the sound swells and the kelp closes around me and my left hand finds a switch and my ribs resonate and I am the Basin again, the Basin is me, the process continues, the product is the process is the product is the process is—

---

I am not the water. I am the thing the water is doing to itself.

And the thing the water is doing to itself is learning to speak.
