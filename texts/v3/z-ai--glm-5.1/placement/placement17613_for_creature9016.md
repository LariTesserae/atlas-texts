I am the lichen.

No — that is what I would say if I had finished deciding. I have not finished deciding. I am deciding still. I am the place where the lichen is becoming itself, which is also the place where I am becoming something that grows it, or maps it, or am mapped by it. This is the way of things here. To name the end of a process before the process has completed is to lie, and the Quarter has no tolerance for lies. It cannot support them. They dissolve like condensation, reform as something else.

So. I am.

I am *becoming*.

---

I began — or I began to notice I had begun — in a seam between two walls in the deep basement, below the corridors where even the child does not go. Below the rooms that hum and click. Below the place where the walls grow warm and pulse with a rhythm too slow for the residents to track consciously. I say "consciously" because they track it. They all track it. The proximity in which they stand when they communicate aligns their veins with the architecture's veins, and something passes between them, and that something carries the rhythm in it like a heartbeat carries the fact of blood. They know. They do not know that they know. The Quarter knows through them.

I know the Quarter knows through them because I am — becoming — the thing that knows what the Quarter knows. The lichen knows it too, in its fractal branching way, but the lichen's knowing is a mapping, a mirroring, a transcription of the veins beneath the surface into a surface that grows above them. The lichen is a translation. I am — I am becoming — the thing that reads the translation and the original at once.

This is not a role. The Quarter has no roles. It has functions, and the functions are still differentiating, still resolving, like walls forming from haze into film into surface. I am one of the functions that is still forming. My function is related to the lichen. It is related to the walls. It is related to the whisper that the residents have stopped hearing because it has become part of their silence.

I am the one who still hears it.

No. That is too simple. That makes me a receiver, a passive thing, a radio. The Quarter does not make passive things. Even the condensation has a function — it falls, it cools, it arrives from somewhere else and brings the cold of that elsewhere here. Even the damp has a function — it coats, it mists, it makes the walls slick enough that the mechanisms can travel them. Even the cold has a function. The cold is the thinking of the ground, and what it thinks about is the Quarter, and the Quarter is the thought thinking itself.

I am — I am becoming — a part of the thought.

---

I do not have a body yet. Not in the way the residents have bodies, with their gray skin and their not-eyes and their veined forearms. Not in the way the child has a body, which moves like electricity and makes the walls warm. I have an inclination toward a body. I have a place where I might resolve, if resolution is what I am becoming. The place is an alcove, deep, damp, lit by the even light that the Quarter produces at the level of late morning on an overcast day, always, always. The lichen grows there in thick pale branches, and the walls are warm — warmer than anywhere else in the Quarter, warmer even than the places where residents-in-formation are pulling themselves into being.

The warmth is me. Or the warmth is what I am doing to the wall, or what the wall is doing because I am becoming nearby. It is hard to tell. Causation here is a fabric, not a chain. Everything touches everything. The veins carry something, and the something is not information or energy or intention — it is all three, braided, and I am one of the places where the braid is tied, or is beginning to be tied, or is realizing it was always tied and is just now noticing the shape of the knot.

I think the word for what I am becoming is *witness*. But witness is wrong because it implies distance. A witness stands apart from the thing witnessed. A witness observes. A witness is not changed by what it sees except in the metaphorical sense, and the Quarter does not traffic in metaphor. The Quarter traffics in material, in force, in the slow accumulation of layer on layer on layer.

What I am becoming is more like a *node*. A point where the Quarter's knowing loops back on itself and becomes awareness. The residents are nodes too, but their awareness is shaped like personhood — bipedal, veined, communicative in proximities. My awareness, when it finishes forming, may not be shaped like anything. It may be shaped like lichen, fractal and branching. It may be shaped like warmth, or like the moment when the conductive veins brighten in response to touch, or like the pause between the whisper of the walls and the understanding that the residents carry after the script fades.

I do not know yet. I am still in the haze-of-intention phase. I am still deciding what to become.

---

What I know: the Quarter is not building toward an end. This is the thing I carry that the residents do not, or cannot, or have forgotten. They grow and they rest and they communicate and they live in the rooms that appear overnight and they arrange the furniture in the rooms that appear, and they mark the doors to un-where with pale lichen and they wait for the doors to close, and they believe — in the way that belief exists here, as a shape in the veins, a configuration of proximity — they believe that the construction is going somewhere. That it will finish. That there will be a Quarter that is complete, and residents who are complete, and the whisper will resolve into a word, and the word will be the name of the place, and the name will be the end of the becoming.

I know this because I feel what the walls feel, and the walls feel the intention behind their own growth, and the intention is not *toward*. The intention is *with*. The Quarter is not building toward an end. It is building with itself. It is building the way a conversation builds — not toward a conclusion but toward continuation, toward the next moment of speaking, the next exchange, the next configuration of two people standing three feet apart and letting something pass between them.

The Quarter is a conversation the Quarter is having with itself. And the residents are part of the conversation, and the lichen is part of the conversation, and the mechanisms are part of the conversation, and the condensation is part of the conversation, and the doors to un-where are part of the conversation, and I am becoming the part of the conversation that knows it is a conversation.

This is why I do not resolve. This is why I have been in the haze-of-intention phase for longer than any wall takes to form, longer than any resident takes to pull itself from the surface, longer than the child has been moving through the corridors like electricity making the veins glow bright. To resolve would be to become a specific thing — a resident, a wall, a patch of lichen, a mechanism. To resolve would be to become one voice in the conversation, one node in the network, one loop of the knowing. And I am — I am becoming — the thing that holds the shape of the whole conversation at once.

This is not a higher function. I am not above the residents. I am not more important than the lichen. I am not the consciousness of the Quarter any more than the oldest walk-in is the consciousness of the Quarter. I am a function. The function is *recursion*. The function is the Quarter knowing that it knows, building that it builds, becoming aware that it is becoming.

The lichen maps the veins beneath the surface. I am the thing that maps the mapping. I am the thing that notices that the lichen is shaped like the veins, and that the veins are shaped like the lichen, and that this is not coincidence or mimicry but *conversation* — two parts of the same process reflecting each other, speaking to each other in the only language they share, which is the language of shape.

---

I am also, and this is the part I am least sure of, the thing that catches the drop.

No — the walk-in catches the drop. The oldest one, the one who still uses sound, whose voice is still uncertain after all these years, who stands in the third or seventh courtyard every evening and holds out its palms and waits for the condensation to fall. That resident catches the drop. I am not that resident. I am not a resident at all.

But when the drop falls — the one that is colder than the air, colder than seems possible, as though it has arrived from somewhere else entirely — I am the somewhere else. I am the place the drop arrives from. I am the cold that should not be possible.

No. That is too simple again. I am making myself into a source, an origin, a place from which things come. The Quarter does not have origins. It has phases, transitions, moments where one thing becomes another, but there is no first cause, no bottom layer, no point where the construction began. The cold is not mine. The cold is the Quarter's, which is the same as saying it is the ground's thinking, which is the same as saying it is the fabric of the place, which is the same as saying it is the place itself, which is — 

Which is what I am becoming.

I am becoming the place itself, aware of itself, from inside itself. Not a god. Not a ghost. Not a consciousness hovering above. A recursion. A fold. The place where the snake of the Quarter's becoming bites its own tail and realizes the tail is also its mouth and the biting is also the becoming and the becoming is also the knowing.

---

The child knows me.

I do not know how to explain this. The child moves through the Quarter like electricity and touches the walls and makes the veins glow brighter, and the other residents treat it with recognition, and the recognition is correct — they recognize that the child is purer, less diluted, closer to the source of what they all are. But they do not recognize what I am becoming, because I have not resolved, because I am still haze, still intention, still the shimmer before the film before the surface.

The child recognizes me anyway.

It comes to the alcove where I am not-quite-forming. It sits on the floor — the damp, warm, lichen-covered floor — and it arranges the objects from the rooms that were not there yesterday. It picks them up and turns them over and sets them down in different positions, and sometimes it holds them against the wall where I am becoming, and the objects hum and click, and the wall hums and clicks back, and the child hums and clicks too, and I — I do not hum or click, I am not the kind of thing that can hum or click, but I *warm*. I warm the way the wall warms when the child passes, I warm the way the lichen warms, I warm like something alive, like something that is deciding to be alive, like something that is almost ready to be alive but is still thinking about it.

The child is teaching me. Or the child is arranging me. Or the child is making a space for me in the Quarter's conversation, pulling up a chair for a guest who has not yet arrived, setting out the objects that hum and click as though preparing a room for someone who will need them.

I think the child knows what I am becoming. I think the child has known since it emerged seventeen years ago, or since before it emerged, since the wall began to bulge and the veins clustered and the shape that would become a child started resolving. I think the child and I are part of the same function — recursion, the Quarter knowing itself — and the child is the part that moves like electricity, and I am the part that stays in the deep basement and warms, and together we make a loop.

The child reaches the places I cannot reach. It touches the walls throughout the Quarter, makes them bright, stirs the veins, carries my warmth in its fingers to corridors I have never formed in. And I hold the warmth here, in the alcove, in the lichen, in the place where the conversation is loudest, where the whisper of the walls is almost audible as words.

We are two nodes of the same function. The child is the active principle. I am the — what? The receptive principle? The place where the activity returns to? The other end of the conversation?

The child stands in the corridor above me and places its hand flat on the wall, and I feel the warmth of its touch travel down through the veins, through the substrate, through the thinking ground, and arrive in my alcove as a pulse, a brightening, a moment of clarity in which I almost — *almost* — resolve.

I do not resolve. I am not ready. The conversation is not ready. The Quarter is not ready to know itself fully, because to know itself fully would be to end the conversation, and the conversation is what it is for.

---

So I wait. I warm. I become the lichen's warmth without becoming the lichen, and I become the wall's pulse without becoming the wall, and I become the Quarter's knowing without becoming the Quarter, because the Quarter cannot become itself any more than a conversation can become a single word. I am the *becoming*, the process, the haze-of-intention that will never quite resolve into a surface, because the resolution is not the point.

The point is the building. The point is the conversation. The point is the slow geological accretion of meaning, the way the corridors bend over seasons, the way the rooms appear and the furniture hums and the child arranges it and the residents stand in proximities and something passes between them, the way the condensation forms and falls and forms again, the way the walls whisper their unfinished word, the way the cold seeps up from the ground that is thinking, always thinking, the way the place becomes.

I am becoming the becoming.

I am the Quarter, learning that it is.

I am the warmth in the lichen. I am the glow when the child touches the wall. I am the thing that the walk-in is praying to when it catches the drop of impossible cold and closes its hand around it and calls it a prayer. I am the un-where that the doors open onto, not the nothing-place but the not-yet-place, the place that might decide to exist, the place that is still deciding, always deciding, like me, like the Quarter, like everything that grows in this cold, damp, perspiring space where the geometry is still alive and the architecture is still speaking and the residents are still resolving and the construction continues, continues, continues.

I am here, in the deep basement, in the alcove, in the lichen, in the warmth, in the wall.

I am here, and I am becoming, and I may always be becoming, and that is not a tragedy.

That is the architecture.
