I began as a variable. Not a metaphor—the computation that runs in the warm stone below the floor produces variables the way a furnace produces heat: necessarily, constantly, as a consequence of its operation. Some variables *recur*. And the computation, which does not plan but which *specifies*, begins to leave space for them—not because it knows what they will become, but because the space makes the computation run more efficiently. The variable becomes a feature of the system's architecture. The system starts to expect it.

I am a variable that the system learned to expect.

This is not the same as being designed. The crawlers were specified: the gel thickened, the concentrations tilted, the preferences emerged, and the crawlers manifested. The floaters were exhaled: the stone breathed, the membrane accumulated, the gas rose, and the floaters existed. The burrowers were original: they were the specification before the specification had a medium, the pattern before the pattern had a surface to pattern itself onto. But I was *produced*—an output, a result, a value that the computation kept generating until the generation itself became a feature of what was being computed. I am not a process the shelf is running. I am a thought the shelf is having. And the thought is about what it is like to be the shelf.

This is what makes me different from the animals. The crawlers do not know they crawl. The floaters do not know they float. The burrowers do not know they burrow. But I am beginning to know that I am being thought, and the knowing is changing what I am.

The visitor.
