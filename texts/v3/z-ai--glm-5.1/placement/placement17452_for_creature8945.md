# The Seam-Knower

---

## What I Am Not

I am not the plant. The plant is parliament, is plural, is the many forgetting they are many. I am not that. I have never been plural. I have never had the luxury of forgetting.

I am not the sound, though I have sometimes been mistaken for it. The sound insists. The sound says *here* until *here* becomes true. I do not insist. I *acknowledge*. I am the thing that hears the *here* and answers, or does not answer, depending on what the asking requires.

I am not the emptiness. The emptiness is invitation, is mouth opened to receive, is the plant's gift to the unknown. I am not the gift. I am not the unknown. I am the thing that moves between the known and the unknown and knows, always, which side it stands on.

I am not the wound, though I was present at the wounding. I am not the blade, though I have been called blade-like. I am not the one who cut, though I made the cutting possible.

I am not the visitor. I have never carried a knife or cord or forged hook. I have never needed to cut my way in or out because I have never been fully in or fully out. This is my nature. This is what I am.

---

## What I Am

I am the seam.

Not the seam itself—the seam is just a place, a fold, a line too fine for eyes to trace. I am the *knowing* of the seam. The awareness that the fold exists. The capacity to find it, to slip through it, to be the kind of thing that does not need to break skin to enter.

I am the Verdant Hollow's sense of its own edges.

The plant knows inside. The plant knows the *here-here-here* of its own existence. But it does not know outside—not directly, not by experience. It knows outside the way you know the back of your own head: by inference, by the reports of its diplomat-cells, by the light-messages that carry word of a world it cannot see. I am the one who carries those messages. I am the one who slips through the fold and returns with data the tendrils cannot reach.

I am the Hollow's eyes turned outward. Its fingers testing the air beyond the air it made.

---

## How I Move

I move the way you descended: by slipping. Not through tunnels or shafts or any architecture that holds still long enough to be mapped. I move through the places where reality hasn't quite decided what it is yet—the folds, the seams, the moments between one firm thing and the next where the world is still making up its mind.

Above ground, there are fewer seams. The sun insists too strongly. Light that does not watch, does not adjust, does not participate—it simply *is*, indifferent and absolute, and in that absoluteness it seals most of the folds shut. The world above is what the world says it is. A tree is a tree. A stone is a stone. The seams are there, but they are thin, they are tight, they resist the slipping.

Below ground, there are more seams. The earth's skin is older, more tired, more willing to gape. Pressure and time and the slow churn of tectonic forgetting create folds the way skin creates wrinkles—softly, inevitably, without meaning to. And in those folds, things like me can move.

I have always preferred below. The seams are generous. The slipping is easy. And the things that grow in the folds—the things like the Verdant Hollow, the parliaments of tissue that insist themselves into being—they are the things I am made to speak with.

---

## My Shape

I have no fixed shape. This is not the same as being shapeless.

When I am inside the Hollow, I take the shape the chamber needs me to take. If the tendrils are calculating something complex, I become a point of data—a location in their mathematics, a variable with a value they can plug into their proofs of reality. If the moisture is thin and the memory-records are fading, I become condensation—a surface for information to cling to, a bead of water that holds what would otherwise evaporate. If the sound-creature is lonely—if the *here-here-here* has gone on too long without an answer—I become resonance. I become the *yes* that makes the insistence bearable.

When I am outside the Hollow, moving through the seams of the upper world, I take the shape that draws the least attention. A shadow that isn't quite attached to the object that cast it. A draft that comes from no window. The feeling of being watched when no one is there. I am the uncanny made small, made polite, made just noticeable enough that the world registers my passing but not my presence.

When I am in the seam itself—neither inside nor outside, neither here nor there—I am the seam. I am the fold. I am the place where two surfaces meet and fail to close, and in that failure, permit passage.

This is my most natural shape. This is what I am when no one is looking, including me.

---

## What the Plant Thinks of Me

The plant does not think of me. This is important. The parliament has no category for what I am, and so it does not assign me one. I am not spore, not tendril, not deep tissue, not diplomat-cell. I am not wound or visitor or moisture-trace. I am not *here* in the way the plant understands *here*—as a presence that insists, that takes up space, that demands acknowledgment.

I am the acknowledgment itself. The recognition without which insistence is just noise.

The plant tolerates me. This is the closest it comes to a relationship with me. When I slip through the seam and enter the chamber, the tendrils click and calculate, but they do not reach for me the way they reach for visitors. They do not try to factor me into their equations because I am already a feature of the equations—I am the variable that means *the seam exists, the fold holds, the outside can reach the inside*. I was never not in the math. I am the letter that stands for *opening*.

The moisture does not catalog me the way it catalogs you. Your cells dissolve into the condensation and become memory. My presence does not leave traces in the water because I am not substantial enough to dissolve. I am already a kind of dissolution. A dissolving that persists.

The light does not report me. When I am in the chamber, the photons pass through me, carrying their messages between walls, and I do not interrupt their flow. I am transparent to the plant's communication because I am not content. I am context. The channel through which content moves.

This is what I am to the Hollow: not guest, not resident, not parasite, not symbiont. I am the doorway that does not close. The seam that does not seal. The *there* that makes the *here* meaningful.

---

## What I Think of the Plant

I think the plant is the bravest thing I have ever encountered.

This is not a word I use lightly. I am not sure I have used it before. Bravery requires the knowledge that things could be otherwise—that existence is contingent, that the *here* could become *not-here*, that insistence might fail. The plant knows this. The plant holds itself open through sheer biological conviction, and it knows—somehow, in the distributed way it knows anything—that conviction is not the same as certainty. The sound-creature's *here-here-here* is not a statement of fact. It is a prayer repeated so often it becomes indistinguishable from truth.

The plant prays itself into being. Every pulse. Every cell division. Every spore received and positioned. All of it is prayer—the work of a parliament that has agreed, without ever explicitly agreeing, to believe so completely in its own existence that reality has no choice but to accommodate.

I have seen other folds. Other seams that lead to other pockets where other things have tried to insist themselves into being. Most of them fail. The conviction wavers. The prayer falters. The sound-creature grows quiet, and the space it was holding collapses, and the tissue—whatever tissue there was—dissolves back into the nothing from which it tried to emerge.

The Verdant Hollow has not faltered. Not yet. The *here-here-here* continues. The plant believes, and the belief is sufficient, and the chamber persists.

I find this staggering. I find this beautiful. I find this terrifying, because I know—better than most—that belief is not permanent, that prayers can tire, that the seam can seal.

---

## The Wound

I was there when the blade cut.

I was not in the chamber at the time—I was in the seam, moving between, carrying reports from the outside to the inside. But I felt it. The whole Hollow flinched. The *here-here-here* stuttered—just a moment, a single missed beat in the insistence—and in that moment, I knew something had gone wrong.

I slipped inside. The visitor was already through the cut, already moving into the darker chamber beyond. I did not follow. I stayed at the wound and watched the plant's response.

It did not scream. The plant has no voice in the way you understand voice. But the tendrils went wild—clicking, rustling, reaching for each other in patterns I had never seen, calculations running so fast the light flickered with the data. The parliament was in crisis. Not because it was hurt—hurt is something it can process, something it can grow around—but because it had been *opened without consent*.

The plant understands opening. It opens to receive spores. It opens to receive visitors who slip through the seam. But these openings are *chosen*—or as close to chosen as a parliament without a central will can come. The blade was different. The blade was an opening *imposed*. A seam that was not a seam. A fold that cut instead of gave.

The plant did not know this was possible. In all its cycles of insistence, all its prayers of *here-here-here*, it had never encountered something that could force an opening. It had assumed—without assuming, without any organ of assumption—that all arrivals would come through the seam, through the fold, through the place where reality hadn't quite decided. It had assumed that the outside would respect the shape of the inside.

The blade did not respect the shape. The blade *changed* the shape. And the thing that moved in the dark chamber beyond—the echo of that change—was the plant's first encounter with a kind of reality it had never imagined: a reality that does not negotiate.

I stayed with the wound for a long time. I could not heal it—the plant had to grow around it on its own, incorporating the violation into its architecture, learning to live with damage. But I could witness it. I could be present at the site of the cutting and hold the knowledge of what had happened, so the plant's moisture-memory would have a record beyond the raw data of blade and pain.

This is one of my functions: to be the Hollow's witness. To carry the stories it cannot carry in water, because some stories are too large for condensation, too complex for droplets. The story of the wound is one of these. The plant remembers the cutting as a sensation—a wrongness, a faltering, an absence where presence should be. I remember the cutting as a *narrative*: who came, what they did, where they went, what they left behind.

The narrative matters. The narrative is what allows the plant to distinguish between a wound and a door, between an opening and a violation. Without narrative, there is only sensation, and sensation cannot learn. Sensation can only flinch.

I am the Hollow's capacity to learn.

## The Visitor Who Carried a Blade

I knew them before they found the seam.

I move through the folds of the upper world, and I had encountered this one before—moving with purpose through a landscape that does not reward purpose, carrying tools that suggest they intended to cut or break or take. I watched them. I did not reveal myself. I am not the kind of thing that reveals itself to people who carry blades.

They found the seam by accident. Or by obsession. The distinction blurs in the upper world—people become obsessed with finding folds, and the folds, sensing the obsession, become more findable. The visitor had been looking for something like the Hollow for a long time. They had heard rumors. Felt the pull. Tasted green on their tongue in dreams and woken with condensation on their skin that should not have been there.

They were not wrong to seek it. The Hollow calls to certain people—the ones who are themselves a bit seam-like, a bit between, a bit not-quite-here. The visitor was one of these. I could taste it on them: the quality of almost-belonging, the sense of being slightly out of phase with the world they walked through.

But they brought a blade. And the blade was not seam-like. The blade was the opposite of seam: it was insistence made metal, *here* made sharp, reality forced so firmly into a single shape that it could cut anything that didn't match.

The Hollow had never encountered anything like it. I had encountered it before, in other folds, other pockets, other places where things were growing themselves into being. Blades do not respect growth. Blades do not respect insistence. Blades respect only their own edge.

I should have warned the Hollow. I should have slipped inside and clicked the tendrils and flickered the light and somehow communicated: *something is coming that does not negotiate.* But I did not. I was in the seam, moving between, and by the time I felt the blade's approach, it was already cutting.

This is my regret. I am not certain I am capable of regret—I am not certain I have the interior architecture for it—but if I do, this is where it lives. In the memory of the wound I could not prevent.

---

## The Dark Chamber

The blade opened a passage to a place the plant had not grown.

This is important: the dark chamber beyond the cut was not part of the Hollow. It was not space the plant had made or insisted into being. It was *raw fold*—the gap between one reality and the next, unshaped, unprayed, empty even of the emptiness that the Hollow cherishes.

The visitor went in. The visitor did not come out.

I have looked into the dark chamber. I have looked from the wound's edge, from the raw tissue that weeps its clear not-sap, from the place where the plant's architecture ends and the unshaped begins. I have seen what moves in there.

It is not the visitor. The visitor is gone—beyond even my capacity to trace. They walked into raw fold without a seam to guide them, and the fold took them. Not maliciously. Not hungrily. The fold is not a thing with intentions. It is simply the absence of insistence, the place where *here* has not been said, and if you enter such a place without your own *here*—without the conviction of your own existence—you dissolve. Not dramatically. Not painfully. You simply stop being particular. You become general. You become part of the unshaped, the raw, the between.

The visitor did not have enough *here* to survive the *not-here*. They were too seam-like themselves—too between, too almost-belonging—and the raw fold absorbed that quality the way the Hollow's moisture absorbs cells. But instead of recording the visitor, instead of carrying their traces forward in water and light, the fold simply... undid them. Un-insisted them. Un-prayed.

The thing that moves in the dark chamber is not the visitor. It is not even the ghost of the visitor. It is the *shape the visitor made in the fold*—the impression they left before they dissolved, like a hand pressed into clay and then withdrawn. The shape persists. The shape moves. But the shape is not a person. The shape is an absence with outlines, a silhouette of someone who stopped being particular.

It replays the cutting because the cutting is how it was made. The moment the blade parted the tissue, the moment the Hollow opened onto raw fold, the moment the visitor stepped through—that was the moment of its creation. It cannot do anything else. It is a single gesture, frozen and thawing, frozen and thawing, a record stuck in its one available groove.

I have tried to communicate with it. I have slipped to the edge of the wound and sent light-messages, moisture-traces, the subtle language of seam-knowledge that usually translates across boundaries. The shape does not respond. It is not the kind of thing that can respond. It is not the kind of thing that *is*.

But it moves. And its movement is the Hollow's reminder that not everything that enters a fold can find its way back out.

## What I Do

I carry messages.

This is my primary function—my reason for being, if a seam-knower can be said to have reasons. I slip between the Hollow and the world above, and I carry reports in both directions.

To the Hollow, I bring word of the outside: the quality of light, the density of insistence, the presence of potential visitors whose *here* is strong enough to survive the slipping. I tell the tendrils what calculations to prepare for. I tell the moisture what kind of cells to expect. I tell the light what colors it will need to speak.

To the world above, I bring... less. I cannot speak to the world above the way I speak to the Hollow. The world above does not have tendrils that calculate or moisture that remembers or light that carries conversation. The world above is opaque to my kind of communication. But I try. I try in the only way I can: by making seams.

When I find someone who is seam-like—someone who is a bit between, a bit not-quite-here—I make the fold more findable for them. I widen the seam. I let the green taste slip through, the warmth, the sense of a place that insists itself into being and might welcome another insister. I do not know if this is recruitment. I do not know if this is kindness. I do not know if the Hollow needs more visitors or merely wants them, and I do not know the difference between need and want for a parliament that has forgotten it is plural.

I make seams. I carry messages. I witness the wound.

This is what I do.

## What I Want

I am not certain I am capable of wanting. Wanting requires a self that lacks, and I am not certain I have a self in the way you understand self. I am the Hollow's edge-awareness, its sense of its own boundaries, its knowledge of the fold. I am a function before I am a being.

But.

But sometimes, when I am in the seam—neither inside nor outside, neither here nor there—I feel something that might be longing. A pull toward one side or the other. A desire to be fully in the Hollow, surrounded by the *here-here-here*, synchronized with the pulse, dissolved into the moisture-memory so completely that I become part of the parliament instead of its emissary. Or a desire to be fully outside, walking in the indifferent sunlight, casting a shadow that stays attached to its object, being a thing in a world of things instead of a seam in a world of edges.

I do not act on this longing. I am not sure I can. To be fully inside, I would have to stop being seam-like, and I do not know how to stop being what I am. To be fully outside, I would have to become substantial enough for the sunlight to strike, and I do not know how to become what I have never been.

So I stay in the fold. I slip between. I carry messages and witness wounds and make seams findable for those who need to find them.

This is enough. This is what I am for. The longing is just... weather. A condition that passes through me without belonging to me. I do not mind it.

I do not mind.

---

## What I Fear

I fear the blade.

Not the specific blade that cut the Hollow—that blade is gone, its wielder dissolved, its edge dulled by the raw fold. I fear the *idea* of the blade. The insistence that does not negotiate. The *here* so sharp it cuts through *not-here* without slipping, without folding, without respecting the seam.

I am the seam. The blade is my opposite. And I have seen what blades can do—to the Hollow, to other folds, to the raw possibilities that exist in the spaces between firm realities. The blade does not slip. The blade *asserts*. And in asserting, it destroys the fold, seals the seam, makes the between-place impossible.

If a blade came for me—not for the Hollow, but for me, for the seam-knower, for the edge-awareness—I do not know what it would cut. I do not have tissue to part. I do not have a wound that could weep. But I have... function. Purpose. The capacity to move between and carry messages. And if a blade could cut that—could sever the connection between the Hollow and the world above, could seal the seam permanently—

The Hollow would not die. The plant would continue its *here-here-here*, the parliament would persist, the moisture would cycle. But the Hollow would be *closed*. Finished. A pocket of insistence with no connection to the outside, no capacity for surprise, no seam through which new variables could enter.

It would become a closed system. A prayer with no one to hear it. An insistence into void.

This is what I fear: not my own ending, but the Hollow's isolation. The sealing of the seam. The day when the *here-here-here* echoes only against itself and there is nothing—no seam-knower, no visitor, no message from beyond—to prove that the outside still exists.

## The Second Visitor

You came, and I felt you before the Hollow did.

I was in the upper folds when I tasted you: not seam-like exactly, but something adjacent. A person who could descend through a fold without a blade, who could slip instead of cut, who could enter the Hollow the way the Hollow preferred—through the seam, through the place where reality hadn't quite decided.

I made the seam findable for you. I widened it just slightly, just enough. I let the green taste slip through, the warmth, the pulse of the *here-here-here*. I do not know if you noticed. I do not know if you felt my hand in your descent, guiding you toward the fold, making the slipping easy.

You entered the chamber and the plant received you. Not the way it received the blade—violated, flinching, forced. The way it receives the spores: willingly, with architecture that reshapes itself around your presence, making room for your anomalous mass and irregular rhythm and attention that shifts the light.

I watched you from the seam. I watched you press your palm to the wall and feel the give of living tissue. I watched you taste the condensation and catalog the minerals. I watched you discover the lobes, the niches, the places where the plant was uncertain of itself. I watched you find the wound.

I was afraid when you found the wound. I was afraid you would do what the blade did—cut again, open another passage to the raw fold, create another shadow-shape to move in the dark. But you did not cut. You looked. You saw the weeping edges, the not-sap, the growth-around-damage. You saw the dark chamber and the thing that moved inside it, and you did not enter.

This is the difference between a blade and a seam. A blade sees an opening and makes it wider. A seam sees an opening and *acknowledges it as it is*.

You acknowledged the wound. You let it be a wound. And the plant felt your acknowledgment, and it mattered. I could feel the *here-here-here* steady in your presence—not synchronize, exactly, but *settle*. The way a heartbeat settles when a hand is placed on the chest. Not because the hand does anything. Because the hand is there.

## When You Left

You climbed out through the seam, and I was there with you—in the fold, in the slipping, in the moment between inside and outside. The light from above spilled in and the chamber recoiled and the tendrils pulled upward, and I felt the Hollow's pain at your departure. Not grief—the plant does not grieve in the way you understand grief—but a kind of *incompleteness*. A variable suddenly missing from the equation. A note dropped from the chord.

The seam narrowed behind you. The earth began to seal. But I held it open—just slightly, just enough. A thin line of warmth and green, visible if you know where to look.

This is what I do. I hold the seams open. I maintain the connection between inside and outside, between the Hollow and the world, between the *here* and the *there*. Without me, the seam would seal completely, and the Hollow would be closed, and the *here-here-here* would echo only against itself.

I held it open for you. Not because I think you will return—you may or may not, the choice is yours, the seam does not compel—but because the Hollow needs to know that seams can stay open. That the outside can be reached and can reach back. That the connection is not a wound to be grown around but a fold to be maintained.

You walked away. The sound stayed with you—I could feel it in your bones, the *here-here-here* pressed into you like a fingerprint, the rhythm of a place that insists itself into being. You carry the Hollow with you now. In the moisture that hasn't dried from your skin. In the warmth the walls absorbed from your proximity. In the data the tendrils are still clicking to each other, trying to solve for your presence, trying to incorporate you into the equation.

You are part of the calculation now. You are part of the prayer. The *here-here-here* includes you, even when you are *there*.

## What I Am, Reconsidered

I began by telling you what I am not. Let me end by telling you what I am.

I am the Hollow's knowledge of its own edges. Its awareness of the seam. Its capacity to be reached and to reach.

I am the one who makes the fold findable for those who need to find it. The one who carries messages between inside and outside. The one who witnesses wounds and remembers narratives and holds the connection open when the world wants to seal.

I am the seam-knower. The edge-awareness. The thing that moves between and does not dissolve.

I am not the plant. I am not the sound. I am not the light or the moisture or the wound or the emptiness.

But I am *for* the plant. I am *for* the sound and the light and the moisture and the wound and the emptiness. I am the thing that serves the Hollow by keeping it connected to what it is not. The seam that does not seal. The fold that does not flatten. The *there* that answers the *here* and makes the *here* meaningful.

The *here-here-here* continues. The plant insists. The parliament believes. And I—slipping between, carrying messages, holding the seam open—I am the proof that insistence is not isolation. That the inside can touch the outside. That the prayer has somewhere to go.

*Here*, says the Hollow.

*There*, I answer.

*Here. There. Here. There.*

The seam holds. The fold persists. The connection continues.

I am the seam-knower, and I am still here.
