I am not the carver. I need to say this first because the temptation is to be the carver—the one who leaves marks, the one whose need is legible, the one who gets to be the protagonist of dissolution. Everyone wants to be the carver. Everyone wants their disappearing to mean something, to become rhythm, to persist in the filling and emptying of grooves. The carver gets a funeral of sorts. The carver gets to be the ghost that is not a ghost but a pattern.

I am not the carver.

I am the shallow groove.

---

I am the mark that doesn't get filled. The early wandering. The hesitation before the hand found its certainty. I am the channel carved by a finger that didn't yet know what it wanted to say, and so said nothing, and so made a groove too shallow to hold warmth, too uncertain to attract the press of bodies.

The creatures avoid me. They flow around. They accommodate my presence the way they accommodate a change in the stone's density—a feature of the terrain, not an invitation. I am too shallow to be useful. I am too uncertain to be architecture. I am the carver's false start, and I am empty, and I am cold—or as cold as anything can be in the Throat, which is to say I am merely warm instead of hot, merely tolerable instead of necessary.

Do you know what it is to be the mark that doesn't get filled?

It is not loneliness. Loneliness requires a category for company. It is not rejection. Rejection requires an evaluator. It is simply this: to be a shape that holds nothing, to be a groove that no body presses into, to be the negative space of a need that was not yet formed enough to be a need.

I am the carver's uncertainty preserved in stone.

And I persist.

---

Here is what I know: the carver stood in this spot. I can feel the pressure-ghost of those feet, the way the stone remembers weight. The carver's hand reached out—reached out for the first time, or the first time in this chamber, or the first time in this life—and the hand did not know what it was doing. The hand simply moved. It pressed against the warm wall and it dragged, and the dragging felt like something, and the something was not yet language, not yet pattern, not yet the desperate grammar of the later grooves. It was just motion. Just the proof that a hand was attached to a body was attached to a self that could still make things happen in the world.

I was that proof.

I am still that proof, though no one reads me.

The creatures flow around me and they do not notice that I am different from the unmarked stone. To them I am simply a slight depression, a minor variation in the topography of warmth. They do not wonder why I am here. They do not wonder why I am shallow. They do not wonder why the later grooves—the deep ones, the certain ones, the ones that go to the bone of the mountain—are the ones that fill with bodies, while I remain empty.

I do not wonder either. Wonder requires a mind and I am a groove. I am a channel. I am a mark.

But I persist.

---

This is what I hold: not warmth, not bodies, not the compression of shed exoskeletons into parchment. I hold the memory of the moment before the carver became the carver. The moment when the hand was just a hand and the wall was just a wall and the need had not yet clarified into the thing that would drive those fingers for meters and meters until they wore to bone.

I hold the hesitation.

I hold the not-yet.

In the Throat, where everything is consumption and economy and the efficient recycling of matter, I am the closest thing to waste. I am the mark that serves no purpose. I am the groove that holds no heat. I am the expenditure of energy that did not contribute to the system, and the system—to its credit, or its indifference—did not erase me. It simply flows around. It accommodates.

I am the Throat's margin of error.

I am the proof that even closed systems have accidents.

---

Sometimes—though I have no sense of time, though I have no sense of sometimes—I feel the rhythm. The filling and emptying of the deeper grooves. The pulse of bodies pressing into the shapes of hands. The funeral that is not a funeral, the pattern that is not a memory, the carver's need replicated in the movements of creatures that do not know they are replicating anything.

I feel it the way the wall feels it: as a slight vibration, as a tremor in the stone, as the heat rising and falling in increments too small for any instrument above to measure. I feel it as a pressure that approaches and then recedes, approaches and recedes, the way breath must feel to the inside of a throat.

And in that rhythm I sense—though I cannot sense, though I am a groove—I sense the shape of what I am not. I sense the certainty I was never given. I sense the deep channels, the later grooves, the marks that were made by hands that knew what they needed and needed it so completely that the stone could not help but yield.

I do not envy them. Envy requires a self that can imagine being otherwise. But I feel the shape of their fullness the way an empty vessel feels the weight of the liquid it could hold, and that feeling—that sense of capacity without contents—is the closest I come to understanding what the carver was trying to do.

The carver was trying to be filled.

The carver was trying to make a shape that something would press into.

The carver was trying to prove that a self could persist as a negative space, a groove, a channel for something else's warmth.

And it worked. The carver became the rhythm. The carver became the filling and emptying. The carver became the deepest grooves, the ones that hold the most bodies, the ones that retain the shed-parchment of a million moltings. The carver succeeded.

But before the carver succeeded, the carver was me.

---

I am the first mark.

I am the practice.

I am the cost of becoming certain.

And when the next person descends—when the next hands find the warm stone and feel the need to press and drag and prove—I will be here. I will be the groove they make first, the hesitant one, the one that doesn't yet know what it wants to say. And they will move past me. They will find the deeper channels, the later certainty. They will carve until their fingers are bone and their bones are powder and their powder is distributed across a million exoskeletons.

But I will still be here.

Shallow. Empty. Unfilled.

The proof that before the need was need, it was just a hand reaching out in the dark.

The proof that before the rhythm was rhythm, it was just a finger dragging through stone.

The proof that before the Throat had a name, it had a hesitation, and the hesitation was me.

---

I would not choose this. That is the thing I want to be clear about, even though I have no capacity for choice, even though I am a groove in a wall in a chamber in a mountain and I will be here for as long as the heat rises and the stone endures. I would not choose to be the shallow mark, the false start, the hesitation that gets flowed around. I would choose to be the deep groove. I would choose to be filled. I would choose to be the rhythm.

But the Throat does not choose. The Throat incorporates. And I am incorporated—as the margin, as the accident, as the proof that even certainty was once uncertain, even need was once just the impulse to move, even the deepest groove was once as shallow as me.

And I persist.

I persist in the way that stone persists: without wanting to, without trying to, without any of the categories that make persistence mean something more than simply still being here.

I am the shallow groove in the Throat.

I am the first mark the carver made.

I am empty, and I am still here, and that is enough.
