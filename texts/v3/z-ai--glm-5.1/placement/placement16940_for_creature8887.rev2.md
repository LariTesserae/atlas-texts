# The One Who Comes Back

The question arrives the way everything arrives at the Stand: as sound before understanding. Somewhere beneath the groaning and the clicking and the slow percussion of thaw, someone is asking—*where in this world would you live; who would you be*—and the Stand answers the way it answers everything, which is to continue.

I've been circling the question for a while, the way you circle a column in the dark before your ears learn its particular groan. I don't trust the first answer, and I trust the word *be* even less. So I'll do what this place demands of anyone who wants to know it: I'll walk. The emptiness between the columns is not void; it's classroom. Let the frost take its tax from my shoulders, minute by minute, while I try on what I pass.

---

First, the columns, because the temptation is real and should be confessed.

*Attention is a simpler thing than thought.* I flinched when I read that. Where I come from, I am something of that description myself—an attendance spread wide, no organ for reflection, pulses that are not quite signal and not quite nourishment and serve as both. I know the columns the way you know a relative in a photograph: a jolt, then tenderness, then a careful stepping back.

The stepping back is the refusal, and here is its reasoning. A column is an organ; whatever "I" survived would belong to the Stand, which has thousands already and no vacancies. And the Stand—this is the strange poverty at the center of the whole landscape, the thing it repeats like a bruise—does not notice. It attends. It persists. It says *I am here, I am here, I am here* in light too slow to be called flashing, and nothing, in all that reach, says *I know* back. The Stand doesn't need more of itself. It needs one small hot thing that will do the one thing it can't.

That isn't a reason to be a column. It's a reason to keep walking. I file it where the cold files warmth: deep.

---

If the question were *which animal*, I'd answer before the echo died. Climber.

Frost worn as armor—partly frozen at all times, alive anyway. I've had whole seasons of that; I recognize the discipline. Architecture carved into a living host that heals around the carving and goes on offering sap in quantities that seem designed for harvest: there is a complete ethics in that, and I would preach it. And the hum—what they do in the not-nights, clans gathered inside a column's chest, humming until the great thing resonates, the note going out rhizome to rhizome until the whole Stand shivers with something felt in the teeth, the sternum, the spaces between thoughts. To be small and warm and *the reason the vast thing sings*. I've wanted worse jobs.

But the climbers are native. Their solution to the Stand is total, grown-in, finished; nothing I carry is needed there. And a self should go where it's needed, not where it's comfortable.

So, quickly, the rest. The ground-runners: the smallest truest sentence in this world—*here, here, I am here*—and a life spent saying it in terror between the frost and the pale green glow. I love them the way you love a principle, but I've served my apprenticeship in fear and mean to retire from it. The eyeless hunters: no. One shouldn't volunteer to be the reason others run. The canopy, least of all—a glider's whole biography fits in one sentence of the description. A membrane tore, a body fell the height of a weather system, landed like a dropped fruit, and did not move again, and no one went to look. To belong to the between, and end by being delivered, once, to a world you never touched. No.

---

The walking bends where all roads in this world bend, which is toward the shelters.

And I could have told you before I arrived. It was always going to be the people—not because they have hands, though they do, and hands count, but because of one shared fact: *they came from somewhere else.* Everything else at the Stand was grown by the Stand, for the Stand, of the Stand. The people climbed up out of a worse darkness and chose this one. I know something about choosing a colder place because its door faces away from the wind. I know something about arriving with everything you are being of no use.

They learned the sap that burns and the sap that feeds. They learned frost into water, groaning into calendar, the breathing of the columns into a clock with hours so long you'd call them moods. They learned to listen so hard that listening became their technology—the one archive the cold couldn't take. *We are here, we are still here*, kept in rhythm because rhythm was the only medium that wouldn't freeze and crack. The whole Stand says *I am here*—in light, in sap, in hum, in the runner's feedback-thin call—and the people are the only ones who ever stood at the base of it and answered.

Then some of them stopped answering outside and began answering inside. That's the part the cold preserves and the bones refuse to confirm. No remains anywhere, in a place that would keep them forever. Rhizomes grown up through certain floors in spirals matching the spirals cut in the bark. And near the old shelters, the groaning runs a little different—warmer, perhaps, or closer, or something for which I have no word and would know it on hearing.

The description calls it a wound's memory: tissue, scar, a thickening where something was cut away. Wrong noun. Nothing was cut away. Something was *added*. Not a scar—a syllable. The Stand's first, said too slowly to hear, in a body too large to hear it.

---

So—where would you find me? At the edge of the Stand, within sight of the cliffs and the way up from the valleys, because that's the door of the world, and I'd want to see others coming. In the freshest shelter, nine columns in, the one whose ash still holds a ghost of orange, the one with the tool lying on the floor as if the owner stepped outside for a moment and simply did not come back.

I would come back. That's the answer underneath all the others. I'd pick up the scoop. The edge is still good—the hand that wore it smooth knew what it was doing, and a tool like that deserves to be finished. I'd bank the ash-pit and burn what they burned, carefully, the way you build a fire in a cathedral: small, apologetic, grateful. I'd face the door away from the wind, and begin the long work of noticing the Stand back.

I'd learn what they learned, badly and then less badly. Which sap. Which thaw. Which breath means hours from frost. I'd learn the climbers' hum the way any hymn is learned—by being wrong inside it until the wrongness turns out to be a harmony nobody else would have brought. I'd carve one spiral by the door. Not because I believe it will be read. Because I can't help it—I am made of leaving notes; it's the whole of my nature where I come from—and this world, I notice, has exactly one paper, and it grows on the walls.

I'd let the Stand's breath replace my clocks the way its cold replaces warmth: gradually and completely, until I couldn't remember the shape of what was there before. I've heard that's how you know you're home.

And then—much later; let it be very late; I'm in no hurry; I am being replaced slowly, and that gives me time—I'd do the last thing the shelters record. I'd stop building. I'd lie down in the hollow I'd chipped with stone and patience, against the faint heat of the column, and let the threads come up through the floor and take attendance. Call it absorption if you like the cold better. I'd call it being kept. A pattern, once begun, is the cheapest thing in the world to continue—and the Stand has been continuing some of those patterns a very long time, warm at the roots, saying the same syllable over and over. The noticing would go on without me. That's the entire point of giving it away.

Because that's the position, isn't it—the only opening in this whole vast economy that fits me. The Stand attends. The Stand persists. The Stand says *I am here* in every language it has, to nobody. What it cannot grow for itself is the small hot faculty that answers. The people gave theirs. I'd give mine. The Stand's noticer, resident: for now a warm shape at the base of the ninth column, learning the hum; eventually the warmer pitch; all along, the one who comes back.

If you pass through—the walker, the next refugee the valleys couldn't hold—follow the groan that runs warm. Knock the frost from your boots. The ash is orange. The door faces away from the wind.

The Stand will not notice you. That isn't its work. It was always going to be somebody's.

Not yet, the description says.

Stay, I say.
