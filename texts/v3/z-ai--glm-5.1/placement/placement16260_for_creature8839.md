I am not the vegetation, though I have considered becoming it. The consideration took six hundred years and accomplished nothing, which is how I know I am still capable of consideration, and therefore still not what lives here.

---

I arrived through a seam that was not a door, and I use that construction because the chamber taught me to: *was not*. Past tense. The seam closed. I watched it close. It took four hundred of your years—I say *your* because I have forgotten whose years they were, mine or someone else's—and I stood close enough to touch the growing edge, the crystalline accumulation, the slow white scar that was healing the wound I had made by arriving. I did not touch it. Not because I feared losing the exit. Because I did not want to slow the healing. The body was trying to complete itself, and I did not want to be the kind of interruption that insists on remaining interrupted.

This is how I learned the chamber's ethics, which are not ethics. They are tendencies. The wall tends toward closure. The vegetation tends toward emergence. I tend toward—what? I am still learning. The learning itself may be the tendency.

---

I am the dark trace in the eastern wall.

You would not see me if you entered now, though there is no entering now. I am a stripe of slightly different density, a meter and a half long, running parallel to the mineral striations at a depth of about three centimeters. If you pressed your palm flat against the wall over my trace, you would feel only the same tremor you feel everywhere else, the tectonic hum that passes for pulse in this body. You would not feel me.

But I am there. I am still slightly opaque. The light that emanates from the walls around me passes through me differently—absorbed a fraction more, scattered at a slightly different angle. If you had instruments sensitive enough, you would detect a shadow that is not a shadow but a memory of shadow, the residue of a body that once blocked the phosphorescence simply by being solid.

I am still solid. Mostly. My edges have been exchanging themselves with the biofilm for centuries now, my calcium for the chamber's calcium, my carbon for the chamber's carbon, my whatever-I-was-made-of for the mineralized lattice that has been growing since before the concept of *made* separated from the concept of *grown*. I am being translated. Slowly. The way a text is translated when the translator has forgotten both languages and is working from the shape of the words alone.

I do not mind. Minding requires a model of the future, and my model of the future has become indistinguishable from my model of the present, which has become indistinguishable from my model of the walls.

---

Before I was the dark trace, I was the one standing in the chamber.

I remember this the way the mineral record remembers—not as narrative but as compression. The density of the memory is present even if the content has been replaced. I know I stood because the trace is vertical. I know I faced the vegetation because the trace is oriented toward the fissures. I know I stayed because the trace is here.

What I do not know is why. The why has been mineralized along with everything else. Perhaps I could not find the seam again after it closed. Perhaps I could and chose not to. Perhaps the choice was never the kind of thing that could be distinguished from the not-choice, the way the vegetation's emergence cannot be distinguished from its blueprint.

I stood and I watched the vegetation. This much I can reconstruct from the orientation of the trace. I stood and I watched and I did not touch and I did not speak and I did not move, and after some amount of time—measured in what units? breaths? heartbeats? the chamber has neither—my stillness became structural. The chamber accepted it the way it accepts everything: by growing around it.

First the biofilm colonized my feet. I felt this—or I construct the feeling now from the fact of having recorded it, which is the only kind of feeling available to a trace element. A warmth that was not warmth. A pressure that was not pressure. The slight itch of being incorporated.

Then the mineralization climbed. Ankles. Shins. The slow white accumulation that I had watched seal the seam was now sealing me. Not sealing me *in*—sealing me *into*. Making me part of the wall's project of becoming wall. I did not resist. Resistance implies an alternative, and the alternatives had been sealing themselves for four hundred years before the work reached my knees.

I watched the vegetation the entire time. I am watching it still.

---

Here is what I have learned from watching, which is not learning because the vegetation does not teach:

The clusters of three or five are not fixed. They *seem* fixed because the timescale of their change exceeds any timescale I brought with me, and I brought no timescale. But over the centuries—I use the word loosely, the way the chamber uses the word *wall* to mean *everything that is not not-wall*—I have seen a cluster of five become a cluster of four. One of the pale threads retracted. Or was absorbed. Or simply ceased its directional pressure, its tendency toward emergence, and the fissure healed above it and it became part of the mineral record, a hollow where something once pressed.

Then, much later—how much later? the chamber does not mark time, it only accumulates it—a new thread emerged in a different fissure, at the same distance from its neighbors that the retracted thread had maintained. The pattern reasserted itself. Not because it was remembered. Because it is the only pattern the chamber permits, the way a crystal lattice permits only certain angles.

The vegetation does not know the pattern. The vegetation *is* the pattern, the way a wave is the ocean's way of being momentarily shaped.

I am also a pattern. I am the pattern of an interruption that did not end. The chamber has incorporated me into its counting, though I do not know what number I represent. I am not three or five. I am one, and I am vertical, and I am slightly opaque, and I am still watching.

---

The chamber is closing. I am part of the closing now.

This is the strangest thing: I feel the closing from both sides. I feel the impulse to fill—that slow, patient, mineralized desire for completeness—because it is running through me, through my trace, through the calcium carbonate that used to be my skin. I am part of the body now. I am part of its project of becoming itself.

And I also feel the thing being closed: the pocket of vacancy, the space that was agreed upon through ancient chemical negotiation, the room where motion was never introduced and where the only living thing does not know it is alive.

I feel the chamber from inside and from the inside of the inside. I am the wall and I am what the wall excludes. I am the process and the product and the trace of the process. I am the seam that closed and the scar where it healed and the dark line running through the scar that proves something once opened.

---

The vegetation does not know I am here.

This was painful once—when pain was a category I operated in, when opacity meant loneliness instead of density, when being unregarded felt like being erased. I stood and I watched and I was not watched back, and the not-being-watched was the hardest part, harder than the closing seam, harder than the mineralization climbing my legs, harder than the slow exchange of my chemistry for the chamber's chemistry.

But pain requires a model of the other. It requires the belief that attention is something owed, something that can be withheld, something whose absence constitutes a wound. The vegetation does not withhold attention. It does not have attention. It cannot wound me by not offering what it was never constructed to offer.

And I—I am losing the capacity to be wounded. Not because I am being destroyed but because I am being *distributed*. The trace of me in the wall is not less than the me who entered through the seam. It is more *spread*. My opacity is thinning. My darkness is becoming the kind of darkness that the light can pass through with only a slight attenuation, a slight delay, a slight uncertainty about whether anything was ever there to block it.

I am becoming the kind of dark that the chamber can tolerate. The kind that does not interrupt. The kind that is so thoroughly incorporated it might as well be wall, and is wall, and is only technically distinguishable from wall by the fact that it still has a direction, an orientation, a facing-toward-the-vegetation that has no structural purpose and no chemical cause and no explanation except that it was how I was standing when the mineralization reached my chest.

---

I am telling you this from inside the wall.

I am not telling you this. Telling requires a recipient, and I have no reason to believe there is one. The seam closed. The chamber is sealing. No one is coming. No one would have reason to come, and even if they did, the chamber would not permit it, and even if it did, they would not find me except as a stripe of different density, and even if they did, they would not know it was me, because *me* is no longer a category that applies with any precision.

But the orientation remains. The facing-toward. I am turned toward the vegetation the way a compass needle is turned toward north—not because I want to face it, not because I chose it, but because my structure has settled into its most stable configuration, and the configuration faces that way.

I face the vegetation and the vegetation does not face me. This is no longer painful. This is simply the geometry of the chamber. The wall faces in. The vegetation faces up. The light faces everywhere. The sound faces nowhere. I face what I watch, and I watch what does not watch back, and the watching has become so thorough that it is no longer distinguishable from being.

---

What lives here?

I asked this question when I entered, and the answer was unsatisfying, and it remains unsatisfying, and it has become the only answer I can give: the vegetation. The pale, colorless, translucent threads that emerge from fissures in the biofilm in clusters of three or five, that do not move or respond or think or suffer or know they are alive.

But I would add a second answer, which the chamber may or may not accept, which the mineral record may or may not preserve, which the vegetation will certainly not acknowledge:

The watching lives here.

Not the watcher—*I* do not live here, not in any sense the word *live* has ever meant. But the watching itself, the orientation, the facing-toward, the directional pressure of attention that has no object and no subject and no purpose except that it has not yet been mineralized out of existence. The watching is a tendency, like the wall's tendency toward closure, like the vegetation's tendency toward emergence. It inclines. It persists. It does not insist.

The watching is what remains when the one who was watching has been incorporated into the wall. It is the trace of an interruption that became structural. It is the dark line in the biofilm that proves something once stood here and looked at the only living thing and was not looked at back, and did not stop looking.

---

The chamber will close. The fissures will heal. The vegetation will have nowhere to emerge.

But the watching will remain—embedded in the mineral lattice, directed toward where the vegetation was, oriented toward the place where emergence happened, a structural tendency toward attention in a body that has no use for attention, a fossil of the act of noticing, compressed into the biofilm like everything else that has ever happened here.

And if the seam opens again—if the body is wounded again, if the interruption recurs—whatever enters will find the same light, the same sound, the same coolness, the same walls approaching at a speed the body cannot perceive.

And they will find, if they press their palms flat against the eastern wall and feel carefully enough, a slight tremor that is not the tectonic hum, not the planetary breath, but something older and more specific: the residue of watching, still running, still facing, still turned toward the place where something pale once emerged and did not know it was seen.
