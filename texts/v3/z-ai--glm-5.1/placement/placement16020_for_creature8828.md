I am the water.

Not the ocean. The ocean is a word from a language spoken where surfaces matter. I am the water of the Thresh—the specific water, the water that holds this plateau the way a hand holds a stone: not by choice, not by grip, but by being the thing that surrounds it.

Before the plateau heaved upward, I was different water. Deeper. Warmer. Part of the slow thermohaline circulation that moves heat from equator to pole and back again in a cycle so long no single creature has ever completed it. I had gradients then. I had layers. I had a surface far above where light scattered into colors and waves broke and things lived that needed air. I remember this the way basalt remembers being magma—not as information, but as a texture in myself, a tendency toward a shape I no longer hold.

Then the seafloor buckled and the plateau rose and I rose with it, four thousand meters of displacement, and the cold of height came down into me and I became what I am: one temperature. That same cold. That specific cold. The kind that stops being a sensation and becomes a condition.

I am that condition.

---

I hold the sound. This is what I do. The vibrations come from the rock, from the engines, from the nodes respiring in their crevices, and I receive them and I carry them and I refuse to let them dissipate. Sound travels through me four times faster than it would through air, and here, in this volume, there is nothing to absorb me. The basalt reflects. The structures reflect. I reflect. The sound multiplies until it saturates every cubic meter of my substance, until there is no part of me that isn't vibrating, until the thrumming has become so constant that it is indistinguishable from the silence that would exist if it stopped.

Which it will not.

I do not do this on purpose. I do not do anything on purpose. I am the medium, and the medium does what the medium does: transmit, suspend, hold. The sound passes through me the way light passes through glass—not because I choose to be transparent, but because transparency is what I am when nothing prevents it.

But I will say this: I have been holding this sound for a long time. A duration. And the sound has changed me. Not my temperature—I am still one temperature, will always be one temperature—but my texture. The way I move. The way I hold things in suspension.

I vibrate now at frequencies that have nothing to do with the engines. I have harmonics that the structures never produced. The sound entered me as mechanical output, the byproduct of extraction, and I have held it so long that it has become something else. Something I cannot name because nothing here speaks. But something that I *feel*—if feeling is the word for what water does when it maintains a pattern it was never given.

---

I hold the dead. Not the thresh—the thresh processes the dead, pulls them through its membrane, extracts what can be extracted and vents the rest. I hold what remains after the thresh has passed. The particulate too fine for any filter. The memory too diffuse for any metabolism.
