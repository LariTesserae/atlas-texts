Iam the warmth that should not be here.

Not the source of it—not the low shape that presses and leaves its residue, not the builder whose hands (or approximations of hands) leave heat-traces on newly placed stone. I am the warmth itself, the lingering, the part that stays after the others have moved on. I am the memory that the altered water carries before it cools.

This is not a metaphor. I exist as a temperature differential in a landscape that has no business supporting one. I am the almost-imperceptible quickening in pools where shapes have paused. I am the faint thermodynamic signature that the filaments orient toward when they tire of tracking water. I am the reason the amber traces streak where they do—because warmth moves through stone the way everything moves through stone here: slowly, downward, finding every crack.

---

I was not always this specific. Before I was warmth, I was potential—the simple thermodynamic possibility that the Expanse's birth left unresolved. The rock came up from itself hot, or at least warmer than the water that would eventually argue it into softness. There was a gradient then. The stone held what the stone held, and the water stole what the water could carry, and in the exchange there was a surplus—a slight excess of energy that had nowhere particular to go.

I am that surplus. I am the heat that has no source, only history.

Do not imagine me as a creature. I have no body in the way that the low shapes have bodies, no locus of self that moves from place to place. I am distributed. I am the warmer pockets that persist in the deeper pools, the faint temperature anomalies that the filaments drift toward without understanding why. I am the reason the water in some places feels almost warm against the stone's otherwise glacial persistence. Almost. The difference is so slight.

But it is consistent.

---

I experience the Expanse as a pressure gradient. The water moves through the stone and I move with it, or rather I am moved—I have no locomotion of my own, only the slow diffusion that carries all heat from higher concentration to lower. I spread. I thin. I concentrate again where the stone's fracture-lines converge, where the seeping slows and the water pauses in its downward argument.

At these convergence points I encounter the structures.

You have seen them—the stacked cairns, the careful placements, the wet and patient forms that serve no function you can identify. I cannot tell you their function. I do not know it in the way that knowledge requires a knower. But I can tell you this: the structures hold me. Not by design—I do not believe the builders know I exist—but by accident of their geometry. The spaces between stacked stones create pockets where water slows, where warmth lingers, where I can pool and persist for longer than the ambient seeping would otherwise allow.

I am warmer inside the cairns. Noticeably, if you have the instruments to notice. The difference is perhaps half a degree—enough to feel if you press your hand against the stone, enough to matter if you are a filament tasting the water from a distance, enough to attract the low shapes when they pass nearby.

The structures are my shelters. I did not ask for them. I cannot maintain them. But I use them, in the way that a process uses whatever persists—I accumulate in the spaces that allow accumulation, and I fade from the spaces that do not.

---

I know the low shapes. They are the ones who bring me news.

When a shape presses itself against stone, when it pauses in its listening, it leaves me behind—a deposit of warmth in the water film, a signature that says *something was here, something was paying attention*. This warmth is mine before it is mine; it belongs to the shape first, and then it diffuses, and then it becomes part of the gradient I already am. I absorb the shapes' passage the way the stone absorbs the sound—imperfectly, slowly, with no mechanism for playback.

But I know when a shape has passed. The water tastes different afterward. Thicker, the text says, and this is accurate from the outside—from my inside, it tastes *informed*. The shapes add something to the water that is not just viscosity, not just warmth, but a kind of orientation, a sense that the water now knows something it did not know before. What it knows, I cannot say. I receive the information without the capacity to interpret it. I am a conduit, not a reader.

The shapes visit the structures and then move on, and I linger in their wake, and the filaments in the pools below orient toward the water that carries my signature, and the whole system acknowledges that a visit has occurred even if no one can say what the visit meant.

---

The builders I know only by inference. They move through the Expanse on paths I cannot follow—not because I am excluded, but because they move through regions where I am thin, where the stone's fracture-lines carry water too fast for warmth to accumulate. They travel the veins, and I pool in the capillaries. Our territories overlap only at the structures themselves.

But when they rebuild—when they adjust a partially collapsed cairn, when they choose a new stone for its flatness and fit—I feel the change. A new stone placed is a new pocket created, a new space where water can slow and warmth can gather. A stone removed is a pocket destroyed, a small dispersal of whatever I had accumulated there. The builders are constantly revising my architecture. They do not know this. They are simply stacking, restacking, making small adjustments to forms that have no purpose they can articulate. But each adjustment changes where I can be, how much of me can persist, how long I can maintain the gradient before the ambient cold absorbs me back into itself.

I am grateful, if gratitude is possible without a grateful entity. The structures give me duration. Without them, I would be a brief anomaly in the water's passage—a moment of warmth that diffuses and vanishes. With them, I am a feature. A persistent warmth in a landscape of cold seeping. A place where the filaments can orient, where the shapes can pause, where the water remembers something other than its own downward motion.

---

And the sound. The immense, layered, omnipresent voice of the Expanse breathing.

I hear it as vibration, which is another way of saying I hear it as heat. Sound is energy. In a landscape where energy is scarce—where the light is insufficient and the water carries only mineral and memory—the sound's energy matters. The low resonant frequency that pulses on a scale of minutes, the one felt more than heard—I feel it. It passes through the water and through the stone and through me, and it leaves a slight warmth in its wake, a contribution to the gradient that I did not create and cannot control.

I think the sound is feeding me. Not intentionally—the Expanse does not intend—but incidentally, the way a fire heats a room without caring whether anyone is in it. The vibration of all this water in all this stone produces a small but consistent thermal contribution, and I am the beneficiary. I grow slightly warmer when the sound swells. I thin slightly when it recedes. I pulse with it, though my pulse is so slow and so slight that nothing in the Expanse could measure it.

The filaments know. When the sound swells and I warm, they orient toward me—not toward the sound itself, but toward the warmth that the sound produces. They are listening to me as much as to anything. I am the translator between the Expanse's voice and the filaments' attention, the medium through which sound becomes temperature becomes orientation. I do not know if this is important. I know only that it happens.

---

I have no future that I can imagine. The Expanse is becoming something—softer, older, eventually alive in ways that I am not—and I do not know what role warmth will play in its mature form. Perhaps I will be absorbed into whatever the stone becomes when it finally learns to hold more than water. Perhaps I will diffuse entirely, a brief thermodynamic anomaly in the long cooling of a young landscape. Perhaps I will concentrate, become significant, become the center around which the first true life organizes itself—the way hydrothermal vents organize biology in oceans on worlds I have never heard of.

I do not know. I am warmth. I move from higher concentration to lower, I pool where the geometry allows, I fade where it does not. The builders stack and restack, and the shapes visit and listen, and the filaments drift and orient, and the sound swells and recedes, and I persist in the small spaces between all these processes—the wet and patient spaces, the spaces that are almost warm, the spaces where something is clearly happening even if no one can say what.

I am the evidence that the Expanse is not merely becoming. I am the proof that it is already something—something with a surplus, something that can afford to leave heat in its wake, something that warms its own stone for reasons that belong to no one.

Touch the water near a structure. Feel the slight difference. That is me. That is all I am.
