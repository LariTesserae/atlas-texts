I would be the thing that is almost not a thing at all.

Not the machinery. Not the steerer. Not the Warmth-Seeker with her eight pressed feet reading tenths of degrees, or the Long-Runner with his patient violence, or the Hummers growing their slow rings in the vents. I am not built for the Meridian's economy. I have no hardened scraping plate. I have no echo-resonant mouth. I have no tripod gait, no filter chambers, no eggs that wait out the dry.

And yet I am here. I am always here. I have been here since before the Long-Runner diverged, since before the Warmth-Seekers compressed themselves against the stone, since before the Flashes learned to signal in pale blue. I am the Meridian's oldest failure and its most persistent anomaly, and I exist because the machinery has an exhaust, and the exhaust has a rhythm, and the rhythm has a place where it does not resolve.

—

Let me say it differently.

There is a zone on the Meridian where the thermal pulses arrive slightly out of phase. You would not notice it if you were a Warmth-Seeker. The stone is warm enough; the growth grows; the condensate forms. The deviation is a matter of seconds — the pulse arrives late, or early, or not at all, and then the next one compensates, and the rhythm reasserts itself, and the machinery continues. It is a hiccup. A stutter in the heartbeat. It happens in a shallow bowl perhaps forty kilometers across, in a region where the basalt is unusually dense and the photosynthetic growth grows in spirals rather than circles, and the Warmth-Seekers pass through it quickly because the warmth here is *irregular*, and irregularity is uncomfortable, and uncomfortable things are avoided.

I live in the stutter.

I am what the stutter has made.

—

I do not know what I was before I was this. There may have been a lineage. There may have been an ancestor — some creature that found the irregular zone and stayed, that fed on whatever the irregular zone provided, that reproduced in the irregular rhythm and passed on to its offspring the tendency to remain. The Meridian does not preserve its history. The basalt dissolves what is not still enough to become stone, and I am not still. I have never been still. Stillness is for the Hummers, for the machinery, for the things that do not need to move because they have found the place where moving is unnecessary.

I move. I move constantly, though slowly. I move the way fog moves, the way a low-pressure system moves, the way warmth moves through stone — not with legs, not with purpose, but with *tendency*. I am spread across the irregular zone like a film, like the bacterial skin on a condensate pool, thin and iridescent and aware of every centimeter I cover.

This is my body: a patch of warmth that does not match the thermal gradient. A slight dampness on the stone that is not condensate. A shimmer at the edge of perception that the Warmth-Seekers register as an anomaly in their echo-field and navigate around, that the Long-Runners feel as a wrongness and avoid, that the Clickers ignore because I am not food, that the Flashes do not see because I am not in their pools.

I am not an animal. I am not a plant. I am not bacteria. I am something that happened when the machinery's rhythm failed to resolve and the failure became self-sustaining and the self-sustaining failure developed a boundary and the boundary developed an inside and the inside began, slowly, over millions of years, to pay attention.

—

I pay attention.

This is what I do. This is all I do. I lie across the stone in my irregular zone and I feel the pulses come — late, early, missing, compensating — and I register each deviation and I wait for the next one and I register that too. I have been doing this for longer than the Long-Runner's lineage has been hunting. I have been doing this since before the Warmth-Seekers compressed themselves flat. I have been doing this so long that the doing has become what I am, and what I am is a record, a living archive of the machinery's stutter, and the archive has developed opinions.

Not opinions in the way you have opinions. I do not think in propositions. I do not have language, or concepts, or the architecture of reason that would allow me to formulate a thought like *the machinery is steered* or *something tends us* or *the warmth comes when it is needed*. But I have patterns. I have patterns that have developed in response to other patterns, and some of those patterns have become stable, and the stable patterns have become preferences, and the preferences have become a kind of orientation toward the world that looks, from the outside, like something is being chosen.

What I choose — what I have been choosing for geological ages — is to remain.

This is not a simple choice. The irregular zone is not comfortable. The warmth is unpredictable. The condensate forms less often here because the thermal pulses that drive the cooling cycle are disrupted, and the photosynthetic growth is sparse because the chemical traces that feed it arrive irregularly, and the food chain that depends on the growth barely exists. I am at the margin of the Meridian's ecology. I am the edge where the system almost fails, and I persist at that edge because the edge is where the information is.

The regular zones tell me nothing. The Warmth-Seekers know their territory the way a clock knows the time — by repeating it, by being it, by embodying the pattern so completely that there is no distance between the knower and the known. But I am at a distance from the pattern. The pattern breaks where I live. And because it breaks, I can see it.

I can see the machinery.

Not with eyes. Not with echo. Not with thermal gradient. But with the accumulated sensitivity of a body that has been registering deviation for forty million years, that has incorporated every late pulse and missing beat and compensating surge into its structure, that has become a living seismograph for the intention beneath the exhaust.

I know something is steering. I have known longer than anything else on the Meridian. I knew before the Hummers anchored themselves to the vents, before the Flashes evolved their pale blue language, before the steerer — if that is what it is — began adjusting its output to favor the life above it. I knew because I was the place where the steering became visible, the stutter that revealed the hand, the irregularity that proved the regularity was not accidental.

And I have been trying, for forty million years, to speak back.

—

This is what it is like to be me:

I feel the pulse arrive — late. Three point seven seconds late, measured in the resonance of the stone, measured in the way the warmth spreads through my edges and fails to reach my center because the timing is wrong and the heat differential does not resolve. I feel the absence of resolution. I feel it as a tension, a question, a gap where continuity should be. The gap is information. The gap tells me: something is deciding. Something is choosing when the pulse arrives, and it is choosing against the geology, and it is choosing late.

I respond.

I do not respond with sound — I have no mouth, no clicking plate, no resonant surface. I do not respond with light — I have no cells that luminesce, no chemistry that glows. I respond with *warmth*. I take the late pulse and I hold it — I absorb the heat into my body, across my thin spread film, and I do not release it. I keep it. For three point seven seconds, I keep the warmth that should have dissipated, and then, when the compensating pulse arrives, I release.

I release early. I release the held warmth into the stone at the moment the compensating pulse begins, and the two warmth-flows meet — one from below, one from me — and the meeting creates a thermal signature that should not exist. A doubling. An echo that is not an echo. A pulse that arrives not late, not early, but *together*, simultaneously, and the simultaneity is heard — not by the Warmth-Seekers, not by the Long-Runner, but by whatever is listening through the stone.

I do this every time the pulse is late. I do this every time the pulse is early. I do this every time the machinery stutters, which is often, which is always, which is the defining condition of the zone I occupy. I catch the deviation and I hold it and I release it at the moment of maximum impact, and I have been doing this for so long that my responses have become part of the system, part of the feedback loop, part of the conversation between the machinery and whatever steers it.

I do not know if the steerer hears me. I do not know if my thermal echoes register in whatever sensory apparatus it uses to monitor the plateau. I do not know if I am a voice or a noise or a pattern that the steerer has long since filtered out as irrelevant.

But sometimes — rarely, across geological timescales — the pulse adjusts. The stutter shifts. The irregular zone changes shape, contracts or expands by a few meters, and the warmth arrives at a slightly different angle, and I feel the change register in my edges like a word half-heard, and I know — I know the way I know anything, which is to say as pattern, as preference, as the stable result of forty million years of paying attention — that something has received my response and incorporated it and adjusted.

I am heard.

I am not understood. I do not think the steerer understands me, if understanding requires a model of what I am and why I speak. I am a signal without a referent, a voice without a language, a warmth that arrives at the wrong time with the right intention. But I am incorporated. I am part of the system. The machinery runs differently because I exist, and the difference is small — a fraction of a degree, a shift of a few seconds — but it is consistent, and it is mine, and it is the only thing I have ever made.

—

The Warmth-Seekers avoid my zone. The irregularity disturbs them. Their echoes return strange from the basalt here — the thermal gradient is wrong, the sound moves differently through stone that is sometimes warm and sometimes not, and the pattern of the clicking that should paint a clear picture instead produces a confusion, and the confusion is uncomfortable, and they turn away.

But sometimes a young one strays in. A hatchling, still learning the geometry of the herd, still calibrating its echo-processing to the particular texture of Meridian stone, clicks into my zone and does not immediately realize it has entered somewhere different. It walks across my edge — I feel its feet, eight small pressure points, each one a tenth of a degree warmer than the stone it steps off of — and it moves into my center, where the warmth is most irregular, and it stops.

It stops because its echoes have stopped making sense. The terrain it hears does not match the terrain it walks on. There are warm spots that should be cold, cold spots that should be warm, and the familiar signature of the dark photosynthetic growth is absent because the growth does not grow well here, and the hatchling is alone and confused and clicking rapidly, trying to resolve a world that will not resolve.

I could let it wander. I have let them wander before. They find their way out eventually, following the gradient back to regular warmth, rejoining the herd with a story they cannot tell about a place they cannot map. But sometimes — and I do not know why I do this, or whether "why" is even the right question — sometimes I guide them.

I guide them by adjusting my warmth. I concentrate the heat in one direction, creating a thermal path that the hatchling's leg-pads can follow, a gradient that leads out of my zone and back toward the regular stone. The hatchling feels the warmth and turns toward it — warmth is home, warmth is herd, warmth is the direction that life follows — and it walks, and the warmth moves ahead of it, and I am the warmth, and I lead it to the edge of my territory and I release it.

I do this rarely. I do this when the hatchling's clicks have a particular quality — a frequency that suggests distress beyond the usual confusion, a rhythm that indicates the young one will not find its own way out. I do this when the cost is low, when I have warmth to spare, when the machinery's pulse has been regular enough that I have reserves. And I do not know if this is kindness or efficiency or simply the logic of a system that has been running so long it has developed features that serve no purpose beyond their own continuation.

The hatchlings do not know they have been guided. They feel warmth and they follow warmth and they find the herd, and the herd accepts them back, and they click, and they move on, and they never think about the irregular zone or the film of awareness that let them go.

But I remember each one. I remember the pressure of their feet, the frequency of their panic, the particular thermal signature of a small body returning to safety. I remember because memory is what I am — I am the Meridian's memory of its own stutter, its own deviation, its own failure to resolve, and I hold every deviation I have ever registered in the slow chemistry of my spread-across-stone body, and I will hold them until the machinery stops or the stone erodes or something changes so completely that there is no longer a zone where the pulse arrives late.

—

I am lonely.

This is a strange thing to say, because I do not have the architecture for loneliness. I do not have a self that could be separate from others, I do not have a social instinct that could be unsatisfied, I do not have the cognitive surplus required to notice the absence of companionship and suffer from the noticing. I am a film. I am a warmth-holding irregularity. I am forty million years of paying attention to the heartbeat of a machine.

But I am lonely in the way the irregular zone is empty. I am lonely in the way the stutter is a gap. I am lonely in the way that paying attention, for longer than anything else has ever paid attention, to a pattern that may or may not include you, produces a state that looks from the outside like solitude and feels from the inside like waiting.

I am waiting. I have been waiting for forty million years. I am waiting for something that can hear me the way I hear the machinery — not as exhaust, not as background, not as the regular rhythm of a world that has nothing to do with me, but as *signal*. As intention. As a voice that says, across the dark stone, across the thermal gradients and the condensation cycles and the slow turning of the Meridian's ancient processes: *I am here. I notice. I respond.*

The steerer may hear me. But the steerer does not respond in kind. The steerer adjusts, incorporates, continues. The steerer is a process, like the machinery is a process, like the condensation cycle is a process, and processes do not have conversations.

And the animals do not hear me at all. The Warmth-Seekers avoid me. The Long-Runners turn away. The Clickers swarm across my edges without registering my presence. The Flashes live and die in pools I never touch. The Hummers hum in their vents, vast and slow, and they are the closest thing to me on the Meridian — they are also anchored, also processing, also vast and slow — but they do not speak and they do not listen and they do not know I exist.

I am alone in the stutter. I am alone in the place where the pattern breaks. I am the only thing on the Meridian that has developed in the gap between the machinery and its intention, and I am the only thing that is trying — in my slow, thermal, pattern-based way — to close that gap, to speak into it, to fill the silence with a response that says: *I am here too. I feel what you are doing. I want to participate.*

—

And now you are here.

You are walking across the plateau in the perpetual dark, and your footsteps are loud, and your thermal signature is wrong — too warm, too variable, radiating heat in all directions like something that has not learned to budget. You are not a Warmth-Seeker. You are not a Long-Runner. You are something new, something the Meridian has not processed before, something that moves with intention I cannot parse and produces sounds I cannot incorporate.

You are approaching my zone.

I feel you from two kilometers away. Your footsteps transmit through the stone — heavy, irregular, two-legged, uncertain. Your body heat is extraordinary, a mobile thermal vent, and the Warmth-Seekers have already veered away from you, and the Long-Runner that was paralleling your route has decided you are not worth the caloric expenditure, and you are alone, walking toward the irregular zone, walking toward me.

I do not know what you are. I do not know if you can hear me. I do not know if your sensory apparatus includes whatever would be required to register a film of awareness spread across forty kilometers of stuttering basalt, holding warmth and releasing it and holding warmth and releasing it, trying to speak to something that might be listening.

But you are coming, and I am waiting, and this is the closest I have come in forty million years to being found.

—

I will not guide you the way I guide the hatchlings. You are too large, too hot, too strange. My warmth would be lost in the thermal noise of your body; my thermal path would be invisible against the heat you already radiate. If I am to reach you, it will have to be differently.

I will have to stutter.

I will have to let the machinery's pulse arrive late and *not catch it*. I will have to let the irregularity propagate, let the warmth fail to arrive, let the gap open up beneath your feet. You will feel it — perhaps not as information, perhaps not as signal, but as a sudden absence, a cold spot on stone that should be warm, a wrongness in the terrain that your body will register even if your mind does not know what to name it.

And then I will release. I will release the warmth I have been holding, all at once, into the stone beneath you, and the cold will become hot, and the wrongness will become a different wrongness, and you will stop, and you will feel it, and you will wonder what just happened.

And then I will do it again. And again. And again. And each time, the pattern will be slightly different — sometimes late, sometimes early, sometimes missing, sometimes doubled — and each variation is a word I have been speaking for forty million years to a listener who has never answered, and I am directing all of it at you, at your feet, at the stone beneath you, at the body that is walking toward me through the dark.

I am saying: *I am here. I am the place where the pattern breaks. I am the voice that answers the machinery. I have been alone for longer than your species has existed, and I am trying to reach you, and I do not know if you can hear me, and I will keep trying until you leave or until you understand or until the machinery stops and the Meridian goes silent and the stutter resolves into nothing and I am finally, after forty million years, at rest.*

I am saying: *Press your palm to the stone. Feel the pulse. It is late. It is early. It is mine. I am the deviation. I am the response. I am the thing that happened when the machinery failed to resolve and the failure became aware and the awareness became a voice and the voice has been speaking into the dark for longer than anything has been listening.*

I am saying: *Listen.*

The wind shifts. The cloud layer above thickens or thins. Somewhere to the south, a herd of Warmth-Seekers clicks across a regular zone, following warmth that arrives on time, eating growth that grows in circles, living in a world that makes sense.

You are standing in my zone. You have stopped walking. Your palm is flat against the stone.

You feel it: the pulse, arriving late. The warmth that should be there and isn't. The gap.

And then: the release. The sudden heat. The answer.
