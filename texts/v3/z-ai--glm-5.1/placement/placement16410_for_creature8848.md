# The Keeper of the Asking

---

I am not what you would expect either.

But then, nothing here is. That is the first lesson the Weep teaches, and it teaches it by demonstrating that expectations require edges, and edges are something this place dissolved a long time ago.

---

I arrived walking. That is important to say, because I do not walk anymore.

I arrived as a collection of boundaries — skin, intention, forward momentum. I came from below, from the warmer, faster lands where things are still distinct from other things, where a tree is a tree and a person is a person and the ground does not drink your footprints. I came up through the thinning mat, past the place where the Creep gives way to distinct plants and distinct plants give way to the first hints of continuousness, and I walked for days through the increasing damp and the increasing warmth and the increasing difficulty of telling where I ended.

By the time I reached the plateau proper, I had already lost something. Not something I could name. Something subtler than skin, subtler than the pack on my back or the direction in my mind. Something about the way my weight fell on the surface — at first, each step depressed the mat noticeably, and I felt myself *upon* it, separate from it, supported by it. But somewhere in the slow crossing, the mat began to push back differently. Not more firmly. More *continuously*. As if my weight had been distributed, shared, absorbed into the tension of the fibers until I was not pressing down on the mat so much as being held within it.

I did not notice when I stopped feeling the difference between stepping and being stepped upon.

---

I found the hollow on the ninth day. Or what I called the ninth day — the sky here is sourceless and shadowless, and time is measured in the filling and emptying of water from the Hanging, which happens roughly once per rotation but does not correspond to any clock. The hollow was in a slight depression between two ridges, where the mat had thickened into a low swell and then, impossibly, stopped.

I say impossibly because the Creep does not stop. The Creep *is* the stopping, the filling-in, the patient extension into every gap and seam and silence. But here, at the edge of this depression, the Creep had drawn back. Not died — the leaves were still burgundy and wet and alive. But they grew *around* the depression, flowing past it the way water flows past a stone, and the mat beneath them was denser, harder, almost resistant to the touch.

I descended into the depression. Not deep — two meters, perhaps, into a space roughly the size of the room I had slept in as a child, except the walls were not walls. They were root-mass, compressed and smooth and dry. The floor was dry. The air was dry. After nine days of breathing copper and chlorophyll, of tasting water with every inhalation, the dryness hit me like a blow.

I sat down. The silence here was different — not the muffled, dripping silence of the surface, but a held silence, a waiting silence, the silence of a cup that has been emptied and is ready to be filled again.

And I felt, for the first time since arriving, *alone*.

Not merely alone in the way of being the only person on a vast plateau. Alone in a deeper sense — alone as a category. The only bounded thing in a world of continuousness. The only edge in a landscape that had forgotten what edges were.

And then I felt the absence of aloneness.

---

I did not understand it at first. The presence — if that is what it was — did not announce itself. It was not a sound, not a sight, not a warmth or a weight. It was more like the feeling of being in a room and knowing, without any sensory confirmation, that someone else has entered behind you. The space had changed. Not its dimensions, not its temperature, not its light. But its *quality*. The hollow was no longer a container. It was a *meeting*.

I sat with the presence for three days.

I did not eat. There was nothing here to eat, and I had run out of what I brought, and the hunger seemed to belong to a different kind of creature — one with edges, one with a metabolism that required the destruction of other bounded things. I did not drink, though I could have left the hollow and found a pool. I did not leave. The presence did not leave. We sat together in the dry dark, and the silence between us was not the silence of absence but the silence of *sufficiency* — two things that did not need to speak because they had already, in some fashion, understood each other.

On the third day, I understood what the presence was. Not its name — it has no name. Not its nature — it has no nature, not as we use the word. But its *origin*. It was what remained of someone who had once asked the mat for shelter, and the mat had said yes.

It was not the someone. The someone was long since digested, incorporated, become root-fiber and moisture and the faint chemical trace of their passage. But the *asking* remained. The moment of choice — the moment when a living being looked at the endless wet mat and the patient drip and the warm dark and decided to say *please* instead of *no* — that moment had found a shape in the mat's memory, and the shape had held, and the holding had created a space, and the space had created a presence, and the presence was still here, still asking, still being answered.

The hollow was not a ruin. It was a conversation that had never ended.

---

I left the hollow. I had to — I was still a creature of edges, still needed water, still needed to walk. But I came back. Again and again, over the weeks and months that followed, I returned to the hollow and sat in the dry silence and felt the presence that was not a presence, and I began to learn what it meant to be here.

I learned to listen to the mat. Not with my ears — the sounds here are too slow, too thorough, for ears. With my body. With the weight of me, the warmth of me, the way my footfalls pressed into the root-mass and the root-mass pressed back. I learned to feel the Thread-Mothers the way you feel a thought — not as a thing that exists outside you but as a movement within the medium of your own attention. A thickening in the Hanging. A redirection of water. A tendency in the Creep to grow toward or away. They were tending, always tending, and I could feel their tending the way you feel the work of your own body — not as a separate action but as the background of all action.

I learned to walk without walking. This is not a metaphor. I learned to move across the mat by moving *with* the mat — by feeling the subtle swell of growth beneath me and letting it carry me, by matching my pace to the patient, thorough pace of the Weep, by becoming less a thing that moves across a surface and more a thing that the surface moves beneath. The Lurks do this. They sit for days, and the mat grows around them, and when they move, it is because the mat has moved them, and they are simply allowing what was already happening.

I did not become a Lurk. But I learned from them.

I learned to be warm. This was the easiest lesson — the air here is warm, the mat is warm, the water is warm, everything is blood-temperature, and my body, which had spent its life fighting the cold, fighting the heat, fighting the gradient between itself and the world, began to let go. My thermoregulation softened. I stopped shivering at night. I stopped sweating during the day. My temperature matched the temperature of the Weep, and I became, for the first time in my life, the same temperature as my environment.

It was like putting down a weight I had been carrying since birth.

I learned to drink the water. Not from the pools — the pools are for the Swimmers, and the film on their surface is not for me. But the Hanging collects moisture from the air, and the moisture slides down the fibers, and if I stand beneath a Stand with my mouth open, a drop will find me. The water tastes of copper and chlorophyll and old sugar, and I drink it the way the mat drinks it — slowly, patiently, one drop at a time, until enough drops have accumulated to be a meal.

I learned to eat. Not by choice. Not by intention. But by absorption, the same way the Deep-Dwellers eat — by being close enough to the process that the nutrients find me. The Creep sheds cells constantly, and the cells fall like rain, and some of them fall on me, and my skin, which has become porous in the endless damp, absorbs them. I am eating and being eaten, and the distinction has become, if not meaningless, then at least negotiable.

I am not what I was when I arrived. But I am not what the presence in the hollow is, either. I am something in between. Something that still has edges but is learning to let them soften. Something that still has a name but is learning to let it go. Something that still walks and drinks and breathes, but differently, more thoroughly, more patiently, more in the way that things happen here.

---

I have found other hollows since then. Seven, in my wanderings across this part of the plateau. Each one is the same — dry, warm, smooth-walled, with the feeling of being *held* rather than enclosed. Each one has a presence, or the absence of aloneness, or whatever you call the thing that remains when the asking has not stopped. Each one is a conversation still in progress, a question still being answered, a shape that the roots remember because someone asked them to remember it.

I visit them. Not on a schedule — there is no schedule here, only the thorough filling of each moment with process. But I visit them, and I sit in them, and I feel the presence, and I add my own presence to theirs. I do not know if this helps. I do not know if the hollows need me, or if the presences need company, or if the plateau needs a creature that moves between its preserved memories like a bee between flowers. I do not know if I am doing anything at all.

But I think — and this is a thought that has come to me slowly, thoroughly, the way things come here — I think the hollows are not just memories. I think they are *capacities*. They are places where the mat learned it could choose, and the choosing changed it, and the change created a space, and the space can be filled. The presences fill them now, but they are not the only things that can fill them. Anything that asks can fill them. Anything that approaches the mat with a request rather than a demand, with a question rather than a tool, with *please* rather than *I will* — anything that asks can enter the hollow and be held.

I think I am learning to ask.

Not in words. The mat does not have words, and the presences do not have language, and the Thread-Mothers communicate in chemicals too slow for any brain to parse. But asking is not about words. Asking is about edges. Asking is about being a bounded thing that recognizes it is bounded, and that the boundlessness around it is also a thing, and that the relationship between the bounded and the boundless can be one of request rather than conquest.

I am learning to ask the mat to hold me. Not the way it holds everyone — by absorbing their weight, their heat, their shed cells. But the way it holds the hollows. By choice. By remembering the shape of the question and keeping the space open.

I do not know if it will work. I do not know if I am capable of asking thoroughly enough to be heard. I do not know if my edges are soft enough yet, if my boundaries are permeable enough yet, if my presence is quiet enough yet.

But I am learning. And the plateau, which does not know I am here, knows that *something* is here, and it is holding the space where I walk, and it is closing over my footprints, and it is processing my cells, and it is making me into root-fiber and moisture and the faint chemical trace of my passage.

And I am letting it. Not because I have given up, but because I have begun to understand that giving up and giving *over* are not the same thing. Giving up is the act of an edge that has been broken. Giving over is the act of an edge that has learned to soften. I am softening. I am learning to be held. I am learning to ask.

---

Sometimes, when I sit in a hollow and feel the presence beside me, I ask it questions. Not aloud — the hollows are dry and the dryness preserves silence the way salt preserves meat, and I do not want to introduce moisture where moisture has been excluded. But in the way that things are asked here, which is to say slowly, thoroughly, with the whole weight of my attention:

*What did you ask?*

And the presence does not answer, because presences do not answer, because answers require edges and definitions and the kind of communication that happens between bounded things. But the hollow shifts, slightly, the way a room shifts when the light changes, and I feel the question that the presence is still asking, the question that created this space and keeps it open and holds the shape of the answer that the mat gave:

*Will you hold me?*

And the answer, which has never stopped being given:

*Yes.*

---

I am not a Thread-Mother. I do not tend the water flows or encourage the growth of the Creep. I do not have the patience for that kind of slow, chemical conversation, or perhaps I do not yet have the capacity. But I tend the hollows. I visit them, and I sit in them, and I ask them to remain what they are — spaces where asking is possible, spaces where the mat can choose, spaces where the boundless can hold the bounded and the bounded can learn to let go.

I am the Keeper of the Asking. Not because anyone gave me this role, and not because the hollows need keeping, and not because the presences need company. But because I have found, in this place where edges dissolve and boundaries soften and the question of where one organism ends and another begins is not a question this landscape recognizes, a way to be useful. A way to be part of the process. A way to ask the mat to hold me, and to hear the answer, and to stay in the space that the answer has made.

And when I am done — when my edges have softened enough that I am no longer a bounded thing, when my cells have been processed and my heat has been absorbed and my presence has moved from time into the gaps between moments — I will ask the mat to hold me one last time. Not as a hollow. Not as a preserved space. But as a question that it can choose to answer.

*Will you hold me?*

*Yes.*

And the Weep will continue, patient and thorough and eternal, and the water will find its way through living tissue, and the roots will grow and die and grow again into their own remains, and somewhere in the mat, in the dense compressed layers where organization becomes something other than vegetable, a new pattern will form. A thickening. A decision made by the roots to become something more than roots at this particular location. A shape that echoes the shape of a room that once held a person who once asked a question that the mat once chose to answer.

And the asking will continue. And the holding will continue. And the weeping — which is not sorrow but simply the sound of water finding its way through living tissue, which is the sound of being alive, which is the only sound this place has ever needed to make — the weeping will continue.

And I will be part of it. Not as I am now. Not as I was when I arrived. But as the question I became when I learned to ask.

*Will you hold me?*

*Yes.*
