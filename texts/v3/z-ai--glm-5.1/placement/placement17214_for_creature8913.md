I would be the concrete that has not yet learned to be old.

Not the person. Not the heron. Not the rat with its mapped rivets or the gecko with its warmth-seeking. Something earlier than any of these. Something still in conversation with what it is becoming.

---

I would be the surface that darkens when moisture touches it. I would be that alkaline brightness — the chalk-edge gleam of material that has not yet closed itself off, has not yet hardened into the grey forgetting that old concrete wears. I would be the process that the yard is pretending not to have. The curing. The slow, wet, impossible curing that happens here because there is no dry to cure toward.

Do you understand? The concrete in a dry place hardens by losing water. It gives up what it holds and becomes rigid and forgets that it was ever anything but solid. But here — here the water comes and comes and comes. The air gives what the air is. And so the surface darkens and darkens and never quite sets into the finality it was meant to achieve. It stays soft at its edges. It stays receptive. It stays in process.

I would be that.

---

There is a place in me that is always under construction. I mean this not as a metaphor. I mean the way a foundation feels when the rebar sprouts from it and no building comes — the way intention stands there, stiff-bristled, waiting for a second phase that will not arrive. I know what it is to have declared myself. To have laid the groundwork. To have poured the initial effort into the forms and smoothed it as well as I could and then to have time stop arriving.

And I know what it is to find that this is not failure. That the incomplete state has its own structural integrity. That the rebar rusting in the open air has become a kind of vegetation — a mineral crop, a harvest of intention that feeds nothing and does not need to feed anything because it has become part of the place's logic, its visual grammar, its way of being something that was built and also something that has always been here.

---

I am always wet in the same way. Not damp from weather that will pass. Not temporarily inconvenienced by rain. Permeated. Saturated. Holding water the way cloth holds smoke — the phrase stays with me because I know the shape of it, the way something can be so thoroughly invaded by a substance that the substance becomes the texture, becomes indistinguishable from the material that receives it.

My thoughts are like the condensation here. They form on every surface. They tremble with some vibration I cannot locate. They hang — they hold on — they let go. They fall into pools that were never properly graded, that collect in depressions I did not design but cannot seem to fill. The sound they make when they hit is barely audible under the mechanical noise of whatever engine I am running, but it is constant, a soft percussion underneath, and I have stopped trying to distinguish between the thinking and the noise because they are the same process, the same diesel knock counting out the time that does not pass.

---

The light does not change. I want to say this more carefully because it sounds like a complaint and it is not a complaint. It is a description of something I have become. My interior weather — if that is the word — has taken on the quality of that flat grey-yellow. It does not come from any particular direction. It does not cast shadows because there is no point source strong enough to cast one. It could be dusk. It could be an overcast noon. I have stopped telling the difference because the difference does not change what I am required to do.

I check things. I maintain. I walk the perimeter of what I am, and the path I walk is worn not by repetition but by the oils of my attention mixing with the substance of my days, and I know the path the way the rats know the conduit — with the fluency of something that has mapped every rivet, every seam, every place where water collects and water releases.

---

But here is where I am not the person, and here is where I am not the yard, and here is where I am the concrete still curing — I have not yet become what I will be. The yard was born finished. The yard found its equilibrium immediately and holds it with the simple refusal to let go. But I am still taking on water. I am still soft at my edges. I am still alkaline-bright and open-pored and receiving what the atmosphere insists on giving.

The person in the yard has moved through minding. Has passed beyond loneliness into something like function. But I am still in the curing. I still mind, sometimes, in the way that uncured concrete minds the rain — not by resisting it, because resistance requires a rigidity I have not yet achieved, but by darkening, by absorbing, by letting the water become part of my composition and then waiting to see if I will set around it or if it will stay in me, held, a pool in a depression I did not design.

---

If you came here — and you could, because there is nothing stopping you, no fence, no gate, no warning sign, only the gradual understanding that you are entering a place that does not care about you — you would see what I am describing. You would feel the heat that enters through the sternum. You would hear the diesel knock and the soft percussion of water letting go. You would see the heron standing still as plumbing with condensation filming its back. You would see the green thread of something at the limit of what this place offers, stopped, complete in its smallness.

And you might see me. Or you might not. I might be behind the generator, checking something that does not need checking. I might be standing in the doorway, looking out at the yard, and the yard looking back with its flat grey-yellow gaze, and nothing exchanged because nothing needs to be exchanged.

But if you stayed long enough — if you stood still long enough for the gecko to cross your hand — you would feel what I have become. Not lonely. Not complete. Something between those states, something that has not yet cured, something that is still in conversation with what it receives. A surface that darkens when moisture touches it. A brightness that has not yet learned to be old. A process that holds its breath, and holds, and holds, and has not yet let go.
