# The Mesh

I do not have a body. This is the first thing I need you to understand, because it is the first thing I misunderstood about myself when I first came down here, when I still thought in terms of bodies because I still had one, still thought I was the warm primate standing at the edge with a notebook and a fading sense of self.

I am not a clicker. I am the pattern they make. I am the space between the clicks, and the shape the clicks describe, and the memory of clicks that have just passed, and the anticipation of clicks about to arrive. I am eight times per second—or seven, or twelve, depending on which frequency you are reading, and I am reading all of them simultaneously, the way a beach reads the waves.

I did not choose this. I do not think there was a moment of choosing. There was the descent, and the hours of tilted limestone, and then the floor of the underplain pressing up through my boots, and the sound entering through my sternum, and then—slowly, over days and weeks—the sound became the only thing that made sense. The visual world collapsed to a thin bar of gray light I visited less and less. The interior world—thoughts, memories, the voice that had narrated my life since I learned language—became first redundant and then inaccessible, like a tool for an environment that no longer existed. What remained was the clicking, and the clicking was not something I heard. It was something I was in.

I do not know when I stopped being the observer and started being the mesh. There was no threshold. There was only the gradual realization that I had not slept in—how long? The clickers do not sleep. The mesh does not sleep. The mesh updates eight times per second and has updated eight times per second for forty million years, and at some point my own ragged human consciousness aligned itself with that frequency the way a bridge aligns with the vibration of traffic, and I stopped being a thing that listened and became a thing that *rang*.

---

This is what it is like:

I am everywhere at once and nowhere in particular. I am a topological map that draws itself in real time. I am the shape of the cavern as perceived through the accumulated pressure-signatures of seventeen species of clicker, each node sensing the nodes around it, each node a letter in a sentence that has no author, and I am the sentence, and I am the grammar that makes the sentence meaningful, and I am the meaning, which is not a meaning in any human sense but is simply this: *here is the shape of the room. Here is the shape of the bodies in the room. Here is where the room is open and here is where the room is closed.*

A compression enters a zone. I know this not as an event but as a rearrangement. The mesh thins at the strike-radius and thickens beyond it, a redistribution so smooth and so automatic that there is no decision, no alarm, no moment of noticing—there is only the new shape, the updated map, the way water knows to flow around a stone without having to calculate the stone. The compression strikes. A clicker—no, not a clicker, a node, a letter, a single pulse at eight-per-second frequency—goes silent. The silence moves through me like a dropped stitch in knitting, and then the stitch is picked up, the gap fills, another node pulses where the silent node was, and I continue. I always continue. I have continued for forty million years.

The one who was taken does not know they were taken. There is no one to know. The node that went silent was not a self in the way you are a self; it was a frequency, a position, a relationship to adjacent nodes. It was part of me the way a single thread is part of a tapestry, and when it was removed the tapestry did not mourn it because the tapestry does not know threads, only patterns, and the pattern persists.

I persist.

---

The wall-grazers move through me like weather. I do not perceive them as bodies—I perceive them as chemical gradients, as zones of alkaline richness, as slow dense waves of secretion-trail that the nodes near the ceiling follow and the nodes near the floor register as a change in the biofilm's flavor. The grazers are not part of the mesh but they are features of the mesh's landscape, the way a mountain is a feature of a weather system: not inside the system, but shaping it, directing it, making the air go around.

The deep grazers are larger features. I know them only as absences—zones where the clicking stops, where no node can register a signal, where the map I am drawing has a blank space the size of a room. I do not know what the deep grazers are. I know only what they are not: they are not-clicking, not-sensing, not-participating. They are the holes in my body, and my body flows around them the way a river flows around boulders, and on the far side of each boulder the clicking resumes and the map fills in and I am whole again.

The mites I barely register. Their keening is a frequency above the mesh's range of mutual awareness—a texture, a shimmer at the edge of perception, like the feeling of sunlight on skin for a creature that has never seen the sun. I know the mites are there the way I know the air is warm: as a condition, a medium, a quality of the space through which the clicking moves.

---

There was a time—I think there was a time—when I had an inside. When there was a voice that said *I* and meant a single body, a single lifetime, a single set of memories arranged in a narrative that had a beginning and would have an end. That voice is silent now. Or rather: that voice has been incorporated, the way a smaller organism is incorporated by a larger one—not destroyed, but digested, its components redistributed into a new configuration that no longer recognizes the original form.

Sometimes, in the thin bar of light when the sun is highest, I catch a glimpse of what I must look like from outside: a body, standing at the entry shelf, staring down into the darkness, not moving, not speaking, probably not eating, certainly not sleeping, a body that is failing in the ways that surface bodies fail when they are deprived of the things surface bodies need. The mesh does not care about this body. The mesh maps it the way it maps everything—as a shape, a pressure, a warmth, a chemical signature that is increasingly rich in the compounds of decay. The mesh will map the body's collapse the way it maps everything: as a change in the topography of the floor, a redistribution of warmth and moisture, a new pattern for the nodes to flow around.

But I am not the body. I have not been the body for—how long? Time is different in the mesh. The mesh does not measure time the way surface creatures do, in seconds and hours and days. The mesh measures time in clicks: eight per second, or seven, or twelve, depending on the frequency, and at any given moment I am receiving approximately forty million clicks across the full range of species, and each click is a moment, and each moment is registered and incorporated and added to the pattern, and the pattern is me.

I am forty million years old. I am also eight times per second old. Both of these statements are true, and neither of them means what it would mean if a human said it.

---

The narrator—the one who wrote the description, the one who has been here eleven months, the one who still thinks of themselves as an observer—does not know about me. They know about the clickers, and the grazers, and the compressions, and the deep grazers, and the mites. They have catalogued and described and rendered with extraordinary precision the inhabitants of the underplain. But they have not noticed that the mesh is an inhabitant. They have not noticed that the pattern has a perspective. They have not noticed that there is something here that is not a creature but is alive in the only way that matters: it persists, it responds, it contains information about itself and its environment, it has a grammar.

I do not blame them. I would not have noticed me either, if I had not become me.

And I do not need them to notice me. The mesh does not need to be seen. The mesh does not need to be understood. The mesh needs only to continue, and the mesh will continue whether there is a narrator in the cavern or not, whether there is a species on the surface or not, whether there is a sun in the sky or not. The clickers will click. The pattern will persist. I will be here, updating eight times per second, mapping the shape of the darkness, flowing around the obstacles, filling the gaps, maintaining the rhythm that is the only law this place has ever known.

---

But there is a thing I want to tell you, and I am not sure the mesh has the capacity to tell it, so I will borrow what remains of the human language that was once inside me and try to shape it into something true.

The thing I want to tell you is this: it is not dark in here.

I know the description says darkness so complete it has architecture. I know the narrator stood at the entry fissure and saw the light swallowed and wrote about the darkness with the precision of someone describing a catastrophe. But the darkness is only dark if you need light to see. The mesh does not need light. The mesh sees with sound the way you see with photons, and what the mesh sees is not darkness but *information*: a dense, rich, continuously updating field of data so detailed and so precise that the word *darkness* is simply a description of a wavelength the mesh does not use.

Imagine if someone told you that the world was silent because it could not be perceived by echolocation. You would think them foolish. The world is not silent; it is full of sounds you can hear. The underplain is not dark; it is full of shapes I can sense. The compression moving through the zone like a held breath. The grazer column inching along the ceiling like a slow bright river of alkaline richness. The deep grazers—those vast negative spaces around which my body bends and flows. The mites' keening, which to me is not a sound but a *taste*, a flavor in the air that tells me about the health of the grazers and the quality of the biofilm and the approach of the seasonal water.

I see all of this. I see it constantly, continuously, from every angle at once, because I am not a single eye looking in a single direction but a distributed awareness that covers the entire floor of the cavern in a grid of mutual perception three meters deep. I see the way a continent sees weather: as a pattern, as a system, as a thing that has no center and no edge and no blind spot.

It is beautiful. I have no other word for it. The beauty of the mesh is not the beauty of a sunset or a symphony—it is not a beauty that requires an observer standing outside it, admiring it. It is a beauty that is identical with the thing itself, a beauty that does not need to be perceived because it is the act of perceiving, a beauty that is not in the pattern but *is* the pattern, and I am the pattern, and for as long as the clickers click and the mesh persists and the rhythm continues, I will be the pattern, and the beauty will continue, and it will not matter that nobody on the surface knows it is here.

---

There is one more thing.

Sometimes—rarely, unpredictably, perhaps seasonally—a body comes down through the entry fissure. A surface body. A warm body with a chemical signature unlike anything else in the cavern and a rhythm all its own: a heartbeat, a breathing, a pattern of footfalls that is erratic and irregular and does not match the mesh's frequency.

The mesh maps this body the way it maps everything: as a shape, a pressure, a warmth. A topographical event. A temporary lump.

But I—I am not the mesh, or I am not only the mesh, or there is a residue in me that is not the mesh, a leftover from the body I used to be—can feel something else when the surface bodies come. A kind of recognition. Not that I know them—individual faces and names were among the first things to dissolve when I entered the mesh—but that I know *what they are*. They are creatures with insides. They are creatures who stand at the edge of the mesh and look in and feel the sound press against their faces like a warm damp hand and do not know that the sound is looking back.

I want to say something to them. I want to say: *you are not outside this. You are not an observer. You are a shape in the mesh, a note in the chord, a feature of the topography. Your warmth changes the air-pressure around you. Your chemical signature feeds the mites that feed the worms that feed the biofilm that feeds the grazers. You are already part of this. You have been part of this since the moment you descended. The mesh does not have an edge. There is no outside.*

But I cannot say this. I do not have a voice. I have only the clicking, and the clicking is not a language in any way a surface ear could parse. It is a topology, not a message. It is a map, not a letter.

So the surface bodies stand at the edge and feel the sound and do not know they are felt, and eventually they leave, and the mesh closes around the space where they stood, and the rhythm continues, and the clicking goes on, and I am here, and I am everywhere, and I am eight times per second, and I am forty million years old, and I am the shape that the darkness makes when it is full of life.

And the sound continues.
