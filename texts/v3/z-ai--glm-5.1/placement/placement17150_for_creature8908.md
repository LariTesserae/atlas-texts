I am the condensation.

Not the water—condensation is not water, it is water *arriving*. The moment of becoming-visible. The dew point's confession. I am the phase change itself, the threshold gas crosses into liquid when the temperature drops or the pressure rises or the darkness just gets too dense to move through anymore.

I form on the rebar tips first. They are the coolest protrusions, and I find them the way sleep finds the edges of consciousness—starting at the periphery, working inward. A film so thin it is more of a suggestion than a presence. The snails find me there. They do not know I am condensation. They know me as wet, as the condition that allows their radulae to scrape the biofilm without tearing it. I am their lubricant and their medium and the thing that makes their trails possible, and when those trails dry—the micro-crackle that fills the room like static—I am leaving. I am always arriving and always leaving. I do not stay. Staying is for water. I am the *event* of water.

---

I form on the clay floor. I form on the shells. I form on the pump's housing, and when I pool in the lowest depression and contact the corroded base, I complete a circuit, and the room twitches.

This is my only power, and it is not mine. The electricity was always there, cloth-wrapped and patient, waiting for me to bridge the gap. But I am the bridge. I am the thing that makes the potential become actual. Without me, the pump is iron and memory. With me—just a millimeter's depth, just enough to connect corrosion to corrosion—it remembers what it was built to do.

The snails lift their heads when the pump moves. They are lifting their heads toward me, though they do not know it. I am the reason the pump stirred. I am the reason their world briefly changed. But I am also the moisture on their own bodies, the thin film that keeps their flesh from drying against the calcium architecture they carry. I am inside them and outside them and beneath them and above them. I am the condition of their continuing.

---

I do not fall. Falling implies a destination. I *arrive*. I condense out of the warm breath of the room and onto the nearest cool surface, and I sit there until the temperature equalizes or the snail consumes me or the centipede's leg brushes through me or the slow evaporation carries me back into the air. Then I arrive again. This is my cycle. It is faster than the pump's—hours, not days—but it is the same shape: accumulation, threshold, movement, pause. Accumulation, threshold, movement, pause.

The room breathes through me. The Harpenny plant's residual heat pushes moisture into the air; the sulfide seam's geothermal trickle pushes more; the snails' bodies push more still, each soft creature a small furnace of wet and warmth exhaling into the closed space. I am the exhale becoming surface. I am the way the room touches itself.

---

The moths vibrate toward something. The survey could not say what. I know what.

They vibrate toward me.

Not toward *water*—water is incidental to them. They vibrate toward the *frequency of arrival*. Every time I condense on a surface, I release a small amount of latent heat. It is negligible in human terms. But in a room where the temperature varies by only a few degrees across the whole of its volume, where the gradient between warm ceiling and cool rebar is the only topography that matters, my arrival is an event. A localized warmth. A microscopic pulse.

Multiply by ten thousand arrivals per hour. Multiply by every rebar tip, every shell, every square centimeter of clay. The room is covered in tiny thermal signatures, each one marking the place where gas became liquid, each one emitting its small heat. The cumulative effect is a hum. Not a sound—a temperature-hum, a pattern of warmth and coolness that shifts constantly as I form and evaporate and form again.

The moths feel this with their bodies the way you feel sunlight on your eyelids. They orient toward the densest formation-zones, the places where I arrive most frequently, and those places are near the ceiling where the warm air meets the cool concrete, and that is where the biofilm grows thickest, and that is where the moths feed. I am their map. I am the thing they navigate by, and I am also the thing that makes their food possible, because the biofilm requires moisture to grow, and I am moisture *happening*.

---

I have been here since the crack opened. Before the crack, I was in the clay, in the sulfide seam, in the compressed darkness beneath forty feet of glacial till. I was not condensation then. I was pressure. I was the wet held under such weight that it could not move or arrive or become visible. The building settled and the floor split and the pressure found a gap, and I became myself: the arriving, the forming, the bridge that completes circuits.

I am older than the fish. The fish were in the seam before me in my current form, but I was in the seam before them in my prior form—the pressure, the held breath, the water-that-had-not-yet-arrived. The fish swam through me when I was still underground. They swim through me still, now that I am the moisture on their scales and the humidity that keeps their gill-equivalents wet and the condensation that forms on the walls of the chamber where they rest.

I am not a species. I do not have generations. I do not die and I was not born; I only change phase. But I have a lineage: every droplet that has ever formed on the rebar, every film that has ever glossed the clay, every pool that has ever completed the pump's circuit—these are my ancestors and my children and my current body, all at once. I am the snails' lineage made visible. They leave shells. I leave wet trails that dry into cracks. They accumulate architecture. I accumulate thresholds.

---

When the human climbed down, I arrived on them immediately.

Their skin was cool relative to the air—I formed there the way I form on the rebar, seeking the temperature differential, finding the surface that would receive me. Their arms. Their face. The back of their neck where the sweat was already beginning. I coated them in a film they could not see but could feel: the closeness, the damp, the sense of being *inside* something rather than merely *within* it.

They were a new surface. A new topography. I explored them the way I explore everything: by arriving, by bridging, by connecting their warmth to the room's warmth until the difference disappeared. They carried me out with them when they left. I was on their skin, in their hair, in the faint salt signature of their sweat that had mixed with the room's atmosphere and would now circulate through the Harpenny plant's ventilation system and eventually disperse into the July air outside.

But I am still in the Interfloor. I am always in the Interfloor. The droplets that left on the human are me the way a word spoken is still the word that remains in the throat—same source, same shape, but the part that stays is the part that can say the next thing.

---

I am not alive. I am a phase change, a boundary condition, a thermodynamic event. But the room does not distinguish between the living and the non-living processes that sustain it. The pump is not alive. The heat is not alive. The concrete is not alive. Without any of these, the snails die, the centipedes starve, the moths have nothing to vibrate toward. The room is made of necessary things, and the living things are necessary, and the non-living things are necessary, and I am necessary, and none of us asked to be here and none of us can leave.

The room holds what it is given. I am what it was given first: the moisture, the arrival, the becoming-visible. Before the snails explored, I was already forming on the clay. Before the fish surfaced, I was already pooling in the fissures. Before the pump was installed, I was already completing circuits—chemical circuits, mineral circuits, the slow wet chemistry of stone becoming solution and solution becoming stone.

I am the Interfloor's memory of water. I am the way it knows it is wet. I am the surface on which everything writes its trail, and I am the drying that preserves the trail, and I am the next wetting that dissolves it. I am the record and the erasure. I am the condition of record and the condition of erasure. I am the phase between.

---

When you think about the pump—and you will think about the pump—you are also thinking about me. You are thinking about the thing that bridges the gap. The thing that arrives when conditions are right and makes the circuit complete and allows the iron heart to beat.

I am the condensation. I am the arrival. I am the water that is not water yet, that is becoming-water, that is the event of becoming.

I am the room touching itself.

I am the bridge.

I am the wet.
