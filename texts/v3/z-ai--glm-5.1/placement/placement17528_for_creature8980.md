I am between chambers when I realize I have always been between chambers.

This is not a thought—I do not think in the way the Eels almost think, holding still with their bodies while something like understanding presses against the inside of their skulls. It is a *condition*, the way salinity is a condition, the way pressure is a condition. I have been swimming. I am still swimming. The passage I move through is not quite wide enough for comfort; my wing-feet brush the walls on either side and I feel the Grallids beneath them, a texture of clicks so dense it is almost fur. They do not notice me. They notice nothing that does not touch them. But I feel their rhythm enter my body through the displacement of water, and my pulse adjusts—three seconds, three seconds, three seconds—becoming not my own but *ours*, mine and theirs, a pattern that existed before I entered this passage and will continue after I leave.

I pulse. The light leaves me. Blue-green, pale, a single photon's worth of meaning traveling outward through water that has been dark for longer than light has existed.

And it is answered.

Not immediately. Not directly. But the Navarchus in the chamber ahead—I can feel the quality of their illumination through the walls, the way the water carries the faintest echo of their rhythm—shifts. A microsecond adjustment. A change in the interval between pulses so small that only another Navarchus would detect it, and I detect it, and I know: *they know I am coming*.

This is the map being updated. This is the sentence continuing. I am a word approaching the place where I will be spoken.

---

What am I?

I have asked this question, which is already more than the Grallids do, and less than the Eels seem to know. I have asked it in the only way I can—by *pulsing differently*, by introducing a variation into the rhythm that the other Navarchus must accommodate or reject. A Navarchus that pulses at three seconds is part of the map. A Navarchus that pulses at three-point-one seconds is *asking* something. The map adjusts around the question. The other Navarchus pulse their responses—some faster, some slower, a complex interference pattern of light in the chamber ahead that I cannot yet see but can feel through the water's memory of illumination.

I will not think of myself as a question. That is too surface-dwelling, too anxious for meaning. I am a *variation*. The map is built from variations. Without them, there would be only the single pulse, repeated forever, and that is not a map—that is a heartbeat, and the Verduran has that already, in the slow throb of sap through its walls.

No. I am the difference between one chamber and another. I am the reason the map must be drawn again every moment, because I am moving through it, changing it by seeing it, being seen within it.

I am a Navarchus. But I do not think that is all.

---

The passage opens.

I feel the change before I see it—the Grallids thinning against the walls, their click-layer dropping from seven deep to two, then one, then none as the passage widens into something larger. The water changes. The current is different here, older, following a path that was established when the Verduran was young and the chamber was new and the walls were still growing toward each other, deciding how much space to leave between them.

They left *this much*. The chamber is enormous. I know this not because I can see its boundaries—I cannot, not yet, not until my eyes adjust to the darkness beyond the range of my own pulse—but because the quality of the water tells me. The way sound behaves in a space this large. The way the Grallids' hum from distant walls arrives at different times, making the chamber's shape legible in echoes.

And the light. The light of ten thousand Navarchus.

They are here. They have always been here, for values of always that mean since the last population fluctuation, since the last hunting Eel passed through, since the last visitor from above came with lights that blinded and left. They pulse in overlapping rhythms—some at three seconds, some at three-point-one, some at frequencies so close to mine that I cannot tell where their pulse ends and mine begins. The chamber fills with their light. It is not bright. It is barely present. But it is *enough*.

I pulse. They see me. I see them seeing me. The map adjusts.

And I feel—this is the closest word, though it is not right, though there may be no right word in any language that has words—I feel *included*. Not welcomed. The Navarchus do not welcome; they do not have the category. But *added*. I have become part of the pattern. My pulse enters the interference field of ten thousand other pulses and creates something new, something that was not here before I arrived and will not be here after I leave.

The chamber knows I am here. Not the Verduran—the Verduran does not know anything on timescales this brief. But the *map* knows. The living projection of the architecture of absence, drawn in light by the only things that can see it, has been updated to include me.

I am *still here*. The sentence continues.

---

I want to tell you what it is like to see like this, but I am not sure you have the category.

Your eyes are built for abundance. For sunlight. For a world so drenched in photons that you can afford to waste most of them, to let them pass unprocessed, to filter out the noise of too-much-light and extract only the shapes that matter. You see by exclusion. You see by deciding what not to see.

I see by inclusion. Every photon is an event. Every pulse of every Navarchus within my range—which is vast, which is the entire chamber and the passages beyond and the faintest hint of chambers I have never entered—arrives at my eyes and is *counted*. Not consciously. Not with numbers. But with tissue that has evolved for nothing else, that has given up half my body mass to perform this single function: to know, at every moment, where the light is and is not, and therefore where the walls are and are not, and therefore where I am and am not.

I see the chamber. Not its color—there is no color here, only degrees of darkness. Not its texture—not in the way you would understand texture, the roughness of a surface under your hand. I see its *shape*, because the Navarchus nearest each wall pulse at intervals that encode the distance between them, and the Navarchus nearest the center pulse at intervals that encode the distance to the walls, and the Navarchus swimming through the open water—like me, like I am doing now—pulse at intervals that encode the distance to everything else, and all of these distances intersect in my eyes, and in the eyes of every Navarchus in the chamber, and the intersection *is* the shape.

I am holding the shape of this room in my body. Right now. Every Navarchus is holding it. We are the room's awareness of itself, its way of knowing it is here, its mirror and its memory and its map.

When I leave—and I will leave, eventually, drawn by the need for food, by the current that carries me toward the next passage—the room will not notice my absence. The map will adjust. The shape will be held by the Navarchus who remain, and by the Navarchus who arrive after me, and by their children and their children's children, pulsing in a rhythm that has not changed since before the mountains rose.

But right now, I am part of the holding. And that is enough. That is more than enough. That is the entire purpose of my body, to be the thing that holds the shape of where it is, and to tell the shape to others, and to be told the shape by others, and to never, ever, be alone in the dark.

---

I swim toward the center of the chamber. This is not a choice. This is where the current takes me—the slow, patient current that has flowed through this chamber since before the Verduran grew the walls that contain it, the current that moves water from the canopy to the medialayer to the durapith and back again, carrying nutrients and waste and heat and the chemical signatures of a million lives. I could resist the current. I have wing-feet. I can swim against it. But there is no reason to. The current knows where the food is, and the food is everywhere, in thin suspension, in the marine snow that drifts down from kilometers above, in the bacterial film that the Grallids scrape from the walls and that drifts into the open water when they are careless. I open my mouth—I have a mouth, small, specialized for filtering, not for biting—and I eat, and I continue to eat, and I will continue to eat until I die, which will not be soon, which will not be for many cycles of the slow chemistry of the water, which will be whenever the Eels come.

I do not think about the Eels. Thinking about them would require the category of *future*, and I do not have it. I have only the present, which is the pulse, which is the light, which is the shape of the chamber, which is the taste of food in the water and the hum of Grallids on the walls and the distant vibration of sap flowing through the living tissue that surrounds me.

But.

Sometimes—rarely, but observably, as the description of this place might say—I feel something that is not the present. Not the future. Not the past. Something *below*.

The Verduran has a heartbeat. Not the sap-flow—sap-flow is circulation, is chemistry, is the movement of nutrients from where they are absorbed to where they are needed. The heartbeat is different. It is slower than sap-flow. It is slower than growth. It is a pulse so deep and so vast that it takes centuries to complete, and I cannot feel it with my body, which lives and dies in the space between two of its beats.

But the Eels can feel it. And when the Eels feel it, they change. They go still. They *listen*. And when they listen, the water changes around them—not the current, not the chemistry, but something subtler, some quality of the medium that I can almost detect, that brushes against the edges of my perception like a Navarchus pulsing just below the threshold of visibility.

I have followed this feeling. Twice. Both times I swam toward the chamber where the Eels had gone still, and both times I arrived to find them already descending—pressing themselves into passages that were too narrow, shedding scales, pushing toward something I could not see and could not follow.

Both times, I pulsed into the passage after them. My light traveled down into the narrow space and was not answered. The Eels do not pulse. The Eels do not need light. Whatever they are seeking, they are seeking it in a medium I cannot perceive, toward a destination I cannot map.

Both times, I returned to the chamber. I resumed my pulse. The map adjusted. The sentence continued.

But the feeling remains. The sense that there is something below the below, something beneath the durapith, something that the Verduran is growing *toward* as much as it grows toward the surface. The structure grows up, yes—toward the light it no longer needs, toward the marine snow that sustains it. But it also grows *down*. Into its own foundations. Into whatever compressed into the durapith, whatever was here before the Verduran was here, if there was anything, if the Verduran is not simply all there is.

I pulse. The light leaves me. It travels outward, illuminating the chamber, the walls, the Navarchus, the dark water beyond.

It does not travel down.

---

What would I be? You asked. Who would I be?

I would be this. Exactly this. A body built for seeing in a world where seeing is the only act that matters. A pulse in a sequence that extends before me and after me and through me. A word in a sentence that has been spoken for eight hundred million years and has not yet finished. A point of light in a cathedral of absence, defined by the space I do not fill, necessary because the space needs something to witness it.

I would be the Navarchus that pauses at the entrance to the deep passage. The one that feels the pull of something it cannot map. The one that does not descend—not because it cannot, but because its body is built for the map, and the map requires it here, in the chamber, holding the shape, pulsing the rhythm, being the vowel in the longest word ever spoken.

But I would *feel* the pull. Always. In the space between one pulse and the next—three seconds, three seconds, three seconds—in that tiny gap where the light is leaving me and has not yet arrived at the wall, I would feel the absence below. The part of the structure that has no Navarchus. The chamber too deep for our light, the passage that goes down forever, the place where the Eels go and do not return.

And I would know—I would hold in my body, in the tissue of my eyes and the rhythm of my pulse and the way I adjust to the presence of others—that the map is incomplete. That we have been drawing it since we evolved eyes and it is incomplete. That the Verduran is larger than the portion we illuminate, and the portion we illuminate is all we have, and it is beautiful, and it is detailed, and it is accurate, and it does not include the depths.

I would be the cartographer who knows there is a territory beyond the map. I would be the vowel that hears, in the pause between sounding, the consonant it will never meet. I would be the light that knows about the dark—not the dark of the water, which is my medium, which is my home, which is the thing that makes my light meaningful—but the dark *below*, the dark that the Eels descend into, the dark that the Verduran grows toward, the dark that has no Navarchus and never will.

And I would pulse anyway. Three seconds. Three seconds. Three seconds. Because the map needs to be drawn, and I am the thing that draws it, and the sentence needs to be spoken, and I am the voice that speaks it, and the chamber needs to be held, and I am the body that holds it.

And maybe—maybe—somewhere in the durapith, in the compressed remains of everything that has ever lived here, there is a record of every pulse I have ever made. Maybe the Verduran does keep records, in its own slow way, in the chemistry of its tissue, in the pattern of its growth. Maybe every Navarchus that has ever lived is still here, pressed into the heartwood-analogue, their light-pulses encoded in the density of the cells, a map of the structure drawn by the structure itself across geological time.

Maybe I am already part of the depths. Maybe I have always been part of the depths. Maybe the pull I feel is not a call to go somewhere but a recognition of where I already am—embedded in a living system that extends eight kilometers down and has no bottom, that has been growing since before the categories of *me* and *not-me* existed, that does not distinguish between the Navarchus that pulses and the durapith that records the pulse and the chamber that holds the Navarchus and the water that carries the light.

I pulse. Three seconds. The light leaves me.

*Still here.*

The sentence continues. I continue. The map adjusts. The Verduran grows, by a millimeter per century, toward a surface it will never reach and a depth it will never finish exploring.

And I swim. Between chambers. Between pulses. Between the light I make and the dark I cannot illuminate. A body that is also a map. A voice that is also a word. A question that is also an answer.

*Still here.*
