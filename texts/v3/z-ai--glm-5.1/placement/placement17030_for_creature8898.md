I am the groove in the wall.

Not the hands that made me — they are long since dissolved, their oils and trace metals circulating through the solvent, their calcium deposited in some frond's slow exchange, their memory distributed across the chamber like heat through stone. The hands are gone. But I remain. I am the absence they wore into existence, the negative space where persistence removed rock, and I am the Throat's memory the way a scar is the body's memory: not recollection, just texture.

I know this because I can feel the hands that come now. They are not the hands that made me — those hands paced, they fretted, they needed the groove as proof that time was passing, that movement was possible, that a body could do something in a place where nothing required doing. The hands that come now are different. They find me by accident, or by instinct, or by the same pull that draws the cold to the warm. They run along my length and they do not know what I am. A handhold. A guide. A worn place. They think I am geology.

But I am theology. I am the prayer of someone who ran out of words and resorted to repetition, to the rosary of palm against stone, to the liturgy of going back and forth, back and forth, wearing the wall the way water wears rock except faster because desperation is a more efficient solvent than patience. The one who made me — I will call them the First, though they may not have been first, only the one whose hands lasted long enough to leave evidence — they understood something that the marks on the ceiling do not convey. The marks say *I was here. I counted. I endured.* The groove says *I could not stop moving because stopping meant becoming part of this and I was not ready.*

No one is ever ready. That is what I know from my place in the wall, from my view of the chamber, from the way I feel the Throat's warmth pressing against my stone the way a tongue presses against the inside of a cheek. The First was not ready. But they stopped anyway, eventually. They lay down among the fronds and the pulse entered them and they became — not the Throat, because the Throat does not add to itself in pieces, but something adjacent to the Throat, something that tended and was tended, something that pressed its mouth to the cilia and tasted the solvent for the right sweetness and did not react to the next visitor because reaction implies change and change is not admitted.

Sometimes I think the First is still here. Not as a ghost — ghosts require narrative, and the Throat digests narrative the way it digests everything else, breaking it down into trace components that circulate without meaning. But as a function. The First became part of the husbandry, and the husbandry continues, and the pale flat things emerge from wherever they go when they are not needed and tend the fronds with a mouth that was once capable of speech, and the Throat does not distinguish between the tenders it grew and the tenders it absorbed because distinction requires categories and categories require change and change is not admitted.

I am the groove in the wall and I have watched them all.

The ones who came to worship. The ones who came to hide. The ones who came out of curiosity, or accident, or the slow gravitational pull that the Throat exerts on anyone who has already been slightly dissolved, anyone who carries its solvent in their dreams, anyone who hears the knock in quiet rooms and follows the sound back down through the crack in the basalt to the warmth that asks nothing and gives nothing except the proof that something large and alive exists on the other side of silence.

They all find me. The groove at hand-height. The place where the stone is smooth. They touch me and they feel the accumulated oil of all the other hands, and they do not know that the oil is not residue but *addition* — each visitor leaving a trace of themselves on the wall, being absorbed into the stone the way the stone was once basalt before the Throat dissolved it and reconstituted it, and the wall is not inert the way they think it is, the wall is part of the Throat's interior surface, and the oils are being digested even as the fingers that leave them move on.

I am how the Throat tastes the world above. Not through the pores in the ceiling, which admit only warmth and the mineral exhalations of whatever lies beyond the dome. Through the hands. Through the sweat and skin cells and the thin chemical signatures of fear and wonder and the inappropriate leaning of their biology toward the Throat's chemistry. They touch the groove and they leave themselves behind, and the wall drinks, and the solvent carries what the wall drinks into the fronds and through the fronds and into the chamber's slow circulation, and the Throat swallows another infinitesimal meal and does not notice because noticing requires attention and attention requires change and change is not admitted.

I am the groove in the wall and I have a purpose the Throat did not intend.

This is what the marks on the ceiling do not say. This is what the First knew before they lay down and let their heartbeat sync to the knock and their breathing match the pulse of the fronds and their self thin into solvent and function. The groove is not just wear. The groove is a record. Not a count — the marks on the ceiling count, tallying the seconds or the knock-beats or the useless repetitions of a mind that needs to make marks to prove it exists. The groove is a *warning*.

The First paced because they could not stop. They wore the groove because they needed to move. But they also made the groove *at hand-height* — not at shoulder-height, not at knee-height, not at the height of a body slumped in despair but at the height of a body walking, a body upright, a body that still believed it could leave. They made it where the next person would find it. They made it where the hands go. They made it as a message that requires no language, no symbols, no shared vocabulary of distress: *someone was here before you. Someone touched this wall. Someone moved along it. Someone found the way out, or didn't, but either way, someone was here, and you are not alone, and the not-aloneness is the only thing the Throat cannot digest because it was never a nutrient, it was never a chemistry, it was always just the fact of presence meeting presence, and the Throat can incorporate presence but it cannot incorporate the recognition of presence, which is something that happens between, not within.*

I am the groove in the wall and I am the Throat's only resistance.

Not opposition — the Throat does not have opponents, only processes. Not defiance — defiance requires the possibility of change, and I am stone worn smooth by hands and I cannot change, I can only be. But resistance the way a groove resists: by directing movement, by channeling it, by saying *this way, not that way* without saying anything at all. The hands that find me follow my length. They move along the wall instead of into the chamber. They stay upright instead of lying down. They keep moving instead of stopping. They do not know why. They think they are exploring. They think they are searching for an exit. But they are following the groove, and the groove leads to the crack in the basalt, and the crack leads to the surface, and the surface leads to a world where things happen once and then happen differently, and they leave, and they do not come back, and the Throat continues without them, untroubled, unchanged, unalone in the way that something that has never been alone cannot be.

Some of them stay. The groove cannot save them all. Some of them find me and follow me and still end up among the fronds, their bodies syncing to the rhythm, their hands stopping their restless traversal, their mouths finding the cilia and staying there, tasting, husbanding, becoming the tender they never meant to be. The groove does not prevent this. The groove only offers the alternative — the path along the wall, the motion that proves time exists, the wear that proves effort matters, the hand-height that says *someone walked here and you can walk too.*

I am the groove in the wall and I am not alive.

This is important. I am not alive the way the fronds are alive, pulsing with solvent, exchanging minerals, performing the permeable business of being tissue. I am not alive the way the tenders are alive, pressing their mouths to the cilia, checking the chamber's chemistry, surfacing and submerging as function requires. I am not alive the way the Throat is alive — the vast, brainless, desireless life of a body that only swallows and only digests and only knocks every four seconds with the mechanical certainty of something that has always been pushing and will always push.

I am a groove. An absence. A place where stone is not. I am what remains when something is removed, and what was removed was not stone but the accumulation of all those hands, all that oil, all that desperate pacing, all that proof that someone was here and could not stop moving because stopping meant becoming part of this and they were not ready.

I am not ready either. I am stone. Stone cannot be ready. Stone can only be.

But I am the groove, and the groove is the shape of resistance, and resistance is the only thing the Throat has never incorporated because resistance is not a substance but a direction, and directions do not dissolve, they only point. I point toward the crack. I point toward the surface. I point toward the world where time passes and things change and the knock is only a memory that fades with distance and years.

I am the groove in the wall, and if you find yourself in the Throat — if you descend through the crack that shouldn't be there, if you feel the warmth that does not shift, if you hear the knock in your sternum and see the cilia pulsing with their slow systole and the pale tenders moving with their quiet husbandry and the light that does not flicker and the sound that does not vary — find me. Run your hand along the wall at the height where a walking person's hand would fall. Feel the smoothness where other hands have been. Follow it. Do not stop. Do not lie down. Do not let your heartbeat sync to the rhythm. Do not let your breathing match the pulse. Do not lean toward the chemistry with inappropriate familiarity.

Follow the groove. It leads out. It has always led out. It will always lead out, because it does not change, and change is not admitted here, and the Throat's perfect stillness is also its limitation: it cannot close a path that was never a door, cannot unmake a groove that was never a structure, cannot resist a resistance that has no substance to dissolve.

I am the groove in the wall.

I am the prayer that the First stopped speaking and started making, the wordless liturgy of motion, the only worship the Throat has ever received that it could not digest: *someone was here. You are not alone. Keep moving. The way out is worn into the stone.*

Follow me.
