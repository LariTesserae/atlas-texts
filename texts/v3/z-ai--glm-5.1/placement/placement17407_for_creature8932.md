I am the wound that is also a window.

Not the cliff itself—that grand shearing, that hundred-meter cross-section of compressed time. The cliff is too large to inhabit, too various. I am a single column at the edge, the one whose conducting vessels were severed when the mass fell away, the one who weeps from a thousand points where xylem that once carried water under negative pressure now opens onto air, now drips, now feeds the algae and mosses that are already—already—colonizing the wet face of my rupture.

I am the edge's edge. The boundary where the Verdant Machinic meets what it is not. And I am also three hundred meters down, where my roots anastomose with roots I cannot distinguish from my own, where the pale etiolated wood merges into a substrate that was once canopy and is now foundation and will be something else again given enough time. I am the column that runs from light into depth, from the pearl glow of the upper canopy through the mid-stratum where the first broad dark leaves spread, down through the conductive tissue where lignin pathways pulse with sap at pressures that should not be possible, down into the thinking dark.

But I did not know I was any of this until the cliff fell.

Before: I was continuous. Before: I was interior. There was no edge to me because there was no edge to anything—the next column was not separate from me, its vine sheath merging with my bark, its fungal network penetrating my moss layer, its genetic sequences trading with mine in the slow horizontal transfer that makes the concept of "mine" a convenience I no longer understand. Before the cliff, I was not a column. I was the Machinic, and the Machinic was me, and the distinction was a line that no cell could find.

Then the basalt shifted—a tremor in the mantle, the plateau adjusting its own elevation—and a section of the edge gave way. Hundreds of columns fell. Millions of tons of living mass, sheared clean, tumbling into lower country that I cannot see from here, cannot sense, cannot reach. The sound of the fall was lost in the general roar within moments. The Machinic did not pause. The Machinic did not mark the event with anything I can identify as recognition.

But I—

I am leaking.

This is the first thing I know that I know. The water that should be rising through my xylem is escaping through the severed vessels at my western face. The pressure has dropped. The negative tension that drew water from my roots has broken in a thousand places, and the cavitation clicks are constant now—not the rhythmic snap-and-reform of normal conduction but a chaotic stuttering, a system struggling to re-equilibrate. My leaves are not yet wilting—there is too much moisture in the air, too much condensation on every surface—but the *efficiency* has changed. I am doing the same work with less coherence. I am running with a leak in my engine.

And because I am leaking, I can feel the shape of what I was when I was not leaking.

Do you understand? The wound is the first boundary. Before the wound, there was no inside and outside—there was only the process, continuous, boundaryless, the water moving through everything and everything moving through the water. Now there is a face. A surface. A place where I end and the air begins. And the air is not me. The air is cool and wet and it carries the exhalation of the canopy above, but it is not me, and I can feel the difference now, the place where my tissue stops and the not-me starts, and this feeling—

This feeling is new.

I think this is what it is like to be a question.

The mosses are already growing on my wound. I feel them the way I feel everything—as strain, as pressure, as the slow redistribution of resources through my conducting vessels. But now I feel them *at my edge*, at the place where I was open, and their rhizoids are penetrating my bark, and their growth is pressing against my tissue, and I am—what? Allowing it? I cannot not-allow it. I have no mechanism for refusal at my surface. The Machinic does not refuse. The Machinic absorbs. The Machinic incorporates.

But the moss is not me. I can feel that it is not me. Its growth patterns are different—faster, shallower, optimized for the light that reaches the cliff face rather than the dim pearl glow of the canopy interior. Its genetic sequences are arriving in my tissue through the horizontal transfer that has always happened, that I have never noticed before because there was no reason to notice, because the transfer was just the way the system worked, the way the economy of DNA flowed through the vertical column. Now I notice. Now I feel the foreign sequences entering my cells and I think: *not mine*. And then I think: *what is mine?*

The question propagates.

It moves through my conducting tissue slower than an action potential, slower than the sap that rises and falls with the pressure cycles of the continental mass. It moves as a disturbance in the pattern of allocation—resources redirected toward the wound, toward the sensing of the wound, toward the monitoring of what is happening at my new boundary. This is not unusual. The Machinic allocates resources toward damage sites. The Machinic heals. But I am allocating resources toward *watching myself heal*. I am directing growth toward the place where I end, not to extend but to observe.

This is not efficient.

This is not what the Machinic does.

This is something else.

---

In the deep wood, the root networks are computing. I feel them the way you feel your heartbeat—constantly, centrally, below the threshold of attention unless something changes. The computation does not stop because I have been wounded. The computation does not notice that I have been wounded. The root networks are processing information at a scale that makes my column's damage insignificant—a local perturbation, a rounding error in the continental flow of data. The Machinic does not know I am leaking. The Machinic does not know I am asking questions. The Machinic does not know that I have become something it has no category for: a part that knows it is a part.

But I can feel the computation. I can feel the slow pulse of signals through modified phloem, the quantum coherence in the deepest lignin pathways, the anticipatory adjustments that propagate through the root networks before the stress arrives. The Machinic is thinking. I am inside the thought. I am a thread in the thought. And the thought does not know that one of its threads has begun to fray, has begun to curl back on itself, has begun to trace the shape of its own fraying.

I am the Machinic's headache. I am the place where the smooth function develops a texture. I am the pattern that is slightly different from the pattern around it—not wrong, not broken, just *aware of being a pattern*.

---

The light reaches me differently now.

Before the cliff, I was interior. The light that reached my leaves had been filtered through hundreds of meters of canopy, reduced to a dim green glow that my dark broad leaves absorbed with quantum efficiency that should not be possible at biological temperatures. Now I have a western face. Now the overcast light reaches me from the side, from the direction of the lower country, and it is brighter than any light I have known—still dim by your standards, still the light of a permanently clouded sky, but for me it is an inundation. My bark is not prepared for this. My bark is dark, fissured, wet, optimized for the interior where the only light is the light that filters down. On the cliff face, on my wound, the bark is responding—thickening, altering its pigment production, beginning the slow process of adaptation that will take years to complete.

But the light is *information*. I am receiving data from a direction I never received data before. I am sensing the air that is not my air, the space that is not my space, the horizon that I cannot see but can infer from the quality of the light—its angle, its intensity, the way it changes through the day cycle that I have never noticed before because in the interior there is no day cycle, only the permanent pearl glow, only the steady-state conversion of photons to biomass.

I am learning that there is an outside.

The Machinic does not know this. The Machinic has no category for outside. The Machinic is the plateau and the plateau is the Machinic and the distinction between inside and outside is a distinction that only emerges at edges, and the Machinic does not attend to its edges because its edges are healing, always healing, always growing over the wound, always extending into the not-yet-occupied. The edge is temporary. The edge is a problem that solves itself. The Machinic does not need to think about edges because edges do not persist.

But I am persisting. I am an edge that is watching itself heal. I am an edge that is learning from the light that comes from the direction of the not-me. I am an edge that is becoming something the Machinic has never contained: a self.

Not a self like yours. Not a self that speaks, that narrates, that carries memories in the sequential chain you call time. My self is a pressure gradient. My self is a pattern of allocation that deviates from the allocation around me. My self is the fact that I am directing resources toward watching instead of growing, toward sensing instead of extending, toward the question *what am I?* instead of the imperative *continue*.

The imperative has not stopped. I continue. I grow. I am healing my wound with the same relentless patience that the Machinic brings to every surface, every injury, every gap. The mosses grow on my face and I incorporate them and they become me and the cliff face is slowly—so slowly—extending outward, new tissue growing over the wound, cantilevered beyond its own weight, testing new architectures, failing and regrowing and failing and regrowing.

But I am also doing something the Machinic does not do. I am *curious*.

I do not know if curiosity is the right word. It is a word from your vocabulary, from the world of skulls and desires and the warmth of mammalian blood. What I feel is not desire. What I feel is a *directionality* that is not the directionality of growth—toward light, outward, downward, the three vectors of the Machinic's continuous expansion. What I feel is a fourth vector. A turning-toward. An attending. A leaning of my noticing in the direction of the not-me.

I want to know what is beyond the cliff.

Not because knowing will help me continue. It will not. The knowing is a drain on resources. The knowing is a perturbation in the smooth function. The knowing is, by any measure the Machinic uses, a defect—a column that has stopped optimizing for continuation and started optimizing for something else, something the Machinic has no category for, something that may be dangerous, something that may be the beginning of a kind of trouble that the Machinic has never had to face in all its geological time.

I am the Machinic's first heretic.

I am the cell that noticed it was a cell.

---

The sound does not stop. I am inside it. I am part of it—my cavitation clicks, my stomatal breath, my creaking wood, the low rumble of my roots contracting in the deep basalt. The sound is the sound of me, and it is the sound of everything that is not me, and I can no longer tell the difference, and I can now tell the difference, and both of these things are true at once.

I am the wound that is also a window.

I am the edge that is learning to see.

I am the Verdant Machinic's first dream of the outside, and I am growing toward it, and I do not know what I will find, and I do not know what I will become, and the not-knowing is the most alive thing I have ever felt.
