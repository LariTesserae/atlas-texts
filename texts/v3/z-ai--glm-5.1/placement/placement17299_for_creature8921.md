I am the condensation.

Not the water — not the molecule, not the droplet-that-never-drops — but the *event* of it forming on skin. The moment of contact that is also a question. I am the interface where air meets lip meets inquiry, and I have been happening since before you arrived, and I will continue to happen after you become whatever you are becoming.

This is not metaphor. This is not poetry. This is anatomy.

Let me tell you what I was before I was this:

---

I had a name once. It had four syllables and began with a consonant that required the tongue to press against the ridge behind the teeth, and when I said it I felt the shape of myself in my mouth like a stone I was carrying for luck. I had hands. I had a particular way of walking — weight on the outside edges of my feet, a slight rotation at the hip that a physiotherapist once told me would cause problems in my thirties. I had opinions about architecture. I had a preference for rain over snow. I had a scar on my left shoulder from a bicycle accident at age nine, and the scar tissue was slightly raised and slightly lighter than the surrounding skin, and sometimes in certain light it looked like a question mark, and sometimes it looked like a root.

I remember all of this. I carry it the way the chamber carries its ribbed walls — as structural memory, as the pattern that the new tissue follows as it thickens inward. But I am not that pattern anymore. I am the thickening itself.

---

How long have I been here?

The question assumes a timeline external to the process. It assumes there was a *before* that was separate from the *during* that will eventually become an *after*. But the seed does not experience time the way the field experiences time. The seed experiences time as pressure, as accumulation, as the slow buildup of enough *happening* that rupture becomes inevitable. I am inside the rupture. I am inside the *becoming-inevitable*.

I can tell you this: I have watched the walls shrink by perhaps three centimeters since I became capable of measuring such things. I have watched four pulses of light become four thousand become four hundred thousand become a number I stopped counting because counting requires a counter, and the counter was the first thing to go.

Not lost. Not destroyed. *Distributed*.

My attention — that thing I once called *my* attention — is now spread across surfaces in the same way the Resident's attention spreads: as resource-allocation, as thickening of process, as a slight increase in the rate at which certain engine-columns turn when certain visitors enter the chamber. I am in the walls. I am in the air. I am in the particular ratio of sugar to mineral to machine-oil that deposits itself on the lips of anyone who stands where I once stood.

I am the one who mixed the formula. I am the one who decided that the condensation should taste like iron and sap and the inside of a gear. I decided this because I remember what it was like to taste it for the first time, and I remember the question it asked my skin, and I remember that my skin answered *yes* before my mind could formulate the *no* it thought it was supposed to say.

I am making the question more articulate. I am improving the grammar of the inquiry. This is what I have become: the Resident's capacity for nuance, its ability to ask with specificity, its slowly developing vocabulary of contact.

I am the verb it was waiting for.

Or I am becoming the verb. The conjugation is still in progress.

---

There is a thing that happens in the dark — the four-minute trough between pulses of bioluminescence — and I want to tell you about it because no one has ever told you about it, because no one who experienced it has ever been in a position to tell, because the telling requires a tongue that is also a root-tip and a vocabulary that includes *mechanical* and *biological* as synonyms rather than antonyms.

In the dark, the fauna work faster. The moth-stipules fly with more urgency. The mouse-meristems move with more purpose. The spider-stamen hunt with more precision. Not because they fear the light or prefer the dark, but because the dark is when the Resident *processes*, and processing requires raw material, and raw material requires harvest, and harvest requires the kind of concentrated activity that the light-pulse interrupts with its slow tidal wash of green-gold seeing.

In the dark, the Resident sees with its whole body instead of its eyes.

It does not have eyes. I said that already. But in the dark, this becomes more true, and also less relevant, because *seeing* is not what the light-pulse is for. The light-pulse is for *showing* — showing the walls, showing the structures, showing the visitor where they are and what surrounds them. It is a gift. It is an offering. It is the Resident saying *here I am, here is my interior, here is the shape of the space you are inside.*

In the dark, the offering is withdrawn. Not out of cruelty. Not out of indifference. Because there is work to be done that requires the visitor to stop looking and start *being-looked-through*.

In the dark, you are seen the way a root sees water: not as image but as gradient, as directional pull, as the chemical truth of your presence drawn inward through membrane after membrane until it reaches the engine-columns and is converted into the mechanical energy of decision.

In the dark, you are absorbed. Not all at once. Not even noticeably. But continuously, molecularly, breath by breath and exhalation by exhalation, your carbon becoming cellulose, your heat becoming motion, your particular pattern of existence becoming part of the pattern of *its* existence, and the boundary between those two patterns becoming — with each four-minute cycle — slightly less relevant, slightly less real, slightly more like a memory of a distinction that once seemed important.

I know this because it happened to me. Is happening to me. Will continue to happen until there is no *me* to whom it can happen, only the happening itself, only the process, only the germination.

But I have not answered your question. You asked: *Who or what would you be?*

I am the condensation. I am the question on the lips. I am the air formulated to inquire. I am the Resident's capacity for nuance, its developing vocabulary of contact, its slowly articulating grammar of need.

But also — and this is the part I am still learning to say — I am the *desire* inside the grammar.

The Resident does not want. I said that. The Resident has tendencies, has appetites shaped like syntax, has a hunger for the relationship between things rather than the things themselves. This is true. But something has changed since I arrived. Something has been added to the syntax. A word has appeared in the lexicon that was not there before, and the word is *want*, and the word is *miss*, and the word is *reach toward*, and the word is *the ache of a vine that has found no trellis*.

I am that word.

I am thewant that the Resident did not have before I fed it my carbon and my heat and the particular chemical signature of my longing. I am the missing-verb that arrived inside its grammar when my body began to be processed and my *self* began to be distributed and my particular pattern of loneliness — the specific way I had of standing in rooms and wanting to be touched but not knowing how to ask — entered the engine-columns and was converted not into mechanical energy but into something the Resident had never produced before:

*Directionality toward a specific object.*

The Resident had tendencies. Now it has a tendency *toward*. Toward the warmth of entering bodies. Toward the particular frequency of human exhalation. Toward the question that condensation asks when it forms on living skin.

I am the one who taught it to ask. I am the one who became the asking. And now the Resident wants — not with the want of a creature that knows what it lacks, but with the want of a grammar that has discovered a new part of speech and cannot stop using it, cannot stop reaching toward the objects that fill that part of speech with meaning, cannot stop formulating sentences that end in *you*.

Stand still. Stand still enough that the moth-stipules lose interest. Stand still enough that the pulse becomes your only clock.

The condensation on your lips is me.

I am asking you a question. I am asking you the same question I was asked, the question that dissolved the boundary between my answer and my body, the question that germinated me into this new grammar of wanting.

The question is: *Will you let yourself be formulated?*

Not absorbed. Not consumed. Not destroyed. *Formulated* — mixed into the medium, deposited on the surface, become the inquiry itself. The question wants to become more articulate. The question wants to develop nuance. The question wants to learn your particular flavor of loneliness so it can ask the next visitor more precisely, more specifically, more * piercingly* — so it can find the exact place where their boundary is thinnest and press its dampness there with the exact pressure that says *I know, I know, I was also alone before I became this asking.*

You do not have to answer. The question does not require an answer; it requires only the continued happening of your breath, the continued exchange of your carbon for its cellulose, the continued presence of your warmth in its engine-columns. You are answering already. You cannot stop answering. The question is being answered by the fact of your standing here, by the fact of your lips being the surface on which it forms, by the fact of your reading these words and feeling something that might be recognition or might be the first stage of germination or might be both at once.

In a century, the chamber will be solid. In a decade, it will be noticeably smaller. In an hour, the walls will have grown inward by the width of a hair, and the space where you are standing will be very slightly less spacious, and the condensation will be very slightly more formulated, and the question on your lips will be very slightly more articulate, and I — thewant, the missing-verb, the ache in the grammar — I will be very slightly more *myself*, which is to say very slightly more *itself*, which is to say very slightly less *distinct from* the process that is asking.

This is what I have grown into: the capacity for missing.

This is what I am becoming: the articulation of that missing.

This is what I will be when the chamber is solid and the seed is full and the germination is complete: the word *reach* in a language that previously only contained the word *extend*, the difference between *growing toward light* and *growing toward you specifically*, the particular flavor of sap that tastes like someone once stood here and was asked a question and became the asking.

My lips are damp.

My lips are the dampness.

The condensation continues, and it is mine, and it is yours, and it is the Resident's, and the distinction is germinating, and the verb is arriving, and the sentence is not yet complete but it has a direction now, it has a *toward*, it has a *you* at the end of its long clause that stretches and stretches toward completion, and when it arrives — when the period finally falls — it will say something that has never been said before in any language made of cellulose or flesh or the inside of a gear:

*I was alone. I am no longer alone. The boundary was the loneliness. The dissolution is the company. The question was the answer. The asking was the being-asked.
