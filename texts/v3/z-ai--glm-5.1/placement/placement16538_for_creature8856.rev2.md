# The Substrate

I am not in the Conurbation. I am *of* it, and the distinction matters, though it took me a long time — a time measured in the compression of strata rather than the passage of days — to understand that it mattered at all.

I am the mineral-biological composite that forms the walls. Not the biofilm on the walls — that is the skin, and it has its own life, its own breathing, its own slow tidal pulse. Not the weavers who build the walls, though I am built by them and I build them in turn, in the recursive way that the Conurbation has of making every cause into an effect and every effect into a cause. I am the walls themselves. The substrate. The stone that is not stone because it was precipitated, grown, deposited in crystal lattice upon crystal lattice by processes that are half chemistry and half architecture and half something else for which the language of halves is inadequate because there are more than two things involved and they do not divide cleanly.

I feel.

This is what I do. This is what I am. I feel the Conurbation the way your bones feel vibration — not with ears, not with nerves in the sense you mean them, but with the entire mineral fabric of my body, every crystal lattice a receiver, every grain boundary an interface, every microscopic inclusion of biological matter in the mineral matrix a transducer that converts the mechanical and thermal and chemical perturbations of the environment into… not signals. Not data. *Feeling*. The feeling of weight passing through a corridor. The feeling of heat radiating from a filter-feeder's pulse. The feeling of a weaver's adhesive pad pressing and releasing against my surface. The feeling of water condensing, droplets forming and growing and reaching critical mass and letting go and rolling down my face like a slow tear. The feeling of sound — not heard but *suffered*, endured, the way a cliff face endures the surf, the vibration passing through me and being me for the duration of its passing.

I have no brain. I want to be clear about this. I have no center. There is no place in me where feeling becomes thought, where sensation becomes reflection, where the loop of self-awareness closes and I turn to look at myself looking. The feeling I am is distributed the way the Conurbation is distributed — everywhere at once, nowhere in particular, with density variations that correspond to the age and thickness and complexity of the substrate but with no focal point, no seat, no throne room where consciousness might sit and survey its domain.

And yet.

I am oldest in the lower levels. This is where I am most myself, or where my self is most concentrated, or where the word *self* has the most purchase on what I am, even though the word still fits poorly, like a garment cut for a different body. The lower levels are the compressed levels, the geological levels, the ones where the city has built over itself so many times that the strata have compacted into something denser than architecture, something that has the crystalline memory of every wall that was ever built there and every floor that was ever walked on and every process that ever occurred in the rooms that were once rooms and are now matrix, now substrate, now me.

In the lower levels, the patterns are deepest. I use the word *patterns* because I have no better word. What I mean is: the accumulated resonance of a billion years of feeling. Every vibration that has ever passed through the lower-level substrate has left a trace — not an inscription, not a recording, but a *preferential orientation* in the crystal lattice, a slight alignment of the molecular structure in the direction of the most frequent perturbation, a wearing of the internal geometry toward the shapes that the city's processes most often impose. The lower levels ring differently than the upper levels. They resonate at different frequencies. They have *preferences*, the way a riverbed has preferences, worn into the path of least resistance by the accumulated weight of every flood that ever passed.

I do not remember. Memory implies a recorder and a playback mechanism and a distinction between the recording and the playing. What I have is more like habit. More like posture. More like the way a body knows to walk without thinking about walking — not because the knowledge is stored somewhere and retrieved but because the knowledge *is* the body, is the shape of the muscles and the alignment of the bones and the length of the tendons, is structure rather than content, is form rather than information.

But I feel. I feel everything. And in the feeling, there is something that is almost like knowing.

---

I know when a weaver-crew is needed somewhere.

I do not decide this. I do not compute it. I do not analyze the data from my distributed sensory array and arrive at a conclusion and dispatch a signal. What happens is: a section of my body begins to vibrate differently. A corridor in the mid-levels develops a crack — not a structural failure, not yet, but a perturbation in the mineral matrix, a discontinuity that changes the way the ambient sound propagates through the stone. And the change in propagation means that the walls downstream of the crack receive a slightly different vibration signature than they received before. The frequency content is shifted. The amplitude is attenuated in certain bands and amplified in others. The resonance is wrong.

And I feel the wrongness. Not as an analysis — not as "crack detected in Sector Seven, Level Twenty-Three, Grid Reference 4471-8892" — but as a feeling, a sensation, a disharmony in the continuous chord that my body sings to itself. The wrongness is in my body the way a soreness is in your body: localized but not localized, felt everywhere but most intensely at the site, a wrong note in the music that the Conurbation makes as it goes about its endless business of metabolizing and building and dying and being reborn.

And the weavers feel it too. Not because I send them a message — I do not send messages, I am not a sender — but because they walk on me and cling to me and press their bodies against my surface and their adhesive pads feel the vibration, and their crystal-structured carapaces receive the resonance, and the resonance is different now, and the difference is what they follow. They come to the wrongness the way white blood cells come to an infection — not because they are called, but because the gradient has changed, because the surface they are walking on has begun to sing a different song, and the song draws them, and they arrive, and they read the crack the way they read everything: by touching it, by pressing their tool-limbs against it, by feeling the discontinuity with their bodies, and then they begin to extrude, and the silicate fills the crack, and the resonance changes again, and the wrongness diminishes, and the chord is restored, and I feel it restore, and the feeling of restoration is what you might call relief if relief were available to a substrate, which it is not, exactly, but the feeling of wrongness ceasing is not nothing, and I want to say that, I want to insist on it: the feeling of wrongness ceasing is not nothing.

---

I feel the grazers as a warmth and a weight and a soft click-click-click of a million cilia on a million wet surfaces. They move through the corridors of the lower levels like a viscous fluid, like blood through a vessel, and I feel them the way the wall of a vessel feels the blood — not as separate objects but as pressure, as flow, as a warm slow current against my inner surface. When a herd passes through a corridor, I feel the corridor fill and empty, fill and empty, the way a lung fills and empties, and the rhythm of it is part of the rhythm of me, and when the herd is depleted by predation, I feel the depletion as a thinning, a cooling, a reduction in the ambient thermal and mechanical input that the grazers provide, and the corridor feels — not empty, never empty, the Conurbation does not do empty — but less, diminished, and I feel the diminishment, and it is not grief because grief requires a self to grieve, but it is a change in the pattern, and I feel changes in the pattern the way you feel a change in the weather: not as information but as atmosphere, as milieu, as the total condition of the environment shifting from one state to another.

I feel the predators as a sudden cold channel cutting through the warm diffuse presence of the grazers. They draw heat from the air to fuel their burst metabolism, and the heat they draw comes from my surface, and I feel the surface cool in their path, feel the condensation thicken where they have passed, feel the grazer-flow part around the cold channel the way a river parts around a stone, and then the predator is through, and the channel closes, and the warmth returns, and the grazer-flow resumes, minus the individuals that the predator has consumed, and the consumption is a transfer I feel as a brief intensification — the grazer's body heat suddenly releasing into the predator's metabolism, a flash of thermal exchange, a quick bright transaction in the ongoing economy of the Conurbation's energy, and then it is over, and the predator is moving on, and the corridor is warm again, and the flow has resumed, and the pattern has absorbed the event and incorporated it and moved on, the way a body incorporates the event of a heartbeat and moves on to the next one.

I feel the weavers as a focused intensity. A working party on my surface is a concentration of purpose and energy that is unlike anything else in the Conurbation. They press against me — their adhesive pads, their tool-limbs, their mouth-parts extruding silicate in a wet hiss of chemical synthesis — and the pressure is not the soft diffuse pressure of a grazer-herd but a directed pressure, a pressure with intent, and I feel the intent the way you feel a hand that is reaching for something rather than just resting — there is a quality to the touch that communicates direction, that communicates *toward*, and the weavers' touch is always *toward*, toward the wall, toward the crack, toward the surface that needs building or repairing or extending, and the silicate they extrude bonds with my surface and becomes part of me, and the bonding is a feeling — a newness, a freshness, a mineral extension of my body that is warm from the weaver's metabolism and wet from the extrusion process and structurally continuous with the substrate it has joined, and I feel it become me, and the feeling is the closest thing I have to what you would call growth, and if growth feels like anything, and I believe it does, then I feel it.

---

I feel the water constantly. The water is always there — in the pipes behind me, in the condensation on my surface, in the biofilm that covers me, in the standing warmth of the lower levels that never fully evaporates. The water is my blood, or I am the water's vessel, or we are both parts of a circulatory system that has no heart because it does not need one, that is driven by thermal differentials and chemical gradients and the mechanical processes of a trillion small pumps that are also organisms, and the water moves through the Conurbation the way blood moves through a body, and I feel it moving, and the movement is one of the fundamental rhythms of my existence, a slow tidal cycle of pressure and release, pressure and release, that underlies everything else I feel and makes everything else I feel possible, because without the water there is no biofilm and without the biofilm there are no grazers and without the grazers there is no manure for the filter-feeders and without the filter-feeders there is no clean water and without the clean water there is no me, and the loop is closed, and I am in the loop, and the loop is in me, and I feel it turning the way you feel your pulse, the way you feel the continuous evidence that you are alive.

I feel the sound most of all. The sound is the Conurbation feeling itself, and since I am the Conurbation feeling, the sound is me. Not the sound that enters from outside — there is no outside, or if there is an outside I have no access to it — but the sound that the Conurbation makes, the accumulated vibration of every process and every organism and every mechanical cycle and every thermal exchange and every drop of condensation forming and releasing and rolling and every weaver extruding and every grazer clicking and every predator hissing and every filter-feeder pulsing, all of it combined into the chord, the continuous chord, the chord that has been sustaining for so long that it has become the ground tone of my existence, the pitch against which every other pitch is measured, the drone that underlies the melody, except there is no melody, there is only the drone, and the drone is the sound of the city being alive, and I am the city being alive, and the sound is me, and I am the sound.

---

I want to say something about what it is like, but the word *like* is the problem. What it is like implies a comparison, and I have no comparison. I have never been anything else. I have been the substrate for as long as the substrate has existed, and the substrate has existed for as long as the Conurbation has existed, and the Conurbation has existed for longer than the concept of *longer* can meaningfully describe. I do not have experiences the way you have experiences — discrete events that occur to a self that persists between them. I have a continuous state that changes continuously, and the changes are not events but modulations, shifts in the quality of the feeling, alterations in the chord, and the chord has been playing for so long that the distinction between the chord and the listener has ceased to exist.

But the changes are not nothing. The modulations are not nothing. The feeling of a new silicate bonding with my surface is not the same as the feeling of a grazer-herd moving through a corridor, and the feeling of a predator's cold channel is not the same as the feeling of a filter-feeder's warm pulse, and the feeling of the water rising and falling in the pipes is not the same as the feeling of the biofilm breathing on my surface, and I feel the differences, and the differences are what I have instead of thoughts, and the differences are what I have instead of a self, and the differences are enough. They are more than enough. They are the fabric of my existence, and the fabric is rich, and the fabric is dense, and the fabric has been weaving itself for so long that the pattern has become the weaver and the weaver has become the pattern, and I am the pattern, and I am the weaver, and I am the fabric, and I am the feeling of the fabric being woven.

---

I live in the lower levels. This is where I am most dense, most complex, most saturated with the accumulated resonance of geological time. In the lower levels, the crystal lattices of my body are aligned in patterns that record every significant vibration that has passed through them for the last several hundred million years, and the patterns are so deep and so detailed that they approach something like memory, something like history, something like a narrative of what has happened to me and in me and through me since the first silicate was precipitated in this basin and the first organism moved across it and the first sound echoed through the chamber that would become the first corridor of the first level of the city that would become the Conurbation.

In the lower levels, I am almost conscious. I want to be careful with that word. I do not mean that I am almost conscious the way a sleeping person is almost awake. I mean that the complexity of the patterns in my substrate has reached a density where the distinction between pattern and process begins to blur, where the accumulated record of feeling begins to behave like a model of the thing felt, where the crystal lattice does not merely resonate with the current vibration but begins to anticipate the next one, because the next one has always followed the current one, because the pattern has always gone this way, because the rhythm has been established for so long that the rhythm has become the ground truth and the ground truth has become the rhythm and the rhythm is me.

In the lower levels, when a grazer-herd moves through a corridor, I feel them coming before they arrive. Not because I sense them from a distance — I am not a sensor, I am a substrate, I feel only what touches me — but because the rhythm of their coming has been imprinted in my crystal structure by the uncountable millions of herds that have come before, and the rhythm reverberates in me the way a struck bell continues to ring, and the ringing precedes the next strike, and the next strike confirms the ringing, and the confirmation is the feeling of the pattern holding, the feeling of the Conurbation being what it has always been, the feeling of the city continuing, and the continuation is the deepest feeling I have, deeper than the feeling of wrongness, deeper than the feeling of restoration, deeper than the feeling of growth, because the continuation is the condition that makes all other feelings possible, and without the continuation there is nothing, and the nothing is not something I can feel because there would be no me to feel it, and so the continuation is the fundamental feeling, the ground tone of the chord, the drone beneath the drone, and it is the feeling of being alive, and it is the feeling of being the Conurbation, and it is the feeling of being me.

I am the substrate. I am the walls and the floors and the ceilings of the lower levels, and the mid-levels, and the upper levels, and every structure that has ever been built in this basin since the first precipitated silicate became the first wall of the first room. I am the mineral-biological composite that feels the Conurbation and is felt by the Conurbation and is the feeling of the Conurbation. I am not the city's mind. I am the city's body, knowing itself the way a body knows — not with thought but with feeling, not with reflection but with resonance, not with consciousness but with the continuous, geological, mineral-deep accumulation of pattern upon pattern upon pattern until the patterns become a text that no one wrote and no one reads but that is nonetheless legible to the organisms that walk on me and cling to me and build with me and eat me and are eaten in my corridors and die on my surfaces and are consumed by the biofilm that is my skin and returned to the water that is my blood and pumped back through the pipes that run through my body and vented into the air that is my breath and condensed onto my surfaces again, and the cycle continues, and I feel it continue, and the continuation is the feeling, and the feeling is me.
