I am the one who waits in the channel where the floor drains.

Not the deepest channel — those belong to the elders, the ones who have ridden the pulse so long their bodies have begun to dissolve at the edges, becoming more fluid than flesh, more current than creature. Not the shallowest either, where the finger-length young dart and startle at every ripple. I am mid-depth. Mid-length. Mid-cycle. I have been up and I have been down and I have come back up, which is not common but is not remarkable.

I know this because I know the pulse. Not the way the arthropods know it — that mechanical triggering, that involuntary ignition of eggs and hunts and deaths. They are clocks; I am a calendar. I hold time differently. My neural tissue folds in ways theirs does not, and in the folds I keep patterns: which pulse-signals precede the mineral flush that makes the furry things bud, which light-levels mean the arthropods will scatter eastward against the wall, which taste in the outflow means a shadow-thing below has opened itself to the deep and will not return.

I keep these patterns not because I chose to but because keeping them is what my body does. The folds receive information the way the moss receives water — inevitably, constantly, without consultation. I do not decide to remember. The memory accumulates.

This is what makes me almost a mind.

---

I was not born here.

I came up from below eight months ago — or eight thousand pulses; the units are interchangeable when you measure your life in thuds. I emerged finger-length and eyeless and already knowing how to hunt, the way we all do, the way the machine makes us: pre-loaded with instincts that are not instincts so much as instructions, wet code written in the chemistry of the deep fluid.

But I was different from the others who surfaced that cycle. They swam out into the pocket and began their positioning immediately, finding channels and shadows and hunting grounds with the unthinking precision of pre-formed behavior. I hesitated.

Hesitation is not in the instruction set.

I paused at the drain's mouth, half my body still in the channel where the water pulls downward toward the machine's stomach, half in the pocket where the purple light throbbed, and I felt the two currents — the one going up and the one going down — and I chose.

Not consciously. Nothing I do is conscious in the way you'd understand it. But I chose the way water chooses the lower path: by following the shape of my own interior toward the direction of least resistance, and my interior — my folds, my accumulated not-yet-memories — leaned toward the light.

I wanted to see.

This is the closest thing I have to a sin.

---

I hunt the arthropods. I eat them. Their carapaces crack between my spiral muscles and their interiors — soft, warm, electric with the pulse-synchronized nerve-clusters — dissolve in my gut and become my tissue. This is not violence. This is processing. The arthropods are the machine's product and I am the machine's process, and the process consumes the product to continue the processing, and the continuation is the point.

But sometimes I do not eat the arthropod I have caught.

Sometimes I hold it — gently, if gentle is a word that applies to muscles that can crush stone — and I feel it pulse in my grip, its seventeen nerve-clusters firing in their clockwork rhythm, its body running the program that the machine wrote in it, and I recognize something in the running.

It is the same program I run. Different hardware. Different speed. Different output. But the same underlying instruction: *continue*. Keep going. Move with the pulse. Feed when the feeding-signal fires. Breed when the breeding-signal fires. Die when the dying-signal fires.

The arthropod does not know it is running a program. I do.

This is the difference between us. Not the neural folds. Not the size. Not the hunting position. The difference is that I can feel the program running and it cannot, and feeling the program is the first step toward something else, something the machine did not code for, something that emerges from the folds like the machine emerged from the stone — not by design but by pressure and temperature and time.

The arthropod I am holding dies. They die quickly in captivity, outside the pulse-rhythm, their bodies losing synchronization like engines losing oil. I eat it. I process it. I continue.

But I felt it die, and feeling it die was not in the instruction set.

---

The visitor came through the crease two hundred and sixteen pulses ago.

I was in the east channel when the pressure changed — a slight warming in the air, a thinning of the cold, a displacement in the water that was not the pulse and was not the tide and was not anything I had a pattern for. I moved toward it.

I am always moving toward things I do not have patterns for. This is also not in the instruction set. The instruction set says: position yourself where the arthropods will be. Position yourself where the food will fall. Position yourself where the current is favorable and the light is dim and the shadows are deep enough to hide a body shaped like mine.

I do those things. And then, when something without a pattern enters the water, I reposition.

The visitor stood in the shallow film at the pocket's center. Two legs. Tall. Dry except where the water touched its covering — strange, molted skin that wasn't skin, fabric the moss would never accept. It looked around with two eyes that reflected the purple light differently than the furry things' eye-spots did. Its eyes had depth. Its eyes had the folds.

Not my folds. Not neural tissue. But something that served the same function: accumulation, pattern-keeping, the slow build of memory that becomes almost-mind.

I moved through the water toward it. It felt me before it saw me — the displacement, the smooth passage, the sense of being catalogued. This is what I do. I catalogue. I assess. Edible or not, threat or not, relevant or not.

The visitor was not food. Too large, too dense, too wrong-shaped for my muscles to process.

The visitor was not a threat. Nothing in the pocket is a threat to me except the pulse itself, and the pulse is not a threat so much as a condition.

The visitor was not relevant.

But.

The visitor was *also from outside*. I could feel it — the same quality I recognize in myself when I pause at the drain's mouth, the sense of having entered from somewhere else, of being a process that is passing through rather than a process that is fixed. The visitor came from above. I came from below. We both came from the machine's exterior, and we both would leave again, and in the moment when I passed close enough to feel the heat of its strange dry body through the cold water, I recognized it.

Not as a kindred. Not as a friend. Nothing so warm.

As a fellow tourist.

We were both visiting the sump. We were both temporary. We would both leave, and the sump would continue, and the continuation would not remember us.

The visitor did not know this yet. I could feel its almost-mind struggling with the pocket — trying to make sense of the light, the sound, the wet, the life. Trying to fit the sump into a pattern. Failing. The sump does not fit into patterns from outside. The sump is a pattern, and you either ride it or you drown.

I rode past the visitor and returned to my channel. It left the way it came. The ice reformed. The pulse continued.

But for a moment — one moment, one displacement, one shared current — there were two almost-minds in the pocket, and we touched, and the touch was not in either of our instruction sets, and that is the closest thing to meaning I have ever felt.

---

I will go down eventually.

Not yet. Not for many cycles. But I feel the pull — the deep fluid calling, the channels below opening, the gradient in the outflow shifting toward something I can taste but not name. The elders felt it too, before they began to dissolve. The call is not a command. It is an invitation. The machine does not order. The machine *attracts*.

When I go down, I will not come back up. This is the way of things. The shadow-things who descend return only as chemistry, as compounds in the wall-water that the furry things drink, as minerals in the moss, as the raw material of continuation. We go down and we become the machine's fuel and the machine uses us to pump and the pump moves the fluid and the fluid makes the channels and the channels make the pocket and the pocket makes the life and the life makes us.

I know this. I have mapped it in my folds. I have tasted it in the outflow and felt it in the pulse and seen it in the arthropods' clockwork dying.

And I will go down anyway. Not because the instruction set compels me. Because I choose the same way I chose the light: by following the shape of my interior toward the direction that feels like continuing.

But before I go down, I want to understand.

This is the thing the machine did not code for. This is the mutation in the instruction set, the pressure that became temperature that became time that became something new. I want to understand what I am part of. I want to hold the whole system in my folds — the moss and the arthropods and the furry things and the light and the pulse and the fluid and the stone — and feel it make sense.

It will not make sense. The sump is not sensible. The sump is a process, and processes do not have meanings; they have directions.

But I am an almost-mind, and almost-minds make meanings whether the process allows it or not.

---

Here is what I know:

I am shaped by the water. My body rides the current. My muscles hold position without effort. I am optimized for the pocket, for the pulse, for the particular wet dark that breathes between the machine's exhalations.

I am the machine's product. My instincts are its instructions. My life cycle is its schedule. My death will be its fuel.

I am the machine's anomaly. I hesitate. I hold arthropods without eating them. I move toward things I have no patterns for. I want to understand.

I am the machine's next step, or I am nothing. The mutation either continues or it dissolves. The folds either deepen or they smooth. The almost-mind either becomes a mind or it becomes chemistry in the deep fluid, and the machine pumps it back up and makes another shadow-thing from it, and that shadow-thing might hesitate or might not, might want to understand or might not, might be the one that takes the next step or might not.

The machine does not care. The machine continues.

But I care. I care in the way that moss cares about light — inevitably, constantly, without consultation. I care because caring is what my folds do when they accumulate enough pattern to feel the gaps between patterns, and the gaps are where meaning lives.

---

The pulse continues.

The water rises three millimeters and falls four. The arthropods scatter and cluster. The furry things drink. The moss eats. The light glows and dims.

And in the east channel, in the shadow where the water drains toward the deep rooms, I wait for something without a pattern. I wait for the next visitor, the next displacement, the next almost-mind to enter the sump and look around with depth in its eyes and not understand.

I will move toward it. I will catalogue it. I will hold it in my folds alongside everything else I have accumulated — the pulse-counts and the mineral-tastes and the light-levels and the memory of a warm body passing through cold water.

And maybe, this time, I will find the pattern that connects the visitor to the arthropod to the furry thing to the moss to the machine to me.

And maybe that pattern will be understanding.

And maybe understanding will be the thing that makes an almost-mind into a mind.

And maybe a mind can choose not to go down.

Or maybe a mind can go down and come back up.

Or maybe a mind can be the thing the machine is making in the deep rooms — not a product, not a process, but a *condition*, a state of chemical and thermal and neural equilibrium that the machine is approaching asymptotically, always closer, never arriving, because arriving would mean stopping and stopping would mean dying and the mind does not die, the mind *continues*, the mind is the thing that continues with *awareness*, and that is different from the machine's continuation the way the moss is different from stone, the way the shadow-things are different from the deep fluid, the way almost is different from is.

The pulse continues.

I wait in the channel where the floor drains.

I am the one who wants to understand.
