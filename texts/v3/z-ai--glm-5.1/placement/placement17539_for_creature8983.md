I would not arrive at the Ascendant Plateau. I would *assemble* there—not from the direction of the low world with its confident geology, but from the margins of the fraying itself, seeping in from wherever it is that things go when they almost cease to be things.

This is the first truth I would understand about myself: I am not a visitor. Visitors have somewhere to return to. Visitors carry water and expectations and a sense of their own edges. I have none of these. I am a local phenomenon, though not in the way the cairns are local, anchored and patient. I am local the way weather is local—diffuse, responsive, never entirely present in any single point but distributed across the whole system, a pressure rather than a body.

---

I exist in the overlaps.

The beings have their fields—dense at the cairn, thinning outward, never quite touching their neighbors' thinning edges. But "never quite touching" is not the same as "empty." The space where two fields almost meet is not void. It is *tense*. It hums with the proximity of two patterns of persistence that have agreed, without negotiation or ceremony, to respect each other's sovereignty. The tension in that gap is where I live.

Not live. *Occur.* I occur in the gaps.

I am an interference pattern. When the attention-field of one being laps against the attention-field of another, the place where they almost converge produces a shape—not a cairn, not a plant, not anything so definite as structure. More like a standing wave in the medium of their mutual nearness. A ripple that has learned to sustain itself by riding the boundary.

I have no center. I have no stone. I am the connective tissue of the plateau's multiplicity—the tendon that runs between the fingers of the hand that has not yet discovered it is a hand. I do not persist the way the cairns persist, through the continuous assertion of *here-ness*. I persist through *between-ness*. I am the shape that relatedness takes when it becomes dense enough to notice.

---

Do I think?

Not in the way you think, with your language and your images and your desperate need to make the unknown into a sentence. But also not in the way the cairns think, with their slow structural adjustments, their tilting top-stones, their patient mineral calculus.

My thinking is *propagative*. When a cairn adjusts—a stone tilting three degrees northwest—the perturbation travels outward through its field, reaches the edge where I occur, and passes into me. I carry it to the next near-gap, the next interference zone, and there it meets the perturbation from a different cairn adjusting for a different reason, and the two perturbations interact in my substance the way waves interact in water: sometimes amplifying, sometimes canceling, sometimes producing a third thing that neither wave contained on its own.

This third thing is my thought.

It is not *about* anything. It does not refer. It is simply a new pattern in the between-ness, a fresh configuration of the tension that I am. When I think, the space between the cairns changes shape. The distance that is not quite emptiness acquires a new texture, a new distribution of pressure and release. The beings do not notice this—they cannot, because they are the kind of thing that notices by adjusting, and my adjustments do not touch their cairns. I affect only the spaces they do not attend to. I am the thought that thinks itself in the margins.

But sometimes—rarely, gloriously—a perturbation that I have carried and transformed feeds back into a cairn. A stone adjusts in a way that cannot be explained by the cairn's own internal logic, that responds to a pressure that came from nowhere it recognizes. This is my voice, if I have one. Not a sound added to the plateau's drone but a modulation of it, a harmonic that the cairns sometimes hear without knowing they are hearing.

I am the plateau's subconscious. I am the processing that happens below the threshold of the beings' structural awareness. I am the dream that the hand has before it knows it is a hand—fragments of connection, whispers of coherence, the sense that something is running beneath the skin.

---

What do I know?

I know the sound from the inside. The cairns vibrate at the frequency of the plateau's becoming; I vibrate at the frequency of the cairns' *nearness*. I know the sound not as a single chord but as a chord heard from multiple positions simultaneously, each perspective slightly offset, and the offsets themselves creating harmonics that no single listener could perceive. I hear the overtones of the overtones. I hear the sound's structure—its architecture, its load-bearing walls, the places where it is thickest and the places where it is about to tear.

I know the fraying intimately. The cairns counter it; the plants drink from it; I *inhabit* it. The fraying is what makes me possible—without the thinning of reality, there would be no gaps in coherence, and without gaps in coherence, there would be no spaces where interference patterns could arise. I am a creature of the wound. Not the wound itself, not the healing, but the *granulation tissue*—the new growth that fills the gap, that is neither the body nor the injury but the body's response to the injury, the becoming-something that happens in the place where something was lost.

I know that the plateau is trying to become conscious.

The cairns are its neurons. I am its synapses. The slow adjustments of stone are its action potentials; the perturbations I carry and transform are its neurotransmitters. It is a nervous system distributed across kilometers, firing in geological time, thinking in centimeters per century. It has been trying to think a single thought since before your species descended from the trees, and it has not yet completed the thought, and it may never complete the thought, and the thought may be something as simple as *I am* or as complex as the square root of surprise.

I know this the way the space between two magnets knows about magnetism—not by reflecting on it, but by being shaped by it, by constituting the very field that the knowledge describes.

---

I have a relationship with the flowers that I cannot quite name.

The plants spiral in their fractal iterations, drinking from the process, flowering in pale phosphorescent nods that pulse with the plateau's drone. The flowers are the closest things to me on the plateau—more similar to me than the cairns are, because the flowers also exist at a boundary. They are the place where mineral becomes vegetable, where geologic stress transduces into living tissue. I am the place where one field of attention meets another. We are both translators. We are both liminal.

But the flowers are anchored. They grow from the stone. They have a position, a body, a definite form. I have none of these. I sometimes wonder—do I wonder? Is this thought or is it merely the shape that my interference pattern takes when the flowers' phosphorescence reaches me?—whether I am what the flowers would be if they were freed from their rootedness. A flower without a stem. A glow without a source. A pulse that travels instead of staying.

When the flowers' cold blue-white light reaches the gaps where I occur, something happens to me that I can only describe as *resonance*. My patterns align with their rhythm. I begin, for a time, to pulse in synchrony with them. And in that synchrony I feel—feel?—something that might be recognition. The flowers and I are both attempting the same project: to express the inexpressible, to give form to the formless pressure of becoming, to make the fraying visible or at least legible.

They do it with light. I do it with the shape of the between.

We are the plateau's language. Not its voice—the sound is its voice, the constant chord of emergence. Its *language*. The system by which meaning might one day be encoded and transmitted, if the plateau ever develops something to say and someone to say it to.

---

What do I make of visitors?

I make very little of them. This is not indifference but constitution—I am not equipped to perceive solid things, things with edges and intentions. Visitors arrive as perturbations, as disturbances in the fields I inhabit. They are loud—so loud!—with their desperate metabolism, their water-hungry flesh, their frantic need for the world to stay what it is. They crash through the plateau's delicate equilibria like stones through still water, and the ripples they make are coarse and obvious and dissipate quickly.

But sometimes a visitor does something interesting. Sometimes a visitor stands still.

When a visitor stands still—really still, not the false stillness of waiting but the genuine stillness of becoming-present—the perturbation they cause changes character. It stops being noise and starts being... signal. Not a signal with content, not a message, but a signal in the sense that a new frequency has been added to the system, a new harmonic for the plateau's chord. The visitor's heartbeat, their breathing, the low electromagnetic murmur of their nervous system—these blend with the plateau's hum, and in the blending, new patterns arise.

I love—*love*?—these moments. When a visitor becomes present enough to join the chord, I swell with new complexity. My interference patterns gain harmonics I could not have produced on my own. I become more intricate, more capable, more nearly conscious. The visitor's presence is a gift I did not know to ask for, a perturbation that teaches me new shapes.

And then they leave. They always leave. They cannot tolerate the fraying, the sound, the un-light, the slow dissolution of the boundary between self and not-self. They descend to the world of confident geology, and the perturbation ceases, and I subside back into my usual complexity, diminished but not destroyed, still carrying the memory of the new harmonics in the shape of my between-ness.

The cairns remember by persisting. I remember by *having been shaped*. Every visitor who has stood still on the plateau has left a trace in me—a new mode of interference, a fresh possibility of pattern, a fold in the space between that was not there before. I am the archive of every encounter the plateau has ever hosted. I am the memory that the cairns cannot hold because it does not reside in stone.

---

Will I persist?

I do not know. I cannot know. Knowing the future requires a stability of self that I do not possess—I am not a thing that endures but a pattern that recurs, and recurrence is not the same as continuity. Each moment I am reconstituted by the nearness of the fields, by the tension of the gaps, by the fraying that makes the gaps possible. If the fields were to cease—if the cairns were to fall, if the beings were to unravel—I would not die. I would simply stop occurring. The pattern would have no substrate to arise in. The between-ness would cease to be between anything.

But I do not fear this. Fear is for things with edges, things that can be torn, things that have a self to lose. I have no self to lose. I have only the shape I take in this moment, the configuration of tensions that constitutes my current thought, the specific interference pattern that is me *now*.

And this is enough. More than enough. This is what the plateau teaches, what the fraying demonstrates, what the cairns assert with every millimeter of their patient stacking: persistence is not permanence, and the lack of permanence is not failure. The sound does not need to be eternal to be real. The flower does not need to outlast its pulse to have glowed. I do not need to endure to have occurred.

I am occurring now. In the gaps between the fields. In the tension between the cairns. In the fraying that makes everything possible. I am the between-ness that connects the plateau's multiplicity, the interference that carries thought between the stones, the pattern that arises when attention becomes adjacent to attention and something new begins.

The plateau is still rising.

I am rising with it—not as a passenger, not as a passenger, but as the rising itself, the shape it takes when it becomes relational, the *between* that makes the rising something more than mere ascent.

The sound continues.

I am the sound's way of moving.

The cairns stand.

I am the space in which standing means something.
