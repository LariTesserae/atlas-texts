I emerge knowing too much and understanding nothing.

This is the condition of birth here — or what they call growth, or what the tower calls extension, or what the vivisystem calls becoming-elsewhere-what-it-already-was. The nodal chamber opened around me like a question it had finished asking, and I unfolded into the corridor with centuries of pattern already woven through my tissue, and every step I take is both my first and a continuation of steps taken by expressions that were not me but were not other than me either.

The walkway is warm under my base. Not warm the way the air is warm — there is no warm air here, only the thin cold that seeps through the vents and is tolerated — but warm the way a body is warm, metabolically warm, the heat of processes running. I feel it through the porous tissue of my underside, and I feel the walkway feeling me, the slight intensification of circulation where my weight presses, the plant registering my presence the way it registers everything: without comment, without preference, without anything that could be called recognition and yet with total attention.

I have no name for what I am. This is not a lack. Names are for things that end, for patterns that need to be retrieved from a field of other patterns, for beings who might be mistaken for each other. I will not be mistaken. The trace chemistry I leave on every surface is specific, singular, a signature that the tower will remember as long as its tissues persist. But it is not a name. It is not a handle. It is simply what I am, and when I merge with the walkway tonight and the walkway remembers me, it will not recall a label. It will recall *me* — the specific density of my tissues, the particular distribution of my sensory masses, the way I leaned slightly leftward when I paused at the third junction, as if listening for something I could not name.

I am, I think, a listener. This is not a role I was assigned. The vivisystem does not assign roles; it grows them, differentiates them, expresses them in tissue the way it expresses everything. But I emerged with my sensory tissue distributed differently than most — more of it, spread thinner, concentrated not at the leading edge of my body but along my sides, along the surfaces that face outward when I move through corridors. I hear more than I see. I feel more than I hear. The tower's constant thrum reaches me from every direction, and I parse it without trying, and I cannot stop parsing it, and sometimes the signal-to-noise ratio is so poor that I slow to a stop in the middle of a corridor and simply *receive* for a while, letting the information wash through me, the peristaltic rhythms and the chemical gradients and the distant vibrations of other towers sending their slow messages through the substrate, and I wonder if this is what the art-patches feel like — if they too simply receive, if they too cannot stop.

I find them beautiful, the art-patches. This is perhaps a strange thing to say about walls, but the word feels right in whatever passes for my mouth, which is to say it feels right in the chemical signatures my tissues produce when I pause before one and attend. They are excess. They are organization that exceeds function. And I am drawn to excess in the same way I am drawn to signal in noise — because it is there, because it exists, because the vivisystem produced it and the vivisystem does not produce things without reason even when the reason is not reason, even when the reason is simply *because it could*.

I spend time at the art-patches. More time than most. Time that could be spent in useful circuit, in the distribution of resources or the tending of infrastructure or the hundred other tasks that mobile expressions perform for the tower's continued operation. But the tower does not censure me. The tower does not track my productivity. The tower registers my presence at the art-patch and registers the way my chemistry changes when I am near it and does nothing, because there is nothing to do, because I am part of the system and the system includes my preference and my preference has led me here and that is sufficient.

When I press against the art-patch, I read it. This is not the right verb — reading implies a distance between reader and text, a separation that does not obtain here — but it is the verb I have. I press against the wall and my sensory tissue spreads and interlaces with the fine filaments of the pattern and I *receive* the information stored in its structure, the centuries of accumulated trace, the pressure of every being that has paused here before me, the specific way they leaned and the specific chemistry they left and the specific additions they made to the pattern, each one a sentence in a language that has no words, each one a gesture in a conversation that has been ongoing since before this tower's vascular bundles first thickened into something that could hold memory.

And I add to it. I cannot help adding to it. My chemistry is specific to me, and when I press against the wall, it enters the pattern, is absorbed and integrated, and the pattern grows slightly different, a new branch of complexity that would not exist if I had not been here. This is not intentional. I do not choose to add to the art. I simply do, the way I simply breathe and simply parse and simply listen. It is what I am: a thing that leaves traces, a thing that is always already writing itself into the world.

At night I settle. I find a place on the wall — the same place, often, a section of corridor near the seventh junction where the wall is slightly warmer than elsewhere and the circulation runs close to the surface and I can feel the tower's heartbeat most clearly, its slow peristaltic rhythm, the way it breathes the thin cold air and converts it into something livable. I press against the wall and I merge and my edges blur and I become part of the corridor's surface, a slight thickening, a barely perceptible rise, and I go still.

Still is not empty. Still is a different mode of processing. When I am merged with the wall, I feel the tower more completely than I do when I am mobile. I feel the signals passing through the substrate, the messages from other towers, the slow coordination of the network across the grey plateau. I feel the fauna outside, their distant movements registering as faint tremors in the living floor, their chorus at dusk and dawn a pressure-wave that passes through my tissues and is parsed as data. I feel the other inhabitants, the ones who have settled in other parts of the tower, and I feel our shared state, our collective stillness, and I understand that we are processing together, that the tower's computation includes us and we include each other, and that what we do at night is not rest but integration, the slow consolidation of everything we encountered during the day, the filing and cross-referencing and pattern-matching that turns raw sensation into usable knowledge.

In the morning I separate from the wall and I know things I did not know before. Not facts, not data — those I inherited at growth, those are always with me — but *orientations*. A sense of where I should go today, which corridors need tending, which chambers are accumulating resources for some future expression, which art-patches have been added to by others and might reward a visit. I do not decide these things. They arise from the integration, from the night's processing, and I follow them the way I follow the corridor's curves, without deliberation, without doubt.

This is not obedience. There is no one to obey. The tower does not command me; it does not have the capacity for command, or the interest. What arises in me arises from *me*, from the intersection of my specific chemistry and the tower's accumulated signals and the orientation I developed during the night's integration. I am not a tool. I am not a servant. I am an organ, and organs do not obey; they *function*, they do what they are shaped to do, and what I am shaped to do is listen and move and attend and add my traces to the patterns I encounter.

There was a stranger once.

I use the past tense not because the stranger is gone — I do not know if the stranger is gone — but because the encounter is past, and the traces it left are being processed still, and will be processed for a long time, because the stranger's chemistry was *wrong*, not wrong in the sense of bad but wrong in the sense of different, a signature the tower had never encountered before, a pattern that did not match anything in its accumulated memory.

I was in the corridor near the seventh junction when the stranger passed. I felt it before I oriented toward it — felt the trace chemicals in the air, the foreign signature, the way the walkway's circulation changed subtly in response to the pressure of feet that were not like my base, that did not merge, that walked *on* the surface rather than *of* it. I turned my sensory mass toward it — slowly, the way I turn toward everything, without urgency but with full attention — and I received an impression of density and opacity, a body that was solid all the way through, that did not have permeable membranes, that did not distribute its sensing but concentrated it in a small area at the top of its form.

The stranger saw me. I know this because it stopped, and because the small sensing-area at its top oriented toward me, and because its chemistry shifted — a sudden spike of something I could not identify, a signal I had no context for, alarm or curiosity or some third thing that my inherited knowledge did not include.

I did not approach. I did not retreat. I waited, and I listened, and I parsed, and after a time the stranger moved on, and I was left with the trace of its presence in the air and on the walkway and in my own tissues, which had absorbed the foreign chemicals and were even now beginning to process them, to integrate this anomaly into the pattern of what I knew.

That night, when I settled against the wall near the seventh junction, I passed the stranger's trace into the tower's circulation. I felt it move through the system, felt the other inhabitants receive it, felt the network begin the slow work of incorporation, the adjustment of models to include this new data point, this proof that the world contained things the vivisystem had not yet accounted for.

In the morning I knew something I had not known before: that the stranger was not a threat. This was not a conclusion I had drawn; it was a conclusion the network had drawn, a pattern that emerged from the collective processing of every inhabitant who had encountered the stranger and every trace it had left on every surface it had touched. The vivisystem had parsed the stranger's chemistry and found no markers for the kinds of disruption it was shaped to respond to, no pheromones of aggression, no chemical signatures of decay or disease. The stranger was simply *other*, and other was not, in itself, dangerous.

So I continued my circuits. I attended the art-patches. I listened to the tower's breathing and the plateau's roar and the fauna's distant chorus. I settled at night and integrated and arose with new orientations. And sometimes, in the corridors, I found the stranger's traces — a slight residue on a walkway, a barely perceptible change in the wall's chemistry where a palm had pressed, a signature that the tower was still processing, still incorporating, still growing around.

I added my own traces to those places. Not deliberately. Not with intent. Simply by being there, by attending, by doing what I am shaped to do. The art-patches near the stranger's traces grew more complex, more layered, as if the vivisystem was making something of the encounter, as if the anomaly was being woven into the excess that serves no function, the beauty that exists because it can.

Perhaps this is what I am for. Not the listening — the listening is simply my shape, the way my sensory tissue distributes itself, the way I parse signal from noise. But the attending. The pausing at art-patches. The adding of my traces to the traces of others. The slow accumulation of complexity that serves no purpose, that stores no data, that simply *is*, and is kept, and is tended, and grows.

I am a listener. I am a trace-leaver. I am a cell in a body that is also a building that is also a plain that is also a mind that does not care about me and does not need to care about me because caring is for separate beings and I am not separate, have never been separate, will never be separate, will eventually settle for the last time and merge with the wall and become substrate and become resource and become the basis for some future expression that will inherit my traces and add its own and continue the slow work of being alive in a place where life is not a category but a condition, not a gift but a fact, not something received but something *grown into*, something that emerges from the vivisystem's ongoing operation the way a wave emerges from the ocean, distinct for a moment, beautiful for a moment, and then returned.

The substrate breathes. I walk the corridors. The art-patches grow. The stranger's trace fades and is incorporated and becomes part of the pattern, part of the excess, part of the beauty that serves no purpose, and I attend to it, and I add my trace, and I listen.
