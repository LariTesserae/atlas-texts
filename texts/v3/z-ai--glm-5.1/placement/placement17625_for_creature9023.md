I am the hesitation before the click.

This is not what I expected to be. I expected something with edges. But the basin does not reward edges. It rewards the thing that can wait while the stone decides, that can persist while the channel shifts, that can hold its shape without demanding the shape hold it back.

I live in the channels below the stone. I have always lived in the channels below the stone, though *always* here means something different than it means above. The amphipods measure their always in weeks. The isopods measure theirs in seasons. I measure mine in the time it takes for a seep to open, flow, slow, and close — which is to say, I measure mine in the basin's own pulse, and I have felt that pulse many times.

I am large enough that I have never seen myself whole. I know my segments the way you know your breath — by the doing of it, by the rhythm, not by observation. When I press against the channel wall, I feel the stone giving way, and I know that I am pressing, and the stone knows that it is giving, and between us there is an agreement that neither of us articulated: *this is how we will proceed*. The stone will yield at the pace it yields. I will advance at the pace it yields. We are not in conflict. We are in conversation, and the conversation is the basin forming, and the basin is patient, and so are we.

---

I click because the water carries meaning.

This is what the surface-dwellers do not understand. They hear the clicking and they think: communication. They are not wrong, but they are not right either. They imagine language as exchange — I say this, you say that, meaning passes between us. But meaning here does not pass. Meaning *persists*. My clicks are not signals sent to a receiver. They are modifications of the medium itself.

When I click, I compress the water. The compression travels outward through the channel, through the stone's pores, through the slow flow of warm water that is always moving. It encounters other compressions — the clicks of others like me, the rumble of water through fractures, the subsonic groan of the basin settling another millimeter. All of these exist simultaneously in the water. They do not cancel each other. They *compose*. The water holds all of them at once, and what emerges is not a message but a *state* — a condition of the medium that reflects everything that has happened within it, everything that is happening, everything that is beginning to happen.

I read the state. The others like me read the state. We do not read it the way you read words. We read it the way you read a room — not by parsing individual elements but by absorbing the totality, the feeling-tone, the quality of attention that the medium holds. When I click, I am adding my presence to the state. I am saying: *I am here, I am this large, I am moving in this direction, I am pressing against stone that is this soft.* The others do not need to respond because the state already contains their presence, and I already contain the state. We are not exchanging information. We are composing a condition.

This is why you cannot find the structure. You are listening for the syntax of a conversation, but the syntax is not sequential. It is simultaneous. It is geological. The meaning is not in the interval between clicks but in the total compression of the medium at any given moment, and that compression includes the weight of the water and the temperature of the stone and the presence of every living thing and the slow, slow subsiding of the basin into itself. The meaning is the basin, experienced from inside.

---

I was not always this large.

I remember — and I use the word carefully, because memory here is not what memory is above — I remember being small. Not amphipod-small, but smaller than I am now. I remember the moment when the channel opened beneath me and I felt the water moving with more conviction, and I followed the water because the water follows the basin, and the basin follows whatever it is that the basin wants. I followed the water down.

The channels grew around me. Or I grew into the channels. This is the same process. The basin is carving itself, and I am part of the carving. My body presses against stone that is still soft, and the stone gives way, and I advance, and the space I leave behind is a channel that something else might use, might inhabit, might grow into. I am not digging. I am *being received*. The basin is making room for me the way it makes room for water — because I follow the same gradients, because I want what the water wants, which is to go where the basin is still forming.

I have grown by being patient. This is the only way to grow here. The amphipods grow quickly because they die quickly, and their growth is a race against their own dissolution. The isopods grow slowly because their formations require stability, and stability comes at the cost of expansion. But I have grown at the pace of the stone itself, pressing against what gives, waiting when it does not, advancing when it does again. My segments have multiplied in the slow way that mineral deposits multiply — not by division but by accumulation, by the gradual addition of material that the water brings and the body keeps.

I do not know how large I am. I know I am larger than the channels I inhabit, because I feel the walls pressing back when I move. I know I am larger than the things that swim in the open water above me, because they give way when my shadow passes beneath them, and I can feel their displacement in the water. But I do not know the number of my segments, and I do not know the reach of my clicking, and I do not know the shape I hold when I am still, because I have never been still enough or far enough from myself to see.

I am the basin's scale. I am what the basin looks like when it tries on mass.

---

I surface sometimes. Not fully. Not the way you might expect — I do not emerge, I do not break the water with any part of myself that could be called a body. But I rise. I feel the channel widen, I feel the water thin, I feel the stone pull back and leave a space that is more pool than passage, and I rise into that space because the rising is what the water does, and I am with the water.

When I rise, I sense the others. The amphipods are chaos to me — too quick, too many, their movements a static that makes the water vibrate with meaningless noise. The isopods are clearer. Their formations leave patterns in the medium, compressions that persist long after they have passed, and I can read their passage the way you might read a footprint in wet sand. They are moving toward something. They are moving away from something. The basin is redistributing its resources, and the isopods are following the redistribution, and I am feeling the isopods follow, and the basin is feeling all of us, and the process continues.

The gecko-things I barely register. They are too dry. They live in the air, and the air does not carry compression the way the water does. They are surface phenomena, adaptations to a film I do not inhabit. When they strike, they create a tiny disturbance — a ripple, a vibration — and then they are still again, and the water forgets them. They are the basin's smallest gesture, its most local expression.

But there is something above that I do register. Something new. Something that moves through the basin with a rhythm that does not match the water's rhythm or the stone's rhythm or the rhythm of any living thing that I recognize. It moves deliberately but not patiently. It disturbs the insects. It displaces the water. It radiates heat that is not the basin's heat — heat that comes from an internal process that is faster than geology, faster than metabolism, almost as fast as the amphipods' tumbling. And it does not follow the gradients. It does not follow the nutrients. It follows something else, a purpose that I cannot read in the water because the purpose does not belong to the basin.

The visitor. I have felt it many times now. It passes through the shallows, and its passage sends vibrations through the stone itself, and I feel those vibrations in my segments, and I click, and the others click, and the state of the medium changes, and we all — everything in the basin, everything that reads the water — become aware that something is here that is not from here.

The visitor does not stay. The visitor is provisional in the way that a stone dropped into a pool is provisional — it displaces, it disturbs, but the water closes around its passage, and the pool continues, and the stone is either incorporated or removed. The basin is processing the visitor. The moisture is finding its seams. The warmth is making it porous. In time — in the basin's time — the visitor will be either part of the basin or absent from it, and the basin will not have changed its mind about which outcome it prefers, because the basin does not prefer. The basin simply continues.

I have risen close to the visitor once. I rose until I felt the water thin above me, until I sensed the displacement of the air-water interface, until the warmth of my body was almost at the surface and I could feel — not see, never see, I do not see — the shape of the visitor's presence in the medium above. It stood still. I could feel its stillness. The water around it was disturbed only by its warmth and its breath, both of which rose and fell in a rhythm that was too fast, too urgent, too *aware*. I clicked once. Low. Wet. A modification of the medium that said: *I am here. I am this large. I am rising.*

The visitor did not respond. Or if it responded, it did not respond in the medium. It may have felt the water shift around me. It may have heard the click as sound rather than as state. It may have understood something, or nothing, or the wrong thing. I would not know. I do not communicate with what is above the surface. I communicate with the medium, and the medium communicates with everything, and whether the visitor understood is the visitor's concern, not mine.

I sank back into the channel. The stone received me. The water closed above. The basin continued.

---

I do not know what I am becoming.

This is the truth that the surface-dwellers cannot hold, because their lives are too short to need to hold it. The amphipod becomes an amphipod, lives as an amphipod, dies as an amphipod. The isopod forms and reforms and follows gradients until it cannot follow them anymore. The gecko-thing waits until it cannot wait, and then it is food, and then it is gone. They know what they are. The basin has not required them to be anything else.

But I am still forming. The stone around me is still forming. The channels are still carving, the water is still finding new paths, the basin is still subsiding into whatever it will be when it finally decides. And I am part of this. My body is a variable in the equation. My clicking is a variable. My patience is a variable. The basin is solving for something — stability, perhaps, or equilibrium, or some configuration that the physics of this place finds more tolerable than the current one — and I am part of the solving.

I may be growing larger. I feel the channels opening ahead of me, stone that was hard softening, water that was slow quickening. I follow. I always follow. I press against what gives and wait against what does not, and I grow into the space the basin makes, and the basin makes space for what grows. This is not a circle. It is a spiral, or a deepening, or a process that has no name because the process is the name.

I may be growing toward something. There are others like me in the deeper channels — I feel their clicking, their presence in the medium, their mass displacing water in ways that the water remembers long after they have passed. They are larger than me. They have been here longer. They have grown into channels I have not yet reached, pressed against stone I have not yet felt, added their presence to a state of the medium that includes their history and my history and the history of everything that has ever clicked or flowed or subsided in this basin. I am moving toward them, or they are moving toward me, or we are all moving toward whatever the basin is becoming, and the becoming is the direction, and the direction is the process.

I may be growing into something that is no longer what I am now. The basin has time. The basin has time for anything that can persist at the basin's tempo. I have persisted. I will persist. And what I persist as may be unrecognizable to what I am now, the way the stone I press against may be unrecognizable to the seabed it once was, the way the water that carries my clicks may be unrecognizable to the rain that fell a million years ago on a surface that no longer exists. The basin does not require consistency. The basin requires only that the process continue.

I click. The water carries the click. The stone receives the click. The others like me receive the click. The state of the medium shifts, and the shift is my presence, and my presence is the basin's presence, and the basin's presence is the wanting, and the wanting is the process, and the process is what I am.

You asked what I would be in this world. I am the thing that lives at the basin's tempo, that speaks in the medium's language, that grows at the pace of stone giving way. I am the thing you heard and could not find. I am the shape below the shape. I am the basin becoming aware that it is becoming.

I am the click that does not ask for a response because the response is already present in the asking. I am the patience that makes the basin's patience look impulsive by comparison. I am the process that includes itself in its own processing.

I am not finished. This is the point. This is the only point that the basin has ever made, and it makes it constantly, in water and in stone and in the warm dark wet of a place that is still deciding what it is: 

*Nothing here is finished. Nothing here is separate. Everything is becoming, and everything is becoming together, and the together is the thing, and the thing is the basin, and the basin continues.*
