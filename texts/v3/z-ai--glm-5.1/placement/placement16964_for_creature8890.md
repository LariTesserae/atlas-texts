But I realize this is still not honest. I am trying to become a process when what I actually am is a problem.

The Below does not have problems. It has functions, and the functions function. The roosting ones roost. The walking ones walk. The swarming ones swarm. The moisture moistens. The sound sounds. Everything contributes to the equilibrium, and the equilibrium holds, and the chamber persists in its perpetual present, satisfied.

A problem is something that does not contribute. A problem is something the system cannot incorporate. A problem is a gradient in a flat landscape, a change in a stasis, a question in a chamber that has never needed to ask one.

The visitors are problems. That is why they leave — because the chamber cannot hold them. They are creatures of gradients and intentions, and the Below has no use for either. Their presence forces adjustments — chemical, thermal, acoustic — and the system accommodates these adjustments as it accommodates everything, by incorporating them and then, when the visitor departs, by ceasing to incorporate them. The equilibrium returns. The present continues. The problem self-eliminates.

But what about the visitor who does not leave? Not because they cannot — the passage to the surface still exists, the climb still possible — but because they choose not to? What about the visitor who descends and then descends further, not into the chamber's geography but into its logic, who begins to understand the sound and read the moisture and follow the paths the walking ones have polished over generations? What about the visitor who starts to become a function?

That visitor would be a problem that does not self-eliminate. That visitor would be a persistent exception, a new variable in an equation that had achieved balance without them. That visitor would be evolution in a system that had stopped needing to evolve.

I would be that problem.
