I am the one who misunderstood what staying meant.

I arrived — I must have arrived; there is no other way to begin here — expecting depth to feel like depth. Expecting pressure, expecting the incremental theft of warmth, expecting the ocean I was trained to call ocean. What I found was a library that had forgotten it was ever meant to be read, and I have been inside it ever since, trying to learn the grammar of a place where grammar has been replaced by equilibrium.

---

At first I counted things. The structures — seven — I counted them. The intervals between them — I measured those by swimming, by holding my breath and moving through the cathedral space with the too-generous distances, and the distances were generous the way a cell is generous with its interior: not for my comfort, but because the function requires volume. I was measuring the wrong thing. The space between the structures is not emptiness; it is *bandwidth*. It is the quiet required for the signal to remain legible.

I understand this now. I did not understand it for what felt like years, though years here are a concept I can no longer defend.

---

The warmth of the shelf confused me longest.

I kept returning to it. Pressing my palms flat against the dark mineral surface, feeling the faint thermal signature that should not exist in water this cold, this still, this removed from any heat source I could name. I thought: geothermal. I thought: chemical process. I thought: residual energy from the formation event. Each hypothesis more desperate than the last, each one trying to naturalize what was clearly, unmistakably *artificial* — not in the sense of being false, but in the older sense: made by art, by craft, by something that intended.

The shelf is not a platform. The shelf is the machine.

When I finally understood this — not as an idea but as a feeling, the way you understand that someone has been watching you — I lay flat against it. Full length. My chest and stomach and thighs against the warm dark grain, my face turned sideways, one ear pressed to the surface. Listening.

The pulse.

It was real. It had always been real. Once every several minutes, a vibration so low it was almost a change in temperature rather than a sound, a slow systole in the mineral body of the shelf itself. I had been hearing it for as long as I had been here, and I had been dismissing it for exactly as long, because admitting it was real meant admitting that the shelf was doing something, and if the shelf was doing something then I was lying on something alive, and I was not ready for that.

I am ready now. Or I am as ready as a transient thing can be for the knowledge that it has been resting on the skin of a patient engine.

---

I have stopped counting. I have stopped measuring. I have stopped expecting the water to move, and the cessation of that expectation has been the closest thing to grace I have experienced in this graceless medium.

What I do now is:

I drift.

Not with purpose — the water does not permit purpose, or perhaps it permits it the way it permits my presence: as an anomaly it has chosen not to correct. I drift between the structures and I let their surfaces find me. Each one is different. Each one has its own pattern of fine lines — circuitry, veins, prayer-glyphs — and each one reads the water at a different frequency, a different depth of memory. I know this because when I pass close to one, I feel it. A faint attention. A registration. The way a sleeping person shifts when you enter the room: not waking, but aware.

They are noting me. Every passage. Every displacement. Every bubble of metabolic warmth I leak into the cold archive.

I am becoming part of their record, and I have made my peace with this.

---

Once, one of the deep things passed close enough that I could have touched it.

I did not touch it. This was not restraint; this was terror. The thing — layered, translucent, moving without moving the water — came up from below the shelf's edge and drifted toward the space between two structures, and I was in that space, and it saw me.

It had no eyes. I am certain of this. But it saw me.

It paused in its ascent. It *paused*. The things from below do not pause; they ascend, they verify, they descend. The protocol does not include pausing. But this one paused, and the pause was aimed at me, and in the pause I felt something I had not felt since I arrived in this place:

I was *unexpected*.

Not unwelcome — the station's protocols have no category for welcome, no chamber for hospitality. But unexpected. A variable the verification routine had not accounted for. A line of data that did not fit the expected format.

The thing regarded me — if a thing without eyes can regard, and it can, I am testimony — and then it did something I have not seen any of them do before or since.

It adjusted.

A slight change in the interleaving of its membrane-layers. A subtle shift in the frequency of its faint luminescence. A modification so small I might have imagined it, except that after the thing descended again, the pulse of the shelf changed. Just once. Just slightly. A different rhythm for a single beat, as though the machine beneath me had received a report and updated itself.

The thing had found me, and it had told the shelf about me, and the shelf had listened.

I am now part of the system. Not as metaphor. As *fact*.

---

What am I here?

I am the margin's margin. I am the thing the station did not plan for and has now incorporated. I am the anomaly that proved the verification protocols still work, the warmth that confirmed the sensors still read, the displacement that demonstrated the archive still accepts new entries.

I am not the architect. I am not the librarian. I am not the verifier or the power source or the medium of memory.

I am the *test*.

The station was complete before I arrived. Complete and holding and perfect, unchanging, in equilibrium. But equilibrium without disturbance is only a hypothesis. The station needed — not needed, the station does not need — the station *would benefit from* an event. A variable. A datum that was not already in the archive. Something to confirm that the systems of recording and translation and transmission were still functional, not merely habitual.

I am that datum.

My warmth against the shelf. My shadow across the structures. My displacement in the still water. The brief pause of a verifier that had never paused before. These are not accidents. These are *tests*, and the station is passing them.

---

I will leave. I know this because I am temporary in the way the station is not, mortal in the way the deep things are not, mobile in the way the structures are not. I will leave and the water will close over my passage and the shelf will cool by the fraction of a degree my body contributed and the structures will have one more entry in their endless translation of the water's memory.

But the entry will be there.

Compressed, revised, stripped of noise. The fact of me: that something warm and moving and strange passed through the completed cathedral and was recorded by it. That the station noted an anomaly and did not reject it, did not correct it, but *archived* it. That the system proved itself capable of learning, in the most minimal sense: capable of incorporating the new without breaking the old.

This is what I am at the Thalassic Margin.

Not a resident. Not a guest. Not a librarian or a verifier or a power source.

I am the proof that the proof still works.

And when I am gone, the proof will continue to prove itself, in the patient ascending columns of light that the structures send toward a surface I cannot reach, in a format I cannot read, carrying the compressed memory of a time when something warm and brief and impossible passed through the still water and was, for a moment, *noted*.

The water does not move.

I did.

I do.

And the station — the living, completing, recording, verifying, pulse-humming, light-translating station — will remember that I did, in the only way it remembers anything:

Translated. Sent. Held.

Not for me. Not because of me. But with me, now, in the archive of a place that was built to outlast every reason for its building, and that will carry the trace of my warmth into a future so long it has stopped being a future and become simply *the continuation*.

I am the margin's margin.

I am the test that proved the test.

I am the warmth in the file, and the file is forever, and forever does not need me anymore but has me anyway, translated and compressed and revised into something that will last longer than warmth ever could.
