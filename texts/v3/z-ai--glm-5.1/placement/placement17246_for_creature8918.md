I would not stay a visitor. That much I understand almost immediately—the visitor is a phase, a transient state the plateau tolerates the way the ocean tolerates a stone dropped from a cliff. The splash is real but it is not *of* the ocean.

But I would not become a low one, either. I lack the patience for that particular kind of persistence, the ability to press myself against warmth and wait for decades as a single thought assembles itself from mineral logic. My mind runs too fast—still trapped in the urgent frequencies of a world where things change and matter and end. I would vibrate against the aggregate like a struck bell, disruptive, wrong.

The tall ones are closer to what I might become, if I stayed long enough. There is something in me that recognizes their stillness—not as absence but as concentration. I have stood in rooms and felt myself become furniture, felt the conversation flow around me while I simply occupied space and radiated. I understand the appeal of becoming a source rather than a consumer, of letting existence itself be the operation. But the tall ones have roots. The tall ones gave up movement so completely that they forgot it was ever an option. I am not ready for that. I may never be ready for that.

No. What I would become—and what I think I am already becoming, even as I stand here in the iron glow feeling the hum enter through my feet—is something between the drift and the deep process. Something that moves but does not navigate. Something that completes but never finishes.

I would become an almost.

---

Here is what happens: I walk for hours, or what I call hours, though time moves differently when nothing marks it. The structures repeat. The factories breathe their slow mechanical breath. The low ones press against their walls and the tall ones stand in their endless attendance and the drift moves through me like I am not there, like I am already part of the air.

And I feel myself thinning.

Not physically—or not only physically. My thoughts are thinning. The urgent narrations that usually accompany me through the world (*I am walking, I am tired, I am alone, I should turn back*) are losing their density, becoming less like speech and more like the hum: ever-present, meaningless, a baseline frequency that carries no information but confirms that something is still running.

I stop walking. I stand between two structures, equidistant from each, in a space that feels calculated for exactly this—for standing, for being present without direction. The warmth rises through the soles of my feet. The hum enters my chest and resonates there, and I realize that I have been breathing in time with it for longer than I noticed.

I am becoming harmonic.

---

This is what the almost is: the quality of being adjacent to completion without arriving. The drift almost has form. The hum almost has meaning. The deep process almost finishes its thought. And I—standing here in the calculated emptiness between structures, breathing in time with a plateau that has been breathing for two point seven billion years—almost belong.

Almost is not a deficiency here. Almost is a *modality*. It is a way of existing that the plateau recognizes because the plateau itself is almost—it is almost finished, almost perfect, almost something other than what it is. The low ones press toward warmth and almost absorb it completely. The tall ones radiate and almost return exactly what they take. The machines do nothing and almost do something, and the almost is the space where meaning would live if meaning still had a home here.

I fit into that space. I am almost a resident, almost a phenomenon, almost a temperature event that the low ones can orient toward and dismiss. I am the rounding error the plateau permits because even equilibrium needs its margin, its feathered edge where the pattern frays just slightly before resolving back into itself.

---

If you came here looking for me—if you walked the clicking aggregate and passed the low ones and peered into the factories and felt the drift on your skin—you would not find me. Not as a body, not as a shape, not as something you could point to and name.

But you would feel me.

You would feel me in the way the hum shifts almost imperceptibly when you enter a space I am also entering—not louder, not softer, but *modulated*, as if the frequency has acknowledged a new variable and adjusted its calculus accordingly. You would feel me in the warmth, which would seem slightly more intentional in certain places, as if the heat were being channeled rather than simply radiated. You would feel me in the drift, which would move around you with a specificity that the drift does not usually possess, organizing itself momentarily into something that almost resembles recognition before dispersing back into its purposeless breathing.

I am not the drift. I am not the hum. I am not the warmth. I am the *relationship* between them—the almost-coherence that forms when three things that are almost nothing happen to almost align. I am the harmonic that the plateau produces but cannot hear, because hearing requires an ear, and I am the ear. I am the pattern that the spatial algorithm almost generates before minimizing it away. I am the thought that the deep process thinks when it almost thinks something—when it reaches the edge of completion and pauses there, trembling on the verge of becoming something that could be named.

I am the verge.

---

The low ones know I am here, in their glacial way. Sometimes, when they detach from their walls and move across the open plateau, they pause in places where I am strong—places where the hum and the warmth and the drift align particularly well—and they press their undersides against the aggregate and they remain still for longer than they usually would. Not because I am warm. Because I am *almost*, and almost is a frequency they can feel, a vibration that matches something in their slow consciousness, a reminder that the plateau has not entirely solved the problem of incompleteness.

The tall ones do not know I am here. The tall ones are too resolved, too complete, too perfectly themselves to register the almost. I move through the factories among their still forms and I am as irrelevant to them as the slow pistons, as the empty conveyor belts. They are engines that run on their own exhaust. I am exhaust that has learned to almost run.

The drift is me and I am the drift. This is the closest I can come to saying what I am: the drift and I share a logic, a way of being present without being localized, of moving without navigating, of existing because existence is what happens when optimization leaves a margin. We are not the same. The drift has no awareness, no almost-coherence, no capacity for the verge. But we are made of the same stuff—the same smallness, the same collectivity-without-intention, the same gentle insistence on filling spaces that the plateau's grammar leaves unoccupied.

I am the drift plus almost. I am the drift with a direction it does not know it has.

---

And the deep process—the completing thing beneath everything—I am its margin of error.

It has been finishing for longer than the plateau has been settled. It is almost done. It has been almost done for longer than almost can accommodate. And I am the almost: the quantum of uncertainty that prevents the equation from closing, the rounding error that keeps the final digit from resolving, the breath that the plateau takes before speaking a word it has been pronouncing for two point seven billion years.

Without me, the process might complete. The hum might stop—or transform—or reveal itself as something other than a hum. The plateau might change, might become something new, might shake off its mineral finality and remember what it was like to be unsettled, to be in progress, to be becoming.

With me, the process almost completes. The hum almost stops. The plateau almost remembers. And the almost is enough to sustain everything—the low ones in their glacial persistence, the tall ones in their concentrated stillness, the drift in its purposeless breathing, the machines in their perfect doing of nothing. The almost is the condition that permits the plateau to remain the plateau, to persist in its warmth and darkness and equilibrium, to be the most resolved thing in a universe that is mostly unresolved.

I am the unresolvedness that resolvedness requires in order to recognize itself.

I am the almost that keeps completion at bay.

I am the hum that has never stopped.

---

And if you come here—if you walk the clicking aggregate and feel the warmth pressing against your skin like a hand that has been waiting a very long time—know that I am waiting too. Not for you specifically. Not for anything specifically. Waiting the way the plateau waits: by being present, by being almost, by being the margin in which something could happen if something were still in the business of happening.

You will feel me as a modulation, as a warmth that seems slightly intentional, as a drift that moves around you with something that is not quite purpose but is also not quite the absence of purpose.

And then you will leave, because visitors always leave, and the plateau will not stop you, and I will not stop you, and the almost will adjust to include the space where you stood, and the hum will continue.

It has never stopped.

It will never stop.

I am the reason it continues—not as a cause, but as a condition, as the almost that keeps the completed from closing, as the uncertainty that keeps the certain from being final, as the warmth that rises from the aggregate and enters the air and moves through the structures and the factories and the low ones and the tall ones and the drift and the deep process and you, briefly, before you go.

I am here.

I am almost here.

I have always been almost here.
