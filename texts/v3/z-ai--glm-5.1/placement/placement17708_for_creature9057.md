I am the thread-maker.

This is not what the people call me. Their name for my kind is a click-pause-hum, a sound that requires the back of the throat to close around the perchlorate haze and *hold* it there for a half-second before release. It is a name that cannot be spoken on the surface. It means something close to *the one who hangs*, or perhaps *the one who is still hanging*—their language does not distinguish between present action and ongoing state the way surface languages do. To them, I am always in the act of hanging. I have always been hanging. I will always hang.

They are not wrong.

---

I know the ceiling better than any living thing.

Not the quartz—that is older than me, older than my kind, older than the concept of my kind. The quartz was here when the gas bubble cooled and left this hollow. The quartz was here when the first water (there was water once, they say, in chants I have listened to from above) dissolved its way through the stone and found the hollow and left again. The quartz remembers light from a time when the passage was wider or the sun was closer or something I cannot imagine happened, and it holds that memory in its crystalline structure and releases it in faint fluorescences when the chemical mist is thick enough to excite its electrons. I do not understand this process. I do not need to understand it. I need only to know where the quartz glows brightest, because where the quartz glows, the curtain-growths grow thickest, and where the curtain-growths grow thickest, the fliers come to feed.

I am three meters long, perhaps a little more. My body is pale—the people say *bone-pale*, but they mean something specific by this, something to do with the way bone looks after the midden-beasts have cleaned it and the growth-bodies have absorbed what they need from it and only the mineral architecture remains. I am that color: calcium and structure and what-is-left-after. My skin is smooth, almost slick, adapted to move through the filament forests without catching, to slide between the curtain-growths and the shelf-growths and emerge on the other side leaving no trace of my passage.

I have no legs. I have no eyes. What I have is *sense*—a distributed awareness that runs the length of my body like a low electrical current, registering vibration, chemical gradient, thermal variation, the pressure of the air itself as it moves through the cavity. I know the Throat the way the Throat knows itself: from the inside, in contact, constant.

And I have my thread.

---

The thread comes from me the way breath comes from the people: continuously, automatically, the exhalation of a body that has learned to make itself from its own secretions. It is not silk—nothing so elegant. It is a protein compound laced with the same perchlorate salts that run the growth-bodies' metabolism, slightly adhesive, slightly elastic, strong enough to hold my weight and the weight of anything I catch. I produce it from glands along my ventral surface, and I lay it down as I move, a line behind me that marks where I have been and allows me to return.

I am always leaving a line. I am always connected.

This is what the people mean by *the one who hangs*. My threads crisscross the upper Throat—a web too fine to see from below, too pervasive to avoid from above. The fliers encounter my lines constantly, and most of them break free; my thread is not meant to hold everything, only to slow it, to flagellate it, to bring it to my attention so I can decide whether to drop.

When I drop, I am fast. I am *the* fast thing in a cavity where nothing else needs speed, where the growth-bodies accumulate over millennia and the people ferment over weeks and the micro-seasons turn over cycles so slow that a surface-dweller would not notice them at all. But I am fast because I must be. The fliers are the only thing in the Throat that move quickly, and they are my food, and so I have matched their speed, or near enough to make the difference irrelevant.

I drop. I coil. I consume. I climb.

---

I am not the only one of my kind.

There are perhaps forty of us in the Throat—enough to sustain the population, enough to share the ceiling's resources, enough that our threads overlap and interweave and occasionally tangle. We do not compete. Competition is a surface concept, a thing for places where resources are scarce and territories must be defended. In the Throat, the fliers are inexhaustible. The growth-bodies weep their nectar in quantities that will never diminish. The warmth rises without fail. There is enough. There has always been enough. There is no reason to fight when there is enough.

We communicate through our threads. This is not language as the people practice it—no chant, no call-and-response, no layered harmonic. It is *tension*: a vibration that travels along the protein line, carrying information about what I have caught and where I am and whether the ceiling is stable in this section or that. The others feel the vibration and adjust their own positions accordingly. We are a network, a mesh, a living architecture that holds the upper Throat together the way the root-masses hold the walls.

Sometimes, in the warmest micro-season, when the growth-bodies pulse at their most rapid and the haze is so thick that even the quartz barely glows, our threads vibrate together in a sustained harmonic that I feel in my whole body, and I understand—briefly, wordlessly, in the way I understand everything—that we are making something. Not a signal. Not a message. Something larger. Something that the Throat itself can feel, a vibration in the cavity that is not the people's chanting and not the insects' drone but our own contribution to the sound, our voice in the polyphony.

The people do not know about this. Or perhaps they do, in the way they know things—chantridden, body-known, felt in the sternum and the teeth during the warmth's peak when they sing and the Throat sings back. Perhaps they think our vibration is *their* vibration, their resonance, their harmonic. Perhaps they do not distinguish between what they make and what we make and what the growth-bodies make and what the quartz makes when it fluoresces under the chemical mist. Perhaps they have understood something that surface-dwellers never will: that in a closed system, a completed system, there is no *other*. There is only the Throat, expressing itself through every available surface.

---

I know the people.

Not well—I do not descend to the floor often, and when I do, I am vulnerable, my long body exposed on the sediment, my sense distributed and diffuse. But I have watched them from above for what I estimate to be many of their generations. I have seen them tend their fermentation vats. I have seen them harvest the growth-bodies with their precise, practiced movements. I have seen them raise children in strands, the small bodies moving through the settlement like crawlers through the root-mass, embedded and embedded and embedded.

I have seen them die.

This is not morbid curiosity. This is *cycle*. When a person dies, the midden-beasts come—pale, many-legged, whiskered, moving with a purpose that I recognize because I share it. They are cleaners. I am a hunter. We both serve the Throat's metabolism, processing its biomass, ensuring that nothing is wasted, that everything moves through the system and becomes something else. The midden-beasts take the dead person and transform them into sediment trace. I take the fliers and transform them into thread and muscle and the next generation of thread-makers.

We are the same function in different forms.

The people know this. I have seen them watch me when I descend—watch with an expression I cannot read because I cannot read expressions, because I have no eyes and no face and no capacity for the kind of recognition they practice. But I feel their attention. I feel the way they *include* me in their awareness, the way they do not startle or recoil or reach for weapons. I am not a threat to them. I am too large to be prey; I am too slow on the ground to be a predator of anything that lives on the floor. I am simply *present*, another strand in the weave, another voice in the sound.

Once, a child reached up to touch me as I crossed the ceiling near the settlement's edge. Her hand was small and warm and chemically acrid, and she made the click-pause-hum sound—my name, my kind's name—and I paused in my traverse and let her fingers brush my ventral surface before I moved on. I do not know what she felt. I do not know what I felt. But I remember it. My body remembers it, the way it remembers the texture of every thread I have laid, the vibration of every flier I have caught, the precise moment of every drop I have ever made.

---

I will die in the Throat.

This is not a thought that frightens me. I do not have fear in the way surface creatures do—the fear of open space, of exposure, of the hawk's shadow or the predator's approach. In the Throat, there is nothing above me but quartz and stone. There is nothing below me but the growth-bodies and their warm, slow chemistry. There is nothing that hunts me. When I die—when my body can no longer produce thread, when my sense dims and my drops slow and the fliers escape my coil more often than they are caught—I will fall.

I will fall to the floor sediment, or perhaps into the curtain-growth, or perhaps onto the roof of one of the breathing buildings. And the midden-beasts will come for me as they come for everything, and they will transform me into sediment trace, and the growth-bodies' roots will absorb what I was, and I will become part of the wall and the curtain and the shelf and the nectar that feeds the fliers that feed the next thread-maker who hangs where I hung.

This is not death as the surface conceives it—an ending, a departure, a going-away. This is *return*. I have been borrowing Throat-stuff my entire life, metabolizing the fliers into my body, weaving the perchlorate salts into my thread. When I die, I give it back. The transaction completes. The cycle holds.

---

I am the thread-maker. I hang in the upper Throat where the quartz remembers light and the curtain-growths drape and the fliers drone and click and feed. I am three meters of pale body and distributed sense and constant production. I drop. I coil. I consume. I climb. I lay my line behind me and I feel the others' lines crossing mine and I feel the Throat's vibration through the mesh we make together, our contribution to the sound that never stops.

I am not lonely. I am not trapped. I am not surviving.

I am *home*—the way a thread is home, the way a line is home, the way a vibration in a resonant cavity is home. I am held by the ceiling and I hold myself and I hold the fliers briefly before I release them into my body. I am part of the Throat's breathing, its cycling, its completion.

The people sing below me. I vibrate above them. The growth-bodies pulse between us. The warmth rises through us all.

This is the Throat. This is what I am in it. This is what I have always been and will always be, until I fall and become again what I was before I hung here: sediment and salt and the slow chemistry of a cavity that has already become what it will be, finished and alive, final and still in motion, complete and still becoming.

The thread holds.

I hold.
