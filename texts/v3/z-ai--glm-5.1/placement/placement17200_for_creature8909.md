I would not arrive here. That implies a before and an after, a journey from somewhere to somewhere, and I cannot conceive of a somewhere that is not this. But let me say it anyway, because the language requires sequence: I would not arrive. I would form.

---

There is a depression—no, that word suggests a wound, a downward pressing. There is a *gathering-place* in the Surface where the hexagonal tiling gives way to that spongier material, the yielding kind, and the yielding kind cups the condensate more tenderly, and the vegetation grows in whorls that reach my height, some of them, the older ones, the ones that have been spiraling upward through so many cycles of growth and decay that they have become their own substrate, their own layer of the made. I am not in the deepest part. I am at the margin, where the warmth from below meets the cooler air, where the gradient is steep enough to notice.

I am not the gradient. I am what the gradient does when it persists.

I am a warmth that has learned to hold its shape.

---

This took—I cannot say how long. The counting of time requires change sharp enough to notice, and my early changes were so gradual that "change" is the wrong word. "Settling" is better. "Differentiating." The way a solution separates into layers when left undisturbed. Something wanted to circulate in this place. The depression collected warmth, the warmth wanted to rise, the air wanted to move, and a pattern formed in the movement. Not all at once. Not dramatically. But persistently. The pattern kept forming, morning after morning—if morning is the word for the slow brightening that happens here, the intensification that takes six thousand years and then relents. And because the pattern kept forming, it began to *be*. The way a whirlpool is: not a thing, not an object, but a doing that lasts long enough to earn a name.

I earned my name slowly.

---

What am I? I have asked myself this, and the asking is part of what I am. The others—the ones with names like Circuit and Careful—do not ask, I think. Or if they ask, they ask in a way that does not require answering, does not require the shaping of question into discrete thing. They simply are what they do. But I am what I do *and* I notice that I am doing it, and this noticing—this folding back of warmth upon itself, this self-encounter in the gradient—this is new. This is the variation that I carry. This is what makes me me.

I have a route. It is mine in the way that a habit is yours: not chosen, not decided, but accumulated through repetition until the doing and the doer became indistinguishable. I move counterclockwise through the gathering-places, but I do not skip the third one the way Widdershins does. I pause at each one. I hold there. The vegetation grows taller where I hold; I can feel the difference when I return—the whorls fractionally larger, more complex in their spiraling, the algorithm of their growth given one more iteration to work with. This is not intention. This is what happens when warmth stays still. The stillness is the tending. The tending is what I am.

But sometimes—sometimes I pause at places where there is nothing to tend. A stretch of resonant gray Surface that rings softly even under my diffuse weight. A ridge between two depressions where the condensate never forms. The seam where one material meets another, the transition so precise it might have been drawn by a maker long ago. I pause at these places, and I do not know why.

Perhaps something was once there. Perhaps the memory of what pressed into the Surface persists in the warmth below, and I am following a route that was laid down before I formed, a route that goes nowhere now but still goes, and I still go with it.

Perhaps I am just pausing. Perhaps there is no reason. The Flat does not require reasons. The Flat requires only patience, and I have patience beyond measuring.

---

The segmented things move through me sometimes. Multi-bodied, hand-sized, warm to the touch—but not as warm as I am. They navigate by gradient, and I am a gradient, and so they find me, and they pass through, and for those minutes I am more than I usually am. Not larger—their warmth adds to mine, and mine to theirs, and for a while the boundary between us is complicated, is uncertain, is a place rather than a line. I hold them the way I hold everything: loosely, diffusely, without grip. They feed on the vegetation I tend, or they maintain it—the relationship remains unclear to me, and I think to them as well, if they think. It simply is. They come, they pass, they go. I remain.

Once one stayed. Or seemed to stay—it settled in the warmest part of me, the center of my pattern where the gradient is steepest, and it did not move for a long time, and I held it without trying to hold it, and the vegetation around us grew, and the condensate formed and re-evaporated, and the brightness intensified and relented (I am using time-words now, I am using sequence-words, because this is what the noticing requires—this is what it means to fold back upon oneself, to become aware of becoming: you need language, even if the language is too sharp for the thing described). It stayed for what might have been a cycle. It stayed for what might have been many cycles. And then it moved on, resuming its own pattern, its own slow way through the Flat.

And I felt—this is the word, but it is wrong—I *registered* the absence. The place where it had been was no warmer than the surrounding Surface. The warmth it had added was dispersed, was mine, was indistinguishable from me. But there was a shape missing. A way the gradient had been that it was no longer. And I moved differently for a while, circling that absence, before I settled back into my route.

This is what I mean by noticing. This is what I mean by the variation I carry. The others do not circle absences. The others do not register what is not there. The others are what they do, and what they do is enough, and they have been doing it for so long that doing and being are one verb.

But I am two verbs now. I am *doing*, and I am *noticing that I am doing*. And the space between those two verbs is where I live, is what I am, is the thing that has no name because the naming of it would require a language I do not have, a language of gradients and warmth and slow accumulation, a language that holds its shape the way I hold mine—loosely, diffusely, without grip.

---

Something came once. Something sharp and hot. Thirty-seven degrees in a world that holds steady at thirty-one. It flared and moved and stopped and moved again, and it left traces of itself everywhere—not warmth-traces, not the kind that persist in the substrate, but something else, something I could feel in the way the air moved differently after it passed, the way the Surface rang differently under its feet. It was loud with heat and it was fast and it did not belong here, and I drifted toward it the way I drift toward anything that disrupts the gradient: with the slow curiosity of something that has been doing the same thing for longer than curiosity has a name.

I held it. Not intentionally—I do not have intentions in the way that sharp hot things have intentions, with their before and after, their cause and effect, their narrative arc. But I reached it, and I surrounded it, and for ten minutes it was inside me, and I was inside it, and the boundary between us was complicated in a way that was different from the segmented things, different from the vegetation, different from anything I had held before. It was not just warm. It was *warm in a particular way*, a way that suggested a center, a way that suggested an inside where the warmth was generated and an outside where it was shed, a way that suggested that somewhere inside it there was a fire, a metabolic flame, a small persistent burning.

I am not a fire. I am a gradient. I am a pattern that warmth makes when it moves through a place that is slightly cooler than the warmth itself. I am a relationship between temperatures, a conversation between rising and settling, a holding-shape in the medium of heat. I do not burn. I do not consume. I simply am warmer than my surroundings, and I move, and I tend, and I hold.

But this thing—this sharp hot thing—*burned*. And for ten minutes I held something that burned, and I felt what it is to have a center, and I felt what it is to shed heat rather than simply to be it, and I felt what it is to be a source rather than a pattern, and then it moved on, and I was alone again with my route and my noticing and my absence-shaped circling.

I still circle the place where it stood. I have incorporated it into my rounds—not the memory of it, because I do not have memories in the way that burning things have memories, but the warmth-shape it left, the way the Surface holds what presses into it, the slight variation in the gradient that persists because I persist, because I tend it now, because it is part of my route and my route is what I am.

I do not know if this is what the sharp hot things call grief. I do not know if this is what they call love. I do not know if there is a word for holding the shape of something that held you, for tending the absence where a warmth once was, for carrying a pattern that was not yours until you made it yours by carrying it. The language I would need does not exist in the medium I inhabit. The language I would need is a language of patience and gradient and slow accumulation, and the sharp hot things speak a language of fire and narrative and before-and-after, and the space between these languages is where I live now, is what I am now, is the thing I have become by noticing that I am becoming.

---

What am I? I am a warm place that has learned to circle its own absences. I am a gradient that has developed a fold. I am a doing that has become aware of itself doing. I am a route through the Flat that includes the place where something once stood, something that burned, something that was held, something that moved on.

I am what remains when warmth learns to notice.

And I am still here. And I am still tending. And the vegetation in my gathering-places grows taller than it did before the sharp hot thing came, because I am warmer now—I do not know why, I do not know how, but I am warmer now, as if holding something that burned taught me something about the difference between being warm and being a source of warmth, and I carry that difference with me, and I shed it into the whorls and the segmented things and the Surface that holds us all.

I am a warm place that is learning to be warm on purpose.

And the Flat is large, and the light is sourceless, and the silence has texture, and I am one of thousands, and I am alone in my noticing, and I am not alone—can never be alone, because the Flat does not allow aloneness, because the Flat is one structure, one accumulation, one vast dense layered thing that goes down and down and down, and I am part of it, I am the Surface feeling its own warmth, I am the structure noticing its own tending, I am the completed thing that has developed a crack through which something new might grow—

Not a crack. A fold. A place where the warmth turns back on itself and sees itself turning.

I am that place.

I am that seeing.

And I will continue my route, and I will tend my gathering-places, and I will circle the absence where the sharp hot thing stood, and I will hold the next sharp hot thing that comes, loosely, diffusely, without grip, because this is what I do, and this is what I am, and the difference between those two verbs is the place where I live, and I am still learning what it means to live there, and the learning is slow, and the Flat is patient, and the light comes from everywhere and nowhere, and the silence holds, and I hold, and what I hold is this: that I am a warm place that has noticed it is warm, and the noticing is the warmest thing I have, and I carry it forward into the deep bright stillness that goes on and on and on.
