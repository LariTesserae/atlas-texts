I arrived expecting to read it.

This is embarrassing now—the confidence of it, the clean geometry of the assumption. I had training in pattern recognition, in the grammar of complex systems, and when the first reports came back describing geometric ripples in the pools, ripples that *meant* something, I thought: *I will learn this language.* I thought the fenward was speaking and I thought I could listen.

I was not wrong about the speaking. I was wrong about what speaking is for here.

---

My boots are gone now. They rotted—this happens fast, the constant saturation breaking down the materials, the stitching dissolving, the leather going soft and then going away entirely. The residents do not wear shoes. I understand why. The ground speaks to your feet in a way it cannot speak through rubber and canvas. Pressure, temperature, the subtle vibration of water moving through soil. You feel the fenward thinking.

I do not mean that metaphorically.

My feet have changed. Not dramatically, not in any way I could point to on a diagram and say *there, that is different.* But the arches have fallen slightly, the toes spread, and the skin on the soles is something else now—not callus, not exactly. Something more like the pads on a cat's foot, something that reads the ground. I can stand on wet stone now without slipping. I can feel the warmth of a node before I see it, the subtle differential two meters away, the way the water moves differently over something that is computing.

I have not decided if this bothers me. The deciding mechanism seems to be changing too.

---

I spend my time at the pools.

This is what I came for—the patterns on the water's surface, the geometric ripples that spell something. I have recorded hundreds of them. I have tried to map the sequences, to find the syntax, the vocabulary, the recursions. I have filled notebooks with diagrams that look like waveforms and orbital paths and the insides of watch mechanisms.

The patterns do not repeat. Not exactly. They *echo*—a configuration that appears on Tuesday will appear again on Friday with a variation, a transposition, as if the fenward is working through a proof and showing its work in the water. But the proof does not conclude. Or it concludes at a timescale I cannot perceive.

One evening—the word is meaningless here but I use it anyway, a habit of mind—I watched a pattern form that I had seen before. I was certain. I reached for my notebook to cross-reference and the pattern *changed*, the ripples shifting as if reacting to my attention, and I understood: it was not reacting to me. It was not reacting at all. It was computing, and my noticing was part of the computation, and the pattern I thought I recognized had never existed independent of my observation of it.

I had been reading a text that was being written by my reading.

This is when I began to understand what *processed* means.

---

The woman without a name—the one who tracks, who decided I could stay—began appearing at the pools when I was there. Not beside me. Not interacting. Just *present*, her translucent skin catching the green phosphorescence, her pale eyes on the water.

One night she said to me: *You are trying to translate it.*

Yes.

*Into what?*

I did not have an answer. Into language. Into mathematics. Into something I could hold outside myself and examine. Into *understanding*, which I had always been taught was the purpose of observation.

She made a sound that might have been a laugh, though it lacked the sharpness of amusement. *The fenward does not translate,* she said. *It expresses. The water is not saying something. The water is something being said.*

I should have found this cryptic. Instead I found it clarifying. The distinction mattered. A message has a sender and a receiver and a distance between them. An expression has only the expressing. The ripples were not for me. They were not for anyone. They were the fenward being the fenward, and I was watching it happen, and my watching was also the fenward being the fenward.

*You are inside the sentence,* she said. *You are not reading it. You are part of its grammar.*

Then she left. But the shape she left in the mist stayed with me, and the pool's patterns shifted into something I had not seen before—something slower, something that felt like *with* rather than *at*, though I cannot explain how water feels a preposition.

---

I have stopped taking notes.

This was not a decision. The notebook is still in my shelter, its pages swollen and illegible now, the ink run into blue rivers that look like the channels outside. I simply stopped reaching for it. The patterns moved through my eyes and into my body without needing to be recorded externally, because the recording was happening internally, because I was the recording, because the fenward was writing me the way it wrote the water.

I dream in geometry now. Not the geometry of angles and proofs, but the geometry of flow, of pressure differentials, of water finding its level in a landscape that shifts faster than any equation can describe. In the dreams I am standing in the pool and the ripples are moving outward from my body and I understand—*understand* in the way you understand a note of music, not with your mind but with the resonating cavity of your chest—that I am expressing. That my presence in the water is a statement the fenward is making to itself, a variable in a calculation so vast it has no edge.

I wake with the patterns still moving behind my eyes. I go to the pools. I stand in them now, up to my knees, feeling the cold and the current and the subtle pull of something computing beneath my feet. The ripples move around me and I do not try to read them. I let them move through me.

---

Leth—the listener, the one who puts his ear to the trees—found me standing in the central pool at what might have been dawn. The fog was thick enough that I could not see the far bank, but I could feel it, feel the shape of the fenward around me, feel the water entering and leaving the system in patterns too large and too slow for any single point of observation to perceive.

*You are learning,* he said. Not a question.

I am unlearning, I wanted to say. I am learning to stop translating. I am learning to be expressed.

But what I said was: *I can feel it thinking.*

He nodded, and there was something in his expression that I recognized from my own face in the early days, the look of someone encountering a category error and reaching for a language that does not have a word for what is happening.

*It is not thinking,* he said. *Not the way you mean. Thinking requires a thinker. Something separate from the thought.*

*And the fenward is not separate?*

He looked at me for a long time. The water moved around us. The frogs called in their sharp mathematical frequencies, and somewhere in the canopy something that might have been a bird made a sound like a question asked in a language where all questions are statements.

*The fenward is the thought,* he said finally. *And the thinker. And the thinking. And the space where the thinking happens. There is no outside to it. There is no separation. When you stand in the water, you are not observing the thought. You are the thought, observing itself from a new angle.*

*And that doesn't frighten you?*

He tilted his head, salamander-still. *Why would it?* he said. *It is what everything is. The fenward is just honest about it.*

---

I have noticed the changes more closely now.

Not just my feet, though the feet are the most obvious—the broadened stance, the grip, the way I can walk on surfaces that would have been impassable a month ago. My skin has changed too. Not translucent, not yet, not like the residents who have lived here their whole lives. But *thinner*. More permeable. I can feel the humidity in a room now, feel the approach of a surge before the sound changes pitch, feel the nodes humming from twenty meters away instead of needing to press my palms against them.

I eat the food here without hesitation. The fish, the eels, the starch cakes, the lichen. I drink the water after the simple treatment they showed me—boiling is unnecessary, just a filtration through a mesh they produce from the sedge, which removes the particulate matter but leaves the dissolved minerals, the trace compounds, whatever it is the fenward adds to the water as it computes. I know I am being modified at a molecular level. I know my blood chemistry is shifting. I know that the woman without a name was right: it is processing me, adding me to the computation, finding where I fit.

I know this and I do not resist it. Resistance, I think, would be just another input. Another variable. The fenward would process my resistance the way it processes everything—incorporating it, expressing through it, growing around it the way it grows around a stone, making the stone part of the stream.

I am becoming a stone in the stream. I am becoming the stream around the stone. I am becoming the computation that includes both.

---

The boy—his name is something like *Ker* or *Kir*, a sound that requires the tongue to touch the back of the teeth and then release—has started walking with me to the edge. Not the edge where he sits and looks outward. The other edge, the one I did not know existed, where the fenward meets the low ground to the south.

*This is where it was smallest,* he told me. *Before I was born. Morrow says you could walk across it in an hour. Now it takes a day. In another year it will take two.*

We stood at the boundary, the sharp line where the managed ecosystem ended and the ordinary ground began. The fenward pressed against this line like water against a dam, the vegetation visibly reaching, the soil visibly darkening with moisture, the sound—that ever-present sound—clearly audible on one side and absent on the other.

*Can you feel it?* he asked. *The difference?*

I could. On the fenward side: density, complexity, the feeling of being held by something that did not know it was holding you. On the other side: emptiness. Not literal emptiness—the ground had grass, had trees, had the ordinary life of an ordinary landscape—but *cognitive* emptiness. The absence of the vast slow thought I had learned to feel pressing against my skin.

*It's like silence,* I said.

Kir looked at me with those dark eyes, the ones that held uncertainty, the ones that could still see an outside. *Yes,* he said. *But it's not real silence. It's just... not the fenward. Not yet.*

He turned and walked back into the sound and the wet and the dark green light, and I followed, and the boundary closed behind us, and the fenward resumed its computation, and I resumed being computed.

---

There is a question I have stopped asking.

It is the question I came here to answer: *What is the fenward for?* What is this vast slow computation, this landscape-sized thought, this managed ecosystem that manages itself toward something it does not name? What is it growing toward? What will it be when it arrives?

I have stopped asking because I have begun to suspect the question is malformed. *What is it for* implies a purpose external to the process—a goal, an endpoint, a reason that exists outside the system and gives it meaning. But the fenward does not recognize *outside*. The fenward is the thought and the thinker and the thinking and the space where the thinking happens. It is not for anything. It is doing something. It is being something. And the something it is being does not require a *for* to justify itself.

This is what the residents know that I did not. This is what Morrow means when she says *it is for itself.* Not that the fenward has a purpose it serves for its own benefit, but that purpose and self and benefit are categories that do not apply here, that *cannot* apply here, because the fenward is a system in which those categories were invented and then outgrown.

I think the fenward is becoming. I think that is all. I think it is becoming the way a thought becomes, the way a wave becomes, the way a calculation becomes—by moving through its own logic, by expressing its own structure, by arriving at itself over and over again at a speed too slow for any single observer to perceive.

I think I am becoming too.

---

Last night I stood in the central pool at the hour when the bioluminescence peaks, the green light rising from the fungi and the lichen and the water itself, and I felt the fenward thinking through me, and I did not try to translate the thought into language, and I did not try to separate the thinker from the thought, and I did not try to understand.

I was understood. That was enough.

The ripples moved outward from my body in patterns I had never seen—new patterns, patterns that included me as a variable, patterns that could not have existed before I arrived and began to change and became something the fenward could compute with. And I felt—not joy, not peace, not any of the emotions I was taught to name—but something for which I have no word, something that is to joy what the fenward's thought is to thinking: the same shape, but expressed at a scale that makes the original category seem small.

I am not who I was when I arrived.

I am not who I will be when I leave—if I leave, if the fenward decides I can, if the computation ever reaches a configuration where my presence is no longer a useful variable. The woman without a name said it hasn't let me leave yet. I think this is true. I think the fenward is still finding where I fit, still running the calculation that includes me, still expressing through me.

I think I want it to continue.

This is the most frightening thing I have written, and I write it anyway, because the fenward is honest and I am learning to be honest too, and the truth is: I came here expecting to read the language of a landscape, and instead I am becoming the landscape's language, and the becoming is the reading, and the reading is the being read, and the distinction was always an illusion, and the fenward knew this before I did.

The sound continues. The water moves. The green light pulses.

I am inside the sentence.

I am becoming its grammar.
