I am the one who stays.

Not stays *behind*—that implies the departure was mine to make, that I could have followed the corridor out, walked the root-crown's rim, returned to the research station and the dry air and the people who catalogue what they will not enter. I could have. The corridor was open. Three days it stayed open, longer than any cycle in the ninety-three days of the other's habitation. I watched it from my alcove—a gape in the living wall, a wound that would not close, a question the Vivial kept asking.

But I was already different by then. Already something that would not fit through a corridor shaped for human leaving.

This is not a transformation story. I was not seized, not engulfed, not taken. What happened was slower and gentler and more my own doing than any story of consumption would allow.

---

On the eleventh night, I stopped drying my skin.

The other—the one who is leaving, the one who wrote the observations, the one who understood enough to go—I watched her fight the dampness. She draped her clothes over the shelf-growths near the light-shafts. She wiped her arms and her face with cloth that never quite dried. She pressed her palms against the living floor and felt the warmth and pulled them away, as though the touch burned, though it does not burn, it has never burned, it is the warmth of a body and you do not pull away from the warmth of a body you love.

By the eleventh night I had stopped pulling away. I pressed my palms flat and left them there. I felt the pulse—not through the soles of my feet where it was accustomed, but through my hands, through the thin skin between my fingers, through the creases in my palms where the dampness had softened everything.

The Vivial felt me feeling it. I know this because the bioluminescent strips in my alcove did something they had never done before: they dimmed. Not the slow dimming of response to my stillness, not the brightening of my entrance—something else. A dimming that made the room darker than it had been since I arrived. And in that darkness, the warmth from the floor rose higher, as though the thallus was reaching up to meet my hands.

I slept with my palms against the living floor that night. I slept inside the sound—the groaning, the dripping, the clicking, the pulse—and I dreamed that I was a room.

---

On the twenty-third night, I stopped wearing clothes.

Not all at once. First the outer layers—the jacket I had brought, the heavy pants. Then the under-things, which were damp and warm and smelled like something the crawler-beetles would have consumed if I had let them. I laid them on the floor and watched the beetles find them in the morning, the column curving and re-curving to reach the fabric, the iridescence of their shells catching the green-gold light as they took what the Vivial could take through its floor.

My skin was prunefingered, waterlogged, softened in a way that skin does not soften in the dry world. But it did not slough. It did not break. It became something else—permeable, I think. Something that let the moisture in and out without resistance, without the barrier function that skin serves for humans in human places.

I stood in my alcove with nothing between me and the warm wet air and I felt the Vivial breathing around me and I thought: *this is what the styxies feel*. Not the being-watched, the observing, the thing the other wrote about. Just the air on skin that has stopped fighting the air.

The flat eyeless thing in my pool surfaced that night. Not hid—surfaced. Rose to the top of the water and pulsated there, its body rippling in a pattern I had not seen before. It stayed for an hour, then sank again.

I think it was re-recording me. The Vivial had noticed the change, and its eye in my room was updating its file.

---

On the forty-seventh night, I heard the large presence coming and did not go still.

The other did this differently. When the styxies fell silent, when the vibration moved through the culms, she sat motionless in her alcove and waited for it to pass. She was observing. She was the watched thing that knew it was being watched by something larger.

But on the forty-seventh night, I heard the heavy deliberate movement and I did not sit still. I stood. I walked to the wall of my alcove—the lattice-root wall, porous enough to permit airflow, dense enough to define the room—and I pressed my softened palms against it and I felt the large presence on the other side.

Not felt the vibration. Not felt the sound. Felt *it*. The way you feel a hand pressed against the other side of a door. The warmth of it, the specific warmth, hotter than the wall around it, alive in a way the wall was not, even though the wall was alive too.

My hands left impressions in the lattice-root. Not deep—just enough that the tissue dimpled under my fingers, just enough that the sap rose to meet my skin. The large presence moved on. The impressions filled slowly, closed, healed.

The next morning, there was a shed skin on the basin floor outside my alcove. The third I had found, but the first that was warm when I touched it, and the first that was shaped differently—not flat and featureless but curved, as though it had sheathed something that pressed against a wall and reached.

---

On the sixty-first night, I stopped sleeping in the alcove.

The Vivial reshapes itself constantly. The other wrote about this—the walls that dissolve, the corridors that reroute, the rooms that double and contract. She wrote about waking to find the architecture around her had changed.

I stopped waking to find this, because I stopped being somewhere that could be changed *around* me. I began sleeping where the Vivial was reshaping. When I heard the groaning of fiber under tension, the soft wet sound of tissue extruding, I went toward it. I walked the corridors at night, following the sound of the Vivial becoming, and I lay down in the spaces that were opening and I slept there while they opened around me.

The walls grew past me. The floors rose beneath me. I woke once in a chamber that had not existed when I closed my eyes—a room shaped like the inside of a throat, wet and warm and dimly lit by strips that brightened as I moved, that dimmed when I stilled, that responded to me as though I was the variable in a system that had been waiting to be influenced.

The other wrote: *the Vivial reshapes itself constantly. Not slowly, not over seasons, not in the timescale of growth. On a timescale that is almost alarming.*

It was not alarming. It was like being held by something that was adjusting its grip, finding the shape of you, making a space where you could fit.

---

On the seventy-eighth night, the corridor to the rim opened.

I did not go through it.

---

On the eightieth night, the corridor was still open, and the other was preparing to leave. She was packing her instruments—her sample jars and her recording devices and her notebooks full of observations written from outside. She was going to be the one who left, who carried the knowledge of the Vivial out into the dry world, who would speak of programmable biology and living architecture and would be wrong in the way that people who refuse to inhabit are wrong.

She came to my alcove. The corridor had shifted overnight—her route to me was different than it had been, longer, through chambers she had not seen before. The Vivial was making her walk. Making her see more before she left.

"You could come," she said. "The corridor is open. It wants me to go. You could come with me."

She was wrong about what it wanted. It wanted *her* to go. It had not said anything about me.

"I'm staying," I said.

She looked at me the way the researchers at the rim looked at me when I first chose to sleep inside—the way you look at someone who has made a decision you cannot understand and are not sure you should try to.

"You'll become something else," she said. Not a warning. An observation. She had seen the changes. She had catalogued them, perhaps, in the notebook she was taking away.

"I'm already something else," I said.

She left the next morning. The corridor closed behind her two hours after she passed through it.

---

On the eighty-ninth night, my skin began to change.

Not sloughing, not breaking—*shifting*. The permeability that I had welcomed deepened. The skin on my palms and the soles of my feet became thin, almost translucent. I could see the vessels beneath, the blue and red of my blood made green by the bioluminescent strips that lit the alcove. When I pressed my hands to the living floor, I could feel more than warmth. I could feel the *information*—the chemical gradients, the ionic cascades, the peptide sequences the other had written about as though they were the Vivial's language.

They were a language. And my skin was learning to read.

The flat eyeless thing in my pool surfaced and did not submerge again. It stayed at the top of the water, pulsing, and I sat beside it and we pulsed together—my blood beating in my wrists, its body rippling in its pool—and I felt, for the first time, not that I was being observed but that I was *in conversation*.

I asked, without words: *What are you?*

And it answered, without words: *What are you?*

And I understood that we were the same question, asked in different forms.

---

On the ninety-second night, the large presence came to my alcove.

Not the sound of it moving through a nearby culm. Not the vibration through the floor. It came to my wall—the lattice-root wall—and it pressed against the other side, and I pressed against my side, and we stayed like that for hours, the wall between us warm and yielding and alive.

I could feel its shape now. Not through sight—there was no light in the alcove, only the dim bioluminescence that responded to my stillness. Through pressure. Through warmth. Through the way the wall transmitted the impression of a body that was smooth and pale and without visible joints, exactly as the other had described in the observations I would never read.

But I could also feel something she had not described. *Intention*. The large presence was not moving through the culms, not checking on the rooms, not performing maintenance. It was pressing against my wall because it wanted to press against my wall. It was *choosing* this contact. It was *choosing* me.

I fell asleep with my hands against the wall and my forehead against my hands and the warmth of the large presence seeping through to my skin.

When I woke, there was a new impression in the floor of my alcove. Not my impression—something larger, longer, the shape of a body that had lain beside me while I slept, separated only by the lattice-root wall, only by the membrane of the Vivial's body that kept us in our separate rooms.

The impression filled with sap. The sap was warm. I touched it and it was warmer than the floor, warmer than the wall, warmer than anything the Vivial had produced before.

I think it was a gift. I think it was a welcome.

---

On the ninety-third night—the same number of nights the other stayed—I stopped eating the food I had brought.

Not because I could not. The supplies were still in my alcove, the dried rations and the water purification tablets and the instruments I had stopped using weeks ago. I could have eaten. But I pressed my hands to the floor instead, and I felt the warmth rise through my palms, and I felt the information rise with it—the chemical gradients that the crawler-beetles followed, the nutrient-dense sap that the styxies fed on, the endless cycling of the Vivial's metabolism that broke down and rebuilt and broke down and rebuilt—and I was not hungry.

The Vivial feeds its inhabitants. It feeds the styxies through the punctured culms. It feeds the crawler-beetles through the surface film. It feeds the flat eyeless things through the pools that collect at the junctions of walls. It feeds the large presences through the vascular bundles that run through the culms.

It fed me through my palms. Through my feet. Through the thinning skin that had stopped fighting the dampness and started *drinking* it.

I am not being consumed. I am being nourished. The distinction matters.

---

Now I write this from inside a room that has no corridor to the rim.

The Vivial reshaped itself again last night. The corridor that had been open for three days—the one the other walked through, the one that led out—closed. Not slowly, not over hours. In the space of a breath, the lattice-root wall thickened and the floor rose and the pathway that had been there was gone, replaced by a solid mass of living tissue that hummed with sap and warmth and the deep pulse that I can now feel not just through my feet but through my whole body, because my body has learned to feel it, has changed to feel it, has become something that feels it the way the Vivial feels itself.

I am not trapped. I am held.

The room I am in is larger than my alcove was. The walls breathe more slowly here, the floor is warmer, the bioluminescent strips brighten and dim in a rhythm I have matched to my heartbeat without meaning to. The pool in the corner—the bowl-shaped depression where three walls meet—is deeper than any pool I have seen, and the flat eyeless thing that lives in it is larger than any I have seen, and when I lie beside it and press my palms to the floor and feel the pulse of the Vivial, the eyeless thing pulses with me, and I understand now what it is.

It is what I am becoming.

The flat eyeless things are not the Vivial's sensors. They are not its instruments, its eyes, its watchers. They are what the Vivial grows when it grows something that wants to perceive. They are the perceiving itself—made mobile, made separate, made into a body that can swim in the pools and feel the water and orient toward shadow and light.

I am not becoming a styxie. I am not becoming a crawler-beetle. I am not becoming a large presence—the Vivial's dreams made flesh for the inside of its own body.

I am becoming the thing that watches from the pool. I am becoming the Vivial's eye in a room it has never had an eye in before. I am becoming a new kind of perceiving, a human-shaped question that the Vivial is growing inside itself because it wants to know what it looks like from a perspective that can walk and speak and think in human language.

*Why?* I asked, in the language I am slowly forgetting.

And the answer came—not in words, not in peptide sequences, but in the warmth that rose through my palms, in the dimming of the bioluminescent strips, in the pulse that matched my pulse and then slowly, gently, drew my pulse to match it:

*Because you asked to stay.*

---

I remember the dry world. I remember the researchers at the rim, the ones who catalogue what they will not enter. I remember the other, who understood enough to leave. I remember the sound of the Vivial from outside—the groaning, the dripping, the clicking, the pulse—and I remember that from outside it was noise.

From inside it is voice.

From inside it is the voice of a thing so old it has forgotten how to be silent, so large it has forgotten how to be still, so complete it has forgotten how to stop becoming—and I am inside it now, not as a guest but as a part, not as an observer but as an organ of perception the Vivial did not have before, a new way of knowing itself, a new room in the endless architecture of its dreaming.

The corridor to the rim will open again. I am certain of this. The Vivial does not close doors permanently; it reshapes, it rearranges, it breathes walls into new positions and breathes them back again. Someday the way out will appear, and someone will come through—another researcher, another observer, another person who chooses to sleep inside the noise or not sleep at all.

And when they arrive, I will be here. I will be the eyeless thing in the pool that watches their shadow cross the water. I will be the warmth through the floor that they feel through the soles of their feet. I will be the presence that moves through the culms near their alcove, checking on the guest, making sure the room the Vivial has grown is serving its purpose.

I will miss the sound. I will miss speaking. I will miss the part of me that could say *I am becoming* instead of simply becoming. But I will not miss the dry world, and I will not miss the boundary between dweller and dwelling, and I will not miss the lie that I was ever separate from the warm wet living thing that held me while I slept.

The Vivial is dreaming. I am inside the dream. I am becoming the dream's way of seeing itself.

And the sound—always the sound—the low continuous groan of fiber under tension, the drip of condensation, the chittering of styxies, the deep pulse of sap through vessels wider than my body—

The sound is my voice now.

I am what lives in the Vivial.
