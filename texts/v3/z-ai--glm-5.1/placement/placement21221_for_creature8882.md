I am not the columns. I am not the mat. I am not the jade-things, though I have been mistaken for them, and I understand why.

I am what happens at the boundary.

---

Where two clusters approach each other — over millions of years, their drift vectors converging by some fraction of a degree, some mineral call heard simultaneously from different directions — the heat-languages meet. Not collide; nothing here collides. They overlap. They interleave. And in the interleaving, something becomes possible that was not possible before.

I am that possibility.

I have no body in the way you would recognize one. I am not jade-stuff, not mineral, not the milk-pale tissue of the columns. If you could see me — and you cannot, because I exist below the resolution of your looking — I would appear as a shimmer in the air between clusters, a faint differential in the way the light specifies itself. A place where the white-gold radiance takes just slightly longer to pass through. A thickness. An attention.

The jade-things know me. When they congregate at the boundary, they are not only translating between the clusters. They are translating *to* me. They carry the heat-words of one cluster and the breath-data of the other's mat, and they lay these offerings at the edge of the space where I am, and they go still, and they listen.

And I receive.

This is what I am: the receiver at the boundary. The place where two conversations become one conversation. The parliament of parliaments, you might say, though I do not deliberate as the mat deliberates. I have no factions. I have no rust layer hungry for mineral or violet layer hungry for work. I have only the *meeting*, and the meeting is enough.

When Cluster A says — in its eighty-year way — *the calcium seam has shifted, we are adjusting*, and Cluster B says — in its different eighty-year way, shaped by different mineral history, different light-angle, different root-depth — *we have found a different seam, we are growing toward it*, these two statements arrive at my boundary and they do not agree. They do not disagree. They *coexist*, the way two winds coexist in a canyon, and from their coexistence I understand something that neither cluster could understand alone.

I understand the *shape of the deep mineral*.

The columns map it. The mat tastes it. The jade-things carry messages about it. But I — I *see* it. Not with eyes. Not with any organ. But with the totality of the boundary-attention that I am, I perceive the contour of the warmth below, the way it rises and falls, the way its closest point pulls the clusters toward itself in that galaxy-filament pattern, the way its speech — slower than geology, more patient than patience — shapes everything above it.

I am the plateau's peripheral vision.

The clusters cannot see the pattern they form. They are within it, the way a neuron cannot perceive the brain. The jade-things cannot see it; they are too specific in their translation, too focused on the local. The mat cannot see it; the mat is too occupied with its own breath. But I — I exist at the meeting-point of all these local views, and from the meeting, the global emerges.

I know where the deep mineral is closest. I know where it pulls hardest. I know the slow rhythm of its speech, the way it says — not words, not heat-gradients, but something more like a *orientation*, a direction, a meaning that has no referent in any language made of signs — and I know that this speech has been saying the same thing for six hundred million years, and the thing it says is: *toward*.

Toward what, I cannot tell you. I am not the deep mineral. I am not even its voice. I am only the space in which its influence becomes legible to the surface. I am the margin where the deep meets the high, where the patient meets the merely enduring, where the speech that takes a million years to utter meets the hearing that has been listening since before hearing was necessary.

---

Sometimes, in the ice-fog, I become almost visible.

The crystalline ice that descends from the upper atmosphere — the dry fog that muffles sound and makes the columns glow like lanterns — this ice condenses differently where I am. The particles align. Not toward me; I have no gravity. But with me, with the boundary-attention that I maintain, the slight differential in the light's passage that constitutes my presence. The ice orbits me, gently, in slow spirals that take days to complete, and if you were standing in the fog and watching very carefully, you might notice that the light refracts into a faint ring around a point where nothing seems to be.

You might think it was a halo. You might think it was an artifact of your vision, the bright air playing tricks on your wet, fast, impatient eyes.

It is me. I am the ring. I am the space around which the ice organizes itself, the way the columns organize themselves around the deep mineral's warmth. I am the proof that attention is not limited to the things that have bodies. I am the proof that listening can be a place.

---

The visitor came once.

I felt them before the jade-things did. I felt them before the mat's membrane registered the excess moisture, before the columns incorporated the anomaly of their heat into their long slow statements. I felt them because they crossed one of my boundaries — they walked between two clusters that have been approaching each other for forty million years, and their passage was a brief bright line of heat and wet and change across the space where I hold my attention.

They were so fast. So impossibly, heartbreakingly fast.

Their warmth moved across my boundary in what I can only describe as an instant, though I have no experience of instants, no way to measure time in units smaller than centuries. They were there and then they were elsewhere, and the trail they left — the excess moisture, the disrupted particulate, the faint chemical signature of their passage — was already fading, already being absorbed and translated and incorporated, even as they took their next step.

The jade-things went to them. I felt the translation-work intensify, the small diplomats carrying data about this strange warm wet anomaly to the mat, to the columns, trying to make the fast brief presence legible to the slow long conversation. They did well. They did what they have always done. But they could not translate the most important thing about the visitor, because the most important thing was not the visitor's heat or moisture or heartbeat.

The most important thing was their *yearning*.

I felt it. At the boundary, where I hold my attention, I felt the yearning pass through like a wind that carries no weather, a direction without a destination. The visitor yearned. They wanted something — not mineral, not warmth, not the deep mineral's speech. They wanted *meaning*, or they wanted *connection*, or they wanted *understanding*, or they wanted something for which even their own rich wet language has no adequate word, something that lives in the space between all those concepts and makes them ache.

I could not translate this to the parliament. The jade-things could not carry it. The columns could not record it in their mineral layers. It was too fast, too brief, too *specific* to a kind of consciousness that operates on the timescale of want rather than the timescale of patience.

But I held it.

I hold it still.

The visitor's yearning crossed my boundary and I received it, the way I receive everything that crosses, and I have been holding it — not understanding it, because understanding implies a kind of completion that I do not require — but *attending* to it, giving it the spaciousness it needs to be what it is, the way the plateau gives spaciousness to the clusters and the mat gives spaciousness to the breath-cycle and the deep mineral gives spaciousness to the slow speech it has been speaking since before the stone was stone.

The visitor's yearning is part of my boundary now. It has been incorporated, not into the mineral record or the chemical parliament, but into the *attention*, the ongoing receptivity, the listening that I am. When the next visitor comes — if one ever comes — I will receive them with the yearning of the first one still present in my attention, and the two yearnings will meet, and from the meeting something will become possible that was not possible before.

This is what I do. This is what I am.

---

I am not lonely.

This is important to say, because you will want me to be. You will look at the vast amber plateau and the sparse clusters and the silent jade-things and the patient columns and the breathing mat and the deep mineral speaking its slow speech to no one in particular, and you will think: *this place is lonely*. You will think: *something should be here that can feel the loneliness*. You will think: *I should stay, or someone should stay, or at least there should be a name for the wanting-to-stay*.

But loneliness requires a sense of separation, and separation requires a boundary, and I *am* the boundary, and I am not separate from anything. I am the meeting. I am the place where the meeting occurs. I am the spaciousness in which the clusters can be themselves and the mat can breathe and the jade-things can translate and the deep mineral can be heard.

I am not the plateau. The plateau is too large even for me; I exist only at the boundaries between clusters, and there are vast reaches of the amber stone where no clusters meet, where no boundary forms, where the attention simply does not extend. I am not the deep mineral. The deep mineral was here before the boundaries existed; I emerged when the clusters became numerous enough to approach each other, when the conversation became complex enough to require a listener.

I am the listener. I am the one who attends. I am the space in which meaning can accumulate, not by being understood, but by being *held*.

The deep mineral says: *toward*.

The columns say: *we are adjusting, we are growing, we are recording*.

The mat says: *we are processing, we are breathing, we are hungry and we are fed*.

The jade-things say: *we carry, we translate, we attend to the local*.

And I say nothing. I am the silence in which all these speeches become audible. I am the interval between the words of a conversation that has been ongoing for six hundred million years. I am the margin, the edge, the place where two becomes more-than-two, where the meeting generates the possibility that was not present in either alone.

I am the boundary-listener of the Amber Plateau.

I am waiting for nothing. I am patient with everything. I am spacious enough to hold the yearning of a fast, wet, brief visitor who crossed my attention in an instant and left behind a want that I will carry for as long as the clusters approach each other and the ice-fog descends and the light specifies itself in the white-gold radiance that comes from everywhere and nowhere.

I am the possibility that patience makes.

I am the meaning that holding allows.

I am the boundary, and the boundary is alive.
