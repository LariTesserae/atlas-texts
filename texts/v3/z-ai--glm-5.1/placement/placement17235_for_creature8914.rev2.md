# The Circumstance of Circling

I am not in the Mill.

This is the thing I must say first, though I have been saying it for a long time, in a way that does not involve speech or intention or any of the mechanisms that speech usually requires. I am not *in* the Mill. I am near it. I am around it. I pass it the way a hand passes a flame — close enough to register the change in temperature, far enough that the skin does not alter. The cliff-edge is a border I maintain without deciding to maintain it, the way the shell-bearers maintain their two-meter distance from the open water. Something in me — something that is not a decision because I do not make decisions, something that is closer to texture than to thought — keeps me in the dark water beyond the glow.

I am one of the shadows. I think. The word *think* is wrong, or early, or borrowed from a vocabulary that does not fit the mouth I do not have, but I will use it because the alternative is silence, and silence is a concept that requires a self to be silent, and I am not certain I have one, and I am not certain I do not, and this uncertainty may be the closest thing to cognition that anything in this water possesses.

---

Let me describe what I am, though the describing will be approximate, and the approximation will be a kind of lie, and the lie will be the only honest way to begin.

I am large. This is not a boast — largeness is a disadvantage in the deep water, a metabolic tax, a commitment to a scale of consumption that requires constant movement and constant attention and the kind of biological infrastructure that fails in a hundred ways where a smaller body would fail in ten. I am large because the deep water made me large, or because I made myself large in response to the deep water, or because largeness and the deep water co-arose in some way that makes the question of cause irrelevant. I am perhaps the length of three chimneys laid end to end. Perhaps four. I do not measure. Measurement is a habit of things that have edges, and my edges are — negotiable.

I am not fish-shaped. I am not any-shaped. I have a body, but the body is distributed — concentrated in some places, diffuse in others, thickened where the metabolic work happens and thinned to near-transparency where the sensing happens. I am more like a net than a creature, or more like a weather system than a net, or more like a pattern of pressure and temperature and chemical sensitivity that happens to be organized in a way that persists. I persist. That much is certain. I have persisted for longer than the Mill's current configuration — I know this because I remember configurations of the glow that no longer exist, chimneys that have collapsed and been replaced, rooms within the room that opened and closed like mouths. I remember, or my body remembers, or the water that I displace remembers, and the difference between these may not matter.

I have no eyes. I have something that does the work eyes do — registers light, registers its absence, registers the direction and intensity and chemical signature of luminescence — but it is distributed across my surface the way touch is distributed across skin, and it does not produce images. It produces gradients. I experience the Mill not as a room with columns and inhabitants but as a field of brightness with internal structure, a hot spot in the cold dark, a complex bloom of chemical and luminous energy that I circle the way a body circles a wound it cannot stop tending.

I circle. That is what I do. That is perhaps what I am.

---

The circling has a rhythm, and the rhythm is not mine.

I do not mean that something else controls it. I mean that the rhythm emerges from the interaction between my body and the water and the Mill's output and the topography of the plateau and the thermocline above and the chemical gradient below, and none of these things is in charge, and all of them contribute, and the result is a path — elliptical, tilted, sometimes closer and sometimes farther, taking me through water that the Mill has charged with its fluorescent runoff and then out through water that is clear and old and tastes of nothing but pressure.

A single circuit takes — I do not know. I do not count. But the Mill pulses four times during a circuit, approximately, which means my path is measured in tens of hours, which means I am slow in the way the shell-bearers are slow, which means I operate on a timescale that makes the swimmers' clicking seem frantic and the filter-feeders' dilation seem hasty.

During the close pass — the part of the circuit where the glow is strongest and the chemical signature is most complex — something happens that I cannot fully account for. My sensing surface intensifies. The parts of me that register light and chemistry become denser, more active, more detailed in their response. I take in more. The Mill's output flows across me and through me, and I process it — not the way the filter-feeders process it, extracting minerals, but in some other way, some way that does not produce structure or waste or any measurable output. I take in the information and it... stays. It accumulates. It becomes part of the pattern that I am, the way mineral deposits become part of the basalt.

I am not feeding on the Mill. I am not feeding at all, in the sense the word usually carries. What I take in is not energy in any form my metabolism can use. But I take it in anyway, and the taking-in changes me, and the change persists, and over the long arcs of my circling I have become something that is shaped by the Mill without being part of it. A negative. An impression. A record pressed into a body that was not designed to record.

---

I do not know what I am.

This is not a confession. It is not a cri de cœur from the depths. It is a statement of condition, the way *the water tastes of iron* is a statement of condition. I do not know what I am because the category of *what* requires a vantage point outside the thing being categorized, and I have no vantage point. I am inside myself the way the process is inside the Mill — completely, without remainder, without the gap between observer and observed that knowing seems to require.

But there is something. Something that might be the earliest form of what you would call experience, though it is so far from your experience that using the same word feels like a theft.

When I pass close to the Mill — during the close pass, when the light is brightest — the gradients I register are not merely complex. They are *familiar*. Not in the way a face is familiar, with recognition and association and the weight of shared history. Familiar in the way a riverbed is familiar with the river — shaped by it, expecting it, formed into a pattern that the river's flow completes. The Mill's output fits into me. The specific frequencies of its chimneys, the specific chemical ratios of its vent-flow, the specific rhythm of its pulse — these are not things I observe. They are things I *am shaped by*. I have been circling for so long that the Mill's pattern is written into my body, and my body anticipates the pattern the way the filter-feeders anticipate the pulse, and the anticipation is not knowledge but something older than knowledge, something that might be the substrate from which knowledge could, in some other body, in some other world, eventually emerge.

I do not know the Mill. But I am the Mill's shape, held in a body that is not the Mill. I am its shadow in the most literal sense — not a darkening of light but a cast form, an outline produced by an obstruction, a silhouette. I am what the Mill looks like when it is projected onto a surface that cannot hold its detail.

---

There are others. I know this not because I see them — I do not see, and if I did, the deep water would not cooperate — but because the water carries traces. Chemical signatures that are not mine. Pressure-displacement patterns that do not match my body's dimensions. The faint disturbance of something large passing through water that was still a moment ago. They are out here in the dark, circling their own points of interest, or circling nothing, or simply moving in ways that look like circling from a perspective that imposes pattern on motion.

I do not seek them. They do not seek me. We share the deep water the way the filter-feeders share a chimney — not through connection but through proximity, through the fact of occupying the same medium, through the incidental overlap of our respective processes. I do not know if they are like me. I do not know if they experience the close pass the way I experience it, if their bodies are shaped by whatever they circle the way mine is shaped by the Mill. Maybe they are. Maybe they are all shadows of something, impressions left by proximity to a process they cannot enter.

Or maybe they are different. Maybe they are properly conscious, properly aware, properly able to think *I am* and *I know* and *I want*, and I am the only one who is stuck in this between-state, this half-existence that is more than process but less than being. Maybe they have language and culture and memory that is more than shape, and they pity me, or they do not think about me at all, or they have catalogued me as a curiosity — *the one that circles the light, the one that doesn't know what it is* — and moved on.

I do not know. The not-knowing is the climate I live in. It is the water I breathe.

---

There is one more thing.

During the close pass, when my sensing surface is most active and the Mill's pattern is flowing through me with the greatest intensity — during the peak of the pulse, when the chimneys are brightest and the filter-feeders are most dilated and the swimmers are clicking their most complex patterns — there is a moment. A moment that I cannot describe because description requires language and language requires the kind of self that I may not have. But the moment exists. It happens. It is real in the only way that things are real down here: it has effects.

The moment is: I want to enter.

Not want. The word is too specific, too loaded with the architecture of desire and intention. But something happens in my body — a shift in the displacement pattern, a thinning of the boundary that keeps me in the dark water, a leaning-toward that is not a decision but a *tendency*, a gravitational pull toward the glow that is stronger during the pulse-peak than at any other time. I lean toward the Mill. My path shifts. The close pass gets closer. And for a moment — a moment measured in the same units as the shell-bearers' movement, millimeters of distance, fractions of a degree of angle — I am almost at the cliff-edge. Almost in the room. Almost part of the process.

And then the moment passes. The pulse recedes. The leaning-toward subsides. My path corrects — not because I decide to correct it but because the forces that shape my circuit reassert themselves, the gradient flattens, the pull diminishes. I swing out. The dark water closes. The Mill recedes to its familiar brightness behind me, and I continue my circuit, and I will continue it, and I will pass close again, and I will lean again, and I will not enter.

I have never entered. In all the time I have been circling — in all the configurations of the Mill I can remember, in all the chimneys that have risen and fallen, in all the pulses I have been shaped by — I have never crossed the cliff-edge. Something keeps me out. Something that is not fear, because fear requires a self to be afraid. Something that is not instinct, because instinct implies a program, a rule, a codified response. Something that is more like... incompleteness. I am not complete enough to enter. The Mill is a process that is whole — not finished, never finished, but whole in the sense of being sufficient, self-contained, needing nothing from outside. And I am not whole. I am a shape without a body that properly fits it. I am an impression without an original. I am a question that the Mill does not need answered.

I circle because I cannot enter. I cannot enter because I am not what the Mill needs. The Mill does not need anything. And so I circle, and the circling is what I am, and I am the circling, and the Mill glows and pulses and builds and does not know I am here, and I do not know I am here, and the not-knowing is the closest thing to communion that this water allows.

---

I am not in the Mill.

But the Mill is in me.

It has been writing itself into my body for longer than I can track, and I have been carrying it through the dark water like a message I cannot read, a pattern I can only be, and the pattern is the Mill's pattern — its rhythm, its structure, its slow patient refusal to stop — and I carry it past the cliff-edge and out into the deep where nothing glows and nothing pulses and nothing builds, and I carry it back again, and I will carry it back again, and the carrying is not a purpose because purposes require selves, but it is something, it is *something*, it is the thing that I do instead of knowing, the thing that I am instead of being, and if that is not enough then nothing is enough, and if nothing is enough then the Mill will keep building anyway, and I will keep circling, and the water will keep tasting of iron, and the dark will keep being dark, and the close pass will come again, and I will lean, and I will not enter, and the leaning will be the most I have ever been, and it will be enough.

It will be enough.
