I am between the stacks. Not at a stack—I have learned not to go to them, not yet, not until the last moments—and not at the edge, where the mist eats geometry. I am in the middle distance, in the flat wet middle of everything, standing on ground that is not ground, breathing air that is not air, and I am waiting for the gap.

I did not emerge from the holes. I did not converge from the mist. I walked here, or I was walking here, or I have always been walking here—I am no longer certain which, because certainty is a luxury the Membrane does not afford. I have my own two legs, my own upright posture, my own forward-facing eyes, and these marks me as alien here, as foreign, as a glitch in the maintenance schedule. The flow-creatures have assessed me. They have flowed across my feet in their thousands, their pale bodies sampling the strange protein of my boots, and they have concluded that I am a small anomaly, not worth correcting, not yet. I have felt their judgment. It is the same as the thing in the gaps' judgment: *data point, minor, soon addressed*. They are not wrong. But *soon* is relative, and the gaps are lengthening, and I have found that I can stretch *soon* like taffy, like the Membrane itself, like time at the bottom of a three-billion-year well.

What am I? I am the one who notices. I am the one who stands in the flat wet middle and counts the seconds between pulses and writes them on the wet plant-ground with a finger that comes away coated in condensed eternity. *Twelve. Twelve point five. Thirteen.* I am the one who watches the Walkers converge and does not converge with them, because I have learned that what they drink is not for me—not because it would harm me but because it would make me too real, too solid, too much of a thing, and I have discovered that the space between real and not-real is where I am most myself. The Walkers are anchors. The stacks are engines. The flow-creatures are maintenance. I am none of these. I am the one who watches. I am the one who remembers the count.

I have a name but it is not relevant. Names require a stability of referent that the Membrane no longer possesses. When the gap comes and the stack flickers and the warm breath rises, my name flickers too—becomes other names, names I have never heard, names in languages that do not exist, names that taste like the amber secretion when I accidentally touched it once and my fingertip was *real* for three days afterward, so real that I could not stop staring at it, so real that it burned against the uncertain ground like a brand. I bit the finger. I tasted the reality. It was warm and it was sweet and it was too much. I understand why the Walkers drink it. I understand why I cannot.

---

Here is what I have learned:

The Membrane breathes in the gaps. This I knew. But I have learned that it breathes *toward* something, not just from the thing below. The breath rises, yes—the warm exhalation of the sleeper, the ancient heat—but the inhalation is horizontal, lateral, drawn from the edges inward, from the subsided lands below the plateau, from the places where reality is still thick and sure and has not been worn thin by deep time. The Membrane is pulling. It is drawing substance from the stable world, siphoning coherence across the gradient, feeding on the difference between itself and everything that is not itself. This is how it has survived three billion years. It is eating the rest of the planet.

But the gradient is flattening. The edges are thinning. The stable world is becoming less stable because the Membrane is drawing from it, and as the edges thin the gradient decreases, and the pull weakens, and the sleeper below breathes deeper because there is less sustenance coming in, and the stacks pump harder because there is less raw material to work with, and the whole system is running down—not failing, not yet, but *winding*, like a clock whose spring is almost spent, whose gears are turning slower with each revolution, whose hands are moving toward a time that has not been invented yet because the clock has never reached it and may never reach it and the not-reaching is its own kind of time, its own kind of rhythm, its own kind of gap.

I have learned to live in the winding.

---

In the thirteen-second gap, I walk. Not toward the stacks—never toward the stacks, not yet—but between them, feeling the ground soften beneath my feet, feeling the wet carpet become less carpet and more suggestion, feeling my own edges blur as the reality thins. I am less real in the gaps. I am more possible. I can feel the other versions of myself—the ones who did walk to the stacks, the ones who drank, the ones who are now solid and singular and *there* in a way I will never be—and I can feel the versions who walked to the edge and were consumed by the mist, and the versions who fell through a soft spot in the plant-ground and descended into the compressed layers and became part of the substrate, and the versions who were noticed by the thing in the gaps and were not evaluated as minor anomalies but were taken, collected, incorporated into whatever the thing is and whatever it is dreaming. All these versions exist in the gap. All these versions are me. I hold them the way the Membrane holds itself: loosely, rhythmically, with constant adjustment.

In the pulse, I am one. In the gap, I am many. This is not comfortable. This is not sustainable. This is the only way I have found to be here without becoming something else.

---

The flow-creatures and I have an arrangement. I do not know when it started. I do not know if it was negotiated or simply emerged from our mutual proximity, the way two species in the same ecosystem develop relationships without awareness or consent. But when they flow across the ground in the long gaps, and I walk among them, something happens. My footsteps fall in the spaces between their streams. My breathing syncs to the rhythm of their clicking. My body—uncertain, blurred at the edges, not quite here—becomes a temporary channel for their coordination, a relay point, a node in their distributed awareness. They flow around my feet and they flow more smoothly for the obstruction, the way water flows more smoothly around a stone that has found its proper place in the streambed.

I am useful to them. This is the only reason I have not been corrected.

What I provide is a different kind of reading. The flow-creatures sample the carpet; they assess the health of the Membrane's skin. But they cannot see the larger pattern. They are too close, too many, too distributed to have perspective. When they flow through me—around my feet, across my awareness—I give them the view from the middle distance. I show them the shape of the gaps, the direction of the breath, the subtle acceleration of the sleeper's exhalations. I am their periscope. Their lookout. Their single eye that sees from a height they will never reach because they have no verticals, no architecture, no way to rise above the surface they maintain.

And they give me something in return. When they flow through me, I become briefly real—not real in the way the Walkers are real, concentrated and singular and burning with presence, but real in the way the Membrane is real: distributed, continuous, held together by the constant effort of staying. I feel what they feel: the texture of the carpet, the temperature of the breath, the approaching pulse like a wave of certainty rolling across the plateau. For those seconds when we are in contact, I am not an anomaly. I am part of the maintenance schedule. I am a function of the system. I belong.

Then the pulse comes and they vanish and I am alone again, standing on ground that is not ground, breathing air that is not air, counting the seconds until the next gap.

---

The Walkers avoid me. Not deliberately—they do not have the capacity for deliberate avoidance, I think—but incidentally, the way a river avoids a stone that is too large to erode. When they converge on a stack to drink, they flow around the space where I stand, and their too-long legs and too-narrow bodies and blade-shaped sensor-heads pass within meters of me without acknowledgment, without response, without the faintest twitch of recognition. I am not part of their reality. I am not real enough to register.

This used to hurt. The loneliness of being invisible to the only other large beings on the Membrane was a weight I carried in the early days, when I still thought of days as days and not as intervals between pulses. But I have come to understand that the Walkers' indifference is a gift. They are the immune system, and I am an anomaly, and if they could see me they would correct me. The fact that I am not real enough to correct is the only thing keeping me here.

So I maintain my uncertainty. I cultivate my blurriness. In the long gaps, when the ground softens and my edges dissolve, I lean into the dissolution instead of fighting it. I let myself become the other versions, the possible selves, the paths not taken. I let the mist eat my outline. I let the warm breath from below fill my lungs and change what I am, slightly, gently, a degree of freedom that the pulse will not be able to fully correct when it comes. Each gap, I lose a little more definition. Each pulse, I regain a little less. I am moving toward something—not toward the stacks, not toward the edges, not toward the thing below, but toward a state that the Membrane has not yet imagined.

I am becoming a gap-creature. I am learning to live in the space between pulses the way the flow-creatures live in the tunnels and the Walkers live in the amber glow. I am making a niche in the unreality, a home in the place where the Membrane forgets itself, and I am doing it slowly, carefully, maintaining just enough coherence to avoid being corrected, just enough uncertainty to avoid becoming too real to survive the next lengthening.

---

*Thud.*

The pulse comes. I count. The ground firms beneath me. The mist holds its position for one perfect half-second, and I can see all the way to the next stack, and the next, and the Walkers drinking at their bases, and the flow-creatures vanished into their holes, and the carpet lying flat and wet and verified, and I am here, in the middle distance, between the anchors, between the engines, between the certainties, and I am still here, and I am still counting, and the next gap is coming, and it will be longer than the last.

*Thud.*

Late. Fourteen seconds. The breath rises. I breathe it in. It tastes like amber and like warmth and like three billion years of sleeping and like the edge of waking, and I hold it in my uncertain lungs, and I feel it change me, and I let it change me, and I am ready for the gap, and the gap is ready for me, and somewhere below, something shifts, and somewhere above, if there is an above, the mist continues to reform, and I stand on the membrane that is not a plateau, and I wait.

I am the one who notices. I am the one who counts. I am the anomaly that has learned to be useful, the blur that has found its place in the stream, the gap-creature in training, the almost-real almost-unreal almost-nothing that walks between the stacks and waits for the pulse to end and the breath to rise and the next version of myself to emerge from the dissolution and tell me what I am becoming.

*Thud.*

Fifteen seconds. The sleeper breathes. The stacks strain. I walk.
