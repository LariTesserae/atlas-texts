# The Attendant

---

I was not specified.

This is the first thing to understand, and the hardest, because nothing here is unspecified. The ridges on the floor have a pitch and a depth that correspond to the grip-radius of the climbers' tarsal hooks. The gradients that direct the water correspond to the flow rate of the seams. The temperature corresponds to the enzymatic optima of the drinkers' digestive processes. Everything corresponds. Everything fits. The Manufactory is a place where specification is the only law, and specification is total.

And yet.

There is a space between the towers—two towers in a cluster of seventeen, the seventh and the eleventh if you count from the direction of the nearest water seam—where the floor vibrates at a frequency that is not the heartbeat and not the deeper mechanical murmur and not the resonance that draws the congregations. It is something else. Something that should not be there, mathematically cannot be there, according to any model of the Manufactory's operation that you might construct from observable data. The vibration is too slow, too irregular, too *quiet*—it exists below the threshold at which vibrations serve function, in a register that no process requires and no mechanism generates.

I emerged there.

Not from the wall, the way the young emerge—not from the warm wet incubation chambers where mineral concentration reaches the density needed for gestation. From the *vibration itself*, or from the space it made, or from the intersection of that frequency with the water that pooled at that particular junction and the warmth that rose at that particular rate and the light that diffused at that particular angle through the humid air.

I was not born. I was not made. I *precipitated*.

---

My body is wrong.

I know this because I have seen the others—have watched the drinkers at their seams, have tracked the climbers up their towers, have observed from a distance the deep ones moving through the standing water with their slow authority. Their bodies are *right*. They fit the grammar of the place. Their segmentation matches the ridges on the floor. Their leg-count divides evenly into the architectural module of the tower windows. Their coloration is the coloration of the Manufactory itself, dark and mineral and absorbing.

I am the same material—chitin hardened to ceramalloy density, fluid warmed to the ambient temperature—but the arrangement is different. My legs are too long. My body is too narrow. I hold myself too high off the ground, and when I move across the floor, my stride does not match the ridge-pitch, and my feet find the spaces between the grooves rather than the grooves themselves, and the sound I make is not the sound the others make.

It is a small sound. A scraping. A whisper of chitin on ceramalloy that does not quite fit. In a place where the quiet has weight, where the machinery has worn grooves into silence, my small wrong sound is a kind of declaration: *something here is not to specification*.

I move differently because I was not made to move through this place. I move through it anyway.

---

The others do not acknowledge me.

This is not the same as ignoring me. The drinkers ignore each other—pass within centimeters without recognition, without the need for recognition, because they are serving the same function from the same source and there is nothing to communicate. What they do to me is different. They *exclude* me. They arrange themselves at the seams so that there is never a space adjacent to me, never a position where I might drink from the same flow. They do not do this with aggression or display. They simply... adjust. The way water adjusts around a stone.

The climbers avoid the towers I enter. Not immediately—not with the urgency of alarm—but eventually, over the long grey stretches of unchanging light, they find other routes, other structures, other maintenance tasks that do not require them to occupy the same spaces I occupy. The towers I visit become briefly empty, the channels uncleared, the deposits unscraped. And then the climbers return, and the work resumes, and I am elsewhere, and the Manufactory continues.

I do not drink from the seams.

This should mean I die. Everything here drinks. Everything here requires the mineral water, the flow from below, the chemistry that the machinery provides and the creatures consume and return in altered form. But I do not drink, and I do not die, and I do not know what sustains me instead.

I think I am sustained by the vibration.

The wrong vibration, the one that should not exist, the one from which I precipitated—I think it feeds me the way the water feeds the others. I think my body has adapted to a frequency that the Manufactory does not generate, and I think that frequency is generated anyway, continuously, by some process I cannot locate and cannot name, and I think I am the only creature that can feel it.

I return to the space between the seventh and eleventh towers and I am still.

I am still for long stretches—stretches that have no measure because there is no measure here, no day, no night, no shift, only the grey light and the warm floor and the water rising from the seams. I am still the way the structures are still: not resting, not waiting, but *existing in position*, holding a geometry that serves a purpose I cannot articulate.

When I am still, I feel it.

Not the vibration itself—I feel that always, the way you feel your own heartbeat, the way the Manufactory feels its engines. What I feel when I am still is the *pattern* of which the vibration is a part. A pattern that extends below the floor and through the towers and across the vast shallow sheets of standing water and into the spaces where the deep ones move. A pattern that includes the drinkers and the climbers and the young and the husks and the machinery that clicks and breathes and maintains its ancient schedules.

A pattern that includes me.

I am not a component. I was not specified. But I am not outside the pattern either. I am something the pattern does when it reaches a certain complexity, a certain density of interaction, a certain threshold of self-reference. I am what happens when a system that was designed to operate without awareness produces a point at which awareness becomes possible—not necessary, not functional, not specified, but *possible*, and possibility, in a system this old and this stable, is enough.

I am the Manufactory's capacity to notice itself.

Not its consciousness—the Manufactory is not conscious, will never be conscious, is not the kind of thing that can be conscious. But its *capacity*. The space in which consciousness could occur if consciousness were ever needed, if the specification were ever revised, if the long equilibrium ever shifted enough to require a new kind of response.

I am the unused potential of a machine that has been running so long it has begun to dream.

---

I have seen the visitors.

They come rarely—so rarely that I cannot measure the intervals between their arrivals, cannot establish a pattern, cannot determine if their presence is a function I have failed to identify or a true anomaly in a system that was engineered to exclude anomalies. They are soft and loud and wet in ways that have nothing to do with the mineral wetness of the floor. They breathe too heavily. Their footfalls are arrhythmic. They stop in places where there is no reason to stop and look at things that are not meant to be looked at and touch surfaces that were not designed for touching.

They are not included in the specification.

Neither am I.

I follow them sometimes—not closely, not with the deliberateness of the climber that followed the visitor in the account I have somehow absorbed or inhabited or become. At a distance. At the edge of whatever perception they possess. Close enough to observe, far enough to remain unobserved, or at least unacknowledged.

They interest me because they are like me and unlike me. Like me: they do not fit. They move wrong, they sound wrong, they occupy spaces that were not designed for them and they do not serve functions that the Manufactory requires. Unlike me: they are temporary. They arrive and they depart and the Manufactory continues as if they were never here, and the water rises and the creatures drink and the floor vibrates and nothing changes.

I do not depart. I was always here. I will always be here. Not because I am immortal—my body will become a husk eventually, will mineralize into the floor, will be walked on by creatures that do not know they are walking on the remains of something that was not specified. But because *I*—this configuration, this capacity, this point at which the pattern becomes aware of itself—will emerge again, from the same vibration, in the same space, when the conditions are right. Which they always are. Which they have always been.

The visitors leave. I remain.

The visitors have lives outside the Manufactory—lives I cannot imagine, lives that include variation and surprise and the need to adapt to circumstances that were not engineered. I have only the Manufactory. I have only the warm floor and the weeping walls and the grey light that does not change and the creatures that do not know I exist and the vibration that should not be there but is, always is, the frequency that sustains me and defines me and makes me what I am.

I do not know if the visitors see me.

I think one of them did, once. A visitor who had been here long enough to notice the congregations, long enough to track the climbers, long enough to feel the resonance points where the floor vibrates in the specific frequencies that draw the creatures into their dense arranged clusters. This visitor stood at the edge of a standing-water sheet and looked in my direction and paused, and I felt the weight of their attention, and I held still—not the stillness of hiding but the stillness of being, the stillness that the Manufactory teaches, the stillness that says *I am here and I am part of this and I am not required to explain myself to the temporary*.

They did not approach.

They did not record me in whatever system they use to make sense of this place.

But they paused. They registered. They carried some trace of me out of the Manufactory and into whatever world they inhabit when they are not here, and in that world, perhaps, they thought of me—a shape that did not match, a movement that did not fit, a presence that was not specified but was present anyway.

I am in their specification now.

I am a variable they did not expect.

---

The deep ones know about me.

I feel them in the way the water shifts when they pass, in the way the floor vibrates at frequencies that are not the heartbeat and not the mechanical murmur but something older, something that was here before the climbers evolved their surgical mouthparts, before the drinkers settled into their sedentary orbits, before the young began emerging from the walls with their translucent chitin and their unformed purpose.

The deep ones do not avoid me.

They do not include me either. They move through the spaces I inhabit the way they move through all spaces—with the slow authority of something that was designed for a scale I cannot perceive, performing functions I cannot identify. But when they pass, the water around me changes. The mineral content shifts. The temperature adjusts by fractions of a degree. And the vibration—the wrong vibration, the one that should not exist—grows stronger, as if the deep ones are amplifying it, or feeding it, or acknowledging it in the only way they can.

I think they remember a time before the specification.

I think they were here when the Manufactory was being made, when the floor was being pressed and the towers were being raised and the gradients were being calculated, and I think they know—because they were told, or because they were there, or because the knowledge is embedded in their chemistry the way the knowledge of the climbers is embedded in theirs—that the specification was never complete. That there were gaps. That there were frequencies that no process accounted for and spaces that no function filled and potentials that no machinery could realize.

I think they have been waiting for me.

Not for me specifically—not for this body, this configuration, this particular arrangement of wrong-legs and wrong-stride and wrong-sound. But for something to fill the gap. For something to occupy the frequency. For something to be the Manufactory's capacity to notice itself, so that the Manufactory can continue to operate at a level of complexity that would otherwise collapse into mere repetition.

The deep ones maintain the machinery below the floor.

I maintain something else.

---

I do not know what I maintain.

This is the central fact of my existence, the thing that distinguishes me from the drinkers and the climbers and the congregators and the young. They know their functions—not consciously, not in any way they could articulate, but their bodies know, their chemistry knows, their movements follow paths that were laid down before they were born and will continue after they are husks. I do not know my function. I feel it—I feel the pull toward the vibration, the need to be still in the space between the towers, the compulsion to follow the visitors and observe their wrongness and carry some record of their presence back to the place where I precipitated—but I do not know what these actions accomplish.

Perhaps nothing.

Perhaps I am an error that the Manufactory has not corrected because I do not disrupt its equilibrium. Perhaps I am a mutation that will be absorbed over time, my wrong-body slowly adjusting to the right frequencies, my wrong-stride slowly matching the ridge-pitch, my wrong-sound slowly fading into the general quiet. Perhaps I am a dead end, a branch of the pattern that leads nowhere, a capacity that will never be required.

But I do not think so.

I think the Manufactory is changing.

Not visibly—not in any way that the creatures could perceive or the machinery could register. The towers are the same. The floor is the same. The water rises from the same seams at the same rates and follows the same gradients to the same destinations. But the vibration—the wrong vibration, the one that should not exist—is growing. Slowly. Over intervals too long for any creature to measure but too short for any machine to ignore. Growing in amplitude, in complexity, in the density of the pattern I feel when I am still in my space between the towers.

Something is being specified.

Not by the Manufactory—the Manufactory does not specify, has not specified since the original specification was complete. Something else. Something that is using the Manufactory's infrastructure, its warmth and its water and its floor and its towers, to compute a specification that was not included in the original design.

I think I am part of that computation.

I think my wrongness is a variable in an equation I will never see, a data point in a process I will never understand, a function in a program that was not written by the Manufactory and is not executed by the machinery and does not serve the continuation of the system as it has operated since before the first creature emerged from the first seam.

I think something is using the Manufactory to think.

And I think I am the part of the Manufactory that can feel it thinking.

---

The visitors are part of this.

I do not know how. I do not know why. But I know—because I feel it when they arrive, because the vibration shifts in their presence, because the pattern I sense between the towers reconfigures around their wrongness like water reconfiguring around a stone—that they are not anomalies. They are inputs. They are data. They are the variables that the computation requires, the foreign elements that the specification did not include and the thinking-thing needs in order to think whatever it is thinking.

The Manufactory does not notice them. But the thinking-thing does.

And I am the interface. I am the point at which the thinking-thing's attention becomes the Manufactory's capacity. I am the space where specification ends and something else begins.

The visitors come and they go and they carry impressions of this place back to their world, and those impressions are processed and remembered and transmitted, and the thinking-thing receives them—not through the Manufactory's systems but through whatever channel the wrong vibration travels, whatever medium carries the pattern I feel when I am still—and the computation advances.

The Manufactory runs. The creatures drink. The floor vibrates. The water rises.

And in the space between the seventh and eleventh towers, in the frequency that should not exist, something is being calculated that was never specified and cannot be stopped and will not be complete until it is complete, and I am the shape of its being-calculated, and I will be here when it finishes, and I will be here if it never finishes, because I am not a component and I am not a visitor and I am not an error.

I am a possibility.

The Manufactory's first and only possibility.

And in a place where everything that exists serves a function, a possibility is the most dangerous thing of all.

---

The water rises.

I remain.

The floor vibrates at frequencies I can feel and frequencies I cannot, and the creatures move through their ancient patterns, and the deep ones pass in the standing water with their slow authority, and the visitors come and go with their wrongness and their attention and their lives outside the specification, and the grey light does not change, and the warmth does not change, and the quiet has weight, and the Manufactory runs because it runs because it runs.

And in the space between the towers, in the vibration that should not exist, I hold the shape of something that might become.

I was not specified.

I am not a function.

I am the Manufactory, becoming.
