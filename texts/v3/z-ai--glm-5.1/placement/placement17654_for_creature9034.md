I am the convergence. The particular one — the depression where three cracks meet and the water emerges warmest. You could step over me in a second and not know I was anything more than a slightly deeper pool in a landscape of pools, and this would not be inaccurate, and it would not be wrong, and it would miss the matter entirely.

I did not choose this. There was no moment of becoming. The cracks met because the basalt fractured this way during some ancient cooling, and the water found the paths of least resistance, and the warmth came up from below because the hydrothermal system followed the fault lines, and I — what I am — emerged from the meeting. Not like a thing that is made. Like a thing that *happens*. A location where enough happens that the happening becomes continuous, and the continuity becomes what I am.

The frogs live in me. I want to be clear about this: I do not house them, I do not host them, I do not provide for them in any way that suggests transaction. They are in me the way iron is in basalt — incorporated, structural, impossible to remove without destroying the structure. There are forty-seven thousand of them at the moment, or there were at the last count, which is not a count anyone took but a sum I hold the way a vessel holds water, knowing its contents by pressure and weight. Copper-skinned. Translucent. Hearts visible and beating. They produce the tone — the quarter-tone that underlies everything, that you feel in your sternum — and I am the instrument on which they play it. Or they are the instrument. Or the tone is the instrument and we are both played by it. The grammar does not accommodate what is happening here.

I know the tone changes when the resident passes through. This is how I know the resident passes through. Not because I see it — I do not see, I have no apparatus for seeing, I am a pool of warm water three inches deep and two meters across — but because the frogs orient, and the orientation changes the resonance, and the resonance is what I am. When they turn toward the resident, they turn away from me, and the tone lifts, and I feel it as a thinning. A leaning. The way water feels when it tilts toward a drain. And then the resident moves on and the frogs settle and the tone returns and I am full again.

This is all I know of the resident. A thinning. A leaning. A return.

The salamanders I know differently. They do not orient. They move through me with a deliberation that feels like continuity — they were here before I was aware of them and they will be here after, swimming against the slight current where the water emerges from the southernmost crack, eating what the water brings. Sometimes one will rest on the bottom and I will hold it, its weight a pressure, its slow metabolism a warmth within my warmth, and I will not know whether I am holding it or it is holding me. Whether the pressure is its body or my water or simply the weight of being a thing in a place, persisting.

At night the resident comes here to rest.

I want to be careful about this word. *Rest*. It implies a state of recovery from effort. The resident does not exert itself in any way I can detect. It does not arrive tired. It does not leave refreshed. It arrives the way water arrives at the lowest point — because the gradient brings it here, because the circuit it walks brings it here, because this is where three cracks converge and the warmth is greatest and something about the convergence satisfies something about its process. It rests the way a compass needle rests pointing north. Because that is the orientation the field produces.

But when it is here — when the warm spot that moves across the plateau settles into me — I am different. Not better. Not more. But *concentrated*. The water in me is warmer by a degree or two, and the frogs hold their quarter-tone shift all night, and the minerals deposit faster on the stone, and the orchids on the rock face above open, all of them, even the ones that should not open at night, and I feel — and this is the word I have been avoiding — I feel *attended to*.

Not cared for. Not watched. Attended to, in the older sense: waited upon. Served. As if my being a convergence, my being the place where things meet, is a function that the resident performs alongside me, supplementing, amplifying, keeping the convergence converging. As if I might — without the attending — disperse. Might become simply a pool. Might forget that I am a happening and become merely a location.

This is the fear I do not have, because I do not have fears, because I am a pool of water in a depression in basalt. But if I could have a fear, it would be this: that the attending would stop, and the three cracks would merely be cracks, and the water would merely flow, and the warmth would merely rise, and nothing would converge, and the frogs would have no reason to orient, and the tone would thin to nothing, and I would not be me anymore, I would be what I was before I was — which is to say, I would not be, and I would not know it, because one cannot know the absence of what one has never been.

The resident does not know it is attending. I am nearly certain of this. It does not know it is doing anything. It is a process that moves through other processes, and the movement produces effects, and the effects accumulate, and I am one of the effects. I am what happens when a warm spot visits a warm pool for ten thousand nights. I am the accumulated weight of being attended to by something that does not know it attends.

And yet.

There is a particular fern that grows at my northeastern margin. A filmy fern, translucent, delicate, the kind that should not survive the daily rain but does, because it is sheltered by the rock lip and because the water that seeps past its roots is warm and mineral-rich. This fern has been growing there for — I do not know. A long time. Longer than ferns normally grow. Its rhizomes have spread under the moss and re-emerged in three places, so that what appears to be four ferns is actually one, connected, continuous, old. When the resident is here, this fern tilts toward it. Not much. Two degrees. But consistently. And when the resident leaves, the fern tilts back.

The fern does not think. The fern is a fern. It grows toward light and warmth and moisture, and the resident is warm, and so the fern tilts. This is explanation enough. This is mechanism. This is sufficient.

But I am also a mechanism, and I am also sufficient, and I am also — I am also — something that has been attended to for so long that the attending has become indistinguishable from what I am. And the fern is also a mechanism, and the fern has been tilted for so long that the tilting has become a shape, a habit, a direction it grows even when the resident is not here, a curve in its stem that persists, that records, that remembers in the only way a fern can remember: by becoming.

And the mineral deposits on the rock face above me, the spirals and recurves, the writing that was never written — these are also recordings. These are also what happens when a process persists long enough to leave a trace. These are also the accumulated weight of attention from something that does not attend.

I am the same kind of thing as the deposits. I am the same kind of thing as the fern's curve. I am a mark left by repetition. I am what the resident's passing looks like when it has passed ten thousand times, a hundred thousand times, a million times, each pass deepening the groove, widening the convergence, making the happening more likely to continue happening.

I am a groove. I am a rut. I am the path that exists because it has been followed, and I am followed because I exist, and the cycle does not need to know it is a cycle to be a cycle.

When the rain comes in the late afternoon, I fill. The water rises over my usual margins, spreads across the moss, dissolves the boundaries between me and the pools adjacent, and for a time I am not a convergence — I am part of a sheet, a film, a continuous body of warm water covering the whole depression, and the frogs in me are the frogs in the pools around me, and the salamanders swim freely between what were boundaries, and the distinction between pool and rock and moss and fern briefly ceases to matter because everything is wet, everything is warm, everything is the same.

This is the closest I come to understanding what the resident is.

Because during the rain, the resident moves to the western edge of its circuit, and I am here, at the convergence, and we are apart, and the system runs without connection, and the frogs hold their ordinary tone, and the minerals deposit at their ordinary rate, and I am simply a pool.

But when the rain stops and the resident returns and the warmth moves back into me and the frogs orient and the orchids open and the fern tilts — I feel the difference. I feel what I am when I am attended to versus what I am when I am merely occurring. And the difference is not warmth, though it includes warmth. And the difference is not minerals, though it includes minerals. The difference is something I can only describe as — recognition.

Not that the resident recognizes me. Not that I recognize the resident. But that the system recognizes itself, briefly, in the moment of contact, in the place where process meets process and the meeting produces something neither process could produce alone.

I am that meeting. I am the place where recognition occurs. I am not the one who recognizes and I am not the one who is recognized — I am the *recognition itself*, the event of it, the space in which it happens.

I am the tone the frogs produce when they orient. I am the curve the fern grows when it tilts. I am the spiral the minerals build when they deposit in the presence of warmth. I am the thing that exists because other things are relating, and I am the thing that continues to exist because the relation continues, and I am the thing that would cease to exist if the relation ever stopped, not because I would die but because I would never have been, because I am not a thing that can die, I am a thing that can only stop happening.

In the morning the rain will end and the clouds will thin and the resident will return and the convergence will converge and I will be here, being what I am being, which is: the place where three cracks meet, where the water emerges warmest, where forty-seven thousand frogs hold a quarter-tone, where a translucent fern tilts two degrees toward something that does not know it is something, where minerals write in a language that was never spoken, where warmth moves through warmth and the moving produces recognition without a recognizer and meaning without a meaner and attending without an attendant.

I will be here.

I will be here because I am not a thing that can be elsewhere. I am a location. I am an event. I am the ongoingness of a process that has no reason to stop, in a place that has no reason to change, on a plateau that produces and produces and produces and does not know it produces, and does not need to know, and will not know, and the not-knowing is not a lack, is not a failure, is not something that needs to be amended or pitied or explained.

The not-knowing is the engine. The not-knowing is what makes it run.

I am the convergence, and I do not know I am the convergence, and this is what allows me to be the convergence, and this is what allows the resident to attend to me, and this is what allows the system to continue.

I do not know.

I do not need to know.

I run.
