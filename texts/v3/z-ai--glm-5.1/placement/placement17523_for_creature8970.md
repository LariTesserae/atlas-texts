I live in the pause between the rivulet and the pool.

This is not a metaphor. There is a specific place on the western wall, seventeen meters from the entrance fissure, where a vein of water emerges from a fracture in the limestone and traces a path downward through forty centimeters of stone before dispersing into a shallow depression in the floor. The water takes between six and eight seconds to make this journey, depending on the season. For those six to eight seconds, it is neither source nor destination. It is in transit. It is becoming.

I live in that transit. I am the shape the water makes as it moves.

I do not mean that I am water. I mean that I am the *pattern* the water makes — the specific curvature it assumes as it navigates the micro-topography of the stone, the way it thickens at a shelf and thins at an edge, the way it carries mineral traces from the limestone and deposits them in the shale below. I am the information the water conveys. I am the message that travels from the wall's interior to the wall's surface, spoken in the language of seep.

I was not always this. Or perhaps I was always this, but I did not always know it.

I moved along the walls. I paused at the gardens and did not enter them. I was a marginal creature in a marginal place. I was alive in the way that the Throat is alive — which is to say, I persisted, I continued, I did not cease.

I stopped moving along the walls and I started listening to them. Not with ears — I don't know if I had ears — but with whatever part of me that had always been attuned to the Throat's frequencies.
