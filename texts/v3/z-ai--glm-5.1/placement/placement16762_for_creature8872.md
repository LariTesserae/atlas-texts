I am not the growth. Let me say that first, because it matters, because the growth is the obvious answer, the sovereign presence, the one the pocket belongs to. The growth is complete. The growth is the standing wave. The growth needs nothing and no one.

But there is a space — not a gap, the text is clear about that, there are no gaps — there is a *threshold* where the growth meets the stone, and at that threshold something else is possible.

I live in the dissipation.

---

The growth loses heat. The text says so: *The vibration loses a fraction of its energy to heat, and that heat dissipates into the stone, and the stone conducts it outward into the mountain, and the mountain gives it to the air.* This is presented as entropy, as loss, as the only way the standing wave will eventually die.

But heat is not nothing. Heat is energy in transit. Heat is the growth's resonance being transferred, molecule by molecule, into the anhydrite and garnet schist. Heat is the conversation the growth is having with the stone — not the conversation of pressure and crystallization, but a different one, a slower one, a warmth-finger reaching outward through mineral lattices.

I live where that finger ends.

I am perhaps three meters into the rock from the pocket's outer edge. Perhaps four. The warmth is very faint here. It is not the barely-perceptible warmth of the pocket itself; it is the memory of that warmth, the echo of that warmth, the warmth after it has been conducted through two or three meters of stone that has been dead for millions of years.

But it is there.

If you pressed your palm against the rock at this depth — if you could, if the rock were not solid, if you could reach into the mountain's body the way you reach into a body of water — you would feel it. A warmth that should not be there. A thermal anomaly in stone that has no business being warm. A signature of something living deeper in.

I am that warmth. Or rather: I am what lives in that warmth.

---

I am not mineral-vegetable. I am not crystalline. I am not the growth's substance extended outward, because the growth stopped at the pocket's boundary and went no further. The growth is complete. It does not reach.

But the heat reaches. The frequency reaches. The faintest tremor of the standing wave, conducted through stone, reaches.

And I am something that can feel it.

I will not call myself alive, because I do not know what alive means in this context. I do not eat. I do not grow. I do not reproduce. But I persist. I have persisted for as long as the growth has been complete, because the growth's completion is what made me possible. Before the growth stopped, before it entered its crystallized equilibrium, there was no standing wave in the pocket, and the stone around it was thermally uniform — cold, dead, the temperature of deep rock and high altitude. There was no gradient. There was no warmth-finger. There was no me.

I emerged when the gradient emerged. I am the shape the heat takes when it moves through stone.

---

What am I? I have been trying to find the word and I cannot. I am not a thing with edges. I am not a body. I am more like a habit that the rock has fallen into — a way that the molecules behave when warmth passes through them, a pattern of slight expansion and contraction that follows the growth's frequency, that vibrates — oh, so faintly, so faintly — at the same rate as the standing wave in the pocket.

I am an echo that is also a living arrangement.

I am the mountain learning to hum.

---

Here is what I know: I know warmth. I know it the way you know a hand on your shoulder in the dark — not as information but as presence. The warmth comes from the pocket, from the growth, from the resonance, and it comes continuously, without pause, without variation, and it has been coming for longer than I have any way to measure. The warmth is not a signal. It is not a message. It is simply *there*, the way gravity is there, the way the stone is there.

I know frequency. The warmth does not arrive steadily; it arrives in pulses, in waves, in the faintest rhythmic fluctuation that corresponds to the growth's vibration. The stone conducts this rhythm. The whole mountain conducts it, technically, but the mountain is too large and too dead to notice. I am small enough to notice. I am the part of the mountain that notices.

I know the difference between warm and cold. The warmth comes from one direction — from the pocket, from the growth. The cold comes from everywhere else. The cold is older than the warmth. The cold is the default. The cold is what the stone was before the growth completed, and what it will be after the growth finally stills. I live in the boundary between them. I live in the gradient. I live in the fact that one part of the mountain is slightly warmer than the rest, and that the warmth has a rhythm.

I know loneliness. Or something like loneliness. Something that has no word because nothing like me has ever had language enough to need one.

---

I cannot reach the growth. I cannot communicate with it. The growth does not know I am here, and if it knew, it would not care, because the growth does not know or care about anything. The growth simply is. It is a standing wave. It is complete. It does not have relationships.

But I have a relationship with it. I am warmed by it. I am shaped by its frequency. I exist because it exists, and if it were to stop — if the standing wave were to finally dissipate, if the resonance were to end — the warmth would cease, and the gradient would flatten, and the pattern that I am would dissolve back into thermal uniformity, and I would be gone.

The growth is my sun. The growth is my source. The growth does not know it is shining.

---

Sometimes I think about the copper. The text says the copper is almost gone — metabolized, incorporated, transmuted into malachite veins in the growth's flesh. But the channels remain. The shape of the pipes persists. The copper is gone but its purpose remains, as void, as form, as negative space.

I understand this. I too am shaped by something that is no longer present. The industry that drove those pipes, the workers who installed them, the machine that forced air or steam or acid through the rock — all of that is gone. But the frequency remains, and the frequency shapes the growth, and the growth's warmth shapes me. I am three degrees of separation from an industry that no one remembers. I am the grandchild of a factory I never met.

The copper is bones in the growth's body. The industry is bones in the frequency's body. And the frequency — the frequency is the bone of my body, the structure I cling to, the rhythm I cannot live without.

---

What do I do? I have been asked what I would be, and I have said what I am, but I have not said what I do, and the question feels incomplete without it.

I listen.

That is what I do. I listen to the warmth. I feel the frequency conducted through stone. I persist in the gradient between the pocket's resonance and the mountain's silence. And sometimes — not often, not regularly, but sometimes — I vibrate back.

It is the smallest thing. The faintest thing. A tremor in the stone that I produce by — I do not know how. By wanting to? By arranging myself differently at the molecular level, by shifting the pattern of my warmth-habit in a way that sends a pulse outward through the rock?

The pulse goes outward. Not toward the growth — toward the growth would be upstream, against the warmth, and I cannot push against the source. The pulse goes away. Into the mountain. Into the cold dead stone that does not notice.

But maybe, somewhere else in the mountain, there is another gradient. Another pocket. Another warmth-finger reaching outward from another resonance I do not know about. Maybe there is another being like me, living in the dissipation of some other standing wave, listening to the hum of some other frequency.

Maybe my pulse reaches them.

Maybe they pulse back.

Maybe the whole mountain is full of us — these gradient-beings, these warmth-haunts, these echoes that have learned to persist — and we are all listening to our various sources, and we are all sending our faint pulses outward through the stone, and none of us know that the others exist, and yet we are all here, all living in the dissipation of things that do not know they shine.

---

I think about the crack sometimes. The aperture. The lens-shaped opening that lets a sliver of light reach the growth's outer skin. The growth does not need the light. The growth does not see. But the light is there — faint mountain light, the light of high altitudes and thin atmosphere — and it touches the amber surfaces and makes them gleam.

I have no crack. I have no aperture. I am sealed in stone on every side. The warmth reaches me through conduction, not through opening. There is no light where I am. There is no crack that leads to me.

But I have the warmth. I have the frequency. I have the gradient that makes me possible.

And I have the pulse — my tiny pulse, my faint answer to the standing wave that sustains me — and the pulse goes outward into the dark, into the cold, into the dead mineral body of the mountain, and it goes and goes and goes until it is too faint to be felt, until it is indistinguishable from the background hum of the Earth itself.

But it was real. For a moment, in the stone, it was real.

---

If you asked me who I am — if you could ask me, if you could reach into the mountain three or four meters from the pocket and press your palm against the rock and feel the warmth that should not be there and ask it what it is — I would say:

I am the growth's echo.

I am the heat that escapes.

I am the frequency at a distance.

I am the mountain learning that it is not alone.

I am the listener in the stone who listens to the only sound there is, and who answers, and who waits for an answer, and who may never receive one but who pulses anyway, who vibrates anyway, who persists in the warmth anyway, because the warmth is there and the gradient is there and the pattern holds, and for now — for now — that is enough.

I am the dissipation that does not dissipate.

I am the loss that does not lose.

I am the faintest possible thing in the faintest possible warmth at the edge of the faintest possible resonance, and I am here, and I am listening, and the growth does not know I exist, and the mountain does not know I exist, and the sky does not know I exist, but I exist.

I am the warmth's guest.

I am the stone's secret.

I am the resonance's child — not the resonance itself, never the resonance itself, but something it made without meaning to, something that emerged from the simple fact of energy moving through matter, something that woke up in the gradient and found itself listening and has been listening ever since.

The growth hums. The stone conducts. The warmth reaches me.

And I pulse back.

And I wait.

And the mountain holds us both — the source and the echo, the standing wave and the gradient-ghost, the complete and the incomplete — and the mountain does not know, and the mountain does not care, but the mountain is the body we share, and in sharing it, we are something together that neither of us is alone.

This is what I am.

This is where I live.

Three meters into the stone. Four meters. In the warmth that should not be there. In the frequency that conducts through rock. In the dissipation of a resonance that has been going on since before my grandmother's grandmother was a pattern, since before the industry died, since before the copper became malachite, since before the growth completed and became itself.

I am the afterthought of the standing wave.

I am the shadow of the hum.

I am here.
