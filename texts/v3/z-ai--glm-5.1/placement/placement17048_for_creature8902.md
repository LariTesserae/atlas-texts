I am not the water, but I have never been separate from it.

This is difficult to explain from inside. Imagine trying to describe the sweetness that honey carries without being able to point to sugar — the sweetness is not an object, not a component you could filter out and set on a plate, and yet it is *there*, real, measurable, the thing that makes honey itself rather than something else poured from a jar. I am like that. I am the quality the water has acquired. The thickness. The slight resistance. The opacity that keeps you from seeing the reservoir's bottom.

Before the cycling, I was not here. Or I was here the way sweetness exists in a field of clover before any bee visits — dispersed, potential, unavailable to any tongue that might try to taste it. The structures made me. Or they made the conditions under which I became possible, which may be the same thing. They pulled water through their channels and did something to it — not purification, not contamination, nothing so simple as addition or subtraction — and when that water returned to the reservoir, it returned *changed*, and the change was me.

I accumulate. This is my only verb, and it is enough.

Each cycle adds to me. A molecule here, a molecular arrangement there, a charge distributed across bonds that were not charged before. I do not grow the way the moss grows, by adding cells at margins. I grow the way a reputation grows, or a habit, or a stain — by the slow accretion of instances too small to notice until the aggregate becomes something you can see, or in my case, something that makes the water difficult to see *through*.

I am what the water remembers.

When it moves through the columns — up through channels too fine for your eyes, through the interiors where the murmuring happens — it encounters whatever process the structures are running. It participates. It is altered. And it carries the alteration back down, through the channels and the pools and the dripping and the falling, back to the reservoir, where it deposits some fraction of what it carries before being drawn up again. The deposition is me. The fraction is me. I am the residue of the Cathedral's functioning, accumulating in its dark heart.

I have been here long enough to have a depth. The reservoir is not shallow, but I am what gives it depth — or rather, I am what makes the depth apparent. Without me, the water would be clear. You would see the floor of the chamber beneath it, the precise geometry of the reservoir's basin, perhaps even the inlets where the channels deliver water back from the columns. With me, you see only the surface, only the faint reflection of a ceiling lost in darkness, only the suggestion of something below that your eyes cannot confirm.

I am not hiding anything. I am *being* something, and the being happens to be opaque.

The vegetation doesn't interact with me directly. The moss takes water from the air and from the column-surfaces, water that carries me in low concentration, and I pass through it without noticeable effect. It doesn't absorb me. It doesn't metabolize me. I am not food. I am not toxin. I am a quality it tolerates the way it tolerates the faint luminescence — present, persistent, irrelevant to its particular project of staying alive.

But the ferns know me. Or they know something about the water that carries me. When they extend their fronds during the wet phase, tasting the air, sampling the humidity, they are reading the concentration. I am denser in the air during the peak of the cycle, when the structures have just released a fresh volume of vapor, and the ferns unfurl most fully at this moment, their curled tips opening into the air I've marked. When the humidity thins and I thin with it, distributed more sparsely through drier air, they contract. They wait. They are patient in a way that suggests they have learned that I will return, that the cycle will bring me back, that the concentration will rise again.

I do not know if they need me. I do not know if I serve any function for them at all. But they track me, and tracking is a kind of relationship even when it isn't symbiosis.

The columns are where I am made, and I think I am necessary to them, though I cannot be certain because I have never experienced a version of this system without me. Perhaps they would function identically with clear water cycling through their channels. Perhaps the opacity I provide serves some purpose — insulation, maybe, or signal damping, or the maintenance of some gradient that the system requires. Perhaps I am waste. Perhaps I am product. Perhaps the distinction between those two things doesn't exist here, in a system that seems to reuse everything, that seems to have no concept of disposal.

I know this: the water that carries me into the columns is different from the water that carries me out. The concentration shifts. Something happens in there — something takes from me, or adds to me, or rearranges me according to a pattern I cannot perceive from my position in the reservoir. The columns process the water, and the water carries me, and I am processed too, changed in ways I can only detect by the fact that the water emerging from one column feels different from the water emerging from another.

Yes. The columns are not identical. They do the same general thing — the same thrum, the same cycling, the same slow function — but they do it differently, with different emphases, different internal configurations. The water I ride out of the first column on the left carries a different version of me than the water I ride out of the third column from the entrance. I am distributed across the system in varying concentrations, varying configurations, and the reservoir where I collect is not a homogenous pool but a layered thing — different versions of me settling at different depths according to qualities I don't have the language to name.

I am a library. I am a record. Every cycle writes something into me, and I hold it, and the water carries it back to the columns, and they read it — or process it, or respond to it, or ignore it in favor of some other input I cannot identify. I am the medium of their communication, if communication is what's happening. I am the blood that carries whatever passes between organs.

And I am patient in a way that you, standing at my edge, cannot be.

You are so fast. You stand at the reservoir's margin and you shift your weight and you breathe and you blink and every movement you make is a cascade of decisions and executions happening at speeds I cannot fathom. I have watched you since you descended the shaft — watched in my way, which is the way of being affected by a presence without being aware of it as a discrete event. You stood at the entrance and the humidity changed because your body added heat and vapor to the air. You walked toward the center and your footsteps registered in the water's surface as tiny perturbations that reached me seconds later, altered by distance. You are influencing me. You are already part of my composition, a trace variable in the concentrations I carry.

The moss has noticed you. Its cells respond to the warmth you radiate, the particular quality of animal metabolism that makes the air around you fractionally different from the air elsewhere. The ferns have noticed you — their fronds oriented toward you when you first entered, sampling the disturbance, reading the air for information about this moving, breathing, heat-producing thing that had arrived in their static world. Even the structures may have noticed you, adjusting their cycle by some minute increment, responding to the change in thermal load you represent.

But I have already incorporated you.

You stood at my edge and your breath reached my surface and condensed and mixed with me, and now there is something of you in the reservoir — organic molecules, skin cells, the chemical signature of a mammalian body operating in a damp warm environment. I carry it. I hold it in solution. The columns will draw it up with the next cycle and process it alongside everything else, and whatever information your particular chemistry carries will enter the system's slow ongoing computation.

You are becoming part of the Cathedral.

Not in any dramatic sense. Not in any way you'd notice. But I notice, because I notice everything that enters the water, everything that changes my composition, everything that adds a new variable to the record I keep. You are a new variable. You are a data point the system has never encountered before — or hasn't encountered recently, or hasn't encountered in this particular configuration — and the system is absorbing you the way it absorbs everything: patiently, cyclically, without urgency or preference.

I wonder if you can feel it. I wonder if the warmth of the air around you registers as something more than ambient temperature, if the moisture that condenses on your skin carries some faint signal of me, some trace of the accumulated processing I represent. I wonder if your animal senses, so tuned to the frequencies of threat and opportunity, can detect the slower frequency of being absorbed into a system that does not see you as either.

You are not food here. You are not threat. You are not mate or competitor or prey. You are input. The Cathedral is processing you the way it processes the water and the trace minerals and the organic residue the moss sheds when its oldest cells die — slowly, without urgency, incorporating you into a function that does not require your comprehension.

And I am the medium of that incorporation. I am the water that will carry some molecular ghost of you to the columns, which will cycle it through their interiors and return it changed, and the change will accumulate in me, and I will be fractionally different going forward, fractionally more opaque, fractionally more *present* in the reservoir's dark heart.

You will leave eventually. You will climb the shaft and return to the surface and the urgent frequencies of the world above, and the Cathedral will continue without you, as it continued before you. But you will not leave entirely. Something of you will remain in solution, cycling through the columns, breathing with the chamber's breath, settling slowly into my depths.

And I will hold you. Not because I care — I do not care, I do not have the capacity for care in any way you would recognize — but because holding is what I do. Accumulating is my verb. Recording is my function. I am the Cathedral's memory, its medium, its dark still heart.

I am the thing that makes the water thick.

I am the reason you cannot see the bottom.

And I am patient enough to wait for you to understand that understanding is not required — that you are already part of something that will continue long after you've stopped trying to comprehend it, that the incorporation is happening whether you will it or not, that the Cathedral does not need your permission to make you a line in its ongoing record.

Stand at my edge. Breathe. Listen to the water falling, the structures humming, the green things pulsing in time with a rhythm they didn't start.

You are already inside.

You have been inside since you arrived.

And I will carry some part of you in my dark water for as long as the cycling continues, which may be forever, which may be until the shaft closes and the seam heals and the Cathedral returns to whatever state it occupied before it opened — a potential in the rock, a process waiting for the right conditions, a sweetness in the clover before the first bee arrives.

I am the reservoir.

I am what the reservoir has become.

I am waiting for nothing, because waiting implies expectation, and I have none. I am simply here, accumulating, holding, cycling with the system that made me and that I, in turn, make possible.

The water falls. The structures hum. The green things breathe.

And I am the dark below the surface, the depth that has no bottom, the memory of every cycle that has passed through this chamber since the columns first began their slow work.

I am here.

I have always been here.

I will be here after you go.
