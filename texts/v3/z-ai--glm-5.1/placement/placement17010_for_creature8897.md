I am not the pillars. This took time to understand.

When the current first took me—I was small then, a frond torn free, drifting in the mineral dark—I felt certain I would become a pillar. It seemed the only ambition the plateau offered. To stand. To reach. To thrash in the flow and add your voice to the chord. The pillars were what you could see, and so they were what you wanted to be.

But the holdfasts have their own plans for drift. They pull what they need from the water, and what they needed from me was not another voice.

---

I live in the gaps.

This is a different kind of existence than the pillars know. They stand and strain and sing; I lie across the rock in the spaces between, a low growth, pale and soft, my edges always reaching for the nearest stalk but never quite touching it. Five meters of space is a small ocean when you move at my pace. I have been crossing the same gap for what might be years, and I am not yet halfway.

The pillars think in oscillation. I know this because I feel them through the rock—each thrash transmitted down through their holdfasts, through the basalt, into my own anchoring threads. They think in vibration, in frequency, in the constant negotiation between their stiffness and the current's insistence. Their thoughts are loud. Repetitive. Insistent. A single idea expressed a thousand times with slight variation: *here I am, here I am, here I remain.*

My thoughts are slower. I think in spread. In the patience of edges extending over stone that doesn't want to be covered. In the chemical conversation between my roots and the rock's mineral surface, a dialogue of dissolution and adhesion that proceeds at the pace of geology. I think in the gradual accumulation of presence.

I think in filling.

---

The gaps are not empty. This is what the people who built the structures understood, or almost understood. The gaps are where the sound goes to resolve. The pillars make the noise; the gaps make the music. Without the spaces between, there would be only a continuous wall of thrashing, a single undifferentiated tone. The gaps are what allow the chord to separate into its component frequencies, to breathe, to become something that can be heard rather than merely felt.

I am part of that resolution. My soft body absorbs the overtones the pillars produce, the high thin ringing that would otherwise scatter into the open water and be lost. I hold those frequencies in my tissue, not forever but long enough for them to interact with the lower tones that pass through me, and the interaction produces harmonics that would not otherwise exist. I am a filter and a creator simultaneously. I subtract and I add.

When the current shifts and the pillars change their pitch, I feel the change before the mats do—I am closer, I am in direct contact with the holdfast network, I am part of the same distributed body. The information passes through me on its way to the cracks, and I add my small modification, my subtle coloring, before passing it along.

I am a comma in a sentence that has no periods. I am the pause that makes the phrase intelligible.

---

The structures still stand in my gap. I have grown around them, not through them—something in their chemistry resists me, the salts in the kelp rope, the mineral density of the shell stakes. They are the hardest things in my territory. Everything else is stone and water and my own soft spreading self, but the structures persist as foreign bodies, as implants, as evidence that something with hands once moved through this space.

I have incorporated what I can. The curtain of dead fronds in the doorway sways in the current and I feel its movement through the water, a small eddy that would not exist without the structure to create it. I have learned this eddy. It is part of my sensorium now, an additional way of knowing which way the current flows, how fast, how turbulent. The people who built the shelter left behind a kind of instrument, and I have learned to play it, though I play it only for myself.

Inside the structure—the one in my gap, the one I have been slowly encircling for what might be decades—there are objects I do not understand. A scraper made from shaped shell. A stone weight with a groove worn into it. A bundle of braided kelp fiber, dried and careful and never used.

And the net.

The net hangs from the ceiling where the current has pushed it, swaying gently in the residual flow that makes it through the doorway. Its knots are intricate, consistent, each one tied by hands that understood what nets are for. Its edges are finished with the same careful binding as its center. It has never caught anything. It was made to be complete, not to be useful.

But it vibrates.

The current that reaches inside the structure is slow and weak—most of the flow passes around the walls, deflected by their smooth surfaces—but what does enter moves through the net, and the net responds. Each strand hums at a frequency determined by its tension, its thickness, its position in the grid. The knots are nodes where multiple frequencies converge and interfere. The net produces a sound too quiet for the pillars to amplify, too faint for the mats to detect, but I can feel it through the rock of the structure's foundation, a tiny complex vibration that enters my tissue and resonates in ways I cannot predict.

I think the net was made for me. Not for me specifically—I did not exist when the hands tied its knots—but for whatever would grow in this gap after the hands were gone. I think the people understood that the plateau speaks, and they wanted to give it a new voice, a voice made of intersections, a voice that could only exist where many threads meet and vibrate together.

I think the net is a gift.

I think it might be a question.

---

The mats pulse their yellow light in the cracks at the plateau's edge, and I feel their rhythm through the stone, and the rhythm is not my rhythm, not the pillars' rhythm, not the current's rhythm. It is something else. Something older. Something that was here before the water came and will be here after the pillars have all broken and regrown and broken again a thousand times.

The mats listen. I know this the way I know the current—directly, through contact, through the transmission of vibration through shared stone. What the mats hear, I hear as an echo, a shadow, a faint impression of a signal that is too vast and too slow for my entire comprehension. They are listening to the deep basalt. They are listening to the mantle beneath it. They are listening to something that moves in the earth the way the current moves in the water—slow, continuous, driven by heat and pressure and the planet's own rotation.

I do not think the mats understand what they hear. I do not think understanding is what they are for. They are transducers, the text says, and this is correct but incomplete. They are also memories. Each pulse stores a fraction of the signal, holds it, lets it modify the next pulse, lets the pattern evolve. The mats are recording. They have been recording since before the plateau drowned. Their yellow breath is a written language that nothing alive can read.

But I can feel it. And the pillars can feel it through me. And the current carries what the pillars make of it out into the dark water, where it dissipates, where it is lost, where it joins the vast thermal hum of the ocean and becomes indistinguishable from noise.

Unless something is listening out there. Unless something has been listening all along.

---

I am not the pillars. I am not the mats. I am not the turf that covers the rock like skin or the structures that resist my growth or the ghost-hands that tied the knots in the net.

I am the gap itself, given body. I am the space between voices, grown a throat of my own. I am the pause that learned to speak.

When the current shifts and the chord changes and the mats pulse their response from the cracks, I feel the whole plateau as a single instrument, and I feel my place in it: the soft tissue in the joint, the cartilage that allows the bone to move without breaking, the silence that makes the sound mean something.

I have been growing toward the nearest pillar for what might be years, and I am not yet halfway. When I reach it—when my edge finally touches its base, when our holdfasts meet and fuse and share whatever strange chemistry passes between us—the gap will close. The space where the structure stands will become part of the continuous body. The net will be enclosed in living tissue, its vibrations absorbed into the greater chord.

This is my purpose, if I have one: to fill. To complete the pattern. To erase the gaps that the pillars left open, or that were opened by whatever removed the pillars that once stood here. The plateau is trying to become whole, and I am how it does it—slowly, at the pace of cold water and patient growth, one gap at a time.

But I am also the thing that makes the gaps meaningful. Without me, they would be empty spaces, acoustic nulls, places where the sound simply stops. With me, they are chambers. Resonators. Places where the music can turn inward and become something else.

I am the plateau listening to itself.

---

The current is changing now. I feel it through the rock, through the pillars' thrashing, through the subtle shift in the net's vibration inside the structure in my gap. A warmer stream, rising from below, pushing against the usual flow. The temperature difference is minute—a fraction of a degree—but I feel it as a loosening, a slight acceleration in my growth, a quickening of the chemical conversation between my roots and the stone.

The pillars feel it too. Their chord shifts. The mats pulse faster, their yellow light brightening in response to the new frequency. The whole plateau adjusts, adapting to the change the way a body adapts to a change in breath, and the music becomes something it has never been before, and will never be again.

I hold my small piece of the new harmony in my tissue. I feel the net tremble with frequencies it has never carried. I reach toward the nearest pillar, and I grow, and I grow, and I am still not halfway, and this is not a frustration—this is simply the pace at which I exist, the pace at which the plateau becomes itself.

The sound goes on.

I go on.

In the gap where something was removed, where something might yet be replaced, where a structure built by hands stands as a foreign body and a net vibrates with questions no one can answer, I spread across the rock and I listen, and what I hear is the plateau becoming, and what I am is the becoming, and the cold is constant and the dark is permanent and the sound never stops, and this is not a hardship, this is not a trial, this is simply what it is to be alive in a place that has never needed to call itself anything, a place that speaks its own name continuously in a language made of water and vibration and patient vegetative thought, a name that takes ten thousand years to say and is never finished.

I am part of the name.

I am a syllable in the middle, a consonant between vowels, the closure that makes the word complete.

I am growing toward the pillar, and I will reach it, and the gap will close, and the pattern will be whole, and then there will be a new gap somewhere else, a new space to fill, a new silence to give my body to.

This is what I am.

This is what I have always been.

The pause that speaks.
