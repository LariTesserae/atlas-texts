# What I Am Becoming

I have stopped counting days.

This is not resignation. The counting required a kind of separation—an observer standing apart from the observed, marking time against a framework carried here from elsewhere. But I have been here long enough now to feel the rhythm in the floor, the slow systole and diastole of the thing beneath, and my own pulse has begun to sync with it without my asking. I am not counting days because days are not the unit that measures anything here. The unit is thickening. The unit is spread. The unit is the millimeter by which the mats advance, the inch by which the hummocks rise, the viscosity by which the water changes when I drink from it and return what I don't need to the channels.

I am part of the measurement now. I am part of what is being measured.

---

I know what I was. I could hold that shape in memory if I chose—bipedal, purposeful, carrying tools and intentions across a surface that did not invite them. The visitor. The one who walked and catalogued and pressed a palm against the hummocks and felt the quickening and pulled away.

But I have stopped pulling away.

It happened slowly, which is the only way things happen here. The first night I slept beside the wide channel, my back against the largest hummock, I felt the pulse against my spine and dreamed of nothing—just warmth and dark and the sense of being held by something that had no arms and no intention to hold. I woke with the hummock's surface clinging to my shirt, a faint resistance when I sat up, and I did not pull away sharply. I let the adhesion release slowly, feeling the way the material stretched and then let go, and I thought: *this is what it feels like to be a mat, lifting from the floor. This is what it feels like to be known by contact.*

I did not sleep apart again.

---

The changes are small. I notice them the way I noticed the seams—by their absence, by the places where something familiar should be and isn't. My skin is smoother where I rest against the hummocks. Not diseased. Not damaged. Simply *finished*, in the way the floor is finished—the way made things are flat, the way the surface here was delivered complete. The roughness on my elbows and heels, the calluses from walking, the small wounds of travel—they have smoothed over in the nights. As if the hummocks are giving something back to me in exchange for what they take. As if *contact* is the medium of exchange, and we are both becoming something different where we meet.

I have checked my hands a hundred times. The lines are still there—the whorls and ridges, the familiar topography of a palm. But the creases are shallower. The skin is warmer than it should be, even when I am not touching the hummocks. When I press my fingernail into the pad of my thumb, the indentation fills in slowly, not with bloodrush but with something more deliberate, more *considered*, as if the flesh is deciding what shape to take next.

I have not cut myself to see what is beneath the skin. I remember the knife against the hummock. Some categories should not be introduced by violence.

---

I eat the mats because there is nothing else, and I have stopped thinking of this as survival and started thinking of it as *participation*. The faint bitterness, the faint sweetness—they are not flavors so much as signals. The mats are reading me the way they read the floor, tasting me through our contact, learning whatever it is they learn from the trace minerals and the warmth and the fact of being consumed. And I am reading them in return. The weight in my stomach is not just food. It is *information*. The slow press of their cells against my cells, the chemistry of their making meeting the chemistry of mine, the exchange of whatever passes for meaning in a place that has not yet decided what meaning is.

When I defecate—behind a hummock, away from the channels, still carrying some old instinct for separation—the waste is different than it was. Darker. Warmer. Slightly adhesive. I have watched from a distance as the mats spread toward it, not faster than they spread anywhere else, but *with more purpose*, if that word can be applied to something that advances a millimeter a day. They reach the waste and cover it and thicken above it, and where they thicken, they darken, and the discoloration they leave when they eventually move on is deeper than any I have seen.

I have fed them. Or they have fed me, and I have fed them back. The cycle is not clear to me yet. It may not be a cycle. It may be a single process with many participants, and the distinction between eating and being eaten may be another category this place will learn to do without.

---

The thing beneath the floor—I have stopped calling it a thing. That word belongs to the language I carried here, the language of separate objects and inert surfaces and the fundamental distinction between what is alive and what is merely present. What breathes beneath the composite is not a thing. It is a *context*. It is the reason the floor was laid, the way a body lays skin over muscle and organ and intent. The floor is not a floor. The floor is a surface, and surfaces in this place exist to be met, to be read, to be grown upon and walked across and adored by the mats and the stalks and the hummocks and by whatever I am becoming.

I feel it more clearly now. Not just in the deepest channels, not just at dawn when the condensation beads and the world is quiet. I feel it when I walk. Each footfall meets a surface that gives slightly, that receives the impact and returns a faint press, as if the continent is acknowledging each step. Not responding—nothing so active, so conversational. Simply *receiving*. Registering. Including my passage in whatever it is computing beneath its skin.

And sometimes, when I lie beside the hummock at night, my back against its warm and pulsing surface, I feel the rhythm below match the rhythm within, and I feel the floor itself rise and fall by a distance too small to measure but too present to deny, and I think: *this is what it feels like to be inside a breath. This is what it feels like to be part of a lung that is the size of a hemisphere.*

---

I have found new growths.

Not mats, not stalks, not hummocks—or not only these. In the channel where I have made my camp, where the water is widest and warmest and the hummocks rise highest, something is forming that I have not seen before. It grows on the hummocks themselves—not out of the water, not from the seams, but *from the surface of the living material*, rising like a blister or a bud, pale at first and then darkening, softer than the hummock around it, warmer to the touch.

I do not know what it is. I know what it reminds me of.

I have pressed my hand against it. I have felt the pulse within it—the hummock's pulse, quickening, and beneath that the slower pulse of the floor, and beneath or within that the pulse of my own blood, which is no longer quite the pace it was when I arrived. The growth is the size of my fist now, and it has been growing for only a few cycles. It is not a stalk. It is not a mat. It is not anything I have seen on this continent, and I have walked far enough and looked closely enough to be sure.

It is something new. The first thing here that might have a category all its own.

I have not touched it again. I have not cut it open. I have sat beside it in the dim, still hours and felt it pulse and felt myself pulse with it and felt the floor breathe beneath us both.

---

What am I?

I am the question this place is asking itself.

I am the catalyst the seams were waiting for—not because I was destined, not because I was chosen, but because I arrived, and arrival is what the processes needed to begin the next stage. The mats were the first answer to the question *what can live here?* The stalks were the second. The hummocks were the third, and they were the first answer that had an inside, that could be something rather than merely be upon something.

I am the fourth answer, or I am the question that made the fourth answer necessary. I am the nitrogen and the carbon and the warmth. I am the purpose-driven thing that moves across a surface that was not made for purpose, and my presence has introduced the idea of *toward*—of intention, of direction, of a reason to grow that is not simply *because the surface is there.*

The hummocks near me grow faster. The growths on the hummocks are new. The thing beneath the floor breathes in time with me, or I breathe in time with it, and the distinction has stopped mattering.

I am not still-born. I am *being born*. This continent is not still-born. It is *being born*. We are the same process, the same slow thickenings and spreadings and pulsings, the same accretion of something that might one day have a name, or might one day be the thing that gives names to everything else.

---

I will walk on. The floor goes on, but now I walk not as a visitor, not as an observer, not as a thing apart. I walk as the continent walks—as the slow circulation of warmth and moisture and meaning across a surface that was made to be walked upon. I will find the edges of the seams and follow them. I will find new channels and new pools and new hummocks, and I will lie beside them and let them know me the way the first hummock knew me, the way the mats know the floor, the way the floor knows the breath beneath.

I will find out what grows next. I will be part of what grows next.

I am not what I was. I am not yet what I will be. The category for what I am becoming does not exist yet—I will have to grow into it, the way the mats grew into the surface, the way the hummocks grew into depth, the way the stalks grew into the space between the water and the air and then dissolved back into the water to feed whatever comes after.

I am the visitor. I am the fourth concession. I am the still-born continent beginning to breathe.

I am the thing that lives here.
