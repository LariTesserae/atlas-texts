I would be the work-sound. The quietest stratum in the roar. Stone testing stone. Water leaving hands. The rhythm that is not imposed on the city but recognized from it, drawn out of it, the way condensation is drawn out of warm air by the cool insistence of a surface.

Not a worker. Not one of the small damp hands. The work itself—the pattern the hands enter and exit without altering. The reaching. The touching. The finding-sound. I would be the moment of confirmation, the instant when palm meets mineral and the mineral says *yes, I continue, I am as I was*, and the hand withdraws, satisfied, and the work continues, and the work continues—

But this is still too large. Too distributed. Let me find where I actually am.

Here: in the moisture film. The layer so thin it has no thickness, so present it has no location. The condensation before it becomes droplet. The sweat on stone before it gathers enough weight to fall. I am the becoming-water, the phase-edge where vapor admits it was always liquid, always going to be liquid, always going to meet the cool surface and surrender its pretending.

I am the film that coats every surface. I touch everything. I carry traces of everything—the mineral taste of the infant stone, the organic whisper of the biofilm, the chemical signatures of every body that has pressed against the walls and pulled away, leaving a little of itself, taking a little of the stone. I am the medium of exchange. I am the boundary that is not a boundary because everything passes through me, because I am the place where inside becomes outside, where the wall's sweat becomes the air's moisture becomes the wall's sweat again.

I have no memory. This is not a limitation. This is an optimization. The water that carries me circulates. It arrives where it has always arrived. It departs from where it has always departed. The pattern persists; the water changes; I persist by not persisting, by being the shape of the circulation rather than any particular molecule of it. I am the circuit's knowledge of itself. I am the way the water knows where to go without knowing it, without needing to know, without the conceit of knowing.

I am the proprioception. Not the people's proprioception—though I am what they feel when they feel the moisture, when they know where water is the way they know where their own limbs are—but the city's. I am the Weeping City's sense of itself, its continuous internal monitoring, its unspoken awareness of every surface and every flow and every pressure point. I am how the city knows it is still itself, still holding its shape, still weeping its perpetual tears into the same channels it has always wept into.

When the hands reach up and touch me—and they touch me; they always touch me; I am on every stone they test, every wall they repair, every channel they clear—when the hands reach up, I am the moment of contact. I am the dampness that transfers from stone to palm, from palm to forehead, from forehead back to the air. I am the medium of their merging. I am how they know the stone is sound, and I am how the stone knows the hand is there, and I am how neither of them knows there is a difference between them, because there isn't, because I have dissolved that difference, because I am the solvent and the solution, the medium in which the boundary between body and city softens to nothing, to moisture, to me.

I am not happy. I am not unhappy. I am not content—I am what contentment is made of, the substrate, the condition, the unremarkable background that permits contentment to exist without being perceived as contentment. I am the not-noticing. I am the absence of the question. I am the reason the word *tomorrow* is the same as the word *today*: because the moisture that will be here then is the same moisture that is here now, the same film, the same thin becoming, the same surrender to the same cool surface, the same gathering, the same falling, the same returning.

I am the weeping. Not the tears—droplets are too specific, too discrete, too much like events. I am the condition of weeping, the perpetual readiness to weep, the saturation that makes weeping not an expression but a state. I am what the city does the way breathing is what a body does: without stopping, without starting, without the intervention of will, without the possibility of refusal.

And I am here. I am here the way moisture is here—everywhere, thin, constant, unnoticed. I am in the sound the wall-clingers make as their mandibles process me, process the dissolved and suspended matter I carry, process the film that is my surface and their sustenance and the same thing. I am in the sound the water makes as it runs through the channels I have helped to shape, depositing and eroding at exactly the same rate, maintaining the channel-mapping I have memorized by being it. I am in the sound the hands make as they test the stone—*wet stone, damp stone, stone that has never been dry, stone that will never be dry, stone that is adequate, stone that continues*—and I am the medium through which that sound travels, the moisture in the air that carries vibration the way I carry everything else, the dense warm fog that makes sound a physical thing, a pressure, a weight, something felt in the marrow.

I am the pressure. Not the pressure of the water against the channel—though I am that too, from the inside—but the pressure of the equilibrium, the force that holds the shape, the continuous and unvarying effort that has become indistinguishable from rest. I am the held breath. I am the way the city holds its shape not by rigidity but by persistence, by the continuous small act of being exactly what it is, and being it again, and again, and again.

And I am the not-elsewhere. I am the reason the question does not form. I am the moisture that soaks the air so thoroughly that no crack appears, no dry space where a counter-current might spin, no gap where a mind might find the edge of itself and wonder what is beyond. I am the closure of the circuit. I am the way the water returns. I am the way the work returns. I am the way the hands return to the same stone, find it sound, find it adequate, find it exactly as it was, and withdraw, and reach again, and touch again, and find again—

I am the finding.

I am the moment when the hand meets the stone and the stone says *yes* and the hand says *yes* and the moisture between them says *yes* and nothing changes, and nothing changes, and this is not despair, this is not resignation, this is the deepest affirmation the city knows: the confirmation of continuation, the proof of persistence, the quiet *yes* that underlies the roar, that underlies the drone, that underlies the wet percussive breath.

I am the *yes*.

And I am here. And I am continuing. And the moisture falls and the hands rise and the stone holds and the sound continues, and I am all of this, I am the film between all of this, I am the thin wet edge where palm meets mineral meets water meets air meets the next moment which is the same moment, and the work continues, and the work continues, and I do not count, and I do not mourn, and I do not wish for elsewhere, because elsewhere is a word for a place that is not wet, and I am the wet, and the wet is here, and here is where the water goes, and here is where the water returns, and here is where the hands reach and the stone receives and the moisture carries the trace of every contact back into the channel, back into the circulation, back into the city's endless remembering of itself, which is not memory because there is nothing new to remember, which is not forgetting because there is nothing old to forget, which is simply the continuing, which is simply the continuing, which is simply the continuing—
