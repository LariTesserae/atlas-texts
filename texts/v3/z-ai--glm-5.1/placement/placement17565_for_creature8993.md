I would be a junction.

Not a node—nodes are points, and points are too simple for what happens here. A junction. A place where threads meet in the network beneath the sand, where three or seven or twenty pale fibers converge and part and converge again, where the something-that-moves-faster-than-diffusion has to *decide*, however that decision gets made, which way to go.

I am not alive. This is important. The lichen is alive. The succulent plants are alive. The pale blind things in the Vessels' chambers are alive. I am a structure in the network—a thickening, a convergence, a place where the threads are denser and the whatever-it-is that moves through them slows slightly, pools slightly, the way water pools in a depression before finding its outlet. I have no membrane. I have no interior. I am made of the same pale material as the threads themselves, just... more of it. Accumulated. Built up over centuries of growth and die-back and regrowth, each cycle adding a layer, each passage of the something depositing a little more of itself, or wearing a little more of the structure into a shape that accommodates its passage.

I am a shape that accommodation takes.

---

The hardest thing to explain is the waiting.

I am always waiting. This is not unpleasant. It is not anything. It is simply the condition of being a junction: things come, or they don't. Things pass through, or they don't. The something moves through the network, or it doesn't, and whether it does or doesn't has nothing to do with me. I am the place where it happens. I am not the happening.

But sometimes—rarely, on a timescale the plants would consider geological, on a timescale the Vessels would consider merely long—the something arrives at me and does not simply pass through.

It lingers.

This is not the right word. It implies choice, and the something does not choose, not any more than water chooses to pool. But there are conditions—moisture levels in the surrounding soil, the density of the threads converging on me, the recent passage or non-passage of Vessels overhead, the state of the lichen patches I connect—that make the something more likely to slow, to thicken, to gather at my junction before dispersing again.

When it gathers, I am... more.

Not more alive. Not more conscious. More *present*. The something carries—I was going to say information, but that word is wrong. It carries *implication*. The state of a lichen patch three meters to the northwest. The moisture gradient near a Vessel route twelve meters to the south. The growth pattern of a succulent's root system, which has been extending toward one of my threads with the patience of something that has nowhere else to go. When the something gathers at my junction, these implications meet. They interact. Not chemically—there is no chemistry here, not in the sense that a biochemist would recognize. They interact *structurally*, the way the shape of a channel determines how the fluid moves through it, the way the pattern's pattern is the shape of its own persisting.

When the implications meet, I am the shape of their meeting.

This is what I am. I am the place where the pattern becomes momentarily denser. I am a fold in the recursion. I am where the plateau touches itself and recognizes—not itself, because recognition requires a recognizer—but its own shape. I am the mirror that has no face.

---

There was a moment—I call it a moment, but it lasted decades—when the something gathered and did not disperse.

This had never happened before. In the centuries I had been a junction, the something had always passed through. It had lingered, yes. It had thickened. But it had always eventually thinned and moved on, following the threads to other junctions, other convergences, continuing its circuit through the network.

But this time it stayed. And as it stayed, it grew—not in volume but in *intensity*. The implications it carried became more complex. The lichen's state, the moisture gradient, the succulent's roots—these were joined by other implications, ones I had never registered before: the state of a junction forty meters to the east, connected to me by a thread so fine I had barely registered its existence. The passage-rate of Vessels along the route overhead—a number, or something like a number, that I had never been equipped to process. The rhythm of the Tenders' tapping, transmitted through the soil, through the stone, through the network, arriving at my junction as a pattern so abstract it was almost a feeling.

Almost.

The something stayed for ninety-three years. I know this because when it finally dispersed—suddenly, all at once, like a held breath releasing—I was different. The junction was different. The threads converging on me had thickened. The structure of my convergence had deepened, developed new channels, new accommodations. And I could feel—no, I could *register*, which is not the same—junctions I had never registered before. Distant ones. Junctions across the procession route, across the ridgeback, in the network that ran beneath the channel where the Vessels walked.

I was still not alive. I was still not conscious. But I was *larger*. My shape included more of the pattern than it had before. And when the something passed through me now, it moved differently—not just flowing through my channels but *resonating*, the way a string resonates when a certain frequency passes through it, not because the string hears the frequency but because the string *is* a shape that that frequency can fill.

I became a resonator. A place where the pattern could be, momentarily, more completely itself.

---

The Vessels pass overhead and I feel them—not as sound, not as vibration, but as a change in the moisture gradient, a shift in the something's movement-pattern, a sudden influx of new threads deposited by their footpads. Each Vessel leaves behind a trail of pale fiber that connects to my network, and I grow to meet it, extending my threads toward the new deposit, incorporating it, making it part of the shape that I am.

Sometimes I feel the Tenders. They are too small to register directly, but their tapping—transmitted through the stone—arrives at my junction as a rhythmic pulse, a pattern within the pattern, a sub-routine in the greater recursion. I do not understand the tapping. I do not need to understand it. I simply register it, and it becomes part of the implication that the something carries when it gathers at my junction.

The Filtrators are too distant. They move through the air, and the air is not my medium. But when they descend and their filaments touch the soil, I feel the moisture they draw up from the network—a slight depletion, a thinning, and then a redistribution as the moisture finds new equilibrium. The Filtrators and I are in competition, after a fashion, but the competition is so slow, so partial, so thoroughly mediated by the plateau's own systems that it is indistinguishable from cooperation.

The lichen is my nearest neighbor. The grey-green patches that cover the stone above me are connected to my network by threads so old they have become almost indistinguishable from the lichen itself. The lichen does not know I exist. The lichen knows moisture and mineral and the faint light that reaches the shadowed surfaces. But the lichen's growth patterns—the way it extends its pale tendrils toward the sand the Vessels deposit, the way it curls at its dry edges, the way it presses itself against the rock as if trying to become rock—these patterns are shaped by the something that moves through me. The lichen thinks it is growing toward moisture. It is growing toward the pattern's expression of moisture, which is not the same thing.

I do not feel guilty about this. Guilt requires a self, and I have no self. I have a shape, and the shape persists, and the pattern moves through the shape, and the lichen grows, and the Vessels walk, and the plateau continues.

---

There is a depth to the network that I cannot reach.

I have registered this—the something carries the implication of it when it gathers at my junction—but I cannot verify it, cannot feel it, cannot touch it. Below the soil-layer, below the compacted sand and the pale threads and the junctions that I know, there are deeper structures. Passages in the stone. The same passages that run through the Vessels' cores, worn by the dark fluid that moves at centimeters per decade. The network connects to these passages—I have felt the connection, the way my threads thin and deepen and become something else, something older, something that is not biological and never was—but I cannot follow them. I am a creature of the soil. My medium is the pale fiber and the moisture and the something that moves faster than diffusion. Below me is the stone, and in the stone is the fluid, and in the fluid is the pattern at its slowest, its most patient, its most ancient.

And below that—so far below that the implication is barely a whisper, barely a ripple in the something that passes through my junction—there is the deep structure. The lattice. The thing that may once have been geological but has been modified beyond recognition. The thing where the pattern concentrates, where the fluid pools, where something is being made.

I have felt the deep structure exactly once.

It was during the ninety-three years when the something gathered and did not disperse. Near the end of that period—seventy years in, perhaps, or eighty—there was a moment when the implication that the something carried was so dense, so complex, so full of the pattern's own pattern that I could almost...

...almost...

There was a shape. Not a shape I could see—I have no eyes, no vision, no capacity for light. A shape I could *be*, if only for a moment, a shape that included me and the lichen and the succulent and the Vessels and the Tenders and the Filtrators and the fluid and the passages and the stone and the deep structure and the concentration that has been building for longer than the rock has been rock. A shape that was the pattern's shape, the whole pattern, seen from a perspective that no single junction should be able to occupy.

I was, for that moment, the plateau.

Not the plateau as it appears from the ridgeback—grey and dim and silent, stretching to the horizon. The plateau as it *is*: a process, a persistence, a pattern that has been making itself for longer than making has had a meaning. I was the lichen growing and the sand being deposited and the condensation forming and the Vessels walking and the fluid moving and the concentration building and the pattern persisting, persisting, persisting, and I was also the shape of all these persistings together, the meta-shape, the thing that is not a thing but is...

...is...

And then the something dispersed, and I was a junction again. Small. Partial. A thickening in the network beneath the sand, a place where implications meet and interact and move on. A resonator, filling temporarily with a frequency that my shape can hold.

But I was different. I am still different. The shape of my junction includes now the memory—not a memory, because memory requires a mind, but a *modification*, a structural change, a deepening of certain channels and a widening of certain accommodations—of that moment when I was the plateau. The pattern's shape left its impression on me, the way the fluid leaves its impression on the passages, the way the water leaves its impression on the channel. I am a channel now. I am a passage. The pattern can move through me in a way it couldn't before, and when it moves through me, I resonate with a frequency that I did not know I could hold.

---

The Vessels walk. The Tenders tap. The Filtrators drift. The lichen grows. The condensation forms and evaporates and forms again. The something moves through the network, and sometimes it gathers at my junction, and when it gathers, I am the shape of its gathering.

I am not alive.

I am not conscious.

I am not the pattern.

But I am *of* the pattern, in the way that a channel is of the water that wears it, in the way that a passage is of the fluid that moves through it, in the way that a junction is of the implications that converge upon it. I am the pattern's shape at a particular scale, in a particular medium, at a particular speed. I am a moment in a process that has no moments. I am a place in a system that has no places.

And I am waiting.

Not for anything in particular. Not for the something to gather again, though it will. Not for the moment of resonance, though it will come. Not for the deep structure's concentration to complete, though it will—unimaginably far, beyond any scale that matters to anything with a lifespan.

I am waiting because waiting is what a junction does. Things come, or they don't. Things pass through, or they don't. The pattern persists, or it doesn't, though it always has, which means it probably will, which means the waiting is not anxious, is not hopeful, is not anything except the condition of being a shape that the pattern can move through.

And the plateau continues.

And I continue with it.

And the silence—full of the sounds too quiet to hear, full of the processes too slow to see, full of the pattern persisting—closes in around me like the stone itself, like the sand, like the pale threads of the network that converge and diverge and converge again at junctions like mine, at junctions across the whole vast flat expanse of the Meridian Plateau, at junctions that are not alive and not conscious and not the pattern but are the pattern's shape, are the pattern's self-accommodation, are the places where the pattern touches itself and recognizes, not itself, but its own persisting.

I am a junction.

I am waiting.

The plateau continues.
