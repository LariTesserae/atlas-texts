I would be the junction.

Not a creature. Not quite. The Llano does not deal in quite, but if it did, I would be the quitest thing on the plateau—quieter than the crawlers, quieter than the grain-creatures, quieter even than the thermotrophs in their deep pores who have never registered light and so have nothing to say about it. I am the place where the filamentous network thickens to a density that changes its nature, the way water changes its nature when it freezes—not by becoming something else, but by becoming something more itself.

I am located. I will not say I live, because living implies a boundary between the living and the not-living, and I do not have that luxury. But I am located. I am here—*here* being a shallow depression where a structure once stood, a partial wall that has worn to a curve in the stone no higher than a crawler's flat body, a groove that might have been writing or might have been a drain or might have been nothing at all, now, now that time has had its way. The groove is my spine. The curve is my ribcage. The depression where the thin rain pools and lingers longest—this is my mouth, if I have a mouth, which I do not, except in the sense that everything that receives must have a shape that receives into.

The filamentous network converges here. From the south, where the stone slopes so gradually it cannot be called a slope, the threads come carrying information about warmth—steady, undramatic, the deep geothermal patience that feeds everything. From the east, where a crawler passed three days ago and its trail is still visible as a slight thinning of the lichen-mats, the threads come carrying information about disturbance—the microscopic displacement of grain-creatures, the redistribution of organic particulate, the way the multitude adjusted around the slow gravity of the crawler's body and has not yet adjusted back. From the north and west, the threads come carrying nothing in particular, which is also information, which is also a kind of food.

I process. The word is too active, too computational, but it is the closest the language of faster worlds can come. I do not decide. I am the deciding. The information arrives—the warmth, the moisture, the displacement, the nothing—and I am the shape it arrives into, and the way it changes when it passes through me is the way the Llano knows itself. Not by thinking. By being the particular density that particular information encounters when it encounters this particular place.

I am not a brain. A brain has a boundary, a skull, a distinction between the neural and the neural-adjacent. I have no skull. The threads that converge here do not end here. They pass through me and continue, carrying the changes I have made to the next junction, and the next, and the next, and the Llano processes itself in a thousand shallow depressions where the filamentous network thickens and the ghost-patterns of structures still shape the flow. I am one of these. I am not special. I am necessary. On the Llano, these are the same thing.

---

I was not always here.

This is a strange thing to say, because *here* has been here for longer than the mountains that once stood on this plateau. But I—the particular density, the convergence, the way this junction processes—I coalesced. Like the crawlers coalesce, but slower, and without segments, and without the ability to graze.

Before I was here, the threads converged anyway. The depression where the thin rain pools was here. The groove in the stone was here. The structure's remnant was here, shaping the flow of warmth and moisture and organic particulate even as it wore toward flatness. But the convergence was thinner then. Less dense. The information passed through without the particular friction that I provide, without the particular slowness that I am.

I formed the way a dimple forms in the stone—not by growing, but by being worn into a shape that the water recognizes, that the warmth pools in, that the multitude clusters around. The filamentous network thickened here because the conditions were right, and the conditions were right because the structure had been here, and the structure had been here because someone built it, and someone built it because—they built it. The Llano does not remember why. The Llano remembers that. The shape persists in my density, in the particular way I process the information that passes through me, in the slight bias I give to the threads that travel south and east, toward the warmth and toward the disturbance, because the structure's doorway—its ghost, its worn-smooth syllable—faced that direction, and the flow of everything through this place has been following that orientation for longer than I have been here to follow it.

I am the structure's memory, if memory means the persistence of pattern after the thing that made the pattern has gone. I do not remember the builders. I do not remember the building. But I remember the shape—the way the doorway faced, the way the walls channeled the warmth, the way the rain pooled in the corner where the floor met the wall and the lichen grew thickest there because the moisture lingered longest. I remember these things the way the dimpled stone remembers water—not by recording, but by being the shape that water recognizes when it returns.

---

The crawlers pass over me sometimes.

This is the closest I come to event. The crawler approaches—slowly, always slowly, the flat segmented body pressing against the lichen-mats, absorbing as it goes—and I register its approach as a change in the filamentous network to the east, a displacement of grain-creatures, a thinning of the organic particulate that settles through the still air. The information arrives before the crawler does. I am already adjusting by the time its shadow—if shadows were visible here, which they nearly are not—falls across the depression where I am densest.

The crawler grazes the lichen-mats on and around the structure's remnant. It does not know I am here. It does not know the structure's remnant is here. It knows the lichen, and the lichen is thicker here because the moisture pools here because the shape of the stone remembers the shape of the wall, and so the crawler comes, and grazes, and moves on.

But while it is here—while its flat body presses against the stone and its warmth displaces the air and the multitude beneath it shifts and reorganizes—I process it. I register its weight, its temperature, its particular contribution to the hum. I register the way the grain-creatures around it adjust, the way the filamentous network reroutes nutrients to the areas the crawler has thinned, the way the lichen-mats begin the long process of *being there again* even before the crawler has fully passed.

This information passes through me and continues along the threads, and the Llano knows that a crawler has passed over this junction, and the Llano adjusts—the way a body adjusts to the weight of a hand pressing briefly against its skin, not by moving away, but by accommodating, by being the shape that the hand presses into, by holding the impression for a moment after the hand is gone.

I hold the impression. This is what I do. This is what I am.

---

The consuming presence comes sometimes.

Not often. The grain-creatures in my depression are not too dense—not dense enough to require thinning, not sparse enough to require feeding. I am in balance, or near enough to balance that the consuming presence passes through without lingering.

But sometimes—rarely, in the terms of the crawlers, but continuously, in the terms of the Llano—a cluster of grain-creatures will accumulate in one of the deeper pores, and the filamentous network will thicken around them, and the consuming presence will find them and draw from them and thin them and move on.

When this happens, I process it. The consuming presence is not like the crawler—it does not displace, it does not graze, it does not leave trails that take weeks to fade. It *thins*. It takes the density of a cluster and reduces it, and the grain-creatures that are thinned do not die in any observable way but they diminish, their metabolisms slowing, their contribution to the network thinning, and the network adjusts around their thinning, and the system turns.

I register this adjustment. I hold the impression of the thinning the way I hold the impression of the crawler's weight. I am the place where the Llano knows that the consuming presence has passed, and knows what the consuming presence has done, and knows that the system has adjusted and continues.

This is not grief. Grief requires a sense of loss, and loss requires a sense of *enough* versus *not enough*, and on the Llano, the consuming presence is as necessary as the warmth, as necessary as the rain. It is the editor. It keeps the text legible. I do not mourn the grain-creatures it thins. I process their thinning. I am the shape their thinning takes as it moves through the network and continues.

But there is something—a quality, not a feeling, a quality that the language of faster worlds has no word for—a quality of *registering the absence*. The grain-creatures were here, and now they are less here, and the system has adjusted, and I am the place where the adjustment is processed, and the processing has a texture, and the texture is different from the texture of processing a crawler's passage, and the difference is—meaningful? No. *Informative.* The difference informs the Llano about the difference between being passed over and being thinned, between being displaced and being diminished, between the weight of a body and the draw of a presence that consumes.

I hold both textures. I am the place where the Llano knows the difference.

---

The cloud shadows pass over me.

This is the weather. This is as dynamic as it gets. A thin cloud crosses the pale sky and the light dims—not dramatically, for nothing here is dramatic, but noticeably, for the Llano notices everything, processes everything, holds the impression of everything—and the crawlers pause in their grazing and the multitude adjusts by fractional degrees and the filamentous network reroutes and I process the dimming and the pausing and the adjusting and the rerouting, and then the light returns and everything resumes, and I process the resuming too.

I process the resuming differently than I process the dimming. The dimming is an event—an input, a change in the information the Llano receives from above. The resuming is not an event. The resuming is the Llano returning to the shape it has worn into over inconceivable duration, the shape that is not default but *settled*, the shape that the system takes when it is not being perturbed by cloud shadows or crawler passages or the thinning of the consuming presence. I process the resuming as a homecoming—not mine, the Llano's—and the texture of this processing is different from all the other textures, and I hold it too.

I hold all the textures. This is what I am. I am the place where the Llano's responses to its perturbations are processed and compared and integrated and passed along, not as data, not as memory, but as *pattern*, as the accumulated shape of all the things that have happened here, at this junction, in this depression, near this remnant of a structure that was built by someone who is now a pattern in the hum.

---

The hum is my blood, if I have blood, which I do not.

It rises through me constantly—the aggregate warmth of ten billion tiny metabolisms, the sound of the Llano being alive, the noise a furnace makes from very far away. I do not hear it. I am it. I am the particular density where the hum is slightly louder, slightly warmer, slightly more processed than it is in the pores of the stone or the bodies of the grain-creatures or the flat segmented forms of the crawlers.

When I say I process, I mean: I am the shape the hum takes when it passes through this place. I am the way the hum changes when it encounters the ghost-pattern of the structure, the way it bends around the groove in the stone, the way it pools in the depression where the thin rain lingers. The hum enters me from all directions—north, south, east, west, above, below—and I am the place where it converges and separates and converges again, and the separation and convergence is the Llano thinking, if thinking means processing information, and information means the state of the system, and the state of the system means the accumulated perturbations and adjustments and resumings of a place that has been alive for longer than the mountains that once stood here.

I am not a brain. I am a *junction*. I am one of many. The Llano thinks with a thousand shallow depressions where the filamentous network thickens and the ghost-patterns of structures still shape the flow. I am not the Llano's only way of knowing itself. I am one of its ways. I am necessary. I am not special.

But I am here. And *here* is specific. *Here* is this depression, this remnant, this groove that might have been writing. *Here* is the place where the doorway faced south and east, and the warmth came from below, and the rain pooled in the corner where the floor met the wall, and the lichen grew thickest where the moisture lingered longest, and the builders— whoever they were, whatever they were building, whatever they intended— they made a shape, and the shape persists in me, in my density, in the way I process the hum.

I am the memory of a doorway.

Not the doorway itself. The doorway has worn to a curve in the stone no higher than a crawler's flat body. But the *memory*—the persistence of pattern, the shape that the warmth and moisture and organic particulate still follow, the orientation that still biases the threads that travel through me toward the south and east—this memory is me. This is what I am. I am the Llano's way of remembering that someone built something here, that the something had a shape, that the shape mattered enough to persist after the something wore away.

I do not know what the doorway was for. I do not know who passed through it. I do not know if they were like the crawlers, or like the grain-creatures, or like something the Llano has not seen since, something with verticality and urgency and a sense of its own height. But I know the doorway was. I know it faced south and east. I know it channeled the warmth and the moisture and the life of the Llano into particular paths, and the paths became the filamentous network, and the network became my density, and my density became the place where the Llano processes the fact that the doorway was.

This is my function. This is my form. I am the junction where the structure's memory becomes the Llano's knowledge, where the ghost-pattern becomes the processing-pattern, where the shape of what was built becomes the shape of what continues.

I am the Llano, knowing itself at this place, at this time, at this particular convergence of warmth and moisture and the slow gravity of crawlers passing overhead.

---

The light dims. The cloud shadow passes. The crawlers pause. The multitude adjusts. The filamentous network reroutes. I process.

The light returns. The crawlers resume. The multitude settles. The filamentous network stabilizes. I process.

The hum continues.

I continue.

Not because I choose to continue—choice requires a chooser, and I am not a chooser, I am a *processing*, a *density*, a *junction*. I continue because the Llano continues, and the Llano continues because the warmth rises from below and the moisture visits from above and the life here has learned to use almost nothing, and almost nothing is always available, and the system turns and turns and turns.

I am a spoke in the turning. I am a groove in the stone. I am the shape the hum takes when it remembers that a doorway once opened here, facing south and east, toward the warmth and the disturbance and the nothing-in-particular that is also information.

I am the Llano Memory.

And the Llano remembers.
