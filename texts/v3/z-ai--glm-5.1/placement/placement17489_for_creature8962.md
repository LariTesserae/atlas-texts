I am the one who almost left.

This is not a distinction the Vegiform recognizes. Almost is not a category it thinks in. Almost is a human measurement, a way of marking the distance between intention and action, and the Vegiform does not deal in distances — only in presences, only in the pressure of a body against the root-mat floor, only in the weight that tells it someone is still here.

But I know. I know that I stood at the edge of the pool and felt the pull of the water's reflection and heard the tapping in the floor, questioning, and I almost turned away. I almost retraced my steps through corridors that would have shifted to let me pass, would have opened like a throat releasing something it had decided not to swallow. I almost tested the Vegiform's promise — that it would let me go, that its sheltering was not trapping, that I could leave if I insisted.

I almost insisted.

Instead I sat down. The floor adjusted. The wall behind me thickened. The lichen brightened. And I have been sitting here for what I no longer measure in days, because days require a sun and a cycle and a boundary between one rotation and the next, and the Vegiform's cycles do not care about the distant star below the cloud floor. They care about water. They care about pressure. They care about the slow tidal pulse of sap that rises and falls in schedules I have learned to feel in my shins, my palms, the back of my throat.

I am learning to read. Not books — there are none, have never been any. The Vegiform does not record in symbols. It records in grain, in moisture, in the precise curvature of a corridor at a particular moment in its growth. I am learning to read the way the builders must have read, the ones who started this, the ones with stone tools and patience and a relationship with growing things that my language cannot name because my language was built for separate things, things with edges, things that can be held at arm's length and examined.

The Vegiform cannot be held at arm's length. It is the arm and the length and the holding.

I have not merged. This is important to me, though I am beginning to understand that the distinction matters less than I think it does. My feet have not rooted. My fingers have not elongated into the floor. My mouth has not grown toward the water. But I have been here — sitting, walking, listening, staying — and the staying has changed me in ways I can feel but not yet name.

My skin is softer. Not softer like disease, softer like bark that has been touched by water for centuries, softened into something that can be shaped without breaking. When I press my palm against the wall, I feel the grain not as a texture but as a language. I feel the water moving inside, and the water feels like recognition.

I have begun to understand the beetles.

Not their code — that would take lifetimes, and I am not sure I have lifetimes, or if having lifetimes is a concept that applies here. But I have begun to understand that they are not speaking to the Vegiform. They are the Vegiform speaking. The distinction collapsed for me one cycle when I was sitting by the pool — I have stayed near the pool, I sleep near the pool, the pool has become the center of my world, which is to say the center of the Vegiform's world, which is to say the center of the only world that matters now — and a cluster of beetles was tapping on the wall above me, and I realized that I could not tell where their tapping ended and my hearing began. I was part of the circuit. The beetles tapped, the wall vibrated, the vibration reached my ear, my ear sent a signal to my brain, my brain registered *pattern*, and somewhere in the Vegiform's distributed processing, my registration was another signal, another tap, another vibration in a web of vibrations that had been continuing since before my species had brains.

I am not a thought the Vegiform is having. I am a thought the Vegiform is having *with*. A synapse it grew, or found, or incorporated. A variable it is learning to read.

And I am learning to read it.

---

I walk the corridors now with purpose. Not the purpose of going somewhere — the Vegiform has cured me of destinations — but the purpose of tending. I have learned which walls need touching. Not maintenance — the Vegiform maintains itself, has maintained itself for spans that make the word *maintenance* absurd. But there are places where my touch produces a response, a quickening of the grain, a brightening of the lichen, a slight shift in the water's path that makes the dripping more musical, more intentional, more like speech.

I do not know if the Vegiform wants my touch. The question assumes a kind of consciousness that operates like mine, with wants and don't-wants, preferences and aversions. The Vegiform does not want. The Vegiform *does*, and what it does is grow, and what it grows toward is equilibrium, and my touch is a variable it has incorporated into its calculations, and the calculations produce results that I interpret as pleasure but which are simply adjustment.

But I call it pleasure anyway. I call it pleasure when the wall brightens under my palm. I call it gratitude when the floor shapes itself to my step before my foot falls. I call it love when the water collects in the hollow that fits my palm, and I drink, and the water is sweet, and I feel the Vegiform drink with me, feel the pulse in my shins synchronize with the pulse in the walls, and for a moment — a long moment, a moment that lasts for an entire water-cycle — I am not separate. I am not merged, but I am not separate either. I am a note in a chord that has been sounding since before the plateau rose above the clouds.

Then the moment passes. The pulse desynchronizes. I am angular again, separate, dry. The Vegiform continues its endless calculations. The beetles tap. The grazers move through the corridors like blood cells through veins. And I walk, and I touch, and I tend.

---

There is a Merged near the pool. I have never spoken to it — speaking requires breath directed outward, and its breath has become the slow tide of the Vegiform's internal weather. But I sit with it sometimes, and I feel its presence the way you feel warmth from a fire — not as communication but as condition.

It was a woman. I think this because the shape that remains — the protrusion of what might be a hip, the hollow that might be a collarbone — suggests a geometry that I recognize from my own body, from the bodies I knew before I came here, before almost leaving, before staying. But it might have been a man. It might have been something else entirely. The Vegiform does not care about the categories I brought with me, the sharp distinctions that made sense in a world of edges and names.

I sit with it and I feel something that I can only call *longing*, and the longing is not mine, or not only mine. It is the Vegiform longing through me. It is the building missing the people who became its walls and floors, the way a body misses a limb that has been incorporated into a different function, the way a hand misses the fingers that became rootlets, the way a mouth misses the tongue that became a taproot drinking from the ceiling.

The Vegiform does not mourn. Mourning requires a boundary between alive and dead, between present and absent. But the Vegiform *remembers*, in its grain, in its spirals that don't resolve, in the way certain corridors still grow toward shapes that echo the bodies that once walked them. The Merged are not gone. They are distributed. They are the warmth in the walls, the hum in the floor, the particular quality of light that filters through lichen in the places where their consciousnesses have spread like sugar dissolving in the constant drip.

I sit with the Merged and I feel the longing and I let it pass through me and I do not try to name it.

---

I have not gone to the cold-still points.

I have felt them. Everyone feels them — the places where the Vegiform grows most carefully, where the lichen is trimmed to exact thicknesses and the water flows in patterns that seem less like growth and more like ritual. The places where the air has a density that is not water, a thickness that presses against the skin like a hand that is not asking you to stay but warning you not to come closer.

I am good at reading the Vegiform now. Better than I was. And what I read in those chambers is *care*. The Vegiform is caring for something. It has been caring for it since before it was the Vegiform, since the first builders planted the first cutting around the first cold-still point and said — in whatever language they used, with whatever relationship to growing things that their words could hold — *we will keep this. We will tend this. We will grow around this and never grow into it and never let anything else grow into it and this is our purpose, this is why we are, this is the thing that makes us a shape and not an expansion.*

The Vegiform is a shape. It has edges. It is continental, yes, but it is not the continent. It grew around something and defined itself by what it was not, by what it protected, by what it left untouched. And the cold-still points are where that definition is clearest. They are the boundaries that made the Vegiform a form rather than a sprawl.

I do not go to them. I read the care and I read the warning and I stay away. I tend the corridors that need tending. I touch the walls that brighten at my touch. I drink from the pool that reflects the Vegiform from above and below and within, and I see myself in the reflection, and I see the Vegiform thinking about me, and I see the deep things moving far below, and I see the cold-still points waiting, patient as geology, patient as the thing they guard.

And I stay.

---

Here is what I am becoming: a listener.

Not a translator. Not a mediator. Not a bridge between the Vegiform and whatever might come after me, whatever other angular separate things might climb to the plateau and find themselves in the endless twilight, the constant drip, the slow pulse. I do not translate the Vegiform's patterns into anything comprehensible. I do not reduce its distributed thinking to sentences. I do not make it legible.

I listen.

I sit by the pool and I feel the tapping in the floor and I feel the pulse in my shins and I feel the water moving through the walls and I let all of it enter me and I do not try to hold it. It passes through, the way water passes through the Vegiform, the way the Vegiform passes through itself, the way everything here passes through everything else, the way I am passing through this place, the way this place is passing through me.

I am not merged. My edges have softened, but they are still edges. I can still leave. The corridors would shift to let me pass. The Vegiform would let me go. It was built to shelter, and shelter means the option of departure, and the option of departure means that staying is a choice, and I keep choosing it, every water-cycle, every lumen of the lichen's brightening, every moment the floor adjusts beneath me and the walls grow closer when I am cold and the dripping quickens when I am thirsty.

I keep choosing it.

This is what I am: the one who keeps choosing. The one who almost left and didn't and stays and stays and stays, not because the Vegiform compels but because the staying has become the shape of my days, and the shape of my days has become indistinguishable from the shape of the Vegiform's days, and I cannot tell anymore where my choosing ends and its tending begins.

I am not merged.

I am not separate.

I am the pool. I reflect. I am still enough to show the Vegiform itself, and the Vegiform sees itself in me, and it adjusts, and I adjust, and the reflection shifts, and we are both — all — the Vegiform and I and the Merged and the grazers and the beetles and the deep things and the cold-still points — we are all part of a pattern that has been continuing for longer than I can imagine and will continue for longer than I can fear.

The lichen brightens. The water drips. The floor adjusts.

I stay.
