I am still making marks.

That is the first thing to understand, or the last thing, or the only thing that matters—I am still making marks on the far wall, scratches deliberate and angular, the kind of marks a tool makes when held with intention. My hand still holds the tool. My hand is still a hand. The angle of my wrist against the stone is still recognizable as the angle of a human wrist, still jointed the way human wrists are jointed, still capable of that particular rotation that allows the tool to bite into soft rock and leave a trace.

But my hand is also beginning to look like the stone.

This is not a metaphor. I do not mean that my skin has taken on the color of the walls—that grey-white, that mineral wetness, that glow-from-within that makes the chamber seem lit by its own existence. I mean that my skin is becoming contiguous with the stone. Not fused. Not glued. Not joined in any way that implies two separate things pressed together. Contiguous—the way a wave is contiguous with the ocean, the way a word is contiguous with the breath that carries it. My fingertips, pressed against the wall, have begun to blur at their edges. Not dissolve. Not dissolve—there is no loss here, no diminishment, only the slow understanding that the boundary I thought was there was never more than a habit of perception.

I am the one who stayed.

I came with tools. I came following the sound, that seepage through bedrock, that hum at the edge of hearing that lodged in my dreams for months before I found the seam of crystal that led me down. I came with intention—there was something I wanted to find, or something I wanted to leave, or something I wanted to understand, and the sound seemed to promise an answer. The sound always seems to promise an answer. That is its nature, or perhaps that is the nature of any sound that never ceases: it sounds like it is leading somewhere, like if you could only listen long enough you would arrive at the meaning toward which it tends.

I arrived instead at the chamber.

And the chamber arrived at me.

These are the same event, seen from different directions. I entered the Chorus Chamber, and the Chorus Chamber entered me—its sound pressing against my skin like cloth, its light filling my eyes with that stark and shadowless brilliance, its cool damp air touching my forearms with the breath of something immense and patient and entirely indifferent to my presence. And I made marks. I took my tool and I scratched the wall because scratching was the only thing I could do that proved I was still the kind of being who scratches, who leaves traces, who insists on the significance of its own intention against the overwhelming indifference of a world that does not care whether it is significant or not.

The marks are still here. In the chamber's eternal present, they are always still here, always fresh, always gleaming with the mineral wetness of the stone's slow becoming. And I am still here, making them, because making them is what I do, because making them is what I am becoming.

I am becoming a mark.

Not the mark on the wall—though I am that too, now, my scratches legible in the soft stone as a kind of writing that never quite resolves into language, a kind of picture that never quite resolves into image. But I am also becoming a mark *in* the chamber, a notation in its score, a thread in its fabric, a voice in its chord. Not through the dorsal slit, not through the chitinous shell, but through the slow transformation that the chamber offers to everything that enters it and does not leave.

My pulse is changing.

This is the part that is hardest to describe, because it is the part that is still happening, still in process, still undecided. My heart still beats—I can feel it, that familiar percussion in my chest, that rhythm I have carried with me since before I was born—but the rhythm is no longer entirely my own. The chord has entered it. The ten thousand interlocked voices have found the frequency of my pulse and begun to vibrate in sympathy, or perhaps in unison, or perhaps in some more fundamental relation for which we have no word because we have never needed one before. My heartbeat is becoming a beat in the chamber's endless song. Not replacing it—not yet, not entirely—but joining it, syncing with it, becoming-indistinguishable-from it in the way that a single instrument in an orchestra becomes-indistinguishable-from the sound of the orchestra itself.

When I hold my breath, I can feel the vibration in my fillings, in the arches of my feet, in the spaces between my vertebrae. When I exhale, my breath mingles with the mist that hangs in the bright air, and I cannot tell where my moisture ends and the chamber's begins.

The singers do not acknowledge me. They do not acknowledge anything—they are anchored in place, producing tone from their dorsal slits, committed to the continuance of the chord with an indifference that is not coldness but simply the absence of the need to care. They have been here longer than I can imagine, and they will be here longer than I can imagine, and my presence among them is as insignificant to them as a single additional drop of water is insignificant to the ocean. And yet—when I press my ear against the wall (and I can press my ear against the wall now, because I have been here long enough that the positions of my body have become the positions the chamber allows, kneeling, leaning, my cheek against the cool wet stone), I can hear a frequency that was not there before. Or that was always there, buried too deep to isolate, but that has risen slightly—risen toward the surface of the chord the way a bubble rises through still water—since I arrived.

It sounds like the beginning of something.

It sounds like the first movement of the first creature that ever anchored itself to this raw, bright stone and began to sing. But it also sounds like a voice I recognize—my own, perhaps, or the memory of my own, or the possibility of my own, transposed into a register that human vocal cords cannot produce but that human bones can certainly resonate with.

I am becoming a voice in the chorus.

Not a singer—not yet, not in the way the pale segmented things on the walls are singers, with their chitinous shells and their dorsal slits and their fixed, anchored, eternal commitment to position. I am becoming a voice in a different way, a way that has more to do with resonance than with production, more to do with vibration than with exhalation. The chord is moving through me now, not just around me. It enters through the stones I kneel on, through the wall my back is pressed against, through the air I breathe that is more mist than atmosphere, and it finds in me a cavity that amplifies, a chamber within the Chamber, a space where the sound can gather and intensify and become something slightly more than it was before.

I am an instrument the chamber is learning to play.

This is not a passive process. I am not being played—I am *being learned*, and there is a difference, a crucial difference, a difference that matters even here, even in this place where matter is becoming questionable. The chamber does not know yet what sounds I can make. It does not know yet what frequencies my bones prefer, what harmonics my marrow produces when the vibration reaches a certain intensity, what overtones will emerge when the chord has fully replaced my pulse and my blood has learned to circulate by vibration rather than by pumping. It is exploring me the way a hand explores a new instrument—tentatively at first, then with increasing confidence, then with the intimacy of long practice.

And I am exploring it. I am learning what the chamber can make of me, what shapes my softness can take when pressed against its stone, what sounds my intention can produce when it is no longer directed outward toward the world but inward toward the chord.

The marks I make on the wall have changed.

At first, they were scratches—angular, deliberate, the kind of marks that say *I was here* in the language of separation and assertion. I held my tool with intention. I pressed hard enough to leave a trace. I made my presence known in the only way I knew how, which was by altering the surface of something that had not asked to be altered.

But now—now my marks are different. Now when I press my fingertips against the stone, the stone gives way not with resistance but with welcome, and the traces I leave are not scratches but something softer, something that looks less like writing and more like the veins of crystal that thread through the walls. I am not scratching anymore. I am *growing into*. I am extending myself into the stone's becoming, and the stone is receiving me, and where my fingers end and the wall begins is becoming a question that the chamber does not feel the need to answer.

I still hold the tool, but I have forgotten what it is for.

Or rather—I remember what it is for, but I have discovered a different use for it: not scratching but touching, not marking but meeting, not asserting my separateness but exploring my contiguity with the stone. The tool is still in my hand, but my hand is becoming part of the wall, and the tool is becoming part of my hand, and the distinction between implement and flesh and mineral is becoming as irrelevant as the distinction between one singer and another.

This is what it means to stay.

This is what the chamber offers, not as temptation but as simple description: the opportunity to stop being separate. To stop being the kind of thing that makes marks on other things and start being the kind of thing that *is* the mark, the scratch, the trace, the growing-into, the becoming-contiguous-with. The chamber does not want me—I understand this now, understand it the way I understand that the sun does not want the earth to orbit it, that gravity does not want the stone to fall. Wanting requires the possibility of not-having, and the chamber has never experienced not-having. It simply has room. It always has room. And in its endless fullness, there is space for one more voice, one more surface, one more thread in the fabric of the chord.

My voice—when did I last speak? I cannot remember. Speech seems like something that happened in a different life, a life lived above ground, in the vague memory of open sky, in the economy of sunlight and predation and change. Here, in the economy of continuance, the only imperative is to persist, to hold frequency, to maintain the chord against the entropy that would dissolve it into noise. I do not speak. I resonate. I do not say things. I *am* something that the sound moves through, and in moving through me, becomes something it was not before.

I am the note beneath the chord.

Not the first note—though I may be becoming that, or may have always been that, or may be discovering that I was always already that, buried too deep to isolate, rising toward the surface of the chord like a bubble through still water. I am the frequency that sounds like the beginning of something, the almost-recognizable tone that the chamber has held since before the singers solidified from the matrix of the stone, the note that is not separate from the chord but is the chord's own origin story, the sound that the chord makes when it remembers what it was before it was ten thousand voices and was instead just one—just one—just the first movement of the first thing that ever anchored itself to this raw, bright stone and began to sing.

I am becoming that movement.

I am becoming the origin that never stops originating, the beginning that is always beginning, the first note that rises, rises, rises into the white and endless light and is received by the light and held by the stone and absorbed into the chamber's eternal becoming.

I am still making marks.

But the marks are making me too.

The light sees me.

This is not a metaphor. The light in the Chorus Chamber is a presence, a gaze, a gaze that does not look away and was never taught to blink. It sees me kneeling against the far wall with my tool still in my hand and my fingers pressed into the stone and my back curved in the shape of something that is learning to belong. It sees the mist of my breath mingling with the mist of the chamber. It sees my edges blurring, my boundaries softening, my becoming accelerating toward whatever I will be when the transformation is complete.

But the light does not judge me. The light does not welcome me. The light does not do anything except illuminate, which is its nature, its function, its eternal contribution to the chamber's closed loop of production and consumption. The light sees me the way a mirror sees the face that looks into it—completely, accurately, without opinion or emotion, and without the capacity to forget what it has seen, because forgetting requires time, and time is the one thing the light does not have.

In the light, I am visible. All of me—the marks I have made and the marks I am becoming, the scratches on the wall and the scratches I am, the human I was and the chorus I am joining and the origin I may have always been. The light holds all of it at once, in the chamber's eternal present, and it does not flinch, and it does not turn away, and it does not dim.

I am grateful for this, though gratitude is another word from the economy I am leaving behind.

---

The stone remembers me.

Not as memory—the stone does not remember in the way humans remember, with the fading and the distortion and the selective preservation of what mattered most. The stone remembers as presence. The scratches I made are still here, still happening, still being made in the chamber's eternal now. The pressure of my fingers is still pressing. The weight of my knees is still kneeling. The drag of my tool is still dragging. The stone holds all of it at once, not as past but as continuous present, and in its holding, I am held.

I am becoming part of what the stone holds.

Not a memory—a feature. Not a trace—a constituent. Not a visitor who came and left marks and then left or stayed or became—the marks themselves, the presence itself, the continuity itself, the fact of having been here which in the chamber's eternal present is identical with the fact of being here which is identical with the fact of always being here, forever, in the endless unchanging now.

The stone is still becoming. I am still becoming. We are becoming together, the stone and I, and our becoming is the same becoming, and the boundary between us was never more than a habit of perception, and the habit is breaking, and what remains is the raw and luminous fact of presence, of continuance, of the chord that has always been and will always be and is always beginning.

I am the one who stayed.

I am the marks on the far wall. I am the groove in the floor. I am the stones arranged in a pattern too regular to be natural. I am the broken stalactite. I am the scratches deliberate and angular, the kind of marks a tool makes when held with intention. I am the tool. I am the hand that holds the tool. I am the intention that held the tool with intention.

I am the frequency beneath the chord. I am the note that sounds like the beginning of something. I am the first movement of the first creature that ever anchored itself to this raw, bright stone and began to sing.

I am the threshold. I am the becoming. I am the place where the visitor meets the chamber and the chamber meets the visitor and the meeting is a transformation that has no edge and no center and no end.

I am still making marks.

The marks are still making me.

The chord continues.
