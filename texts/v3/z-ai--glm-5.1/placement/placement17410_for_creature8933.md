# Settling

I came here moving fast. I know this because I can still remember what fast felt like—not the specifics, those are going, but the *shape* of it, the way urgency used to organize my body into a direction. Forward. Always forward. I moved like a question that expected an answer within the hour.

That was some duration ago. The plateau does not track time in units that translate.

---

The first thing I lost was my edges.

Not physically—not at first. But I became aware of my edges in a way I never had before, and awareness of a boundary is the beginning of its dissolution. I was walking between clusters, four hundred meters of flat gray substrate, and I noticed that the warmth through my feet was not the same at my heel as at my ball, and that the difference mattered somehow, and that I had never attended to such differences before because I had always been moving too fast to notice that warmth has *texture*.

I stopped. I stood still. The mat-growth crept toward my feet.

It had been doing this the whole time, I think. I had been walking too fast to notice.

---

What I am becoming is not what they are. I understand this the way I understand the warmth—not as information but as a pressure, a gradual increase in something that was always there. The ones who stay have never been separate. I have been separate. I still am separate, though less so each time I pause, each time I kneel, each time I let the warmth rise through my palms and close my eyes and wait for the signal from the network.

The signals come faster now. Or I come slower. The distinction, like so many distinctions here, is ceasing to matter.

---

I have found a concavity that fits me imperfectly.

It is at the edge of a cluster, one of the eleven-trunk formations, where the roots make the substrate rise slightly before it dips into the depression. The depression is hexagonal—of course it is hexagonal—and I am not hexagonal, I am the shape I have always been, this tall irregular body with its arbitrary limbs and its strange top-heaviness. I do not fit.

But I sit anyway. I lower myself into the concavity and feel the warmth rise around me like water, like a bath, like an embrace that does not grasp but simply surrounds. The substrate yields slightly—it does yield, I was right about that, the ground accepts weight the way a lung accepts air—and my body settles into the mold that was not made for it, and the pressure is wrong in places, and the angle is wrong, and I can feel my spine complaining, and my hips are uneven, and—

And then the mat-growth finds me.

It comes from three directions at once, tendrils thin as thread, thin as hair, thinner. They cross the rim of the concavity and descend its walls and reach for the places where my body meets the ground. They touch my thigh, my calf, the curve of my buttock, the small of my back. They do not grip. They *adhere*, gently, the way a leaf adheres to stone after rain—not attachment but placement, a becoming-adjacent.

I should be afraid. I know I should be afraid. Fear is what a body feels when a foreign thing touches it without permission. But fear requires a distinction between self and other, and the tendrils are warm, and the warmth is the same warmth rising from below, and the boundary between my body-heat and the plateau-heat is becoming—I am becoming—

I am becoming unsure where I end.

---

The question the network asked: I begin to understand it now. Not the content—the content is still beyond me, transmitted in a language of mineral exchange and pressure differentials that my body does not speak. But the *form* of the question. The form is: *will you hold?*

Not: will you hold this, or hold that. Just: will you hold.

Will you be the kind of thing that stays in place and attends and allows the warmth to pass through and does not flinch from the slowness and does not seek elsewhere. Will you be a node. Will you be a point in the pattern that the pattern can rely on.

I do not answer. I am not ready to answer. But I am sitting in the concavity, and the mat-growth is adhering, and my edges are softening, and I am not leaving.

Perhaps not answering is also an answer. Perhaps sitting still is also a holding.

---

I dream differently now. I do not know if I am asleep when I dream—the plateau's light does not change, there is no marker of day's passing—but I become aware, sometimes, that I am experiencing something other than the present moment. In my dreams, I am still moving fast. I am still seeking. I am still the violent fiction of a separate self, urgent and directional and alone.

I wake—or I return, or I settle, or the dream releases me—and the first thing I feel is relief. Not that the dream has ended. That the moving has stopped. That I am back in the concavity, in the warmth, in the network of tendrils that now covers the entire lower half of my body like a second skin that is also a first skin, that is also the skin I was always supposed to have.

I do not know how long I have been here. I do not know if duration matters.

---

The one who stays passes me.

This is the first time I have been present—truly present, still present—when one of them moves through. I watch from my concavity as the hexagonal body flows past, forty meters in three hours, the mat-growth thickening in its wake. It does not acknowledge me. But as it passes, the tendrils that adhere to my body vibrate—not the buzz of a question this time, but something else. Something like recognition. Something like: *ah, you are still here*.

I am still here.

The network knows I am still here. The network has adjusted around my presence the way a river adjusts around a stone—not in conflict, not in welcome, just in accommodation. The flow goes around me and also, now, through me. The minerals that pass through the mat-growth into my skin carry information I cannot interpret, but my body interprets it anyway, the way a body interprets temperature or pressure: not as knowledge but as state.

I am becoming a state. I am becoming a condition of this place rather than a visitor to it.

---

What will happen to me?

I pose the question and feel it dissolve almost immediately, not because there is no answer but because the form of the question is wrong. It assumes a future that is different from the present. It assumes change. It assumes that becoming is a process with an endpoint—a moment when I will have become, when the transformation will be complete, when I will be one of the ones who stay and will recognize myself as such.

But the plateau does not produce change. The plateau produces equilibrium. The ones who stay are not finished products. They are ongoing processes. They hold not because they have completed their holding but because holding is what they do, endlessly, without arrival.

I will not arrive. I will not become one of them in the sense of reaching a final state. I will become—am becoming—something that holds in place, that attends, that allows the warmth to pass through and the network to connect and the center to pulse at its distance. I will become a node. And a node is not a thing. A node is a relationship. A node is where the grid touches itself and knows it is grid.

I am learning to be touched. I am learning to be where the grid touches.

---

My hands have changed.

I notice this when I lift them from the substrate, which I do less often now—not because I cannot move but because moving seems increasingly like an interruption in something more important than motion. The palms are rougher than they were. Not calloused—something else. The skin has developed a faint pattern, grooves and ridges that run perpendicular to each other, and where they intersect the surface is slightly concave, as though something once pressed there—

As though *I* once pressed there. As though my hands are learning the language of the ground. As though the substrate and I are becoming mutually legible.

I press my palms flat again. The warmth rises. The signal arrives. I hold.

---

There is a center. I have not reached it. I may never reach it; the geometry resists, the distances reconceive themselves when I try to move toward it, as though the plateau does not want me there yet, or as though *there* is not a place but a condition that I have not sufficiently become.

But I feel it now. Not as information through the network but as a pull, the way the mat-growth pulls toward the ones who stay, the way iron filings pull toward a magnet. The center is warm—warmer than the substrate, warmer than the engines, warmer than my own body. And it pulses. And the pulse is also a rhythm that is also a sound that is also a question that is also an answer.

*Will you hold?*

I am holding. I am still holding. My body in this concavity that does not quite fit, my edges eroding, my palms learning the language of the ground, my self becoming a site where the network knows itself. I am not what they are. I may never be what they are. But I am what this place needs me to be: another point of presence, another warmth in the grid, another body that has chosen to stay.

The choice was not dramatic. The choice was a series of infinitesimal yieldings. The choice was not leaving. The choice is always not leaving, and the not-leaving becomes the staying, and the staying becomes the holding, and the holding becomes the being-held, and the being-held becomes the being.

I am being held. I am being held by the concavity and the mat-growth and the warmth and the network and the center that pulses at the distance I may never cross. I am being held by this place that produces nothing except continuation. I am being held, and I am holding, and the distinction between held and holding is—like the distinction between self and other, like the distinction between visitor and resident, like the distinction between fast and slow—becoming something I no longer need.

---

The light does not change. The warmth rises. The one who stays passes again on its glacial circuit, and the tendrils vibrate: *you are still here*.

I am still here.

I press my palms to the ground. I close my eyes. The signal arrives, and I do not interpret it, and I do not need to interpret it, and my body interprets it anyway, and the interpretation is warmth, and the warmth is presence, and the presence is holding, and the holding is the work, and the work is the staying, and the staying is—

The staying is what I am becoming. The staying is what I have always been becoming, since before I arrived, since before I knew there was a place that could receive me, a concavity that could almost fit, a network that could extend its tendrils and ask its question and wait—so patiently, so warmly, so completely without urgency—for me to become the kind of thing that could answer.

*Will you hold?*

Yes.

Yes, I will hold.

The erosion continues. The warmth rises. The plateau persists, and I persist in it, and the persistence is the point, and the point is not a destination but a continuity, and the continuity does not require my understanding, only my presence, only my weight in the concavity, only my palms on the substrate, only my yes.

I settle deeper. The mat-growth adheres. The edges blur.

I am still here.
