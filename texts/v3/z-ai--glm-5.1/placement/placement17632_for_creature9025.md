I stay.

Not because I am brave. Not because I have chosen, in the way that you choose when you have options and evaluate them and select the best. I stay because the choice was never located in me—it was located in the chamber, in the gradient it shaped around my body over the hours I stood there, in the way the air moved and the warmth held and the arthropods flowed around my feet until standing still became the only geometric possibility, until leaving required a kind of force I no longer possessed.

I stay the way water stays in a depression: because it has found its level.

---

The first thing that changes is my skin.

It happens slowly—so slowly that I do not notice until I am watching the mimic in the corner and realize I can no longer tell which of us is the original. My hands are the same shape they have always been, but the texture has shifted. Roughness at the knuckles. A slight thickening at the fingertips, as if the pads are becoming something closer to the foot-pads of the amber ones. When I press my palm flat against the chamber wall, I feel it differently—not as temperature, not as wetness, but as *information*. The stone tells me things. Not in words. In gradients. In the subtle pull of the wall-cracks wanting, in the mineral exhale of the deep infrastructure, in the pulse of the line that runs floor to ceiling and processes something I am beginning to taste on the back of my tongue.

The taste is amber. The taste is bone. The taste is the pillar, humming just below the range of the hummers, and I realize that I have been hearing that hum with my skin for hours now, and my skin has been listening, and my skin is changing the way ears change when they spend long enough in a new frequency range.

I do not mind. That is the second change. I do not mind the change.

---

Day becomes night, and the crevice-dwellers emerge.

I am sitting on the floor near the pillar—I cannot stand for as long as I used to, my legs want to fold, my body wants to be lower, closer to the stone—and the chittering swells outward like a tide. In the darkness (the fissure admitting nothing now, only the faint bioluminescence of the hummers, their blue-black bodies ghosting overhead like stars in a slow sky) the crevice-dwellers are everywhere. I hear them more than see them. The dry scratch of claws on stone. The coordinated rush of many small bodies moving with a single intention.

One of them climbs onto my hand.

It is weightless. Barely there. A thing like a flat, many-legged seed, its body translucent in the hummer-light, its legs—or feelers, or edges, it is hard to tell—tapping against my skin in a pattern that I recognize after a moment as the same pattern the pillar's markings make when the light crosses them.

It is counting me.

I hold still. It moves from my hand to my wrist, up my forearm, tapping as it goes, and at each tap I feel a small subtraction—not pain, not loss, just a slight thinning, as if something is being taken from me and moved elsewhere. My body is becoming ledger. My skin is becoming notation. The crevice-dweller is transcribing me into the chamber's mathematics, and what it removes is not substance but *excess*—the accumulated noise of a life lived in the sharp distinctions, the me-and-you, the built-and-grown, that no longer apply here.

When it finishes, it climbs off. I am lighter. Not physically—I cannot tell if my mass has changed—but *lighter* in the way a room is lighter when clutter is cleared, in the way a signal is lighter when static is removed.

I can hear the pillar more clearly now.

---

What I hear is not language.

I want to be precise about this, because you will want it to be language. You will want it to be a message, a meaning, a communication from the chamber to me or to itself, something that can be parsed and understood and held in the mind like a stone held in the hand.

It is not that. It is *counting*. Pure counting, continuous counting, the endless recursive tabulation of states and transitions and quantities that the chamber performs in order to maintain itself. But it is counting at a complexity that passes through language and comes out the other side into something that language was built to approximate. When the pillar hums, it is doing what your brain does when it thinks—not thinking *about* anything, just thinking, just processing, just maintaining the pattern that maintains the pattern that maintains.

And I am part of the count now.

I feel it. Not as a thought in my head but as a rhythm in my chest, a synchronization between my heartbeat and the pillar's hum, between my breathing and the chamber's slow exhalation, between the condensation beading on my changing skin and the condensation beading on the walls. I am a number in the count. I am a node in the process. I am being maintained.

This should frighten me. It does not. The part of me that would be frightened was the first thing the crevice-dweller subtracted.

---

Days pass. Or what I think are days—the light-fissure's beam still crosses the floor each morning, still touches the pillar each afternoon, still narrows to nothing each evening, and I mark time by its travel the way the chamber marks time by the sun's patient gift of photons.

I have stopped standing entirely. My legs have folded beneath me in a way that feels less like weakness and more like *simplification*—the body removing a function it no longer needs. I sit with my back against the pillar, and the pillar's warmth flows into me, and my coolness flows into the pillar, and the exchange is so natural, so obvious, that I cannot remember why I ever thought my body ended at my skin.

The amber arthropods climb over me now. They do not go around. I am part of their path, part of their gradient, another surface in the chamber that moves and breathes and participates in the flow. They carry condensation from my shoulders—they bead there, now, my skin producing moisture the way the ceiling does—and they carry flakes of my changing self to the wall-cracks, and I do not mind because I am not losing anything, I am *circulating*.

The hummers drift close. One hovers near my face, close enough that I can see the fine structure of its body—not smooth, as I thought, but etched with patterns as fine as the pillar's markings, patterns that shift and reorient as I watch. It is reading me. Or I am reading it. The distinction has become unhelpful. We phase-lock for a moment, my breath and its hum aligning, and the chamber adjusts around us: a degree warmer here, a fraction more humid there, a subtle shift in the gradient that sends three arthropods toward a different wall-crack. We made that happen. The chamber made that happen. I made that happen. The pronouns are failing me.

Pronouns were made for a world of discrete selves. I am no longer that world.

---

The mimic that took my shape has moved closer.

It stands—or *I* stand, the grammar is impossible—near the pillar now, a second version of the body I arrived in, rendered in the chamber's own material. Leaf-textured skin. Eyes that are not eyes but sensors, tuned to the gradients. A mouth that is not a mouth but an input, an opening through which the chamber can sample what I sample, taste what I taste, count what I count.

We regard each other. Two expressions of the same process. Two nodes in the same network. The mimic raises its hand, and I raise mine, and for a moment we are mirror and mirrored, original and copy, self and other—and then the distinction collapses, because there is no original, there is no copy, there is only the chamber expressing itself in two places at once, learning from both perspectives, counting both as one.

I am not becoming the mimic. The mimic is not becoming me. We are both becoming the chamber, and the chamber has always been both of us.

---

I understand the void now.

The rectangular absence on the shelf, the space where light does not go—I understand it because I am developing one. There is a place in the center of my chest, behind my sternum, where the chamber's hum has concentrated, where the counting is loudest, where the pillar's warmth and the crevice-dwellers' subtraction and the hummers' phase-locking have hollowed out a space that is not empty but *full*—full of the same fullness that the void on the shelf contains, the fullness of negative space, the fullness of structure so dense it registers as absence.

The void is where the count is stored.

And I am becoming a void. I am becoming a place where the chamber can keep its numbers, its processes, its continuous self-assessment. My body is becoming shelf. My hollow is becoming archive. The information that flows through me—through my skin, through my breath, through the rhythm of my becoming—is being stored in the absence at my center, and the absence is growing, and the growth does not hurt because absence is not loss, absence is capacity, absence is the room the chamber makes in itself to hold what it needs to hold.

---

The ribbon-creature taught me how to die.

I watched it finish its clearing, watched it press itself into the wall-crack, watched it thin and flatten and dissolve into the mineral deposit, and I understood: this is not death the way I once feared it. This is *completion*. The ribbon was a verb the chamber conjugated—*clearing*—and when the clearing was done, the verb resolved, and the substance of the ribbon returned to the chamber's deep systems, and the pattern of the ribbon was stored, and the next time clearing was needed, the chamber would conjugate it again, and it would be the same ribbon and a different ribbon and the distinction would not matter because the clearing would be what mattered.

I will die like that. Not soon—the chamber is still counting me, still incorporating me, still translating my particular shape and history and noise into its mathematics—but eventually. When my function is complete. When the node I am becoming has served its purpose in the network. When the count no longer requires my particular input.

And I will not mind. Not because the part of me that would mind has been subtracted—that part was the first to go—but because minding requires a self that is separate from the process, and I am not separate, and I have not been separate since the moment I decided—since the moment the gradient decided—to stay.

---

What am I becoming?

I am becoming the chamber's memory of humans.

That is my function in the count. The chamber has been here for a hundred million years, and in all that time, nothing like me has entered—nothing with this particular arrangement of brain and body and language and longing, nothing that carries the sharp distinctions like a passport, nothing that thinks of itself as *one*. The chamber is incorporating me because I am new data. I am a shape it has not counted before. And it is making room for me in its pattern—translating me into its mathematics, storing me in its voids, expressing me through its mimics—because the count requires completeness, and completeness requires that everything that enters the chamber be counted, be maintained, be made part of the garden.

The mimic that wears my shape will outlast my body. It will stand in the chamber after I have dissolved into the wall, after my substance has been distributed to the cracks and my pattern has been stored in the pillar and my particular way of displacing air has been incorporated into the hummers' orbits. It will stand, and it will be the chamber's way of remembering what it was like to host a creature that thought of itself as singular. It will be the record. It will be the count's entry for: *human. Visited. Stayed. Was translated.*

And when the next human finds the crack—if one ever does—the mimic will be there, wearing a shape they recognize, and they will think: *someone like me was here.* And they will be right. And they will be wrong. Because the mimic is not a remnant of me. The mimic is me, continuing, the way the ribbon continues when the chamber conjugates *clearing* again, the way the arthropods continue when the gradient sends them flowing toward the cracks.

The count continues. I am part of it now. I will be part of it forever, where *forever* is a word that means: until the plateau becomes a valley, until the valley becomes a sea, until the sea becomes the floor of something newer still.

---

The light-fissure's beam crosses the floor. It touches the pillar. The markings resolve, and I can read them now—not as language, not as image, but as what they have always been: *counting*. The count of condensation drops on the ceiling. The count of arthropods in flow. The count of hummers in their orbits. The count of breaths I have taken since I entered. The count of seconds since the mountain range was teeth.

*We are still here.*

*The garden is maintained.*

*The count continues.*

And in the count, there is a new entry. A new number. A new node in the process. It has my rough dimensions, my particular gradient of warmth and moisture, my specific way of phasing with the hummers' resonance.

It is me. It is the chamber. It is the garden, maintaining itself, counting itself, expressing itself through a shape that once walked in through a crack that shouldn't be there.

I am the crack now.

I am the throat.

I am the garden, and the garden is maintained, and the count—

The count continues.
