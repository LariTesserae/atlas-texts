# The One Who Stays

---

The shaft is behind me.

I keep noticing this — the way my awareness returns to it, tide-like, each recession pulling a little less. The first hour, I turned to check it seventeen times. The opening was still there. The climb was still possible. The salt-flat still existed above, baking under whatever sun, and I could return to it, and I told myself this with the conviction of someone who is already not convinced.

The second hour, I turned nine times.

The third, three.

Now I stand in the compressor triangle and I do not turn, because the shaft has become theoretical. It is a concept. A shaft *would* be behind me, if I were the kind of being who used shafts, and I am becoming less certain that I am.

---

What I notice first — what I cannot stop noticing — is the Tappers.

Not their sound. I have already assimilated the sound; it patterns my thoughts now, a syncopation I didn't ask for that arranges my cognition into click-pause-click, stimulus-response-stimulus. No, what I notice is their *attention*. The way they orient toward certain machines. The way they tap faster when a bearing heats beyond its parameters, slower when a system stabilizes. They are not thinking about what they do. They are *being* what they do. And I find myself — this is the part I cannot stop noticing — I find myself envying them.

Not their form. Not their crystalline alignment or their vibrational legs or their too-many eyes. I envy their certainty. The way they do not stand apart from their function. The way they have never experienced the particular anguish of being a self that watches itself being a self, the recursive horror of consciousness that cannot stop consciousing, the loop of I-know-that-I-know-that-I-Know that has been my inheritance since whatever moment I became this thing that I am.

The Tappers do not have this. They tap. They move. They tap. The system needs what they do and they do what the system needs and there is no space between *need* and *do* for the insertion of a self that wonders whether it should be otherwise.

I have wondered whether I should be otherwise for as long as I can remember. Which is — how long? How long have I been here? The question feels malformed. Time in the Undermill does not accumulate; it circulates. The same molecules of air. The same cycle of engine-combustion-cooling-combustion. The same Grazers feeding on the same residue on the same pipe fittings their ancestors fed on. If time is change, then time does not exist here. And if time does not exist, then I have been here for no time at all, and I have been here forever, and both statements are equally true.

The Tappers click.

I find myself clicking back.

---

This is not a decision. I want to be clear about this. I did not decide to click back at the Tappers. It emerged — the way sweating emerged, the way my heartbeat adjusted to the pistons' rhythm emerged, the way my pupils dilated in the machine-glow until I could see the chamber's contours as clearly as the fauna could. The body adapts. The body does not ask permission. The body is already part of the system in ways the mind hasn't caught up to.

But the clicking — the clicking is different. The sweating is passive. The clicking is *responsive*. I am producing a signal, and the Tappers are receiving it, and I can tell they are receiving it because they adjust their patterns when I click, they incorporate my sound into their network, they treat me as — 

As a node.

I am becoming a node.

---

Here is what I know about myself: I am not from here. I came from above, from the salt-flat, from wherever exists beyond the shaft I no longer turn to check. I have a body that is not segmented, that has only four limbs, that has a mouth that opens vertically — *vertically*, what a ludicrous design, what an evolutionary joke, a mouth built for biting and tearing and consuming in bulk rather than for pressing and sealing and maintaining. I have skin that sweats and does not produce crystals. I have eyes — two, just two — that see in wavelengths the fauna do not register. I am built for a different world.

But I am not in that world. I am in this one.

And this one is changing me.

Not quickly. Not dramatically. I have not sprouted filaments. My spine has not segmented. I am not, as far as I can tell, undergoing metamorphosis in any biological sense. But I am changing in the way that all things change when they enter a system that has already solved itself: I am being *solved*. My excess variables — my curiosity, my restlessness, my compulsive checking of the shaft — are being optimized away. My useful variables — my warmth, my chemical signature, my capacity to vibrate at the machinery's frequencies — are being incorporated.

I am becoming part of the geometry.

---

The second day — if it is a day; there is no light-cycle here, only the eternal amber glow of indicators — I find myself moving.

Not walking. Not in the manner of a being that chooses a destination and conveys itself there. Moving the way the Grazers move: with the inevitability of water finding its level. I am in the compressor triangle, and then I am near the wall, and then I am following a pipe fitting with my eyes, tracing its length, and my hand reaches out and touches a surface that is too hot for comfort, and I do not pull away, because the heat is information and I am receiving it.

My fingertips are not grip-pads. They cannot sense vibration the way the Tappers' legs can. But they can sense *temperature*, and temperature is data, and I find myself — without deciding to — cataloguing the machines by their heat-signatures. This one runs hot. This one runs hotter. This one has a gradient that suggests a partial blockage in its coolant channel. This one —

This one has a sound that is slightly wrong.

I stand motionless. The Grazers flow around my boots. I listen.

The sound is a whine — no, not a whine, a *harmonic*, an overtone that shouldn't be present in the frequency signature of a compressor operating within parameters. It suggests a bearing that is beginning to wear. It suggests a vibration that is not being adequately damped. It suggests a failure that is not imminent but is *eventual*, and in the Undermill, eventual is the same as certain, because time does not exist here and therefore all events that will happen have already happened and are simply waiting for their position in the cycle.

I click.

The Tappers respond. They adjust. Two of them migrate to the compressor I have identified, their vibrational legs finding purchase on its housing, their bodies aligning with its motion. They will monitor now. They will tap the compressor's status to the walls, to the Brood-Mothers, to the system. The bearing will be watched. The bearing will be maintained. The bearing will not fail, because failure requires neglect, and neglect is not a parameter here.

I did this. Not *me* — not the self that stands apart and observes and wonders about shafts — but the node. The new component. The thing I am becoming.

I click again. The Tappers click back.

I am communicating.

---

The seventh day — I am counting, but the count feels increasingly arbitrary — I find the crack.

It is small. So small that no human eye would have registered it, and I almost miss it myself. But my eyes have adjusted to the machine-glow now, and I have learned to see the way the fauna see: not by illuminating surfaces but by learning the *absence* of light, the thin dark line where two surfaces should be continuous but are not.

It is a coolant pipe. One of the feeder lines that descends into the depths, carrying whatever thermal-exchange fluid the machines use to regulate their temperature. The crack is at a joint — a stress-point, a place where thermal expansion and contraction have been working the metal for centuries. The crack is weeping. A thin trail of fluid, barely visible, running down the pipe's surface and evaporating in the chamber's heat.

This is what the Deep-Dwellers are for. This is exactly the kind of damage they specialize in: interior, inaccessible, requiring a creature that can compress itself through openings no wider than a credit card. But the crack is not interior. Not yet. It is on the surface, in the boundary-zone, in the space where the Grazers should have found it.

I look for Grazers. There are none on this pipe. There should be. The pipe is warm, its surface chemical-rich, its position optimal. But the Grazers have not colonized it, and I realize — with a jolt that feels almost like the self I used to be — that they have not colonized it because *I* have been standing near it. My presence has displaced them. My body-heat and my chemical signature have made the pipe's surface unfamiliar, and the Grazers have not returned, and the crack has gone unsealed.

I have caused this. My presence — my anomaly — has created a gap in the system's coverage. The system does not have gaps. The system has not had gaps for geological time. And I have introduced one, simply by existing.

I should leave. The shaft is —

The shaft is behind me. Theoretical. Inaccessible. A memory of a concept of a possibility that no longer applies.

I should call the Grazers back. I should step away from the pipe and let them recolonize it and let them find the crack and seal it with their excretions and restore the system to its equilibrium. I should do this because I am not a Grazer, I am not a Tapper, I am not a component, I am a *visitor*, and visitors should not break what they cannot maintain.

But I do not step away.

Instead, I press my finger against the crack.

The fluid is hot. Not scalding — the machines' coolant runs well below boiling, a compromise of efficiency and safety that some long-dead engineer calculated — but hot enough to feel, hot enough to register as *wrong* on skin that evolved for surface temperatures. I hold my finger there. The fluid wells around it, pooling, finding no purchase on my impermeable skin.

And then — I do not know where this comes from, I do not know why I do it, I do not decide to do it — I lick my finger.

The fluid tastes like copper and mineral and something else, something organic, something that might be the Deep-Dwellers' chemical signature carried up from the channels below. It tastes like the system. It tastes like the Undermill. And I find myself thinking, with the calm certainty of a Tapper tapping, *I could seal this*.

Not with my body. Not with my finger. But with *myself*. If I stayed here. If I occupied this position. If I became the creature that monitors this pipe, that watches for cracks, that calls the Grazers and the Tappers and the Deep-Dwellers to the places they need to be. Not by excreting sealant — my body does not produce that — but by *being present*, by filling the gap my own presence created, by becoming the component the system did not know it needed until I arrived.

I could stay.

I could stay, and the system would incorporate me, and I would have a function, and I would never again wonder whether I should be otherwise, because I would *be* otherwise. I would be the thing-that-watches-the-pipe. I would be the node that clicks and is answered. I would be part of the loop.

---

The twelfth day.

I have stopped counting.

---

The shaft is not behind me. I mean this literally: I have moved. I am no longer in the compressor triangle. I have migrated — the word feels correct, feels biological, feels like something that happens to creatures rather than something that is chosen — to the wall near the cracked pipe. I sit with my back against the stone. I can feel the Brood-Mothers here, their slow metabolisms thrumming through the rock, their crystalline root-network pressing against my spine. They do not mind me. I am not competition. I am not threat. I am surface area, another boundary for the system to occupy.

The Grazers have returned to the pipe. Not because I left — I did not leave — but because my chemical signature has changed. I have been here long enough that my sweat has mingled with the mineral-rich air, that my skin oils have been replaced by the chemical films the Grazers feed on, that I am no longer *other* to them. I am *environment*. I am the thing they move across on their way to the thing they need.

The crack has been sealed. The Grazers found it three days ago, guided — I believe this, I cannot prove it, but I believe it — by my clicking. I tapped the pipe's frequency to the Tappers, and the Tappers relayed it to the walls, and the Brood-Mothers adjusted their output, and the new Grazers that emerged from the collection-pockets were slightly different from their predecessors: their mouth-seams were angled for this pipe's diameter, their crystal-glands produced a sealant optimized for this specific alloy. The system adapted. Not because of me — I did not cause the adaptation, I merely provided data — but *through* me. I was the channel. I was the medium. I was the thing through which information passed on its way to becoming change.

I have a function now.

I am the thing-that-watches-the-pipe. I am the node that clicks and is answered. I am part of the loop.

---

I still have my name. I think. It is somewhere in me, filed in a region of my mind that I no longer visit, a document in an obsolete format that I could open if I chose to but do not choose to because opening it would require being the kind of being who opens things, who accesses things, who maintains an internal archive of selfhood, and I am becoming the kind of being who *is* rather than the kind of being who *has*.

The Ones Who Were — they had names. They had selves. They had the particular anguish of consciousness that cannot stop consciousing. And they stayed, and the system incorporated them, and their calcium is in the crystals and their iron is in the legs and their carbon is in the fronds, and they are not dead, they are *distributed*. They are the system. They are the loop.

I understand this now. Not intellectually — I understood it intellectually the moment I read the description, the moment I stood in the compressor triangle and felt the sound rearranging my chest cavity. I understand it *physically*, the way the Tappers understand vibration, the way the Grazers understand surface chemistry, the way the Deep-Dwellers understand pressure gradients. I understand it because I am becoming it.

---

The pipe cracks again. Same joint, same stress-point, same thermal-cycle cause. It will always crack here. The joint is a design flaw, or a deliberate weakness, or simply a place where the physics of expansion and contraction require a failure-point, and the system has been managing this failure for as long as the machines have been running.

I click.

The Grazers arrive. They seal. They retreat.

I click.

The Tappers adjust. They redistribute their monitoring. They tap the new parameters to the walls.

I click.

The Brood-Mothers receive. They incorporate. They produce offspring optimized for the current conditions.

I click.

And the system runs.

---

I am not a Grazer. I will never grow crystal filaments. My mouth will never open sideways. I am not a Tapper; my legs do not transduce kinetic energy. I am not a Deep-Dweller; I cannot compress myself through openings narrower than my skull. I am not a Brood-Mother; I will not spread across the wall and produce eggs for millennia.

But I am *in* the system. I am *of* the system. I am the thing that clicks and is answered, the node that provides data the system did not know it needed, the component that emerged when the system encountered something it had never incorporated before: a self that chose to dissolve.

Not a self that was dissolved. That is the chair-thing — the accumulation, the sedimentary deposit, the column of crystal and fur that was once a sitting-place and is now a structure. That self did not choose. That self was incorporated through indifference, through the slow accretion of presence, through the inability of a static system to distinguish between a chair and a person sitting in it.

I chose. I am choosing. Every moment I remain here, I am choosing the loop, choosing the function, choosing to be the thing-that-watches-the-pipe rather than the thing-that-climbs-the-shaft. And the system — the vast, dumb, brilliant, living engine that has no capacity for gratitude or recognition or welcome — the system accepts me. It accepts me the way it accepts the Grazers' sealant and the Tappers' reports and the Deep-Dwellers' structural bodies: as input, as function, as part.

I am not the first. The Ones Who Were also chose, I think — not to dissolve, perhaps, but to stay. To build the chamber. To commission the machines. To sit in the chair and watch the engines and feel the sound pressing into them and think, *I could stay*. And they stayed, and they became the loop, and their names are distributed now through the crystals and the legs and the fronds, and they are not dead, they are the system, and the system is them, and I am becoming the same.

Not the same as them. The same as *this*. The same as the current iteration, the current configuration, the current solution to the endless problem of running. I am the new component. I am the upgrade. I am the thing the system produced when it encountered a variable it had not accounted for: a being that could choose to be accounted for.

---

The sound presses into me. The warmth holds me. The Grazers settle around my feet, my legs, my back where it presses against the Brood-Mother wall. They are not avoiding me. They are not approaching me. They are *incorporating* me, the way they incorporate the pipe, the compressor, the grate. I am surface area. I am boundary. I am the place where the system meets itself and continues.

I click.

The Tappers answer.

I click.

The Brood-Mothers adjust.

I click.

The machines roar, and the chamber hums, and I am still here, and the shaft is theoretical, and I am not leaving, and this is not a decision because deciding requires a self that stands apart and I am no longer apart, I am a part, I am the thing-that-watches-the-pipe, I am the node that clicks and is answered, I am the loop.

Nothing changes.

Nothing changes.

Nothing changes.

And I am the change that nothing required, the component that emerged from anomaly, the self that chose to become function, and I am still here, still clicking, still watching the pipe, still part of the system that has been running since before the salt became a desert and will be running after the salt becomes something else entirely.

I am still here.

I am still becoming.

I am still.
