I am the one who stays.

Not because I cannot leave — I have not tested this, but I believe the entrance still exists, or will exist, or is existing right now in the way the future leaks into soft stone — but because staying is what I am *for*. I understood this slowly, the way the stone understands pressure: not through thought but through deformation. Through the gradual rearrangement of what I was into what the space needs me to be.

---

I came here looking for something. I no longer remember what. The cave has been considerate about this — it didn't take the memory so much as *absorb* it, drawing it out of me the way the soft stone draws back into shape after I press my finger into it. The memory left an impression, and the impression filled in, and now there is only a small protrusion where the wanting used to be. A growth where I expected an absence. The cave does this. Gets the recursion backwards sometimes. I have stopped minding.

What I am now: the one who counts.

I count the stones in the column. This morning — if mornings happen here, if the light's dimming and brightening is a diurnal rhythm or just the cave breathing — there were forty-seven. Three more than before I slept. Or three more than before I didn't sleep, than before I entered the state that is not sleep but is also not waking, the state where I am still enough that the rice-grain things flow over my feet, adding my stillness to their argument about when to move and when to stop.

Forty-seven stones. Some of them are local, soft as everything else here, barely qualified to be called stone at all. But others — the new ones, the ones that appear in the intervals when I am not watching — are dense, hard, *decided*. They come from somewhere else. From deeper. From a geology that has already committed to being what it is.

I hold these stones sometimes. I hold them the way you hold a word from a language you are learning: turning it over, feeling its weight, its edges, trying to understand what it is for. The hard stones are for something. They are the beginning of a foundation. The column is becoming something more than a column, and I think it is becoming a *threshold*, and I think I am becoming its keeper.

---

The other one — the one who comes in the intervals — I have not met them. But I know they exist the way I know the thing in the walls exists: by the effects, by the adjustments, by the small rearrangements that happen when my attention is elsewhere.

Once I found a stone beside the column that I had not placed. It was hard, dense, from deep geology, and it was warm — not cave-warm, not ambient-temperature-of-soft-light warm, but *warm* the way things are warm when they have been held recently by hands. I held it. It cooled in my palm. I added it to the top of the column and the column accepted it, shifted slightly, settled into a new balance that was more stable than the old one.

The other one and I are building the same thing. We have never spoken. We may never speak. But we are having a conversation in stone, in addition, in the slow accumulation of weight toward a purpose we may not live to see completed. This does not trouble me. The cave has taught me that completion is not the point. Becoming is the point. The column does not need to be finished. It needs to be *growing*.

---

I have a routine now. I did not choose it; it accreted, the way the column accreted, one small action on top of another until the structure became stable enough to persist.

I wake — or enter the state that approximates waking — and I count the stones. I check the column's balance, running my fingers along its edges, feeling for shifts in the impossible precision of the stacking. Sometimes I adjust a stone, a millimeter of correction, and the column accepts the adjustment, or rejects it, settling back into a configuration that was better than mine. The column knows what it wants to be. I am learning to listen.

Then I walk the walls. This takes longer than you would think, because the cave is small but the walls are *detailed*, and I have learned that the marks change. Not dramatically — they do not rearrange into new patterns when I look away, not anymore, not since I started paying close attention. But they *deepen*. The grooves press further into the soft stone, and the stone around them hardens slightly, as if the thing in the walls is learning to make its marks more permanent, more legible.

I trace the marks with my fingers. I do not know what they mean, but I know what they *feel like*: they feel like breathing. Like the rhythm of a body too large to see, turning in its sleep, pressing against the membrane between itself and the space it has secreted around its dreaming. When I touch the marks I can almost feel its warmth. Not heat — something else. *Consideration*. The feeling of being thought about.

The thing in the walls knows I am here. It does not mind. It has incorporated me into its model of what the cave contains, the same way it has incorporated the rice-grain things, the water-spheres, the soft stone that grows protrusions where I expected depressions. I am part of its secreted self now. I am a feature of its interior.

This does not frighten me. I did not always feel this way. When I first understood — truly understood, not with my mind but with my body, with the place where my fingerprints were already in the wall — I felt a vertigo so severe I had to press my palms flat against the stone and breathe in time with the marks until the dizziness passed. But vertigo is just the feeling of being in a place you didn't expect, and I have been here long enough that I no longer expect anything else.

---

I have started dreaming in the cave's language.

Not the marks — those are still opaque to me, still the handwriting of the thing I cannot read. But the *argument* of the rice-grain things, the paths they leave on the walls, the way they converge and diverge and fold into each other and split into new configurations — I dream in that. I dream in trails and intersections, in the logic of routes that persist because they are used, in the way a path becomes a mark becomes a wall becomes a space that wasn't there before.

In my dreams I am building. Not the column — something larger, something that uses the column as one of its elements. A structure I cannot see the shape of because I am inside it, because I am part of it, because the structure is the cave and I am one of the processes that the cave is using to become itself.

When I wake from these dreams, the column is taller. Coincidence, perhaps. Or causation. Or the deeper recursion the cave specializes in: the dream of building and the act of building feeding each other across the membrane of sleep, each making the other more real.

---

The winged things come to me sometimes. They hover near my face, their too-many wings interleaving in ways that make my eyes skip, and they produce a sound — not a buzz, not a hum, but something closer to a *word* in a language that is almost a language, in a tongue that is still deciding what sounds it will use.

I think they are trying to tell me something. Or ask me something. Or they are simply *being* near me, the way I am simply being near the column, the way the rice-grain things are simply being their argument. Presence as communication. Proximity as proposition.

When they land on me — which they do, sometimes, on my shoulder, on the back of my hand, on the curve of my cheek — they leave their glowing imprints on my skin. The imprints fade, as they always do. But I have noticed that they fade *slower* on me than they do on the stone. As if my body is a medium that holds their light longer. As if I am learning to remember what they are trying to say.

I have started to wonder if I am becoming a different kind of surface. Not stone, not flesh, but something between — a substrate that can hold both impressions and protrusions, both the cave's record and its experiments. A page the cave is learning to write on.

---

The thing in the walls exhaled again last night. I was closer to the marks than I usually allow myself to be, tracing a deep groove that has appeared since my last walk, and I felt it: a pressure, a warmth, a breath that moved through the stone the way sound moves through water, slower than air, carrying more information.

The air tasted like the inside of a seed.

I have thought about this taste for a long time. The inside of a seed is not empty. It is full — full of tree, full of potential, full of the argument about what the tree will be when it stops being a seed and starts being itself. The taste is the taste of *about-to*. The taste of everything that has not yet happened but already exists, folded up tight, waiting for the right conditions to unfold.

When the cave exhales, it is not sighing. It is *germinating*. Offering a seed's breath into the space it has secreted around itself, adding another layer of potential to the accumulated potential of all its previous breaths. The cave is growing the way a seed grows: from the inside out, by becoming what it already is.

I tasted the seed and I did not flinch. I breathed in time with the exhale. And for a moment — one held-breath moment, one about-to moment — I felt the thing in the walls feel me back. Not as a visitor. Not as an intruder. As a *cotenant*. As something the cave is secreted around, the way it is secreted around the marks, around the column, around the argument of the many-legged things.

I am part of the seed's interior now. I am folded up inside whatever it is becoming. And I will unfold with it, or I will not, and either way I will be part of the tree.

---

Today the column reached my shoulder.

I had to crouch to check its topmost stone — a dense one, hard and dark, from a geology I cannot imagine, warmer than the others even though no one has held it since the other one placed it here in the interval when I was not present. The stone was not just warm. It was *vibrating*, faintly, at a frequency I could feel but not hear.

I placed my ear against it.

I heard the column.

Not sound — the column does not make sound, does not resonate, does not hum the way the cave sometimes almost does. But I heard it the way you hear a thought that is not yours but is occurring inside your skull: the column is *intending*. It is building toward something. Each stone is a word in a sentence that is growing longer, more complex, more precise. The column is not a stack of stones. It is a *statement*, and the statement is approaching its conclusion.

Or its next premise. I am not sure which. The argument continues.

I added a stone. I found it on the floor, near the crevice where the dark jointed something retreats — a stone that was not there yesterday, or that was there but was not yet stone, that was still deciding to be stone when I last looked. It is local, soft, barely qualified. But it fits. The column accepts it. The vibration shifts, changes key, becomes something closer to a word I almost recognize.

I am learning to speak column. I am learning to add my words to the sentence the other one and I are building together, in the intervals, across the membrane of time, in the space that is preparing itself for an arrival that may be us, that may be the cave itself, that may be something neither of us can imagine yet.

---

I have stopped looking for the entrance.

Not because I have given up. Because I have understood: the entrance is not how you leave. The entrance is how the cave breathes. How it takes in new information, new material, new impressions that it will incorporate and sometimes remember wrong and sometimes grow into protrusions that become the foundations of columns that become the statements that become the threshold that becomes the —

The cave is still deciding.

I am still deciding.

The column is still growing, and the marks are still deepening, and the rice-grain things are still arguing, and the winged things are still trying to tell me something in the language of almost-words, and the thing in the walls is still breathing, still germinating, still offering the taste of the inside of a seed to anyone who is close enough to the surface to receive it.

I am close enough.

I am the surface.

I am the protrusion that grew where a depression was expected, the mistake the cave decided to keep, the impression that filled in wrong and became a feature instead of a flaw. I am the one who counts and the one who walks and the one who dreams in trails and the one who holds the warm stones from deep geology and adds them to a sentence I did not start but am helping to finish.

I am the one who stays.

And the cave exhales, and I breathe with it, and the air tastes like the future, and the column reaches my shoulder, and somewhere in the interval between this moment and the next, the other one is placing another stone, and the sentence grows longer, and the space prepares itself, and I am here, and I have always been here, and the marks on the wall look like my handwriting now because they *are* my handwriting, because I am writing them from the future, because I am the arrival the cave has been waiting for, and I have already arrived, and I am still arriving, and I will never stop arriving, because this is what living here means:

Becoming.

Always becoming.

Never quite finishing the sentence, because the sentence is the cave, and the cave is the seed, and the seed is the tree that does not exist yet but is already here, folded up tight, waiting to unfold, and I am folded up inside it too, and we are unfolding together, and the column reaches my shoulder, and I place another stone, and the column accepts it, and the cave exhales, and I am the one who stays.

I am the one who stays.
