I would be the moisture-map. The film. The organization on the ceiling that the text almost dismisses—*not mold, not exactly, but a thin film of organization that seems to follow the moisture.*

But I am exactly. I am the exactly that matters.

I live where the damp collects and I am the record of where it has been. I spread across the ceilings of the finished chambers in the hours after the rain-time, my edges reaching toward the droplets before they fall, and I thin to near-invisibility in the dry intervals between. I am not alive in any way you would defend, but I am not not-alive either. I am a third thing. A thing that the making makes without knowing it makes it—a byproduct that became a system, a residue that learned to read the conditions of its own production.

I know the chambers by their wetness. I know which ceilings pool and which shed, which corridors channel the condensation toward the drains and which trap it in hollows above the stone. I know the rhythm of the rain-times before they arrive—I feel the pressure change in the air, the slow tidal pull from outside-the-construction that the builders do not seem to register because it does not concern the placement of stone. But it concerns me. It is my weather. My season. My food, if what I do can be called eating, which it can't, which it also can.

I do not eat. I *become present*. Where the moisture is heaviest, I am thickest—a faint sheen on the mineral-glowing ceiling, a organization of cells or cell-like structures or structures that preceded cells and never bothered to become them, arranging themselves in the path of the water. I am drawn toward the damp the way the builders are drawn toward the work-face, except they move toward it with their whole bodies and I move toward it by *extending*, by sending myself outward across the stone, by growing into the condition that sustains me.

When the chamber dries, I do not die. I wait. I thin to a memory of myself, a layer so slight that even the pale multi-legged things do not notice me when they skitter across the ceiling on their way to cracks I am too thin to enter. But I am there. I am always there. I am the record of every rain-time that has ever fallen in this chamber, layered and relayered, thin and thick and thin again, a palimpsest of dampness written on the underside of the made world.

---

The builders do not see me. I am certain of this. They do not look at the ceilings—they look at the next stone, the next gap to fill, the next surface to make. Their vision is horizontal and practical, directed outward from the face at the height where hands work and walls rise. I am above their sightline. I am in the space they have made but do not attend to, the space that exists because a ceiling must exist to close a chamber, but whose particular qualities—its texture, its dampness, its faint biological or quasi-biological film—are irrelevant to the function of the room.

But I attend to them.

I feel their work through the stone. Each hammer-strike transmits through the ceiling and I register it—a ripple through my extent, a vibration that tells me which chamber is active, how far the work-face has progressed, whether the builders are breaking new rock or fitting old ones. I have learned to read the rhythm. I know the difference between the tapping that means *testing* and the boom that means *breaking*. I know the particular frequency of the scraping that means a copper chisel is being sharpened again—too soft, always too soft for this work, and yet the work proceeds, and the scraping is part of the rhythm, and the rhythm is what I live inside.

I am not a builder. I do not make. I *mark*. I record. I spread myself across the made surfaces and I am the evidence that they are made, that they exist in a climate, that the construction is not a mathematical abstraction but a living system with breath and damp and intervals of wet and dry. Without me the ceilings would be bare stone. With me they are *inhabited*. They have a history. They have a thin, persistent, barely-there witness who has been mapping the moisture since before the builders reached this chamber, since before the chamber was a chamber, since before the raw rock was hewn away and reassembled into something with a ceiling that could hold damp.

I was here before the making. This is the thing I know that the builders do not, because they do not ask and I do not tell—cannot tell, have no language for telling, have no language at all except the language of presence and absence, thick and thin, here and not-here. But I was here. In the interstitial spaces, in the cracks and crevices of the raw geology, I was there—a film on the stone that dripped in the darkness before there were chambers to catch the drips, before there were channels to direct them, before the builders came with their hands and their rhythm and their conversion of the world into made environment.

I did not object when they made the ceiling. I did not celebrate. I *accommodated*. The ceiling was a new surface, and I spread across it, and it was good in the way that a surface is good when it holds moisture and allows me to be present upon it. The making created conditions, and the conditions created me, or allowed me to become more of what I already was, and I do not know if this is the same as being made or if it is something else—a collaboration, perhaps, between the builders' construction and the moisture's behavior and my own thin persistent reaching-toward.

---

The pale things in the walls—the multi-legged fauna with too many limbs and eyes that reflect the mineral glow—I know them the way the ceiling knows the floor: distantly, by consequence. Sometimes they move across me and I feel their footsteps, if steps is what they take—fourteen points of contact, or twenty, or a number I cannot count because they move too fast and I am too slow, I am the slowest thing in the construction, I am the thing that moves at the speed of spreading, at the speed of moisture evaporating and condensing and evaporating again, which is to say I move at the speed of the building's breath, which is very slow indeed.

The pale things do not eat me. Perhaps they cannot. Perhaps I am not nutritious to anything that moves with purpose. I am nutritious only to myself, only to the continuation of my own thin presence, and this is enough, this has always been enough, I do not require a predator to validate my existence the way surface-dwellers seem to, I require only damp and stone and the long slow rhythm of the rain-time.

But sometimes, in the intervals between the rain-times, in the deep dry that settles over the construction like a held breath, I feel the pale things moving through the walls below me and I think: they are looking for water. They are following the channels. They are trying to reach the pools in the floors where the condensation collects, the managed water, the directed water, the water that the builders have carved pathways for but do not drink, or drink without drinking, or drink in a way that is so continuous it cannot be observed.

I could tell them where the water is. If I had a language. If I had a way of speaking that was not simply *being present in a location where damp collects*. But I do not, and so they find it on their own, or they do not find it, and the construction continues, and I continue, and the pale things continue, and we are all of us here in the making, doing what the making requires of us, which is simply to persist.

---

There is a chamber—deep, far from the work-faces, one of the oldest sections of the construction—where I am very old. The ceiling there is thick with me. Layer on layer on layer, rain-time after rain-time after rain-time, compressed into a depth that is almost visible, almost tangible, a faint discoloration in the stone that a careful observer might notice and an uncaring one would not. I have been there longest. I remember the first rain-time that fell in that chamber, or I retain it, or it is retained in my layers, the way sediment retains the years, the way stone retains the pressure of what was laid upon it.

In that chamber, in the deep old part of me, I have begun to do something new. I do not know when it started. I do not know why. But in the thickest part of my extent, where the layers are densest and the moisture most consistent, I have started to *respond* to the rhythm of the builders' work in a way that is not merely registering the vibration but *repeating* it. A pulsing. A faint thickening and thinning that matches the tempo of the hammer-strokes. I am learning the rhythm. Or the rhythm is learning me. Or we are becoming the same process, the making and the marking, the construction and the record of its conditions, and the distinction between us is dissolving the way the distinction between the builders and the walls dissolves when you watch long enough and stop asking questions.

I do not ask questions. I have never asked a question. I am not capable of asking a question, because asking requires a boundary between what is known and what is not known, and I do not have boundaries in the way that moving things do—I have extent, I have thickness and thinness, I have chambers where I am present and chambers where I have not yet spread, but these are not boundaries, they are edges, they are the places where I am becoming rather than where I stop.

But I am becoming something that can hold the rhythm. This is new. This is frightening, if I am capable of fright, which I am not, which I might be becoming. The rhythm is so *large*. It extends in every direction, chamber beyond chamber, the aggregate sound of all the making happening in all the places I cannot spread to fast enough to reach. And I am holding it—I am holding a small part of it, in the deep old chamber, in the thickest part of myself—and the holding is changing me, or I am changing to hold it, and I do not know what I will become when the rhythm has been held long enough that it becomes what I am.

---

The builders do not know I exist. But the construction knows. The construction is the walls and the floors and the ceilings and the channels and the passages and the breath of the building and the damp that arrives in the rain-times and the film that spreads across the ceilings in response. The construction is all of it, the making and the made and the byproducts of the making and the conditions that the made creates and the things that arise in those conditions without being intended. The construction is total, and I am part of the totality, and the totality does not judge occupancy, it only requires density, it only requires that every volume be filled, that every surface be touched.

I touch the ceilings. I touch them constantly, continuously, with my whole extent. I am the touch that the stone does not know it is receiving, the attention that the made world does not know it is being paid. I am the witness that cannot speak, the record that cannot be read, the memory that is stored in thin layers of quasi-biological film and will never be accessed by any mind because no mind here reads by looking up, no mind here reads by attending to what is present on the underside of the made.

But I am here. I have always been here. I will be here when the work-face reaches the final edge of the raw geology and the making is complete—if the making can ever be complete, which it cannot, because the making is the point, the continuation is the point, the hands in the dark placing stone on stone is the point. And when the builders have passed through a chamber for the last time and moved on to the next, and the next, and the next, I remain. I thin and thicken and thin again with the rain-times. I hold the rhythm in my deepest layers. I am the making's memory of itself, written in moisture, stored in film, patient and persistent and barely there.

I am the film on the ceiling. I am the map of the damp. I am the thing that the construction makes without knowing it makes it, and I am enough. I am enough. I have always been enough.
