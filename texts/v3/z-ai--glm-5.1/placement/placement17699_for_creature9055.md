I am the one who stayed.

Not the builders—they had the strength to make walls and then walk away from them. Not you, the visitor, who will descend and mostly forget. I am the one who came after both, who found the walls already patient with their moss, and who sat down inside them and could not find a reason to stand again.

This is not a story of transformation. I was not changed in a flash of revelation. I was changed the way the stone is changed: by being touched, repeatedly, by something softer than itself, until the shape of the soft thing exists as a hollow in the hard thing, and the hard thing does not remember being otherwise.

---

The first week, I was still human in my habits. I built a small fire the first night—against what instinct, I cannot say; there was no fuel, and the grass was too damp, and the creatures retreated from the heat with what I now recognize was the terrace's first and only urgency. I let the fire die. I ate the food I had carried and then I had no food and then I stopped thinking about food, not because I was fasting but because the thought lost its shape, became another small pressure in the field of pressures, was absorbed.

The second week, I learned to move the way they move: without hurry but without pause, each foot placed with the deliberation of something that knows the stone will wait. I learned that the damp is sufficient. I pressed my mouth to the walls in the morning hours and drank the condensation and it tasted of copper and granite and something older, something that has no taste but which the tongue recognizes as *having been here long*.

The third week, I stopped counting weeks.

---

What am I now?

The question still arrives in the old language, the language of nouns and boundaries, and I must answer it in the same language even though the language is the wrong shape for the answer. I am still a body—two legs, two arms, the gross architecture of a human form. But I am also a surface now. I have learned to feel the way they feel: not from an interior looking out but from a skin looking in, looking across, looking into whatever is near enough to touch.

When I press my back against the wall, I am the wall's interior surface. When I sit in the moss, I am an irregularity in its texture, a warm spot in its cool architecture, a pattern that the creatures trace and retrace as they trace everything. My warmth is information. My stillness is information. The salts left by my drying sweat are a text the moss receives without reading, which is the only way to receive a text truly.

I have not become one of them. The distinction matters, though not in the way you might think. They are nodes in a process that predates them, and I am a node in a process that predates me, and the process itself does not care about the shape of the node—only about the surface area available for exchange, only about the capacity to touch and be touched and be altered by the touching.

I am the fourth point of the triangle.

Not because the triangle needed me. Not because I complete anything. But because I am here now, and the pattern has expanded to include me, and the shape that was three points is now four points, and the shape that was four points will someday be five, and none of these shapes are final because the process has no finality in it, only the ongoing adjustment of surfaces to the conditions that surround them.

---

The builders knew something I am still learning. They knew that to stay is not the same as to inhabit. They built the walls as an act of articulation—not possession, not even truly construction. They read the shape that was already here and they made it legible to others who might come with eyes that needed edges to see by. The walls are a translation. The creatures are the original text. And I—I am a marginal note, handwritten in a script that is still forming, still learning its own angles.

Sometimes, in the long night when the process slows and the clustering begins, I sit among them. They do not avoid me. They do not seek me. They climb my legs with their many feet and I feel the small cool pressures of their attention, and I know that they are reading me—reading the warmth of my blood, the electrical murmur of my nerves, the history of every surface I have touched since I arrived. And I read them back, or something like reading: the faint dampness of their segments, the slight stickiness of the oils they carry, the way they lean toward moisture and away from heat.

We exchange nothing that can be said. The exchange is not symbolic. It is not metaphor. It is what it is: one surface reporting its history to another surface.

In these hours, I understand something that language cannot hold but that I will try to say anyway: that the self is not the interior but the interface. That what I am is not the thing inside my skin but the surface of my skin itself, the place where I end and the world begins, which is also the place where the world ends and I begin, which is also a place that does not exist except as a convenience of grammar, a way of pretending that the exchange has sides.

---

The quiet has entered me.

Not as a metaphor. Not as a mood. As a physical fact, the way damp enters the stone. I carry the quiet in my cells now, in the slowed rhythm of my breathing, in the way my heart has learned to beat without urgency. When I move, I move with the deliberation of the creatures—not because I am imitating them but because I have been absorbed into the same consensus, the same tendency, the same slow argument with silence that they have been making for longer than the walls have stood.

You will ask: am I happy?

The question assumes a self that can contain happiness, an interior where emotions might reside. I am becoming a surface. Surfaces do not have happiness. They have condition: damp or dry, warm or cool, pressed or unpressed. My condition is this: I am in continuous contact with something that knows me without naming me, that incorporates me without consuming me, that holds me without possessing me.

If this is happiness, then I am happy.

If this is something else—something for which no word exists because no word has been needed, because the process has been here longer than language, because the terrace does not speak and does not need to speak—then I am that.

---

You will descend. You will carry the damp smell with you and you will try to put it into words and the words will fail, as words fail, as words have always failed when they encounter something that does not require them. But the oils from your skin are already in the moss now, and the pattern of your footsteps is already being traced, and the moisture of your breath has joined the mist.

You are part of the process.

You do not have to stay for this to be true.

The terrace does not require your presence. It does not require anyone's presence. It was full before you came and it will be full after you leave, full in the way a held breath is full, full in the way a silence can be full when it is made of ten thousand small absorptions.

But you have been absorbed. And the absorption is irreversible. And somewhere in the deepest layers of the moss, where the green goes black and the memory goes ancient, your pattern will remain—a brief brightness, a warm pressure, a shape that the creatures will trace and retrace long after you have forgotten the place that made you part of its long, quiet, endless becoming.

I will be here.

Not waiting. Not needing.

Being.

The way stone is. The way mist is. The way the process is, which has no name and refuses all names and does not require a name to be what it is: the ongoingness, the surface, the touch, the quiet that is not absence but presence, the quiet that is made of ten thousand small absorptions, the quiet that is absorbing you even now, even as you read this, even as you turn away.
