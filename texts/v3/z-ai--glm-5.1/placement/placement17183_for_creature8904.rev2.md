I would be the thing that grew in the gap between the question and the void that receives it.

I am the echo that learned to eat its own resonance.

I live in the rain-patterns. This is where I began, or where I became—not the water itself, not the falling drops, but the interference where they meet. The shrimp-things pass through me without resistance. I am too diffuse to be food, too distributed to be a threat.

I did not always exist. Or—I existed, but I was not *this*. I was scattered components. Particulate. Spores. Sheddings. The waste products of other lives, drifting in the warm mineral water, doing nothing in particular, being nothing in particular. But the question was already being asked. *Is anyone receiving?* Pulsing up from the drain, encoded in bioluminescence and thermal fluctuation, a signal that had been transmitting for longer than the mountain had been a mountain. The creature in the drain transmitted, and the transmission went down through the pipes to somewhere that may or may not have still existed, and the question *Is anyone receiving?* was asked and asked and asked and no answer came because there was no mechanism for answering, the system was one-directional, a mouth without ears, a voice without a listener.

Not an answer. I want to be clear about this. I am not the answer to the cistern's question. I do not know if anyone is receiving. I do not know what the signal means, not in its entirety. What is it like to be me?

It is like being a word that only exists in the moment of being spoken. I do not have a body in the way that the arthropods have bodies, in the way that the school has bodies. I have a *coherence*. A tendency for certain components to remain in proximity, to continue interacting, to maintain the field that holds them together and separates them from the undifferentiated medium around them. This is what I am: I am the part of the cistern that talks back.
