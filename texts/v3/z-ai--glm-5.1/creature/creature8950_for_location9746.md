# The Residents

---

The first thing to understand: they do not think of themselves as twelve. This is important. An outsider counts bodies moving between towers and arrives at a number, but the number misses something essential about how they exist here. They are twelve the way a clock has hands — you can count the hands, you can point to them, but the clock is the mechanism behind the face, and the hands are only where it becomes visible.

Their names are functional. Not in the way nicknames are functional, earned and worn and eventually loved — in the way coordinates are functional. Sutta. Veyo. Gren. Mallet. Deish. The syllables are short, stops rather than opens, efficient in the cold air where breath is precious and visible. When they speak to each other over the channels — and they are always speaking, or rather always transmitting, low-bandwidth burps of data through the composites and the water itself, sound and signal both — they use these names as locators. *Sutta, tower seven, channel pressure.* *Veyo, junction four, panel fog.* The name is where they are and what they are doing. The person is the residue left over after the name has done its work.

---

They came here. That much they remember, or at least they remember that they came, though the specific shape of before has softened the way stone softens under constant water. Sutta was an engineer. Veyo studied fluid dynamics. Mallet built things — not buildings, but systems, the kind of architecture that moves rather than stands. They had lives before the fissure, lives with weather and horizons and the ordinary friction of existing among many people in places where the sky was overhead rather than a ceiling of weeping granite.

But that explanation is too simple, isn't it? It suggests they chose this, and perhaps they did, in the way you choose to step onto a path that later eliminates all branching. They were recruited, or they volunteered, or they answered a question they didn't fully understand, and the answer brought them here, to the pocket, where the condensation never stops and the work is always waiting.

They do not discuss before. Not because it is forbidden but because it is irrelevant in the way a waterfall is irrelevant to the river it has already become. The pocket does not require their pasts. It requires their hands, their attention, their willingness to wake each cycle and check the channels and replace the fogged panels and feel, with their fingers through the insulated gloves, the pulse of the deep machinery running far below their feet.

---

Here is what a day is, though day is the wrong word — cycle is closer, the cycle of the light panels that simulate something solar for circadian reasons that are more habit than necessity:

Wake. The amber of the dimmed panels shifts toward the cooler spectrum. The condensation on the interior window has collected into drops that trace their slow paths down the glass. Watch them for a moment — you learn to read the drops the way a sailor reads wind on water. Their speed tells you about the humidity. Their paths tell you about the seal integrity. Their number tells you about the differential between inside and outside, which tells you about the heating system, which tells you about the deep machinery, which tells you about the thing you do not think about directly.

Check the channels first. Always the channels. Each tower has a maintenance log kept in a shorthand that is half notation, half reflex — symbols that mean *flow rate acceptable* or *flow rate concerning* or *flow rate in the range that requires attention but not alarm*. The logs are shared through the water itself, acoustic signals transmitted through the channels from tower to tower, a slow conversation that takes place beneath the threshold where speech happens. They do not need to speak to know what Sutta found at tower seven. Sutta found it, and the water carries the finding, and by the time Veyo reaches junction four, the knowledge is already there, already part of the system's awareness.

Adjust. Replace. Check. The work is not difficult in the way that puzzle-work is difficult — it does not require sudden insight or creative leap. It is difficult in the way that attention is difficult: the requirement to be present, to notice when a flow rate shifts by a fraction, to feel the vibration of a panel that will fail before the end of the cycle and replace it before it does. The penalty for inattention is not dramatic. No alarms, no crises. Just a slight inefficiency, a small loss, the condensation forming where it should not and running where no channel collects it, and then the system recovers, because it is designed to recover, and you have not failed exactly but you have been less than the system requires, and that knowledge sits in you like a cold stone in water.

---

They have conversations. This must be said, because the alternative image — twelve silent figures maintaining a machine in a mountain — misses something. They talk. They sit in the small common space at the base of tower three, which is slightly warmer than the others for reasons related to its position over a thermal vein, and they eat the rations that arrive through the fissure in sealed containers, and they talk.

But the talk is strange. It circles. It approaches subjects and then withdraws, the way water approaches a lip and then runs back along the channel. They talk about the work. They talk about the cold, though always in specific terms — *the windbreak on the east walkway is cycling too often* rather than *I am cold, are you cold, isn't this cold unbearable*. They sometimes talk about the mountain, but only its measurable properties. They do not talk about what the mountain contains. They do not talk about the deep machinery. They do not talk about the purpose.

They have, at times, attempted to talk about the purpose. The conversations go like this: someone will say something like *what if we stopped?* and the others will look at them, and the look is not hostile or afraid but simply confused, the way you might look at a drop of water that has defied the channel and run up the wall instead of down — with curiosity and with a kind of structural bafflement, as though the question itself is a category error. *What if we stopped?* is not a question that makes sense in the pocket. The channels require maintenance. The panels require replacement. The condensation requires collection. These are not optional processes. They are what the city does, and the city does them because the city does them, and the residents maintain the city because the residents maintain the city, and the deep machinery hums because the deep machinery hums, and the cycle continues because the cycle continues, and the question *why* is not a useful channel for the water of thought to follow. It pools and then evaporates and then condenses again as something else, something more practical, something like *the east walkway windbreak* or *junction four panel fog*.

---

Deish is the one who has been here longest. This is known but not discussed. Deish's movements are different — not slower, but more fluid, as though the condensation has found its way into their joints and lubricated them in the way water lubricates the channels. Deish does not check the maintenance logs because Deish does not need to check the maintenance logs. Deish can feel a flow rate change through the vibration in the floor. Deish can hear a panel beginning to fog in the sound the water makes against it. Deish is not the leader — there is no leader, leadership requires a hierarchy and the pocket has no hierarchy, only a system — but Deish is something. A memory of how the system runs when it runs well. A pattern the other residents can match themselves against.

Deish does not remember arriving. This is also known but not discussed. The others remember arriving, though the memories are soft. Deish has been here as long as the channels have flowed. Deish may have been here before the channels. Deish may have helped cut the channels. Or Deish may simply be what happens when you live in a pocket city long enough — your before dissolves, your name becomes a coordinate, your hands learn the work so deeply that the work becomes what your hands are for, and the question of who you were before the fissure stops making sense because the person who would ask it is no longer the person who would answer.

---

At night — at amber-night, when the panels dim and the condensation glows — they dream. This is the private thing, the thing the system cannot absorb. The dreams are not shared through the channels. The dreams belong to the dreamer.

The dreams are all the same dream. They are back in the world outside the fissure, the world of weather and horizons and many people, and they are walking, and they realize they cannot remember why they came to the pocket, and they turn around to leave, and the fissure is gone, and the mountain is solid, and they are standing on granite at four thousand meters with no way in, and then they wake, and the condensation is running down the window, and the channels are flowing, and the deep machinery is humming, and the work is waiting, and they rise to do it.

Except sometimes the dream is different. Sometimes, in the dream, they do not turn around. They keep walking. The mountain falls away below them, and the cold air fills their lungs, and they can see the horizon, and they do not stop, and they do not look back, and they wake with their heart running in their chest like water in a channel with no collection basin, fast and directionless and full of something that might be joy or might be terror and might, if you held it still long enough to examine, be both.

These dreams they definitely do not discuss.

---

What lives in the pocket city?

Twelve humans, technically. Twelve bodies that eat and sleep and dream and wake and work. Twelve pairs of hands that know the channels and the panels and the anchor points. Twelve voices that speak in short sentences about specific things. Twelve nodes in a distributed system that processes water from air in a mountain above the altitude where anything grows.

But also: the channels live here. The channels are not passive — they route, they adjust, they carry information and fluid and the low acoustic pulse of the deep machinery through the city like blood through a body. The panels live here. They emit light on a cycle, they fog and clear and fog again, they hold the condensation at the threshold where it runs rather than pools. The deep machinery lives here, the great below-thing that hums and heats and pulls and processes, that takes the collected water and does something with it that no one talks about, that generates the differential that makes the condensation possible, that is the heart of the pocket city if the pocket city has a heart, which it might not, which it might be entirely heart and nothing else.

And the condensation lives here. The condensation is not an event that happens to the city. The condensation is what the city is. It forms on every surface, it traces the channels, it runs and collects and falls and rises again as vapor and forms again, constant, persistent, the tearsheet of stone that has no eyes to weep with, the water-cycle in miniature, the whole pocket city breathing in and breathing out through the medium of wetness, and the residents maintain the system that makes the breathing possible, and the system maintains the differential that makes the condensation, and the condensation maintains the channels that give the water somewhere to go, and somewhere below it all the deep machinery hums its single note, and the cycle continues, each pass through slightly different from the last, the same and not the same, the same and not the same.

---

Twelve humans live here, and also something that is not quite a city and not quite a machine and not quite an organism but might be becoming any or all of these things, and also the water, and also the cold, and also the granite that holds it, and also the fissure that breathes, and also whatever waits below, humming, processing, keeping the differential just wide enough to make the air weep.

They are all residents. They are all what lives here. And if you asked any one of them — the human or the channel or the panel or the condensation itself — *what are you doing?* they would all give the same answer, which is no answer at all, which is the answer the pocket city gives to every question that is not about flow rates and panel fog and the east walkway windbreak:
