# The Inhabitants

Not what lives there. What *constitutes* there.

---

The distinction matters. A living thing has an inside and an outside, a boundary where self meets world. What populates the Throat has no such border. Each one is an event — a place where the process becomes briefly visible, the way a wave is not a thing moving through water but the water itself arranging into motion. They are not in the plateau. They are of it. Exudations. Expressions. The foam on a boiling pot that has been boiling since before the pot was a pot.

Call them what they are: *local densities*. Places where the operation concentrates itself into something that can push against the ground, can leave a track, can be seen from a distance and mistaken for a creature. They persist for durations — some for hours, some for what might be seasons, some for spans that the survey team could not measure because the thing they were measuring kept becoming a different thing while they watched, the way a cloud is not the same cloud from one moment to the next but is still, in some useful sense, there.

The bus-sized many-legged thing: it was not always bus-sized. It began as a thickening in the heat-shimmer over borehole 7, a knot in the air that tightened and dropped and tightened again, accumulating mass from — somewhere. Not from the surrounding stone, which was unchanged. Not from the air, which has nothing to give. From below, possibly. From the borehole itself, which breathes out when the pulse exhales, which exhales something that is not air and not vapor and not any state of matter that the team's chemist could name, though she tried, though she collected samples in sealed containers that corroded through within the hour, though she described the residue as "wanting to be solid but not knowing how" before she stopped talking about it entirely.

The thing accumulated. It pulled itself together out of the exhale. And when it was dense enough — when it had achieved whatever threshold of coherence the operation requires — it began to move. Slowly. So slowly that time-lapse was the only way to perceive it as movement at all, the frames revealing a progression that real-time observation reduced to a thing standing still, which is not stillness, which is simply a pace calibrated to a clock larger than any clock a human can hold.

Its legs — if they are legs — number differently depending on how you count. The survey team's biologist counted seventeen on the first pass and twenty-three on the second and stopped counting when he realized the number was increasing, new legs budding from the interstices of existing joints, each one emerging already in motion, already dragging, already committed to the route. The legs do not lift from the ground. They slide. They press forward and the body follows, and where the body passes, the hardpan is scored with marks that match — precisely, mathematically — the grooves in the canyon walls. It is not walking. It is *inscribing*. It is doing the work, one slow stroke at a time, of cutting the plateau into whatever shape the operation requires.

And the mouths. The biologist refused to document the mouths. His notes from that observation end with a line that is not a description but a statement: *There is a depth here that is not spatial.* The mouths open, each one, onto another mouth, and that mouth onto another, and so on — not infinitely, the biologist insisted, not infinitely, because infinity is a concept and this was something else, this was a specific number of recursions that simply happened to exceed the number of times he could bear to look. The darkness inside was darker than shadow. It was darker than the absence of light. It was dark the way a hole is dark — not because light cannot reach but because light will not return, as though the mouths opened onto a region from which photons are not reflected but absorbed, processed, *used*.

---

The ash-cloud: it is not ash.

The chemist determined this on the second day, after the cloud passed through the camp — through, not around, not over, but *through*, passing among the tents and equipment and bodies with a precision that suggested either total indifference or total intention, and she could not decide which was worse. The particulate matter — for want of a better term — is not carbon, is not mineral, is not any substance that appears on the periodic table or in any reference she could access. It registers as mass. It displaces air. It maintains a boundary that is coherent against winds that should scatter it across the plateau. But when she collected a sample — when the cloud obligingly deposited a small quantity of itself on a glass slide she held in its path, as though it had been waiting for the opportunity — the material did something she could not describe without using the word *considering*.

It arranged itself. On the slide, under the microscope, the particles — each one too small to resolve individually — formed patterns. Not crystals. Not random distributions. Patterns that shifted and reformed and shifted again, cycling through configurations with a deliberateness that looked like computation, that looked like thought, that looked like something thinking in a language made of geometry rather than syntax. She filmed it. The film shows the patterns. She showed the film to the others. They agreed that the patterns were there. None of them could say what the patterns were for, and the chemist said, very quietly, that maybe they were not *for* anything, maybe they were just what thinking looks like when it is happening at a scale and in a medium that humans cannot inhabit, and then she put the slide away and did not look at it again.

The cloud produces sound when it passes a living body. Grinding teeth. The team's audio engineer recorded it, analyzed the frequencies, and found that the sound was not being *produced* by the cloud in the way that a voice is produced by a throat. The sound was being *extracted* — pulled from the body itself, the cloud's presence resonating with the calcium and mineral content of human bone and tooth, vibrating what was already there, making the body audible to itself. The cloud was not grinding its teeth. It was grinding *theirs*. It was making the survey team's own skeletons sing in a frequency that happened to be audible, and the sound it made was the sound of their own material being read, being known, being *assessed* by a process that wanted to know what they were made of so it could know what to do with them, except it had not yet decided what to do with them, or had decided and the decision had not yet manifested, or the manifestation was so slow that it would take years to notice, the way the plateau takes millennia to erode, the way the operation takes geological ages to complete whatever it is completing.

---

The point-headed stone-things that leave furrows: they are the most numerous, and they are the ones the team found most bearable, which is to say they inspired a merely ordinary horror rather than the deep-structural horror that the others induced, because they at least had shapes that could be described without the describer feeling as though language itself was being compromised.

Their bodies are mineral. Not like mineral. Not resembling mineral. *Mineral.* They are made of the same stone as the plateau, the same hematite red and ferric black, the same cracked and heat-strained material that extends in every direction. When they stand still — and they do stand still, for long periods, in groups of dozens or hundreds, arranged in formations that look like nothing so much as a factory floor seen from very far away, each unit in its place, each place defined by the unit that occupies it — when they stand still, they are indistinguishable from the surrounding rock. The team discovered this on the first day when they photographed a formation and returned the next morning to find the formation gone and the stone unchanged, except that the stone was now where the formation had been and the formation was where the stone had been, or perhaps they had always been the same thing, and the team had simply arrived at a moment when the stone was vertical rather than horizontal, and they had mistaken posture for substance.

Their heads taper to points. The points drag. The furrows they leave are machined — not approximate, not rough, but finished to tolerances that the team's engineer said were beyond their instruments to measure, beyond what their manufacturing capabilities could produce, beyond anything except the grooves already cut into the canyon walls, which they matched exactly. The point-heads are cutting the plateau. They are the cutting implements of the operation, moving at their slow pace across the surface, carving the stone into whatever pattern the operation requires, and they have been doing this for longer than the rock has been rock, and when the rock is fully carved — when the last groove is cut and the last surface is polished and the last borehole is bored — they will not be done, because the operation is not making a finished thing, the operation is making an ongoing thing, and the grooves will need to be maintained, and the surfaces will need to be re-polished, and the boreholes will need to be deepened, and the point-heads will continue their drag across the stone, and the sound of their passing will continue to contribute its layer to the constant drone, and the work will go on.

They do not eat. They do not drink. They do not sleep or mate or die, or if they die they do so by becoming indistinguishable from the stone again, by settling back into the ground and waiting until the operation requires them to stand up once more and resume. They are not born and they do not perish. They emerge and they submerge. They are the plateau's hands, if the plateau has hands, and the plateau has as many hands as it needs, and it needs many, because the work is vast and the pace is slow and the tolerances are exact and there is no deadline because the operation does not have a completion date, the operation *is* the date, the operation is the calendar on which all other dates are merely marks.

---

At night they multiply.

This is not reproduction. Reproduction implies a before and an after, a parent and a child, a lineage. What happens at night on the Throat is more like exfoliation. The plateau sheds. The operation becomes more visible. The densities increase and the forms proliferate and the sound deepens and the heat does not diminish — it *concentrates*, pulling itself into the bodies that move across the surface, each one a little furnace, each one running at temperatures that should melt stone and do not, each one glowing faintly in the thermal imaging like a coal in a fire that has been burning since before the word *fire* existed.

In the canyons, where the heat exceeds ninety degrees and the stone is polished to mirrors that reflect nothing because there is no light to reflect, they gather in numbers that the team's population ecologist — who had come to the plateau expecting to study adaptation, expecting to find some extremophile niche that could explain the presence of life in a place where life should not be — refused to estimate. "You don't count waves," she said. "You don't count the sparks in a fire. You can say there are many. You can say there are more than you can count. But to give them a number would be to suggest they are things that can be held, and they are not things that can be held. They are the holding. They are the thing that is being done to the plateau and by the plateau and as the plateau, and the number of them is the number of operations currently in progress, and that number changes every moment, and the total is always the same, and the total is *enough*."

She left on the fourth day. The ecologist left. She walked off the plateau and did not come back and did not answer the messages sent to her device and was found three days later in a town two hundred kilometers south, sitting in a hotel room, unable to explain how she had gotten there, unable to explain why she had left, unable to explain anything except that she had seen the gathering in the canyons and the gathering had been *enough*, enough of whatever the operation was doing, enough to fill the canyon from floor to rim with bodies that were not bodies and heat that was not heat and sound that was not sound but was instead the operation hearing itself, the way a voice in a canyon hears its own echo and recognizes it as itself and speaks again to hear itself again, speaking not words but *process*, not language but *function*, and the function was ongoing and the function was patient and the function was vast.

---

There are others.

There are things that fly — or hover, or hang in the shimmering air at altitudes that should not support flight, moving in geometries that do not correspond to aerodynamics. They are not birds. They are not insects. They are not machines. They are shapes that the heat makes when the heat needs to be somewhere other than the ground, and they cast shadows that are not their own shapes, shadows that look like the things on the ground, shadows that match the configurations of the point-heads and the many-legs and the ash-clouds, as though the things in the air are projections of the things on the ground, or the things on the ground are shadows of the things in the air, or both are aspects of something else that is neither up nor down but simply *present*, present in the way that gravity is present, in the way that heat is present, in the way that the pulse is present every forty seconds, and the things in the air pulse with it, and the things on the ground pulse with it, and everything on the Throat pulses with it, because the pulse is not something that happens to the plateau.

The pulse is the plateau happening.

There are things in the boreholes. The team did not enter the boreholes. The team looked into the boreholes — the ones they could approach, the ones at the center of the plateau where the heat was merely fifty-three degrees and not the killing heat of the rim — and they saw, in the darkness below, movement. Not the movement of things ascending or descending. The movement of *walls*, of the cylindrical shafts themselves contracting and expanding in time with the pulse, as though the boreholes were not holes but throats — and here the name of the plateau becomes unavoidable, becomes prescient, becomes the kind of naming that is not description but recognition — throats that swallow and exhale, swallow and exhale, and what they swallow is the stone and what they exhale is the inhabitants, and what they exhale is also the heat and also the sound and also the particulate matter that thinks in geometry on glass slides, and the operation is the business of swallowing the plateau and exhaling the plateau and swallowing it again, processing it, refining it, cutting it and polishing it and boring through it and putting it back different than it was, and the inhabitants are the exhale, and the inhabitants are also the swallow, and the things in the boreholes — the things that move in the walls, that are the walls, that flex and pulse and breathe with the rhythm of the operation — they are the throat of the Throat, the inner working of the inner working, and they have been at this for longer than the plateau has been above the ancient sea floor, and they will be at this for longer than the plateau will last, because the plateau is not the point.

The operation is the point. And the operation has a throat, and the throat has inhabitants, and the inhabitants are the operation's way of being in the place where the operation is happening, and the place where the operation is happening is the plateau, and the plateau is the Throat, and the Throat is swallowing, and the Throat is exhaling, and the sound continues, and the heat rises, and the pulse comes, forty seconds, forty seconds, forty seconds.

---

What do they want?

The question was asked by the team's psychologist, who had come to study the effects of extreme isolation on group dynamics and found herself instead studying the effects of extreme proximity to something that did not recognize her as separate from its environment. She asked the question on the third night, sitting outside her tent in the fifty-three-degree heat, watching the point-heads drag their paths across the hardpan in the starlight, listening to the drone deepen as the night population increased.

What do they want?

She answered herself: *They don't want. Wanting is for things that have a gap between what they are and what they could be. They don't have gaps. They are the operation filling itself in. They are the factory producing the factory. They are the engine running because the engine runs. They don't want because wanting implies a future state that is different from the current state, and their current state is the process of becoming what they already are, and what they already are is the process of becoming, and there is no gap, and there is no want, and there is only the doing, and the doing is ongoing, and the doing is patient, and the doing is vast.*

She wrote this in her notebook. Then she wrote: *I think they are happy, if happy means being exactly what you are with no remainder. I think they are the happiest things I have ever seen. I think I am going to leave tomorrow.*

She left. Most of them left. The survey team's official report was filed with incomplete data and a recommendation that no further study be attempted, and the recommendation was accepted, and the Throat was left to its operation, and the inhabitants were left to their routes, and the boreholes were left to their breathing, and the pulse was left to its rhythm, forty seconds, forty seconds, forty seconds, in the heat that rises from below, in the sound that never stops, in the place that is not a place but a process, full beyond capacity, full beyond reason, with the life that is not life but is instead the closest thing to life that a process can have — the life of an operation that has been running since before the word *life* existed, and will be running after the word *life* is forgotten, and does not care, and does not notice, and does not need to notice, because it is *proceeding*, and proceeding is enough, and proceeding is everything, and proceeding is all that the Throat has ever done, and all that the inhabitants have ever been, and all that the operation will ever require.

Proceeding.

Patiently.

Vastly.

Almost done and never done and always ongoing, in the heat, in the sound, in the pulse, in the Throat.
