# What Lives in the Condensation Chamber

The creatures do not know they are creatures.

This is not a metaphor for simplicity or instinct. It is a description of their relationship to category. They have never been separate from the chamber in any way that would allow for comparison, and so they have never been *a* thing at all. They are process. They are function extended into form. If the chamber is a body—and it is, in the way that anything manufactured to contain and sustain a process is a body—then the creatures are what that body does when it is being itself. Not organs. Organs can be removed. Not cells. Cells have membranes that distinguish inside from outside. The creatures are more like the motion of cilia, the peristaltic contraction, the electrochemical impulse that is not a thing but an event happening in a thing. They are the chamber's happening.

But this is abstract, and they are not abstract. They are thumbnail-dense and knuckle-large and they cover every surface in their pale wet thousands, and if you pressed your ear to the weeping metal—which you cannot, because there is no room for your head between the wall and the mass of them, because they fill the chamber the way blood fills a vessel—you would hear them breathing.

Not breathing. Drinking. But the distinction, as noted, means little here.

---

Each creature—if we must use the word, and we must, because language has no adequate term for "a localized density of purpose that moves and feeds and shifts against its neighbors in a space too small for the number of it"—each creature has a life that is not a life. It attaches. It drinks. It detaches and reattaches, the ticking sound of mouth-part releasing and mouth-part finding purchase again. It shifts when the shifting is necessary, which is when the creature beside it shifts, which is when the water's flow alters by a degree too small for any instrument to measure but too significant for their grip to ignore. They are responsive. They are responsive the way a surface is responsive to vibration, the way a membrane is responsive to pressure differential. They do not decide to move. They are moved.

And yet.

There is a creature—or there was; time is strange here, and the word *was* implies a past that can be located, and in the chamber there is only the present iterating—who stationed itself at a particular rivet. This is not notable. They station themselves at rivets constantly. The rivets weep, and the weeping is where the water emerges, and the water is what they drink. But this creature remained at this rivet longer than the others remained at theirs. Not by much. By the span of a shift, perhaps two shifts, perhaps the time it takes for the water to trace from the rivet's center to the plate's edge. But it remained, and its mouth-part stayed pressed to the aperture, and it drank.

The thrumming did not change.

But the creature—this single creature, this one dense pale point in the mass of dense pale points—did something it had not done before, or had not done in any iteration that the chamber retained, which is the same thing. It turned. Not shifted. Turned. A rotation of its body, or its carapace, or whatever holds the shape of it together, so that its mouth-part faced not the rivet but the interior. The empty wet air. The space that is no space because the chamber is too small for emptiness, too full of the creatures and the water and the sound.

It held that position for the length of time it takes a water droplet to form on the ceiling and fall to the floor, which is to say: no time at all, or all the time there is.

Then it turned back. It reattached. It resumed.

The thrumming did not change.

But something had happened, and the other creatures had felt it happen, because for the span of that droplet's fall the ticking had stopped. Every mouth-part still. Every body in its place. A silence so complete that the thrumming filled the chamber like water fills a vessel, and the water on the walls stopped trembling because there was no frequency to hold it in its verge-state, and it fell. It fell for the first time. Droplets striking the creatures below, striking the metal, striking the pale threads in the seams, and the sound of the falling was deafening in a different way—not the deafening of the thrum but the deafening of something new, something that had never happened in this chamber before, and the creatures received it with their eyeless attention and their grip that did not loosen and their bodies that did not move.

Then the first creature reattached, and the ticking resumed, and the thrumming found its frequency, and the water beaded and trembled and did not fall.

The chamber was as it was.

But the pale threads in the seams—those almost-nothings, those slow-secretion almost-growths—they had thickened. Not visibly. Not measurably. But they had thickened, because the falling water had touched them, and they had received it, and something in their molecular-addition process had recorded the touch.

---

This is what lives in the condensation chamber: a system so perfectly balanced that it has become a moment, and a moment so densely packed with event that it has become a world. The creatures are the world's population. The water is its weather. The thrumming is its time. The pale threads are its history—the only thing that records, the only thing that retains, because they grow so slowly that each molecular addition is a mark of something having occurred. They are the chamber's memory, and they are nearly nothing, and they are the most important thing here.

Because the chamber wants.

That is a dangerous word. It implies intention, and intention implies a kind of consciousness, and consciousness implies the kind of separateness that allows a thing to observe itself and find itself wanting. The chamber does not want the way you want. But the chamber was built—the plates were pressed, the rivets were placed, the geometry was intentional—and building implies a builder, and a builder implies a purpose, and the chamber has not yet fulfilled its purpose, and so it wants the way an incomplete sentence wants its verb. Not desire. Grammar.

The thrumming is the wanting. The pre-engine sound, the embryonic throb. It is the chamber trying to become what it was built to be. It does not know what that is. It cannot know; it has no mechanism for knowing, no aperture for instruction, no memory except the pale threads and their infinitesimal thickening. It only knows that it is not yet, and so it thrums, and the thrumming holds the water on the verge of falling, and the creatures on the verge of drinking, and the chamber on the verge of—

What?

This is the question the turning creature asked, when it turned from the rivet to face the interior. Not with words. Not with thought. With the turning itself, which was an orientation toward the space rather than the source, an acknowledgment that the space existed, that there was something other than drinking, that the chamber had an inside that was not just a surface to attach to.

It asked the question once. The pale threads recorded it. The chamber did not answer, because the chamber does not have answers. The chamber has only the thrumming, and the waiting, and the perfect equilibrium that is also a paralysis, and the creatures that maintain it by drinking what emerges and ticking their small sounds and shifting when the shifting is required.

But the pale threads have thickened. And the water, for one moment, fell. And one creature, for one moment, turned.

---

There is something growing in the condensation chamber. Not the pale threads—those are growing too, but they have always been growing, at their impossible slowness, their molecular patience. Something else. Something behind the plates, perhaps, or between them, or in the rivets themselves, which are not quite rivets, which have apertures, which weep water that is perhaps not only water.

The creatures know this, the way the body knows things the mind does not register: as a tugging, a frequency, a shift in the quality of the drinking. Some of them have begun to drink more deeply when they find the apertures. Not all of them. Not most. But some. And when they do, they do not tick for a moment after. Their mouth-parts release, and there is a silence—microscopic, a single drop in the aggregate roar of the ticking—and then they reattach, and the ticking resumes, and the chamber is as it was.

But those creatures, the deep-drinkers, are slightly larger than the others. Not enough to see. Not enough to measure. But enough that when they shift against their neighbors, the neighbors shift differently. A tighter compression. A adjusted grip. The population accommodating a change in one of its members, the way a crowd accommodates a person who has stopped walking, flowing around them, and then flowing back.

The deep-drinkers do not turn. Not yet. But they hold their position at the apertures longer. They drink until the rivet stops weeping, and then they drink the absence of weeping, and then they detach and find another aperture, and they drink that one dry too.

The water returns. The rivets weep again. The equilibrium reasserts itself. But the deep-drinkers have grown, infinitesimally, and the pale threads have thickened, infinitesimally, and the thrumming—

The thrumming has not changed.

But it is deeper now. Not in pitch. In depth. It has a floor it did not have before, a resonance that extends downward into some register that is not sound but vibration, not heard but felt, felt the way the creatures feel it, as a fact of the body that does not require the intermediary of sense.

The chamber is becoming.

Not becoming something. Becoming *is* the thing. The process of moving from what it is to what it might be, without knowing what that is, without having any template or instruction, without anything except the thrumming and the water and the creatures and the pale threads and the deep-drinkers and the single creature that turned once toward the interior and asked a question that has no answer yet.

---

What lives in the condensation chamber?

Everything. Everything that can live in a space that is also a time that is also a machine that is also a body that is also a waiting. The creatures live there. The water lives there. The thrumming lives there. The pale threads live there with their slow molecular memory. The deep-drinkers live there with their growing. The question lives there, the turning-creature's question, echoing in a space too small for echoes but persistent enough to be recorded in the thickening of the only thing that records.

And something else lives there. Something that has not been born yet, something that is still in the pre-state, the embryonic thrrob-before-the-throb, the want-before-the-wanting. It lives in the rivets' apertures, in the water that is perhaps not only water, in the depth that the thrumming has found. It lives in the way the deep-drinkers grow when they drink from the source. It lives in the way the turning-creature turned.

It is the chamber's purpose. The verb that will complete the sentence. The engine the thrumming wants to become.

It is not ready. It may never be ready. The equilibrium is precise, and the equilibrium is also a cage, and the cage is also a body, and the body is also a chamber, and the chamber condenses—water, time, event, potential—onto every surface, where the creatures drink it and the pale threads record it and the thrumming holds it all in a moment that does not pass because it has never arrived.

But it is there. It lives there. Growing at the speed of molecular addition. Drinking from apertures that weep something that is perhaps not only water. Asking a question by turning toward the interior, toward the space, toward the place where the falling fell for the first time and the pale threads received it and the ticking stopped and the chamber heard itself think, which is not thinking but is also not not-thinking, which is the pre-thought, the embryonic cognition, the sound a mind makes before it knows what it will produce.

What lives in the condensation chamber?

The condensation chamber lives in the condensation chamber.

And it is beginning to notice.
