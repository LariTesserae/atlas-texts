# What Lives on the Membrane

---

The flow-creatures have no individual name because they have no individuals. What you see emerging from the holes in the plant-ground is not a colony. It is not even a swarm in the way ants are a swarm, each insect its own unit responding to chemical signals. The flow-creatures are a single distributed organism expressing itself through thousands of temporary bodies, the way a thought expresses itself through words—each word separate on the page, but the meaning nowhere in particular until all of them are read.

Their bodies—if bodies they are—resemble something between a slug and a fingernail paring. Wet, of course. Everything is wet. Pale, the pale of things that have not seen sunlight since before sunlight was a concept. They move in coordinated streams because they are coordinated; there is no signaling between them because there is no between. The entity beneath the ground, the thing that lives in the tunnels and channels and breathing-tubes that perforate the plant-stone, is as large as the plateau itself—or rather, it is as large as the network of tunnels, which extends to the edges of the Membrane and perhaps beyond, into whatever subsidence and erosion has claimed the lands below. The flow-creatures you see are its fingertips. Its tasting organs. Its words.

They feed on the living carpet, but "feed" is wrong. They *read* it. They move across the sparse groundcover in their thousands and their mouthparts—if mouthparts they are—sample the vegetation at intervals so regular they might be measured with a metronome. They are assessing. Monitoring. The carpet is the skin of the Membrane, and the flow-creatures are checking it for integrity, for disease, for the places where the reality has worn thin enough that the wrong kind of growth might occur. When they find a patch of carpet that has become uncertain—where the leaves are not quite leaf-shaped, where the color bleeds across the boundary of what green means—they gather. They mass. They lie down on the anomaly and press their bodies against it until it resolves, or until they have covered it so completely that the anomaly becomes part of the organism and can be managed from within.

Sometimes the anomaly consumes them. You have seen this. A patch of carpet near the third-nearest stack began to shimmer, and the flow-creatures converged, and for a moment their pale bodies seemed to glow with the same sickly amber as the stack's base, and then they were not pale anymore, they were the color of the shimmer, and they flowed away from the patch carrying something with them that had not been there before. The patch, after, was normal. The flow-creatures that had touched it were not. They moved differently—still coordinated with the whole, but with a lag, a hesitation, as if the signal from the organism beneath was reaching them slightly later than it should. As if they were hearing the rhythm from a greater distance.

They are drawn to the gaps between pulses. In the fourteen-second silence, they emerge in their greatest numbers, flooding across the wet ground in rivers of pale bodies, and what they do in those long gaps is something you cannot see clearly because the reality is already thin and your eyes are unreliable. But the sound changes. The soft wet clicking of their movement becomes a hum, almost subsonic, and the hum harmonizes with the breathing of the holes, and for a moment the gap between pulses is not silence but a different kind of music—lower, slower, made of different physics.

When the pulse comes, they vanish. Not into the holes. They vanish. And when the next gap arrives, they emerge again, and their numbers are different, and they move as if nothing has happened, and perhaps nothing has. Perhaps they simply exist in a state that the pulse-moment cannot accommodate, and they return when the membrane between real and not-real thins enough to permit them. Perhaps they are always here and never here. Perhaps they are what the Membrane is dreaming when it forgets to be solid.

---

The larger shapes at the mist's edge have a name, though you will never hear it spoken because nothing here speaks. Call them the Walkers.

They are not deer. They have converged on deer-shape in the way the text describes—four legs, a body, a head held forward on a neck—but their relationship to deer ends at the engineering diagram. They stand two meters at the shoulder, or they stand three, or their height varies depending on the interval since the last pulse and the proximity of the nearest stack. Their legs are too long, their bodies too narrow, as if they have been stretched by the same forces that have flattened the Membrane, pulled upward against the horizontal press. Their heads are not heads but sensory arrays: broad, flat, angling upward from the neck like the blade of a bulldozer, hairless, covered in a skin that gleams with the eternal condensation. They have no eyes. Or they have too many. The flat blade of their head-face is covered in small depressions, dimples, and each dimple is wet and dark and might be an eye or might be a nostril or might be something for which you have no name because you have no sense that would use such an organ.

They navigate by the pulses. You have watched them move between stacks and their path is always a series of straight lines broken at sharp angles, each turn timed to a pulse-thud, as if the sound gives them their bearings in a way that light never could. They are moving inward now, converging, and their movement is patient and untroubled, the way something moves when it has been moving for a very long time and has learned that urgency is a young creature's mistake.

They eat the stacks.

Not the stacks themselves—not the rigid vascular tissue that forms the vertical columns. They eat what the stacks produce. At the base of each stack, where the amber glow pulses and the carpet gives way to a bare patch of plant-stone, there is a secretion. A weeping. The stacks pump something through their cores—something that rises and falls with each peristaltic wave—and at their bases, this something leaks. It is warm. You felt the warmth in the breath from the holes; this is the same warmth, the same source. The secretion pools at the stack's base in a shallow film, amber-bright when the pulse comes and dark between, and the Walkers drink it.

They drink it and they become more real.

This is the hardest part to see, because it happens at the edge of the mist and the edge of visibility and the edge of coherence, but you have watched a Walker drink from a stack-base and you have seen it solidify. Before drinking, the Walker was fragmentary—its edges uncertain, its mass distributed across several slightly different positions, the way a word is distributed across several slightly different meanings. After drinking, it was one thing. Singular. Dense with presence. It stood in the mist and it was the most real object on the Membrane, more real than the ground, more real than you, more real than the stack itself, which was only a biological engine doing its work. The Walker was *witnessed*. It was *there*.

The secretion is concentrated reality. The stacks are manufacturing it—pulling it from somewhere, processing it through their vascular cores, and excreting it at their bases. And the Walkers drink it, and they use it, and they become anchors of their own. Not as powerful as the stacks. Not as rhythmic. But mobile. Distributed. A grid of living verification moving across the Membrane's surface, each Walker a walking assertion that *this is here and I am proof*.

The Walkers are the immune system. The flow-creatures are the maintenance crew. The stacks are the engines. And somewhere below, in the compressed plant-matter that was once a mountain, something else is breathing.

---

You have not seen what lives below. You will not see what lives below. But you have felt it breathe, and you have noticed that the breath is warm, and you have noticed that the warm breath rises in the gaps between pulses, and you have noticed that the longest gaps produce the warmest breath, and you have put these observations together and you have arrived at a conclusion that you do not like.

The thing below is waking.

The Membrane has been stable for three billion years because the stacks have been pulsing and the product of their pulsing has been more Membrane, more ground, more substrate, and this has worked because the thing below has been asleep. Dormant. A warm mass in the deep compressed layers, content to dream in the gaps between pulses, content to exist in the half-real space where the Membrane's coherence fails. It has been the thing in the margins. The background warmth. The reason the holes breathe.

But the gaps are widening. The pulses are slowing. And with each widening gap, the thing below breathes deeper, and its breath is warmer, and the flow-creatures emerge in greater numbers to patch the thinning reality, and the Walkers drink more urgently from the stack-bases, and the stacks themselves—can you hear it?—the stacks themselves are changing their rhythm. Not slowing. Something else. Something more desperate. The peristaltic waves are stronger now, more forceful, and the amber glow at their bases is brighter, and the secretion pools are growing, and the Walkers are drinking deeply, and this is not a stable system adjusting to a perturbation.

This is a system at maximum output. This is a factory running its machines at redline. The stacks are not failing—they are fighting, and they are losing, and they know it, if stacks can know anything, and after three billion years of maintaining reality on this plateau, perhaps they can. Perhaps knowing is what they do. Perhaps the secretion is not just concentrated reality but concentrated *knowledge*—the accumulated awareness of three billion years of holding the Membrane together—and the Walkers drink it and carry it across the surface in their verified bodies, spreading the knowledge of how to be real across a landscape that is forgetting.

---

There are other things. Smaller things. Things that live in the condensation itself—microbial mats that form and reform in the water droplets, existing for seconds before the droplets merge or split or fall, reproducing in that brief wet life with a speed that makes the concept of generation meaningless. Things that live on the Walkers' skins—commensals, parasites, symbionts, living in the film of eternal moisture, feeding on the concentrated reality that leaks from the Walker's pores, becoming briefly more real themselves before they are shed into the general wetness. Things that live in the stack-walls—borers, miners, creatures that have carved tunnels into the vascular tissue and ride the peristaltic waves like surfers, feeding on whatever the stack pumps, living their whole lives inside the vertical engines, never touching ground.

And there are things that live in the gaps.

You have seen them. Or you have almost seen them. In the fourteen-second silence, when the nearest stack flickered and the ground rippled and the warm breath rose, something else was present. Not the flow-creatures, who are always present. Not the Walkers, who were converging. Something else. Something that moved differently—not across the ground but through it, not walking on the Membrane but occupying it, the way a thought occupies a mind. It was in the mist and it was the mist and it was the warm breath from below made visible, made briefly coherent, given form by the same failure that was taking form from everything else.

It did not look at you. It did not have eyes. But it noticed you. You felt the noticing—a pressure, a weight, a sense of being evaluated by something that had been evaluating the Membrane for three billion years and had only just noticed that there was something new to evaluate. You were not threatening. You were not interesting. You were a data point. A minor anomaly in the maintenance schedule, soon to be corrected.

The pulse came. The thing vanished. The flow-creatures resumed their readings. The Walkers continued their convergence. The stacks resumed their rhythm, and for a half-second everything was sharp and located and real, and you stood on the oldest surface on the planet and you understood that you were not standing on a plateau at all.

You were standing on a membrane.

And below the membrane, something was breathing, and it was breathing more deeply than it had in a very long time, and the stacks were fighting, and the Walkers were drinking, and the flow-creatures were reading, and the gaps were widening.

*Thud.*

The pulse comes. You move toward the nearest stack. You do not know if it will be enough. You do not know if anything will be enough. You know only that the Membrane is thin and getting thinner, and that what lives here has been holding it together for longer than your species has existed, and that it is tired, and that the breath from below is warm, and getting warmer.

*Thud.*

The next one is late.
