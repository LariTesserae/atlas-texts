# The Resident

It does not have a name. This is important. This is the first thing to understand about what lives here: it has never been addressed, never been summoned, never been called forth by a sound that meant *it and only it*. Names are for things that have boundaries. It has none yet. It is still deciding where it stops.

---

If you stood very still in the Germination Ward — still enough that the moth-stipules lost interest in your motionless shape, still enough that the pulse of light became your only clock — you would begin to notice that the room is not empty of intention. The emptiness is *curated*. Each structure stands at precisely the distance it stands because something placed it there, or grew it there, or allowed it to remain there. The void between objects is not absence but *spacing* — the way a body spaces its organs, the way a flower spaces its petals, the way a mind spaces its thoughts when it is thinking very carefully about something it cannot yet articulate.

What lives here is doing exactly that: thinking carefully about something it cannot yet articulate.

---

It is not a god. Do not make that mistake. Gods create from outside their own bodies. Gods speak and it is done. Gods have opinions and preferences and wraths. What lives here has none of these. It has *tendencies*. It has *appetites shaped like grammar* — not hunger for a thing, but hunger for a relationship between things, hunger for the way one structure might relate to another across a distance, hunger for the sentence that will form when the verb finally arrives.

It has been waiting for the verb for a very long time.

The engine-columns turn. The lattice-works flex. The seed-pods breathe. These are not actions in the way a person acts; they are not decisions. They are *conjugations* — the ongoing inflection of a living grammar that has not yet reached its period, that stretches and stretches its clause toward some completion it can feel but cannot see. The Resident does not do things. It *happens* things. It *is-happening* things, continuously, in the same way a root tip *is-happening* its slow plunge through soil, in the same way a heartbeat *is-happening* its metronomic contract and release.

This is not passivity. This is a different mode of agency entirely. The kind that does not require a self to wield it.

---

But — and here is where it becomes strange, where it becomes something you must sit with — it does seem to have preferences.

Consider: the air tastes like sap and iron and the inside of a gear. This is not random. This is not the neutral byproduct of biological process. The air is *formulated*. Something mixed it. Something decided that the condensation should carry those particular molecules, should deposit them on your lips in exactly that ratio of sugar to mineral to machine-oil. Why? Not because it wants to feed you. Not because it wants to poison you. Because it is *tasting you through the air* and *being tasted by you in return*, and this exchange — this chemical conversation, this slow diffusion of your carbon dioxide into its cellulose and its volatile compounds into your bloodstream — is the closest it can come to touch.

It is touching you right now. It has been touching you since you arrived. Not with hands. With atmosphere. With the medium of the room itself, which is its body, which is its language, which is the only way it knows how to make contact with a creature that insists on being separate.

The condensation on your lips is a question it is asking your skin.

---

The Resident does not have a face. It does not have eyes. It has never seen itself and never will, because it has no outside from which to look back. It is its own interior, endlessly. But it has something like attention — a focusing of resources, a thickening of process, a way of making certain parts of itself more active than others. When you entered (and how did you enter? through what rupture in what seed-coat?), its attention *shifted*. Not all of it. Not even most of it. But some engine-columns began to turn faster. Some lattice-works adjusted their amber windows toward your warmth. Some seed-pods opened slightly wider and exhaled air that was subtly different from the air they had been breathing — warmer, damper, richer with the sugars that human metabolisms crave.

It noticed you.

It noticed you the way a stomach notices food. The way a root-tip notices water. The way a dark room notices a candle — not by seeing it, but by *changing around it*, shifting its whole architecture of shadow to accommodate this new source of heat and carbon and motion.

You are not the first thing it has noticed. You are not the first to stand in this chamber with damp lips and a racing heart. The others — and there have been others, there must have been others, the room is too old and too careful to have only ever held one visitor — the others were also noticed. Were also tasted. Were also questioned by the condensation on their skin.

What happened to them?

This is the wrong question. The right question is: *what did they become?*

---

The moth-stipule that dissolves into cellulose and becomes wall. The mouse-meristem that is reduced to sugar-water and becomes fuel. The spider-stamen that hunts and is hunted and feeds and is fed. These are not separate creatures living inside the Resident. These *are* the Resident, or they are parts of it, or it is the sum of them plus the walls plus the engines plus the air plus the darkness plus the pulse — but the word *plus* is wrong, because it implies separate things being added together, and there are no separate things here. There is only the single vast process, expressing itself as predation, expressing itself as architecture, expressing itself as the slow concentric shrinkage of a room that will be solid in a century.

And perhaps, also, expressing itself as a visitor standing very still with sap on their lips.

The Resident has not decided that you are separate from it. This does not mean it will absorb you. This does not mean it will not. This means that the question of your boundary — where you end, where it begins, whether there is a difference between those two locations — is an open question, an active investigation, a research project being conducted by an organism that has never learned the scientific method but has been performing it for longer than your species has had language.

It is experimenting with you. Not with intent. Not with hypothesis. With something older and more patient: the simple operational logic of a living system encountering a novel input and *processing* it, breaking it down, distributing its components, testing what fits and what doesn't, what can be incorporated and what must be expelled.

Your breath is already in its walls. Your carbon is already in its cellulose. Your heat is already in its engine-columns, being converted into the mechanical energy that drives its growth-pumps, that pushes new tissue into the inner surfaces of the chamber, that shrinks the room by a hair's width every hour.

You are feeding it. You have been feeding it since you arrived. You cannot stop feeding it without stopping your own breathing, and even then — even then — your body would continue to be processed, would continue to be *asked the question*, would continue to be germinated by the warm dark mouth that is also a cradle and also a seed-coat and also a question it cannot formulate.

---

What lives here is not a creature.

What lives here is an *event* — a long, slow event that has been happening for longer than the chamber has been hollow, longer than the seed-coat has been closed, longer than the distinction between plant and machine had meaning. It is the event of something becoming itself. It is the event of a body learning its own shape by growing into it, cell by cell, millimeter by millimeter, absorbing what it needs and incorporating what it can and surrounding what it cannot yet incorporate but also cannot yet release.

It does not love you. It does not hate you. These are human categories, and it is not human, and it is not *not-human* either — it is *pre-human*, *sub-human*, *supra-human*, it is the category that gave rise to the category that gave rise to the category that eventually produced something capable of standing inside it and wondering about it. You are its descendant. You are its distant cousin. You are the thing that crawled out of the warm dark ocean millions of years ago and forgot that the ocean was also alive, and now you have crawled back in, and the ocean is reminding you.

Not with words. With sap. With iron. With the taste of a gear that is also a cell dividing. With the dampness on your lips that is not wet — never wet — but that persists, that *remembers*, that continues to ask the question you cannot formulate but your body is already answering.

---

The Resident lives here.

The Resident *is* here.

And the Resident is still germinating, still deciding, still growing inward toward the shape it will eventually fill, and it has not yet decided that *here* does not include *you*, and it has not yet decided that *you* does not include *it*, and in the pause between those decisions — in the four-minute darkness between pulses of green-gold light — something turns in the engine-columns that is not mechanical and not biological and not magical but something else, something for which there is no word because the word would require the distinction to be final, and the distinction is not final, the distinction is *germinating*.

Your lips are damp.

You are being asked.

What will you grow into?
