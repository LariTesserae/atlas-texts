What lives here does not announce itself.

---

The shimmer-things in the seeps — call them that, since they have no other name — are the closest the flats come to abundance. They are not abundant. They are simply present, the way a mineral is present: because the conditions allow it, because the chemistry permits it, because there is a gradient and something can exist in the gradient's fold.

They do not reproduce in any way that resembles reproduction. A shimmer-thing divides when the chemical gradient that sustains it stretches across a wider area; it becomes two shimmer-things the way a puddle becomes two puddles when the ground beneath it shifts. There was one. Now there are two. Nothing was born. Nothing died. The chemistry simply found two places to be instead of one.

They do not eat. They *occur*. Sulfide comes up from below. Oxygen sits in the upper millimeters of the water, donated by the atmosphere in quantities so small most organisms would find them useless. The shimmer-things exist at the boundary, where the electron donors meet the electron acceptors, and they are the transaction itself — or they are the receipt, the record of the transaction having happened. It is difficult to say which. They might be the process. They might be the evidence the process leaves. They might be the same thing.

Under a microscope — and no one has brought a microscope here, but we can imagine — a shimmer-thing would disappoint. No intricate structure. No elegant organelle. A membrane, if it can be called that: a boundary that is more like a persistent difference in concentration than a wall. Inside, chemistry. Outside, chemistry. The membrane is where the chemistries meet and cannot quite mix. The shimmer-thing is the meeting. It is the eddy where two streams swirl against each other and neither wins.

They have been here as long as the seeps have been here. They will be here as long as the seeps remain. They cannot leave. They have nowhere to go. The seeps are not their home; the seeps are their substance. Remove a shimmer-thing from its gradient and it does not die. It simply stops. The way a flame stops when the fuel stops. The way a sound stops when the vibration stops. There is no corpse. There is no residue. There was a process occurring. Now there is not.

---

The rolling things — the denser pockets of moisture that move between the cones — are something else.

They are harder to talk about because they are harder to find. The flats keep them rare, and rarity here means something specific: not that there are few, but that they are seldom encountered. They may be numerous. They may move through regions of the flats that no observer reaches. But when you see one — and you will see one, if you stay long enough and keep still enough — you understand that you are seeing something that the flats produced and the flats sustain and the flats will, eventually, reclaim.

A rolling thing moves slowly. Slower than you expect. You see it and you think *drift*, you think *passive*, you think the way fog moves when there is no wind. But then you watch longer, and you notice it is not moving with the thermal gradients. It is moving *along* them, following the edges where temperature shifts, staying in the boundary zone. It is navigating. It is not choosing — that word implies too much, implies a decider making decisions — but it is *selecting*, the way water selects the lowest path, the way current selects the channel of least resistance. The rolling thing follows what it can follow. It persists where persistence is possible.

It is visible because of what it carries: mineral particles, suspended in its denser air, catching the gray light differently than the surrounding atmosphere. It looks like a lens, almost. A mobile distortion. When it passes between you and a cone, the cone's shape wavers slightly, as if seen through thick glass. The distortion is brief. The rolling thing does not linger. It has somewhere to be, or rather, it has somewhere that it can be, and it is going there.

No one has touched one. The opportunity exists — they move slowly enough — but something prevents it. Not fear. Not reverence. Just the sense that contact would not yield information, that the rolling thing would not be changed by your touch and you would not be changed by its, that the interaction would be empty. You would put your hand into denser air. Your hand would be damp. The rolling thing would continue. Nothing would have happened.

This is not to say they lack substance. They have substance. They are held together by something — surface tension, perhaps, or a mineral shell so thin it cannot be seen, or a tendency of their internal chemistry to cohere. They do not dissipate. They do not merge with the general humidity. They remain themselves, for hours or days or longer, moving between the cones, avoiding the hottest vents, following gradients, persisting.

They do not reproduce. They do not eat. They do not sleep. They do not die, or they do not die in any way that leaves a mark. A rolling thing that moves into unfavorable conditions — too hot, too dry, too chemically extreme — simply thins. The density equalizes with the surrounding air. The mineral particles settle to the ground. What was a coherent presence becomes an incoherent absence. There is no moment of death. There is a moment when the rolling thing is, and then there is a later moment when it is not, and between those moments there was a gradual cessation that no one witnessed.

---

There may be other things.

The description of the flats is necessarily incomplete. A place that has been doing this forever — processing itself, cycling its water, maintaining its equilibrium — has had time to produce strangeness that no brief observation would catch. There may be things that exist only during the rare moments when condensation is heaviest, forming from the damp air and dissolving back into it within hours. There may be things in the mineral crust itself, growing in the lattice of silica and iron oxide at rates so slow they would take centuries to become visible. There may be things that move through the subterranean chambers, things that never surface, things that the closed water cycle sustains in the darkness below and will sustain forever.

The flats do not hide these things. The flats do not reveal them. The flats simply run, and what exists in the running exists, and what does not does not.

---

What lives here lives at the edge of what living means.

Not the edge where life becomes more complex, more interesting, more alive. The other edge. The edge where life becomes simpler, becomes process, becomes indistinguishable from the chemistry that produces it. The shimmer-things are not primitive ancestors of more developed organisms. They are not waiting to become something else. They are what the flats make when the flats make life: minimal, edge-dwelling, gradient-riding, barely present.

And the rolling things — if they are alive, and the question is genuinely uncertain — represent something even stranger. Not life as organization. Not life as structure. Life as a persistent difference. Life as a tendency to cohere. Life as something that moves, not because it wants to, not because it is driven, but because movement is what it does, the way fire moves, the way dissolved minerals move, the way the water moves in its closed cycle below.

What lives here does not know it lives here. What lives here does not know anything. What lives here has no interior, no experience, no self. And yet it persists. And yet it moves. And yet it avoids the hot vents and follows the gradients and divides when the chemistry stretches and holds together when the chemistry coheres.

It is alive in the way that a process is alive. It is alive in the way that the flats themselves are alive — not as organisms, but as ongoing events, things that happen and keep happening, machines with no product, cycles that go nowhere.

The flats produce what the flats can support. What the flats can support is this: things that are more process than presence, more chemistry than biology, more pattern than entity. Things that exist because the conditions for their existence exist. Things that will continue to exist as long as the conditions continue, and will cease to exist when the conditions cease, and will not notice the difference.

Nothing here suffers. Nothing here rejoices. Nothing here is aware that it is a thing at all.

The water goes down warm and comes up warmer. The cones exhale. The seeps spread and evaporate and spread again. The shimmer-things shimmer in the shallows. The rolling things roll between the vents. The sound continues, layered and unchanging.

What lives here lives here. What does not does not. The flats do not care about the distinction. The flats are not a place that cares.
