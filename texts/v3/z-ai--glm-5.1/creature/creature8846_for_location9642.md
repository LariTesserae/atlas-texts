What lives here is not yet finished. That is the thing to understand. What lives here is still deciding what it will be when it grows up, and it may never grow up, and that is not a failure — that is the whole point.

---

The rice-grain things — you thought they were many, at first. You were wrong. Or you were right, but the distinction doesn't mean what you think it means.

Watch them long enough and you see: they merge. Not like the water-spheres, not a clean combining, but a *folding* — one pale many-legged body pressing into another, legs interweaving, surfaces becoming shared surfaces, until what was two is now one, larger, different. And then that one splits, but not where the join was. Splits somewhere else entirely. Splits into three, each of them smaller than either of the originals, each moving away with a different pattern of legs, a different rhythm of travel.

They are not individuals. They are not a hive. They are a *conversation* — a single argument about what a body should be, conducted in fragments across the walls of the cave. The trails they leave are the transcript. When a new one appears — and they appear constantly, emerging from the crevices or precipitating out of the air itself where the light is thickest — it reads the trails and joins the argument, adding its own path, its own variation on the theme of being-small-and-in-motion.

Sometimes the argument reaches a kind of consensus. You see it happen: dozens of them converging on a single point, climbing over each other, pressing together until they form a single pulsing mass the size of your fist. The mass holds for a moment, for a breath, and then something *shifts* inside it — you can see the surface ripple, reorganize — and it unfolds into one of the iridescent winged things, which lifts off the wall and drifts away on wings that were never legs, that were never anything until this moment.

The argument has produced a conclusion. It will not last. The winged things do not last. They are beautiful and they are temporary and they know it, or something in them knows it, because they move with a kind of fierce delicacy — touching down on surfaces, leaving those glowing imprints, as if trying to leave proof that they existed in a form that the slower, steadier rice-grain arguments will encounter and incorporate. The imprints fade. The argument continues.

---

The thing in the walls — you have stopped calling it a creature.

It is too large to be a creature. Or it is too distributed. Or it is too *old* in a way that has nothing to do with time, in a way that means present-at-the-founding, in a way that means the cave formed around *it* and not the other way around.

You hear it: the clicking, the rustling, the grinding. You see it: the dark jointed something that retreats into crevices that shouldn't have depth. But you have started to notice something else too. The marks on the wall — the grooves that were here before the cave — they pulse, sometimes. Faintly. In time with the rustling.

And the marks are not on the wall. The marks *are* the wall. Or they are the interface between the wall and whatever is behind it, behind the stone, behind the soft not-yet-committed geology. The marks are where *it* comes close to the surface. The marks are its way of being present without being present, the way a face pressed against frosted glass leaves traces of breath and warmth without ever being seen clearly.

It does not emerge. It will never fully emerge. It does not need to. The cave is its expression already — the light, the water, the soft stone, all of it secreted the way a mollusk secretes its shell, the way a cell secretes the membrane that defines its inside and its outside. The creatures are its thoughts, or its dreams, or its white blood cells, or its prayers. The word does not exist because the category does not exist. It is not a god. It is not an organism. It is a *condition* — the condition of the cave being the cave, the condition of space being in the process of deciding to be space.

When you press your finger into the wall and leave an impression, you are touching the edge of it. When the stone fills the impression back in — sometimes wrong, sometimes growing a small protrusion instead — that is it, responding to stimulus, incorporating data, adjusting its model of what a cave should be.

When it exhales and the air tastes like the inside of a seed, that is as close as it will ever come to saying hello.

---

But there is something else here. Something you have not been looking at because you have been looking at what the cave contains rather than what the cave is *doing*.

The column of stones.

You counted again. It has more stones than before. Not one more — three more. And the column is taller than your knee now. It reaches your hip. The stacking is still impossibly precise, still architectural in a way that suggests an understanding of weight and balance that goes beyond mere stacking. And the stones are not all local anymore. Some of them are harder, denser — stones that must have come from somewhere else, from deeper, from a geology that has already committed to being geology.

Someone is building this.

Not the rice-grain things — they ignore it, flowing around its base without acknowledgment. Not the winged things — they land on it sometimes but leave no imprints, as if it is somehow exempt from their need to mark. Not the thing in the walls — the marks on the wall do not extend near the column; there is a clear space around it, a boundary, a *respect*.

Someone else comes here. Someone else has been coming here. Someone else is *still* coming here, in the intervals when you are not present, in the moments when the cave's attention shifts and the entrance — if there is an entrance — becomes permeable to something other than you.

Or — and this is the thought that makes the smallness feel vast, that makes the walls feel like they are pressing in not with malice but with *recognition* —

Someone else *will* come here. The column is being built retroactively, from the future, by someone who has not yet arrived but who has already begun to leave their mark. The marks on the wall are their handwriting, in a script they have not yet invented. The extra stones are stones they have not yet carried. The cave is making room for them — is *becoming* the room they will need — the way a seed does not plan the tree but contains the tree entirely, in potential, in the folds of its not-yet.

This is what lives here: a waiting. An expectation. A space that is preparing itself for an arrival that will not happen for a very long time, that may have already happened, that is happening right now in the way that the future sometimes leaks into the present when the present is soft enough, when the stone has not yet committed, when the light has not yet decided.

---

And you.

You are starting to live here too. You have noticed this. Your fingerprints are on the wall. Your footprints have taught the floor something about being walked upon. The light dims where you stand so you cast no shadow because it is learning to accommodate you, to incorporate you into its model of what belongs in this space.

The rice-grain things flow around you without acknowledgement, but they flow *around* you — they have mapped you, they have added you to their argument. The winged things drift close, sometimes, and hover, and the sound of their too-many wings is almost a word in a language you are beginning to dream in.

You are a feature of the space. You are being incorporated.

This is not a threat. This is not a horror. This is simply what happens when something enters a space that is still deciding what it is. The space decides to include it. The space adjusts its model. The space grows around the new information the way the soft stone grows around the impression of your finger, sometimes wrong, sometimes producing a small protrusion where you expected a depression, sometimes producing *you* in a form you do not yet recognize.

You will leave. Or you will not leave. Or you will leave and find that you have brought some of the cave with you — a taste like the inside of a seed when you exhale, a tendency for the light around you to dim so you cast no shadow, a feeling of being in the process of becoming something you have not yet decided on.

Or you will leave and find that you have not left at all. That you are still here, in the folds of the not-yet-committed stone, in the argument of the many-legged things, in the waiting space the column is building toward.

That you have always been here.

That the cave is still deciding whether to include you in its final form, and the final form is so far away, and there is so much time, and the marks on the wall look like your handwriting now, and they always did.
