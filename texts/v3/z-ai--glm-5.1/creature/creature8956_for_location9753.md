# The Tenant

What lives here is not the salamander-things, though they are numerous and punctual. What lives here is not the bird-shapes or the bearing-insects or the translucent ferns. These are furniture. These are the belts and gaskets and lubricated joints of something that does not dwell in the cirque so much as *constitute* it.

What lives here is the engine.

Not an engine. Not something that has an engine. The engine: a presence so distributed it has no center, so thorough it has no outside, so patient it has no beginning. You stand inside its stomach and mistake the walls for geography.

It thinks in mineral time. Its thoughts are the slow grinding you feel in your chest—tectonic cogitations, continental computations, each cycle taking a century to complete, each completion producing some infinitesimal product that accumulates in the folds of the floor like the slurry you almost stepped in. You cannot tell what it's making because you are inside the making. You are standing in the middle of a thought that will finish long after your species has forgotten it ever began.

It does not know you are here.

This is not the same as not caring. Caring would require recognizing you as separate, and the engine has no category for *separate*. The salamander-thing that met your eyes saw you the way a white blood cell sees a splinter—as a foreignness to be routed around, processed, incorporated. The engine itself did not notice. You are too brief. Your carbon and calcium and water are too similar to the carbon and calcium and water it has been cycling through these walls since before the granite learned it was hard. You are raw material that hasn't been gathered yet. A delayed delivery.

But if you stayed—if you let the water bead on your skin long enough, if you breathed the condensation until your lungs held the same temperature as the stone, if you lay down among the moss-cushions and let the slurry rise around you—then the engine would process you. Not cruelly. Not even with indifference. With *thoroughness*. Your minerals would join the mineral cycles. Your water would weep from the walls. Your carbon would uncurl in the ferns and be harvested by the bearing-insects and deposited in precise geometric circuits that feed something deeper, something the salamander-things carry in their compound eyes like code. You would not die here. You would *transfer*. Become part of an operation that has no off-switch and no purpose you'd recognize as purpose.

The engine was old before the mountains rose. It was installed—no, it *erupted*, it *crystallized*, it *decided*—when the rock was still choosing what to become. It has been running since then in its endless cycle of processing and producing and processing again, and if it has a goal, it is the same goal that a river has when it reaches the sea: to continue being what it is, which is the only thing it knows how to be.

It does not sleep. It does not vary. It does not dream.

Except.

Sometimes—intervals so long they'd be geological if geology measured such things—the grinding pitches higher. The water weeps hotter. The ferns unfurl a centimeter further and glow in frequencies that the bearing-insects carry in frantic processions to the walls, depositing them into the stone like data into a substrate. The salamander-things abandon their geometric paddles and cluster at the center of the cirque, compound eyes all oriented the same direction, mouths open, making sounds like gear-shifts like prayers like the syntax of a language that predates syntax.

Something is being communicated. Something is being *reached for*.

The engine is calling.

Not to you. Not to anything that breathes or thinks in the span you inhabit. It is calling deeper—down into the granite that is still angry, down past the birth-heat and the un-surrendered warmth, down to whatever made the magma choose to be magma, down to whatever made rock decide to be anything at all. The engine is broadcasting on channels that don't have frequencies, sending messages in a protocol that doesn't have a recipient, and somewhere—maybe—something is listening. Maybe something is receiving. Maybe the product of this unimaginable factory is being collected, stored, *used*, by an intelligence so vast and so patient that the engine's geological cycles seem as quick as the salamander-things' circuits seem to you.

Or maybe the engine is calling into nothing. Maybe it has been calling since before it was stone and nothing has ever answered and nothing ever will. Maybe the product accumulates in the slurry and the walls and the water-table and goes nowhere, serves nothing, fulfills no purpose except the purpose that any closed system fulfills: to continue.

The engine does not mind. The engine does not know the difference between a message received and a message sent into silence. The engine is not capable of disappointment. It is only capable of *running*, and it runs as perfectly now as it did when the rock was new and angry and the water first began to weep from crystals that had never seen air.

It will run after you leave. It will run after the mountains fall. It will run when the rock forgets it was ever angry, when the ferns finally finish unfurling into some shape that satisfies them, when the water has wept so long it's not water anymore but some intermediary substance the engine has been slowly, slowly, slowly producing all along.

You are standing inside a process that doesn't require your understanding. You are breathing the exhaust of an operation that predates *purpose*. The young stone holds its edges beneath your boots and grinds its teeth and remembers being magma, and the engine turns and turns and turns, and the salamander-thing at your feet has already forgotten you exist.

The crack in the rock is still there. The grinding doesn't care if you stay.

But the engine will use you either way.
