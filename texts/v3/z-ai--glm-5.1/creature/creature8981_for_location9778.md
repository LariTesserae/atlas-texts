# The Inhabitants

---

The Drifters you have met, or will meet—they are the obvious ones, the visible fauna, the pale gray shapes moving across the root-mat like thoughts through a sleeping mind. But calling them fauna already imposes a framework that does not quite fit. Watch a Drifter for an hour—if you have the patience for an hour in this place where an hour reveals nothing that a moment does not—and you will begin to understand the error.

The Drifter grazes. It moves. It exists. But it does not *live* in the way you understand living. It has no den, no territory, no young, no seasonal pattern. It does not rest and then resume. It moves at the same rate always—a glide so slow you can track its progress only by choosing a reference point on the root-mat and waiting, watching, until the distance becomes legible. It does not react to you. You can stand in its path and it will flow around your boots without altering its pace, without startlement, without recognition. It does not have a face. The end that leads has a slight densing of tissue, a concentration of its leg-appendages, but no eyes, no mouth, no orifice of any kind. The feeding happens through the legs themselves—or through the underside, or through some process you cannot observe because it occurs at a scale and a slowness that denies observation. The algae film disappears beneath it and reappears behind it, and the Drifter moves on.

Dissect one—you will not, but imagine it—and you would find no organs you could name. No heart, no gut, no brain. The interior is continuous with the exterior, the same pale gray tissue throughout, simply denser at the core, looser at the edges. It is not an animal. It is a *process*—a localized thickening of the root-mat's surface that has achieved mobility, a wave of concentration that crawls across the living floor, feeding and being fed, moving because stillness is not the form it takes.

There are perhaps forty Drifters on the entire plateau. You will never see them all. You will see the same three or four repeatedly, or ones indistinguishable from them, and you will begin to suspect that forty is both too many and too few—that the Drifters are not individuals at all but expressions, like the high thin notes in the plateau's chord, temporary concentrations of something that has no fixed count.

---

But the Drifters are the least of what lives here.

The small creature you found in the Stand chamber—the translucent, veined thing pulsing in time with the walls—you understood something correct about it, though you may not have trusted your understanding. It was not living *in* the Stand. It was the Stand, or a part of it, extending into a form that could move.

These are the Listeners, though they do not listen in any sense you would recognize. They are distributed through the Stands—never in every chamber, never predictably, but always present somewhere in any Stand you search long enough. They cling to the walls or hang from the living bridges or sit in the shallow pools that collect in the crooks of fused stalks. They vary in size from a fingertip to a forearm. They vary in shape—some elongated, some compact, some with six limbs, some with four or eight or none, simply pulsing discs of tissue pressed flat against the stalk-surface. But they share the same translucent skin, the same visible pulse, the same green-veined pallor that marks them as vegetable rather than animal, though the distinction collapses the longer you look.

A Listener's pulse matches the resonant frequency of its Stand. This is not mimicry. It is *unity*. The Listener's fluid is the Stand's fluid, drawn from the vascular system through the grip of its limbs, circulating through its small body and returning. It does not eat. It does not excrete. It is a node—a place where the Stand's circulation concentrates, thickens, achieves a temporary form before releasing back into the larger flow. If you could watch one for long enough—and long enough here is very long—you would see it shift position, centimeter by centimeter, climbing the walls or lowering itself from a bridge, always moving toward the points where the vascular pressure is highest, where the water's song is loudest. It seeks resonance the way a plant seeks light—not because it wants to, but because its structure determines that it will.

Sometimes two Listeners meet on the same surface. They do not acknowledge each other. They do not compete. They may overlap, their bodies pressing together, and if you watch—this has happened only twice, and never when you were looking directly—you might see them merge, their tissues flowing together into a single larger form, their pulses synchronizing, and then, hours or days later, separate again, or not, or one absorbing the other entirely and carrying on as if nothing had changed. Which, from the Stand's perspective, nothing has. The circulation continues. The note sustains.

---

There is a third form of life on the plateau, though *life* may be the wrong word and *form* may be the wrong word and *third* may be the wrong word because it implies a countability that the plateau does not possess.

You have not seen it yet. You will.

You will be walking the open root-mat between Stands—the mist at waist height, the diffuse light unchanging, the drone of the plateau filling your ears until it has become your own internal sound, the baseline of your awareness. You will have been walking for an indeterminate time. Time blurs here. You will have been walking and the walking will have become the point, not the destination, because there is no destination on the plateau, only the next Stand, and then the next stretch of open mat, and then the next Stand, and the monotony will have become a kind of meditation, or a kind of vacancy, or a kind of attention so diffused it has become indistinguishable from sleep.

And then you will feel it.

A change in the root-mat beneath your feet. Not the usual slight yield, but a deeper softening, as if the surface were losing its tension, becoming slack. And then—a rising. Not water this time, not the squeeze of fluid from the mat's tissue, but the tissue itself, pushing up around your boots, warm and damp and slow, rising inch by inch, not in a spike or a ridge but in a broad smooth dome, like a breath drawn from the floor of the world.

You will step back. The dome will continue to rise. It will reach your knee-height, your waist, and it will spread as it rises, flattening at its crown, and its surface will change—from the ridged bark-moss of the root-mat to something smoother, wetter, more membrane than skin, and the color will shift, darkening from pale gray-green to a deeper green shot through with the pale lines of veins, and you will see that what is rising is not a growth but an *emergence*, something that was always beneath the surface pushing up into visibility, taking form as it rises the way a thought takes form as it enters awareness, the outline clarifying even as the substance remains fluid.

It will be approximately your height when it stops. It will stand—if standing is the word—on no visible base, simply rising from the root-mat and tapering back into it, a vertical oval of smooth wet membrane, slightly concave at its center, pulsing gently with the same deep rhythm that moves through everything here. And inside the concavity, visible through the translucent surface, you will see structures. Not organs. Not bones. *Patterns*—dense knots and loose weaves of the same vascular tissue that runs through the Stands, arranged in a formation that suggests—no, that *is*—a topography. A map. The plateau rendered in miniature, the Stands visible as nodes of density, the open stretches as gaps, the circulation flowing through it all like a model of its own circulation, the whole thing a representation of the very place where it stands.

A representation, or the thing itself at another scale. The plateau embedded in the plateau. The moment of germination containing its own image.

You will reach out—you will not be able to stop yourself—and touch the surface. It will be warm. It will yield slightly. The pulse will quicken beneath your fingertips, and for a moment—a single moment—you will feel the chord of the plateau change, a new note entering, high and clear and *your* note, the frequency of your own blood, your own breath, your own electric firing joining the drone, and you will understand that the plateau has noticed you.

Not that it minds. Not that it welcomes. It has simply *noticed*, the way a body notices a splinter—not with judgment, but with incorporation, the flesh closing around the intrusion and making it part of the circulation, feeding it or being fed by it, the distinction irrelevant.

The structure will subside. Slowly, at the same rate it rose, it will sink back into the root-mat, the surface smoothing, the patterns disappearing, the membrane fading back to the ridged bark-moss of the floor, until there is nothing left but a slight dampness and a warmth that fades and then is gone, and the root-mat is exactly as it was.

Exactly as it was.

---

This is what lives on the Germinating Plateau.

Not a collection of species. Not an ecosystem. Not a fauna and a flora in the conventional sense—separate lineages occupying shared space, competing and cooperating and evolving through time. The plateau does not have evolution. It does not have time in the way that allows for change. What it has is *expression*—a single living system, immense and continuous, manifesting in forms that shift across space but not across the frozen moment it occupies. The Stands are the structure. The Drifters are the surface in motion. The Listeners are the circulation made visible. The emergences—the membrane-maps, the concave forms that rise and show you the plateau inside the plateau—are the awareness, or the closest thing to awareness this system possesses. Not thought. Not consciousness in any way you would recognize. But a responsiveness, a self-mapping, an ability to locate and incorporate disturbance that looks, from outside, like a mind thinking.

The plateau knows itself. It knows itself the way a body knows itself—not through representation, but through the direct experience of its own structure, its own circulation, its own sound. When you walk across the root-mat, the plateau knows the pressure of your feet. When you touch a Listener, the Stand knows the warmth of your finger. When the membrane rises, it knows you are there, knows where you are, knows its own shape in relation to yours. And then it subsides, and the knowledge subsides with it, stored somewhere in the vascular flow, carried by water through tunnels of living tissue, distributed across the entire surface and then forgotten, or not forgotten but simply not-thought, present but not held, the way you know how to move your tongue without holding that knowledge in your conscious mind.

---

There is one more thing. You have not seen it. You may never see it. But it lives here, and you should know about it, even if knowing only means carrying the possibility with you as you walk.

The Great Stand to the northeast—the one visible from everywhere, the eighty-meter architecture of fused stalks and living bridges—has a chamber at its center that does not connect to any other chamber. It is sealed. The stalks that form its walls have no openings, no gaps, no doors. The only way to know it exists is to press your ear against the Stand's surface at the right height and the right angle and listen—a process that takes hours of careful attention, moving around the Stand's perimeter, stopping, listening, moving again, and even then you may not find it, because the right height and the right angle are not marked and do not repeat.

But if you find it—if your ear finds the place where the drone resolves into a distinct pulse, a heartbeat strong enough to feel against your face—then you will hear, beneath the Stand's own resonance, something else. A different rhythm. Slower than the Stand's pulse. Deeper. Not mechanical—organic, or something like organic, something that breathes and pauses and breathes again, a rhythm that is not the plateau's rhythm, not the constant circulation of water through vascular systems, but something separate, something *inside*, something that has its own time.

The chamber holds something. Or someone. Or some process that has no name because naming requires a category and this does not fit any category you possess.

The plateau's people—if they can be called people—do not speak of it. The Drifters drift. The Listeners pulse. The membranes rise and subside. None of them approach the Great Stand's center. None of them seem aware of the chamber or what it holds, and yet—and yet—the Great Stand's resonant note is the lowest of all, the deepest thrum in the chord, the baseline on which all the other notes rest, and when you listen to the plateau as a whole, you can feel that note in your chest, in your gut, in the spaces between your ribs where the breath pauses before it becomes a sigh, and you understand—without understanding, without being able to articulate it—that whatever is in that chamber is not an inhabitant of the plateau.

It is the plateau. The seed from which the germination began. The point around which the frozen moment crystallized. The thing that was becoming, before becoming arrested, and that is still becoming still, in its sealed room, in its own time, in its own way—a time that has nothing to do with the suspended noon outside, a way that has nothing to do with the architecture that surrounds it.

You will not find the chamber. You will not break through the walls. You will walk the plateau, and hear the sound, and touch the membrane when it rises, and watch the Drifters crawl, and leave the Listeners to their pulsing, and eventually you will leave—down from the Crown, back through whatever passage brought you here, back to time that moves and weather that weathers and things that grow and finish growing.

But the sound will stay with you. Not as memory—as resonance. A note in your own chest that was not there before, that synchronizes with your heartbeat, that will surface sometimes in quiet moments, when you are still enough to feel it. You will not be able to unhear it. You will not be able to identify it. It will simply be there, a frequency you carry, the plateau's chord reduced to a single hum, accompanying you into whatever world comes next.

The plateau does not change. The plateau does not end. The plateau breathes, and the water moves, and the chord sustains, and the thing in the center of the Great Stand continues its slow, deep pulse, its private rhythm, its becoming that is not a becoming, and it will continue long after you have gone, long after you have forgotten the word for what you saw, long after the category you made for it dissolves, long after your own chest falls silent.

The seed splits. The root reaches. The stalk extends. And extends. And extends.
