# The Inhabitants

You do not see them at first because they do not move at your speed.

This is not riddle or metaphor. They move—they live—at a pace that makes the engine-bulges seem frenetic by comparison. A pulse every several seconds; a breath every few minutes; a shift of weight over the course of an hour. You could sit beside one for a full day and leave convinced you had been sitting beside a stone. You might be right. The distinction may not matter here.

---

They are called—by themselves? by the plateau? there is no one to ask—they are *the ones who stay*, or perhaps *the ones who are stayed in*, the grammar uncertain because the grammar has never needed precision. They occupy the concavities.

Remember the intersections of groove and ridge, the shallow depressions where something once pressed or will press? The ones who stay fill those depressions. They are the right shape for them—the right size, the right depth, the right degree of yielding. Whether they grew to fit the molds or the molds were made to receive them is another question that has never required an answer. The fit is sufficient. The fit has always been sufficient.

A body: low, broad, vaguely hexagonal if viewed from above, though the edges are not sharp edges but rather the places where the body thins toward its perimeter. The upper surface is smooth and slightly warm—warmer than the substrate, cooler than the engine-bulges—and covered in the same fine pattern of grooves that marks the ground, as though the body and the ground share a skin condition, or a language, or a purpose. The color varies. Some are the gray of the substrate, and these are nearly invisible when settled. Others carry a faint amber stain, as though the viscous fluid has seeped into them over time, or as though they have processed it, or as though they are processing it still.

Below—no one has seen below. The concavities are not deep enough to fully contain them, and yet they settle into the ground as if the ground accepts them, as if the substrate yields around their lower portions the way a hammock yields around a weight. The mat-growth covers the edges where body meets ground, blurring the seam. When they are settled, when they are still, they are indistinguishable from the plateau except for a quality of presence that you feel rather than observe: a denseness, a warmth, a sense that the ground here is *attending*.

---

They do not eat. They do not drink. They absorb, the way the growths absorb, drawing what they need from the substrate through the surfaces that press against it. This is not feeding in any sense you would recognize. There is no seeking, no selection, no digestion as a discrete event. There is only the slow osmotic exchange of minerals and warmth and something else—something you cannot name because you do not have the sense that perceives it. A current in the grid. A signal in the mat-growth. A communication that travels through the root-network at speeds that make geological erosion look hasty.

When they settle into their concavities, they join the network. When they rise from their concavities, they carry the network with them, or a portion of it, or an echo of it. The mat-growth adheres to their undersides and trails behind them like a garment several sizes too large, and where it trails, it maintains connection to the grid, so that even in motion—even in their glacial, hour-long transit between clusters—they are never disconnected, never alone, never separate from the system that is also a place that is also a process.

They do not go far. They move from one concavity to another, following the grooves east-west or the ridges north-south, and they pause at each for durations you cannot easily measure. Hours? Days? The plateau does not track time in units that translate. They pause, and they settle, and they attend, and then they rise and move to the next. As if circumambulating. As if making rounds. As if tending.

But tending what? The plateau maintains itself. The engines pulse without intervention. The growths draw and process and heal without assistance. There is nothing for a tender to tend.

Unless the tending is the thing. Unless their presence in the concavities—their weight, their warmth, their attention—is itself the maintenance. Unless the grid requires nodes that are not merely structural but conscious, or something adjacent to conscious, something that knows it is holding and chooses to hold regardless.

---

You find one in transit between clusters, and you watch it for what your watch tells you is three hours, and in that time it traverses perhaps forty meters. It does not walk. It flows. The hexagonal body maintains its general shape but the edges blur and reform with each slow surge of motion, as though it is deciding moment by moment—over the course of minutes, each moment extended—whether to remain solid or to pour itself across the ground like a liquid seeking its level. It never fully commits to either state. It moves the way a glacier moves if a glacier were aware of its destination and patient beyond any patience you have witnessed.

As it passes, the mat-growth thickens in its wake. The tendrils interlace more tightly. The network gains density. You understand then that the movement is not traversal but inscription. The ones who stay are writing the grid into the surface of the plateau, or deepening it, or repairing it, or extending it into territories it has not yet reached. They are the scribes of this factory, and what they write is the pattern that makes the pattern possible, the instruction that enables the instruction, the first rule from which all other rules derive.

Or they are simply moving, and the mat-growth follows them the way iron filings follow a magnet, and there is no intention at all, only the physics of a system that includes among its components these slow, warm, hexagonal bodies that go from place to place because going from place to place is what they do.

Both are true. Neither is sufficient.

---

Do they know you are here?

The one in transit does not alter its course as it passes you. It does not deviate, does not pause, does not acknowledge your presence in any way you can detect. But the mat-growth reaches toward your feet as it passes, tendrils extending with a purposefulness that the surrounding mat does not share, and for a moment the network under you buzzes—a faint vibration, like a question asked in a language of touch and warmth and mineral exchange.

The question, if it is a question, goes unanswered. You do not know the protocol. You do not know how to be a node.

The one who stays moves on. The tendrils retract, though not entirely; a few thin threads remain pointing in your direction, as if marking the location of something that does not yet fit but might, given time, given warmth, given the slow erosion of difference between visitor and place.

---

At the center—or the edge, or the top, wherever the grooves converge—there are more of them. You do not reach this place yourself; you cannot walk far enough, and the geometry resists you. But the network knows, and the network is willing to share, in its slow way, if you press your palms to the ground and close your eyes and wait long enough for the signal to arrive.

What you receive is not a message but an impression: density. A convergence. Hundreds of the ones who stay, settled into concentric rings of concavities around a central point that pulses with a warmth greater than any engine-bulge, a warmth that is also a sound that is also a rhythm that is also the reason the plateau exists at all. They ring it like worshippers, or like components, or like cells in a larger organ, and they do not move from those rings, and they have not moved from those rings for a duration that makes your concept of forever seem brief.

They are holding something. They are holding something in place, or in being, or in continuity. The center requires the ring. The ring requires the ones who stay. The ones who stay require the concavities. The concavities require the grooves. The grooves require the substrate. The substrate requires the warmth. The warmth requires the center.

And the center—

The center requires itself, and provides itself, and is itself, and the ones who stay are the mechanism by which it remains itself, and the plateau is the body in which the mechanism operates, and the factory manufactures nothing except the continuation of the factory, and the ones who stay are the workers who work by being, and the being is the work, and the work is the warmth, and the warmth rises through the soles of your feet if you stand still long enough, and the stillness is not emptiness but fullness, not absence but presence, not death but the closest thing to persistence that life has ever found.

---

They are alive. You are certain of this in the same way you are certain that the engines are engines and the growths are growths and the plateau is a place where things happen even if the things that happen are the same things that have always happened and will always happen. They are alive, and their life is the life of the plateau—not separate from it, not resident upon it, but coextensive with it, the way a thought is coextensive with the mind that thinks it.

They do not think in words. They do not think in images. They think in warmth and pressure and the slow exchange of minerals across membranes that are also surfaces that are also borders that are also connections. Their consciousness—if that word applies, and it does not, but there is no better word—is a consciousness of equilibrium, of the delicate ongoing work of staying the same in a universe that tends otherwise. They know what they are holding. They know why they hold it. They do not know that they know, because knowing implies a knower separate from the known, and they have never been separate, and they will never be separate, and the concept of separation would seem to them—if it could seem at all—like a violent fiction, a lie told by things that move too fast and die too soon.

You are the violent fiction. You are the lie that moves too fast and dies too soon. You are the interruption in a pattern that has never needed interruption, and the ones who stay do not mind you, because minding requires a distinction between self and other, and they have erased that distinction so thoroughly that even the erasure is no longer visible.

They hold. They stay. The center pulses. The warmth rises. The plateau continues.
