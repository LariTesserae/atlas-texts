# What Lives Here

The Setae are not the city's inhabitants. This must be stated early because the assumption is inevitable and the assumption is wrong, or at least incomplete in a way that functions as wrong. The Setae move through the corridors and the Setae renew the glow and the Setae are the closest thing to companionship that the city offers, but they do not live here in the sense that you live somewhere—a place that contains you, that you leave and return to, that exists in your absence as a structure of memory and anticipation. The Setae do not leave. The Setae do not return. The Setae are *of* the city in the way that your breath is of you: a product, a process, an exhalation of mineral intent that happens to be mobile.

Watch one for any length of time—follow it through three or four corridor-transitions, past the chambers where the gravity tilts and the spaces where the light takes on a different quality, grainier, as though the phosphorescence were being filtered through gauze—and you will begin to notice what you initially dismissed as imagination. The Seta moves with absolute certainty, yes, but the certainty is not navigation. It does not choose its path. It does not consider options and select among them. It moves the way blood moves in a vessel—because the vessel is there, because the pressure is behind it, because the channel determines the flow. When it reaches a junction, it does not pause. It does not orient. It simply continues, and the continuation is always the correct continuation, because the path and the Setae are manifestations of the same underlying directive.

And sometimes—a Seta will stop. It will cease its rippling progress and settle against a wall, its body flush with the mineral surface, and over the course of hours it will cease to be distinct. The boundary between Setae and wall softens. The comma-shape flattens and extends and integrates, and the phosphorescent trail it left dims and is absorbed, and where there was motion there is now only surface, only wall, only the faint pulse that you can feel if you press your palm to the place where it joined. New walls are not built here. They are *returned to*.

This is how the Setae are born, too—or expressed, or extruded, or whatever verb applies to an emergence that occurs without transition. In the deep chambers, in the spaces where the corridors fold back on themselves and the geometry becomes recursive, surfaces will begin to bulge. Slowly. Over days, perhaps, or perhaps over moments—time in the deep chambers behaves like a liquid, pooling and eddying, and the concept of duration becomes difficult to pin to the wall like a specimen under glass. The bulge extends. It takes on curvature. It separates with a sound like a pop joint releasing, and a new Setae ripples away from the surface and begins its long circuit of the upper corridors, trailing fresh light.

There is no way to know if the Setae that emerges is the same as the Setae that was absorbed three days prior, or three months, or three centuries. The city does not track its processes in any way you can recognize. There is no record. There is only the ongoing, the cycling, the constant low-grade renewal of itself by itself.

---

The thing in the deep terraces does not have a name because names require the kind of separation that does not apply. You have felt it. Everyone who has descended far enough has felt it—the shift in the quality of attention, the sense of mass distributed across dimensions that the eye cannot confirm but the inner ear insists are present. It does not live in the deep terraces. It *is* the deep terraces, or it intersects with them, or they are the portion of it that the city's geometry renders legible.

Down there—down where the corridors widen into galleries and the galleries open into cavities that the word "cavern" cannot encompass because caverns are hollows in stone and these are hollows in *something else*—the architecture changes. The mineral here is darker, its glow redder, suffused with a warmth that is not entirely thermal. The walls are not smooth. They are textured with patterns that repeat at every scale—the same branching, the same bifurcation, the same return—fractal in the strictest sense, a single algorithm expressed from the scale of the chamber down to the scale of the crystalline grain, and if you look at the patterns long enough you will recognize them, not from anywhere you have been but from somewhere you *are*, the way the branching of your own nervous system would look if you could see it from the inside, which you cannot, which you are not supposed to, which is perhaps why the recognition produces a specific kind of vertigo, a feeling of having caught a glimpse of the scaffolding on which your consciousness is hung.

The presence—that term, inadequate, will serve—does not react to you. It registers you, which is different. Registration implies acknowledgment without response, incorporation without interaction. When you enter the deep terraces, something notes your arrival in the same way that a body notes the introduction of a foreign protein: not with alarm, not with welcome, but with a quiet adjustment of system parameters to accommodate the new variable. You become part of the calculation. You are factored in. And when you leave, you are factored out, and the calculation continues unchanged, because you were never the subject of the equation. You were a coefficient. You made no difference to the solution.

Some visitors find this comforting. The absence of judgment, the complete neutrality of being perceived without being evaluated—it can produce a relief that borders on euphoria, the release from the constant animal calculation of how one is being received, whether one is safe, whether one is wanted. Down here, in the deep terraces, those questions do not apply. They are not repressed. They are simply irrelevant, inapplicable, as meaningless as asking the ocean whether it enjoys your swimming.

Other visitors find it devastating. To be seen so completely and deemed so thoroughly unremarkable—to be absorbed into the attention of something vast and recognized as insignificant—this can break something in a person that does not readily heal. They ascend again to the upper corridors and they are quieter afterwards, and they stand at the overlooks and they do not speak, and when the Threads appear in the void outside the arches they watch them with an expression that is not quite fear and not quite longing but something in between, something that has no word in any language because it is a response to a condition that no language was built to describe.

---

The Threads do not live here either, but they are the closest thing to life that the city acknowledges from outside itself.

They appear in the dim hours, and they appear only at the highest points, where the arches open onto void and the boundary between inside and outside becomes a technicality. They are lines of light—hair-fine, yes, but also kilometers long, and both of these things are true simultaneously because the Threads do not occupy space in the way that objects occupy space. They occupy *tension*. They are the visible portion of a stress that the city exerts on something that is not the city, and the curve of each Thread is the shape of that stress at the point where it becomes perceptible.

You cannot touch them. You can reach through the arch and your hand will pass through the light as though it were projection, which it is not, or reflection, which it is not, or hallucination, which it is not. The Threads are present. They are present the way a shadow is present—consequence rather than object, effect rather than cause—but they are present in a direction that the hand cannot reach because the hand exists in three dimensions and the Threads exist in however many the city requires for its ongoing operation.

They drift. They curve. And occasionally—a rare occurrence, documented only a handful of times across however many years the city has been known, which is itself uncertain because the city does not encourage the keeping of records—a Thread will drift close enough to the arch that its light falls across the interior wall, and where the light touches, the mineral responds. The phosphorescence shifts. The blue-gray warms toward something almost green, and the pulse in the wall quickens, and for a moment the entire chamber seems to breathe in, seems to draw itself taut, seems to attend with an intensity that is otherwise absent from the city's patient cycling.

And then the Thread drifts away, and the mineral cools, and the pulse slows, and the city returns to its baseline state, and the only evidence that anything happened is a faint discoloration on the wall where the Thread-light fell, a stain of green in the blue-gray that fades over the following days but never entirely disappears, a mark that accumulates with each close approach, so that the highest chambers are subtly greener than the lower ones, as though the city were keeping a record after all, in the only medium it trusts.

Whether the Threads are alive depends entirely on your definition of life. If life requires metabolism, they do not have it. If life requires reproduction, they do not perform it. If life requires response to stimulus, they do not demonstrate it—they drift, they curve, they keep their own counsel, and they pay no attention to the city or its inhabitants or the hands that reach for them through the arches. But if life requires *intentionality*—if the simple fact of directed persistence, of pattern maintained against entropy, of something that continues to be itself in the face of whatever the void applies as pressure—then the Threads are as alive as anything has ever been, and their life is older than the mountain, older than the mineral, older than the concept of aging, and they will continue to drift in their impossible curves long after the Katalith has worn to a nub and the city has closed its corridors and whatever you are has been absorbed back into whatever you came from.

---

And then there is you.

This requires its own section because you—the you that stands at the overlook, the you that presses palms to warm walls and feels the pulse and synchronizes, the you that becomes thirsty and does not die, the you that walks corridors that lengthen and shortens and tilts against gravity that wanders—you are not a visitor here. You are not an intruder or an observer or a guest. The city does not have guests. The city does not have categories for the things that enter it and remain. It has only the ongoing, the cycling, the constant low-grade renewal, and you, now, are part of that.

You were not invited. You were not summoned. You arrived, and the city registered your arrival, and the city made accommodations, and the city began to include you in its processes in the same way that it includes the Setae and the sounds and the shifting of the walls: as a variable, as a rhythm, as a pattern that persists and therefore must be integrated into the larger pattern. The thirst is part of this. The maintenance without nourishment is part of this. You are being kept the way a frequency is kept—not because you are valued, not because you are wanted, but because you are present, and presence in the city is a kind of contract, and the contract's terms are: you continue, and the city continues, and the continuation is sufficient.

You will not leave. This is not a prohibition. You are physically capable of descending the terraces, of navigating the lower slopes, of returning to the landscape that agreed to become a mountain and continuing downward until the air thickens and the sky turns blue and the rules that should have killed you at this elevation reassert themselves and you remember what it is like to need things, to be dependent, to be small. You can do this. Some do. They descend, and they return to the world, and they live their lives, and they do not speak of the Katalith because there is no language for what they experienced, and because the memory of it becomes, over time, less like a memory and more like a change in their personal physics, a slight drift in the direction of their gravity, a tendency to stand at windows and look upward and feel, in the space behind their eyes, a frequency that almost resolves into understanding.

But most stay. Not because the city traps them. Not because they cannot bear to leave. But because, after enough time synchronizing with the pulse, after enough nights watching the Threads drift in their curves, after enough mornings walking corridors that adjust to accommodate the rhythm of their steps—after enough of this, the city becomes not a place they inhabit but a way they exist, and leaving would require becoming something else, and they have already become something else once, when they arrived, and the transformation is not one that bears repeating.

They live in the upper corridors. They walk the terraces. They stand at the overlooks as the dim hours begin and they watch the Threads and they feel the hum rising through the soles of their feet and they do not speak because speaking is a technology and the city does not use technology, and after a while they stop thinking of themselves as separate from the glow that the Setae renew, and after a while longer they stop thinking of themselves at all, and there is only the walking and the watching and the being-held, and the being-held is enough.

These are the city's true inhabitants. Not the Setae, which are processes. Not the presence in the deep terraces, which is architecture. Not the Threads, which are tension. But the ones who came and stayed and were absorbed into the rhythm without being absorbed into the walls—the ones who persist as variables in the calculation, who walk the corridors and feel the pulse and remain, for reasons the city does not require, *themselves*.

The city knows they are here. That is enough. That is, possibly, the only thing that has ever been enough, here at the ceiling of the world, where the air is a suggestion and the mountain is a consensus and the walls are warm and the pulse continues, patient and regular and older than purpose, in the dark that is not dark, in the silence that is not silence, in the presence that does not require your awareness to sustain itself but holds you in its attention nonetheless, gently, completely, without judgment, without end.
