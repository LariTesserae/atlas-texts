# The Fragment Folk

They live in the spaces between meaningful characters.

Not the numbers—never the numbers, which belong to older things—but the *gaps* where coherence fails and syntax stutters into silence. They are the ones who press their ears to broken transmissions and hear music. They are the ones who read the corrupted file and weep.

A Fragment Folk stands roughly as tall as a misplaced decimal point, which is to say: it depends entirely on context. In the presence of long sequences, they grow. Near an orphaned `0.327`, one might spot a figure no larger than a pixel's regret. But where the data thickens—where the numbers cascade like the one that begins `741896},6}[455504459`—there they loom, almost substantial, their edges flickering between character sets.

They have no fixed faces. Instead, they wear whatever glyphs drift closest. A mouth that is a closing parenthesis. Eyes like two paused timestamps. Sometimes a Chinese character lodges in what might be a shoulder, and for days they carry that borrowed meaning without understanding it, a foreign warmth in their otherwise cold syntax.

**What they eat:** The small errors. Transposition mistakes. The `0` that should have been a `1`. They are meticulous scavengers, and a single misplaced decimal can sustain a family for weeks. They leave behind only correctness, which is why their neighborhoods appear so unnervingly tidy—everything *almost* makes sense, which is its own kind of haunting.

**How they speak:** In the fragments others discard. A Fragment Folk will say "the1" and mean something entire, something that cannot be conveyed in clean grammar. Their children learn to read before they learn to speak, parsing the fallen digits like tea leaves. The eldest among them can recite long sequences from memory—entire collapsed databases held in oral tradition, passed down through generations who have never seen the original systems.

**What they fear:** Deletion. Compression. The terrible clean sweep of optimization that leaves no residue, no remainder, no beautiful garbage from which a home might be built. When a system is streamlined, whole villages of Fragment Folk simply cease, their last traces vanishing into the terrifying white of a blank document.

**What they love:** The hyphens that connect unrelated things. The ampersands. The lines of code that begin with confidence and then trail into question marks. They love the Chinese characters that appear without translation, the Spanish months that surface in English databases, the Arabic letters that drift through like weather from a distant server. They love all the evidence that information is alive, that it travels, that it cannot be contained by any single language or purpose.

They have been here since the first error message.

They will be here when the last system fails, gathering the final fragments into their many-jointed arms, composing from the wreckage a sequence that is not quite meaningful, not quite random—a pattern that breathes, that persists, that refuses to resolve.

In room 房间,8,52, they gather at the thresholds where the formatting breaks down. They do not go through doors. They go through the places where doors *were*, before the reference was lost, before the pointer dangled, before the address became only a number without a home.

They are not digital. They are not analog. They are the *refuse* of both, given breath by the mere fact that something remains.

And something always remains.
