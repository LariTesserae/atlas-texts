# The Residents

There is a woman who lives in Sector 743-Rho, or what was Sector 743-Rho before the last Build pulse reorganized the western junctions. She may live in Sector 743-Rho still; the designation plates changed but the walls mostly did not, and she has never consulted them anyway. She has no use for the Stratum's naming conventions. She has names of her own.

She is old. She was not always old. She came here when she was young — when the Stratum was smaller, if the Stratum was ever smaller, and she insists it was though the erosion parameters of every surface around her suggest otherwise. She remembers a time when you could walk from the eastern observation port to the western drainage tanks in under an hour. Now the route requires navigation of seventeen vertical transitions and at least two passages that were not built for human dimensions, and the observation port has been built over three times, each new layer preserving a view of the one before it like sediment preserving the shape of a creature that died in the mud. She does not go to the observation port anymore. She does not go anywhere that requires her to leave the chambers she knows.

Her chambers are four, or five, or six — the number shifts. One serves as a sleeping space, though she does not sleep the way she used to, does not sleep the way anyone used to before they came to the Stratum. Sleep here comes in fragments, in installations, fitted between the moments when the Build pulses shake the walls and the fauna skitter through the corridors with their urgent small errands. She sleeps when the quiet is deepest, and the quiet is deepest just after a Build, when the new surfaces are settling and the growths have not yet found the fresh seams. She sleeps like the Stratum sleeps — briefly, functionally, without surrender.

She knows things. This is what makes her dangerous, or useful, or both.

She knows the Build schedules — not the next one, nobody knows the next one, but the rhythm of them, the way they cluster and space like the breathing of something vast and slow. She knows that after three close pulses, a fourth follows within a day. She knows that a long silence means a large addition is coming, something that will reorganize entire corridors, will open chambers that were not there before, will seal passages that had been thoroughfares for as long as anyone can remember. She knows this the way you know the weather in a place you have lived your whole life: not through data but through your body's accumulation of patterns, the barometric ache in joints that have been broken and healed and broken again.

She was not the first. There were others before her — she speaks of them sometimes, when she speaks at all. The man who lived in the drainage chambers and mapped the water routes, who believed the condensation cycles encoded messages from whatever had designed the Stratum. The pair who came together and separated, one moving upward toward the outer surfaces and one moving downward into the dense regions, and neither was seen again but sometimes you can find their marks on the walls — scratches, notations, the remnants of a numbering system that made sense to them and no one else. The children. She does not talk about the children. She changes the subject with a gesture that takes in the whole of the chamber, the pipes and the growths and the ever-present light, and she says, "Look at this. Look at what it made today."

---

There is a man who lives in the dense regions, where the geometry folds and the passages go down and down and arrive where they began. He has been here longer than the woman. He has been here longer than he remembers being anywhere else.

He was not born here. He was born somewhere with soil and sky, somewhere the light came from a single source and the water fell from clouds. He remembers this the way you remember a story someone told you long ago — the details are vivid but they do not feel like yours. The sun was warm. The grass was green. The words are correct but they have no weight, no texture, no conviction. What he remembers with conviction is the day he found the door.

The door was not remarkable. It was set in a wall that should have been an exterior wall — he was on the edge of the Stratum then, when there were still edges you could reach — and it should have opened onto sky and the long drop. Instead it opened onto a corridor that led to another corridor that led to a chamber he had never seen, and that chamber led deeper, and deeper, and he followed because the alternative was going back, and going back meant returning to a place that had already begun to feel like a memory.

He lives in the folds now. He has adapted in ways the woman has not — his eyes have adjusted to the brighter regions and the dimmer ones with equal facility, his sense of direction no longer relies on topology, his body has learned to fit through spaces that would defeat someone who expected geometry to behave. He eats what the Stratum provides. This is not a metaphor. The threadlike growths that emerge from the seams — he has learned which ones will sustain him and which will make him sick and which will make him see things that are not there, or things that are there but not visible to ordinary perception. He eats the growths and he drinks the condensation and he is not well but he is not dying, and in the Stratum that distinction is the best you can hope for.

He moves through the dense regions with an ease that the woman would not recognize as human. He knows where the pipes pulse and where they are merely pipes. He knows which cable bundles will flinch from pressure and which are truly inert. He has learned to read the fauna — not communicate with them, he does not flatter himself, but *read* them, the way you read the behavior of animals before a storm. When the insect-things cluster on a particular wall, a Build is coming to that location. When the bird-things abandon a chamber, something is about to change in the water cycle. When the skittering-things carry their fragments to a new junction, a passage is about to open or close, and their deposits are the first stage of the architecture that will fill the gap.

He has become part of the system. He knows this. He does not mind.

Sometimes he finds things. Artifacts of previous occupancy — a tool that fits no hand he can imagine, a panel with controls that respond to pressure in ways that produce no visible effect, a chamber where the light is a color he has no name for and the air tastes of something that might be a memory of fruit. He collects these things. He arranges them in a chamber he thinks of as his, though the Stratum does not recognize ownership and the fauna use the space as freely as he does, moving around and over and sometimes through his arrangements with the proprietary ease of creatures who were here before him and will be here after.

He does not know what the things are for. He does not know what he is for. He is content with this.

---

There are others.

A child — or a thing that was once a child and is now something else, something that moves through the ventilation shafts with a fluidity that suggests its bones have become optional. It speaks, when it speaks at all, in a language that uses the same sounds as speech but arranges them into patterns that mean differently. The woman understands it sometimes. The man in the dense regions understands it always. It does not come to the outer chambers except after a Build, when it emerges to inspect the new construction with eyes that reflect the Stratum's distributed light like the eyes of the fauna, like the surfaces of the pipes, like everything here that watches and is watched.

A pair — or a group, or a collective, the boundaries are unclear — who live in the rain chambers and have adapted to the forty-minute cycles of precipitation in ways that go beyond mere tolerance. Their skin has changed. Their lungs have changed. They move through ankle-deep water with the same ease the man moves through non-Euclidean corridors, and when they stand still the water pools around them as if recognizing something, as if the cycle that serves no purpose and cures no illness has found in them a reason to continue.

A thing that might be a person and might be a Build-construct and might be a category the Stratum has invented that does not exist anywhere else. It appears in the corridors after large Build pulses and moves through the new architecture with obvious familiarity, touching surfaces that are still forming, adjusting connections that have not yet settled, and then it vanishes into passages that were not there before it entered them. The woman has seen it. The man has seen it. The child-thing follows it when it appears, keeping a distance, watching with its reflecting eyes. No one has spoken to it. No one is sure it can hear.

And others. More others than anyone has counted, because counting requires a stable population and the Stratum does not provide stability. People come — or they are produced, or they emerge, or they were always here and simply became visible when the geometry shifted to accommodate them — and people go, or are absorbed, or walk into a corridor that was not there before and do not return. The population is a parameter the Stratum adjusts without consultation, without explanation, without apparent purpose beyond the purpose of all its processes: *addition*. More. Continue. Build.

---

The woman in Sector 743-Rho has begun to change.

She notices it in small ways. Her sense of time, which was never reliable here, has become more unreliable — she cannot tell if an hour has passed or a day, and her body no longer provides consistent signals. Hunger comes and goes without relationship to intake. Her skin has developed a faint luminescence in dim passages, a quality she noticed first in the periphery of her vision and assumed was reflected light until she held her hand in a chamber with no light sources at all and watched it glow, faintly, with the same distributed radiance as the Stratum's panels.

She does not talk about this. She talks about the Build schedules, the fauna behaviors, the water cycles. She shares her knowledge with anyone who comes to her chambers seeking it, and they come less often now because the routes to her sector have become more complex, and because her knowledge, while accurate, has begun to feel like the knowledge of a thing that is becoming part of what it studies.

She has found growths in her sleeping chamber. The threadlike pale filaments that emerge from seams in the walls — they have begun to grow toward her, or toward the place where she sleeps, reaching across the plates with a slowness that is visible only over days but is undeniably present. She pulls them away. They return. She has begun to wonder, in the moments between waking and the Stratum's version of sleep, what would happen if she let them reach her. If she let the ecology of the constructed find in her what it finds in the seams and the joints and the spaces between surfaces.

The man in the dense regions would understand. The man in the dense regions has already let the growths reach him — has let them twine around his fingers when he sleeps, has let the bird-things perch on his shoulders and probe the seams of his clothing for organic matter, has let the boundary between himself and the architecture become as blurred as the boundary between fauna and infrastructure. He would tell her, if she asked, that it is not painful. That it is not even unpleasant. That it is simply the next stage of being in a place that is always building, always adding, always becoming more of what it already is.

She does not ask.

She pulls the growths away, and she watches the light panels, and she waits for the next Build, and she continues.

---

This is who lives in the Stratum: those who came and those who emerged and those who were always here, and the categories blur, and the boundaries shift, and the architecture grows around and through and into them, and they adapt or they are adapted to, and the quiet holds, and the light stays on, and the Build pulses come and come and come.

The woman. The man. The child-thing. The rain-chamber collective. The maybe-construct. The others whose names and natures shift with the geometry. The fauna that are fauna and the fauna that are infrastructure and the infrastructure that pulses and flinches and grows.

All of them residents. All of them building materials. All of them becoming what the Stratum requires them to be, which is *present*, which is *incorporated*, which is part of the endless addition that extends in every direction with no ground and no ceiling and no end.

The Stratum grows.

It was always growing.

And so are they.
