# The Vegetation

They have no name. They have never needed one. Names are for things that might be addressed, and nothing here addresses anything.

---

They are not a they. This is the first error any observer makes—the imposition of plurality on what may be a single organism distributed across seven fissures, or seventy, or some number that fluctuates on a timescale too slow for counting. The clusters of three or five may be fingers of the same hand, protrusions of the same root-system, the same body pressing upward through the mineral crust wherever the crust permits. Or they may be individuals who have never touched, never communicated, never acknowledged each other's existence. There is no way to know. They offer nothing by which to distinguish self from other. They are pale and they are present and they do not change, and if they are one or many, they keep the secret with the same absolute indifference.

---

They are older than the seam.

This is difficult to accept because the seam is the only entrance, and the logic of entrances implies that nothing arrived before the opening. But the vegetation was here. The mineral record—compressed in the biofilm like fossils in shale—preserves ghostly traces of their former positions, hollows where they stood before the walls grew around them and then receded, over and over, a slow tide of mineralization that lifts and falls on a cycle longer than civilizations. They have been emerging and being consumed and emerging again since the chamber was less of a chamber and more of a slow collision, when the growing things had not yet agreed to leave this pocket unfilled and were still negotiating the terms.

They may be the negotiators.

They may be the terms.

---

They do not think. This must be said plainly, because the mind insists on finding cognition in anything that persists, anything that outlasts us. The vegetation is not thinking. It is not planning, not remembering, not deciding. It is occupying its fissure with the same thoroughness that the walls occupy everything else—a directional pressure, a tendency, an inclination so ingrained it has become indistinguishable from the substance of the thing itself. The walls incline toward closure. The vegetation inclines toward emergence. Neither inclination is deliberate. Both are structural. They are what they do, and they do what they are, and there is no space between the two where something like choice could enter.

And yet.

The spacing. Three or five. Never more, never less. Always the same distance between clusters, a distance that corresponds to nothing obvious—no wavelength, no chemical gradient, no structural feature of the fissure they inhabit. It is a pattern that recurs without cause, without variation, without the possibility of learning or transmission. It simply is. The way a crystal lattice is. The way a Fibonacci sequence unfolds in a sunflower head—not because the sunflower knows mathematics, but because the mathematics and the sunflower are the same process, viewed from different speeds.

Perhaps three or five is how the chamber counts. Perhaps it is the only number the chamber knows.

---

They do not sense you.

This is the hardest part. You enter through the seam that was not a door, and you carry with you every assumption about encounter: that presence demands acknowledgment, that proximity creates relationship, that to be in the same space is to be in the same story. But the vegetation does not register your arrival. Your shadow—your opacity, your darkness—falls across them and they do not flinch, do not turn, do not alter their metabolism by a single degree. You could uproot them and they would not resist. You could press your palm against their surface and feel nothing—no pulse, no warmth, no response except the mild resistance of a thing that has never needed to be firm.

They are not ignoring you. Ignorance requires the capacity for attention. They are simply not in the category of things that attend. You are an event too brief, too irregular, too novel for a process that has been running since before novelty was possible. You are a weather pattern that passes too quickly for the climate to notice. You are a sound at a frequency the ear cannot construe.

You are, for the only time in your life, truly irrelevant.

---

They are not suffering.

The chamber is closing. The seam is sealing. In a million years—a blink, a hesitation, a single breath in the long respiration of the biofilm—the vegetation will have nowhere to emerge. The fissures will have healed. The mineralization will have covered the last trace of them, and the chamber will be full of itself entirely, complete at last, a body with no vacant spaces, no concessions, no thin places where something pale and colorless might press upward into the light that is not for them.

And the vegetation will end. Or it will not. It may persist inside the walls, threaded through the calcium carbonate like copper wire through insulation, still emerging in whatever microscopic voids remain, still growing in clusters of three or five, still following a blueprint it was issued at the beginning. It may become part of the mineral record, a trace element in the biofilm, a stripe of chitin in the strata that future geologists will puzzle over if future geologists ever find their way into a chamber that has sealed itself against entry. It may simply wait—the way everything here waits, without impatience, without expectation—for the next seam, the next wound, the next moment of interruption in the body's long project of becoming itself.

They do not know the chamber is closing. They do not know the seam opened. They do not know you are standing there, opaque and dark and brief, watching them with eyes that evolved for faces, for movement, for the constant flicker of animal recognition that means *predator* or *prey* or *mate* or *danger*.

They are not watching you back.

---

They are what lives here.

This is the answer, and it is unsatisfying, and it is true. What lives in the Chamber in the Body of the World is something that does not move, does not respond, does not think, does not suffer, does not know it is alive by any definition that living things would recognize. It is a process that has been happening for longer than the mineral record reliably records. It is a tendency toward emergence. It is a pattern of three or five. It is pale and colorless and translucent and small.

It is the only thing in this space that the word *alive* can touch. And the word *alive* cannot touch it. It slips off, the way water slips off chitin. The word was made for something else—for blood and breath and the desperate engine of staying alive, the gasping insistence on continuance that characterizes everything we recognize as being. The vegetation does not insist. It persists. It is the difference between a shout and a hum, between a flame and a glow, between a body that fights to remain a body and a body that has never conceived of being anything else.

It does not want to be here.

It does not want to leave.

---

It is what remains when everything that can move has moved on.
