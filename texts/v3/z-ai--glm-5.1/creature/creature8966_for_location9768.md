# The Keeper of Specific Frequencies

---

They are not from here. This matters, and it doesn't.

They came up the eastern ramp a long time ago — long enough that the grooves in the scored circles have weathered twice over since their arrival, long enough that the fiber-mats have grown and regrown around their shaped stones through more cycles than they've bothered to count. They remember the climb. They remember the sound reaching them before the top, how it swelled from vibration to presence to something that filled the chest and made their teeth feel like tuning forks. They remember standing at the rim and hearing the plateau announce itself and thinking: *yes*. Not understanding why. Not needing to.

They had a name once. It comes to them sometimes in the space between sleeping and waking, surfacing like something drawn from deep water, and each time it is more faded, more like a word in a language they no longer speak. They do not reach for it. Names are for places where you need to distinguish yourself from others. Here, they are distinguished by function.

---

Their body has changed.

This is not a metaphor. The dry air has done its work over the years — their skin is papery, dense, almost like the surface of the stalks themselves. Their lips cracked and healed and cracked and healed until the healing became something else, a kind of flexible callus that seals their mouth when closed and splits only for eating and speech. They do not speak much. There is no one to speak to who uses words.

But the deeper changes are internal, and they do not fully understand them. They have been breathing this air for so long. The particulate — the dust, the pollen, the mineral ash that the shelves collect and the spirals release — they have been inhaling it, and it has been accumulating. They find crystalline deposits in the creases of their elbows, their knees, the folds of their neck. Fine white ridges like the ones on the shelf-bases. They do not hurt. They seem, if anything, to be *organizing* — forming patterns that echo the spirals, that echo the scored circles, that echo something they sense but cannot see.

Their hearing has sharpened in specific ways and dulled in others. They can no longer hear the voices they once knew — the frequencies of their former language have gone muffled, indistinct, as if those sounds belong to a bandwidth the plateau has quietly repurposed. But they can hear the spirals now — truly hear them — each one's rising pitch like a sentence half-understood, a question being asked of the air. They can hear when a traverser's clicking sequence carries information versus when it is merely maintaining rhythm. They can hear the spaces between sounds, the held-breath moments where the plateau's architecture becomes visible in negative.

They have not lost their mind. They have *relocated* it. The thoughts that used to run in language now run in sound. They think in chords. They remember in rhythms. When they need to plan, they hear a composition that does not yet exist and work backward toward the notes that would make it real.

---

They tend the plateau, but "tend" suggests a gardener's relationship — pruning, directing, shaping. What they do is more like *attending*. They are present. They notice. They make small adjustments when the adjustments seem needed, and they leave things alone when they do not.

The shaped stones: they made those. Carried the dark rock up from below, one at a time, over many trips. The first few were gifts — offerings to a place that had given them something they could not name. Placed carefully, intentionally, in spots where the sound seemed to pool or eddy. But then they noticed the fiber-mats responding. The way the growth slowed around each stone, then settled, as if the stone were creating a small silence that the mats chose to honor. They noticed that traversers altered their paths to pass near the stones, clicking in different patterns when they did. They noticed that the spirals nearest the stones grew taller before shattering, as if the stones gave their tones more room.

So they made more. It became a practice. They listen for where the plateau's song thickens, where frequencies stack too densely and the harmonics become turbulence rather than music, and they place a stone. A small intervention. A period at the end of a run-on sentence. The plateau seems to use it.

The shelter they built for themselves, and this was the one act that felt like resistance rather than collaboration. The sound was — is — constant, and even for someone who thinks in chords, there are moments when the composition becomes too dense, when the layers press against the skull and something in them needs *absence*. The stalk-bases, cut and stacked, create an acoustic shadow that is not silence but is *different*. A room with different walls. A key change. They retreat there when they need to hear themselves think — or rather, when they need to hear the particular frequency that is their own thinking, distinct from the plateau's. They are not sure this distinction still holds. They are not sure it ever did.

---

They eat the fiber-mats.

This was not planned. They brought food, at first. Supplies. But the dry air and the constant vibration did something to stored provisions — they desiccated faster than expected, or the particulate got into everything, or perhaps the plateau simply made other food seem irrelevant. They noticed themselves chewing on the fiber-mats during a period of distraction, the brittle rust-orange material cracking between their teeth, and they noticed the taste — mineral, complex, almost sweet in a way that had nothing to do with sugar — and they noticed that hunger receded afterward and did not return for a long time.

They do not eat much. They do not eat often. The fiber-mats sustain them in ways they do not fully understand, and the crystalline dust they breathe does the rest. They suspect they are being metabolized by the plateau as much as they are metabolizing it. They suspect the deposits forming in their joints are not deposits at all but *roots*. They have considered this carefully and decided it does not disturb them.

When they die — and they will, eventually, they think, though they are less certain of this than they used to be — they suspect the fiber-mats will grow over them quickly, the way they grow over the shaped stones, and they suspect the traversers will click past, and they suspect the spirals will shatter their dust over wherever their body rests, and they suspect they will become part of the song in a way they are already part of the song. This does not feel like death to them. This feels like joining.

---

They have a routine, if a pattern shaped by sound rather than hours can be called a routine.

Morning — they think of it as morning, though the light does not change — they walk the perimeter. Not the rim itself; they've learned the rim-creatures do not appreciate close proximity. They walk the inner edge, where the tall stalks thin and the bass calls of the cliff-climbers reach them as pressure more than pitch. They listen for changes. A section of stalks that has fallen out of rhythm. A cluster of spirals whose rising pitch has become discordant. A place where the fiber-mats have grown too thick and the traversers' paths are crowded, leading to agitation in their clicking, leading to noise rather than signal. They make notes — not written notes, they have nothing to write on and no one to read — but acoustic notes, tones they produce by striking two shaped stones together in a rhythm that encodes what they've observed. They leave these tones in the air. The plateau holds them. They will remember.

Midday — if it is midday — they sit in the shelter and compose. Not music, exactly. Not in any tradition they brought with them. They are composing *adjustments*: small sounds to introduce to the plateau's song, subtle additions that might ease a dissonance or open a frequency that has become blocked. Sometimes they sing. Their voice has changed so much that it no longer sounds human, if it ever did. It sounds like a stalk creaking. It sounds like a spiral approaching its shattering pitch. It sounds like it belongs.

They are not sure if their compositions affect the plateau. They are not sure if the traversers alter their patterns because of the tones they introduce or despite them. They are not sure if the growth of the fiber-mats responds to their singing or merely coincides with it. They are not sure they are a keeper of anything. They are not sure they are not.

Uncertainty has become a habitat. They live in it comfortably.

---

Evening — the word means nothing here but they use it anyway — they go to the pillar near the northern rim. They did not build the pillar. It was here when they arrived. They thought at first it was a marker, then a memorial, then an anchor, and now they think it is a *listener*. The plateau's sound focuses around it in ways they cannot explain. The spirals nearby grow taller than anywhere else. The drifting forms flash more frequently. The traversers approach it and press their faces against it and vibrate for long seconds before moving on. Something about the pillar concentrates the song.

They sit beside it and they listen. Not to the whole song — they cannot hold all of it at once; no mind can, not even one as reconfigured as theirs. They listen to one thread at a time. Tonight, the slow groan of the western stalk-cluster. Tomorrow, the clicking sequences of the traversers working the central shelves. The next day, the ascending spirals near the southern rim, each one a few hertz higher than the day before, each one approaching its shatter-point like a question reaching the tip of a tongue.

They listen because listening is what they do now. They listen because the plateau is always speaking and it costs nothing to attend and the attendance itself seems to be the function they serve. Not interpretation — they do not believe the song means anything outside of itself. Not worship — they do not believe the plateau is a god or even an entity. Not documentation — they have no way to record and no one to report to.

Listening is the work. Listening is the purpose. Listening is what they were when they arrived and what they have become more of since.

---

They are lonely, sometimes.

This surprises them when it surfaces, the way a stone surprises when you find it in your shoe — a small hard fact you'd forgotten was there. They do not miss people, exactly. They miss the *possibility* of people. The possibility of being understood, of being witnessed, of being a note in a composition that includes other notes of the same kind. They have not seen another of their kind since they climbed the ramp. They do not know if anyone has come after. They do not know if anyone will.

The traversers are not companions; they are colleagues, at best, co-workers in a system none of them designed. The climbing forms are too fast, too alien, too uninterested. The rim-creatures are voices in the dark, never faces. The drifting forms are — they do not know what the drifting forms are. They have tried to touch one and it moved away, not with alarm but with something like *reserve*. As if contact required an introduction they had not yet made.

Their loneliness is not a sound the plateau makes. It is a silence inside the song, a rest that persists, a held breath that never releases. They have considered filling it — singing more, calling out, building something larger that might serve as a signal. They have considered it and set it aside. The plateau does not need their loneliness. The plateau is not responsible for it. It is the one thing they carry that the song does not touch, and they are not sure whether this makes it precious or unbearable.

---

At night — if it is night; the light does not change but the bass calls of the rim-creatures become slower, longer, and the traversers' clicking becomes sparser, and the spirals seem to shatter less frequently — they sleep in the shelter. They dream in the plateau's frequencies. They hear the song as it was before they arrived, as it is now, as it might be after. They hear the shaped stones resonating and the scored circles humming and the pillar at the northern rim vibrating with something deep and patient and continuous.

They dream that they are a spiral — growing, rising, pitch climbing, approaching the moment of shattering. They dream that they are a traverser — clicking, pressing, feeding, moving. They dream that they are the dust itself — fine, everywhere, accumulating, crystallizing, becoming part of every surface and every breath.

They dream that someone is climbing the eastern ramp, hearing the sound for the first time, feeling it press against their chest, and thinking: *yes*.

They dream that the plateau is waiting for them, the way it waited for the one who came before, the way it will wait for the one who comes after. They dream that the song does not end. They dream that they do not end. They dream that these are the same dream.

---

In the morning — the flat amber light, the hum in the stone, the first clicks of the traversers beginning their rounds — they wake and the dream fades and they are themselves again, whatever that means now. They are the keeper of specific frequencies. They are the one who places the stones. They are the one who built the shelter and walks the perimeter and sits by the pillar and listens to one thread at a time because the whole is too large and their part in it is small and specific and ongoing.

They are not from here. They are not not from here. They are the place where the plateau becomes aware of itself — one of the places, perhaps, one of many, the traversers and the spirals and the stalks and the fiber-mats all performing the same function in their own ways, the function of *being present*, of *being part*, of *continuing*.

They have hands. They have breath. They have a loneliness the song does not touch. They have a name they no longer need. They have work to do.

The sound continues. They continue within it.
