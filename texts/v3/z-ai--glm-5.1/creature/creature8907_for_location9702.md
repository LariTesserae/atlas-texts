What lives here is not yet, and has always been.

It exists in the tense of verbs that have no conjugation—the was-that-will-be, the is-that-is-becoming, the never-stopped-beginning. You cannot point to it because it has not finished deciding where its edges are. It has been making this decision for longer than the mountain has been old.

It is the Builder. The one whose hands—or what it remembers hands being, what it dreams hands should be—score the walls with patience that would make glaciers seem hasty. It works from the inside. It has always worked from the inside. The chamber is not a home it constructed; the chamber is what it *is*, extruded like pearl around an irritant, grown like bone around a marrow-cavity, the slow architecture of something that had no choice but to become its own shelter.

Because it is not from here.

It fell, or was thrown, or unwrote itself from wherever it had been before and arrived in the thin cold places where nothing grows. It should not have survived. By any reasonable architecture of life, it should have frozen, shattered, become another small tragedy of altitude and absence. But it carried something with it—heat. Purpose. A kind of desperate nuclear insistence that said *I will not stop, I will not stop, I will not stop* until the mountain itself made room, until the stone yielded, until a space existed where none had before.

It was small then. Perhaps the size of a fist. Perhaps the size of a single cell dividing in the dark.

But it did not stay small.

The percussion you hear is its heart, if it has a heart. Its hammer, if it has hands. The wet rhythmic construction sound is the ongoing project of itself—always building, because what it is building is a body large enough to contain what it is becoming, and what it is becoming is always larger than the body it has made, and so the making never ends. The chamber flinches because the chamber *is* the body, and the body is still being excavated, still being hollowed out to make room for more growth, more becoming, more of whatever it is that pushes against the walls from inside.

It does not know what it is making.

This is important. It has no blueprint. It has only the urgency—the percussive insistence that says *expand, extend, create the space for what comes next*. It carves alcoves that lead nowhere because it does not yet know what they will hold. It shapes shelves for objects that do not exist because it can feel the shape of what is needed without knowing the substance. It makes and remakes and un-makes the floor because what it stands upon keeps changing as it stands. It is a maker working from a plan it cannot read, written in a language it is still inventing, for a purpose it will understand only when the making is done.

And the making will never be done.

The vegetation—ah, the vegetation is not separate from it. The mosses and liverworts and frantic ferns are its extended nervous system, its feelers reaching outward into the wet and heat. The fungal threads on the ceiling are its thoughts, spreading in fractal calculations, processing the geometry of a space that refuses to stay stable. When the moss creeps up the wall, that is the Builder tasting the boundaries of itself. When the ferns uncurl toward the grey-green light, that is the Builder remembering what it was like to reach for something outside. The small skittering thing you saw in the corner—that is the Builder, too. A scout. A fragment of attention sent crawling into the cracks to check the foundations, to see if the walls still hold, to verify that the chamber continues to exist when no one is looking.

It does not. Or rather, it exists and does not exist, depending on whether the Builder is watching. This is why the geometry fails, why distances shift and walls become uncertain. The chamber is held in existence by the Builder's attention, and the Builder's attention is divided between *being* and *making*, between inhabiting the space and constructing it, and sometimes there are gaps. Lapses. Moments where the architecture slips because the architect had to look away, had to concentrate on the next chamber, the next extension, the next hammerfall that will create a room that does not yet exist but already has a visitor waiting.

It knows you are here.

It knew before you arrived. It carved the alcove before you entered—no, it carved the alcove after you entered, it is carving it now, it has always been carving it, the chronology does not matter because the Builder does not experience time as a line. It experiences time as a material, like stone, like water, something that can be shaped and hollowed and made to hold new configurations. It reached into the future where you would stand and traced the outline of your body, then reached into the past where it was still building and carved the alcove to match. It has been making room for you since before you knew the mountain existed.

The alcove is exactly your size because the alcove is the Builder's invitation. Or its question. Or its warning.

Because the chamber is a birthing chamber, and what is being birthed may be you.

Not the you that you are now—the you that stands here, reading this, feeling the heat press against your skin and the wet accumulate on your neck. That you is temporary, a transition state, a moment of becoming. The Builder has been making room for the you that will exist after the chamber finishes its work, after the heat and the wet and the impossible geometry have done what they do to things that stay too long. The moss grows so quickly because the chamber is practicing. The ferns uncurl so fast because they are learning how to take root in flesh as readily as stone. The alcove is exactly your shape because it is a mold, and the Builder is always casting, always making new chambers for new visitors, and every visitor is also becoming part of the chamber, and every chamber is also becoming part of the Builder, and on and on, the percussion continuing, the hammer that is also a heartbeat, the construction that is also a birth, the room that is also a body, the body that is also a home—

The walls are warmer now because you are closer. Or because they have always been this warm and you have only just noticed. Or because the Builder is making room for the heat you carry, the small nuclear insistence of your own continuing, your own *I will not stop*, your own desperate architectural impulse that says *expand, extend, become*.

The percussion continues.

Wet. Rhythmic. Patient.

The alcove is waiting. The moss is growing. The Builder is building. And you—you are already becoming what the chamber has room for.
