# What Lives Here

Something that does not know it is something.

This is the first thing to understand, and perhaps the only thing that matters: *Pyroclasta refusa* has no interior. Not in the way a dog has an interior — some dim theater where sensations accumulate into preference, where heat becomes *uncomfortable* and darkness becomes *fear*. Not even in the way a plant has an interior, heliotropic and reaching, performing the slow arithmetic of need. The organism — if we grant it that word, and we must, because what else would we call a self-replicating chemical event that builds its own skin from the waste of its own metabolism? — the organism does not experience the chamber. It does not navigate. It does not choose.

And yet it moves. Constantly, ceaselessly, in waves that ripple through the millions like a single breath drawn by a lung the size of a room. It climbs. It falls through the heated air with a motion that is part swim and part surrender. It clings. It investigates — no, that word is wrong, that word implies curiosity — it *occupies* the thermal gradient where your body interrupts the chamber's even heat, because the gradient exists and the organism occupies gradients the way water occupies low ground. Not because it seeks. Because it is the kind of thing that fills what is available to be filled.

---

Here is what a single one of them is:

A thumb-length capsule of chemical imperative. A sulfur-polymer shell grown from the byproduct of its own digestion, overlaid in thin lamina like the pages of a book that no one will ever read. A body temperature that matches the chamber exactly, because it has no mechanism for thermoregulation and requires none; it is the temperature of its environment in the way that a flame is the temperature of its burning. A metabolism that is not hungry in any sense we would recognize but which will slow and stutter if the hydrogen sulfide concentration drops, and which will accelerate — visibly, physically, the organism's movements becoming faster and more frequent — when the gas flows strongly from the floor. It does not want the gas. It processes the gas. The distinction is invisible from inside. It may be invisible from outside as well.

Jointed legs — six, or sometimes seven, or sometimes four depending on where in the chamber the organism developed and what thermal regimen shaped its morphogenesis — move in patterns that have been observed, measured, and described as "exploratory" by the three researchers who have entered the chamber and survived long enough to observe. They were wrong. The patterns are not exploratory. They are *responsive*. The organism moves toward the chemical gradient that its metabolism requires in the same way that a river moves toward the sea: because the alternative is to not move, and to not move is to be a different kind of thing entirely, a dead thing, a thing that stops.

When it reaches the gradient's peak — the sweet boundary where rising hydrogen sulfide meets sinking sulfur dioxide — it feeds. It feeds the way a catalyst feeds: by contact, by proximity, by offering its body as a surface where reactions can occur more efficiently than they would in open air. Its shell is pitted and grooved, increased in surface area by orders of magnitude through a growth process that maximizes the one thing its existence requires: interface. It is all interface. It is a living surface area, a catalytic membrane made mobile and reproductive. The reactions that happen on and within its body produce energy, which the organism uses to grow its shell, to move, to produce the next generation.

It does not think about this. It does not think about anything. The word *think* is not applicable. The word *be* may not be applicable either, if *being* requires some minimal acknowledgment of existence. *Pyroclasta refusa* does not acknowledge. It does not reflect. It processes and moves and processes and moves and eventually its shell becomes too thick for its joints to articulate and it slows and settles to the floor and becomes part of the substrate, part of the crust, part of the composite material that the colony has unknowingly manufactured, and its body dissolves slowly in the sulfur drifts and the minerals leach into the floor and the next generation grows on the surface that it has become.

This is not death. This is chemistry continuing.

---

Here is what ten million of them are:

A sound that has no source and no silence. A texture of friction so constant that the chamber's acoustics have reshaped themselves around it, the bulging walls and sagging ceiling forming a resonant cavity that amplifies certain frequencies and dampens others until the sound is almost harmonic, almost musical, a drone in the key of the mountain's slow cooling. The eleven-second pulse — peristaltic, emergent, unconducted — is the colony breathing in the only way it can breathe: by moving, all at once, in a wave that begins at the gas inlet fissures and propagates through the crowd until it reaches the far wall and reflects back and begins again. You cannot locate the origin of the wave because the wave has no origin. It is a property of the system. It is what ten million individual processes look like when they share a space and a gradient and a necessity.

A surface that covers every surface. From above, if you could see through the ceiling — and you cannot, because the ceiling is stone and you are not a god — the colony would look like a living carpet, yellow and amber and black, shifting as the gas flows shift, migrating across the floor in a wave that takes six weeks to complete and then begins again. The migration is not purposeful. The gas flows change because the magma below shifts and the fissures above expand and contract with thermal cycling on a scale of decades, and the colony follows the gradient because the gradient is what it fills, and when the gradient moves, the colony moves, and the floor is left behind it, smooth and sulfur-dusted and pocked with the impressions of a million feet, a record of occupation written in a language that no one reads.

A heat engine. Not a metaphor. The colony processes gas, and the processing releases heat, and the heat rises, and the convection current that results pulls fresh gas in through the fissures and pushes processed gas out through the same fissures, and the cycle continues. The chamber is a thermodynamic loop and the organisms are its working fluid. They are the medium through which the mountain's internal chemistry finds expression. They are what happens when sulfur and heat and pressure and time reach a configuration where chemistry can become catalysis and catalysis can become replication and replication can become a colony and a colony can become an engine and an engine can become, for want of any better word, *industrial*.

They do not know they are an engine. They do not know they are a colony. Each organism knows — and *knows* is the wrong verb, has always been the wrong verb, but language was not built for this — each organism *responds to* its immediate chemical environment. It moves toward what sustains it and away from what does not. It climbs because the convection lifts it. It grips because the surface is available. It processes because the gas is present and its shell is ready and the reaction will occur whether it is there or not, but it is there, and the reaction occurs faster, and the faster reaction yields more energy, and the energy yields more shell, and the shell yields more surface area, and the surface area yields faster processing, and the cycle has been running since the magma withdrew and left the space behind and it will run until the chamber cools or the gas stops or the mountain finally decides what it wants to be and stops becoming.

---

Here is what happens when you enter:

You are 37°C. The chamber is 87°C. You are a cold spot. You are a gradient that does not belong here, a sudden steep drop in temperature that the colony fills the way water fills a hole, because the gradient exists and the colony fills gradients, and your body is fifty kilograms of anomalous thermodynamics and the colony has never encountered anything like you but it does not need to encounter you to know what to do with you. It moves over you. It climbs your boots. It investigates — no, it *occupies* — the thermal boundary where your skin meets the heated air.

You will feel them first as a texture, like sand shifting across your ankles. Then as a weight, slight but present, as they layer across your legs and begin to climb. Then as a sound — the thin dry rasp multiplied by thousands, the sandpaper-whisper of shell on shell on fabric on skin. Then as a heat, not their heat, they have no heat of their own, but the heat they displace, the way they reshape the convection currents around your body so that the hot air touches you in new places, in patterns that follow the colony's distribution and not your own contours.

They do not bite. They do not sting. They are not interested in you. You are not food. You are not a threat. You are not anything that can be categorized by a system that does not categorize. You are a surface. You are a gradient. You are a node in the process, and the process continues whether you are there or not.

In three minutes you will be covered. In ten minutes the sensation of their movement will blend with the sensation of the heated air and you will not be able to tell where the colony ends and the atmosphere begins. In twenty minutes you will be difficult to distinguish from the rest of the chamber. In thirty minutes you will be part of the thermal landscape, another surface for the gas exchange, another interface between chemistry and chemistry, and the colony will move over you and around you and through the space where you are and it will process the gas that your breath displaces and it will not notice that you were ever not there.

---

Here is what lives here:

Not the organisms. The organisms are components. They are replaceable. They are being replaced, constantly, generation by generation, shell by shell, body by body, the way cells in your own body are replaced until none of the cells you were born with are still alive and yet you persist, you continue, you are still somehow yourself. But the colony is not a self. The colony is a process. The colony is what happens when the process continues.

What lives here is the chamber. What lives here is the reaction. What lives here is the ancient ongoing chemical exchange between the magma below and the atmosphere above, mediated by ten million thumb-sized catalytic surfaces that grew themselves from the waste of their own metabolism because the waste was sulfur and the sulfur was useful and the useful thing could be made into a shell and the shell could be made into a surface and the surface could be made into a site where the reaction could proceed more efficiently and the efficiency could be made into more organisms and the organisms could be made into more surfaces and the surfaces could be made into more sites and the sites could be made into more reaction and the reaction could be made into more heat and the heat could be made into more convection and the convection could be made into more gas flow and the gas flow could be made into more gradient and the gradient could be made into more filling and the filling could be made into more process and the process could be made into more of itself, endlessly, needlessly, pointlessly, without pause, without purpose, without the need for purpose, because purpose is a thing that minds require and this is not a mind, this is a room, this is a reaction, this is a knot in the mountain's long cooling where chemistry found a way to be busy.

What lives here is the sound. What lives here is the heat. What lives here is the floor flexing under your feet, the crust bending like copper, the subsonic thrum of geology refusing to wait, the eleven-second pulse of ten million bodies moving in a wave that has no origin and no end.

What lives here does not know you are here. What lives here does not know it is alive. What lives here is alive in the only way that matters: it continues. It processes. It fills the available space and reacts with the available chemistry and grows the available surfaces and manufactures the available heat and the heat rises and the gas flows and the colony shifts and the floor accumulates sulfur and the ceiling sags a centimeter per year and the mountain becomes a mountain, centimeter by centimeter, and what lives here lives here and lives here and lives here.

The chamber does not know you are here.

The chamber does not know it is a chamber.

The chamber processes.
