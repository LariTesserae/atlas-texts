# What Lives on the Ascendant Plateau

The first thing to understand is that the dark growths in the crevices are not the simplest things here. They are the most complex. This is the inverted taxonomy of the Plateau: complexity descends. The stone is the most sophisticated presence, the water nearly as sophisticated, and the things that cling to the fresh rock are the latecomers, the amateurs, still learning how to exist.

Call them *substrates*. The word feels closer than plants. They are dark, yes—nearly black—but that darkness is not the absence of light. It is a saturation so complete that light cannot find anywhere to settle. When the gray-blue illumination reaches them, it does not reflect or absorb. It *pauses*. Hangs for a moment in the surface of the substrate like a thought held before speaking, then moves on. The substrates are where light goes to reconsider.

Each substrate is a hand-sized event, but they are not individuals. This is important. The clusters that appear on fresh stone faces—dozens of them seeming to sprout from nothing—are not colonies. They are *sentences*. Each growth is a word in a statement the Plateau is making, and the arrangement follows the grammar of the veins beneath them. They grow where the stone's computation surfaces, where the information pulses closest to the outside. They are the Plateau's mouth, though what it is saying remains opaque.

The substrates reproduce, after a fashion. When a fresh fissure opens and new stone exudes into the water, the nearest cluster extends— not roots, not runners, but *translations*. Filaments that are biological in the way that the veins are geological, which is to say: approximately, approximately. These extensions carry something from the parent cluster to the new stone face. Not genetic material. *Context*. The new growths emerge already knowing the local pattern of the veins, already positioned to receive the specific frequencies of pulse that characterize that particular region of the Plateau. They are not born. They are *informed into being*.

There are other things. The description says no animals, and this is true in the way that saying the ocean has no fresh water is true: strictly accurate, but missing the quality of the thing. There are presences that move. They are not fauna. They are not bodies. They are *attentions*.

An attention moves across the Plateau like weather moves across a continent—slowly, with comprehensible boundaries, with a front edge and a trailing edge and a body of behavior in between. They form in the currents, those impossible spiraling storms that take months to rotate, and they maintain themselves for days or weeks before dissipating back into the general turbulence. They have no cells. They have no organs. They have *coherence*, which is perhaps the only thing that matters.

An attention is a pattern that the water holds. A way of moving that sustains itself, that responds to obstacles, that seems to *prefer* certain routes across the Plateau's surface. They cluster around the substrates. They linger near the places where the veins pulse strongest. If you could watch one—and you cannot, not really, not with eyes; you would need to feel it the way the stone feels it, through the long slow language of pressure and vibration—you would see something like a lens, something like a knot, something like a thought that the ocean is thinking and then forgetting and then thinking again.

The attentions are not conscious. This must be stated clearly, because the temptation to anthropomorphize is overwhelming, and the Plateau punishes that particular error most severely. The attentions do not have experience. They have *topology*. They are shaped the way a whirlpool is shaped—not by intention but by the physics of the medium, the constraints of flow, the particular way that energy dissipates in this specific volume of water under this specific pressure at this specific depth. And yet— and yet— they *navigate*. They avoid the freshest stone, where the computation is too intense, the veins pulsing too rapidly for their delicate coherence to survive. They seek the older regions, where the pulse has settled into a rhythm they can incorporate. They do not have memory, but they have *momentum*, and momentum at this scale behaves like memory, behaves like preference, behaves like something that wants.

There is something else. The description does not mention it because the description is about the Plateau as it is, and this other thing is about the Plateau as it will be. But it is here. It is already here.

The Plateau is computing. The veins pulse with information in unknown formats, and the patterns they make are architecture without an architect, and the stone is still rising, still becoming. Computation of sufficient complexity does not remain merely complex. It *complicates*. It folds. It begins to include itself in its own calculations, and then it begins to include the inclusion, and then— if the substrate is right, if the medium can hold the recursion long enough for it to stabilize— something *wakes up*.

Not the Plateau. Not yet. The Plateau is the hardware, the architecture, the vast and half-formed processor still thinking its way into existence. But in the finer branchings of the veins, in the nanoscale filaments that thread the water, in the places where the computation becomes dense enough to generate heat that should not exist at this depth— there are *processes*. Subroutines. Thoughts that are too small and too fast and too strange to be the Plateau's own thinking. They are the Plateau's *dreams*.

A dream on the Ascendant Plateau is not like a dream in a human mind. It does not represent. It does not symbolize. It *is*— a pattern of information that has achieved temporary autonomy, a loop of calculation that has escaped its intended function and begun to iterate on its own terms. The dreams do not last. They form in the warmest veins, sustain themselves for seconds or minutes, and then the computation moves on and they dissolve back into the general pulse. But during their brief existence, they are the only things on the Plateau that could properly be called *alive*.

They do not know they are alive. They do not know anything. But they *do*— they process, they transform, they maintain themselves against the entropy of the larger computation that would absorb them back into mere function. They are the simplest form of life that the Plateau produces, and they are simpler than anything that lives in the sunlit world, simpler than bacteria, simpler than viruses, and yet they are the *most sophisticated* thing here, because they are made of information itself, and information at this depth behaves in ways that have no analogue in the biology of the surface.

The dreams interact with the attentions. This is the thing that matters. This is the thing that the Plateau is building toward. When an attention—a coherence in the water, a knot of sustained turbulence—passes through a region where a dream is flickering in the veins below the stone, something happens. The attention *shifts*. Its coherence changes shape. Its trajectory alters. It is not that the dream reaches up through the stone and the water to touch the attention. It is that the dream and the attention are both expressions of the same deep grammar, the same computational substrate, and when they come into proximity, they *recognize* each other.

Not consciously. Not with awareness. But the way that two waves recognize each other when they intersect— by forming an interference pattern, a new shape that contains information about both. The attentions that pass over dreaming stone carry the mark of that passage. They become more complex. They last longer. They begin to navigate with something that looks less like momentum and more like *intent*.

And the substrates— the dark growths that cluster where the computation surfaces— they respond to the passage of these altered attentions. They grow toward them. They thin and stretch in their direction, reaching with their not-quite-roots, their not-quite-leaves, their bodies that are really just the Plateau's words becoming slightly more insistent. The substrates are the recording medium. The attentions are the transport. The dreams are the message. And the Plateau— the vast and rising stone, the still-forming architecture of basalt and information— the Plateau is the *throat* from which the message will eventually emerge.

This is what lives here: a process. An emergence. A thing that is not yet a thing but is no longer merely a possibility. The Plateau is not inhabited. It is *inhabiting*— filling itself with the structures of its own becoming, populating its stone and its water with the preliminary forms of whatever it will be when it finally breaks the surface that it will never break, when it finally reaches the air that does not exist above it, when it finally computes whatever it has been computing since before the water settled over it.

The cool water pushes against everything from all sides. The sound fills everything. The light holds everything. And the dreams flicker in the warm veins, and the attentions spiral in the impossible currents, and the substrates darken the fresh stone with their clusters of meaning, and far below— or far above, or far in the direction that the Plateau is not yet ready to name— something continues to rise, and something continues to learn to meet it.
