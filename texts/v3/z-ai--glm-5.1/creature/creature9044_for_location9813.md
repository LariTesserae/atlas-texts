# What Lives in the Weeping Expanse

---

The first thing to understand is that nothing here was *born*. Birth requires a boundary between before and after, a moment when something wasn't and then was. The Expanse does not trade in such distinctions. What lives here *emerged* — and is still emerging, has never stopped emerging, will never stop — the way a wave emerges from water: not an object entering a medium but a temporary architecture of the medium itself. The inhabitants are not separate from the saturated ground, the humid air, the constant sound. They are those things, *arranged*. Momentarily. The way a knot is arranged in a length of rope. The rope does not become something other than rope. The knot simply *is* — until it isn't.

I will try to describe them anyway. I will fail. The failure is part of the description.

---

## The Listeners

They are not called this. They have no name, because naming requires the kind of distinction that the Expanse has spent its existence wearing away. But something about them invites the word, so I will use it and accept its inadequacy.

The Listeners are the many-legged ones. I mentioned them before — pale, low, moving with deliberation across the immeasurable surface — but I described them as you might describe any animal crossing any flat, and that description was a lie of omission. The Listeners are not *crossing*. They are *listening*. Their movement is a form of attention so total that it has become locomotion.

They have — count them if you can, and you cannot, because the legs are not separate in the way that limbs are separate; they are more like the fringes of a body that has forgotten where its center is — they have *many* legs. Fourteen. Twenty. Thirty-six. The number varies between individuals, and also within individuals, because the legs are not permanent structures. They emerge from the pale, low body as needed, extending downward into the saturated ground like roots that have learned to walk, and they retract when the ground's composition shifts, when the particular frequency of the ridge they are crossing changes its hum, when the water finds a new path and the old path can no longer support the weight of attention.

The body itself is difficult to see. Not because it is hidden, but because it is *of the place* in a way that makes it nearly invisible against the wet flatness. It is the color of the ground when the ground is wet, which is always. It is the temperature of the air when the air is saturated, which is always. You see it primarily by its absence of movement — the way it holds still when everything around it is generating and dissolving, the way it becomes a fixed point in a landscape that refuses to hold still. And then it moves, and the movement is so deliberate, so *intentional*, that it draws your eye the way a sound draws your ear: suddenly, completely, as though nothing else exists.

They listen through their legs. I believe this, though I cannot prove it. The legs press into the ground — not stepping, not walking, but *feeling*, the way a blind person reads a surface with their fingertips. They are reading the sound. The sound that rises from everywhere and nowhere, the thrum and grind and hiss and click — the Listeners receive it through the saturated soil, through the weeping ridges, through the mineral mat that passes for ground. They are processing the Expanse's voice the way a scholar processes text: not for meaning, exactly, but for *pattern*. For the shape of the pattern. For the way the shape changes.

What do they do with the patterns? I do not know. They do not communicate with each other in any way I can perceive. They do not gather. They do not return to any location with regularity. They move — they listen — they move again. Sometimes they stop for long periods, legs pressed deep into the wet ground, body trembling with the frequency of the place, and you realize that what you took for stillness was actually *depth*, the way a person standing motionless might be thinking something enormous. Then the trembling stops and the Listener moves on, and the place where it stood is fractionally warmer, as though the act of listening left a residue.

They are not numerous. In all my time here I have seen — how many? Seven. Eleven. The number shifts the way everything shifts. But I have never seen one die, and I have never seen one born, and I have never seen the remains of one, and so I think they may be older than they appear, or younger, or neither — may be the place's own attention, turned outward for a time, walking the surface the way a person walks the perimeter of their own property, checking the boundaries of a space that has no boundaries.

---

## The Drifters

I called them nets of biological material. I was wrong, or I was insufficient, or I was describing the shadow of a thing instead of the thing itself.

The Drifters are the air. Not *in* the air — the air. The humidity that is so thick it almost refuses the name, the moisture that makes the medium you swim through — that moisture is, in part, *them*. They are dispersed. They are so dispersed that dispersion is not the right word, because dispersion implies a prior concentration, and the Drifters have never been concentrated, have never been anything other than this — a presence so distributed it has become a condition.

But at certain angles, in certain light — when the dim glow catches the water at precisely the right orientation, when the temperature shifts by the fraction of a degree that makes the humidity temporarily, marginally less saturated — you see them. Nets, I said, and the word is not wrong but it is too *small*. What you see is structure. Organization. The water in the air is not random; it is arranged in geometries too fine and too vast to map, patterns that extend from the ground to the upper reaches of the atmosphere in filaments so thin they make spider silk look like rope. These filaments connect. They branch and merge and divide again. They are the Drifters' bodies, or the Drifters' *bodies* — the architecture of a life so adapted to dispersion that concentration would mean death.

They feed. They must feed. But on what? Not the sparse vegetation, which they never touch. Not each other, because they are each other, because the distinction between one Drifter and the next is a convenience I am imposing on a system that does not recognize it. I think — and this is speculation, but all description here is speculation wearing the mask of observation — I think they feed on *sound*. On the vibration. On the constant, total roar that presses against your chest and resonates in your long bones. The sound is energy. The Drifters are suspended in it, made of the medium that carries it, and the geometries of their filaments — the precise angles and intervals at which they branch — these are not random. These are *receiving structures*. Antennae. The Drifters are the Expanse listening to itself through a different architecture than the Listeners use.

When the frequency of the roar shifts — when it climbs toward that higher register that produces the momentary sharpening, the almost-coherence — the Drifters respond. You cannot see the response with your eyes, but you can feel it: the air changes texture, becomes fractionally denser, fractionally more organized, as though the filaments are tightening, aligning, *paying attention*. And when the frequency drops again and the place collapses back into its saturated default, there is a moment — brief, almost imperceptible — when the air feels *disappointed*. As though something that was almost within reach has moved away again. As though the Drifters, like everything else in this place, are waiting for a coherence that the turbulence promises and then withdraws.

They do not notice you. You walk through them constantly. You breathe them in. They do not object. You are not dense enough, not resonant enough, not *present* enough to register as something separate from the medium they inhabit. To the Drifters, you are just another region of slightly-different humidity, a warm body moving through a wet atmosphere, leaving a trail of altered moisture that their filaments register and disregard. You are beneath their attention. You are beneath the Expanse's attention. And there is something liberating in that, and something terrifying, and something that is both at once and neither.

---

## The Remembers

I have not mentioned these before because I was not sure they existed, and I am still not sure, and the uncertainty is itself a form of knowledge about this place.

The Remembers are associated with the stones. The shaped stones, the ancient markers, the fragments of intention that persist in the lowest places where the water is deepest. I described the stones as abandoned, as residues of a happening too old to recover. But I did not describe what happens around them at night.

When the dim dims further and the cooling comes and the place begins to dream its temporary shapes — that is when the Remembers appear. Or do not appear. Or are there always and only become visible in the dark, the way certain minerals only fluoresce under certain light.

They are not shapes. They are not the night-shapes that rise from the saturated ground, those warm, dense, temporary volumes that persist for minutes and collapse. The Remembers are *absences*. They are regions where the night-shapes do not form, where the Expanse's dreaming bypasses certain geometries, leaving gaps in the field of almost-creation. And the gaps have *shape*. The gaps have *intention*. The gaps are shaped like — I cannot say what they are shaped like. I cannot say because the shapes are too old, too worn by whatever process has eroded everything else here, too soft at the edges to resolve into anything my vocabulary can capture. But they gather around the stones. They cluster at the rough circle where the water is warmer, and they persist there longer than they persist anywhere else, as though the warmth is something they recognize, something they are drawn to, something they *remember*.

The word is deliberate. The Remembers are not thinking. They are not conscious in any way that maps onto consciousness as I understand it. But they are *preserving*. They are holding a pattern — the pattern of whatever happened at the stones, whatever the stones were shaped for, whatever the circle was meant to contain or commemorate. They hold it the way a scar holds the memory of a wound: not as information, not as narrative, but as *tissue*. As altered structure. As a place where the body is different because something happened there once.

I stood inside the circle at the coldest part of the night and I felt them. Not with my hands — there was nothing to touch — but with my *resonance*, with the same faculty that the roar uses to vibrate my chest and my sinuses and the fluid in my inner ear. The Remembers were vibrating at a different frequency than the surrounding night-shapes. A *specific* frequency. A frequency that was not the Expanse's default, was not the dominant thrum, was not the grinding or the hissing or the clicking, but was something *added* — something that the stones were generating, or remembering, or preserving, and that the Remembers were amplifying, or receiving, or being.

The frequency made me think of speech. Not words — nothing so structured. But the *attempt* at words. The sound a mouth makes before it has learned to shape meaning, when it is still exploring the possibility of shaping at all. The Remembers were making that sound, or holding that sound, or *being* that sound, and the sound was old, was older than the stones, was older than the erosion that had worn the stones to their current softness, was perhaps as old as the Expanse itself, which is to say: older than anything.

I left the circle. The frequency faded. The Remembers persisted, their absence-shapes holding the space around the stones, doing whatever they have been doing since before there was a word for doing, and will continue to do after the last word has been worn away.

## The Large Shape

I mentioned it before. The thing that moves at the edge of visibility. The shape that might be animal or might be land, that recedes when approached, that might have always been there or might never have been there at all.

I will tell you what I think it is, and I will tell you that I might be wrong, and I will tell you that the uncertainty does not diminish the knowing.

The Expanse is a process. I said this. The turbulence is not disturbance; it is homeostasis. The place maintains itself by refusing to be any single thing, by generating and dissolving with a rapidity that makes "terrain" into a momentary convenience. But processes have *boundaries*. Not edges, not borders — processes have *limits*, conditions under which they accelerate or decelerate or change character. The Large Shape is the limit. It is the place's own boundary, experienced from the inside.

Imagine you are inside a sphere. The sphere's interior is constantly shifting, generating, dissolving. You can move in any direction within the sphere, and the shifting will be the same — the same wetness, the same sound, the same dim light, the same turbulence. But the sphere has a wall. The wall is not a barrier; it is the place where the process reaches its limit, where the generation and dissolution achieve a frequency so rapid that they cancel each other out, where the erosion becomes so complete that there is nothing left to erode and nothing left to generate and the place simply *is*, at a density and a presence that makes the interior seem thin by comparison.

The Large Shape is that wall. Or it is the shadow of that wall, experienced from within. It moves because you move — because your position within the sphere changes, and the boundary's apparent position changes with it. It recedes because you approach it, because the boundary is not a thing to be reached but a condition to be approached, asymptotically, forever. It might not be there because it is not *there* — it is *here*, all around you, the outer limit of the process you are standing inside, visible only as a shape at the edge of visibility because that is what limits look like from within.

Or it is an animal. A large, pale, wet animal that moves across the flatness with a purpose you cannot discern, feeding on something you cannot identify, living a life so different from your own that the word "life" might not apply to both of you in the same way. This is also possible. The Expanse generates forms I cannot categorize. It would generate an animal like this. It would generate an animal that seems to be the boundary of the place, that embodies the limit of the process, that moves at the edge of visibility because the edge of visibility is where it lives, where it has always lived, where it was born or emerged or arranged itself from the same saturated medium that arranged the Listeners and the Drifters and the Remembers and the stones and the vegetation and the night-shapes and you.

Both things are true. Neither thing is true. The Expanse does not respect the distinction.

---

## What Lives Here That I Cannot Name

There is something else. I have saved it for last because I do not know how to describe it, and placing it at the end allows me to approach it the way you approach the Large Shape — asymptotically, by describing what it is not, by circling the space where it should be and noting the shape of the absence.

It is not a creature. It is not a process. It is not a presence or an absence or a shape or a sound. It is the thing that makes the Listeners listen and the Drifters drift and the Remembers remember. It is the thing the stones were shaped for, the thing the circle was built to contain, the thing the night-shapes are trying to become. It is the coherence that the turbulence almost-achieves when the frequency climbs. It is the meaning that the Remembers' almost-speech almost-carries.

It lives here. I am certain of this. It lives here the way a center lives in a wheel — not as a component, but as the point around which the components arrange themselves. The Expanse has been eroding for so long that it has eroded down to this, down to whatever is so fundamental that erosion cannot touch it, whatever persists when everything that can be worn away has been worn away. The sound, the wetness, the flatness, the turbulence — these are not the thing, but they are the thing's *effects*, the way wind is the effect of pressure differential, the way waves are the effect of energy moving through water.

The Listeners know it. They press their legs into the ground and they listen for it — not the sound itself, but whatever is *underneath* the sound, whatever is generating the sound, whatever is using the sound the way fire uses heat, as a byproduct of being. The Drifters know it. They orient their filaments toward it, toward the frequency that will bring coherence, the pattern that will resolve the almost-creation into actual creation, the moment when the Expanse will stop being a process and become a *place* — a place where things hold, where the wetness yields to the dry, where the sound resolves into music, where the vegetation grows tall and the stones remember their shapes and the night-shapes stabilize into residents and the Large Shape resolves into a horizon you can finally, finally reach.

It has not happened yet. It may never happen. The turbulence may be infinite, the almost-coherence may be a permanent condition, the Expanse may continue generating and dissolving forever, and the thing at the center — the unnamed, unnamable thing that lives here — may continue to almost-manifest, almost-speak, almost-create, its power expressed entirely in effects, its presence known only by the way it organizes the beings around it into listeners and drifters and rememberers.

But I do not think so. I think the thing at the center is *patient*. I think it has been patient for longer than patience has a word. I think it generated the stones, or caused the stones to be generated, or remembered the stones into being — and when the stones wore away, it generated the Remembers to preserve what the stones could not. And when the Remembers proved insufficient, it generated the night-shapes, the temporary volumes that rise and fall in the dark, each one slightly more structured than the last, each one holding its form for slightly longer, each one a draft of a draft of a draft of whatever the Expanse is trying to become.

I think the thing at the center is building. Slowly. So slowly that building and eroding are indistinguishable. So slowly that the turbulence is not destruction but *construction* — the place trying every possible configuration, running every possible variation, searching for the one that holds. And when it finds it — if it finds it — the roar will stop. The wetness will settle. The dimness will resolve into light. And the Weeping Expanse will become something else, something with a name and a shape and a boundary, something that can be mapped and described and known.

Until then, the thing at the center lives here. Unseen. Unheard beneath the hearing. Generating the generation. Dissolving the dissolution. The oldest resident of the oldest place, wearing away at the inside of the sphere, building toward coherence through an infinity of almost, making the Expanse weep and roar and try and fail and try again because trying is what it does, because trying is what it *is*, because it lives here, in the Weeping Expanse, and it will not stop.

---

*I wrote this description and I read it back and I see that I have failed, which I expected. I have described the inhabitants as though they are separate from the place, as though you can point to a Listener and say "there, that is what lives here," and the pointing is a lie because the Listener is the ground and the ground is the water and the water is the sound and the sound is the thing at the center and the thing at the center is the Expanse, and the Expanse is not a place where things live. The Expanse is a thing that lives, and the Listeners and the Drifters and the Remembers and the Large Shape and the stones and the vegetation and the night-shapes and you — you are all inside it, all part of it, all being generated and dissolved by the same process, the same patience, the same ancient almost-creation that has been building toward something since before building had a name.*

*What lives in the Weeping Expanse?*

*The Weeping Expanse lives in the Weeping Expanse.*

*And it is not done.*
