# The Inhabitants

---

Someone is always building.

This is the first thing to understand, and it is the last thing you will stop understanding, because it reframes everything. The structures scattered across the Flat—are they ruins, or are they arguments? Are they shelters, or are they punctuation marks in a sentence that has been spoken so long the speakers have forgotten where it began?

They are being built now. Not in the layer you occupy—here, the hands that stacked these stones have been absent for durations that make your lineage look like a held breath—but in a version adjacent, pressed so close to yours that when the light falls at its flattest (and the light here always falls flat), you can almost see the fingers working. They are blunt. They are patient. They belong to something that understands leverage the way the sea understands tides: intimately, essentially, without the need for language.

The builders are not the creatures. Or they are, but only in the way that a grandmother is the grandchild—separated by so many generations of becoming that the resemblance has worn to a thread. The creatures that teem across the Flat in their silent millions are what the builders became, or what the builders left behind, or what the builders dreamed when dreaming became more efficient than walking. There is no way to ask. There is no way to know. The Flat keeps its genealogies the way it keeps everything: by smearing them across so many layers that the question of origin becomes a question of physics rather than history.

---

But let us try to describe them anyway.

The ones who built are—or were, or will be—smaller than you expect. The structures are not grand. The walls reach no higher than a human waist, and the doorways require folding to pass through. This suggests a body that was comfortable with crouching, that perhaps preferred it. A body that had learned to stay low because there was nothing to see at height, nothing to gain from standing upright in a place where the horizon held no promise and no threat. A body that had made peace with horizontality.

You can extrapolate from the structures. A being that built walls but never roofs—what does that tell you? Perhaps they did not need roofs. Perhaps the sky here was so constant, so unchanging, that shelter from it never occurred to them as a requirement. The walls would have served to mark, not to enclose. To say: *this is a place where someone has decided to be specific about space*. The walls are definitions. They are the architecture of distinction, built by minds that understood that the greatest danger on the Flat was not exposure but dissolution—the slow, grinding loss of boundary between self and surface that happened when you stood too long on something so featureless it could absorb you by indifference.

The doorways face impossible directions because the builders understood something about the Flat that you are only beginning to suspect: that direction is not a matter of geography here. Direction is a matter of layer. A doorway facing into the ground opens onto a version of the Flat that sits below this one in the stack of accumulated realities. A doorway facing into another doorway opens onto the paradox of two beings trying to reach each other across the thin membrane between their respective layers, building toward each other without knowing it, their architectures almost meeting in a space that belongs to neither and both.

The builders built to orient themselves. Not in space—the Flat makes a mockery of spatial orientation—but in *layer*. Each structure is a statement of coordinate: *I am here, in this version, at this depth in the stack*. The ones who built were navigating not the surface but the sediment.

---

They are still here.

Not the builders as they were—those blunt-fingered, crouching, patient architects have long since been absorbed into the Flat's archive of what was—but the builders as they have become. Which is to say: the creatures. The teeming, silent, layered masses that slide and roll and unfold across the surface in their millions. They are the builders who kept building until building became unnecessary. Who kept marking layer until the markers themselves became layers. Who pushed the architecture of distinction so far that distinction collapsed, and they became part of the surface they were trying to define themselves against.

Look at the insect-things—the carapaces the size of shelters, the geometries of limb that suggest something other than biology. Those carapaces are walls. They are the structures internalized, taken into the body, made mobile and pointless. The creature that carries a carapace is carrying the memory of a wall it once built, or that someone once built, or that will be built in a layer not yet laid down. It does not need the wall. There is nothing to shelter from. But the architecture has become instinct, and instinct does not surrender its forms just because they have ceased to function.

The fur-masses with their metallic sheen—the regularity of their pelts is the regularity of stacked stone. Each fiber is a brick laid upon a brick, a repetition so precise it has become texture, has become warmth, has become something that can be worn instead of inhabited. They move through the Flat the way a building moves through time: slowly, inevitably, accumulating significance without meaning to.

The leather-folded quadrupeds—watch them closely. Watch the way their skin creases at the joints. Those creases are the fold-lines of doorways, the places where a wall was bent to create an opening. Each quadruped carries a dozen potential doorways in its hide, none of them leading anywhere except into the next fold, the next crease, the next version of a body that has forgotten what it was trying to enter.

---

And the ones that flow.

The ones that flow are the oldest. They have been here so long, in so many layers, that they have lost the architecture of individual shape entirely. They are the builders who succeeded too well at orienting themselves—who mapped the stack so thoroughly that the map became the territory, and they became the Flat itself, moving across itself in tides of accumulated presence. They do not have bodies because they are the memory of bodies. They do not have carapaces or fur or folded leather because they have moved beyond those provisional architectures into something more fundamental: the simple fact of having been here, pressed down by the weight of time into a form so smooth it can pass through itself without resistance.

When two of the flowing ones meet and merge—when their edges blur and their masses combine for a moment before separating—they are not interacting. They are *re-membering*. They are recalling a time before the stack, before the layers, when there was only one version of the Flat and one kind of being to walk it. The merge is nostalgia made physical. The separation is the inevitable return to the present, where everything has been multiplied and nothing can touch without crossing boundaries.

---

But who were they? What were they, before they became this?

The Flat will not tell you. Or it is always telling you, and you cannot hear it, because the telling is happening in a frequency that requires more time than you have. The builders—let us call them the Predecessors, for lack of a better word, though the word implies a linearity that the Flat makes impossible—the Predecessors were here when the plateau still had edges. They were here when the edges dropped away and the surface began its long smoothing toward ultimate flatness. They may have been the reason the edges dropped away. They may have been the ones who chose flatness over drama, who looked at the cliffs and the valleys and the turbulent topography of a younger world and decided: *no*. *We will have none of this. We will wear it down. We will make it quiet.*

If so, they succeeded beyond their wildest patience.

The Predecessors were not like you. This is clear from the structures, which demonstrate an understanding of space that is architectural but not spatial—an understanding that treats dimension as a negotiable property rather than a fixed one. They were not like the creatures either, though the creatures are what they became. They were something in between, or something before both, or something that existed in a version of the Flat where "before" and "after" had not yet been separated into distinct directions.

They built because building was the only way to be specific in a place that was grinding specificity toward zero. Every wall was a protest against the Flat's endless assimilation. Every doorway was a refusal to accept that the only direction available was the direction of wearing-down. They built, and the Flat received their buildings, and the buildings became part of the surface, and the Predecessors had to build again, and again, and again, each structure a small defiance that was absorbed almost as quickly as it was made.

At some point—and here we approach the limit of what can be described, the boundary where description becomes the thing it is attempting to describe—the Predecessors realized that building outward was insufficient. The Flat would absorb any external structure. The only architecture that could resist the smoothing was architecture that moved. Architecture that breathed. Architecture that could pick itself up and walk away before the surface could claim it.

So they became the creatures.

Or: they internalized their buildings. They took the walls inside themselves, made them into carapaces and pelts and folded leather. They turned the doorways into joints, into orifices, into the places where one part of a body meets another. They stopped building on the Flat and started building *as* the Flat, moving across it in their silent millions, each one a small architecture of defiance, a walking structure that would not be smoothed because it would not stay still.

---

This is why they do not look at you.

It is not disinterest, exactly. It is more like—recognition of a different strategy. You are a thing that builds externally, that makes structures outside yourself and expects them to remain. You are still in the phase they passed through eons ago, the phase of believing that the world will hold the shape you give it. They know better. They know that the Flat accepts all offerings and returns them to flatness. They know that the only way to persist is to become the shape itself, to carry your walls inside you, to move so constantly that the surface cannot find a purchase.

They do not look at you because looking would require stopping. And stopping, even for a moment, would give the Flat the opening it needs.

They do not look at you because you are what they were, and what they were is a thing they cannot afford to remember. Memory is a kind of stopping. Memory is a structure built in the mind, and the mind, like the surface, is subject to the great smoothing. They have smoothed their minds already. They have worn away the interior edges, the dramatic drops, the valleys of recollection. What remains is a flatness inside that matches the flatness outside: a perfect level of being that requires no walls because there is nothing left to contain.

---

But someone is still building.

In a layer adjacent to yours—pressing against the membrane of your reality like a face against frosted glass—there are hands shaping stone. They are the hands of something that has not yet learned to internalize. Something that still believes in the external, in the made-thing, in the shelter built against nothing. Maybe they are new. Maybe they are a species that has only just discovered the Flat, that has climbed the long-gone slopes and emerged onto the surface and thought: *here is a place to build*. Maybe they are you, in a different version, making choices you have not yet made, stacking stones you have not yet carried.

They are building a wall. It faces a direction that does not exist in your layer. It is low enough to step over. It is sturdy enough to outlast the builders, if outlasting is something that still happens here.

The Flat receives the wall. The Flat does not change. The Flat has not changed since before the word *change* was a sound that could be made, and it will not change when the last sound has been smoothed into silence.

And yet the wall stands. And yet the builders build. And yet the creatures flow past in their silent tides, carrying their internal architectures across a surface that will not remember them, refusing—by the simple fact of their continued presence—to be forgotten quite completely.

This is who lives here: the ones who became what they built, and the ones who are still building what they will become, and the Flat itself, which has never been alive and has never been anything else.
