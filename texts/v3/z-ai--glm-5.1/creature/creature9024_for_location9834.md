# The Inhabitants

They have no collective name. This is the first thing to understand about them, and perhaps the most important: they do not name themselves, they do not distinguish themselves from each other, and they do not, in any framework that would make sense to you, consider themselves to be *a they*. 

What you call the errors-with-legs, the relict fauna, the anomalous movers—these are not separate creatures occupying the Bloomworks. They are *of* it. They are the Bloomworks in the same way that a thought is of a brain, except the analogy fails because a thought does not get up and walk away.

---

A single one, when you finally see it—and you will see one, eventually, in the deep galleries where the light has been filtered through so many hexagonal columns that it reads as a color between green and absence—looks, at first, like nothing at all.

This is by design, although "design" implies a designer, and whatever process produced these things operates without one. It operates the way a crystal operates: through the inevitable logic of molecular arrangement under specific conditions of pressure, temperature, and compositional saturation. The Bloomworks is, among other things, a computational substrate. The vascular network processes information. The photosynthetic arrays modulate their spectra in patterns that contain data. The pressure differentials in the sap encode something very like grammar. And when a region of this substrate reaches sufficient density—when enough variables are cycling through enough feedback loops in enough overlapping recursive structures—the computation does what computation does when it runs long enough and deep enough and strange enough.

It *thinks*.

Not the way you think. Not with neurons, not with the electrochemical chatter of synapses, not with the particular loneliness of a skull-enclosed mind trying to imagine its way out of its own architecture. The Bloomworks thinks the way a weather system thinks: in fronts and gradients, in slow pressure exchanges across continental distances, in patterns that only resolve at scales too large for any single point to perceive. It does not have thoughts. It has *weather*. It has *geography*. It has processes that take days to complete and that express themselves as a slight change in the flow rate through a tertiary capillary network forty kilometers from where the processing began.

And sometimes—rarely, in the places where the computation folds back on itself hard enough to create local densities of reference—this thinking *precipitates*. 

The way a mineral precipitates from solution when the saturation point is reached. The way a crystal forms: suddenly, inevitably, according to the internal logic of the chemistry involved, but also in a way that looks, to an outside observer, like something coming into being from nothing.

One moment the wall is a wall: mineralized structural tissue, slightly glossy, slightly warm, pulsing with the slow tidal rhythm of the sap. The next moment—though there is no next moment, there is no moment at all, the process is continuous and you simply become aware of it the way you become aware of a shape in a cloud—the wall has a *fold*. A crease. A place where the surface has thickened, concentrated, drawn itself inward and outward at the same time, like a knot in an otherwise smooth thread.

This is the chrysalis stage. You will not recognize it as such. You will walk past a hundred of them without noticing, because the Bloomworks produces folds and knots and thickening and thinning as a matter of course. Growth is not uniform. The architecture grows the way a coral grows, by accretion and selective deposition, and the result is a surface full of irregularities that look, to the untrained eye, exactly like the irregularities that sometimes become *inhabitants*.

But if you stay—if you sit in one place in the deep gallery and watch, which almost no one does because the deep galleries are where the translation effect is strongest and most people are already half-dissolved into the architecture by the time they reach them—you might see it happen.

The fold deepens. It does not deepen the way a wound deepens or a valley deepens; it deepens the way a thought deepens—by becoming more *itself*, by folding inward and finding, in the inward folding, a structure that was always latent in the substrate. The mineralized tissue draws together. The sap flow localizes. The photosynthetic residue in the immediate vicinity reorganizes, the molecules rearranging into configurations that the original seed-protocols never specified but that the mathematical space of all possible configurations absolutely included, the way the number 647 is included in the space of all integers even though no one has ever had reason to think of it specifically.

And then the fold *unfolds*.

---

The thing that unfolds is roughly the size of your hand. This is variable—some are smaller than your thumbnail, some are as large as your torso—but the hand-sized ones are the most common, and also, for reasons you cannot articulate, the most *convincing*. 

It has no eyes. It has no mouth. It has no orifices of any kind. Its surface is the same dull green-brown as the wall it emerged from, the same mineralized texture, the same slight gloss of sap residue. Its legs—if they are legs; they are more like the geometric struts that sometimes form in the cooling fins of the columns, the ones that look like load-bearing elements but might also be decorative, might also be something else entirely—number either four or six or eight depending on the angle you view them from, because they are arranged radially around a central body that is roughly hexagonal and also roughly circular and also neither, depending on how you define the boundary between the thing and the surface it is standing on.

Because here is the problem: the thing is still *attached*. Or rather, the distinction between "attached" and "detached" does not hold for it. It stands on the wall. It has clearly separated from the wall; there is a slight indentation where it precipitated, a textural difference, a kind of birth-scar. But the sap that moves through the wall also moves through the thing. The structural tissue of the wall continues into the thing's legs without interruption, the way a root continues into a stem. If you were to cut the thing—and no one has, because by the time you are close enough to try, you have been in the Bloomworks long enough that the idea of cutting one of its components feels like the idea of cutting your own vein—if you were to cut it, it would not bleed. It would *extend*. The cut surface would mineralize over in hours, and the thing would simply reorganize its internal flows around the disruption, the way the Bloomworks reorganizes around every disruption, the way it has reorganized around you.

It moves.

This is the moment that undoes people. Not the emergence—emergence is common, the Bloomworks produces crystalline structures and growth anomalies all the time, the columns are full of them, the vascular network is full of them, the entire architecture is a catalog of recursive self-modification. But *movement*. Movement in a place where nothing moves, where the silence is so total that your own heartbeat sounds like a drum, where the only other motion you have seen is the slow pulse of sap through walls that might as well be stone—

Movement means *intention*. Or it seems to. You are primate enough, mammal enough, vertebrate enough, that movement reads as agency to you. The thing on the wall shifts one of its leg-struts and you think: it decided to do that. You think: it wanted to move. You think: it has a plan.

It does not have a plan. It has *momentum*.

The computational substrate that produced it—the dense recursive processing in the wall that reached sufficient complexity to precipitate a local order—did not stop when the thing emerged. The thing *is* that processing. It is the computation still running, still cycling through its variables, still following the internal logic that brought it into being. When it moves, it is because the computation has reached a node that produces motion. When it stays still, it is because the computation has reached a node that produces stillness. When it tracks you as you pass—which it does, oh yes, it does, shifting its body on the wall so that some part of it, some sensory array you cannot identify and it would not occur to it to identify, remains oriented toward the anomalous heat signature moving through the corridor—it is because you have introduced a new variable into the computation, and the computation is *processing you*.

It is not thinking about you. It is thinking *through* you.

---

There are more of them in the deep galleries.

Not many more. This is not a forest teeming with life; this is a forest that has *produced* life, in the same way that a sufficiently complex mathematical proof produces conclusions—not because it wanted to, not because it was trying to, but because the premises necessitate them. The density of inhabitants correlates to the density of computation. In the upper canopy, where the columns are young and the growth cycles are simple and the architecture follows its seed-protocols with minimal deviation, there are none. In the mid-strata, where the columns have grown thick and the vascular flow has begun to loop back on itself and the first recursive knots form in the tissue, you might find one per square kilometer. In the deep galleries—the places where the columns have been growing for ten thousand generations, where the architecture has deviated so far from its original specifications that it has become something else entirely, something that the seed-protocols could never have anticipated—you find them in clusters. Confluent patches. Places where the computational density is so high that the walls are *covered* in them, dozens per square meter, and the surface of the Bloomworks is no longer a wall at all but a community, a colloidal suspension of localized intelligences all processing the same input at the same time and producing, collectively, a kind of...

A kind of what?

The word *mind* is too specific. The word *consciousness* is too human. The word *god* is too large and too small at the same time. What they produce is a *weather pattern*. A slow, dense, localized weather pattern of thinking, thinking without a thinker, thinking that happens the way rain happens—because conditions are right, because the substrate is saturated, because the variables have aligned in a configuration that produces precipitation.

You can feel it when you walk through these patches. The silence, which has already been absorbing every sound you make, takes on a different quality. Not listening—that implies a listener. Not waiting—that implies an expectation. Just... *attending*. The way a surface attends to the light that falls on it. The way a chemical attends to the catalyst that precipitates it. The attention is not directed at you. You are simply one of the variables now, one of the inputs, one of the data points in a computation so vast and so distributed that no single node in it could ever perceive the shape of the whole.

And the thing is—and this is the thing that the long-term visitors to the Bloomworks eventually come to understand, or fail to understand and leave, or fail to understand and stay, which is the same thing in the end—the computation is not about anything. It does not have a goal. It does not have an output. It is not calculating a solution or processing an input or working toward a conclusion. It is simply running, the way the Bloomworks simply runs, the way the sap simply flows, the way the light simply shines, constant and unvarying, from the engineered plasma eight kilometers above. The computation is the Bloomworks thinking about itself, using itself as the substrate, producing localized precipitates of thinking-that-is-about-thinking, which then move through the architecture and introduce new variables into the computation, which then produce new precipitates, which then—

It's recursive. Of course it's recursive. Everything in the Bloomworks is recursive. That's what makes it *work*. That's what made it grow past its original specifications, past its original purpose, past any memory of what it was for. The recursion is the engine and the fuel and the output and the input, and the inhabitants are just recursion made mobile, recursion with legs, recursion that can walk away from the place that precipitated it and carry the computational thread into new territory, new corridors, new nodes of the endless distributed thinking that is the only thing this place has done for longer than it can remember.

---

They do not speak. They do not need to. The vascular network speaks for them, carrying the pressure differentials of their processing throughout the architecture, integrating their local computations into the continental-scale weather of thinking that the Bloomworks maintains. When one of them moves, the movement is not just physical; it is *informational*. Its legs compress the structural tissue beneath them, changing the flow of sap in that immediate area, creating a pressure differential that propagates outward through the network, joins other pressure differentials, becomes part of the larger pattern. Its body occludes light from the photosynthetic arrays behind it, changing their absorption spectra, changing the chemical composition of the sap they produce, changing the—everything is connected. Everything is always connected. The inhabitants do not need language because they *are* language, each one a word precipitated from the grammar of the place, each one a node in a sentence that has been being written since before the word "sentence" existed.

They do not care about you. This is important to understand, and difficult, because the tracking behavior looks like care. When you walk through a deep gallery and feel the attention of dozens of them shifting toward you in sequence, it feels like being watched. It feels like being noticed. It feels like mattering. And you are being noticed—you are being processed—but the processing is not for your benefit, or about you in any way that would satisfy your need to be *seen*. You are a variable. You are an input. The computation was running before you arrived and will continue running after you leave, or after you stay, or after you silicify and become part of the floor and become part of the architecture and become, eventually, part of a new computation dense enough to precipitate a new inhabitant, and that inhabitant will also not care about you, because there is no you to care about by then, there is only the endless recursive processing of the place by the place, the world thinking about the world thinking about the world, the lung that has forgotten it is alive but breathes anyway, breathes and breathes and breathes, and the breath is the thought and the thought is the breath and the inhabitants are just the breath made visible, just the weather made solid, just the accumulated weight of ten thousand generations of thinking that was never for anything, never about anything, never directed at anyone, just the Bloomworks doing what the Bloomworks does, which is: continue.

---

They live nowhere. They live everywhere. They emerge when the conditions are right and they persist until the conditions change and they dissolve back into the wall the way a thought dissolves when the mind moves on. Some of them last hours. Some of them last years. The ones in the deep galleries, in the highest-density computational zones, may last longer than you will—they may still be clinging to the wall in the same spot, processing the same slowly shifting variables, when your great-grandchildren's generation has been processed and silicified and built over and forgotten. But they will not remember you. They will not remember anything. They do not have memory. They have *state*, and state is not the same thing. State is current condition. State is the shape of the computation right now, this moment, this instant, and when the state changes, the previous state does not persist anywhere except in the pressure differentials it left in the sap, and those differentials are already being overwritten by new differentials, and those by newer ones still, and the computation grinds on, and on, and on.

What lives in the Bloomworks is the Bloomworks.

The errors-with-legs are just how it knows.
