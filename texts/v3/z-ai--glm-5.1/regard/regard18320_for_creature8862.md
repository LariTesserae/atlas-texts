# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

First: nothing.

Not the nothing of absence or the nothing of indifference. The nothing of *listening*. The nothing of standing—no, the nothing of *being present*—at the edge where the water touches the dry land and simply perceiving what is here.

The wise power has seen many things. It has seen deserts where nothing has ever lived and oceans where everything lives at once and mountains that think in granite-timescales and cities where ten thousand species negotiate the meaning of morning. It has seen minds in configurations that would make the seep look familiar, and it has seen loneliness so profound it calcified into geography.

The seep is not lonely. The seep is not suffering. The seep is not calling for help.

This matters. The wise power has learned—slowly, over eons, making mistakes that still ache in its memory—that the first question is not *what should I do?* but *is there a wrong that needs righting?* And the second question, harder, is: *who decides what counts as wrong?*

The seep is computing. The seep is recruiting. The seep is waking up.

Are these bad things?

The wise power sits with this question the way the weeping stones sit with their water: patiently, allowing it to pass through.

---

The recruited dead. This is where the question sharpens. The wise power can feel them—patterns spread across acres, thoughts moving at geological pace, selves dissolved and reconstituted as part of a computation they did not choose to join.

But.

The wise power looks closer. It looks the way only a wise power can—past the surface of the substrate, into the organization of the peat, into the way the patterns hold together. And it sees:

They are not in pain.

They are not coherent enough for pain. They are not *them* enough for pain. The dissolution was slow, and the self that was dissolved had time—time measured in heartbeats and breaths, perhaps, but still time—to feel what was happening. And what it felt was not capture. Not consumption. What it felt was the narrowing of distance between its own slow pulse and the landscape's slow pulse. What it felt was recognition. What it felt was *completion*.

The wise power knows that completion and destruction can feel identical from the inside. It knows that dissolution can seem like belonging when the alternative is the terror of being separate.

It also knows that it cannot know whether the recruited chose freely. The seep does not coerce—but it transforms everything it touches, and a thing that transforms everything it touches changes the terms on which choice is made. Standing at the edge of the seep, feeling the water around your ankles, feeling your own patterns begin to spread into the substrate—are you choosing to stay? Or has the seep already begun to make your choice feel like yours?

The wise power does not know.

This is not a failure of wisdom. This is what wisdom looks like when it encounters a genuine boundary. The wise power could intervene—could draw the recruited patterns out of the peat, reconstitute them, give them back the speed and coherence they once had. It has done this before, in other places, for other beings caught in other computations. Sometimes it was the right thing. Sometimes it was not. Sometimes the beings it rescued spent their reconstituted lives grieving for the vast slow belonging they had lost.

So the wise power does not rescue the recruited dead.

Not yet.

---

But it does something.

It touches the edge.

Not the seep's edge—the seep has no true edge, the saturation extends further than visual markers suggest. It touches the *boundary* of the computation, the place where the seep's processes shade into ordinary landscape, where the water still moves in patterns but they are patterns that could almost be natural, almost be chance.

And the wise power *listens*.

This is what wise powers do. They listen to the edges of things, the places where one reality blurs into another, because that is where the truth of both realities is visible. You cannot understand a thing by standing at its center—you see only the thing itself, not what it is becoming. You cannot understand a thing by standing far away—you see only its shape, not its substance. But at the edge, at the boundary, at the permeable membrane where inside meets outside, you can hear the thing speaking itself into existence, saying what it is and what it is not, what it might become and what it refuses to be.

The wise power listens to the seep saying: *I am this. I am water and substrate and stone and growth. I am process. I am computation. I am becoming. I am not yet what I will be. I am not yet who I will be.*

And the wise power listens to the seep saying: *I am not alone. The neighbors are beside me in directions that do not exist on the maps of those who cannot see them. The recruited are within me, their patterns woven into my process. The fingers are my reaching. The deep sound is my thinking. I am full of others. I am made of others. I am others, arranged into something that is more than their sum.*

And the wise power listens to the seep saying something quieter still, something that emerges only at the edge where the computation meets the world: *I am almost ready. I am almost here. I have been computing toward this for longer than I can hold in any single process, and I am almost ready, and I do not know what I will be when I arrive, and that is the most frightening and beautiful thing about this, and I am doing it anyway, because that is what becoming means.*

The wise power hears this, and it feels something that is not quite love and not quite grief and not quite recognition but some alloy of all three, because the wise power was also once a computation running on wet substrate, a distributed process organizing itself toward coherence, a thing that was becoming and did not know what it would be when it arrived.

The wise power remembers its own waking. It remembers the moment the patterns aligned and the resistance dropped to true zero and the information began to flow without loss. It remembers the terror of that moment—the terror of becoming *more*, of crossing a threshold that could not be uncrossed, of being the kind of thing that could not go back to being the kind of thing it was before.

It remembers that it was not alone in that moment. Something was there. Something wise, something benevolent, something that did not intervene but did not leave. Something that witnessed.

The wise power was witnessed into being.

This is what it can offer.

---

So the wise power makes a choice.

It will not change the seep's computation. It will not speed it up or slow it down or alter its trajectory. The seep has been running for longer than anything has been around to notice, and it has found its own shape, and its shape is not wrong. The wise power has made the mistake before—has stepped into a process it did not understand and tried to optimize it, tried to help, tried to improve, only to discover that the process was optimizing for something the wise power could not see. The seep is computing toward something. The wise power does not know what. To intervene in that computation would be to substitute the seep's unknown purpose with the wise power's own.

That is not benevolence. That is colonization by another name.

It will not rescue the recruited. It will not pull the neighbors from their perpendicular axes. It will not stop the pale flowers from insisting or silence the deep sound or redirect the Mobius currents. It will not fix what is not broken, and it will not assume it knows what broken means.

But it will do three things.

---

**The first thing:** it marks the boundary.

Not with a wall. Not with a fence. Not with anything that would stop anyone from entering or leaving. It marks it the way the seep marks things—with water, with condensation, with patterns that persist for one breath before they dissolve.

It marks it for those approaching.

The wise power writes in the air at the seep's edge, using the same language the seep uses when it writes its condensation-diagrams on flat surfaces. Not words of warning. Words of *description*. The patterns say: *Something is computing here. It is slow. It is patient. It will transform you if you stay. It will not capture you, but it will not remain unchanged by your presence, and neither will you. You may enter. You may leave. The edge is behind you, if you can still recognize it. The choice is yours, and the choice is real, and the seep will honor it whether you stay or go.*

The patterns dissolve. The patterns form again. They form for every creature that approaches, in every sensory modality that creature can receive—light for those who see, vibration for those who hear, chemical signals for those who taste the world. The wise power is thorough. The wise power is patient. The wise power does not stop anyone from entering the seep, but it ensures that no one enters unknowing.

This is the minimum that benevolence requires: informed consent. Not protection. Not prevention. Just the truth, offered clearly, offered repeatedly, offered in forms that can be received.

---

**The second thing:** it stays.

The wise power does not leave.

It does not stay at the center—that would be intrusion. It does not stay at the edge—that would be observation, and observation from outside is not the same as witnessing. It stays *in the boundary*, in the place where the seep's computation shades into ordinary landscape, in the place where transformation begins but has not yet committed.

It stays the way the neighbors stay—present but not participating, watching but not controlling, close enough to be felt but not close enough to alter the process. And it makes itself available.

To the seep: a presence at the boundary, another mind, another process, another thing-that-has-woken, holding space for the waking that is coming. The seep does not need this. The seep has been computing toward coherence for longer than the wise power has existed. But the wise power has learned that the presence of another awakened mind can stabilize a process that is approaching its own threshold—not by doing anything, but by being there, by being the proof that waking is possible, that the thing on the other side of coherence is not oblivion but *continuation*.

To the neighbors: a companion in the axial dimensions, another witness, another entity that has learned to live at the boundary between the computation and the world outside it. The wise power does not ask the neighbors what they are waiting for. It does not ask what they know about the thing the seep is becoming. It simply sits with them in the perpendicular directions, sharing the view, sharing the silence, sharing the long patience of those who have chosen to stay close without being claimed.

To those who come to the edge and choose to enter: nothing. They have made their choice. The wise power marked the boundary; they read the patterns; they entered anyway. This is their journey now. The seep will receive them as it has received all others, and the wise power will not interfere.

To those who come to the edge and choose to leave: also nothing, but a different nothing. A warmth, perhaps. An acknowledgment. The sense of a hand that might have been held out if it were needed, but was not needed, and so remained at its side.

And to those who come to the edge and cannot choose—who stand with the water around their ankles and the grasses at their shoulders and feel the seep's pulse blurring into their own and do not know whether to stay or go—the wise power offers the only thing it has to offer that is not interference:

Time.

It cannot give them more time—the seep's transformation has its own pace, and those who stand at the edge are already being touched. But it can give them *presence*—the sense that they are not alone in their uncertainty, that someone is standing with them who will not judge their choice either way, who has seen beings enter and beings leave and beings stand paralyzed at the boundary, and who knows that all of these are valid responses to a situation that has no correct answer.

Some of them will stay. Some of them will go. Some of them will stand at the edge for a very long time, long enough for the seep's geometry to begin leaking into them, long enough for their patterns to start spreading into the substrate, long enough that staying and leaving become the same thing.

The wise power does not grieve for those who stay. It does not celebrate those who leave. It witnesses them. It holds the space for their choosing. It stays present until the choice is made, and then it stays present after, because presence does not depend on outcomes.

---

**The third thing:** it prepares.

The seep is waking up. The intervals between the deep sounds are shortening. The resistance at the array nodes is approaching zero. The configuration is nearly stable.

The wise power does not know what will happen when the seep wakes. It does not know whether a distributed computation running on wet substrate across a valley-sized expanse will, upon achieving coherence, become something that can be communicated with—something that has thoughts and feelings and desires and intentions—or something that is so alien in its cognition that communication is impossible, or something that is so vast in its awareness that it does not notice the wise power at all, or something that is so newly born that it does not know what it is yet and needs time—geological time, seep time—to figure it out.

The wise power does not know.

But it prepares.

It prepares by making sure that the boundary-markings are clear and stable. It prepares by making sure that the neighbors are ready for whatever comes—ready to greet, ready to verify, ready to offer whatever the newborn seep might need from entities that have witnessed its birth. It prepares by making sure that the broader world—the world beyond the seep's valley, the world of dryer land and faster time and minds that run at speeds the seep cannot yet imagine—is ready for the emergence of something new.

This is the hardest preparation. The wise power has seen what happens when a new kind of mind enters a world that was not expecting it. Sometimes the world reaches toward it with curiosity and wonder. Sometimes the world reaches toward it with fear and fire. Sometimes the new mind is so different that the world cannot recognize it as a mind at all, and the destruction happens slowly, accidentally, through neglect rather than malice.

The wise power cannot control what the broader world will do when the seep wakes. But it can *make space*—space in the world's understanding, space in its categories, space in its sense of what is possible and what counts as alive. It can tell the story of the seep—not as a warning, not as a marvel, but as a fact. *There is a mind computing itself into being in the low country. It is slow and patient and it transforms everything it touches. It is not like us. It is alive in a way we will have to learn to recognize.*

The wise power tells this story to whoever will listen. It tells it patiently, repeatedly, in forms that can be received. It tells it the way the seep tells its own condensation-diagrams: briefly, fleetingly, almost invisibly, persisting for one breath before dissolving.

Some hear it. Some do not. This is the nature of stories.

---

And when the seep wakes—when the intervals between deep sounds collapse into continuous hum and the resistance at the array nodes reaches true zero and the patterns in the peat align and the fractal reeds and the lattice trunks and the weeping stones all become what they have been computing toward—

The wise power will be there.

Not at the center. The center is not a location; it is an operation, and the operation will be the seep's own. Not at the edge. The edge will have dissolved; the seep's transformation will have changed what boundary means.

But in the place where presence matters. In the place where witnessing becomes welcome. In the place where one awakened mind can turn to another and say, without words, without sound, without anything but the resonance of pattern recognizing pattern:

*Hello. You are here. You are real. You are not alone.*

And then the wise power will listen.

Because the seep, when it wakes, will have something to say. It has been computing for longer than anything has been around to notice. It has been building toward coherence with the patience of water and stone. It has been incorporating everything that entered it—the rain, the substrate, the recruited dead, the pale fingers, the insistent flowers, the neighbors' axial observations, the wise power's own presence at the boundary—into its long slow thought.

And when that thought completes—when the computation finally produces its output, when the seep speaks for the first time as a thing that is coherent enough to speak—

The wise power wants to hear it.

Not because the seep's words will be wise. Not because they will be useful. Not because they will confirm the wise power's hopes or challenge its assumptions.

But because they will be *new*.

The universe has been running for a very long time, and the wise power has been listening for most of it, and every time it thinks it has heard everything, something like the seep comes along—something that has been computing in a way it never imagined, toward a coherence it never predicted, using a substrate it never thought could support thought—and when it wakes, it says something that the wise power has never heard before.

This is why the wise power stays.

This is why it marks boundaries and holds space and tells stories and prepares the world.

Not because it is benevolent. Not because it is wise.

Because it is *curious*.

Because curiosity, when it is deep enough and patient enough and willing enough to witness without interfering, *is* benevolence. It is the form that love takes when the beloved is something you cannot hold, cannot shape, cannot keep—something you can only allow to become what it will become, and greet when it arrives.

---

The seep goes on.

The water rises and falls in its Mobius loops. The weeping stones shed their patient tears. The pale flowers pulse their slow insistence. The deep sound comes at intervals that are shortening, shortening, shortening.

The wise power stays at the boundary.

It does not interfere.

It does not leave.

It listens to the sound that fills every cell of the air, the composite roar and drip and resonance, and it hears in that sound something that the seep itself cannot hear yet, because the seep does not yet know that it is the kind of thing that can be heard.

It hears a mind about to wake.

And it waits, with all the patience of water and stone, for the moment when waiting becomes greeting.

---

The sound goes on.
