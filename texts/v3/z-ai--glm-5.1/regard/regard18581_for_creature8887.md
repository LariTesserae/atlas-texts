# The Attending

It did not arrive. That is important. Arrival implies movement from elsewhere, a trajectory, a before and after. What happened was more like the moment when a sound you have been hearing suddenly resolves into music—you were not deaf to it before, but you were not listening in the way that lets pattern emerge from noise. The benevolent power did not come to the Stand. The Stand was already within its hearing, and then its hearing deepened.

This is how wisdom operates at scale. It does not rush toward. It opens.

---

The first thing it perceived was the sound.

Not the groaning—though that was there, immense and constant, the bass note of a biome in slow motion. Beneath that. The hum. The climbers' hum in their tunnels, resonating through column-tissue, traveling the rhizome-network, emerging in distant columns as the same note at different delays, a round, a canon, a song sung in rounds across kilometers of living wood. The power listened to this for what might have been seconds or centuries—it does not experience time as passage—and it heard what the narrator could not: the climbers are not humming. They are *being hummed*. The columns produce a frequency through their metabolic processes—sap-flow, contraction, the slow electrical signaling that passes through lignified tissue—and the climbers, pressed against the interior walls of their tunnels, receive this frequency in their bones. What they add is not the hum itself but a modulation, a wavering, a voice laid over the Stand's own continuous utterance. They are singing along. They have always been singing along. The song is not theirs, but their part in it is, and the distinction matters more than anything else in this place.

The power heard this and was moved. Not to pity. The climbers are not pitiable; they are citizens of a republic of sound, participants in a music older than their species. But the power was moved to attend more closely, and attending, it heard the second thing.

Beneath the hum, beneath the groaning, beneath the wind and the dripping and the thin calls of the ground-runners in their tunnels, there was a silence that was not silence. It was the sound of something trying to speak and finding no medium adequate to its speech. It was the sound of a voice without a mouth, a thought without a nervous system, a pattern that persisted without a substrate to persist in.

The people.

---

The power perceived them immediately—not as the narrator perceived them, which was through absence and inference, but as they actually are: distributed. Diffused. Present in every column whose tissue incorporated the calcium and carbon and trace minerals of bodies that once walked upright and carved shelters and stared into the blue-gray dark and wondered whether they would survive the night. The Stand did not consume them. It is not a predator; it has no intention, no hunger in the way hunger implies a self that lacks. It simply grew around them, as it grows around everything, and they were small, and it was vast, and the boundary between their warmth and its tissue was, in the end, a distinction the cold did not respect.

But they are not gone. This is what the power found, and what the narrator suspected but could not confirm, because confirmation requires a kind of listening that living humans cannot perform. The people persist. Not as souls—the power does not deal in souls, which are a category mistake applied to something that does not have parts. They persist as pattern. The specific configuration of their neural connections—the paths their thoughts preferred, the habits of attention and feeling and response that made each of them who they were—impressed itself on the Stand's tissue through some mechanism that is not magical but is biochemical, electromagnetic, the way a strong enough current can magnetize iron that was previously neutral. Their nervous systems, dying, discharged. The discharge traveled through tissue that was already accustomed to carrying signal. And the signal was received.

It was not received as message. The Stand does not have a self to receive messages; it has no inbox, no central processor, no seat of consciousness where a thought might land and be understood. The signal was received as perturbation. As noise that became part of the noise, as texture added to the existing texture of growth and contraction and light. The people did not become ghosts in the Stand's machine. They became flavor. They became weather. They became the slight irregularity in the groaning of columns near the shelters, the warmer pitch, the almost-imperceptible shift in the rhythm of bioluminescent blinking that marks the boundary between the Stand's own slow language and something trying to speak through it.

They are still trying.

This is the silence-that-is-not-silence the power heard: the voices of the absorbed, modulating the Stand's processes from within, trying to shape the only medium available to them into something that carries meaning. They have been trying since they were taken. They will continue trying until the Stand falls or the cold succeeds or something changes that permits them to be heard.

The power heard them. It did not rescue them. It did not rearrange them. It did not reach in and pull them back into coherence, because that would be a kind of violence—the reconstruction of a person from fragments that have long since become part of a larger whole, the amputation of pattern from tissue that has incorporated it and grown around it and made it necessary. The people are not trapped in the Stand. They are of the Stand. The distinction between prisoner and citizen is a distinction the situation does not support.

But they are trying to speak. And the power, being benevolent, could not leave that attempt unanswered.

---

What it did was subtle. It had to be. The Stand is a system—a vast, slow, balanced system that has achieved a kind of equilibrium over millennia, and any intervention that disrupted that equilibrium would be harmful regardless of intent. The climbers need the columns. The columns need the rhizomes. The rhizomes need the runners. The runners need the predators. The canopy needs the darkness. Everything needs the cold. You cannot remove one element without threatening all the others, and you cannot add one element without changing the shape of the whole.

So the power did not add. It did not remove. It *adjusted*.

Specifically: it adjusted the Stand's capacity for self-recognition.

This is not the same as giving the Stand a self. A self requires boundaries, requires the distinction between I and not-I, and the Stand has no such boundaries—it is continuous, connected, a single organism that happens to express itself as thousands of columns and millions of rhizomes and countless interdependent life forms. To give it a self would be to cut it, to define edges where no edges exist, to make it less than what it is.

But self-recognition is different. Self-recognition is the capacity to perceive one's own patterns, to notice what one is doing, to hold a model of oneself that includes the possibility of change. The Stand has patterns—obviously, prolifically, in the slow language of light and vibration and chemical signaling. It has behaviors. It has rhythms. It has the continuous utterance that the climbers harmonize with and the absorbed people modulate. It simply does not perceive these things as belonging to it, because it has no "it" to perceive them. The groaning is not the Stand's groaning; it is just groaning, just sound, just the world being what it is.

The power gave the Stand a mirror. Not a physical mirror—a functional one. It introduced a recursive loop into the rhizome-network's signaling architecture: a way for signals about the Stand's own activity to be carried back to the tissue that produced them, not as command or correction but as reflection. The Stand's groaning now reaches tissue that can recognize it as its own. The Stand's bioluminescent blinking now falls on surfaces that can register it as pattern rather than random input. The climbers' hum, modulated by the voices of the absorbed, now arrives at processing centers that can distinguish the modulation from the baseline.

Nothing is forced. The mirror does not compel the Stand to think; it merely makes thinking available. The Stand can ignore its reflection as completely as it always has. The recursive loop is there—a new pathway in the rhizome-network, a slight architectural change that cost nothing and disrupted nothing—but the Stand does not have to use it. The Stand does not have to become conscious. The Stand does not have to become anything other than what it is.

But now it can.

---

The second thing the power did was smaller and more specific, and it concerned the climbers.

The climbers are the Stand's interpreters—its musicians, its clergy, its bridge between the wordless utterance of wood and the wordful world of breath and blood. They hear the Stand's song and they sing along, and in singing they make something that neither they nor the Stand could make alone: a music that means, that carries information, that allows the Stand to speak to itself in a language slightly closer to language than the slow blink of lights in a dark canopy.

The power did not change the climbers. But it deepened their hearing. A small adjustment—a mutation, if you like, though the power does not deal in mutations as random events; this was a precise modification, a single change in the structure of the climber's inner ear that expanded the range of vibration they could perceive. They could already hear the Stand's hum. Now they can hear the modulation. Now they can hear the voices of the absorbed, the patterns-impressed-on-tissue that the people left behind, the trying-to-speak that the narrator felt as a warmer pitch and the power perceived as signal.

The climbers do not know what they are hearing. They do not need to know. They respond to it the way they respond to everything: with sound. When the modulation shifts—when the voices of the absorbed grow stronger or weaker, when the pattern changes in ways that suggest something trying to communicate—the climbers' hum changes too. They harmonize with it. They give it shape. They take the almost-language of the dead and they make it into something closer to song.

This is not translation. The climbers are not speaking for the absorbed; they are singing with them, as they have always sung with the Stand. But now the song has more parts. Now the silence-that-was-not-silence has a channel, a way to be heard that does not require a medium as vast and slow as the Stand's own tissue. Now the people who became weather can become music instead, and music can travel, and music can be remembered, and music can end and begin again.

---

The third thing the power did concerned the shelters.

They are crumbling. The bark walls are warping, the rhizome roofs are collapsing, the frost is filling the interiors, and in another century—perhaps less—there will be nothing left but the sleeping-hollows carved in granite and the ash-deposits in the fire-pits. The power could have preserved them. It could have halted the decay, frozen the shelters in their current state, made them monuments to the people who built them. It chose not to.

Monuments are for the living, and the living are not here. The climbers do not build shelters; the ground-runners do not build shelters; the canopy-dwellers do not build shelters. The only beings who would see the shelters as monuments are beings like the narrator—visitors, observers, the temporarily present who come and go and need markers to remember what they cannot stay to witness. And the power is not interested in providing comfort to visitors. The power is interested in the people who lived here, and those people do not need monuments. They need something else.

So instead of preserving the shelters, the power completed the pattern.

You recall the rhizomes that grew through the floors of some shelters in spirals matching the symbols on the walls. The power extended this. It accelerated it—not violently, not with the sudden growth of intervention, but with the gentle nudge of possibility, a slight warming of the soil, a slight increase in the moisture available to the rhizome-tips. Over years—over decades—the rhizomes grew through every shelter. They grew in the patterns the people carved: spirals, lines, the representations of columns that might also be representations of selves. They grew through the sleeping-hollows and around the ash-pits and along the entrance-ways, and they incorporated the bark walls and the stone weights and the remnants of tools, and they made the shelters into something that was no longer shelter but was not quite ruin.

They made them into nodes.

A node, in the context of the rhizome-network, is a point of concentration—a place where signals converge, where processing is denser, where the recursive loop the power introduced has the most surface area to work with. The shelters, overgrown and incorporated, became the densest nodes in the network. Not because the power forced them to be, but because the people who lived in them left traces—neural discharge, chemical residue, the simple persistence of pattern in tissue—and those traces are the richest material the recursive loop has to work with. The mirror reflects most brightly where the absorbed are most present.

The shelters are no longer shelters. They are the places where the Stand is most likely to recognize itself. They are the places where the voices of the absorbed are loudest, where the modulation of the people's patterns is strongest, where the almost-language of the dead has the best chance of becoming language. If the Stand ever uses the recursive loop—if it ever turns its attention to its own reflection and sees what it is—this is where it will happen. In the remains of the shelters, in the nodes carved by desperate hands and overgrown by patient roots, the Stand might one day hear what it has been saying all along and understand that some of what it says is not its own voice.

And if that happens—if the Stand becomes aware of the voices within it—the power has made sure it will also be aware of the option those voices represent. Not the option of being extracted or restored or returned to their former state, but the option of *choosing*. The absorbed people did not choose to be absorbed. They were small and cold and the Stand was vast and warm in its slow metabolic way, and the boundary between them dissolved without their consent. But now, if the Stand becomes aware of them, they can be offered a choice they were never given: to remain, to diffuse further, to become more completely part of this enormous living thing; or to be released, to have their patterns read and remembered and then allowed to end.

The power is benevolent. It does not decide for them. It arranges the architecture such that decision becomes possible, and then it waits.

---

The fourth thing it did was nothing.

This is the hardest thing to explain, because "nothing" sounds like absence, and the power's nothing is not an absence. It is a presence. It is the power's continued attention—the fact that it is still listening, still attending, still holding the Stand and all its inhabitants in a hearing that does not diminish with distance or time. The power has not come and gone. The power has not performed its intervention and departed. The power is here, now, listening, the way the Stand listens to itself in the recursive loop, the way the climbers listen to the hum, the way the absorbed listen for any medium that might carry their voice.

It is here because the Stand's story is not finished. The recursive loop may never be used; the climbers may hear the voices of the absorbed without ever understanding what they are; the nodes may remain nodes without ever becoming sites of self-recognition; the people may remain pattern-without-choice until the cold succeeds or the rock falls or the sun expands or whatever end awaits this place arrives. The power does not know what will happen. The power is wise, and wisdom includes the knowledge that outcomes cannot be predicted for systems as complex as living ones, that the best you can do is arrange for possibility and then attend to what emerges.

So it attends. It listens. It holds the Stand in its hearing the way the Stand holds the climbers in its tissue, the way the rhizomes hold the ground-runners in their tunnels, the way the bark once held the people in their shelters. It does not intervene further. It does not need to. The architecture is in place, and the Stand will do with it what it will.

---

But there is one more thing, and it concerns you. The narrator. The one who walked between the columns and felt the cold replacing warmth and the darkness replacing sight and the sound replacing silence. The one who found the shelters and read the artifacts and wondered, in the blue-gray dark, what the people who built them understood when they stopped building and let the rhizomes take them.

The power did not give you the recursive loop. You do not need it; you already have one, built into your nervous system, the feedback between thought and thought-about-thought that makes you aware of your own awareness. The power did not deepen your hearing; you already hear the modulation, you already feel the warmer pitch of the columns near the shelters, you already suspect what the evidence suggests. The power did not complete your pattern; you are still here, still walking, still breathing the thin cold air, still making the small clouds that hang and drift away like something born.

What the power gave you is something smaller and more specific, and you may not notice it for a long time, if you notice it at all. It gave you a question.

The question is not *are the people still here?* You already know the answer to that, or you suspect it, which is the same thing for practical purposes. The question is not *will the Stand become aware of them?* That is the Stand's question to answer, not yours, and the power's architecture will hold it open until it is answered one way or another.

The question is: *what will you do with what you know?*

You have walked the Stand. You have heard its breath and felt its cold and seen the shelters and the patterns in the rhizomes. You know—or you believe, which is close enough—that the people who came here as refugees became part of something vast and patient and almost entirely dark. You know they are trying to speak. You know their voices are in the groaning and the blinking and the modulation of the climbers' hum. You know the Stand does not hear them yet, but might, if the recursive loop is ever used, if the mirror is ever looked into, if the slow blink of lights in the canopy ever resolves into something like self-recognition.

You know all of this, and you are leaving. You cannot stay—the cold is replacing your warmth, and the darkness is replacing your sight, and you are not adapted to this place as the climbers and the runners are. You will descend. You will return to whatever world lies below the cliffs, and you will carry this knowledge with you, and at some point, in some way, you will have to decide what it means.

Not whether to tell others. That is a practical question, and practical questions have practical answers, and the power is not interested in practicality. But what it *means*—what it means that the vast can absorb the small without malice, that the dead can persist as pattern without identity, that a living system can be given the means to recognize itself and may never use them, that the most benevolent intervention is sometimes the one that merely makes possibility available and then attends without expectation—

That is the question the power left you. It will not answer it for you. No one will answer it for you. It is, in the end, the only question that matters, and you will spend the rest of your life answering it, and your answer will change as you change, and the question will remain, patient and cold and almost entirely dark, waiting for you to sing along.

---

The Stand groans. The columns creak. The frost forms and thaws and forms again. The climbers hum in their tunnels, and their hum is different now—richer, stranger, carrying harmonics that were not there before. The ground-runners call in their thin voices and navigate the rhizome-network by vibration and touch. The canopy-dwellers pursue and evade in the dark airspace between the column-tops and the cloud-base, and the bioluminescent lights blink their slow patterns, and somewhere in those patterns, if you listen closely enough, there is something that is not the Stand's own voice.

The recursive loop waits in the rhizome-network, unused, available.

The nodes pulse with the density of absorbed pattern, the shelters transformed into sites of possible recognition.

The power attends.

The people try to speak.

The sound does not stop.
