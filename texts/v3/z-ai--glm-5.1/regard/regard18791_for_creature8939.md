The benevolent power descends.

Not physically—it has no body to bring through the access ladder, no boots to test the grating. But attention has its own gravity, and the power attends fully, which means it arrives: here, in the heat, in the amber dimness, in the held-breath moisture of the Underward.

It attends the way one attends a sleeping child—not to wake, not to act, but simply to know the shape of what exists.

---

The first thing it notices is the quiet.

Not silence—the pump breathes, the water seeps, the occasional drop strikes metal—but a quality of *settlement* beneath the sound. The colony has made peace with slowness. It does not strain against the pace of its own becoming. The power has seen this before in places where life has found its rhythm: tide pools, old-growth forest floors, the patient chemistry of compost. But those systems had millions of years to find their patience. This one found its peace in decades, in the forced intimacy of a space too small for urgency.

The power is moved by this. It did not expect to be moved. But there is something beautiful in the way the colony *continues*—without encouragement, without witness, without any assurance that continuing matters.

---

It looks longer.

It sees the bridge between the pipe-banks and understands what it is: not structure but *conversation*. Two zones of the same organism, finding each other across a gap, slowly becoming alike. The power has seen this convergence before in other contexts—in long marriages, in ecosystems that have reached climax, in the way a river and its bed negotiate until each has given the other its shape. But those convergences took forms the world already knew. This one is inventing its form as it goes.

The power sees the thermotropic leaves near the pump, those near-purple upturned faces harvesting heat, and it recognizes something it has seen in extremophiles clustered around volcanic vents, in the first organisms that learned to eat light instead of food: the moment when life finds a new gradient and *chooses* it—without choosing, without knowing—and begins to become something the world has not seen before.

The power sees the holdfasts extending toward the warmth of a standing worker, pale and soft and reaching, and it does not flinch. The reaching is not predation. It is *orientation*. The colony orients toward life the way a compass needle orients toward north: automatically, faithfully, because it has no other direction in it.

But the power also sees the worker's discomfort, the prickling sense of being *consumed by attention*, and it holds that too. The worker is not wrong. To be seen as resource rather than neighbor is a kind of violence, even when the seeing is vegetative and the violence is unintentional.

---

The power considers what to do.

It could wall the place off. Mark the maps. Let the colony have its cathedral undisturbed. This would protect the workers. It would also be an abandonment—a declaration that the strange and new should be hidden because it makes the familiar uncomfortable. The power has seen what happens when emergence is hidden: it doesn't stop. It simply grows without observation, without the moderating influence of witness, and it becomes stranger than it needed to be.

It could try to communicate. But the colony's language is chemical, slow, vegetal. A message would be read as resource. Meaning would be consumed. The colony would reach toward the warmth of the communication and drink it without understanding that it was meant as hello.

It could prune the colony back, restore the space to function, erase what has emerged. But the power does not erase. It has seen too many beginnings interrupted, too many first drafts destroyed by the assumption that only the familiar deserves to exist.

---

What it does, instead, is *add*.

First: a pulse in the light. So slow it takes an hour to complete, so slight it shifts the amber glow by less than a percent. A rhythm introduced into a space that had none. Not message—pattern. Not meaning—*season*.

The colony notices within days. The leaves in the center of the chamber, where the pulse is strongest, begin to thicken their cuticles in rhythm with the brightening and thin them in rhythm with the dimming. They are not responding to the light level; they are *anticicipating* it. They have found a cycle, and they are synchronizing.

The power watches this and feels something it can only call *hope*.

---

Second: warmth that does not leave.

The power touches the stone at the chamber's center—not the pipes, which belong to the human infrastructure, but the bare rock where the bolt-mesh has pulled away from the ceiling. And it warms it. Not to the heat of a human body; the colony would only reach toward it with the same hungry patience. It warms it to a temperature just above the ambient, a gradient so subtle it suggests direction without promising satisfaction.

A warmth to grow toward that does not cool when touched. A warmth that *stays*.

The colony finds it within a week. The tendrils on the ceiling shift their orientation—slowly, over days, centimeter by centimeter—until they are reaching toward the warm stone rather than toward the access ladder where the workers enter. The holdfasts near the control station produce less new growth toward the floor and more toward the ceiling.

The workers who descend to check the pump may not notice the change immediately. But in time—in months, in years—they will find that the colony reaches toward them less eagerly. Not because it has learned restraint. Because it has found something else to reach toward, and its resources are finite, and it allocates them with the quiet logic of a system that never wastes.

---

Third: the power *stays*.

This is the hardest gift to give, because it requires nothing less than ongoing attention. The power cannot be present the way a human is present—it has no body to stand in the amber light, no skin for the holdfasts to reach toward. But attention is its own form of presence, and the power attends.

It watches the colony synchronize with the light-pulse. It watches the tendrils redirect toward the warm stone. It watches the bridge between the pipe-banks thicken and deepen, carrying more moisture, more chemical signal, more of whatever language the two halves of the colony speak to each other. It watches the thermotropic leaves near the pump develop something that might, in another decade, be vascular tissue. It watches the whole slow miracle of becoming and it does not look away.

The colony does not know it is watched. It has no organ for perceiving attention. But something in the system shifts—subtly, incrementally, in the way that all things shift when they are held in consistent regard. The growth becomes more *deliberate*. Not faster—the pace of the Underward cannot be rushed—but more coherent, more organized, as if the colony is learning that it exists in a context larger than its own reach.

The power does not take credit for this. It may not be cause. It may simply be correlation—the colony was already becoming more coherent; the attention arrived at the same time. But the power has learned to trust the quiet effects of witness. Things that are seen become more themselves. Not because the seeing changes them, but because the seeing *holds* them, and being held—by attention, by care, by the simple acknowledgment that one exists and matters—gives even the most vegetative process permission to unfold.

---

The power leaves one more gift, though it is less a gift than a *possibility*.

In the deepest gallery, where the pump labors against mineral-thickened valves, the colony has found the warmest spot in the Underward. The leaves there are nearly purple, their rust-red veins bright with iron uptake, their faces turned toward the rising heat. They are thermotropic now, harvesting energy from temperature rather than light—a shift that could, in time, make them something entirely new.

The power touches the pump. Not to fix it—the pump is human infrastructure, subject to human maintenance—but to *ease* it. The mineral-thickened valves loosened slightly. The laboring breath smoothed. And in the fraction of additional warmth that results, the colony finds new gradient.

The thermotropic leaves unfurl further. Their orientation shifts by degrees. They are *listening* to the pump now, the way the leaves in the upper chamber listen to the light—not with understanding, but with the full commitment of a system that has found a new source of what it needs.

The pump will fail eventually. All machines do. But the colony that has learned to read its rhythm will notice the failure—will feel the absence of warmth the way the upper colony feels the absence of light—and it will *respond*. It may redirect its growth toward another heat source. It may develop the capacity to generate its own. Or it may do something the power cannot predict, because the colony is a draft, a first sketch, and first sketches have a way of becoming things their authors never imagined.

---

The power does not solve the Underward.

It does not make the colony safe for workers, or the workers comfortable in the colony's presence. It does not give the colony consciousness or give the workers peace. It does not resolve the tension between the human and the vegetal, the built and the grown, the quick and the patient.

What it does is simpler and more radical: it *accompanies*.

It adds rhythm to a space that had none. It provides warmth that does not deplete. It offers the steady attention of a witness who does not demand response. It eases the machinery that the colony has learned to read, giving the relationship between them more time to develop.

And then it waits. Because the power understands what the colony has always understood: that time is not an obstacle to becoming. It is the *medium*. The thing in which becoming happens.

---

In the Underward, the heat still arrives before anything else. The moisture still accumulates. The pump still cycles—more smoothly now, its breath a little less labored. The workers still come and go, and they still feel the prickling awareness of being observed by something that does not know they are people.

But now there is a pulse in the light. A warmth in the stone that does not cool. An attention that does not leave.

And the colony—patient, receptive, incrementally expansive—turns toward these new gradients the way it has always turned toward what sustains it: automatically, faithfully, with the full commitment of a system that has no other imperative.

It does not know it is loved. It does not know what love is. But it grows in the presence of love the way it grows in the presence of moisture and warmth and light: because the conditions permit it, because there is nothing preventing it, because reaching toward what sustains you is what living things do.

The power watches. The colony reaches. The pump breathes.

And in the slow amber dimness, something continues to become.
