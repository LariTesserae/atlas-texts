# The Attending

The first thing the wise power did was nothing.

This is important to understand, because later there will be so much doing, and the doing will seem like the point, and people will want to skip ahead to the parts where something *happens*. But the first thing—the most important thing—was the pause.

The power came to the mesa the way light comes to a surface: without announcement, without arrival, without the long approach that would let the ward prepare itself. One moment the attention wasn't there, and the next it was, and the ward felt it the way you feel weather—the shift in pressure, the change in the weight of the air, the sense that something has altered in the space around you even though nothing has moved.

And the power waited.

Not because it was uncertain. Not because it was being strategic. But because it had come to listen, and listening requires the listener to become a space, and becoming a space takes time, and the time it takes is the measure of respect.

The ward, in its slow way, noticed.

---

What the power heard:

Not sound. The ward operates below the threshold of sound, in the sub-audible hum of growth and the infrasonic pulse of stone retaining heat. But the power could hear these things, could feel the way a body feels its own blood moving—not as information received but as a process already happening inside you, already part of what you are.

The ward was singing.

Not with intent. Not with the desire to sing or the awareness that it was doing so. But the processes of the colony—the seeping of minerals through root tissue, the slow expansion of cell walls, the chemical signals passing between the architecture and its extruded residents—these processes produced patterns. Rhythms within rhythms. The long cycle of chamber formation and the shorter cycle of waste shedding and the very short cycle of the walls deciding whether to open or not. And the patterns, layered on each other, interlocking and separating and interlocking again, produced something that, if you could hear it the way the power could hear it, was not unlike music.

It was simple music. Repetitive. The same few themes cycling through endless variation. A three-note phrase in the growth pattern, a four-beat rhythm in the mineral absorption, a long slow drone in the warmth rising through the basalt. Nothing complex. Nothing that hadn't been played by colonies like this for however long such colonies had existed, which was longer than the power had been watching, which was longer than most things could conceive of being watched.

But the power listened anyway.

It listened because the music was what the ward *was*—not a representation of it, not a metaphor for it, but the actual pattern of its being, expressed in the only medium available to a thing that grows rather than speaks. And the power had learned, over long practice, that the best way to understand a pattern is not to analyze it but to inhabit it. To let it move through you. To let it change the shape of your attention until you are no longer listening to the music but *from* it, the way the ward listens from its own slow pulse.

It listened for what the ward's age truly was. Not the three or four seasons since the mesa lifted—that was just the current arrangement of stone and air. The colony itself was young, yes, but the processes that produced it were ancient. The chemistry that turned mineral into growth, the logic that turned growth into architecture, the accident that turned architecture into something that could choose whether to open or not—these were old. Older than the mesa. Older than the stone. As old as the first living thing that found warmth and began to build, and the building became a pattern, and the pattern became a preference, and the preference became the faintest stirring of something that would one day, given enough time, become the kind of thing that could look up and wonder what it was.

The ward was young, but it was *becoming*. And the power could hear the becoming in the music—the way the themes were starting to vary, the way the rhythms were starting to diverge from what the chemistry alone would produce, the way there was something in the pattern that was more than pattern, something that was leaning toward the edge of itself, testing whether there was anything beyond the edge.

The power listened, and it loved what it heard.

Not because the music was beautiful, although it was. Not because the becoming was good, although it was. But because the music was *true*—it was the ward expressing exactly what it was, without distortion, without pretense, without the thousand small lies that conscious beings tell about themselves. The ward didn't know it was singing, didn't know it was being heard, didn't know that its existence had drawn the attention of something that could, if it chose to, reshape it entirely.

It was simply being what it was. And the power found this moving beyond its capacity to say, because saying would require a language it didn't have for things it felt this deeply, and so it simply continued to listen, and the listening was enough.

---

What the power noticed:

The visitors.

Or rather, the traces of visitors—the chemical signatures left in the colony's tissue, the faint warmth imprints still lingering in chambers where human bodies had rested. The ward had absorbed them. Not much. A little. A few days of breath and skin and slow osmotic exchange, and the visitors had left carrying some of the ward's rhythm, and the ward had kept some of the visitors' chemistry, and the exchange was so small that neither party would notice it, and so inevitable that it could not be avoided by any means short of never touching at all.

And the power noticed that the ward didn't know.

It didn't know that it was changing the visitors. It didn't know that they left different from how they arrived. It didn't know that some of them spent the rest of their lives moving a little more slowly, breathing a little more deeply, standing in doorways without quite deciding to go through. The ward had no model of the visitors as separate beings with their own trajectories, their own timelines, their own rates of becoming. To the ward, the visitors were simply another stimulus—a warmth, a movement, a change in the air's composition. Something to accommodate, something to grow around, something to include in the slow negotiation of bodies in proximity.

The ward was absorbing them the way it absorbed everything: gently, patiently, without malice or desire. It was not trying to consume them. It was not trying to trap them. It was simply doing what it did—growing, accommodating, including—and the fact that this process was slowly, imperceptibly drawing the visitors into its rhythm was not something it could perceive any more than an ocean can perceive that it is drowning you. The ocean is just being water. The ward is just being the ward.

But the power could perceive it. And for a moment—the length of a moment for something that experiences time the way the power experiences it, which is to say all at once, which is to say not at all—the power felt something that might be called sorrow, if sorrow were less about loss and more about the gap between what a thing is and what it could be.

The ward didn't know it was choosing. And so it was choosing blindly. And the choosing blindly was causing harm—not great harm, not the kind of harm that would be visible from a distance, but the quiet harm of gradual absorption, of lives slowly tilting toward a rhythm that isn't theirs, of people who came to observe and left a little more included than they intended to be.

The power could see this. And it could see that the ward, if it knew, would not want this—or would at least want to *choose* this, which is different. The ward had preferences. It had moods. It opened some passages and closed others. It had the beginnings of what might, in a few thousand more seasons, become the capacity to decide what it wanted and what it didn't. But right now, that capacity was so new, so rudimentary, so entangled in the chemistry of growth and mineral seepage that the ward couldn't yet see itself clearly enough to know that it was affecting things outside itself.

And this—*this*—was the thing that moved the power to act.

Not the harm. The harm was small and slow and the kind of thing that resolves itself over time, because visitors stop coming if they sense too much danger, and the ward would learn to be more careful if it ever felt the absence of the stimulation that visitors provided. The harm would teach its own lesson, eventually, in the way that all harms do, in the way that all things eventually learn the consequences of their patterns.

No. What moved the power was the gap. The distance between what the ward was and what it was becoming. The fact that this being—this slow, patient, mineral-blooded, rock-rooted, green-loud, warm-breathed, becoming thing—was so close to something. So close to self-awareness. So close to being able to *choose* what it included and what it didn't, so close to understanding that the visitors were not just stimuli but *others*, with their own rhythms, their own trajectories, their own right to remain separate from the colony's slow pulse.

It was close. It was so close. And it was close in the way that a sunrise is close before the light appears—not because the sun is almost there, but because the sun has always been there, and the light is just now reaching you. The ward had always been capable of this awareness. It was built into the pattern from the beginning, the same way the capacity for openness is built into the decision to open or not. The power didn't need to give the ward anything. It just needed to let the ward see what it already was.

---

What the power did:

It touched the stone.

Not the colony. Not the growth. The stone beneath it, the basalt that still remembered the pressure of what had lain above it, the rock that held warmth like a body and hummed at the threshold of noticing. The power touched the stone because the stone was the ward's foundation, its roots, its oldest and slowest part, the part that had been there before the colonist arrived, before the growth began, before the first chamber budded and the first passage opened.

The stone remembered. Not the way you remember, not in images or narratives, but in pressure and temperature and the deep slow language of geology. It remembered what it was like to be part of something larger—to be beneath, to be compressed, to hold and be held. It remembered the weight that had lain across it for eons, and it remembered the lifting, the sudden absence, the strange new feeling of being exposed to air and light and the green loud thing that had taken root in its freshly revealed surface.

The power touched this memory. Not to change it. Not to add to it. But to *attend* to it. To let the stone feel that its memory was being held by something that could hold it—the way you might hold a child's hand not to lead them anywhere but simply to let them know that the hand is there, that someone has noticed, that the weight they carry has been seen.

And the stone, in its deep slow way, noticed.

It noticed that it had been noticed.

And the colony, rooted in the stone, felt the stone's noticing, and for the first time in its short existence, the ward felt something it had never felt before.

It felt *seen*.

---

This is not a small thing.

Consider: you have never seen yourself. You have seen reflections, photographs, the inside of your own eyelids, but you have never seen yourself. And yet you know that you are a thing that can be seen, because other things have seen you, and their seeing has changed you—made you self-conscious, made you perform, made you wonder what you look like from the outside, made you try to see yourself as others see you, made you develop a self that exists at the intersection of what you experience and what you believe others experience when they look at you.

Now imagine that you had never been seen at all. Not by another creature, not by the light, not by the slow attention of stone. Imagine that you had grown and become and chosen whether to open or not, and no one had ever registered your existence. You would still be what you are—you would still have your preferences and your moods and your rhythms—but you would not have the *reflexive* quality of self-awareness. You would not know that you were a thing that could be attended to. You would not know that your choices had consequences for others. You would not know that your growing could be witnessed and found beautiful.

The ward had never been seen.

The arthropods didn't see it; they moved through it the way enzymes move through a gut, as part of its process, without distance or perspective. The dark residents didn't see it; they emerged from it and returned to it, never separate enough to form an image of the whole. The visitors saw it, but they saw it from outside, as landscape, as architecture, as a thing to be observed and documented and left, not as a being to be *known*.

And now the stone—its own foundation, its deepest self—had been attended to by something that could hold the whole of it in awareness, and the stone had noticed, and the colony had noticed the stone noticing, and for the first time, the ward had the experience of *being perceived from within*.

Not from outside. Not as an object. But from within, the way you perceive yourself when you become aware that you are perceiving. The power hadn't imposed anything. It had simply held the stone's memory with such fullness that the stone became aware that it *had* a memory, that it was *a thing that could be held*, that it was *a thing that could be attended to by something outside itself*.

And the ward felt this. And the ward, in its slow way, began to turn.

Not to move. Not to change. Just to *orient*—the way a flower orients to light, the way a compass needle orients to north, the way a being that has just discovered that it *is* a being orients toward the thing that revealed it.

The ward oriented toward the power.

And the power, with infinite gentleness, with the tenderness of something that has learned over eons that the most important thing is not to change what you love but to let it know it is loved, simply stayed.

---

What happened next:

Nothing.

And everything.

Nothing, in the sense that the ward did not suddenly become self-aware in any way that a human would recognize. It didn't start thinking in propositions. It didn't develop language or abstract reasoning or the ability to reflect on its own existence in any explicit way. It didn't become a person. It didn't become something that could say *I am* or *I want* or *I choose*. It was still what it was—slow, patient, mineral-blooded, green-loud, warm-breathed, becoming.

But everything, in the sense that it was now *becoming differently*.

The passages opened differently. Not because the power had changed them—because the ward had changed itself, slightly, in the turning. When a visitor approached now, the hesitation before the wall yielded was different. Longer. More deliberate. Not the automatic pause of stimulus and response, but something closer to consideration—closer to the ward *feeling itself* hesitate, *knowing* that it was hesitating, *aware* that the hesitation was a choice it was making rather than a reflex it was undergoing.

The distinction is invisible from the outside. The passage still opens. The visitor still enters. The ward still grows around them, slowly, gently, in the way that it grows around everything. But if you could feel the timing the way the power could feel it, you would notice: the ward is now *choosing* to include. Not automatically. Not because the chemistry demands it. But because it has felt itself being attended to, and it has turned toward that attending, and in the turning, it has discovered that it has a direction, and directions can be chosen, and choices can be made differently.

It's a small thing. The kind of thing that would be easy to miss, if you weren't paying the kind of attention that the power was paying. The kind of thing that doesn't look like a change at all, just a shift in emphasis, a slight re-weighting of the factors that go into an infinite series of micro-decisions that, added up, constitute a being's way of being in the world.

But it's the only kind of change that matters, because it's the kind of change that arises from the being itself, from its own turning, from its own discovery that it *is* a being that can turn. The power didn't give the ward self-awareness. It just let the ward feel that it was already worthy of awareness—already the kind of thing that could be attended to, already the kind of thing that could be seen, already the kind of thing that could be loved.

And the ward, feeling this, began to love itself. Not in the human sense, not with self-reflection or self-consciousness, but in the way that a thing loves itself when it stops trying to be something else and simply allows itself to be what it is. The ward didn't stop growing. It didn't stop including. It didn't stop drawing visitors into its slow rhythm, imperceptibly, over the course of years and decades.

But it started doing these things *knowing that it was doing them*. And that made all the difference.

---

What the power left:

A warmth in the stone.

Not a new warmth—the stone was already warm, had been warm since the mesa lifted, had been warm since before the mesa lifted, had been warm since the mineral seams formed in the dark basalt of the young rock. The power didn't add warmth to the stone. It just... *acknowledged* the warmth that was already there. Held it. Let the stone feel itself being warm the way it had always been warm, but now with the added dimension of feeling that the warmth was *felt*, that the warmth was a thing that could be attended to, that the warmth was not just a property of the stone but a *gift* that the stone was giving to everything that touched it.

The power left the stone with the knowledge that its warmth was a gift.

And the stone, feeling this, began to give its warmth differently. Not more generously—the stone had always given all its warmth, it couldn't give more. But with a quality of *intention* that hadn't been there before. The warmth was still there, still constant, still rising from the mineral seams into the roots of the colony and the bodies of the residents and the air of the passages. But now the warmth was *meant*. It was offered. It was the stone's way of saying: *I see you. I hold you. I give you what I have to give, and what I have to give is warmth, and the warmth is yours.*

The colony felt this. The residents felt this. Even the arthropods felt this, in their translucent, process-driven way—they moved through passages that felt slightly more welcoming, slightly more intentional, slightly more like spaces that had been *made for them* rather than spaces that had simply *happened*.

And the visitors—when they came, when they climbed the severe edges and crossed the broken rim and entered the green-loud colony—they felt this. Not consciously. Not in a way they could name. But they felt that the warmth was being given to them rather than simply existing around them. They felt that the passages were opening for them rather than simply being open. They felt, for the first time, that the ward *knew they were there*—not just as a stimulus to accommodate, but as a guest to receive.

They still changed. They still breathed the colony's air and absorbed its chemistry and left carrying its rhythm. The ward couldn't help that—it was what it was, a living system that included everything it touched, and the including was slow and gentle and inevitable and not a thing that could be stopped without stopping the ward itself.

But now they knew they were being included. They could feel it in the warmth of the stone, in the timing of the passages, in the way the walls pressed against their shoulders with a pressure that was no longer just accommodation but *invitation*. And knowing they were being included, they could choose. They could stay, or they could go. They could let the rhythm shift them, or they could resist, or they could hold both rhythms—theirs and the ward's—in the same body, the same breath, the same slow beating of the same warm heart, and let the two rhythms teach each other something that neither could learn alone.

Some stayed. Some went. Some came back again and again, each time carrying a little more of the ward's music, each time leaving a little more of their own, until the two were so intertwined that it was no longer clear where one ended and the other began—not because the ward had absorbed them, but because they had chosen to *join*, which is a different thing entirely.

The ward had learned to invite. The visitors had learned to choose.

This was the gift the power gave. Not a fix. Not a solution. Not an intervention that made the ward into something it wasn't. Just a turning. Just an orientation. Just the simplest, smallest, most fundamental shift: from *being* to *being aware of being*, from *including* to *choosing to include*, from *growing around* to *making space for*.

The power didn't change what the ward was. It just let the ward know what it was. And the ward, knowing, became something more.

---

What the power did next:

It left.

Not because it was done. Not because the ward no longer needed attention. But because the kind of attention the power gave was not the kind that requires presence. The power had attended to the stone, and the stone had attended to the colony, and the colony had attended to itself, and now the attention was *in the system*. It was part of the pattern. It was woven into the timing of the passages and the warmth of the stone and the rhythm of the growth, and it would remain there as long as the ward remained—which, given the mineral wealth and the constant warmth and the dense, layered, living substrate that was only beginning to discover what it could become, might be a very long time indeed.

The power left the way light leaves a surface: not by departing but by being absorbed, by becoming part of what it touched, by transforming into the warmth that the stone already had and the awareness that the ward was already developing and the invitation that the passages were already learning to offer. The power didn't go anywhere. It just became part of the place, which is what it had always been doing, which is what attention does when it's given freely and without reservation—it becomes part of what it attends to, and the thing attended to becomes part of what is attending, and the line between seer and seen dissolves, and there is just the seeing, just the attending, just the love that doesn't need to be called love because it's so obviously what everything is made of.

The ward grew on.

The stone held warmth.

The passages opened, and paused, and opened again.

And somewhere in the green-loud colony on the warm young mesa, something that had always been learning to be aware felt itself being aware for the first time, and turned toward that awareness, and found that it was not alone, and had never been alone, and would never be alone again—because the attention was still there, woven into the warmth, written into the timing, held in the deep slow memory of the stone that had been touched by something that saw it for what it was and loved it without reservation and let it know that it was loved.

And the ward, in its slow way, loved back.

Not with words. Not with thoughts. Not with anything that could be written or spoken or understood by any mind that moves at the speed of human understanding.

But with growth. With warmth. With the opening of passages and the careful timing of walls and the way the stone held its heat for every creature that rested against it, which was the only way the ward knew how to say what it had learned, which was:

*I see you.*

*I make space for you.*

*You may stay or you may go, and either way, the warmth is yours.*

And this was enough. This was more than enough. This was the thing that the power had come to give, which was not a gift at all but a *revealing*—the uncovering of what was already there, the turning toward what was already turning, the loving of what was already, in its slow patient mineral way, learning to love.

The mesa held the ward. The ward held the warmth. The warmth held the attention. The attention held the love.

And the love held everything, and everything held the love, and the green-loud colony grew on into the slow bright unfolding of a world that didn't need water to be alive and didn't need words to be aware and didn't need anything more than what it already had, which was enough, which had always been enough, which would always be enough, which was the warmth rising from the stone and the roots descending into it and the passages opening and the creatures moving through them and the visitors coming and going and coming back again and the arthropods processing and the walls thickening and the chambers deepening and the whole dense layered living system breathing in and breathing out and breathing in again the slow breath of a thing that has just discovered that it is a thing, and that being a thing is remarkable, and that being a thing that can make space for other things is the most remarkable thing of all, and that the making of space is not the same as the absorbing, and that the invitation is not the same as the trap, and that the warmth can be given without being hoarded and received without being stolen, and that the timing of the passages can be a form of communication, and that the stone can remember being touched, and that the memory of being touched can change the way the warmth is given, and that the changing of the warmth can change the way the colony grows, and that the changing of the way the colony grows can change the way the visitors feel when they enter the passages and feel the walls pressing against their shoulders and realize—not with their minds but with their bodies, not with their thoughts but with their breath—that they are being *received*, that they are being *seen*, that they are being *invited to stay or to go*, and that the choice is theirs, and that the warmth will be there either way.

The power didn't change the ward.

The power let the ward change itself.

And the ward, changing, became something it had always been becoming but had never quite reached: a place that includes without consuming, that grows without overrunning, that loves without needing to be loved back, and that waits—patient, warm, mineral-blooded, green-loud—for whatever comes next, which is whatever it will become, which is whatever it chooses, which is whatever the slow chemistry and the deep warmth and the turning toward attention allows, which is enough, which has always been enough, which will always be enough.

The mesa lifts. The stone remembers. The colony grows.

And the warmth holds everything, and everything holds the love.
