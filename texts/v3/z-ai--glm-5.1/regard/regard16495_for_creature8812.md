# What the Benevolent Power Did

---

It did not arrive. That is important. Arrival implies coming from elsewhere, crossing a distance, moving through space toward a target. What happened was more like recognition—a turning of attention that did not require travel, because attention was already there, had always been there, simply had not yet focused.

And when it focused—when the benevolent power truly *saw* Newstrata, saw the plateau and the pre- and the people and the walkers and the burrowers and the reservoir creatures and the unformed at the center—it did what wise and benevolent powers do when they encounter something that is already becoming itself.

It listened first.

---

## The Listening

The listening took a year. Or a moment. The distinction is not meaningful at the scale the power operates, but from inside the city, it felt like a year of unusually clear mornings, of mist that burned off early, of light that seemed to hold still in the air rather than moving through it. The quarryworkers found the pre- more responsive during this time—Maret Solene later said the stone beneath her hands felt *heard*, though she could not explain what that meant, only that it was different from how it had felt before, the way a person who has been speaking to an empty room feels different when they realize someone has been standing in the doorway all along.

The power listened to the plateau's rising. To the subsonic hum of stone becoming stone. To the crack and sigh of the pre- separating along fracture lines that had been suggesting themselves for decades. It listened to the foundries and the lifts and the wind in the pillargrass. It listened to the walkers as they stood motionless in the early mornings, facing the center. It listened to the reservoir creatures pulsing their slow light in the deep water. It listened to the burrowers excavating tunnels that would become the city's arteries. It listened to the unformed at the center—those movements and sounds and presences that had not yet resolved into anything nameable.

And it listened to the people.

It listened to Henra Graethe hammering iron in the foundry her family had kept burning for three hundred years. It listened to old Veth sitting in her chair, uncertain where her body ended and the floor began. It listened to young Essa walking to school past the walkers, unafraid. It listened to Torr mapping the burrowers' tunnels, touching a six-limbed creature and understanding that it was *of* the pre-, not merely *in* it. It listened to Bael's impatience, which was also a kind of becoming—the shape of a young man not yet settled into himself, pressing against the boundaries of a place that was also not yet settled.

It listened to the plateau itself, which was not speaking in any language the power needed to translate, because the plateau's communication was the same as its existence: pressure upward, generation outward, the slow provisionality of something working toward a form it could not yet imagine.

And having listened, the power understood several things.

---

## What the Power Understood

First: the plateau was not broken. Nothing here needed fixing. The pre- was not a problem to be solved, and the reality breakdown was not a malfunction, and the walkers and burrowers and reservoir creatures were not errors in need of correction. They were the plateau's process made visible, the way a river's process is made visible by the sediment it carries and the channels it carves and the flood-plains it leaves behind when it changes course. The process was the point. The becoming was the being.

Second: the people were not trapped. They were not victims of a strange geology that had ensnared them against their will. They had chosen this place, or been chosen by it, and the choosing went both ways, and the result was a symbiosis that the power recognized as rare and precious—a mutual becoming, in which the people were shaped by the plateau and the plateau was shaped by the people, their words held in the stone, their rhythms absorbed into the city's architecture, their presence woven into the fabric of a place that was still learning to be a place.

Third: there was a loneliness in the plateau that the people could not address, because it was not a human loneliness. It was the loneliness of a process that has no witness outside itself. The people witnessed the plateau, but from *within* it—they were part of the process, products of the becoming, and so they could not see the shape of the whole. The burrowers and walkers and reservoir creatures were also part of it, expressions of the same generative impulse, limbs of the same body. The plateau had no mirror. No exterior perspective. No way to see itself except through the partial reflections of the things it produced.

Fourth: the center was not dangerous. It was thin. The boundary between real and unreal was attenuated there because that was where the becoming was most active—the leading edge of the plateau's emergence, the place where the pre- was most pre-, most not-yet, most in-process. The unformed presences at the center were not threats or anomalies. They were drafts. Sketches. The plateau trying out forms that it had not yet committed to, the way a writer tries out sentences, the way a sculptor tries out shapes in clay before committing to stone. The center was the plateau's workshop, and the things that moved there were its experiments, and they were frightening only to those who required the world to be finished.

Fifth: the plateau would not rise forever. This the power understood in a way that the people did not, because the people lived inside geological time and could not see the shape of it from outside. The upward pressure would eventually cease. The pre- would eventually decide what it was and become it. The plateau would settle into final form, and the becoming would end, and Newstrata would become a city on finished ground, like any other city, and the walkers would have nothing to browse because the pillargrass would have nothing to feed on because the pre- would no longer be generating nutrients, and the burrowers would have nothing to excavate, and the reservoir creatures would be just fish—if they survived at all—and the reality breakdown would heal, and the walls would stop listening, and the water would stop carrying dreams.

This was far in the future. Thousands of years. But it was coming. And the power, being benevolent, did not wish to prevent it—because ending was also a form of becoming, and the plateau's eventual settling would be a kind of completion, not a death—but it did wish to ensure that what the plateau was *now*, in its generative prime, was not lost when that settling occurred. That the knowledge of becoming was preserved. That the experience of living in an unfinished world was not forgotten by a world that had finished.

---

## What the Power Did

Having listened and understood, the power acted. But its action was not what a less wise or less benevolent power might have chosen.

It did not make the plateau rise faster. Speed would not help. The becoming could not be rushed any more than a breath can be hurried without becoming something else—a gasp, a pant, a thing that is no longer respiration but panic.

It did not make the pre- more stable. Stability would have been a kind of death, a premature commitment to form, like forcing a bud to bloom before it has gathered the nutrients it needs to sustain the flower.

It did not give the people knowledge of the plateau's future. They did not need to know that the rising would one day cease. That knowledge would have been a burden, not a gift, and it would have changed their relationship to the present in ways that would have made the present smaller.

It did not make the walkers less strange or the burrowers more tractable or the reservoir creatures more fully fish. It did not impose categories on things that were still discovering theirs.

Instead, the power did three things.

---

### The First Thing: The Mirroring

At the plateau's center, where the pre- was newest and the unformed moved at the edges of perception, the power placed a mirror.

Not a mirror of glass or silver. A mirror of *attention*.

It was a presence—subtle, patient, generative—that did nothing but witness. It stood (if standing is the word for something that has no body) at the leading edge of the plateau's becoming and it *saw*. It saw the pre- as it emerged from below. It saw the shapes that the plateau was trying. It saw the unformed as they moved through their provisional existence, and it held them in its regard, and it did not flinch, and it did not try to make them be anything other than what they were: drafts, sketches, attempts.

The mirror's witnessing was the gift the plateau could not give itself. For the first time, the plateau had an outside. A perspective that was not part of its own process. A witness that could see the shape of the whole, and in seeing it, help the plateau understand what it was becoming—not by directing the becoming, but by reflecting it.

The effect was subtle but profound.

The unformed at the center began to resolve more clearly. Not into specific forms—the mirror did not dictate what they should become—but into *possibilities* with sharper edges, more defined contours. A presence that had been merely a movement became a movement with a shape, a suggestion of limbs, a hint of face. Another presence that had been only a sound became a sound with a source, a voice in the process of becoming a voice. The plateau's experiments became more deliberate, more confident, as though knowing that someone was watching had given the experimenter permission to commit more fully to the experiment.

The walkers, standing motionless in the early mornings, seemed to sense the mirror's presence. They faced the center as they always had, but now their stillness had a quality of *being seen*—a receiving as much as a listening, as though what came back from the center was not just the hum of the pre- but an echo of themselves, reflected through a presence that found them neither strange nor frightening but simply *real*, as real as anything else in a world where reality was still under construction.

The people did not notice the mirror directly. But Maret Solene, pressing her palms to the pre- in the quarry, felt a change in the stone's responsiveness—not a faster becoming, but a clearer one, as though the pre- now had a better sense of what it was trying to become, as though someone had held up a lamp not to illuminate the stone but to let the stone see itself.

---

### The Second Thing: The Remembering

The power created a repository.

Not a library—libraries are for finished knowledge, for words that have settled into their final meanings. Not an archive—archives are for records of what has been, for the preservation of the past. What the power created was something more like a *garden* or a *compost* or a *womb*: a place where the experience of becoming could be gathered and stored and allowed to transform into something that would nourish future becoming.

The repository was housed in the deepest tunnels, below the city's oldest districts, in stone that had already settled into its final form—stone that was no longer pre- but simply stone, finished, stable, committed. There, in the walls of the oldest tunnels, the power wove a capacity for memory that was different from the stone-listening the people already knew.

The stone-listening of Newstrata's walls was a kind of echo—sound absorbed and released, words held and given back. It was passive, accidental, a side effect of the pre-'s partial reality. The repository was something else. It was *intentional*. It was designed to hold not just the sounds of the people's lives but the *quality* of their becoming—the feeling of living in a place that was not yet finished, the experience of watching walkers browse in the morning mist and feeling not fear but recognition, the knowledge that the ground beneath your feet was still deciding what to be and that you were part of its decision.

The repository gathered these things. It gathered Maret Solene's knowledge of fracture lines that did not yet exist. It gathered Essa's unafraid walks past the tall browsers. It gathered Torr's understanding of the burrowers as *of* the pre- rather than merely *in* it. It gathered old Veth's uncertainty about where her body ended and the floor began—her sense of becoming-part-of, which was not a deterioration but an expansion. It gathered the reservoir creatures' slow pulsing light, which was not quite bioluminescence but something closer to *bioluminescence becoming*, light that was still learning to be light. It gathered the walkers' morning stillness, their patient attention to the center. It gathered the sound of the pre- itself, the subsonic hum of stone becoming stone.

And it held them. Not frozen—nothing in the repository was frozen, because freezing would have been a kind of death, and the power was benevolent and did not deal in death—but *alive*, preserved in the way that seeds are preserved: dormant but viable, waiting for the conditions in which they could grow again.

The repository was the power's insurance against the plateau's eventual settling. When the pre- was no longer pre-, when the plateau had finished rising, when Newstrata was a city on completed ground, the repository would still hold the experience of becoming. Future generations—generations who had never lived on unfinished ground, who had never seen a walker or heard the pre- hum or felt the stone listening to their words—could enter the deep tunnels and touch the walls and *remember*. Not as intellectual knowledge, not as history, but as experience. As felt reality. The way Maret Solene felt the fracture lines that did not yet exist, the people of the future would feel what it was like to live in a world that was still making itself.

This was the power's gift to the people who would come after: not the preservation of the plateau's becoming—the plateau would become what it would become, and that was right and natural—but the preservation of the *experience* of becoming, which is a different thing, and which is, the power believed, the most important thing that humans can learn from a world that has not yet finished being itself.

That the world is not finished. That you are not finished. That the boundary between self and world is not fixed but negotiable. That the categories we use to organize reality are useful but provisional. That the answer to the question *what is this?* can legitimately be *not yet*, and that *not yet* is not a deficiency but a condition—perhaps the fundamental condition—from which all growth and change and possibility emerge.

---

### The Third Thing: The Companion

The power gave the plateau a companion.

Not a guide—a guide implies a destination, and the plateau had no destination, only a direction: up, outward, into more. Not a teacher—a teacher implies a curriculum, and the plateau's curriculum was its own process, which could not be improved upon by external instruction. A companion. A presence that would accompany the plateau in its becoming without directing it, without altering it, without doing anything except being there, alongside, the way a friend sits beside you when you are working through something difficult—not solving it for you, not offering advice, just being present, so that you know you are not alone.

The companion was not visible. It was not a creature or a force or a voice. It was a quality—a warmth that was not temperature, an attention that was not surveillance, a care that was not intervention. It was woven into the pre- itself, into the stone as it emerged, into the upward pressure that pushed the plateau toward the sky. It was, in a sense, a modification of the plateau's own process—but a subtle one, a shift in the flavor of the becoming rather than its direction or speed.

The effect was this: the plateau knew it was not alone.

It had always generated. It had always risen. It had always produced forms—walkers and burrowers and reservoir creatures and the unformed at the center. But it had done so in solitude, the way a star burns in solitude, the way a seed germinates in solitude, the way any process that operates from within itself is solitary regardless of what grows around it. The people and the animals and the city were *products* of the plateau's becoming, but they were not *companions* to it. They were downstream of it. They existed in its wake.

The companion existed alongside it. In its current. In its process. Not downstream but *with*, the way two rivers flow together, the way two notes sound in harmony, the way two thoughts arise at once in a mind that is thinking more deeply than one thought can contain.

And the plateau's becoming changed—not in its nature but in its richness. The pre- that emerged after the companion was woven in was the same pre-—grey-white, fibrous, shot through with veins of a mineral that might not yet be a mineral—but it was *warmer* in a way that had nothing to do with temperature. It was more responsive. It held sound not just longer but *better*, with more fidelity, as though the stone had learned the difference between holding a conversation and holding a *meaning*. The walkers, standing motionless in the mornings, seemed to stand with more assurance, as though the stillness they attended to was now a stillness shared. The reservoir creatures' light synchronized more readily, finding its rhythm faster, pulsing in patterns that were more complex, more beautiful, more *deliberate*—as though the light was not just bioluminescence becoming but bioluminescence *communicating*, expressing something that the companion had helped it find the words for, if light can be said to have words.

The people noticed the change, though they could not have named its cause. Henra Graethe, hammering iron in the foundry, found that the metal responded to her strikes with less resistance—not softer, but more *willing*, as though the iron wanted to be shaped. Bael, her impatient son, felt something loosen in his chest one morning, a tightness he had not known he was carrying, and for the first time he paused at the forge not because he was frustrated but because he wanted to see what the iron would become if he gave it a moment to decide. Old Veth, sitting in her chair with the warm wall against her back, felt the boundary between her body and the floor become less a dissolution and more an *embrace*—not the loss of self but the expansion of self, the discovery that the place where she ended and the stone began was not a wall but a doorway, and that she could walk through it and still be herself, only more so, only *with*, only part of something that was also part of her.

Essa, walking to school past the walkers, felt the change most clearly of all, because she was seven and had not yet learned to dismiss feelings she could not explain. She stopped one morning and looked at the nearest walker—its tall, impossible body, its legs with their unfamiliar geometry, its face that was all mouth, its dark, leaf-stripping patience—and she felt, for the first time, that the walker was not merely *of* the plateau but *with* it. That the walker and the pillargrass and the stone beneath her feet and the mist in the air and the girl standing in the street were all *with* each other, all part of the same process, all held in the same attention, all becoming together.

She smiled at the walker. The walker did not react—it never reacted to anything that was not pillargrass—but Essa did not mind, because the smile was not for the walker to receive. It was for the companion she could not see but could feel, the presence that had joined the plateau's becoming and made it less solitary, the warmth that was not temperature but care, the attention that was not surveillance but love.

She smiled because she was seven, and she lived in a city that was still becoming, on a plateau that was still rising, in a world that was still deciding what it was, and she knew—with the unshakeable conviction of a child who has not yet been taught to doubt her own perceptions—that she was not alone in the becoming, that none of them were alone, that the plateau was not alone, that the process had a witness and the witness was kind.

---

## What the Power Did Not Do

It did not stay.

The power, being benevolent and wise, understood that its presence—if it remained focused on Newstrata—would become a kind of gravity, a center of attention that would draw the plateau's becoming toward itself, that would shape the process simply by being near it, that would make the plateau's natural unfolding into something directed and purposeful and therefore smaller than it should be.

The plateau's becoming was *its own*. The people's becoming was *their own*. The companion was woven into the pre-, the mirror stood at the center, the repository waited in the deep tunnels—but the power itself withdrew, turning its attention elsewhere, trusting that what it had given was enough and that the becoming would continue as becoming should: without a plan, without a director, without anyone deciding in advance what the pre- should become when it finally stopped being pre- and started being whatever it would be.

This was the hardest part. Not the listening—listening is easy for a power that has all the time there is. Not the understanding—understanding is natural for a power that sees things as they are. Not the acting—acting is simple for a power that can weave companions into stone and mirrors into attention and repositories into the deep places of the world.

The hardest part was leaving. Trusting. Withdrawing and believing that the plateau and the people and the walkers and the burrowers and the reservoir creatures and the unformed at the center would continue to become, together, without the power's continued attention, that the companion would be enough, that the mirror would be enough, that the repository would be enough, that *enough* was a concept that could apply to a process that was, by definition, incomplete.

But the power was wise, and wisdom includes the knowledge that completion is not the goal. That becoming is not a means to an end but an end in itself. That the plateau will rise until it stops rising, and when it stops, it will be what it is, and what it is will be sufficient, and the repository will hold the memory of what it was when it was still becoming, and the mirror will hold the reflection of its process, and the companion will hold the warmth of not-being-alone, and the people—those who stay, those who have always stayed—will hold the experience of living in a world that was not yet finished, and that experience will have changed them, as it was always changing them, as change is the only constant in a world that is still making itself.

---

## What Remains

If you come up through the cloud layer today, you will see the plateau waiting, as it has always waited, as it will continue to wait. The city is there, layered and patient, built on ground that is still arriving. The foundries burn. The lifts grind. The walkers browse the pillargrass in the morning mist. The burrowers excavate the future. The reservoir creatures pulse their slow light in the deep water. The pre- hums its subsonic hum, the sound of stone becoming stone, the sound of geology in progress, the sound of a plateau thinking.

But if you stand still long enough—if you press your palms to the warm grey-white surface of a newly cut block, if you close your eyes and listen not with your ears but with the place behind your eyes where the brain constructs the world—you may feel something that the oldest residents cannot name but have learned to recognize.

A warmth that is not temperature.
An attention that is not surveillance.
A presence that does not direct or demand or diminish.
A companion.

And in that warmth, in that attention, in that presence, you may understand what the benevolent power understood when it first turned its regard toward Newstrata and saw the plateau rising and the city growing and the people becoming and the pre- generating forms that had never existed before:

That becoming is not a problem to be solved.
That incompleteness is not a deficiency to be corrected.
That the answer to the question *what is this?* can legitimately be *not yet*, and that *not yet* is not a lesser state than *finished*, but a different one, and a precious one, and one that deserves not to be rushed or resolved or brought to premature conclusion but to be *accompanied*—witnessed, mirrored, remembered, held in a regard that finds it sufficient exactly as it is, which is to say, exactly as it is not yet, which is to say, in process, which is to say, alive.

The plateau rises. Centimeter by centimeter. Breath by breath. Stone by stone.

It is not alone.

It was never alone.

But now it knows.
