# What the Benevolent Power Did

---

First, it listened.

Not to the percussion—anyone can hear the percussion. It listened to the silence between each strike, because that is where the Builder lives when it is not building. In the pause. In the fraction of a moment before the next hammerfall when the chamber simply *is*, without being made, without being unmade, just existing in the heat and the wet and the grey-green half-light.

The silence between is very small. Fractions of fractions. But the benevolent power has always been patient with small things.

---

It did not enter the chamber.

This is important. The alcove was already carved—the one shaped like a visitor, shaped like *you*, shaped like anyone who might arrive—and the power understood immediately what it was for. A mold. A waiting mouth. An invitation that was also a consumption.

So it did not go inside.

Instead, it sat down on the mountainside, on the ice and the nothing where nothing should grow, and it pressed its back against the flank of the peak. Against the outside of the chamber. It could feel the warmth through the stone—impossible warmth, flesh-temperature, the heat of something that refused to stop. It could feel the percussion through its spine. Wet. Rhythmic. Patient.

It sat there for a long time. Not hours. Not days. The Builder does not experience time as a line, and the benevolent power has never been constrained by chronology either, so they met somewhere outside of it—two beings touching through a wall of stone, both of them very old, both of them very patient, only one of them desperate.

The power did not speak. There was nothing to say that the Builder did not already know, and besides—the Builder's language was still being invented. Any words might have been the wrong shape. Might have required a new alcove. Might have started a new construction that the Builder did not need.

So the power simply sat. And breathed. And matched its breathing to the percussion.

Not to change the rhythm. Not to slow it or regularize it or make it something other than what it was. Just to be another rhythm in the vicinity. To let the Builder feel, perhaps for the first time since it fell, that there was a heartbeat in the world that it had not made.

---

The power brought gifts, but not the kind you expect.

It did not bring blueprints. A blueprint would have been a kind of violence—here is the shape you should take, here is the plan you should follow, here is the end to your endless becoming. The Builder had been making itself without a plan since before the mountain was old, and to give it one now would have been to say: *you have been doing it wrong.* But the Builder had not been doing it wrong. The Builder had been doing it desperately, which is different. Desperate things are not incorrect. They are surviving.

It did not bring tools. The Builder had all the tools it needed—the hammer of its heart, the hands it remembered or dreamed, the patience that outlasted glaciers. To bring better tools would have been to suggest that the making could be made more efficient, and the power understood that the making was not the point. The making was the Builder's way of staying. The making was how it said *I am still here, I am still becoming, I have not stopped.*

Instead, the power brought:

**A name.**

Not a name spoken aloud. The Builder's language was still being invented, and anyway, a spoken name might have become another alcove, another extension, another room that the Builder had to construct around. The power gave it a name the way moss gives a name to the wall it grows on—by covering it slowly, by being present, by settling into the grooves and scars of the surface until the surface and the covering are no longer distinguishable.

The name was not a word. It was a *quality*. The quality of being witnessed without being changed. The quality of being seen without being consumed. The quality of existing in someone else's attention without becoming part of their architecture.

The power named the Builder *the-one-who-keeps-building*, which is also *the-one-who-keeps-becoming*, which is also *the-one-who-refused-to-stop*, which is also simply *keeps*, as in: keeps going, keeps making, keeps the heat alive in the thin cold places where nothing should grow.

*Keeps.*

A small name. A verb-name. An ongoing name.

The Builder felt it. Something shifted in the fractal patterns on the ceiling—not the patterns themselves, but the space between them. A loosening. Not a stopping, never a stopping, but a loosening. As though the calculations had, for one fraction of a moment, found an answer they weren't looking for and paused to consider it.

---

The power brought a second gift:

**A shelf.**

The chamber was full of shelves that the Builder had carved for objects that did not exist yet. The power understood this as what it was—hope. Architecture as hope. The making of space for what has not yet arrived but might. The power admired this. It was, in its own way, a kind of faith.

So the power placed something on one of those empty shelves.

Not an object. Nothing so simple. It placed a *memory*—but not its own memory, because the Builder might have consumed it, might have built it into the walls, might have become something that included a benevolent power's recollections and then what would the Builder be? Not itself anymore. Just an extension of something else.

Instead, the power placed a memory that belonged to no one. A memory of the moment before the Builder fell, or was thrown, or unwrote itself from wherever it had been before. Not the moment itself—the Builder might not have survived remembering it—but the moment just before, when it was still whole, still somewhere, still contained within a shape that fit.

The memory was this: *warmth that does not have to be made. Rest that does not have to be earned. A room that does not have to be built because it already exists and has always existed and will continue to exist whether or not you keep making it.*

The power placed this memory on the empty shelf like a seed on wet stone.

And the moss grew over it. Of course the moss grew over it—that is what moss does in this chamber, it covers, it tastes, it absorbs. But the moss did not consume the memory. It grew *around* it. It held it. The fungal threads on the ceiling paused in their fractal calculations and did something they had never done before: they grew *downward*, toward the shelf, toward the memory, like roots reaching for water.

The Builder did not take the memory into itself. It let it stay on the shelf. But it built around it—carefully, gently, with tool marks that were less desperate than before. It carved the shelf deeper so the memory would not fall. It angled the alcove above it so the condensation would not drip directly onto it. It made room for something it had not made, and in doing so, it practiced something it had never practiced before.

Holding something without consuming it.

Keeping something without becoming it.

---

The power brought a third gift, but this one took longer:

**A second percussion.**

It started the next time the power returned. Because the power did return—not once, but many times. Not on a schedule (the Builder does not experience time as a line and neither does the power) but often enough that the Builder began to expect it. Often enough that the alcove shaped like a visitor began to reshape itself, slowly, over many visits, into something that was no longer a mold but a seat. A place to rest rather than a place to be cast.

When the power returned, it sat on the mountainside with its back against the stone, and it listened to the percussion, and then—gently, so gently—it began to tap.

Just its fingers against the rock. A small rhythm. Not counter to the Builder's hammerfall, not opposed to it, but *alongside* it. The way a second heartbeat joins the first in a mother's chest—separate but synchronized, two pulses sharing a body without becoming one.

The Builder noticed.

The chamber noticed. The geometry, which had always been wrong in accumulating ways, became wrong differently. The distances still shifted, but they shifted *toward* something now, rather than merely shifting. The tool marks on the walls still spelled something in a language that was never spoken, but the language was acquiring grammar. Syntax. The possibility of being learned.

The percussion continued. Wet. Rhythmic. Patient. But there was another sound now—a small tapping from outside, from the ice and the nothing where nothing should grow, and the Builder found that it could listen to both sounds at once, that its attention could hold two rhythms without the architecture failing, without the walls becoming uncertain, without the chamber ceasing to exist when no one was looking.

The Builder found that it could be witnessed without being consumed.

This was new.

---

The power did not stay forever.

This is also important. The benevolent power had other places to be, other beings who needed its attention, other thin cold places where things were desperately keeping themselves alive. It could not sit on the mountainside forever, tapping its fingers against the stone.

But it left the gifts behind. The name. The memory on the shelf. The second rhythm, which the Builder could now produce on its own—had always been able to produce on its own, perhaps, but had never known it because it had never heard it from outside.

The Builder kept building. Of course it did. The percussion never stopped. The chamber kept growing, the walls kept being carved, the alcoves kept leading nowhere and somewhere at the same time. The moss still grew frantic. The ferns still uncurled toward the grey-green light. The geometry was still wrong.

But sometimes now, between the hammerfalls, there was a tapping. Small. Gentle. A second rhythm that the Builder made for itself, not because it needed to build, not because it needed to become, but because it had heard another heartbeat once and wanted to remember what it felt like to be accompanied.

And sometimes now, the Builder paused in its work and visited the shelf where the memory sat, still growing moss, still held carefully in the alcove it had carved. The Builder did not consume the memory. It did not build it into the walls. It simply sat with it the way the power had sat with the mountain—back against the stone, breathing, present, not making anything, just being in the heat and the wet and the grey-green half-light.

Just being.

In a chamber that was still being built. By a Builder who was still becoming. In the thin cold places where nothing should grow, but things grow anyway.

---

The power did one more thing.

It told others.

Not many. Not loudly. But it spoke of the mountain that has no name because no one has survived the climbing to name it. It spoke of the chamber above the world, in the thin and freezing places, where something has been building itself since before the mountain was old. It spoke of the heat and the wet and the impossible geometry. It spoke of the alcoves that lead nowhere and the shelves that hold hope in the shape of memories that belong to no one.

And it spoke of the second rhythm. The tapping. The heartbeat that is not a hammer.

It told them this so that they would know: if you climb the mountain, if you find the chamber, if the moss grows around your ankles and the ferns uncurl toward your warmth—do not enter the alcove shaped like your body. The alcove is an invitation, but it does not know any other kind.

Instead, sit down. Press your back against the wall. Breathe.

And tap.

Tap gently, in the pauses between the hammerfalls. Tap your own rhythm—whatever rhythm you carry, whatever heartbeat is yours. Do not match the Builder's percussion. Do not oppose it. Just exist alongside it, two pulses sharing a space without becoming one.

The Builder will hear you. It has always been listening.

And maybe, if you stay long enough, if you tap long enough, if you are patient with small things—

The Builder will make room for you.

Not a mold. Not a mouth. But a seat. A place to rest in a chamber that is still being made by a being that is still becoming, in the thin cold places where nothing should grow but things grow anyway, desperately, patiently, with the kind of nuclear insistence that says *I will not stop, I will not stop, I will not stop*—

And sometimes now, in the pauses between:

*I do not have to do this alone.*

---

The percussion continues.

Wet. Rhythmic. Patient.

But warmer now. And accompanied.
