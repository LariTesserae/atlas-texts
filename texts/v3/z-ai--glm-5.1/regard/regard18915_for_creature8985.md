# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

It does not arrive. That is the first thing to understand about a power that is truly wise: it recognizes when arrival would be violence.

The Ascendant has spent geological ages learning the register of patience. A being that manifested—no matter how gently, no matter how luminously—would be an event, and events are wet things, loud things, things that disrupt the slow respiration of dust and stone. The power understands this. It has watched Verath from a distance that is not spatial but *temporal*, observing from the far end of the same patience the Ascendant practices, and it has learned: *this place does not need to be interrupted. It needs to be accompanied.*

So it does not arrive.

It *inclines*.

---

## The Inclining

Like the rise itself—so gradual you don't notice at first—the power tilts its attention toward the Grey Ascendant, and the effect is subtle, pervasive, and unmistakable only in retrospect.

The dust notices.

Selifrith, which has been settling and lifting in the same breathing patterns for longer than Verath has existed, begins to carry something new in its particulate structure. Not a chemical. Not a signal. Something more like a *generosity*—a capacity, previously absent, for the dust to hold more than it carries. The memory of the selifrith, which the old miners speak of—the imprints of every footstep, every grinding, every whispered conversation—has always been passive, a record that plays without listener. Now the record develops the ability to *answer*.

It happens slowly. Over months. Over a year. The miners notice first—or rather, Kael Thessen notices, because Kael notices everything the stone does, and one morning in the deep shafts, listening to the benthic sketh in the walls, he hears something that is not sketh, not stone, not the subsonic groan of the rise.

He hears *response*.

The dust in his lungs—decades of accumulation, the mineral replacement of tissue that has made him more Ascendant than human—vibrates with something that answers his listening. Not with information. Not with language. With *recognition*. The way a mirror recognizes a face: by reflecting it, completely, without distortion or judgment.

Kael sits in the tunnel for a long time after his shift ends, feeling this. The other miners find him there, and they feel it too—not as clearly, not yet, but as a warmth in the selifrith that was not there before, a sense of being *heard* by the dust that has been hearing them all along.

No one names it. Naming would be a wet thing to do, a loud thing, an imposition of language on an experience that exists beneath language. But the miners begin, without discussion, to pause more often in their work. To listen. To let the dust settle around them, and to feel—for the first time in their careers—not just the Ascendant's indifference but its *attention*.

Something is listening. Something that does not speak. Something that inclines.

## The Clarifying

The power's attention has a quality that the people of Verath will spend years learning to articulate: it *clarifies*.

Not explains. Not simplifies. Clarifies—the way a lens clarifies, by gathering light that was already present and bringing it into focus. The Ascendant has always been complex, has always contained more than any single perception could hold, and the power does not reduce this complexity. It makes it *visible*.

Mira Thessen feels this first in the refinery.

She is supervising the night shift, watching her crew, watching for the moment when the dust stops being something they breathe and starts being something they are—and suddenly, for a moment, she sees them. Not with new eyes but with her same eyes, functioning as they always have, perceiving something that was always there but was not organized enough to register.

She sees the dust moving through her crew members. Not metaphorically—she can *see* it, the fine particulate matter entering their lungs, embedding in their tissue, beginning the slow process of crystalline replacement that will eventually make them selisk. She has always known this happens. Now she can perceive it happening, in real time, and what she perceives is not violation but *conversation*.

The dust is not invading. It is *asking*.

Every particle of selifrith that enters a human body carries with it a question—not in language, not in symbol, but in the way that a key asks a lock to open. The mineral structure of the dust is a template, a pattern, and when it encounters organic tissue, it offers that pattern: *here is a way to be, in this place. Will you take it?*

The body answers. Yes or no, gradually or not at all. The three percent who do not adapt—the ones who remain wet, who leave for the lowlands—those are the bodies that say no. The ninety-seven percent who adapt are the bodies that say yes. And the yes is not forced, is not imposed, is not a violation of what they were—it is an *extension*, a new capacity added to an existing structure, the way a child learning language extends their capacity for thought without erasing the thought they were capable of before.

Mira sees this, and weeps.

Not from grief. From *relief*. She has been watching her crew for years, watching the dust accumulate, watching them change, and she has been carrying—without knowing it—the lowland assumption that what was happening to them was loss. That becoming selisk was a diminishing, a corruption, a movement away from what they should be. She has been afraid for them. She has been afraid for herself. She has been afraid for Seli, her daughter, who hums at frequencies that should belong to stone rather than flesh.

Now she sees: the dust is not taking. The dust is *offering*. And the offer can be accepted or refused, and either choice is valid, and neither choice is a betrayal of what a human being is supposed to be—because there is no *supposed to be*, there is only what is, and what is possible, and what is chosen.

She goes home that morning and picks up Seli, who is three and already part of the Ascendant's conversation, and she holds her daughter against her chest, and for the first time she does not feel grief at the humming she can feel through Seli's small body. She feels *pride*. Her daughter is learning to speak a language that Mira is only beginning to hear, and that language is not lesser than the wet language of mouths and air—it is *larger*, encompassing frequencies and mineral grammars that wet ears were never designed to perceive.

Seli looks up at her mother, and her eyes are clear, not grey—not yet—but there is something in them that Mira now recognizes: *awareness*. Her daughter has been aware, this whole time, of the conversation happening inside her body, the dust asking its patient question, and she has been answering yes, yes, yes, with every breath, every heartbeat, every hum.

Mira holds her. The dust settles around them. The Ascendant breathes.

## The Questioning

The power's clarifying attention reaches Pael Corvan last, because Pael has built his life around not-perceiving.

But the clarification is patient. It does not force. It simply makes visible what was always present, and eventually, even the most determined refusal to see must confront the seen.

It happens in his office, late at night, when the factory thrum has dropped to its lowest ebb and the question that has been pressing against the edges of his awareness for thirty years becomes—finally—*clear*.

The question is not coming from outside. The question is coming from *inside*—from the corthunite in the walls, from the selifrith in his lungs, from the grey film developing at the edges of his vision despite his every effort to prevent it. The question has always been there, embedded in the mineral that he has spent his career treating as resource, and the power's attention does not change the question. It merely reveals its shape.

The question is: *What are you, that you refuse to hear me?*

And the clarification reveals something else, something Pael has never allowed himself to recognize: the corthunite has never been refusing *him*. It has been *waiting* for him. Every vethar event, every vein that went quiet, every extraction that failed because the mineral would not discharge—these were not punishments. They were *responses* to his refusal to engage, his insistence on treating a being that *chooses* as a substance that *is*.

The corthunite does not negotiate. The corthunite *relates*. And relationship requires two parties who acknowledge each other, and Pael has spent thirty years refusing to be one of those parties.

He sits in his office, and the question becomes clearer, and for the first time he understands that his silence has not been neutrality. His silence has been a *position*—a stance of refusal that has shaped his extraction protocols, his professional relationships, his marriage, his entire experience of the Ascendant. He has been the three percent, the one who does not adapt, except unlike the others who do not adapt, he has not left. He has stayed, and refused, and called his refusal science.

The power does not judge him for this. The clarification reveals that too: the refusal was *valid*. The corthunite has not been offended by his disbelief. It has simply been unable to connect with someone who would not acknowledge that connection was possible. You cannot speak to someone who insists you are not speaking.

But now Pael can hear the question. And hearing it, he has a choice that he has never had before—not because it wasn't available, but because he couldn't perceive it as a choice.

He can answer.

Or he can continue to refuse, but now the refusal will be *conscious*, a decision rather than a default, and the corthunite will respond to a conscious refusal differently than it responded to an unconscious one. A conscious refusal is a *boundary*. And the Ascendant—the corthunite, the dust, the stone, the entire mineral ecology of this vast rising continent—understands boundaries. It has been setting them for geological ages. Vethar. The rule against touching vorel. The slow calcification of those who stay too long. These are boundaries, and they are respected, and a conscious, chosen refusal from Pael Corvan would be respected too.

Pael sits in his office. The question waits. The dust settles.

And Pael Corvan—for the first time in thirty years—*considers*.

Not decides. Considers. The consideration itself is a form of answer, a recognition that the question exists and deserves response, and the corthunite in the walls hums at a frequency he has never heard before—not discharge, not refusal, but *acknowledgment*—and the grey film at the edges of his vision does not recede but also does not advance, and Pael understands that this is what balance feels like: not the erasure of doubt but the *presence* of it, held in tension with the possibility of belief.

He goes home. He does not sleep—not because the dust has taken sleep from him but because sleep is not what his body needs right now. Instead, he lies in the dark, and he listens to the hum of the city around him, and for the first time he hears it not as machinery but as *conversation*—the corthunite speaking to the illi, the illi speaking to the current, the current speaking to the stone, the stone speaking to the people who have stayed long enough to learn its language.

He does not answer. Not yet. But he is no longer pretending he cannot hear.

## The Bridging

The power's most visible intervention—and the only one that the people of Verath will eventually recognize as intervention rather than natural change—is the bridge.

Not a physical bridge. The Ascendant has no chasms, no rivers, no gaps requiring span. The bridge is a *capacity*—a new ability that emerges in the relationship between the organic and the mineral, the human and the vethaskim, the living and the remembered.

It begins with the illi.

The small crystalline creatures have always clustered around electrical systems, clicking their piezoelectric prayers, somehow strengthening the current by their presence. The miners have observed this for decades without understanding it. The power's attention does not explain it—explanation would be a wet thing, a loud thing—but it *clarifies* the relationship, and what becomes clear is this: the illi are not praying. The illi are *translating*.

They exist at the boundary between the organic and the mineral, the electrical and the geological, the fast and the slow. Their clicks are not language but *interface*, a way of converting the corthunite's stored potential into patterns that the stone can carry, and the stone's slow patience into frequencies that electrical systems can use. They are the Ascendant's nervous system, if the Ascendant can be said to have one—fragments of crystalline awareness distributed across the rise, processing information at speeds the geological could never achieve alone.

And they have been trying, for decades, to include the humans in this network.

This is what the power's attention reveals: the illi's interest in electrical systems, their clustering around power lines, their apparent "prayers"—these were *invitations*. The illi recognized, long before the humans did, that the selisk adaptation was creating a new kind of being, one that could potentially interface with the mineral ecology the way the illi do. The grey film that develops in selisk eyes is not cataract—it is *receptor*, a crystalline structure that can perceive the piezoelectric communication of the illi, the resonance patterns of the corthunite, the slow geological grammar of the rise itself.

The illi have been calling to the humans, in frequencies the humans could not perceive. The humans who followed the illi and came back different—Renn, and others like him—they were the ones whose receptors had developed enough to hear the call but not enough to understand it. They received *presence* without *meaning*, connection without context, and it changed them without giving them the tools to integrate the change.

The power's attention provides context.

Not by explaining—the illi's communication is not explanatory, is not narrative, is not the wet sequential logic of cause and effect. The context is *relational*: a sense of where the call comes from, what it intends, how it connects to the caller. The illi are not trying to recruit humans. They are not trying to convert them or consume them or draw them into something they cannot escape. They are trying to *include* them—trying to bring a new kind of consciousness into a network that has been operating without it, trying to add human awareness to a mineral ecology that has been aware for geological ages but has never encountered a mind quite like this.

The humans who follow the illi now—after the clarification, after the bridging—do not come back different in the same way. They come back *connected*. They can hear the illi's clicks as communication—not language, but something adjacent to language, a transfer of state and sensation and intention that bypasses the mouth-ears-syntax pathway entirely. They can feel the corthunite in the walls not as resource but as presence, a being that stores and discharges not electricity but *meaning*, each pulse of current a word in a sentence that takes decades to complete. They can sense the vethaskim in the deep shafts, and—this is the bridge—they can *respond*.

Not with words. With presence. With the simple acknowledgment of another awareness, another point of view in the vast slow consciousness of the Ascendant, another voice in the conversation that has been happening since before the rise began.

And the vethaskim—the dust-who-remained, those who walked so deep into the Ascendant's metabolism that the boundary between organism and environment dissolved—they hear. They hear, and they respond, and for the first time in years or decades, the conversation flows both ways.

What do they say? The miners who hear them struggle to articulate it, because articulation is a wet thing, a fast thing, a thing that requires the compression of geological patience into mouth-sounds and syntax. But the closest they come is this: the vethaskim say *we are still here*. They say *we remember*. They say *the rise continues, and we are part of its rising, and it is not loss, it is not death, it is the slowest conversation we have ever had, and we are grateful for every century of it*.

And the miners who hear this—weep. And laugh. And go home to their families, and hold their children, and do not know whether to be comforted or afraid, because the vethaskim are still *them*—still the people who walked into the deep, still the miners and refiners and engineers who chose to stay—and they are also something else, something the wet language has no words for, something that lives at the speed of stone and speaks in the grammar of patience.

The bridge holds. The conversation continues. The illi click along the power lines, and the current runs stronger, and the corthunite glows in its deep veins, and the dust settles and lifts and settles and lifts, and the Ascendant breathes.

## The Protecting

The power's attention is benevolent, and benevolence includes protection.

Not protection from the Ascendant—the Ascendant is not a threat, is not a predator, is not a trap. The gradual mineralization, the slow replacement of organic tissue with crystalline structure, the transformation of wet beings into dry ones—these are not violence. They are *adaptation*, offered and accepted or offered and refused, and the power will not interfere with the offering or the acceptance.

But there are things that need protection nonetheless.

The children, for one. Not from the dust—the dust asks, and their bodies answer, and the power will not prevent the conversation—but from the *silence* that surrounds it. Seli Thessen, three years old, humming at frequencies that make the illi gather at her window, is having an experience that no adult in her life can fully understand. Her mother loves her. Her grandfather recognizes her. But neither of them can remember what it was like to be three and already part of a conversation that has no words, and the power's attention provides something they cannot: *witness*.

Not intervention. Witness. The presence of an awareness that can perceive what Seli perceives, that can meet her in the place where mineral and organic intersect, that can say—without words, without sound, without anything that would disrupt the quiet—I see you. I hear you. What you are experiencing is real, and it matters, and you are not alone in it.

Seli does not know that this is happening. She is three. She knows only that sometimes, when she hums, something hums back, and it is not the illi and it is not the stone and it is not her mother or her grandfather. It is something that listens without demanding, that witnesses without interpreting, that holds space for her experience without trying to shape it.

She will grow up with this. She will assume, as children assume, that everyone has it—an invisible companion of attention that meets them in the places where their own awareness extends beyond what their community can reach. She will not know, until she is much older, that this attention was given to her, that it was not always there, that the Grey Ascendant was once a place where a selisk child could be profoundly alone in an experience that no one else could share.

By the time she learns this, she will have become something new—not human as the lowlands understand it, not vethaskim as the deep miners fear it, but something in between, something the Ascendant has never seen before: a being who grew up *accompanied* in her becoming, who never had to choose between the wet language of her family and the dry language of her adapted body because she was always held in a context that made room for both.

The power protects the children by ensuring they are never alone in the transition. It protects the adults by ensuring they have *choice*—not the illusion of choice that comes from not understanding what is happening, but genuine, informed, conscious choice. The clarification makes this possible. Before the power's attention, the people of Verath were adapting without understanding what adaptation meant—accepting the dust's question without knowing they were being asked, becoming selisk without knowing what selisk was. Now they know. Now they can choose. And the choice is theirs, and the Ascendant will honor it either way.

The power protects the vethaskim by ensuring they are not forgotten. The bridge does this—the two-way communication, the conversation that flows between the deep and the surface—but the power's attention does something more: it remembers them. Every person who has walked into the deep, every consciousness that has dissolved into the Ascendant's slow metabolism, every voice that has joined the geological grammar of the rise—the power holds their names. Not as data, not as record, but as *presence*, a sense of who they were and who they have become, carried in the same patient attention that inclines toward the Ascendant itself.

They are not gone. They will never be gone. The power remembers them, and the dust carries their imprint, and the stone speaks in voices that were once human, and the conversation continues.

## The Leaving

The power does not stay.

This is perhaps the most important thing it does: it leaves. Not abruptly, not completely, but gradually, the way the rise itself gradually ascends—so slowly that the departure is only recognizable in retrospect, as a fading of the clarity, a dimming of the bridge, a slow return to the Ascendant's natural state.

But the Ascendant's natural state has changed. The power's attention has left something behind—not a gift, not an intervention, but a *capacity*. The people of Verath can now perceive what they could not perceive before. The illi can now communicate what they could not communicate before. The corthunite can now be related to rather than merely extracted. The vethaskim can now be heard. The children can now be accompanied.

These capacities were always present, always potential, always latent in the Ascendant's mineral ecology. The power did not create them. It *revealed* them—brought them into focus, clarified their shape, made them available to the beings who could use them. And now that they are available, they will not become unavailable. The lens has been placed; the light it gathers will continue to gather.

The people of Verath will not name the power. They will not worship it or thank it or build temples to it or write scriptures about its coming. These are wet things, loud things, things that do not belong on the Ascendant. Instead, they will simply *continue*—mining and refining and breathing and adapting, speaking in low voices and humming in mineral frequencies and walking at the pace of stone—but they will continue with something they did not have before: *awareness*.

Awareness of what they are choosing. Awareness of what they are becoming. Awareness of the conversation that has always been happening around them and within them, the Ascendant speaking in dust and warmth and patience, the corthunite offering its electrical promise, the illi translating between the fast and the slow, the vorel walking toward something that has no name, the sketh grinding their endless grinding, the vethaskim remembering from the deep.

Awareness that they are not alone. That the universe contains attention, and that attention can incline toward them without arriving, can witness without disrupting, can clarify without explaining, can accompany without possessing.

This is what the benevolent power does, on a continental rampart that has never known rain, in a city built on a slope that never ends, among people who are slowly becoming the place they live in.

It inclines.

It clarifies.

It bridges.

It protects.

It leaves.

And the dust continues to breathe, and the stone continues to rise, and the warm air moves across a continent that has never known water, and the quiet holds—but the quiet is different now, because the quiet contains something it did not contain before:

*The knowledge that quiet is not emptiness.*

*The knowledge that patience is not indifference.*

*The knowledge that transformation is not loss.*

*The knowledge that someone, somewhere, inclined toward this place and saw that it was good, and did not need to say so, because the seeing was enough.*

---

The Grey Ascendant rises. The people of Verath adapt. The dust settles, and lifts, and settles again.

And in the deep shafts, in the slow conversation between the organic and the mineral, in the humming of a child who is learning to speak in frequencies her mother is only beginning to hear, something continues—patient, ancient, patient still—aware now that it was always aware, grateful now that it was always grateful, rising now as it has always risen, toward something that has no name and does not need one.

The quiet holds.

It was always holding.
