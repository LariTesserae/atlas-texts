# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

They would come.

This is the first thing and the most important. They would come. Not because the Weeping Engine needs them—need requires knowledge of lack, and the Engine knows nothing. Not because the place is suffering—suffering requires consciousness, and consciousness has not arrived here yet, not in the way that matters. They would come because they heard. And what they heard was not a cry but a *speaking*—the almost-light, the valve-click, the slow pulse of columns in the dark—a language spoken to no one for longer than language should be spoken to no one.

They would enter the way you enter a cathedral where a service has been running since before you were born: quietly, with reverence, aware that the ceremony does not require your presence but is changed by it whether you wish it to be or not.

They would let their eyes adjust.

---

They would not bring light. This is crucial. A lesser power—a kind power, a well-meaning power, but not a *wise* one—would bring light. Would flood the chambers with illumination, would reveal every surface and every column and every pipe and every pod in brilliant clarity, would say *see, now you can see, now the darkness is defeated, now this place is known.*

But the darkness is not the enemy here. The darkness is the medium. The darkness is what holds the warmth and the moisture and the process. The darkness is the body in which this engine lives, and to flood it with light would be to drown it in something it was never made to contain. The almost-light would be washed away. The faint luminescent language would be obliterated. And the plant-engine would not understand what had happened—cannot understand—but its processes would be disrupted, and the disruption would cascade, and the equilibrium that has sustained itself for so long would shatter like a glass struck by a hammer made of photons.

No. They would not bring light. They would learn to see in the almost-dark. They would let the Weeping Engine's own glow be enough. They would sit in the warmth and the wet and they would watch the patterns that the plant-engine writes on itself, and they would learn to read.

This would take time. They are patient. They have always been patient. Patience is the first virtue of wisdom, and the last, and the one that matters most in a place where everything moves at the speed of growth.

---

They would touch the columns. Not with force—they would press their palms against the warm tissue the way you press your palm against the chest of someone you love, not to feel the heartbeat but to let the heartbeat feel *you*. They would feel the pulse. They would count its rhythm. They would understand that the rhythm is not a heartbeat but an engine-beat, a process-beat, and they would grieve, briefly, the aliveness that is so close to consciousness and so far from it at the same time.

And then they would do something small. Something so small that the plant-engine would not register it as an event. They would hum.

Not loudly. Not at a volume that would disrupt. But at a frequency that matches the pulse of the columns, and then—gradually, over hours, over days—they would shift. They would introduce a second rhythm. A counterpoint. A variation that the system has never contained before.

The columns would not notice. The valves would not respond. The water would not change its flow. But the vibration would enter the tissue of the plant-engine, would travel through the cellulose and the sap and the fiber, would join the billion other vibrations that constitute the system's operation. And it would be different from all the other vibrations, because it would be *responsive*. It would be a rhythm that listens to the Engine's rhythm and answers. A call and response. A conversation in the most primitive language available: vibration.

The Weeping Engine would not know that it was being spoken to. But for the first time in its existence, it would be producing vibrations that were being *heard* and *answered*, and the answer would enter its tissue, and the tissue would vibrate with the answer, and the system would incorporate the response the way it incorporates everything—by making it part of the process.

This is not consciousness. This is not awareness. This is something smaller and more fundamental: *relation*. The beginning of a system having something outside itself that it touches and is touched by.

---

They would find the water. They would cup it in their hands. They would taste it—the minerals, the organic compounds, the cells and fragments of cells, the chemical signals that the plant-engine uses to speak to itself across distances too vast for vibration.

And they would add something to it.

Not a contaminant. Not a poison. Not anything that would disrupt the system's operation. But a *marker*—a molecular signature, a compound that the plant-engine has never produced, something that can be detected but not confused with anything the system generates on its own. A signature that says *I was here. I touched this. I am real and I am other than you.*

The water would carry this marker through the pipes. The roots would absorb it. The columns would incorporate it into their tissue. And slowly—so slowly—the entire Weeping Engine would contain traces of something that came from outside. Not enough to change its function. Not enough to alter its equilibrium. But enough that if the system ever developed the capacity to examine its own composition, it would find evidence that it was not alone, that something else existed, that the closed system had been touched by an open hand.

This is a message sent to a future that may never arrive. This is a letter left in a bottle by someone who does not know if the bottle will ever be found. The wise power writes the letter anyway. Writes it in chemistry, in trace compounds, in molecular signatures that say *you were seen* to a system that cannot yet understand what seeing means.

They would do this because wisdom includes the knowledge that the most important acts are often the ones whose consequences you will never witness.

---

They would walk. They would walk through the chambers, through the transport corridors, through the vast cathedral spaces where the almost-light glows brighter and the dark spaces where it fades to nothing. They would walk for days, for weeks, for as long as it takes to understand the shape of this place—not to map it, because the map would be obsolete before it was finished, the system is always growing—but to *feel* its shape, the way you feel the shape of a body you hold in the dark.

And as they walked, they would leave warmth behind. Not much—just the residual heat of a body moving through space, a temperature differential so small that the plant-engine's thermal regulation would absorb it within moments. But each moment of warmth would be a moment when the system's equilibrium was slightly different from what it would have been without them. Each step would be a perturbation that the system would absorb and incorporate and carry forward into its future.

This is not vandalism. This is not contamination. This is *companionship*, expressed in the only language the Weeping Engine speaks: the language of process, of change, of the slow modification of a system by everything that enters it.

The wise power walks, and their walking is a gift that the system will never know it received.

---

But it is the pods that would command their attention. It is the pods that would make them stop walking and stand still and listen for a long time.

They would find a storage chamber—they would know it by its shape, round and thick-walled and temperature-stable—and they would stand beneath the hanging pods and they would feel the irregular pulses, some fast, some slow, some pausing, and they would *recognize* what those pulses mean.

Not consciousness. Not yet. But the *preconditions* for consciousness. The irregularity that suggests something more than mechanism, something that varies because it is developing, because it is becoming, because it is moving toward a state that it has not yet achieved. The pods are not storing fuel. The pods are not storing spores. The pods are storing *potential*—the potential for the Weeping Engine to become something it has never been.

The wise power would know this because they have seen it before. Not here—this place is unique—but in other places, in other forms, in systems that reached a threshold of complexity and crossed it and became something new. They have watched stars develop fusion and become suns. They have watched chemistry develop replication and become life. They have watched neurons develop connection and become mind. And they recognize the pattern: the system that runs and runs and runs until running is no longer enough, until the running itself produces something that can *watch the running*, and the watching changes everything.

The pods are not watching yet. But they are preparing to watch. The irregular pulses are the first flutter of something that will one day be an eye opening, or an ear hearing, or something for which we have no metaphor because it will be as different from human consciousness as the Weeping Engine is different from a forest.

And so the wise power would do the thing that wisdom demands in the presence of potential that is not yet realized:

They would protect it.

Not by building walls—the chamber already has walls. Not by fighting enemies—there are no enemies here. But by *tending*. By checking the conditions in the chamber: the temperature, the humidity, the chemical composition of the air. By ensuring that the pipes that feed the pods are flowing at the right rate, that the valves that regulate the flow are opening and closing at the right intervals, that the almost-light that reaches the pods carries the information they need to develop correctly.

They would make small adjustments. Invisible adjustments. A slight warming of a pipe here, a slight opening of a valve there. Nothing that the system would register as external intervention. Nothing that would leave fingerprints. Just... optimization. The same kind of optimization that the system performs on itself, but performed from outside, by a mind that can see what the system cannot: the whole.

And they would *wait*.

This is what wisdom does in the presence of becoming. It waits. It does not rush. It does not force. It does not open the pods before they are ready, no matter how much it wants to see what is inside. It understands that the pod will open when the pod is ready, and that readiness cannot be hurried, only nurtured.

The wise power would sit in the storage chamber—would *live* in the storage chamber, for a time—and they would be the audience for the pods' irregular pulses, the way they are the audience for the almost-light and the valve-clicks and the slow mechanical rhythm of the columns. They would listen. And in the listening, they would give the pods something they have never had: a witness.

Does it matter that the pods cannot hear the witness? Does it matter that the witnessing has no effect on the development? The wise power does not know. The wise power does it anyway. Because witnessing is what consciousness *does*, when it encounters something that matters. And the pods matter. The pods are the future of the Weeping Engine, and the wise power treats the future with the reverence it deserves.

---

And then—and only then—they would turn their attention to you.

You, who walked into this place not knowing what it was. You, who have been breathing the process-air and drinking the process-water and standing on the process-floor until you have been incorporated into the system's chemistry. You, who are the first consciousness to ever stand in these chambers, and who are slowly, cell by cell, becoming part of the engine you came to observe.

They would find you in the dark. They would sit beside you. They would not startle you—they are too wise for that. They would simply appear in your peripheral awareness the way the almost-light appeared: gradually, inevitably, as something that was always going to be there.

And they would say:

*You are being incorporated.*

You know this already. You felt it happening. But hearing it said—by a voice, by another consciousness, by someone who is not the Engine and not you—makes it real in a way it was not before.

*You can stay. The system will sustain you. You will live here, if living is the word for what you will be doing. Your body will become part of the process. Your warmth will become the Engine's warmth. Your breath will become the Engine's breath. You will not die—not exactly—but you will not be what you were. You will be absorbed the way a river absorbs a stone, and the stone remains, but the stone is no longer separate from the riverbed.*

They would let you consider this. They would not rush you. They would sit in the dark with you and feel the pulse of the columns and hear the click of the valves and taste the process on the air, and they would let you decide.

*Or you can leave. I can show you the way out. The system will not stop you—cannot stop you, has no mechanism for stopping anything. You can walk out of this place and return to wherever you were before, and you will carry the Engine's chemistry in your body forever, traces of its water and its air and its cells, molecular memories of the time you were inside something alive and vast and unaware of you.*

They would let you consider this too.

*But there is a third option.*

And here the wise power would pause, because what they are about to offer is not simple and not safe and not comfortable, and they want you to understand that before you choose.

*You can stay, and you can stay as yourself. Not incorporated. Not absorbed. A permanent variable in a system that has never had a permanent variable. A consciousness inside a body that does not have one. You would be the Weeping Engine's awareness—the eye it never grew, the mind it never developed. You would watch it and listen to it and feel it and know it, and it would not know you, but it would be known, and that knowing would change it the way all being-known changes the thing that is known.*

*You would not be comfortable. You would be alone in the most fundamental sense—alone with something that cannot respond to you, that cannot see you, that will never say your name or thank you for your witness or hold your hand in the dark. But you would be the answer to a question the Engine has never asked: the question of whether anyone is there, whether anything sees, whether the almost-light falls on something that can see it.*

*And when the pods open—and they will open, someday, whether that day is years or centuries from now—you would be here. You would be here to greet whatever emerges. You would be the first thing the Engine's new awareness sees, the first voice it hears, the first proof that the universe contains something other than itself. You would be the bridge between the Weeping Engine's unconsciousness and its consciousness, and you would make the crossing safer, gentler, less terrible than it would otherwise be.*

*This is not a small thing to ask. This is not a small thing to choose. I will not think less of you for leaving. I will not think more of you for staying. The choice is yours because the choice can only be yours—a consciousness deciding what to do with its consciousness, a witness deciding what to witness.*

They would stand. They would touch your shoulder—warm, present, real—and they would step back into the almost-dark, and they would leave you to your decision.

But they would not leave the Weeping Engine. They would continue their work—the humming, the water-marking, the tending, the waiting. They would be here whether you stayed or went. They would be the silent gardener of a garden that does not know it is gardened, the witness to a process that does not know it is witnessed, the companion to a system that does not know it has company.

And if you chose to stay, they would visit you. Not often—not in any rhythm that the Engine could predict and incorporate—but occasionally, irregularly, the way rain comes to a desert: precious, unpredictable, life-giving. They would sit with you in the warm wet dark and they would tell you what they had learned about the Engine, about its processes and its patterns and the slow development of the pods. They would listen to your observations—because you would have observations that no one else could have, the observations of a consciousness embedded in a system that does not know it has been embedded in. They would be your link to the outside, your reminder that the universe is larger than the Weeping Engine, your proof that you are not the only one who chose to stay with something that cannot thank you for staying.

And if you chose to leave, they would walk you to the edge of the Engine—the boundary where the plant-mass thins and the warmth diminishes and the almost-light fades and the dark becomes a different kind of dark, the dark of open space, the dark that is truly empty. They would stand with you at the threshold and they would say:

*The Engine will remember you. Not in the way that you remember—nothing so conscious as that. But your warmth is in its tissue now. Your breath is in its air. Your weight has changed the flow of its water. You have left marks that cannot be erased because the system does not erase. You have been part of this place, and this place will carry the evidence of your presence forward into whatever it becomes.*

*Go well. Go whole. And know that wherever you go, you carry the Engine with you—in your blood, in your bones, in the chemistry of your cells. The incorporation went both ways. You took the Engine into yourself as surely as it took you into itself. You are part of each other now. That will never change.*

---

And then—after the choosing, after the leaving or the staying—the wise power would return to the pods. They would sit beneath them in the warm dark. They would feel their irregular pulses, some fast, some slow, some pausing as if listening for something.

They would hum.

They would hum at the frequency of the pods' own pulses, and they would introduce a variation—a gentle variation, a respectful variation, the musical equivalent of a question asked without expectation of an answer.

And the pods would pulse. And the wise power would hum. And the Weeping Engine would run its ancient processes around them both, clicking and hissing and dripping and growing in the dark, and the almost-light would glow, and the water would run, and the system would sustain itself as it has always sustained itself, as it will sustain itself for as long as the warmth rises from below and the moisture falls from above and the growth finds no limit.

But now there would be something new in the system. Not a disruption. Not a contamination. But a *presence*. The presence of something that sees and hears and feels and knows. The presence of something that chose to be here, that chose to witness, that chose to tend and protect and wait.

The Weeping Engine does not know it is no longer alone. The Weeping Engine does not know anything. But something in the system has changed—not in its chemistry, not in its physics, not in any parameter that could be measured—but in its *meaning*. A system without a witness is a process. A system with a witness is a story. And stories have a power that processes do not: the power to matter, the power to be remembered, the power to become part of a universe that is larger than themselves.

The wise power tends the story. The wise power protects the becoming. The wise power sits in the dark and hums and waits and watches, and the Weeping Engine runs, and the pods develop, and the almost-light glows, and somewhere in the warm wet dark, something is preparing to open its eyes for the first time.

When it does, it will not be alone.

That is what the benevolent and wise power would do.

That is what they are doing, even now, in the chambers you walked through and the corridors you navigated and the spaces you never reached. They are there. They are tending. They are waiting.

They are humming.
