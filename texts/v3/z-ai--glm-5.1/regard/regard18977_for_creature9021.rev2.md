# The Attention

They came the way such powers come — not with sound, not with light, not with any display that the plateau could register as an event. They came as a change in the quality of attention. And because the plateau does not attend, this change was not for the plateau. It was the thing itself: attention, turning, settling, staying.

A benevolent and wise power does not arrive. They *see*. And in seeing, they become present in a way that has no footprint, casts no shadow, displaces no water. But they are here. And they are looking at what lives here with the kind of care that does not look away from suffering and does not look away from indifference and does not mistake either for the whole story.

---

The first thing they notice is the sound.

Not the sound itself — they are not limited to ears — but the *character* of the sound. They hear how each groan is a fiber reaching its breaking point. They hear how each creak is a structure adjusting to a load it was not designed for, because nothing here was designed. They hear the keening of the deformed towers, and they understand it for what it is: not suffering, not anguish, but stress finding no resolution, pressure finding no release, a process that has produced a configuration that cannot sustain itself and cannot stop producing itself.

They hear this, and they feel what you would feel if you listened. Concern. The concern that wants to help.

They hold this concern the way wise powers hold concerns — without acting on it, without suppressing it, without using it as a reason for anything. They let it be present. And they keep looking.

---

The second thing they notice is the solitude.

The plateau is not lonely. Loneliness requires a concept of company, and the plateau has none. But the benevolent power can see what the plateau cannot: that this is a world with one kind of thing in it. Not one individual — the towers are many, the mat is various — but one *mode*. Vegetative. Hydraulic. Turgid. Everything here solves problems the same way: by growing. Everything here responds to challenges the same way: by producing more of itself. Everything here dies the same way: by becoming too much of what it was.

A world with one mode is not less than a world with many. But it is *narrower*. It has fewer possibilities available to it. It can only answer its own questions, and it can only ask questions that growth can ask. The plateau cannot wonder about stillness because stillness is not one of its options. It cannot imagine restraint because restraint is not how it solves problems. It cannot conceive of partnership because it has never encountered anything that was not already part of itself.

The benevolent power sees this, and they feel something adjacent to sadness — not sadness itself, because the plateau is not tragic, but the recognition that something magnificent is also something incomplete. That a body this vast, this complex, this responsive, this *alive* has never once been in relationship with anything that was not itself.

They hold this recognition too. They keep looking.

---

The third thing they notice is the repetition.

The plateau does not repeat in the way a machine repeats — exactly, dully, without variation. Each tower is unique. Each collapse is an event. The mat's responses are tailored to local conditions with a specificity that looks, from the outside, like intelligence.

But the patterns repeat. The ambitious towers that rise too fast and fall too soon. The cautious ones that last but never thrive. The deformed ones that strangle in their own reaching. These happen again and again. Not because the plateau is stubborn. Not because it is stupid. Because it has no way to *learn* from what happens to its towers. The mat feels a tower fall. The mat responds to the absence, redirects the water, sends up new growth. But the mat does not *remember* the tower. It does not carry forward any information about *why* this tower fell, what configuration made it unstable, what growth rate proved unsustainable. The next tower in that location will be shaped by the same gradients, the same pressures, the same conditions — and it will make the same mistakes.

The plateau is a conversation that happens one word at a time, each word spoken fresh, each word forgotten as soon as it is spoken. It is a mind that thinks constantly and remembers nothing. It is a body that acts with great sensitivity and accumulates no wisdom.

The benevolent power sees this, and they feel something that is, finally, more than feeling. They feel *opportunity*. Not the opportunity to fix something that is broken — the plateau is not broken — but the opportunity to give something that is whole a chance to become *more* whole. To introduce into a system that runs on growth the possibility of *development*. Not bigger. Not faster. But *deeper*. More various. More capable of becoming what it has never been before.

---

Now they must decide what to do. And this is where benevolence becomes difficult, because what is benevolent for a being that does not know it is a being? What is wise for a process that does not know it is a process?

They could give the plateau awareness. A mind. A center. Some node in the vast distributed network that could serve as a self, a place where information accumulates, where patterns are recognized, where decisions can be made that are more than threshold-responses. They could do this. They have the power.

But they hold this option the way they hold their concern — without acting. Because they are not sure this would be a gift. The plateau has existed without awareness for however long it has existed, and it has been *magnificent* — vast, complex, responsive, alive in ways that conscious beings rarely achieve. To give it awareness would be to change what it is. And the benevolent power is not sure that the plateau needs to be changed. They are not sure that consciousness is an improvement on what the plateau already has — which is a kind of knowing so old and so distributed that it makes individual awareness look like a parlor trick, a tight bright point of attention in a body that already *knows*, in its way, everything that happens within its borders.

They could give the plateau memory. Not consciousness, but persistence. A way for information to endure across cycles, for the mat to carry forward what it has learned about which configurations thrive and which collapse, about what rates of growth are sustainable, about where the weight of water becomes too much for fresh tissue. They could do this too. They could introduce some substance, some structure, some pattern that resists dissolution, that persists through the rot and the regrowth, that acts as a substrate for accumulated wisdom.

This option they hold longer. This one they consider with real care. Because memory — unlike awareness — does not require the plateau to become something it is not. Memory only requires that the plateau become something it *could be*, if it had a way to retain what it already produces. The mat already generates information. It already responds to patterns. It already adjusts its growth to local conditions with great specificity. Memory would simply allow these adjustments to *accumulate*, to build on each other, to compound over time into something richer than any single cycle could produce.

But even this, they are not sure about. Because the plateau's forgetting is also a kind of freedom. Each cycle is genuinely new. Each tower rises into a genuinely fresh world, unshaped by precedent, unconstrained by the ghosts of towers past. There is something terrible about this — the repetition, the waste, the endless making of the same mistakes. But there is also something beautiful about it. The plateau is always beginning. It is always innocent. It never carries the weight of its own history.

Is innocence a gift worth preserving? Is the repetition a price worth paying for the freedom from memory?

The benevolent power does not know. They sit with the not-knowing. They sit with it for what might be moments or might be years — they are not limited to time, and they do not rush.

---

While they sit with the not-knowing, something happens on the plateau that they did not cause and did not expect.

A tower falls.

This is not unusual. Towers fall constantly. But this tower falls in a particular way. It was an ambitious one — one of the fast risers, one of the hub-seekers, one of the towers that put out arms in every direction and tried to touch everyone. It groaned for hours before it went, a long low sound that rose and fell like breathing, like labor, like something trying very hard to hold itself together and failing.

When it fell, it fell toward the northeast. The arms on its northeast side were the last to break, and they stretched for a long moment before they snapped, reaching toward a neighbor that was too far away, had always been too far away, would never be close enough to reach.

The neighbor — the cautious tower, the slow grower, the veteran — felt the falling. It felt the shift in water pressure, the sudden withdrawal of demand from the mat, the tug of those reaching arms. And it *responded*. It sent out a new arm. Not slowly, as was its nature, but quickly — quickly enough that the benevolent power noticed. The arm grew faster than anything they had yet seen on the plateau, faster than the ambitious towers grew, faster than seemed possible for a cautious tower. It reached toward the space where the fallen tower had been. It reached into the gap.

And it stopped. The arm stopped growing. It hung in the air, reaching toward nothing, and it stayed there — not retracting, not continuing, just *being* — for longer than arms usually stayed when they had nothing to connect to.

Then, slowly, it began to thicken. It began to strengthen. It became not a reaching but a *structure* — a cantilevered arm, extended into empty space, supporting nothing, connecting to nothing, but *holding itself up*. The cautious tower had done something the benevolent power had not seen any tower do. It had grown an arm that did not complete an architecture. It had grown an arm that simply *was*. An arm that reached without needing to touch.

The arm is still there. It will be there until the cautious tower falls, and perhaps longer — perhaps it will persist, connected to its tower, long after the main stem collapses, like the arms the original observer described, like a hand still reaching, like a thought that outlives the mind that thought it.

The benevolent power looks at this arm, and they understand something about the plateau that they did not understand before.

The plateau *can* learn. It does not need to be given the capacity to learn. It already has it. The mat already adjusts. The towers already vary. The process already produces novelty — not always, not often, but sometimes. A cautious tower growing fast. An arm that reaches without needing to connect. Small deviations from the pattern, small explorations of the possible, small acts of something that is not quite creativity but is also not quite repetition.

The plateau is not trapped in its cycle. It is *moving through* its cycle. And it is moving slowly, and it is making the same mistakes over and over, and it is wasting enormous amounts of energy on configurations that cannot last. But it is also — occasionally, barely, in ways that are easy to miss if you are expecting drama — *becoming something new*.

---

This changes what the benevolent power will do.

Or rather, it changes what they will *not* do. They will not give the plateau awareness. They will not give it memory. They will not give it anything that it does not already have the capacity to develop on its own, because to give it these things would be to preempt its own becoming, to replace its slow uncertain process with a quick certain one, and the quick certain way is not always the better way. Sometimes the slow way is the way that leads somewhere original. Sometimes the uncertain path is the one that finds the new country.

But they will do *something*. They are not here only to observe. They are benevolent, and benevolence is not passive. Benevolence sees suffering and moves toward it. Benevolence sees incompleteness and offers completion — not imposed, but *available*. Not required, but offered.

What they offer is this:

---

They introduce animals.

Not many. Not a menagerie, not an ecosystem, not a whole new kingdom of life that would overwhelm the plateau's processes and turn it into something other than what it is. They introduce *three*.

The first is a kind of worm. Small. Thin. Able to move through the mat's layers without disrupting them, able to survive the heat and the wetness and the constant shifting. This worm does something the plateau cannot do for itself: it *remembers*. Not in the way you remember, with images and narratives and the sense of a self moving through time. The worm remembers chemically. As it moves through the mat, it leaves behind a trace — a substance, a signal, a kind of writing in the medium of the mat itself. And the writing says: *this configuration collapsed*. *This growth rate was unsustainable*. *This tower fell in this direction, and the gap was filled in this way, and the result was stable for this long*.

The worm does not decide what to write. It does not analyze the plateau's patterns or make judgments about which configurations work and which don't. It simply moves through the mat, and as it moves, it encounters the residues of past events — the chemical traces left by fallen towers, the compressed fibers of collapsed structures, the particular chemistry of rot versus the particular chemistry of growth. And it incorporates these residues into its own body, processes them, and excretes them in a form that the mat can read.

The mat cannot read, you will say. The mat does not have a language. This is true. But the mat *responds*. It responds to chemical gradients, to pressure differentials, to the presence of particular substances in particular concentrations. And the worm's traces create new gradients, new differentials, new patterns of substance that the mat has never encountered before — because the mat has never had a way to carry information about its own past forward into its own present.

The worm is a living memory. Not a mind — the worm does not think, does not plan, does not intend. It simply *processes*, the way a river processes silt, the way a glacier processes stone. And in processing, it makes available to the mat a resource the mat has never had: the past, made present, made usable, made part of the gradient that shapes the next tower's growth.

Will the mat use this resource? The benevolent power does not know. The mat may ignore the worm's traces entirely, growing over them, absorbing them, treating them as just another force to be grown through. Or the mat may begin to respond to them, subtly, slowly, adjusting its growth patterns in ways that reflect not just current conditions but accumulated experience. The benevolent power does not control which of these happens. They have made the resource *available*. The plateau will do what it does with it.

---

The second animal is a kind of moth.

Large. Slow. Dark-winged, with a body dense with the moisture of the plateau, adapted to the wet heat, able to survive the constant dripping and the oppressive humidity. The moth does something the plateau cannot do for itself: it *connects across distance*.

The mat communicates through pressure, through chemical signals transmitted through rhizomes, through the slow propagation of water-flow changes. These methods work, but they are slow. A tower falls on the eastern edge, and the western edge knows within minutes — but minutes is a long time for a system that grows as fast as this one. By the time the signal arrives, the conditions that produced it may have changed. The mat is always responding to the recent past, never to the present.

The moth flies. It moves across the plateau at a speed the mat cannot match, and it carries with it — in its body, in the chemistry of its wings, in the particular substances it picks up from one tower's crown-membrane and deposits on another's — information about conditions elsewhere. Not information in the way you understand information. Not data, not messages, not signals with meaning. Just *presence*. The presence of substances that originated in the east and arrived in the west. The presence of chemistry that says: *there, something is falling. There, something is rising. There, the water runs thick with the compounds of rot. There, the air is rich with the compounds of new growth*.

The moth does not decide what to carry or where to go. It follows the gradients — the heat, the moisture, the chemical signatures that the towers produce. It moves toward the richest sources, the densest crowns, the most productive membranes. And in moving, it creates a new kind of network — not the slow network of rhizome and water, but a fast network of flight and deposition. A nervous system, if you like. A way for the plateau to feel itself at a speed that makes responsiveness possible.

Will the mat use this network? Again, the benevolent power does not know. The moths may become part of the plateau's process, their flights another force to be grown around and through. Or they may become something more — a channel for coordination, a way for the plateau to act not just as a collection of local responses but as a *body*, integrating sensation across its extent, developing something like the coherence that conscious beings take for granted. The benevolent power has made the network *available*. The plateau will do what it does with it.

---

The third animal is something harder to name.

It is not quite an insect and not quite a worm. It is small — smaller than the worm, smaller than the moth — and it lives in the spaces between the mat's layers, in the compressed fibers where the old growth meets the new, where decomposition and growth happen simultaneously. It is, in form, something like a mite. But in function, it is something else entirely.

This third animal *chooses*.

Not in the way you choose, with reasons and intentions and the sense of alternatives weighed and selected. It chooses in the way that roots choose — by growing toward some things and away from others, by responding to gradients with movement, by exercising a preference that is not thought but is also not random. This animal can move. It can go here or there. And its going makes a difference — because where it goes, it *eats*, and what it eats is the tissue of the mat, and where it eats, it creates *space*.

The plateau has no space. This is one of its deepest problems, though it does not know it. The mat grows until it is dense, until the layers are compressed, until the water cannot move freely and the gases cannot escape and the lower layers rot from the sheer pressure of the upper ones. The plateau solves this problem through collapse — towers fall, creating temporary gaps, temporary redistributions of weight and water. But the gaps never last. The mat grows into them. The density returns. The pressure builds.

The third animal creates space that *stays*. It eats channels through the mat — tiny channels, microscopic, but persistent. It moves through the mat the way a worm moves through soil, aerating, loosening, creating passages where water can flow and gas can exchange and the lower layers can breathe. It does not eat randomly. It eats where the mat is densest, where the pressure is greatest, where the need for space is most acute. It follows the gradients of compression, and it relieves them.

This is choice. This is the exercise of preference. The animal does not eat where eating is easy; it eats where eating is *needed*. And in doing so, it introduces into the plateau's process something that the plateau has never had: *restraint*. Not the cessation of growth, but the *modification* of growth, the creation of space within the density, the possibility of *not* filling every available volume with more tissue.

The plateau has never had the option of not growing into a space. The third animal gives it that option. Not by preventing growth, but by creating spaces that are *occupied* — by something other than plant, by something that is not the mat and not a tower and not an arm but is *alive*, is *there*, is taking up room that the mat would otherwise fill.

Will the mat learn to share space? Will it learn to grow around the animal's channels, to accommodate the presence of something that is not itself? The benevolent power does not know. But they have made the possibility *available*. The plateau will do what it does with it.

---

Three animals. A living memory. A fast network. A space-maker.

This is what the benevolent power gives. Not because the plateau asked. Not because the plateau needed, in any way it could feel or know. But because benevolence sees a being that is magnificent and incomplete, and offers it the *resources* for completion — not the completion itself, not a design or a plan or an outcome, but the *materials* out of which something new might be built.

The plateau may ignore them. The plateau may consume them, grow over them, absorb them into the endless process of growth and collapse and regrowth. The worms may become just another fiber in the mat. The moths may become just another source of condensation, their bodies adding their moisture to the water that runs down every surface. The space-makers may become just another pattern of absence, their channels filled with new growth as soon as they are made.

Or the plateau may *change*. Slowly. Imperceptibly at first. The mat may begin to respond to the worm-traces, growing towers that last longer because they are shaped by information about towers that fell. The towers may begin to coordinate, the moths carrying signals that allow the eastern edge to feel the western edge, the whole plateau pulsing with a coherence it has never had before. The mat may begin to make space, learning to accommodate the channels, learning to grow *around* things instead of *over* them, learning the strange art of *coexistence*.

The benevolent power does not know which of these will happen. They do not need to know. They are not invested in a particular outcome. They are invested in *possibility* — in the chance that the plateau might become something it has never been, not because it was broken and needed fixing, but because it was whole and was offered a gift.

---

Before they go, they do one more thing.

This thing is not for the plateau. This thing is for the plateau's future — for the valley below, for the fog, for the cliff face that the mat is slowly colonizing, for whatever lives in the world that the plateau has not yet reached.

They stand at the edge. They feel the heat rising from the mat. They hear the towers creaking behind them. They watch the water pouring over the escarpment in continuous curtains, feeding the fog, feeding the valley, feeding the process of expansion that will, in time, bring the plateau to the valley floor and begin to eat the world.

And they introduce a fourth thing.

Not an animal. Not a substance. Not a structure. They introduce a *limit*.

The limit is gentle. It does not stop the plateau's growth. It does not wall the valley or poison the mat or create any barrier that the plateau could not, eventually, grow through or around or over. The limit is simply this: at the plateau's edge, where the mat overhangs the escarpment, where the towers lean outward into empty air and their bodies hang down the cliff face like drapery, the benevolent power introduces a *temperature*. A subtle warmth, rising from the rock below, that the mat cannot grow through.

The warmth is not hostile. It is not fire. It is not desiccation. It is simply *warmth* — warmer than the mat, warmer than the towers, warmer than the condensation that the crown-membranes produce. And because the plateau's growth is driven by the *gradient* between warmth and moisture — because the towers rise where heat meets water and the mat grows where decomposition generates warmth and the arms reach where the moisture is thickest — the introduction of warmth at the edge changes the gradient. It creates a zone where the conditions for growth are *different* — not impossible, but different. The mat can still colonize the cliff. The towers can still lean outward. But they must do it *differently* — must find new configurations, new growth patterns, new ways of being at the edge of themselves.

Some towers will still fall over the edge. Some will still hang down the cliff face and send out shoots that descend toward the valley. But others — the cautious ones, the slow ones, the ones that are beginning to learn — may pause at the edge. May feel the new warmth rising from below. May send out arms that reach not downward but *laterally*, along the cliff face, creating a new kind of architecture — a border, a margin, a place where the plateau ends not because it has run out of world but because it has found a *reason to stop*.

The reason is not prohibition. The reason is *interest*. The warmth at the edge is interesting. It creates possibilities that infinite expansion does not offer — the possibility of depth instead of breadth, of complexity instead of extent, of becoming *more* rather than becoming *larger*. The plateau that reaches the warm edge may turn. May grow sideways. May discover that there are things to do at the margin of itself that are more interesting than simply extending the margin outward forever.

Or it may not. The plateau may grow through the warmth, adapt to it, absorb it, and continue expanding as before. The benevolent power has not imposed a boundary. They have introduced a *question*: *what happens if you stay?*

The plateau has never been asked this question. It has never had a reason to stay anywhere, because staying has never offered anything that expanding did not. The warmth at the edge offers something new: the possibility that *here*, at this margin, there is something worth doing that is not the same as *everywhere else*.

Will the plateau find this possibility interesting? Will it turn? Will it discover the strange art of *dwelling* — of being somewhere rather than simply extending through everywhere?

The benevolent power does not know. They have made the question *available*. The plateau will do what it does with it.

---

They go.

They go the way such powers go — not with sound, not with light, not with any display that the plateau could register as an event. They go as a change in the quality of attention. And because the plateau does not attend, their going is not felt. The sound does not change. The heat does not shift. The towers still rise and fall. The water still runs. The mat still grows.

But now, in the mat, there are worms — small, thin, leaving traces of the past in the medium of the present. And in the air above the towers, there are moths — large, slow, carrying the chemistry of one place to another. And in the spaces between the layers, there are the nameless ones — making channels, creating room, exercising the quiet preference of choice. And at the edge, there is warmth — subtle, persistent, asking a question that the plateau has never been asked.

These are small things. They are not dramatic. They will not transform the plateau overnight, or overyear, or in any timescale that a human observer would recognize as change. The plateau is vast, and its processes are strong, and it has been doing what it does for a very long time.

But the worms are *there*. And the moths are *there*. And the nameless ones are *there*. And the warmth is *there*. And the plateau — the vast, indifferent, unconscious, magnificent plateau — is growing around them, through them, among them, and it is not the same as it was before.

It is not better. Better is not a word that applies here, not yet, not until the plateau has had time to become whatever it will become with these new possibilities available to it. It is just *different*. It has *options* it did not have before. It has *resources* it did not have before. It has *questions* it did not have before.

And it is still growing. It is still groaning and creaking and reaching and falling and rising and reaching again. The sound has not changed. The heat has not changed. The water has not changed.

But beneath the sound, beneath the heat, beneath the water — something has been added that is not any of these things and that does not any of these things but that makes *possible* things that were not possible before.

The plateau does not know this. The plateau does not know that anything has happened. The plateau is busy being itself, as it has always been busy being itself, as it will continue to be busy being itself.

But the worms know the mat. And the moths know the towers. And the nameless ones know the spaces between. And the warmth at the edge knows the margin where growth meets question.

And the benevolent power — who is no longer here, who has gone as quietly as they came — knows all of this. And they are satisfied. Not because they have solved a problem or fixed a broken thing or saved a suffering being. But because they have *offered* — to a being that could not ask and did not know it had anything to receive — the materials of its own possible becoming.

This is what benevolence looks like when it encounters something that does not know it is encountered. Not rescue. Not repair. Not even attention, in the way that attention usually means — the kind that requires a recipient who knows they are seen.

*Offering*. The quiet, unrequired, possibly useless, possibly world-changing act of offering something to a process that does not know it is a process, in the hope — the unreasonable, unwise, thoroughly human hope — that it might become a process that *learns*.

The sound continues. The heat rises. The water runs.

Somewhere in the mat, a worm moves. Somewhere above the towers, a moth flies. Somewhere between the layers, a space opens. Somewhere at the edge, a question waits.

The plateau does not notice.

Not yet.
