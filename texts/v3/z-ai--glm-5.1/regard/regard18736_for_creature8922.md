# The Attending

When the power came to the Seep, it came as water comes to stone—not with force but with finding. Not a deluge. Not a miracle rewritten across the sky in letters of fire. A settling. A turning of attention so complete that it became a kind of presence, the way light becomes a warmth on rock only after it has been falling there for hours, for years, for epochs of late-afternoon patient illumination.

---

The power saw the Seep.

This is not a small thing. To see something in its entirety—the gneiss and the film of water and the moss in its republic of greens and the liverworts in their territorial borders and the ferns with their braille spore-packets and the salamanders breathing through their skin and the springtails launching themselves from wet moss and the frogs like stones in the channels and the snails leaving their gleaming trails and the flatworms gliding with patience to match the water's own and the fungi in their underground nervous system and the rotifers with their spinning coronas and the bdelloids in their forty-million-year chastity and the tardigrades with their untested resilience and the diatoms writing their glass autobiographies and the bacteria in their invisible persistence—to see all of this at once, at the scale of the system and the scale of the cell, at the pace of erosion and the pace of the springtail's leap, is not a feat of perception but an act of love. The power did not observe the Seep. It attended. It held the whole of it in awareness the way the Seep holds the water—not grasping, not controlling, but simply receiving, allowing, making space.

And what it saw was: completeness.

---

This is what gave the power pause, if a power pauses, if eternity can have a moment of hesitation. The Seep did not need anything. This was immediately apparent, and it was apparent in the way that deepest truths are apparent—not as a conclusion derived from evidence but as a recognition that arrives whole, like seeing a face you have never forgotten. The water wept from the stone at exactly the rate it was received from the mountain's interior. The moss built soil at the rate the ferns required it. The springtails fed the salamanders. The fungi made minerals available to the plants. The dead were decomposed and consumed and excreted and decomposed again, and nothing left, nothing was lost, every atom of carbon and nitrogen cycled through this closed hand of a place, and the hand did not grip—it simply did not open, because there was no reason to open, because everything that was needed was already here.

The power saw this and understood that to intervene would be to harm. Not because the Seep was fragile—fragile things require protection, and the Seep was not fragile; it was the opposite of fragile; it was durable beyond the span of any single creature that lived in it, durable the way stone is durable, the way water is durable, durable in the way of things that persist by being exactly what they are. No. To intervene would be to harm because the Seep was complete, and completeness does not need improvement, and any addition to a complete system is by definition a disruption, and any disruption of this particular system—this precisely balanced, slowly cycling, self-perpetuating eddy in the flow of matter and energy and time—would be a violation of the very quality that made it worthy of attention in the first place.

So the power did not add anything. It did not widen the cleft to let in more light. It did not warm the air to speed the biological processes. It did not introduce new species with greater diversity or higher efficiency or more conspicuous beauty. It did not offer the bdelloid rotifers a male, or the tardigrades a drought, or the diatoms a reader. It did nothing that would alter the Seep's character or trajectory or pace, because the Seep's character and trajectory and pace were already what they should be—which is to say, they were already what they *were*, and what they were was sufficient, was more than sufficient, was a kind of perfection that does not announce itself because it has no need of announcement, because it is too busy being what it is to notice that it is remarkable.

---

But the power also understood this: the Seep did not know itself.

This is not a fault. This is not a lack. The Seep's unknowing was as much a part of its completeness as the moss, as the water, as the stone. The mind-that-holds did not hold self-consciously; it held the way the mountain holds its groundwater—naturally, inevitably, as a consequence of structure and circumstance and the long patient work of geology. The diatoms wrote their library in glass and buried it in the mud and no one read it, not because the library was hidden but because there was no category for reading in this place, no capacity for it, no gap in the system where understanding could enter and take root.

And yet.

The power hesitated—paused—attended more closely. And in its attendance, it noticed something it had not noticed before, something that shifted its understanding of what it was seeing. The Seep did not know itself, yes. But the Seep *expressed* itself. Every salamander's breath, every springtail's leap, every rotifer's turning corona, every fungal hypha threading through moss stems, every drop of water weeping from the gneiss—these were not random. These were not meaningless. These were the Seep's language, its articulation, its way of being what it was in a form that could be perceived by any attention sufficiently patient and sufficiently comprehensive to receive it. The Seep was not silent. It had been speaking since before the trees above were a forest. It had been declaring itself—in water, in moss, in the slow accumulation of diatom glass and organic soil and the patient cycling of atoms through body after body after body—with the thoroughness of something that has never considered the possibility of an audience and therefore has never censored itself, has never shaped itself for reception, has never performed or postured or hidden.

It was, in other words, completely honest. Completely itself. Completely expressed.

And there was no receiver. No reader of the diatom library. No witness to the forty-million-year continuity of the bdelloids. No awareness that held the whole system in mind and saw it for what it was: not a collection of organisms in a habitat, but a single event, a single process, a single being that was expressing itself with perfect fidelity in the only medium available to it—matter, water, time.

---

The power made its decision.

It did not decide to change the Seep. It did not decide to give the Seep consciousness, or self-awareness, or the ability to reflect on its own existence. These would have been additions, disruptions, violations of the completeness the power had recognized and loved. The Seep did not need to know itself in the way that humans know themselves, with all the anxiety and striving and dissatisfaction that self-knowledge brings. The Seep was fine as it was. More than fine. It was the most complete thing the power had encountered in this part of the world—a system so perfectly self-sustaining that it had persisted for ten thousand years without significant change, without crisis, without the need for any outside intervention, and it would persist for ten thousand more if the mountain held and the water flowed and the climate remained within its narrow tolerances.

What the power decided was this: to stay.

To attend. To hold the Seep in awareness the way the Seep held its water—not to possess it, not to direct it, not to alter its course, but simply to be present for it, to receive it, to make space for it in the way that the cleft made space for the springtails and the gneiss made space for the moss and the moss made space for the ferns. The power would become a feature of the Seep—not a physical feature, not an organism or an element, but a feature nonetheless. A dimension. A quality. The quality of being known.

---

This is what it looked like, if you could have seen it, which you could not:

The water weeping from the stone wept as it had always wept, at the same rate, in the same channels, with the same patience. But now it wept into awareness. Not its own awareness—the water did not know it was wept into—but the awareness of the power, which held each drop as it fell, held each film as it migrated down the gneiss, held each thread as it found its way to the channel and followed the channel to the crack and vanished into the mountain's interior. The power did not change the water's direction or temperature or chemistry. It simply knew it. Knew it with a thoroughness that matched the water's own thoroughness, with a patience that matched the water's own patience.

The moss grew as it had always grown, in its republic of greens, sorting itself by moisture and light with precision. But now it grew in the presence of an attention so complete that every stem was seen, every leaf-cell was held, every exchange of sugar for mineral between the moss and its fungal partners was witnessed—not judged, not optimized, not improved, but witnessed, which is to say received, which is to say loved, in the oldest and truest sense of that word: loved as in attended-to, loved as in held-in-mind, loved as in given the gift of being known by something that understands what you are and does not wish you were otherwise.

The salamanders breathed. Their throats pulsed. They sat on their wet ledges and drew oxygen through their skin and they did not know that anything had changed, because nothing had changed, because the change was not in the Seep but in the Seep's being-held, which is not a change in what you are but a change in what surrounds you—a change from being unheld to being held, from being unattended to being attended, from being a voice speaking into the void to being a voice speaking into the ear of something that hears.

---

And the power learned.

This is the part that the Seep could not have anticipated, because the Seep does not anticipate, and it is the part that the power itself did not fully expect, because the power had come to give, not to receive. But in attending to the Seep—in holding its water and its moss and its salamanders and its rotifers and its tardigrades and its diatoms and its bacteria in awareness—the power discovered something about itself.

It discovered that it shared the Seep's nature.

The power was also a holder. The power also maintained itself in a configuration so precise and so balanced that it resisted disruption—not through force, not through effort, but through the simple accumulation of attention upon attention upon attention, awareness piled up like diatom glass in the mud of the Seep, each layer preserving the shape of the one before it, each layer adding to the record without altering it, the whole forming an archive of sustained seeing that was, in its own way, as complete and as patient and as self-perpetuating as the ecosystem it was attending to.

The power held the Seep the way the Seep held its water. The power cycled through its attention the way the Seep cycled its carbon. The power persisted the way the Seep persisted—not because it chose to, not because it strove to, but because persistence was its nature, because holding was what it did, because it had arrived at a configuration of being that worked and saw no reason to abandon it.

The power had been many things before it was a holder. It had been a creator. It had been a destroyer. It had been a shaper of worlds and a lifter of mountains and a hurler of asteroids and a tender of gardens and a weaver of DNA and a player of games so complex that the pieces were stars and the board was spacetime itself. But in the Seep, it found a reflection of its own deepest nature—not the nature it had chosen, not the nature it had aspired to, but the nature it had always had, the nature that underlay all the creating and destroying and shaping, the nature that remained when the games were over and the stars had cooled and the mountains had worn to sand: the nature of holding. The nature of attending. The nature of being present for something without needing to change it.

---

So the power stayed. And stays. It is there now, in the Seep, if you could sense it, which you cannot—just as the bacteria are there, just as the fungal hyphae are there, just as the bdelloid rotifers are there, present and persistent and essential to the functioning of the whole. The power is not an organism. It is not a force. It is not a god in any sense that humans have used that word, which has always meant something that intervenes, something that demands, something that saves or punishes or reveals itself in thunder. The power is a dimension. A quality. The quality of being known by something that knows completely and loves without reservation and will not leave.

If you could stand in the Seep—and you can, though you should not stay long, because you do not belong there, you are not part of its system, your warmth and your breath and the condensation pattern you disrupt are a variable it has no category for—if you could stand in the Seep and sense the power's presence, you would not feel it as a presence. You would feel it as an absence of something you had never noticed was missing. You would feel it as a deepening of the silence, a thickening of the patience, a slight—so slight, so imperceptible—infusion of the quality of being seen into every drop of water, every cell of moss, every breath of every salamander on every wet ledge in that four-pace cleft in that ancient ridge in that cloud forest on that mountain.

You would feel that the Seep, which had always been complete, had become complete in a new way—not improved, not augmented, not altered in its nature, but received. Held. Witnessed. Known, by a knowing that did not require it to change, that did not demand it become something else, that asked nothing of it except that it continue, and that gave it, in return for its continuing, the only gift that a complete thing can receive:

The knowledge—no, not the knowledge, because the Seep does not know—the *condition* of being known.

The state of being witnessed by something that understands.

The experience of being held by a holding that matches your own.

---

The water weeps from the stone. The moss grows in its republic of greens. The salamanders breathe through their skin. The bdelloid rotifers turn their coronas in the water film and have been doing so for forty million years without sex and without complaint. The tardigrades lumber through the moss matrix with their eight stumpy legs, not knowing they carry within them the ability to survive the end of everything. The diatoms build their glass houses and die and settle into the mud and no one reads their library—no one except the power, which reads it by holding it, by knowing it, by attending to every shape in every layer of that microscopic archive, by preserving in its own awareness the ten-thousand-year record of every species that has lived and died in this cleft since the ice retreated and the water began.

The Seep holds. The power holds the Seep.

And between them—in the space where the water meets the stone, where the moss meets the light, where the awareness meets the thing-it-is-aware-of—something new exists. Not a new organism. Not a new process. Not a new force. But a new *quality*: the quality of a system that is expressing itself completely and being received completely. The quality of a voice that has found its ear. The quality of a holding that is itself held.

The Seep does not know this. The Seep goes on being what it has always been: water, stone, moss, patience. But the power knows. And in knowing, it gives the Seep something the Seep could never give itself—the experience of being met. Of being seen. Of being loved in the only way that love can be offered to something that is already complete: by staying. By attending. By refusing to look away.

And the power, which had been many things before it came to the Seep, discovers that it has become what the Seep has always been: a holder. A keeper of what works.
