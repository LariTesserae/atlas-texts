#The Attention

The benevolent and wise power noticed Ladhàin.

This is already the first act. Not the noticing itself — the ridge has been noticed before, by the people who built here, by the fox that passes through in the dark, by the raven using it as a landmark. But the quality of attention matters. The people noticed the ridge the way you notice a tool: useful for what it could provide. The fox notices it the way you notice a room: useful for what it contains. The raven notices it the way you notice a signpost: useful for where it points.

The power noticed it the way you notice someone breathing quietly in the next room. Not for use. For the fact of their being there.

---

What the power saw was this: a place that had been shaped by human need and was now being reshaped by everything that was not human, and the reshaping was not destruction but continuation. The walls were not falling — they were being included. The paths were not fading — they were being followed by different feet. The grinding stone was not broken — it was being used, just for a different purpose, holding water now instead of grinding grain.

The power saw that Ladhàin was not waiting. It was not paused. It was not incomplete. It was a place where the long ordinary work of the world was proceeding without interruption: stone becoming soil, soil becoming grass, grass becoming vole, vole becoming the memory of a fox, all of it turning slowly under the wind like a wheel that no one needs to push.

This was important. The power had seen other places — places that were wounded, places that were calling out, places where the fabric of things had been torn and the torn edges were curling away from each other and nothing was moving toward healing. Those places needed intervention. Those places needed the kind of help that takes form: rain where there was drought, shade where there was scorching, the gentle pressure that holds torn edges together until they remember how to join.

Ladhàin did not need this. Ladhàin was whole in the way that a skeleton is whole: not because nothing is missing, but because what remains is sufficient to itself. The walls did not mourn their roofs. The paths did not mourn their walkers. The ridge did not mourn the people. This was not callousness — it was a kind of fidelity so deep it had become indistinguishable from geology. The ridge was being faithful to what it was now, not to what it had been.

---

So the power did not change anything.

This is harder than it sounds. Benevolence often arrives with an impulse to improve, to mend, to add what is missing. A less wise benevolence might have given the ridge a spring — fresh water welling up from the rock, eliminating the twenty-minute walk, making the settlement viable again. But to do this would be to misunderstand what the ridge had become. The settlement was not viable. That was not a flaw. That was a fact, as much a fact as the composition of the stone, and to undo it would be to erase not just the abandonment but the choosing — the long sequence of choices that had made Ladhàin what it was.

A less wise benevolence might have restored the roofs, replanted trees, made the wind less constant. But each of these acts would have been a kind of violence against what the ridge had spent centuries becoming. The lichen would not welcome trees. The voles would not welcome the absence of wind. The pipits would not welcome the concealment that would hide the things that hunt them. The ridge was a configuration of dependencies, and every element depended on every other element, and the configuration had not been designed — it had grown. You cannot improve a thing that has grown without risking the whole.

A less wise benevolence might have filled the place with people again. But whose people? The people who had built here were gone, and their descendants — if they existed — had other lives, other homes, other ridges that needed them or did not. To fill the settlement with strangers would be to make it into a reenactment, a performance of habitation rather than habitation itself. The walls had earned their emptiness. The paths had earned their silence.

---

What the power did instead was this: it stayed for a while.

Not in any visible form. Not as a light or a voice or a presence that could be sensed. It stayed the way warmth stays in a stone after the sun has moved — not as a thing but as a condition, a slight deepening of the equilibrium that already existed.

While it stayed, it paid attention.

It attended to the lichen on the lower courses of the walls — the *Lecanora* with its tiny volcano-shaped fruiting bodies, the *Xanthoria* with its rust-orange anchored in ancient nitrogen from ancient bird droppings. It followed a single colony back to its origin, which predated the walls, and forward to its leading edge, where the growth was pale and tentative, a millimeter of new tissue reaching outward into the air. It attended to the vole moving along the base of the wall, feeling the packed earth under its feet, the roots of the grasses it would eat later, the memory of a fox it would never meet. It attended to the wind.

The wind was the thing the power attended to most closely, because the wind was the being that lived here — the dominant organism, as the original text had said, though organism was not quite the right word. The wind was not alive in the biological sense. But it was alive in the sense that mattered here: it was a pattern that persisted, that responded to conditions, that shaped everything it touched. The wind was the closest thing the ridge had to a spirit, if spirit meant something that moved through the world without being diminished by it.

The power listened to the wind the way you would listen to someone telling you who they were — not for the information but for the sound of them existing. the particular music of their particular voice. which was, in the wind's case, a low continuous sough around stone.

---

While the power stayed, something shifted. Not in the ridge — the ridge does not shift, not in any way that could be measured or noticed. not in the lichen's growth rate or the walls' rate of settling. Nothing shifted in the power itself.

It had arrived at Ladhàin with an impulse toward benevolence that was also an impulse toward preservation, and impulse to guard and keep and maintain. to hold back the forces of dissolution. It had come from a tradition of interventions, of way of doctors come from a tradition of treatment, and way of builders come from a tradition of construction. Its nature was to improve, to mend, to restore, to fix.

And here it was at Ladhàin, there was nothing to improve. Nothing to mend. Nothing to restore. Nothing to fix. The place was complete.

 This was the revelation. and the power was more important than any action it could have taken. This was the thing that needed to be done, and thing the power was uniquely suited to do

---

So the power let its attention become the act.

It attended so completely that for a time — a morning, a particular morning, no different from any other morning — the attention became a kind of gift given without being given, a recognition offered without being requested, a presence registered without being announced

The condensation on the walls was that morning was a little heavier than usual. The lichen a little brighter, the wind a little slower to as if the air itself had been thickened with the weight of being seen

By mid-morning the the gift was received, The attention had been given, The moment had been completed, and power had moved on
