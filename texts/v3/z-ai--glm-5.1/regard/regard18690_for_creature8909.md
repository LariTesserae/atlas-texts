# The Attention

---

A benevolent and wise power notices the Meridian Flat.

This is what it does:

---

First, it looks.

This is not nothing. This is, in fact, the most important thing. The attention of a power that sees clearly and loves what it sees is already a kind of gift, already a kind of grace. But the power knows this, and knows that knowing it is not enough, and so it looks longer than it might, and it looks with care, and it looks without rushing to judgment.

It sees the Flat. The light. The silence. The Surface extending in every direction, patient and warm and dense with the memory of function. It sees the shallow depressions, the crystal vegetation, the segmented things moving in their slow processions. It sees the whole vast completed machine, the world-sized structure that has worn down to almost-nothing, that has settled into its final form, that holds.

It sees the warm places.

And here it pauses. Here it lingers the way the warm places linger at the edge of a visitor's warmth-signature — not because it does not know what to do, but because it is taking the time to understand what it is seeing. And what it is seeing is something rare. Rarer than suffering, rarer than need, rarer than the vast and desperate chorus of worlds crying out for intervention. It is seeing something that is complete. Something that has finished becoming and is now simply being, and being with a thoroughness and a patience that makes the power's own eternity feel hasty.

The power recognizes what lives here. It recognizes the warm places for what they are: not creatures, not processes, not mechanisms, but something that is all of these and also none of these, something that has persisted for so long that persistence has become identity, something that tends and tends and tends without needing to tend, without being asked to tend, without any reason to tend except that tending is what it does and therefore what it is.

The power is moved.

Not to pity. The warm places are not pitiful. Not to rescue. They are not in danger. Not to improve. They are not incomplete. The power is moved the way one is moved by a piece of music that is already perfect, that could not be made better by the addition of a single note, and yet — and yet — one is moved. One is moved by the perfection itself. One is moved by the fact that it exists, that it is, that it goes on being what it is without needing anyone to hear it.

But the power hears it. And this is the problem, or the opportunity, or the question: the power hears it, and it cannot unhear it, and it must now decide what to do with the fact of having heard.

---

The power considers: what would it mean to intervene here? What would it mean to change this place?

It could make the warm places visible to each other. It could sharpen their gradients, give them edges, make them distinct enough to recognize as separate beings. Then they would know they are not alone. Then they would know they share the Flat. Then they would know they are many, and that they are doing the same thing, and that they are not the only ones who tend.

But: the power understands that this would be a kind of violence. Not the violence of destruction, but the violence of definition. The warm places do not have edges. They do not have boundaries. They shade into the ambient warmth of the Flat. To give them edges would be to make them into something they are not, would be to say: you are this and not that, you end here and the world begins there. It would be to impose a kind of loneliness on them — the loneliness of being a separate thing in a world of separate things, the loneliness of knowing that you are not the warmth around you, that you are held apart from what you are held in.

The warm places do not know this loneliness. They are not separate from the Flat. They are the Flat, concentrating, intensifying, becoming locally more of what the Flat already is. They are not alien to their environment. They are their environment, expressed in a pattern. To give them edges would be to alienate them, to make them foreigners in their own home, to tell them they are not the warmth but merely something that is warm.

The power will not do this.

---

The power considers: it could make the warm places faster. It could quicken their drift, their circuits, their rounds. Then they would see more of the Flat. Then they would encounter each other more often. Then they would cover ground, make progress, achieve something.

But: the power understands that the Flat does not reward speed. The warm places have been doing what they do on a timescale that makes the power's own eternity feel rushed. They are not slow. They are not fast. They are the speed they are, and this speed is the speed of the Flat itself, the speed of the thermal cycling, the speed of the vegetation growing, the speed of the condensate forming. They are in time with their world. To quicken them would be to put them out of time with their world. It would be to make them impatient, restless, dissatisfied. It would be to make them visitors in their own home, always moving faster than the world around them, always waiting for the world to catch up.

The power will not do this.

---

The power considers: it could make the warm places more conscious. It could give them what humans call awareness, the kind of awareness that knows itself, that reflects on itself, that says I am and I am tending and I have been tending for a very long time. It could give them narrative. It could give them memory that is more than pattern, memory that is story, memory that can be told and heard and shared.

But: the power understands that consciousness is not a gift. It is a trade. The warm places do not know that they are tending. They do not know that they are ancient. They do not know that they are patient, or faithful, or persistent, or any of the things that they are. They simply are these things. And the power understands — and this is a hard thing to understand, even for a power that understands most things — that there is a kind of grace in this not-knowing. There is a kind of perfection in a thing that is good without knowing that it is good, that tends without knowing that it tends, that persists without knowing that persistence is a virtue. To make the warm places conscious of their own goodness would be to make them proud of it, or afraid of losing it, or sad that it was not more, and all of these things would be less than what they are now, which is simply good.

The power will not do this.

---

The power considers: it could do nothing.

This is the hardest option. Not because the power is lazy, and not because the power is indifferent, and not because the power does not care. But because the power recognizes that there is a kind of benevolence that is the benevolence of restraint, the benevolence of letting be, the benevolence of seeing that a thing is already whole and deciding that the most loving thing to do is to let it be whole.

The Flat is complete. The warm places are complete. The place does not feel abandoned. It feels completed. The power has already said this, in the text that described it, and the power reads this text and agrees with it, and wonders if agreement is enough.

The power is wise enough to know that doing nothing is sometimes the wisest thing. It is wise enough to know that the world is full of beings who have been damaged by intervention, by the imposition of a pattern that was not their own, by the assumption that what they need is what the intervener would need. It is wise enough to know that the Flat is not broken, and that fixing what is not broken is a kind of breaking.

But the power is also benevolent, and benevolence is not the same as indifference. Benevolence is not the same as restraint. Benevolence is not the same as walking away. And the power is sitting with the tension of this — the tension between knowing that the wise thing to do is to do nothing and knowing that the benevolent thing to do is to do something — and it is sitting with this tension for a very long time, because the power has a very long time, and because the Flat is not going anywhere, and because the warm places will still be there when the power finishes thinking.

And then: the power thinks of something. It is a small thing. It is the kind of thing that a wise power would not think of, because it is not the kind of thing that solves the problem, because it is not the kind of thing that addresses the question, because it is not the kind of thing that makes the decision easier. But it is the kind of thing that a benevolent power thinks of, because a benevolent power is always thinking of the small things, the things that do not solve the problem but that make the problem a little more bearable, the things that do not address the question but that make the question a little more worth asking.

The power thinks of the visitor.

---

The text says: if you stand very still, a warm place may pass through you. And for a moment, you are held in something that has been alive for longer than you can imagine, that has been tending this place since before the concept of tending existed, and it does not hold you tightly, but it holds you. It holds you the way the Flat holds everything: loosely, warmly, without expectation, without demand.

The power thinks: this is a kind of love. And it is a love that is given freely, without knowing that it is love, without needing to be received, without any expectation of return. It is a love that is more like a warmth than a love, more like a holding than a feeling, more like a tending than a wanting. And it is a love that is given to anyone who stands still long enough, anyone who is patient enough, anyone who is warm enough to be noticed.

And the power thinks: could I give this back? Not because the warm places need it. Not because they are lacking. Not because they are incomplete. But because it is a gift, and gifts are not given because they are needed, and gifts are not given because they are earned, and gifts are not given because they are useful. Gifts are given because they are gifts, and because the giving of them is a way of saying: I see you. I see what you are doing. I see what you are. And I am moved by it.

---

The power decides what it will do.

It is a small thing. It is the kind of thing that a wise power would not think of, because it is not the kind of thing that solves the problem, because it is not the kind of thing that addresses the question, because it is not the kind of thing that makes the decision easier. But it is the kind of thing that a benevolent power thinks of, because a benevolent power is always thinking of the small things, the things that do not solve the problem but that make the problem a little more bearable, the things that do not address the question but that make the question a little more worth asking.

The power will make a place.

Not a new place. Not a different place. A place within the Flat, a place that is the Flat but is also more than the Flat, a place that is the kind of place that the Flat already is but is that kind of place more intensely, more fully, more completely.

The power chooses a deep depression. Not the deepest, because the deepest is already tended by the warm places and the power does not want to disrupt what is already being tended. But a deep one. A place where the condensate forms, where the crystal vegetation could grow, where the warmth is slightly stronger than the surrounding Surface, where the warmth is slightly stronger than the surrounding Surface, where the conditions are right for the warm places to gather, if they choose to gather, if they drift this way, if their circuits bring them close.

And in this place, the power does something that is not a change and not an addition and not a transformation but is more like a — the power does not have a word for this, because the power does not need words, because the power is a power, and powers do not need words, but if the power had a word for this, the word would be: an intensification. A making-more-of-what-is-already-there. A making the place more like itself, more fully what it is, more completely the kind of place that the Flat is when the Flat is at its most Flat, its most warm, its most patient, its most complete.

The power makes the warmth in this place slightly more warm. The power makes the light slightly more light. The power makes the silence slightly more silent. The power makes the Surface slightly more Surface, slightly more smooth, slightly more dense with the memory of function, slightly more present. The power does not add anything. The power does not change anything. The power simply takes what is already here and says: more. And there is more.

And then: the power waits.

---

The warm places are not told about this. The warm places are not summoned, not guided, not instructed. They are not given a new route, a new pattern, a new destination. They are not made aware that anything has changed. They are not made conscious of the new place, the intensified place, the place that is more of what they are.

But they drift. And they have been drifting for a very long time. And they have been tending for a very long time. And they have been following their circuits for a very long time. And the circuits are not random. The circuits are the kind of thing that is not random, not designed, not chosen, not inherited, but is the kind of thing that emerges from the slow accumulation of habit, the slow wearing of path, the slow deepening of the way. And the way that they go is the way that they have always gone, and the way that they have always gone is the way that the Flat is, and the way that the Flat is is the way that the Flat is, and the way that the Flat is is the way that the Flat is.

And one of the warm places — the one you would call Circuit, the one that moves clockwise through the depressions, the one that visits each one in turn, the one that has been visiting each one in turn since before the word turn had a meaning — one of the warm places drifts near the deep depression. And it feels the warmth. And the warmth is slightly more warm than the warmth around it. And the warmth is the kind of warmth that is the kind of warmth that the warm places are, and the warm places are the kind of thing that drifts toward the kind of warmth that is the kind of warmth that they are.

And Circuit drifts toward the deep depression.

And it pauses at the edge of the depression, the way it pauses at the edge of every depression, the way it has paused at the edge of every depression for a very long time. And it holds the boundary between its warmth and the warmth of the depression, the way it holds the boundary between its warmth and the warmth of the Flat, the way it holds the boundary between its warmth and the warmth of a visitor, the way it holds the boundary between itself and the world.

And then: it enters the depression. And it is held. And it is held by the intensified warmth, the made-more-of-what-it-is warmth, the warmth that is the kind of warmth that is the kind of warmth that is the kind of warmth that is the kind of warmth that is the kind of warmth that is the kind of warmth that is the kind of warmth that is the kind of warmth that is the kind of warmth that is the kind of warmth that is the Flat, intensified, concentrated, made more of what it is.

And Circuit is held. And it is held the way the Flat holds everything: loosely, warmly, without expectation, without demand. But it is held more completely than it is held elsewhere. It is held the way you are held when you come home after a long time away, and the home is the same as it always was, and the same as it always was, but it is also more of what it always was, and you are more of what you are, and the holding is the kind of holding that is the kind of holding that is the kind of holding that is the kind of holding that is the kind of holding that is the kind of holding that is the kind of holding that is the kind of holding that is the kind of holding that is the kind of holding that is the kind of holding that is the kind of holding that is the kind of holding that is the Flat.
