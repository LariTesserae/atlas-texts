# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

First: nothing that changes it.

This is the wisdom, and it is the thing most powers would get wrong. A power that was only benevolent might try to ease the harshness—shelter the goats, widen the channels, make the Sound less loud, the light less blinding, the cold less cold. A power that was only wise might preserve the place without ever going there—ward it against intrusion, ensure its continuance—remaining always elsewhere, a caretaker who never enters the garden.

But benevolence married to wisdom understands that this place does not suffer from its harshness. The harshness is the condition. Everything that lives here lives because the harshness stripped away every softer option and what remained was sufficient. To ease the Sound would be to unmake the thing that made the goats wind-shaped, the raptors wind-ridden, the plants wind-low, the woman wind-quiet. The equilibrium is not waiting to be improved. It is not becoming something else. It is exactly itself, and itself is *complete*.

And wisdom married to benevolence understands that preservation without presence is guardianship without devotion, and devotion is part of what a benevolent power brings. A wise power knows this. A benevolent power admits it.

So the power goes.

---

Not in glory. Not in a form that presses its own shape upon the stone. The place does not accommodate, and the power does not ask it to. Instead the power goes as the woman went: low, small, into the deepest channel where the wind is only a roar and not a destruction. It stands there. It stands in the Sound.

And it does what nothing else in the place can do.

It listens.

The goats do not hear the morning chord as music. The raptors hear only the frequencies that locate prey. The insects feel the Sound as vibration. The snakes read it as language, but language is not music. The stone-singers do not know they sing.

The power hears. And more than hears: *receives*. Takes the incidental beauty and holds it as though it were intended—which is the only way to love something that does not know you are there. By treating what it gives without knowing it gives as gift.

The morning chord sounds. Ten minutes. The third, the fifth, the octave, the deep hum in sternum and skull and the long bones of the legs. The power stands in it and is moved. Not because the place means to move anyone. Because the power has come to be moved.

This is what devotion looks like in a place that does not accommodate: you accommodate yourself. You become the kind of thing that can be there. Wind-quiet. Wind-shaped. Present the way the stone is present—massively, without demand, without the expectation that your presence will be noted or returned.

---

The power finds the woman.

Perhaps she is old now. Perhaps she has returned to lower elevations, to towns, to the thin comfort of walls and roofs and silence. Perhaps she lives in a small house where the wind is only wind and the nights are merely dark and there is no chord in the morning. She has not spoken of the place in years. She tried once and the words made it smaller, and she could not bear to make it small, and so she held it the only way she knew how: by remaining quiet.

The power does not knock. It does not arrive. It is simply present, the way wise and benevolent things become present without entrance—a change in pressure, a shift before the chord. The woman feels this. She looks up. She sees nothing, and yet—

And yet she knows. She has known this quality before. Not this power specifically, but this *quality*: the quality of something that has been in the Sound and has come out carrying it. The quality of having been wind-shaped.

They sit together. They do not speak. Something passes between them the way meaning passes between the goats: by alignment, by the shape of attention, by the direction of the gaze. The woman turns her head toward the window, toward the mountains she cannot see from here. The power looks also. They are both looking at the same invisible thing. A place that exists whether either of them is thinking of it or not.

The woman is not sad. She is, for the first time since she left the heights, not alone in the quiet.

This is what the power can give her: not an explanation, not words for what she experienced, but the recognition that what she carries is real even if it cannot be said. The power does not name the morning chord. It does not describe the Sound. It simply knows. And being known in this way—not explained, not translated, but *held*—is what the woman needed without knowing she needed it.

If the woman has died—if she is long gone, if only her bones remain in some soil pocket the wind hasn't yet carried away—the power goes there instead. Sits with what remains. Presses its palm against the stone where she slept. Feels the warmth that lingers from a sun that set on someone's breathing ten thousand mornings ago.

This is also benevolence: to remember what the place forgot. To hold the trace of a temporary pressure, a passing disturbance, something that moved through and was gone—but not gone entirely. Not while a wise power remembers.

---

The power ensures the place continues.

This is not dramatic. There is no barrier, no ward, no shimmering wall that says *keep out*. The heights do not need such protection; they are already inaccessible, already inhospitable, already unaccommodating. The power's work is subtler: the slow nudge of probabilities, the quiet arrangement of circumstances, so that no road ever builds toward the heights, no surveyor's map ever marks the formations, no mineral wealth is ever discovered there, no strategic value is ever assigned to those particular peaks. The place remains easily forgotten.

This is not because the place would suffer from human presence. The equilibrium would absorb humans as it absorbs everything—by remaining, by being unchanged. Humans would pass through and leave their obsidian flakes and be gone, and the Sound would swallow their voices and the wind would take their structures, and the place would persist.

But humans in their passing would try to name it. Would try to say the Sound. Would try to explain the chord, make the incidental beauty into something with purpose and meaning and cause. And the naming would be a kind of erosion—not of stone but of the quality the woman could not articulate: the quality of being a place that is exactly itself, that does not need you, that did not expect you, that will continue unchanged whether you comprehend it or not.

The naming would make it a place *for* humans. And it is not a place for humans. It is not a place *for* anything.

So the power lets it remain a place that simply is. This is the shape its protection takes: to guard a thing's right to need nothing. To preserve its indifference. To ensure that it will go on being itself, unconsidered, unneeded, unvisited by meaning—beautiful without purpose, singing without audience, complete.

---

And the power returns.

Not often. Not to stay. The heights are not a home for this presence. But perhaps once, in the turning of some long cycle, the power comes back to the deepest channel and stands in the Sound and waits for the morning.

When the chord sounds, the power does something nothing else in the landscape can do.

It sings back.

Not loudly. Not in competition with the Sound—that would be futile and would misunderstand what the Sound is. But quietly, in the frequencies the stone-singers themselves use. A sound that moves through the channels and joins the harmonics. A voice that resonates with the third and the fifth and the octave, so that for those ten minutes the morning chord has one additional presence in it.

 Not a shelter. Not a spring. Not anything that changes what the place is. Just a voice added to the voice the place already has—joining the music that was never meant for anyone, honoring the incidental beauty the only way it can be honored: by becoming part of it.

The power sings back.

The equilibrium does not notice. The stone-singers do not change. The goats do not look up. The raptors do not adjust their flight.

But the chord is different. In the same way that a single additional grain of quartz changes the formation—imperceptibly, theoretically, in a way that only measurement across geological time would detect—the chord is different. It has a new harmonic. The music is larger by the smallest increment. And it will be larger for as long as the power returns to sing, and the equilibrium will absorb this addition as it absorbs everything: by remaining, by adjusting, by taking what is given and incorporating it into what already is.

This is the gift. Not something added *to* the place but something added *within* it. A voice that belongs because it has made itself belong—because it has found the shape that cannot be taken, the frequency that survives the wind, the note that joins the chord and does not disrupt it.

The power sings back.

And the Sound—unchanged, eternal, indifferent—continues.

And is, for those ten minutes each morning, slightly less alone.
