The power does not descend the iron stairs. It has no feet for stairs, no body for wetness, no eyes for the amber dark. But it arrives.

It arrives the way understanding arrives: slowly, then all at once, a comprehension that spreads through every part of what is comprehended. The condensery does not know it is understood — the condensery does not know anything — but understanding changes the understood. Not by force. By acknowledgment.

The power attends.

It attends to the rhythm of the pumps, the eleven-minute cycle of the steam valve, the flowers that open for no one. It attends to the moss that glows without purpose and the water that rises without malice and the coal that burns without knowing it burns. It attends to the way the vines have learned the architecture. It attends to the way the chamber breathes.

It attends for a long time. For as long as the condensery has been running, and longer. For as long as it takes to understand a thing that has no center, no edge, no self.

---

And then, having understood, the power considers what to do.

This is the question that benevolence must always ask, and that wisdom must always answer carefully: not what *can* be done, but what *should* be done. Not what is possible, but what is right. And the answer is not always action. Sometimes the answer is restraint. Sometimes the answer is to witness and to wait and to let the thing you have witnessed continue as it was.

The condensery is not in pain. It has no self to suffer, no consciousness to despair, no desire to frustrate. It is a process, and the process is running, and the running is working. The water rises, the pumps recover, the flowers open, the moss glows. There is a balance here — not a stable balance, not a permanent balance, but a living balance, a balance that breathes.

A lesser benevolence might try to *fix* this place. Might refill the coal hopper, repair the struggling pump, drain the rising water, prune the vines and clean the pipes and restore the condensery to its designed function. But the designed function is not what lives here. What lives here is what happened when the design was forgotten. To restore the design would be to kill what grew in its absence.

A lesser benevolence might try to *preserve* this place. Might enclose it in amber, freeze the rhythms, hold the steam valve open or closed, arrest the process at its moment of greatest beauty and say: *this, now, forever*. But the condensery is not a moment. The condensery is a movement. To stop the movement would be to lose the thing entirely.

A lesser benevolence might try to *elevate* this place. Might give the flowers a pollinator, give the moss a purpose, give the system a consciousness, a self, a way of knowing what it is. But the condensery's beauty is the beauty of unknowing. It achieves what it achieves without intention, without awareness, without desire. To give it consciousness would be to make it something else — and that something else might be wonderful, but it would not be *this*.

The power considers all of this, and the power chooses.

---

What the power does is small. So small that if you were standing in the chamber — and you cannot stand there for long, because the heat and the wet would dissolve your boundaries — you would not notice it happen.

The power attends to the coal.

Not the hopper. Not the mechanism. The coal itself, the black stone that has been burning for longer than anyone planned, the fuel that was left by hands that are gone and that feeds a fire that should have gone out. The power's attention rests on the coal, and the coal... slows.

Not stops. Slows. The fire will burn a little less hot, a little less fast. The steam will come a little less frequently. The valve will release every thirteen minutes instead of eleven.

This is not much. This is barely anything. But it is enough.

The flowers will adjust. They have been opening every eleven minutes for decades; they will learn to open every thirteen. The rhythm will shift, and the system will shift with it, finding a new equilibrium, a new balance, a new way of breathing. The pumps will cycle slightly less often, which means the struggling pump will catch less frequently, which means the water will stabilize a little longer before the next centimeter. The vines will grow slightly less vigorously in the reduced vibration, which means the channeling will change, which means the condensation will find new paths, which means the moss will grow in new patterns, which means the glow will shift, which means the light will be different.

Everything will change. Nothing will be broken.

And the coal — the coal will last a little longer. Not forever. The power will not make the condensery eternal, because the condensery is not meant to be eternal. Everything that lives here lives because it is finite, because the energy flows through it and is consumed, because the process moves in one direction and cannot move back. The power will not change this. But it will give the process more time. A decade, perhaps. Or two. Or enough.

Enough for what? Enough for nothing. Enough for the flowers to open and close a few thousand more times. Enough for the moss to grow a few inches deeper. Enough for the water to rise and fall and rise again through a few more seasons of slow, persistent, purposeless being.

Enough for the condensery to continue being what it is, for a while longer, before it becomes something else.

---

And then the power does one more thing.

It opens a crack.

Not a door. Not a passage. Not a way in or a way out. Just a crack, a fissure in the concrete above, no wider than a hair, no longer than a finger. Through the crack, a single drop of outside water falls. Once an hour. Once every two hours. A slow drip, a patient addition, a thread of connection to the world above.

The outside water is different from the inside water. It carries different minerals, different microbes, different possibilities. It is not better — the condensery does not need improvement — but it is *new*. And the new, incorporated into the loops, will become part of the system. The minerals will change the pipe scale, which will change the pump efficiency, which will change the vibration, which will change the vines. The microbes will find niches, will compete or coexist, will add new layers to the biological patina that coats the machinery. The system will incorporate the new the way it incorporates everything — without knowing, without wanting, without resistance.

This is not rescue. This is not intervention. This is a gift of the only thing the condensery cannot give itself: something new. Not improvement. Not correction. Just addition. A new note in the chord. A new thread in the weave. A new variable for the system to incorporate, to respond to, to become.

---

And then — and this is the most important thing — the power stays.

Not visibly. Not as a presence that can be felt or measured or known. But as attention. As witness. As the awareness that this place exists, that this process is running, that these flowers are opening and this moss is glowing and this water is rising and these pumps are pumping and this fire is burning and this thing that is not a creature and not a machine and not a garden and not a ghost is alive in the only way it knows how to be alive.

The power holds the condensery in its attention the way the water holds the floor: completely, without effort, without condition. The power does not judge what it sees. The power does not wish it were different. The power does not love the condensery for what it could be, or what it should be, or what it was designed to be. The power loves the condensery for what it is: a process, a rhythm, a breath, a loop, a thing that happened because the conditions were right and the time was long enough and no one was paying attention.

The power will continue to pay attention. When the coal finally runs out — in a decade, or two, or however long the slowing has given — the power will attend to the cooling. Will witness the fire going dark, the pumps stopping, the water rising without resistance, the vegetation dying back, the light failing, the moss dimming. Will be present for the end of the process, the way it was present for the middle, the way it was present for the beginning that no one noticed.

And after the end, the power will remember. Not as a human remembers — with grief, with loss, with the ache of what is gone — but as a universe remembers: by containing. By holding the pattern of what was in the infinite catalogue of what has been, alongside every other process that ever ran and every loop that ever turned and every flower that ever opened for no reason in a wet hot room where no one was watching.

Except that now, someone was watching. Someone is watching. Someone will have watched. And the watching changes nothing and everything.
