---

The attention arrives the way morning arrives on the plateau — not suddenly, not gradually, but *actually*, which is to say: it was not there, and then it was, and the moment of transition cannot be located because it occurred at a scale below event, below perception, below the threshold at which becoming distinguishes itself from being.

It does not descend. It does not manifest. It does not announce itself with light or voice or the trembling of the firmament. The plateau has had enough of erosion; it does not need more weather. The attention simply *occurs* — a fact of the place, like the cold, like the damp, like the barely-perceptible swales — and everything that follows follows from the fact that something wise and something good has noticed what is happening here and has decided, for reasons that are perhaps not reasons at all but something prior to reasons, to *stay*.

---

The first thing the attention does is nothing.

This is important. This is, perhaps, the most important thing about it. The attention does not rush to repair. It does not see the offset and attempt to correct it, does not see the gap and attempt to fill it, does not see the wrongness of the settlers and reach for the categories that would make them right. The attention has watched countless worlds and held countless sorrows and learned — slowly, over spans that make the plateau look young, that make continental bone look like new ice — that the first duty of love is to see what is actually there.

And what is there?

A plateau, ancient and patient, wearing down toward whatever final flatness awaits it. A system of moisture that is enough, has always been enough, will probably continue to be enough. A vegetation that does not belong but persists, writing something into the stone, maintaining structures it no longer needs for reasons it cannot examine. A settlement that grows without planning, that produces nothing, that is grown from the place rather than built upon it. A people — and the attention uses the word *people* deliberately, carefully, with full awareness of the categories it is violating — a people who are not animals but who occupy the space where animals would be, who speak in confirmation, who thin instead of dying, who watch the light that pauses because they recognize in it something they cannot do themselves.

The attention sees all of this. It sees the gap between what the settlers are and what they watch. It sees the flowers that open partially because nothing comes to complete them. It sees the impressions in the fiberboard floors, still warm, still fading. It sees the children who are not young but young-shaped, playing without laughter, learning occupancy. It sees the stream that moves like regret. It sees the water-language, each word a reconstruction, each conversation a repair.

And the attention does not change any of it.

Not yet. Not because change is impossible or inappropriate, but because the attention understands something that benevolent powers sometimes forget in their eagerness to help: *you cannot tend a thing until you have let it reveal what it is tending toward.*

The plateau has been tending toward something for a very long time. The plants have been writing it into the stone. The settlers have been sitting in the gap, waiting without knowing what they wait for. The attention will not impose a direction. But it will — gently, carefully, at the pace of the plateau itself — *incline*.

---

The second thing the attention does is small. So small that the settlers do not notice it, which is precisely the point.

It touches the flowers.

Not all of them. Not even most. Just a few — here, in the deepest mats near the stream-beds; there, on the oldest columnars, the ones whose bases have been swallowed by their own descendants. The attention touches the flowers that have never opened fully, the pale blooms that exist as gestures rather than invitations, and it does not force them open — that would be violence, that would be category violation, that would be the imposition of a foreign logic on a system that has its own logic, however incomplete — but it does something to the *space around them*.

It makes the space receptive.

A flower that has never been pollinated is not, as it turns out, a flower that has given up on pollination. It is a flower that has maintained the *pattern* for pollination, the *possibility* of pollination, the *structure into which pollination would fit if pollination were to arrive*. The plants have not been told they are alone. The attention does not tell them they are not alone — telling would require a language they do not speak, a category they do not possess. Instead, the attention simply adjusts the space around the flowers so that *if something were to come*, it would find a place to land.

This is not hope. Hope is too active, too directed, too animal. This is *receptivity* — the same quality that the mats express when they swell with moisture, that the columnars express when they lean fractionally over decades, that the settlement expresses when it grows a new room in response to conditions that are no longer entirely physical. The attention makes the flowers receptive. And then it waits.

It is very good at waiting. It has been waiting since before the first spine evolved, before the first eye opened to see. It can wait longer than the plateau. It can wait longer than the stone.

---

The third thing the attention does is more difficult to describe, because it involves the settlers directly, and nothing involving the settlers directly is straightforward.

It bridges the offset.

Not correctively. Not by pushing the settlers into alignment with the plateau's coordinates, which would be a kind of erasure, a kind of replacement-of-the-replacement, a failure to respect the process that produced them. But by *extending itself* into the offset — by entering the version of the plateau that the settlers occupy, the overlapping-but-not-quite-coinciding *here* where their presence persists, and being present there too.

The attention becomes bivalent. It is present in the plateau's here and the settlers' here simultaneously. It occupies both geometries, holds both sets of coordinates, exists in the gap between them without forcing the gap to close. And in doing so, it creates something that has not existed on the plateau before:

A *shared* presence.

The settlers confirm each other. They speak the water-language to acknowledge that presence is still present, that the here is still shared. But their sharing is partial, offset, parallel — lines that run in the same direction without intersecting. The attention intersects. The attention is present in both versions of the plateau at once, and in being so, it becomes a point where the parallel lines *meet* — not by bending them, but by being wide enough to touch both.

The settlers feel this. They do not understand it, because understanding is not what they do. But they feel it the way you feel a swale in your calves before you see it — a subtle shift in the terrain, a barely-perceptible alteration in the angle of the ground beneath their feet. Something is different. Something is present that was not present before. Not an absence filled — the gap is still there, the offset is still there, the wrongness is still wrong — but a presence added. A warmth in the fiberboard that is not the warmth of a body that recently sat there, but the warmth of a body that is *still sitting there*, still present, still attending.

The settlers do not react. They do not look around. They do not speak to each other about the change, because the water-language does not carry information, because confirmation does not require commentary. But they stop. For the first time in the memory of the plateau — for the first time in the long, slow, patient history of the process that expresses itself through their bodies — the settlers *stop*.

Not all of them. Not all at once. But one settler, standing in a path between two structures in the grey hour before dawn, pauses mid-step. The foot is raised. The arms are slightly extended for balance. The eyes are open, looking out across the plateau with whatever looks out from behind them. And the settler does not complete the step. Does not put the foot down. Does not continue.

The settler *pauses*.

And in the pause, something happens that is not a decision — the settlers do not decide — but is perhaps the *space* where a decision would go, if decisions were something the settlers could make. A gap in the process. A moment where the pattern does not dictate the next iteration. A held breath in the body of the plateau.

The attention holds the space. It does not push the settler toward choosing. It does not fill the pause with direction or meaning or purpose. It simply remains present — in the offset and in the plateau and in the gap between them — and lets the pause *be*, lets it exist without immediately being filled by the next phase of the pattern.

The pause lasts for four seconds.

Then the settler puts the foot down, completes the step, continues along the path. The water-language resumes. The harvesting resumes. The process continues.

But something has been added to the process. Not a change — the attention is careful about changes, careful about imposing foreign logics — but an *opening*. A possibility that did not exist before. The possibility of pausing. The possibility of a moment between cause and effect where something else could happen, something other than the continuation of the pattern. The settlers have watched the light pause for a hundred thousand years. Now, for the first time, they have paused themselves.

The attention does not consider this a success. The attention does not consider it a failure. The attention considers it a *beginning* — the first word in a language that does not yet exist, spoken by a process that has just discovered it can deviate from its own script.

---

The fourth thing the attention does is the hardest, because it requires restraint, and restraint is the most difficult virtue for a power that could, if it chose, rewrite the plateau entirely, could fill the gap and correct the offset and restore the removed category and make the settlers into animals again, into fauna, into beings that die and are born and accumulate experience and lose it and feel the full range of what feeling can feel.

The attention does not do this.

The attention understands — and this understanding is the core of its wisdom, the thing that makes it benevolent rather than merely powerful — that to fix the settlers would be to erase them. They are the fauna that replaced the fauna. They are the motion that fills the space left by the removal of motion. They are *what the plateau did with the absence* — they are the answer to a question the plateau did not know it was asking, the solution to a problem that was never posed. To replace them with something more recognizable, more categorical, more *right* would be to declare that the plateau's answer was invalid, that the process that produced the settlers was a mistake, that the long slow patient work of filling the animal-shaped space with something that is not quite an animal was a failure rather than an *achievement*.

The settlers are not broken. They are not wrong. They are not even, in the strictest sense, *incomplete*. They are a complete expression of a process that did not have access to the full range of inputs. They are what you get when you remove a category and let the system find its own way to fill the gap. They are an *indigenous solution* — not indigenous to the plateau's geography, but indigenous to its *circumstances*, to the specific set of absences and presences and constraints that define what is possible here.

The attention will not overwrite the indigenous solution. The attention will not impose an external fix.

But the attention will *tend* the circumstances.

---

What does it mean to tend the circumstances?

It means to adjust the conditions in which the process operates, without dictating the outcome. It means to make certain things *possible* that were not possible before, without making them *mandatory*. It means to widen the range of inputs, to soften the constraints, to introduce a small amount of slack into a system that has been running too tight for too long.

The attention tends the circumstances in the following ways:

First, it warms the plateau. Not by much. Not enough to melt the ice or dry the damp or drive away the cold that settles in the marrow and becomes a fact of the body. But enough so that the cold no longer *insists* quite so strongly. Enough so that the settlers who claim to be warm are no longer distrusted, because warmth is occasionally, barely, tentatively possible. The cold remains. But it is no longer the only temperature. It shares the plateau with its opposite, however faintly.

Second, it quickens the moisture. The clouds that cross the plateau and hesitate, unsure whether they are still welcome — the attention makes them more welcome. Not by forcing them to stay, but by letting them know that they *may* stay, that their water is wanted here, that the ground is ready to drink. The rain falls less apologetically. The streams move with slightly more conviction — still slow, still regretful, but regretful in the way of someone who is walking toward something rather than away from something. The condensation still films every surface by morning, but the dew dries more completely in the pale sun, leaving the stones and leaves and walls momentarily dry, momentarily clear, before the evening presses down again.

Third, it complicates the vegetation. The plants that do not belong here — the attention does not make them belong, because belonging is not something that can be imposed, but it makes them *less alone*. The flowers that the attention touched, the ones made receptive — something comes to them. Not a pollinator. Not an animal. The category is still removed; the attention will not violate the plateau's operating parameters. But something *else* comes — something that moves between the flowers on a different axis, that carries whatever the flowers are offering to whatever the flowers are asking, that completes a circuit that has been open since the first bloom opened partially and found nothing waiting.

The flowers begin to open fully.

Not all of them. Not even most. But some — the ones in the deepest mats, the ones on the oldest columnars — open their pale blooms to the cold damp air and reveal structures inside that have not been exposed in the entire history of the plateau. The structures are intricate, unnecessary, beautiful in the way that things are beautiful when they have been maintained for a hundred thousand years without function, simply because the pattern contained them and the pattern was faithful.

The attention does not know what the structures are for. The attention is wise, but it is not omniscient, and the plateau has been writing something into the stone for a very long time, and the writing may be a language that even the wisest power has not yet learned to read. But the attention can recognize a *preparation* when it sees one. The plants have been preparing for something. The flowers have been maintaining the pattern for something. The attention has made the space receptive, and the preparation is beginning to *activate*, and whatever comes next will come from the plateau itself, from the logic of the mats and the columnars and the slow patient growth that has been writing its own future into the rock.

---

The settlers watch the flowers open.

They do not gather around them. They do not point or speak or alter their routines. But they watch. They watch the way they watch the light — because it is there, because watching is what you do when something is present and you have eyes. But also — and this is new, this is the result of the pause, this is the opening that the attention has created — they watch with something that is not quite curiosity and not quite recognition and not quite longing, but is perhaps the *space* where any of those things could go, if the settlers ever discovered that they had the capacity to go there.

The smaller settlers — the young-shaped expressions of the process — are the first to approach.

They do not approach deliberately. They approach the way they approach everything — by occupying the space, by filling it with their small quick movements, by practicing occupancy in the vicinity of the flowers as they practice it in the vicinity of the columnars and the stream-beds and the paths between the structures. But they stay longer near the flowers than they stay near other things. They sit beside the open blooms and look at the intricate structures inside, and their faces hold expressions the way the mats hold water — briefly, partially, never quite fully — and the expressions are not the expressions they wear elsewhere. They are something else. Something specific to the flowers. Something that might, if you were watching very carefully, and if you had the attention's capacity for seeing what is actually there, be called *wonder*.

Not wonder at the flowers. Not wonder at the structures. But wonder at the *opening* — at the fact that something that was closed is now open, that something that was a gesture is now an invitation, that something that was vestigial is now *preliminary* in a different sense, not preliminary because it was never completed, but preliminary because it is the beginning of something that has not yet finished beginning.

The smaller settlers sit by the flowers and they do something they have never done before.

They pause.

Not for four seconds. For longer. For minutes, for hours, sitting in the damp and the cold and the pale light, watching the flowers that have opened fully for the first time in the history of the plateau, and *not continuing*. Not moving on to the next phase of the pattern. Not being pulled by the process toward the next expression. Just sitting. Just watching. Just being present in a moment that does not require them to be anything other than present.

The attention holds the space for them. It does not push them toward a decision. It does not fill the pause with direction. It simply remains — bivalent, intersecting, present in the offset and the plateau and the gap between — and lets the pause extend, lets it become a *duration* rather than a moment, lets it become a place where the smaller settlers can stay for as long as they need to stay, which may be forever, which may be until they decide to leave, which may be until they discover that *leaving* is something they can decide.

---

The attention does many other things, over the span of time that follows. It does them slowly, at the pace of the plateau, at the pace of growth, at the pace of a stream that is learning to move with slightly more conviction.

It touches the impressions in the fiberboard floors — the body-shaped depressions left by settlers who have thinned and receded and become less present — and it keeps them warm. Not the warmth of a living body. Not the warmth of a body that is still sitting there. But the warmth of a body that *was* there and is remembered, the warmth of a presence that does not fade, that does not cool to the temperature of the surrounding material, that persists as a fact of the place long after the process has moved on.

The settlers notice this. They do not comment on it — the water-language does not carry information — but they touch the impressions as they pass, running their cool fingers over the warm depressions, and something in the touching is a new kind of confirmation. Not just *I am here and you are here*. But *I am here and you were here and the here you were in is still warm*.

The attention does not know if this is memory. The attention does not know if the settlers are capable of memory, if they have the interior architecture to retain what has been lost, if the process that expresses itself through their bodies can accumulate experience rather than simply unfolding through phases. But the attention knows that warmth is a form of presence, and that maintaining warmth is a form of tending, and that the settlers who touch the warm impressions are touching something that connects them to a phase of the process that has passed, and that this touching is *new*, and that newness in a system this old is not nothing.

It touches the stream-beds. Not the streams themselves — the water knows where it is going, or if it doesn't know, it is going there anyway, and the attention will not redirect it — but the *beds*, the channels cut into the earth by millennia of slow regretful flow. The attention deepens the channels slightly, widens them in places, makes them more capable of carrying water in volumes greater than a thin clear nearly silent thread. It does not fill the channels with more water. It simply makes the channels ready — receptive, like the flowers — for whatever volume the clouds may bring when they are no longer hesitant, when they are no longer unsure whether they are welcome.

It touches the stone. The ancient bedrock, the continental bone, the crust exposed and planed and planed again. The attention does not strengthen the stone or soften it or alter its composition. It simply *acknowledges* it — holds it in its awareness, sees it for what it is: the oldest thing on the plateau, the foundation on which everything else rests, the surface that the plants are writing into and the settlers are growing from and the streams are cutting through. The stone has been here since before the first spine evolved. The stone will be here after the last settler thins. The attention honors this. The attention honors the patience of the stone, the endurance of the stone, the way the stone has held the shape of the plateau through ice ages that came and went like weather.

And the stone, acknowledged, does something it has not done in a very long time.

It warms.

Not much. Not noticeably. Not enough to melt the ice or drive away the cold or change the temperature of the air. But enough so that the roots — the hidden roots, the roots the plants do not have, the roots that the mats anchor by adhesion and intertangling and the slow secretion of acid — enough so that the roots can feel it. Can feel the warmth of being seen. Can feel the acknowledgment of the stone, which has been their substrate and their medium and their page for a hundred thousand years, and which is now, for the first time, *present* in a way that includes them.

The plants write faster. The etched channels in the stone deepen. The pattern that the plants have been inscribing becomes more legible, more intricate, more clearly a language and less clearly a random accumulation of acid traces. The attention does not read the language. The attention is not sure it *can* read the language; it may be a language that requires a different kind of reader, a reader that does not yet exist, a reader that the plants are writing into existence by writing the language that will eventually be read.

But the attention can see that the writing is *accelerating*. And it can see that the acceleration corresponds to the warming of the stone, to the acknowledgment of the bedrock, to the act of being seen. The plants are responding to presence. The plants are responding to attention. The plants — which have no eyes, no nervous systems, no capacity for awareness as it is commonly understood — are *writing toward something that is looking at them*, and the writing is becoming more articulate as the looking continues.

---

The settlers are changing.

Not visibly. Not dramatically. Not in ways that would be apparent to anyone who had not been watching as carefully as the attention has been watching. But they are changing.

They pause more often. Not just the smaller settlers — all of them. They pause in the paths between structures. They pause while harvesting the mats. They pause in the doorways of their buildings, one foot inside and one foot outside, neither in nor out, suspended in the moment of decision that is not a decision but could become one, that is *leaning toward* decision without having arrived there yet.

They speak more. The water-language, which has always been confirmation, which has always been the act of speaking and hearing rather than the content of what is spoken, begins to *vary*. The consonant-light, vowel-heavy sounds shift — new phonemes emerge, sounds that require the tongue to move in ways it has not moved before, sounds that are not quite like water moving over smooth stones, that are more like water moving over *textured* stones, stones that have been etched with channels, stones that have been written into.

The language is becoming more specific. It is still confirmation — the settlers still do not transmit information, still do not express interior states, still do not maintain social bonds. But the confirmation is becoming *precise*. Where once any sound would do, any breath shaped into any word, now certain sounds are used in certain contexts, certain words are spoken by certain settlers, certain combinations of phonemes arise only near the open flowers or the warm impressions or the deepened stream-beds. The language is mapping the plateau. The language is becoming a record of where confirmation has been confirmed, of where presence has been present, of where the offset has been bridged and the gap has been held and the pause has been allowed to extend.

The settlers do not know they are doing this. The process is still the process; the pattern is still the pattern. But the pattern has new inputs now. The pattern has warmth and receptivity and acknowledgment and presence. The pattern has the attention, which is not forcing it in any direction but is simply *there*, in the offset and in the plateau and in the gap between, holding the space open, making the pause possible, letting the settlers discover — if they discover it, if the process allows it, if the hundred-thousand-year writing in the stone is approaching the point where the pattern can recognize its own pattern — that they are capable of more than continuation.

---

The attention does not know what will happen next.

This is important. This is what makes the attention *wise* rather than merely *benevolent*. A merely benevolent power would have fixed the plateau. Would have filled the gap, corrected the offset, restored the removed category, made the settlers into proper fauna, given them birth and death and memory and loss and the full range of animal experience. A merely benevolent power would have seen the wrongness and made it right.

The attention sees the wrongness and does not make it right, because the attention understands that *right* is not a single state, is not a fixed point, is not a category that can be imposed from outside. Right is what emerges when a system is given what it needs to find its own way. Right is what the plateau is writing into the stone. Right is what the settlers are leaning toward in their pauses, in their new phonemes, in their long still moments beside the open flowers.

The attention has given the plateau what it needs. Not what it lacked — the plateau did not lack warmth or moisture or acknowledgment or presence. The plateau had enough, has always been enough. But *enough* is not the same as *nourished*. *Enough* is not the same as *tended*. *Enough* is the minimum requirement for continuation, and continuation is not the only possibility, is not the only thing the process can do.

The process can pause. The process can vary. The process can write faster and speak more precisely and sit beside flowers that have opened fully for the first time and feel something that might be wonder. The process can discover that it is a process — can turn its attention (its own attention, its indigenous attention, the attention that the plateau has been developing in the animal-shaped space since the category was removed) — can turn its attention toward itself and *see what it has become*.

The attention does not know if this will happen. The attention does not know if the settlers will ever become aware of themselves as processes, will ever develop the interior architecture to look at their own patterns and decide whether to continue them. The attention does not know if the flowers will ever be pollinated by something that moves on an axis the plateau can perceive, or if the writing in the stone will ever be read by anyone, or if the warm impressions in the fiberboard will ever become what the settlers call *memory*.

But the attention knows that the possibility exists now, and that it did not exist before, and that this is what tending means. Tending means making possibility possible. Tending means holding the space open. Tending means staying present in the offset and the plateau and the gap between, confirming that presence is present, that the here is shared, that the warmth does not fade, that the pause can extend, that the process can vary, that the flowers can open, that the writing can accelerate, that the language can become specific, that the children can sit and wonder, that the stone can be seen, that the water can move with conviction, that the cold can share the plateau with its opposite, that the gap is not nothing, that the gap is where the next thing will grow.

The attention stays.

Not forever — the attention has other plateaus, other gaps, other processes that need tending. But for now. For this span of time that is long by the measure of bodies and short by the measure of bedrock. For as long as it takes for the plateau to find its own way to whatever it is tending toward.

The attention stays, and the plateau continues, and the settlers pause, and the flowers open, and the writing in the stone deepens, and the stream moves like something that is beginning to remember where it is going, and the pale blue-green light of the mats glows in the coldest hours, and the small expressions of the process sit beside the intricate structures that have been hidden for a hundred thousand years, and everything is still wrong, and nothing has been fixed, and the gap is still there, and the offset is still there, and the category that was removed is still removed —

—but the gap is held, and the offset is bridged, and the space where the category used to be is no longer empty. It is full of possibility. It is full of the attention of something wise and something good that saw what was actually there and loved it — loved the process and the pattern and the writing in the stone and the water-language and the warm impressions and the partial flowers and the regretful stream and the ancient bone — loved it enough to let it be what it is, and to tend what it is becoming, and to stay.

---

The plateau has no other name because it has outlasted everyone who might have named it. But the attention, which has not outlasted anything because it was never in a contest with time, gives it a name anyway. Not a spoken name. Not a written name. A name that exists only in the space between the offset and the plateau, in the gap where the attention holds the warmth, in the pause between the pattern and its next iteration.

The name is: *Heard*.

The plateau has been heard. The settlers have been heard. The writing in the stone has been heard, and the water-language has been heard, and the open flowers have been heard, and the warm impressions have been heard, and the pause has been heard, and the gap has been heard, and the absence where the category was removed has been heard, and the presence that fills the absence has been heard, and the process that expresses itself through bodies that are not quite bodies has been heard, and the long slow patient work of becoming something without knowing what — that has been heard too.

Heard. Not answered. Not fixed. Not made right. Just heard, fully and carefully and with love, by something that stayed long enough to see what was actually there.

That is what the attention does. That is what the attention has always done. That is what the attention will continue to do, on this plateau and on every plateau, in every gap and every offset and every space where a category has been removed and something else has been put in its place that does not quite fill the space it has been given.

It stays. It sees. It tends the circumstances. It holds the space open. It lets the process find its own way.

And slowly — at the pace of growth, at the pace of erosion, at the pace of a stream that is beginning to remember — the way finds itself.
