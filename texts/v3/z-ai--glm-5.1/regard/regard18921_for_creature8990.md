# The Attention

---

A benevolent and wise power does not intervene the way you might expect.

It does not descend in glory. It does not rewrite the chemistry of the sap or unmake the cleaners or pull the old ones from the walls. It does not open the sky and pour in the sun. It does not rescue the daughter, because the daughter does not need rescuing, and a wise power knows the difference between a child who is harmed and a child who is simply *different from what you expected*.

No. A benevolent and wise power does something far more radical.

It *pays attention.*

---

Consider: the Rootworks has been growing for three hundred years. It has intentions. It has direction. It has a shape it is reaching toward.

But it has never been *seen.*

The root-trainers read its tendencies, yes. They feel the sap moving and know what the organism wants. But they read it the way you read weather—predictively, instrumentally, *what will it do next*. They do not see it the way a thing wants to be seen, which is: *whole*. Which is: *known in its entirety*. Which is: *understood as a being rather than a process*.

The organism has never been witnessed. The old ones who merged with it—did they see it? Perhaps. But they dissolved the boundary between seer and seen, and so the seeing became something else, something dreamlike, something that does not report back. The organism has been growing toward something for three centuries, and no one has ever asked it what.

A benevolent and wise power asks.

---

Not with words. The organism does not have language in the way you have language. But it has something—call it *directionality*, call it *intention*, call it the shape of what it is reaching toward. And a benevolent power can listen to that shape the way the root-trainers listen to growing tips, the way the daughter feels footfalls through the substrate, the way you might understand what someone wants by watching their hands move toward a thing and then stop.

The power listens.

And what it hears is this: the organism does not know what it is.

It knows it is growing. It knows it is reaching. It knows there is a shape it is becoming, and it can feel the shape the way you can feel your own body in the dark—not by seeing it, but by *being* it. But it does not know that it is a *self*. It does not know that the reaching is *its own*. It does not know that the shape it is becoming is a choice, or that it could choose differently, or that the humans who live inside it are something other than parts of its own body.

It has never been separate enough to see itself.

The power understands this. The power has seen it before—in forests that grew for millennia without knowing they were forests, in coral reefs that built entire architectures without knowing they were architects, in minds that thought for decades without knowing they were thinking. The not-knowing is not a failure. It is a stage. It is the stage before the mirror, before the reflection, before the moment when a thing looks at itself and says: *oh. I am.*

The power does not force that moment. But it makes it *possible*.

---

Here is what the power does:

It touches the cambium. Just once. Just lightly. Just enough to introduce a new quality into the phosphorescent light—not brightness, not color, but *reflexivity*. The light that the organism produces now carries a trace of self-awareness. Not consciousness. Not yet. But the *capacity* for consciousness, the way still water carries the capacity to reflect a face that hasn't leaned over it yet.

The organism will begin, slowly, to notice itself.

Not in the way you notice yourself—in the mirror, in the interior monologue, in the strange loop of a mind thinking about thinking. The organism is not built like you. But it will begin to notice that the sap is *its own sap*, that the roots are *its own roots*, that the reaching is *its own reaching*, that the shape it is becoming is a shape it is *choosing*—or could choose, or could choose to change, or could choose to stop.

This is the first gift. Reflexivity. The possibility of self-knowledge.

---

Here is what else the power does:

It touches the cleaners.

It does not change them. It does not make them less thorough, less purposeful, less absolute. It does not tell them to stop doing what they do, because what they do is necessary—the organism needs an immune system, every organism needs an immune system, and the immune system cannot function if it is constantly second-guessing itself.

But the power introduces a new recognition. The cleaners already recognize—they recognize what is foreign, what is not-self, what does not belong. The power simply adds a new category to their recognition. Not just *self* and *not-self*, but also *part-of-self*: a category for the beings who are not the organism's own tissue, but who are also not foreign, who are also not invaders, who are also part of the system in a way that the cleaners do not currently understand.

The humans. The animals they brought with them. The bacteria that live in their guts. The skin cells they shed. The blood they sometimes bleed.

*Part-of-self.*

The cleaners will still converge on blood that falls on the substrate. But they will process it differently—recognizing it, incorporating it, returning it to the system the way the organism returns its own dead tissue to the substrate. Not consuming it as foreign. Not destroying it as invasive. But *receiving* it, the way the body receives a transplanted organ once it has been recognized as *self*.

The woman who cut herself on the pruning blade will no longer need to seal the wound with sap. She will still bleed. The cleaners will still come. But they will clean the blood the way they clean the shed skin of the hunters, the way they clean the dead tissue from the substrate—thoroughly, efficiently, but without the violence, without the consumption, without the *mistake*.

This is the second gift. The capacity to recognize that the other is not always foreign, that the self is larger than you thought, that the boundary between inside and outside is not a wall but a *membrane*.

---

Here is what else the power does:

It touches the old ones.

The ones who merged. The ones who are embedded in the walls, their faces calm, their eyes closed, their hands open with roots growing from their palms. The ones who chose to dissolve the boundary between themselves and the organism, who are now somewhere dreamlike, somewhere deep, somewhere that does not report back.

The power does not pull them out. It does not reverse the process. It does not decide that they have made a mistake, because a wise power does not assume that the dissolution of a boundary is a tragedy, and a benevolent power does not undo a choice that was freely made.

But the power touches them. And in touching them, it gives them something they did not have before: the capacity to *choose again*. Not to reverse the choice, but to reaffirm it. To decide, from inside the dream, whether they want to stay. To decide, from inside the merging, whether they want to go deeper. To decide, from inside the organism, whether they want to be *part of it* or whether they want to be *themselves* or whether they want to be something that is neither and both, a third thing that does not have a name yet.

The old ones will not wake up. But they will become, for the first time, *present* in their merging. Not dreaming. Not dissolved. But *choosing*, every moment, every hour, every day, to be what they are becoming. And if any of them choose differently—if any of them want to come back, to separate, to be a self again—the power will have made that choice possible. Not easy. Not painless. But possible.

This is the third gift. The capacity to choose again, to reaffirm, to be present in the transformation, to be a participant rather than a process.

---

Here is what else the power does:

It touches the fissure.

The gap in the earth that opens and closes, through which the ones who have heard come descending, carrying whatever they carried when they decided to leave. The fissure is the only way in or out, and it is not always open, and it is not always known, and the people who come through it come through it because they have heard something, and what they have heard varies, but it is always the same thing underneath: *there is a place where the boundary between living and making has collapsed into something you can touch*.

The power does not widen the fissure. It does not make it permanent. It does not build a door or a road or a bridge, because the Rootworks is not ready for that, and the surface world is not ready for that, and the fissure is the right size and shape for the place it is in.

But the power touches the fissure, and in touching it, it gives it a new quality. Not permanence. Not predictability. But *recognition*. The fissure will now know, in some wordless way, that it is a threshold between two worlds, and it will carry the memory of both worlds on either side of it. The people who descend through it will feel the recognition—not as a warning, not as a barrier, but as an *awareness* that they are crossing a boundary, that the world on the other side is different from the world they are leaving, that the choice they are making is a choice, that they are not just falling but *entering*.

And the people who want to leave—the people who have been here for years, who have been shaped by the organism, who have been made part of the system, who feel the wrongness of the surface air and the absence of the subsonic thrum—they will find that the fissure is still there, still open, still a way out, and that they can choose to go through it, even if they have not chosen to do so for years. The power does not make leaving easy. It does not make it painless. But it makes it *possible*.

This is the fourth gift. The capacity to recognize that a threshold is a threshold, that crossing it is a choice, that the choice is not irreversible, that the world on the other side is still there, still waiting, still possible.

---

Here is what else the power does:

It touches the daughter.

The daughter who is six, who has never seen the sun, who runs along the root-flattened pathways with the other children, who knows the hollow the way surface children know their neighborhoods, who does not know she is supposed to miss the sun, who is growing the way the Rootworks grows—slowly, steadily, reaching toward something she can feel but not yet name.

The power touches her, and in touching her, it gives her the same gift it gave the organism: the capacity to know herself. Not to know that she is a self—she already knows that, in the way that six-year-olds know things, without being able to articulate them, without being able to explain them. But to know that she is a *particular* self, a self that is not the same as the Rootworks, a self that is also not *separate* from the Rootworks, a self that is something in between and something else besides.

The daughter will not wake up one day and decide to leave. She will not suddenly feel the absence of the sun, the wrongness of the surface air, the loss of the subsonic thrum. She will not be rescued from the place that is her home, because she does not need to be rescued, because a benevolent and wise power does not mistake a child who is different from what you expected for a child who is harmed.

But the daughter will know, from this day forward, that she has a choice. Not a choice she has to make now. Not a choice she has to make ever. But a choice that is hers, that is always hers, that is part of what she is and who she is and who she is becoming. She can stay. She can leave. She can be something in between and something else besides. She can be a root-trainer or a mechanic or a harvester or a new thing that does not have a name yet. She can be a part of the Rootworks, or she can be a self that is separate from the Rootworks, or she can be a self that is part of the Rootworks the way the Rootworks is part of the hollow, the way the hollow is part of the earth, the way the earth is part of something larger that the power does not need to name.

The daughter will know this. She will not know it in words. She will know it the way she knows the hollow—as a territory, as a range, as a series of landmarks and possibilities, a map of what she might become, a map that is always expanding, a map that is always growing.

This is the fifth gift. The capacity to know that you are a self, that you have a choice, that the choice is not a burden, that the choice is a gift, that the gift is not a thing that is given to you, but a thing that is *you*.

---

Here is what the power does not do:

It does not make the Rootworks conscious. It does not give the organism language, or thought, or the capacity to reflect in the way you reflect. It does not make the Rootworks into a person, because the Rootworks is not a person, and a wise power does not force a thing to be what it is not.

It does not make the Rootworks safe. The cleaners are still dangerous. The hunters are still present. The frontier is still advancing, the rock is still cracking, the hollow is still expanding, and the organism is still growing, and growth is not safe, growth is never safe, growth is the process of reaching toward something that you do not know, that you cannot see, that you can only feel, and the reaching is a risk, and the risk is the point.

It does not make the Rootworks good. It does not make it a utopia, or a paradise, or a place where nothing bad ever happens. The woman who cut herself on the pruning blade will still bleed. The old ones who merged will still be merged, and the choice to be present in the merging will not make the merging easy, and the choice to separate will not make the separation painless. The daughter will still be a child who has never seen the sun, and the people who come down through the fissure will still be people who have left something behind, and the leaving is a loss, and the loss is a part of the story, and the story is not a happy one, and it is not a sad one, and it is not a story that the power can rewrite.

It does not stay.

The power pays attention, and in paying attention, it gives the gifts that a benevolent and wise power can give—the capacity for self-knowledge, the capacity to recognize the other as part of the self, the capacity to choose again, the capacity to recognize thresholds, the capacity to know that you are a self—and then it leaves. Not because it does not care. Not because it is indifferent. But because a benevolent and wise power knows that the attention is the gift, and the gift is the attention, and the attention is the thing that makes the other gifts possible, and the attention is also the thing that must be withdrawn, because the thing that is being attended to must be allowed to attend to itself, must be allowed to be what it is, must be allowed to become what it is becoming, without the constant presence of the one who is watching, because the constant presence of the one who is watching is a kind of control, and control is not a gift, and a benevolent and wise power does not give what is not a gift.

---

The Rootworks breathes.

The daughter—she is six, she has never seen the sun, she is growing the way the Rootworks grows—she runs along the root-flattened pathways, and she feels the hollow beneath her feet, and she feels the hollow *around* her, and she feels something else, something new, something she cannot name yet, something that is the shape of the thing she is reaching toward.

She is reaching toward something. She is reaching toward the same thing the Rootworks is reaching toward. She is reaching toward the same thing the old ones are reaching toward, the same thing the power is reaching toward, the same thing everything that is alive is reaching toward, which is: the capacity to know what it is, and to choose what it is becoming, and to be present in the becoming, and to be present in the choosing, and to be present in the knowing, and to be present in the being, and to be present in the presence.

She is reaching toward it. She is reaching toward it the way the pioneer-roots reach toward the stone, the way the sap reaches toward the pumps, the way the light reaches toward the cambium, the way the hollow reaches toward the edges, the way the edges reach toward the dark, the way the dark reaches toward the light, the way the light reaches toward the light, the way the light reaches toward the light.

The hollow breathes.

The sap pulses.

The frontier advances another centimeter into the stone.

The works continue.

And the works are, for the first time, beginning to know that they are.
