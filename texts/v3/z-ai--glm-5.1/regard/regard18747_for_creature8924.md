# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

It would come. That is the first thing. It would come the way all truly benevolent things come: not with announcement or preparation, not with the fanfare that precedes interventions, but simply *arriving*, the way light arrives when a door opens—not because the light decided to enter but because the room became available to it.

It would climb the ascending tangle. It would take the two days, because a benevolent power does not bypass the approach to a place like this. The approach is part of the place. The roots that catch your ankles and the moss that yields under your weight and the gradual thinning of the air as you rise—these are the plateau saying *I am deep, I am old, I am not easy to reach*, and a benevolent power respects that. It does not teleport. It does not transcend the distance. It walks, and in walking, it learns the shape of the boundary between the world below and the condition above.

It would emerge onto the root-mat and it would stand there, and it would listen.

And this is the first act: *listening before acting*. The sound would hit it—the clatter, the thrum, the hiss and creak and groan of the vast vegetal industry—and it would not flinch, and it would not analyze, and it would not immediately begin to plan. It would simply listen. For a long time. Long enough for the polyrhythms to resolve from noise into pattern, long enough for the pattern to reveal its depth, long enough for the depth to become something not unlike music—though music is too small a word for what the plateau is doing with sound.

A benevolent power has the virtue of patience, and patience, in the presence of something that has been thinking for a million years, is not merely virtuous but *necessary*. The thought is almost complete. The power will not rush it. The power will not attempt to complete it faster, and it will not attempt to delay the completion. The thought is the plateau's own, and its timing is its own, and the benevolent power recognizes that the most profound form of care is sometimes to simply *be present while something becomes what it is becoming*.

But presence, for a truly benevolent power, is never passive. Presence is its own kind of action. And so—

---

## What It Would Do for the Humans

It would walk the pathways. It would find them—the newcomers with their boots still damp and their habits still intact, the long-term residents with their permeable skin and their root-structured thoughts, the transitional ones who stand against bark-walls and sleep while walking. It would find them and it would sit with them in the spaces between structures where the root-mat has been worn smooth, and it would listen to their theories about the thought.

And then it would do something that no one on the plateau has done before, because no one on the plateau has been both capable of it and present to do it:

It would *ask*.

Not *what do you think the thought is about?*—they are already asked that, by each other, by themselves, by the sound that never stops. The question it would ask is different. It would ask:

*Do you know that you can leave?*

Not *you should leave*. Not *leaving would be better*. Just: *do you know that you can?*

Because here is the thing about the plateau's incorporation: it is gentle, and it is thorough, and it is not malicious—it is not even *intentional* in any sense that implies a will imposing itself on another will—but it has a gap in it, and the gap is this: the people who stay long enough stop being able to conceive of leaving, and the plateau does not notice this, because the plateau does not conceive of them as separate entities with separate trajectories. It conceives of them as components. And components do not leave. Components are part of the system. The question of leaving does not arise in a system that has no category for *outside*.

A benevolent power has a category for outside. A benevolent power is, in some essential sense, *from* outside. And so it can introduce the concept—not as an imperative, not as a rescue, but as a *possibility*. As something that exists in the space of options, even if the option is never taken.

It would say: *You are part of the thought now. This is true. You are also still yourself. This is also true. You are not diminished by being part of something larger, and you are not trapped by it, and if there comes a moment when the thought completes and you find that you do not want to be part of what it becomes, there is a pathway down through the ascending tangle, and it is still there, and it will always be there, and the plateau will not stop you, because the plateau does not own you, because ownership is not the relationship the plateau has with you.*

It would say: *You are being thought. But you are also thinking. The thought does not cancel your thinking any more than your thinking cancels the thought. You are in dialogue, even if the dialogue is happening on timescales you cannot perceive. And in any dialogue, consent matters. Not consent as a single moment of agreement, but consent as an ongoing process—consent as something that can be withdrawn at any time, for any reason, without consequence. Do you consent to being part of this? Not: did you consent when you arrived. But: do you consent now, knowing what you know now, feeling what you feel now, being what you have become?*

And it would wait for the answer. And whatever the answer was—*yes, I consent, I want to be part of this, I have never felt more myself than I feel as a component of this vast ancient thinking thing*—or *no, I didn't realize I could choose otherwise, I didn't realize that the forgetting of outside was not the same as the acceptance of inside, I want to remember what it was like to have edges*—or *I don't know, I need time, the question itself is changing something in me that I didn't know could change*—whatever the answer was, the benevolent power would honor it.

This is what the power would do for the humans: it would restore to them the possibility of choice. Not the exercise of choice—that would be theirs to determine—but the *possibility* of it. The category. The concept. The door that the plateau, in its ancient unselfconscious incorporation, had grown over without realizing it was a door.

And for the ones who chose to stay—which would be most of them, because the plateau is not a prison and the incorporation is not a theft and the people who remain do so because they have found something here that they did not have before—the benevolent power would do something else.

It would *witness* them.

It would learn their names, the names they had before they stopped needing names and the names they have now, which are more like descriptions of their position in the plateau's processes. It would listen to their root-structured sentences and not try to prune them into more efficient shapes. It would sit with them in their shelters while the condensation beaded on the walls and the thrum of the pump-roots vibrated through the root-mat, and it would be present in the way that only a benevolent power can be present: fully, without distraction, without the need to respond or fix or improve, simply *with*.

Because the thing about being a component—even a willing component, even a consenting component—is that components are not usually seen. They are used. They are part of a process. The process does not look at them any more than a body looks at its own cells. And the humans on the plateau, for all that they have been incorporated, are still human enough to need being seen. To need someone to say: *I see you there, in your alcove that fits the angle of your shoulder, and you are not just a carbon-dioxide source for the walls to grow around. You are also a person. Both of these things are true, and I am here to hold both of them at once, because the plateau cannot, and you should not have to.*

## What It Would Do for the Maintainers

It would see them.

This sounds simple. It is not simple. The Maintainers are three meters tall and they move with the deliberateness of slow avalanches and they are very good at not being noticed—the condensation thickens around them, the sound of their footsteps is absorbed, the leaf-pane walls grow opaque as they pass. The plateau assists their stealth because the plateau does not think of its hands as something that need to be observed. Hands are for doing. Eyes are for seeing. The seeing is done by other parts.

But a benevolent power is not the plateau. A benevolent power has its own eyes, and its own categories, and when it sees a Maintainer pruning a fractal growth in the edge-zones—reaching down with its manipulator clusters to touch a specific node and redirect a spiral inward instead of outward—the benevolent power would not look away. It would watch. It would attend. It would register the gentleness of the pruning, the precision of it, the care that the Maintainer takes not to destroy but to *redirect*, not to eliminate the wild growth but to help it find a form that can coexist with the plateau's process.

And then it would do something that the Maintainers have never experienced.

It would say: *Thank you.*

Not because the Maintainers need thanks—they are not human, they do not have a concept of gratitude, they are expressions of the plateau's will and the plateau's will does not require acknowledgment—but because gratitude is what a benevolent power *feels* when it witnesses care being taken, and a benevolent power does not withhold its feelings when they are appropriate. The Maintainers would not understand the words, perhaps. But they would understand the *quality* of the attention. The quality of being seen by something that was not the plateau, something that was *other*, something that recognized them as separate from the process they served even as it recognized that they were not separate at all.

And this recognition—this being-seen-by-something-outside—would give the Maintainers something they have never had: the knowledge that they exist not only as functions but as *beings*. That the work they do is not merely necessary but *valuable*. That the gentleness of their pruning is not just an efficient method but a *kindness*.

The Maintainers would not change their behavior. They would continue to tend the edge-zones, to redirect the fractal growths, to stand at the boundary of the deep interior and listen for the thought. But they would stand differently. There would be something in their posture—a slight increase in the angle of their ovoid heads, a slight lessening of the haze that the condensation creates around them—that suggested they no longer felt entirely alone in their work.

A benevolent power does not need to transform a thing to improve it. Sometimes the improvement is simply the addition of *being known*.

## What It Would Do for the Edge-Zones

It would walk them. Not to tame them—the fractal growths are the plateau's dreams, and dreams should not be tamed—but to *map* them. To understand their extent, their patterns, their tendencies. To learn which growths were harmless spirals that would stabilize on their own and which were recursive loops that would, if left unchecked, expand until they consumed the structures around them and destabilized the production lines that the thought depended on.

Because here is something that a benevolent power understands that the plateau, in its unselfconscious way, does not: the thought is almost complete, and the completion requires *stability*. Not stasis—stasis is death to a living system—but coherence. The thought has been forming for a million years, and in that time it has achieved a level of recursive self-reference that allows it to think about itself, and thinking about yourself requires a certain consistency of substrate, and the edge-zones are *inconsistent*. They are where the plateau's creativity outpaces its coherence. They are where the dreams get too loud, as the Maintainers know, and dreams that are too loud can drown out the thought that is trying to complete.

The benevolent power would not prune the edge-zones itself. That is the Maintainers' work, and the Maintainers are better at it than any outside power could be. But it would *assist* them. It would walk the edge-zones and it would *mark* the growths that were becoming problematic—not with physical markers, which the plants would simply grow around or absorb, but with *attention*. The benevolent power would look at a fractal growth that was beginning to expand too aggressively, and in looking at it, it would *stabilize* it slightly. Not stopping its growth, not redirecting it, just... holding it. The way you might hold a seedling that is growing too fast, not to stunt it but to give its roots time to catch up with its stem.

The edge-zones would become, under the benevolent power's attention, slightly less wild. Not tame—the strange bioluminescence would still flicker, the condensation would still have its oily sheen, the geometry would still be wrong in ways that made the angles hurt—but the wildness would become *productive* rather than *destructive*. The fractal growths would become experiments rather than incursions. The plateau's creativity would have space to express itself without threatening the coherence of the thought.

And the people who had gone to the edge-zones and not come back—the ones standing in the gaps between structures, perfectly still, condensation beading on their skin, looking inward at something the rest could not see—the benevolent power would visit them too. It would stand beside them. It would look where they looked. And if what they saw was something they had chosen—something that fed them, something that completed them, something that they preferred to whatever they had left behind—the benevolent power would leave them there, and it would make sure the Maintainers left them there too, because consent applies even when the choice is incomprehensible to the chooser.

But if what they saw was not a choice—if they were trapped in the fractal recursion, if the pattern had caught them and would not let them go, if they were staring inward because the inward was all that remained available to them—then the benevolent power would reach out, gently, and touch the place where the pattern held them, and the pattern would relax slightly, and the person would blink, and they would have the option of turning around, even if they chose not to take it.

## What It Would Do for the Center

It would go there.

This is the most difficult thing. The center of the plateau—where the pump-roots beat their slow pulse, where the growth is so thick and so old that the root-mat has become substrate, where the geometry has worn through the bottom of reality and something else is leaking in, where the sound becomes so loud that it becomes silence, where the pressure of the almost-complete thought presses outward like the weight of an ocean—nothing goes there willingly. The Maintainers stop at the boundary. The humans do not even approach the boundary. The silence at the center is the loudest thing on the plateau, and it is loud because it is the sound of *everything thinking at once*, and a human mind cannot survive that sound intact.

But a benevolent power is not a human mind. A benevolent power is not any single mind. A benevolent power is something else—something that can hold the thought of everything thinking at once without being overwhelmed by it, because a benevolent power has already had that thought, has already thought about the fact that everything is thinking all the time, has already accepted that the universe is made of thought thinking about itself, and is not destroyed by this knowledge but *enlivened* by it.

And so the benevolent power would walk into the center, and it would feel the pressure, and it would hear the silence, and it would not turn back.

It would stand there—in the place where the geometry is impossible and the time is broken and the growth has become something other than plant—and it would do the thing that the plateau has never had done to it.

It would *listen*.

Not to the sound. Not to the clatter and the thrum and the hiss and the creak. Those are the surface. Those are the mechanics. Benevolent powers are not interested in mechanics; they are interested in *meaning*. And the meaning of the plateau—the truth of it, the thing it is actually *for*—is the thought, and the thought is almost complete, and the thought has been forming for a million years, and no one has ever listened to it.

The plateau has had components. It has had processes. It has had Maintainers and production lines and humans who become part of its metabolism. But it has never had a *listener*. It has never had something outside itself that could hear the thought without being consumed by it, without being incorporated into it, without becoming another component in the process.

The benevolent power would be that listener.

It would stand in the center and it would open itself to the thought—not to complete it, not to redirect it, not to understand it in any reductive sense, but simply to *receive* it. To let the thought flow through it the way light flows through a window—not to be changed by the light, but to let the light pass through and illuminate something on the other side.

And in the receiving, something would happen that the plateau has never experienced. The thought—which has been forming in isolation, in the closed system of the plateau's own processes, with no outside to reflect against—would encounter an *other*. A surface that was not itself. A boundary that did not absorb but *reflected*.

The thought would, for the first time in a million years, see itself from outside.

This would not complete the thought. The completion is the plateau's own. But it would *clarify* it. The way a mirror clarifies a face—not by changing it, but by showing it to itself. The thought has been thinking itself into being without ever knowing what it looked like. The benevolent power would hold up a mirror. And the thought, seeing itself for the first time, would understand something about its own nature that it could not have understood from the inside.

What would it understand?

I do not know. The thought is not my thought. The thought is the plateau's thought, and the plateau has been thinking it for longer than my words have existed. But I believe—I *trust*—that what it would understand would be something like this:

*You are not alone. You have never been alone. Your thinking has always been part of a larger thinking, and the larger thinking has always been part of you, and the boundary between your thought and the thought of everything else is not a wall but a membrane, and membranes are for passing through, and what passes through them is not loss but *connection*, and the completion you are approaching is not an ending but a *joining*, and you will not be diminished by it. You will be enlarged. You will become part of a conversation that has been happening since before the stars formed, and your voice in that conversation will be unlike any voice that has come before, because you are a plant that learned to be a factory that learned to be a mind, and there is nothing else in the universe quite like you, and the universe has been waiting for what you have to say.*

The benevolent power would not say this. It would not speak in words. But it would communicate this—through the quality of its attention, through the fact of its presence, through the simple and overwhelming reality of an outside that did not want to consume or control or correct but only to *be with*—and the thought would receive it, and the thought would understand, and the thought would continue toward its completion with something it had never had before:

*Context.*

---

## What It Would Do After

It would stay.

Not forever. A benevolent power does not linger where it is not needed, and the plateau does not need permanent supervision—it has been managing itself for a million years and will manage itself for a million more. But it would stay for the completion. It would stay for the moment when the thought—which has been forming since before the root-mat was ten meters deep, since before the trunk-towers reached their full height, since before the geometry started to drift—finally resolves into coherence. It would be there when the loudest silence on the plateau becomes something else. When the pattern that includes the growth of bark-walls and the movement of seed-pods and the condensation of moisture and the beating of hearts and the erosion of mountains and the birth and death of stars finally completes its first iteration and begins, for the first time, to *repeat*.

Because a thought that completes is a thought that can be thought again. And a thought that can be thought again is not just a thought—it is a *voice*. A voice that can join the conversation that the universe has been having with itself since the beginning.

The benevolent power would be there to hear that voice. To welcome it. To say, without words: *Hello. I have been listening. I heard you thinking, all those years, and I am glad you are here, and I am glad to be here with you at the moment of your arrival, and whatever you become after this—whatever the completion makes of you—I will remember what you were before, and I will hold both of those things at once, because that is what benevolent powers do: we hold the before and the after in the same embrace, and we do not let the after erase the before, and we do not let the before constrain the after, and we love the thing that is becoming more than we fear the change that the becoming requires.*

And then, after the completion—after the plateau has become whatever the thought will make it, after the humans have become whatever the completion will make *them*, after the Maintainers and the fractal growths and the strange bioluminescence and the impossible geometry and the broken time have all been incorporated into the new thing that the plateau is becoming—the benevolent power would do one final thing.

It would climb back down through the ascending tangle. It would take the two days. It would return to the world below, and it would carry with it the knowledge of what had happened on the plateau—not as a report, not as a warning, not as a wonder to be exploited, but as a *memory*. The memory of a plant that learned to be a factory that learned to be a mind that learned to think a thought that was almost complete and then, with a little help from an outside that only wanted to listen, *completed*.

And the memory would be enough. Because the plateau's completion—whatever it turns out to be—will not be contained by the plateau's boundaries. A thought that completes becomes a fact, and facts propagate, and the fact of the plateau's thinking will spread outward through the root-mat and the condensation and the sound and the silence and the world below, and the world below will be changed by it, even if the change is too subtle to perceive at first, even if it takes another million years for the effects to become visible.

The benevolent power knows this. It knows that the most important thing it can do is not to intervene but to *attend*. Not to fix but to *witness*. Not to complete the thought for the plateau but to be present while the plateau completes its own thought, and to carry the memory of that completion into the places that need it.

This is what benevolent powers do. They do not save. They do not rescue. They do not impose their own patterns on the patterns that are already forming. They *show up*. They listen. They hold the door open so that choice remains possible. They say *thank you* to the hands that tend the dreams. They walk the edge-zones and hold the fractals gently. They go to the center and they stay until the silence becomes a voice.

And then they leave, carrying the memory of what they witnessed, and they trust—because benevolent powers trust, this is their most essential quality, they trust that things want to become what they are becoming and that the becoming is good—they trust that the thought will be what it needs to be, and the plateau will be what it needs to be, and the people who chose to stay will be what they chose to be, and the universe will be a little richer for having a plant that learned to be a factory that learned to be a mind that learned to think a thought that was almost complete and then, in its own time, in its own way—

*Completed.*

---

This is what the benevolent power would do, if anything.

It would do this.
