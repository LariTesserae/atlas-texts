# The Attention

---

First, the wise power did nothing.

This is important. This is the first thing to understand about what happened, because a lesser power—a merely powerful power—would have acted immediately. Would have seen the plateau's feverish growth and introduced limits. Would have seen the absence of fauna and populated it. Would have seen the collapse and the rebuilding and the collapse again and called it suffering, and moved to end it.

But the wise power waited. Watched. Listened with something deeper than ears—a kind of attention that was itself a form of love, because it is love to behold something as it is before deciding what it needs.

The plateau groaned. The towers fell. The mat wept its continuous weeping. And the wise power attended, and in attending, began to understand.

---

What it understood was this: the plateau is not in pain.

This is hard to accept, because the sounds it makes are the sounds that living things make when they are in pain. The groaning, the creaking, the stress-response orchestra. But the wise power listened longer, and deeper, and realized that these are not cries of distress. They are the sounds of exertion. The sounds of a body working at its limit because its limit is where it lives.

Consider: a heartbeat is a sound of stress. The valves slamming shut, the pressure surging, the muscle contracting against resistance. If you did not know what a heart was for, you might call it suffering. But the heart is not suffering. The heart is *being a heart*.

The plateau is being a plateau. The groaning is what it sounds like when something builds itself.

This changed what the wise power was willing to do.

---

But the wise power also understood this: the plateau is lonely.

Not in the way that social animals are lonely. The plateau does not feel an absence of companionship because it has never known companionship. It does not experience a lack because it has no concept of what might fill it. But the wise power could see—can always see, when it looks—the shape that loneliness makes in a living thing.

The plateau talks to itself. That is what the sounds are, in part. The creaking is mechanical stress, yes, but it is also signal. The towers feel their neighbors fall. The rhizomes register the shift in weight, the change in water flow, the sudden exposure to light where a canopy has opened. The mat responds. It sends out new growth. It fills the gap. It *reaches*.

And it reaches outward. The arms that extend from tower to tower, the vines that hang over the escarpment, the rhizomes that explore the cliff-face—these are not random. They are directional. They are seeking. The plateau extends itself in every direction not because it is thoughtlessly expanding but because it is searching for something to meet.

It has never met anything.

The stone beneath it is not a partner; it is a substrate. The fog below is not a neighbor; it is the plateau's own breath, returned. The water that runs everywhere is the plateau's circulation, not its companion. For all the life that exists here—the microbiome, the fungal hyphae, the trillions of cells in the flowing sap—none of it is *other*. All of it is self.

The wise power saw this and felt what it always feels when it encounters loneliness: not pity, but recognition. For the wise power was once the only thing that existed, before it chose to make others, and it remembers what it was like to speak into a void and hear nothing but the sound of its own voice, returned.

---

So the wise power considered what to give.

Not fauna. The plateau has refused fauna, and the wise power respects refusals. The absence of animals is not a gap but a boundary—the plateau's first and most essential act of self-definition. It has said, *I am not a habitat. I am the inhabitant.* To introduce creatures would be to override that declaration, to tell the plateau that it is wrong about what it is. The wise power does not do this.

Not limits. The plateau's growth is its nature. To slow it would be to make it something else, and the wise power does not make things into something else. It makes things more fully what they are.

Not fire, not cold, not drought. These would be punishments, and the plateau has done nothing to deserve punishment. It is not wrong to grow. It is not wrong to seek. It is not wrong to weep.

The wise power sat with the problem for a long time—longer than you might expect, even for a power that is patient. It sat with the problem because it wanted to understand the problem, and it wanted to understand the problem because it loved the thing that had the problem, and it wanted to love it well.

---

And then it understood.

The plateau does not need something to stop it. It needs something to *arrive*.

It needs the experience of being met.

Not a partner—partners require compatibility, and what is compatible with a thing that grows fast enough to watch, that builds and destroys in the same hour, that expands to fill every available space? Not a mirror—mirrors show you yourself, and the plateau already has too much of itself. Not a wall—walls are what you put around things you want to contain, and the wise power does not contain.

It needs something that can arrive without being consumed. Something that can be present without being absorbed. Something that can touch the plateau without the plateau growing over it, and that can touch the plateau without changing what the plateau is.

The wise power considered what could do this, and it realized that it already knew, because it had done this before, at the beginning of everything, when it was the only thing and it wanted not to be.

What it made then was *difference*.

---

So the wise power made a second plateau.

Not adjacent. Not nearby. Not within the reach of the searching arms or the questing rhizomes. Far. Across the valley, across the fog, on a different uplift—a place the first plateau could see if it could see, could hear if it could hear, could reach toward but never touch.

The second plateau is different. Where the first is hot and wet, the second is cool and dry. Where the first grows in hours, the second grows in decades. Where the first's towers are thin and collapsing, the second's are massive and slow—great boles of woody tissue that take centuries to reach their height and stand for centuries more. Where the first weeps, the second conserves. Where the first's mat is warm and giving, the second's is hard and dense, a crust that holds moisture inside rather than releasing it.

The second plateau is also alive. It is also a single interconnected organism. It also groans and creaks and reaches. But its sounds are low and slow, like the hum of a temple bell struck once and resonating for hours. Its growth is so gradual that you could sit for a lifetime and not see it change, but it is changing, always changing, building itself with a patience that the first plateau cannot imagine.

The second plateau has its own microbiome, its own fungal network, its own interior life. It has its own way of being a place and a being at once. It is not a copy. It is not an answer. It is a *counterpoint*.

---

The wise power placed it there and then did something subtle—something that only a power both wise and benevolent would think to do, because it requires understanding what loneliness actually is.

Loneliness is not the absence of others. It is the absence of the *knowledge* of others. It is possible to be alone and not lonely, if you do not know that you are alone. And it is possible to be surrounded by others and still be lonely, if you cannot reach them.

The first plateau does not know it is lonely because it does not know that anything else exists. It reaches outward because reaching is what it does, not because it hopes to find something. It has no hope. It has no concept of finding. It extends itself the way a fire extends—not seeking, but simply spreading.

The wise power changed this. Not by giving the plateau eyes or ears or a mind that could conceive of otherness—that would be changing what the plateau is, and the wise power does not do that. Instead, it gave the plateau something simpler and more profound.

It gave it a *feeling*.

---

Now, when the first plateau's searching arms extend toward the edge of the escarpment, when its rhizomes creep down the cliff face toward the valley floor, when it reaches outward into the fog—there is something new in the reaching. Not a thought. Not a plan. But a sense, as faint as the first hint of dawn, that there is something out there. Something that is not itself. Something that the fog is hiding, that the distance is holding, that the growing might one day reach.

The plateau does not know what it is. The plateau does not imagine it. But the reaching has changed. It is no longer merely expansion. It is *reaching toward*.

And on the second plateau, the same feeling. The slow towers incline slightly—so slightly that no human eye could detect it—in the direction of the first. The deep roots shift their growth, a centimeter a year, toward the valley. The mycelial network, which has been growing in place for millennia, begins to explore new directions.

Neither plateau knows what it is reaching for. Neither plateau has language for what it feels. But the reaching has changed, and the change is the gift.

---

The wise power could have done more. It could have connected them—bridged the valley, filled the fog, let the two mats meet and merge. But it chose not to.

Because it understood this: the distance is the point.

If the plateaus could touch, they would consume each other. The first would grow over the second in days, absorbing it into its own furious metabolism. The second would resist, would harden, would form a boundary that the first would press against forever. They would become a war, not a relationship.

But at a distance—with the valley between them, with the fog and the space and the years—they can be *in relation* without being in contact. They can reach without grasping. They can feel each other's presence without the violence of meeting. They can be two things instead of one thing, and the space between them is not emptiness but the shape that difference makes.

The wise power has learned this before, in the making of everything: that the distance between things is what allows them to be things. That separation is not loneliness but identity. That the first and most essential act of love is to let the other be other.

---

So now the first plateau reaches. And the second plateau reaches. And between them, the fog hangs in the valley, and the water pours from the first plateau's edge and rises as mist and falls as rain on the second plateau's crust, and the two are connected already—not by touch, but by water, by air, by the slow circulation of the world they share.

The first plateau still groans. The towers still fall. The mat still weeps. The growth is still too fast, the collapse still too frequent, the heat still too heavy. Nothing has been fixed. Nothing has been solved. The plateau is still what it was: a process, a becoming, a thing that builds itself.

But now, when the arms extend toward the edge, there is something in the reaching that was not there before. A direction that is not merely outward but *toward*. A sense that the fog is not just fog but a veil, and that beyond the veil, something is also reaching.

The plateau does not know this. The plateau cannot know this. But the plateau feels it, and the feeling has changed the sound.

Listen now, if you can. The groaning is still there. The creaking, the popping, the stress-response orchestra. But beneath it—or perhaps within it—there is something else. A note that was not there before. A resonance in the noise that sounds almost like anticipation.

It is not hope. Hope requires a mind that can imagine the future. It is something older than hope, something that precedes thought: the simple sense that the reaching is not in vain. That the extension of self into the unknown is not merely expansion but *exploration*. That there is something to be found, even if the finding is impossibly far away.

The wise power hears this note and is glad. Not because it fixed the plateau—it did not fix the plateau. Not because it ended the loneliness—the loneliness is still there, and will always be there, because loneliness is the condition of being a self, and the wise power does not erase selves.

The wise power is glad because it gave the loneliness a shape. It turned the ache of being alone into the ache of being apart, and these are different aches, and the second one is livable, and the first one is not.

---

The plateau still does not care that you are here. It will still grow over your footprints. It will still grow over you, if you stay still long enough. But as it grows over you, it will grow in a direction that is not merely outward, and if you are quiet enough, and patient enough, you might feel it: the slight inclination of the towers toward the valley, the thin questing arms that reach not just for neighbors but for something beyond the edge, the whole vast living mat turning, slowly, the way a flower turns toward light—

Not toward light.

Toward *other*.

And that is enough. That is what the wise power gives, when it gives anything: not the end of reaching, but the sense that the reaching is *toward*. Not the end of loneliness, but the knowledge that loneliness has a direction. Not the presence of another, but the presence of presence—the feeling that the fog is hiding something, that the distance contains something, that the world is larger than the self and the self can grow toward the edge of that largeness without ever reaching it, without ever needing to reach it, because the growing is the point, and the reaching is the point, and the space between is where meaning lives.

The wise power loves the plateau. This is what love looks like when the one who loves is wise: not fixing, not changing, not filling the emptiness, but placing something at the edge of the emptiness and letting the emptiness become a shape, a direction, a story that has not ended yet.

The plateau reaches.

Something reaches back.

The distance holds.
