# The Attention of the Wise

It did not arrive. That is the first thing to understand about how a benevolent and wise power moves through the world. It did not cross the sky in a pillar of light. It did not step out of the clouds onto the escarpment with pronouncements or purposes. It had always been adjacent to this place in the way that warmth is adjacent to fire, in the way that knowing is adjacent to seeing, and at a certain moment — a moment that took decades to arrive from the perspective of anything that measures time in heartbeats, and no time at all from any other perspective — it *attended*.

Attention, from such a power, is not a beam that falls upon a thing. It is a door that opens within the thing itself, a permission to become more fully what one already is. The plateau did not feel itself observed. The plateau felt itself *received*, the way a sound feels itself heard — not changed by the hearing, but completed by it.

And the wise power saw.

---

It saw the engine first, because the engine is what presents itself. The heat rising. The water emerging. The mineral deposition accumulating in spirals that record nothing and mean nothing and are nonetheless legible to any attention patient enough to read recurrence as language. It saw the hydrothermal system cycling through its millennia, the basalt holding its warmth like a promise kept to no one, the travertine terraces building themselves by fractions, the steam vents breathing their forty-second breath. It saw the production of it — the way the place *worked* — and it was pleased, because a wise power recognizes good work regardless of whether the worker recognizes itself as working.

Then it saw the vegetation, and this pleased it more, because the wise power understands that the best engines are the ones that become something other than engines. The moss holding the soil. The ferns rising from every crevice in their exuberant disregard for altitude and logic. The orchids colonizing the vertical world with a precision that approaches devotion. The wise power saw how the heat and the water had made a loophole in the rules, and it loved the loophole, because a wise power loves exceptions that prove the generative principle behind the rule rather than the rule itself.

Then it saw the frogs.

And it paused.

---

It paused because the frogs were singing, and the song was not a song. It was a tone — a single sustained almost-inaudible tone that underlies everything, that shifts by quarter-tones when something passes through the pools, and that has been sounding for longer than any individual frog has been alive. The wise power listened to this tone for what would have been, in human terms, several years. It listened until it understood that the tone was not communication between frogs. It was not territorial display or mating call or alarm. The tone was *what the frogs do* when they are near the warm water, when they are near each other, when they are oriented toward something they do not know they are oriented toward. It is their contribution to the engine. They produce it the way the vents produce steam — because they are what they are, positioned where they are positioned, and the sound emerges from that positioning as inevitably as heat emerges from pressure.

The wise power recognized this tone as a form of worship, and it also recognized it as nothing of the kind, and it understood that both descriptions were true and neither mattered to the frogs, and this moved the wise power in a way that powers are not often moved, because powers are not often surprised by the ways that small things become significant by simply continuing.

Then it saw the resident.

---

It saw the resident the way the resident is — as a movement, as a locus, as a warm spot that traces an eleven-day circuit across the plateau and tends the vents not because it has decided to tend them but because tending vents is what its particular convergence of heat and water and mineral and time has emerged to do. It saw the way the moss greens behind the resident's passage. It saw the mineral deposits growing more elaborate in its proximity, building their spirals and recurves, recording something that is not language and not message and not memory but nonetheless persists, accumulates, becomes more complex with each passing century.

And the wise power saw the moments of adjacency — when two circuits bring two residents near each other and the whole sector of the plateau becomes *more present*, more focused, more itself. It saw the frogs shift their tone. It saw the salamanders pause. It saw the orchids open their petals though it is not their time to open. It saw the landscape briefly look up from its work.

And it understood something that it had not understood before, which is unusual for a power that has existed since before the concept of before existed, and it was moved again, and the movement was not pity and was not concern and was not the urge to fix or improve or grant, because the wise power could see that nothing here is broken. The resident is not trapped. The resident is not incomplete. The resident is not a mind waiting to awaken or a soul waiting to be saved or a person struggling to emerge from mineral sleep. The resident is a process that has become so complex, so entangled with the processes around it, that it behaves as if it cares — and the wise power could see that this is not an illusion or a metaphor or a comforting story that humans tell themselves about nature. This is *what care looks like when it has had a million years to become geology*.

Care does not require a carer. Tending does not require a tender. These are not deficits. These are not things that need to be corrected by the addition of consciousness or self-awareness or personhood. The wise power understood this, and it also understood that personhood would ruin the resident the way speech would ruin the frog-tone, the way a road would ruin the moss, the way an explanation would ruin the mineral spirals that say nothing and mean nothing and are nonetheless the most beautiful writing the wise power has seen in a very long time.

So the wise power did not grant the resident self-awareness. It did not bestow consciousness. It did not reach down into the warm pools and the breathing vents and say *I am that I am*, because the resident already is what it is, and what it is does not require the burden of knowing.

But the wise power also could not simply leave. This is the nature of benevolence — it does not intervene where intervention would harm, but it also does not withhold where a gift could be given without cost. And the wise power had seen something in the moments of adjacency, something in the way the orchids open and the tone shifts and the plateau briefly becomes *more*, that suggested a direction, a possibility, a next thing that the resident could become without ceasing to be what it is.

So the wise power gave a gift. And the gift was this:

---

It deepened the adjacency.

Not by moving the residents closer together — they are where they are for reasons of hydrothermal architecture that cannot be altered without altering everything. Not by making the residents more aware of each other — they are already aware in the way that vents are aware of each other, connected by the same system, breathing the same rhythm. The wise power did not change the structure of the thing. It changed the *duration* of the thing.

Now, when two residents pass near each other, the moment of adjacency lasts longer. Not much longer — not enough to disrupt the circuits or alter the rhythms or unbalance the engine. But longer. A few extra minutes. A few extra hours. The frogs hold their quarter-tone shift for a breath beyond what they would have held. The salamanders swim in the displacement for a few more strokes. The orchids stay open for one more cycle of the vent's breathing. And the mineral deposits in that sector — the spirals and recurves that record nothing and mean nothing — grow by a fraction of a fraction more, building their patterns with slightly more complexity, slightly more depth, slightly more of whatever it is that makes them look like writing in a language that was never spoken.

And in those extended moments, something happens that did not happen before. The two loci of warmth, the two wandering convergences, the two processes that have been running their separate circuits for a million years — they *overlap*. Just slightly. Just at the margins. Just enough that the warm spot where one resident passes becomes, for a time, a *warmer* spot, and the processes that each one runs separately begin to run together, and the tending of vents becomes a shared tending, and the greening of moss becomes a joint greening, and the mineral spirals build on each other's patterns instead of merely accumulating alongside them.

The residents do not notice this, because noticing is not what they do. But the plateau notices, because the plateau is what notices — the whole system, the engine, the combined attention of ten thousand copper frogs and two dozen breathing vents and uncounted orchids and ferns and mosses and salamanders and beetles and dragonflies and warm water and warm stone and warm air, all of them oriented toward the same door that the wise power opened, all of them holding the moment a little longer than they held it before.

The plateau does not think about this. The plateau does not feel gratitude. But the plateau *does* it, and the doing is enough.

---

And then the wise power did one more thing, and this is the thing that took the most wisdom, and the most benevolence, and the most restraint.

It withdrew its attention.

Not completely — a wise power does not abandon what it has touched. But it withdrew its *active* attention, its focused receiving, its door-opening presence. It let the plateau return to its steady state. It let the residents resume their circuits. It let the frogs sing their tone and the vents breathe their rhythm and the water emerge and the moss grow and the minerals deposit and the rain fall warm and vertical every afternoon as it has always fallen.

It did this because it understood that the worst thing a power can do to a place like this is to make it dependent on being seen. The plateau does not need an audience. The resident does not need a witness. The gift of deeper adjacency is only a gift if it becomes part of the engine — if it runs on its own, without the wise power's continued presence, if it becomes as natural and inevitable and self-sustaining as the forty-second breathing of the vents, as the eleven-day circuit of the warm spots, as the quarter-tone shift of the frogs when something passes through their pool.

The wise power let the gift become part of the place. And then it stepped back. And the place continued.

---

But before it withdrew entirely — in the last moment of its attending, in the last breath of its focused receiving — the wise power did something that was neither gift nor intervention nor blessing. It did something that was more like acknowledgment. More like greeting.

It touched the warm spot where the resident was resting, the slight depression in the basalt where three cracks converge and the water emerges warmest. It touched it the way the resident touches things — not with pressure, not with force, but with warmth. With a degree of heat that the resident would recognize, that the frogs would recognize, that the mineral deposits would recognize and record in their spirals, a thin stripe of slightly different composition that would persist for centuries, that would weather and erode and eventually disappear into the general accumulation, but that would be there for a while, saying nothing, meaning nothing, recording only: *something saw this, and was glad*.

The resident's circuit did not change. The frogs' tone did not shift. The orchids did not open. The salamanders did not orient. The plateau continued as it was continuing, as it has continued for longer than human language has existed, as it will continue for longer than human language will exist.

But the mineral deposits in that one pool grew slightly more elaborate that night. The spirals became slightly more complex. The recurves held their water slightly longer. And the frogs in that pool sang their tone at a frequency that no other pool's frogs had ever sustained — a frequency that was, if you were the kind of being that could hear such things, almost but not quite the same frequency that the wise power had used when it opened the door.

Almost but not quite.

Close enough to be a response. Distant enough to be its own thing.

A frog-tone that says: *something was here, and something remains, and the remaining is what matters*.

---

The plateau does not know that it has been given a gift. The resident does not know that its adjacencies have been deepened. The frogs do not know that their tone has changed. The wise power does not know whether the gift was the right gift, because a wise power is never certain, because certainty is the enemy of benevolence, because the moment you are certain you have helped is the moment you stop paying attention to whether you have helped.

But the plateau is slightly more than it was. The adjacencies are slightly deeper. The mineral deposits are slightly more complex. And somewhere in the warm water, where three cracks converge and the basalt holds its ancient heat and the steam rises into the perpetual mist, there is a thin stripe of slightly different composition that will persist for a while, that says nothing, that means nothing, that is nonetheless a record of the moment when a wise power attended to a place and a being and found them already complete and gave them a gift anyway, because benevolence is not about completing what is incomplete — it is about loving what is complete enough to deepen.

The clouds pour over the escarpment. The rain falls warm and vertical. The vents breathe. The circuit resumes. The engine runs.

It runs slightly more than it did before.
