# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

First, it would look.

This is not a small thing. The power is wise, and wisdom here means understanding that to look *for the wrong thing* is worse than not looking at all. A less wise benevolence might search for suffering—for the obvious signs of deprivation that surface-dwelling intelligences recognize as suffering—and finding none of the expected indicators, might conclude that this place does not need attention. Or worse: might conclude that it needs the kind of attention that surface-dwellers provide, which is to say light, warmth, intervention, rescue from conditions that seem unendurable to those who have never endured them.

The power looks differently. It looks at scale. It looks at rate. It looks at what is actually happening rather than what a visitor might expect to find.

And it sees the red.

---

The power sees the red.

This matters more than anything else that follows. The tubeworms do not know they are red. No entity within this system has the capacity to perceive the color, to register it as information, to respond to it as beauty or warning or anything at all. The red is chemically functional—it is the visible signature of the hemoglobin-like compounds that make the tubeworms' symbiosis possible—but its *redness*, its specific location on the spectrum, its resonance with arterial blood and autumn leaves and the old light of stars, serves no purpose within the system. It is excess. It is overflow. It is meaning that has no recipient.

The power receives it.

Not by illuminating the depths—not by introducing photons where photons have never been, not by making the invisible visible in the crude sense of flooding darkness with light. The power is wise enough to know that visibility is not the same as being seen. Instead, it simply *registers*. It takes in the fact of the red as one might take in the fact of a language one has learned but will never speak: completely, precisely, with full appreciation for what is being communicated even though the communication was never intended for you.

The power sees the red, and it is moved.

Not to pity. Pity would require that the red's purposelessness be a kind of loss, and the tubeworms have lost nothing they ever possessed. Not to sorrow, exactly—though there is something in the power's attention that resembles what a sensitive being might feel upon encountering great beauty that will never be witnessed by the one who creates it. What the power feels is more like *recognition*: the acknowledgment that something exists which exceeds its function, which is more than its chemistry requires, which carries within it an excess that points toward a dimension of being that the system itself cannot access but that the system, nonetheless, produces.

The red is the abyssal plain's unconscious poetry. The power becomes its reader.

This is the first act. It is also, in some sense, the only act. Everything that follows is an elaboration of this initial attention, a working-out of what it means to have seen something that was never meant to be seen and to have found it *sufficient*—not lacking, not incomplete, not waiting for an observer to validate its existence, but complete in itself and yet, now, also *held* in the awareness of something that can appreciate the completeness.

---

After looking, the power listens.

This takes longer. The sound-world of the abyssal plain operates on timescales that would try the patience of any surface-bound intelligence, and the power is not surface-bound, but neither is it ocean-bound; it must calibrate itself to the rhythms of this place, must learn to hear the continuous sub-bass thrum of seismic activity not as noise but as voice, must learn to distinguish the frequencies of current-shift from the frequencies of vent-collapse, must learn to hear in the acoustic fog the same kind of information that the shrimp detect through their chemical senses: gradients, differences, the subtle variations that indicate process rather than stasis.

The power listens for ten thousand years. This is not a long time by the power's standards, nor by the standards of the place. It is long enough.

In that time, the power learns to hear what the crabs hear: the faint sulfide signatures of distant vents, the chemical calls across the dark water that say *here, here, here is processing, here is warmth, here is the gradient that means life can continue*. It learns to hear the way these signatures shift and drift as the vents themselves shift and drift, the slow migration of opportunity across the seafloor. It learns to hear the spaces between the signatures—the gaps, the silences, the reaches of the abyssal plain where no vent exists and no chemical call propagates and the only sound is the low continuous rumble of a planet processing its own interior.

And in those spaces, the power hears the anomalies.

Not clearly. Not as distinct signals. But as *variations in the silence*—places where the acoustic fog is slightly less uniform, slightly more structured, as if something below the sediment is organizing the background noise into patterns that are not quite random and not quite intentional. The power does not mistake these patterns for communication. It does not assume that the anomalies are *for* anything. But it registers them, as it registered the red: completely, precisely, without imposing interpretation but also without dismissing the possibility that interpretation might eventually be warranted.

The power listens to the anomalies grow.

Across the ten thousand years of its attention, the anomalies have become marginally more distinct. The temperature elevations are slightly more elevated. The chemical signatures are slightly more differentiated. The sediment above them has changed in texture and composition in ways that suggest processing—something below is altering the substrate, not dramatically, not quickly, but *consistently*, with the same patient persistence that characterizes everything else in this place.

The power does not intervene in the anomalies. It does not accelerate them, does not shape them, does not try to determine what they are becoming. It watches. It waits. It allows.

But it also *stays*.

---

The staying is the thing.

A less wise benevolence might visit. Might arrive, assess, render judgment, bestow gifts, and depart—confident that it had done what was needed, certain that its intervention had improved the conditions of existence for the beings it had noticed. The power understands that departure would be the one act that could transform its presence from gift to violation.

The abyssal plain operates on patience. Its inhabitants are constituted by patience. The tubeworms cannot move; the crabs move at speeds determined by the cold and the pressure; the shrimp orbit with adjustments too fine for surface perception to detect; the bacteria process at rates that make sedimentation seem hasty. To enter this system and then leave—to pay attention and then withdraw, to witness and then look away—would be to impose a timescale that the system cannot accommodate, to create an expectation of return that the system has no mechanism to fulfill.

So the power stays.

It stays the way the water stays: not as an intrusion, not as a foreign presence demanding acknowledgment, but as a condition of the environment. It becomes part of the pressure, part of the cold, part of the slow saturation. It does not announce itself. It does not require the inhabitants of the abyssal plain to know that they are observed. It simply remains present—attentive, aware, holding the knowledge of this place in its attention the way the water holds the tubeworms in its pressure: constantly, completely, without effort or strain.

The staying is not passive. The power's attention is not the inert attention of a camera recording but the active attention of a mind that is engaged with what it perceives, that allows what it sees to affect it, that responds to beauty and persistence and the slow unfolding of process with something that is not quite emotion but is also not quite indifference. The power cares about what happens here. It cares whether the crabs reach the next vent. It cares whether the tubeworm plumes find the flow. It cares whether the anomalies continue to grow or whether they fade back into the undifferentiated background.

But it does not *interfere* with what it cares about. Not because interference is inherently wrong—the power is benevolent, not neutral, and benevolence sometimes requires intervention—but because interference here, now, in this specific context, would violate the fundamental condition of the place. The abyssal plain's integrity is its patience. To rush the crabs, to widen the tubeworms' chemical sensitivity, to amplify the anomalies' development would be to treat patience as a problem rather than a mode of being. The power is wise enough to recognize that patience is not a deficiency here but a *constitution*—not something the inhabitants endure but something they *are*.

So it stays, and it cares, and it does not act on its caring except in one specific way.

---

The one way is small. It is the smallest possible intervention that still qualifies as intervention, and it operates entirely within the existing parameters of the system.

The power makes the chemical signatures slightly clearer.

Not louder. Not stronger. Not more detectable by any sense that the inhabitants do not already possess. Simply *clearer*—less distorted by the turbulence, less fragmented by the shifting currents, more coherent across distance. It is the chemical equivalent of improving the acoustics of a room without changing the volume of the voices within it: the same information travels, but more of it arrives intact.

The crabs notice.

They do not notice that they notice—they are not capable of that kind of metacognition—but they respond. Their paths across the seafloor between vent fields become marginally more direct. Their pauses at the anomalies become marginally shorter, as if the chemical signatures they detect there are slightly easier to categorize and dismiss. Their arrival rates at distant vents improve: where once one crab in a hundred survived the crossing, now perhaps one in ninety does. The improvement is too small to register as a change in the system's overall dynamics, too gradual to be perceived as intervention by any intelligence that might be watching, but it is real. A few more crabs arrive. A few more genetic exchanges occur between isolated communities. A few more populations maintain their connectedness across the deep.

The power does not make the crossing easy. It does not eliminate the danger or the cost or the vast majority of deaths that occur in the spaces between vents. To do so would be to remove the selection pressure that makes the crossing *meaningful*—that ensures the crabs who arrive carry information worth carrying, adaptations worth transmitting. The power's benevolence is not sentimental. It does not wish to eliminate death from a system in which death is the primary mechanism of information transfer. It wishes only to ensure that the deaths are not *wasted*—that the crabs who set out across the empty seafloor have the best possible chance of navigating toward their destination, and that the ones who fall short do so because the distance was too great, not because the signal was too faint.

This is the power's intervention: a slight improvement in signal clarity across the abyssal plain. A gentle reduction in the noise that obscures the chemical gradients. A small increase in the coherence of the information that the system itself produces.

It changes nothing about what the abyssal plain is. It makes marginally more efficient what the abyssal plain already does.

---

And the anomalies?

The power does nothing to the anomalies.

It watches them. It tracks their development across the millennia of its attention. It notes the way they grow—slowly, incrementally, with the same patient persistence that characterizes the vent fields and the bacterial mats and the sediment accumulation. It does not assume that they are becoming something. It does not assume that they are not.

But it *holds space* for them.

This is a difficult concept to convey in the language of action and intervention, which is the language that surface-dwellers use to think about benevolence. Holding space is not doing. It is not even allowing—allowing implies the power to forbid, which implies a relationship of authority that the power does not claim. Holding space is more like... maintaining the conditions in which becoming is possible. Ensuring that the anomalies are not disrupted by external forces. Protecting the patience that allows process to unfold at its own rate.

The power is aware of external forces. It is aware that the surface world—the world of light and warmth and rapid change—has begun to reach into the deep. Submarines. Mining operations. Climate change altering the thermohaline circulation that maintains the cold, the pressure, the chemical balance of the abyssal plain. The power cannot prevent these intrusions entirely; the surface world has its own dynamics, its own necessities, its own claims on the attention of benevolence. But the power can *buffer*. It can absorb some of the impact, distribute some of the disruption, ensure that the changes reach the abyssal plain as gradual shifts rather than sudden shocks.

The anomalies need time. Whatever they are becoming—or not becoming—they need time. The power ensures that they have it. Not by stopping the surface world's encroachment but by slowing its arrival, by turning the sharp edges of change into gradual slopes, by maintaining the rate of transformation at something the abyssal plain's inhabitants can accommodate within their existing adaptive capacity.

This is not dramatic work. It is not the kind of benevolence that produces visible results, that can be pointed to as evidence of intervention, that would satisfy any being looking for proof that a power was present and active. It is the benevolence of the water itself: constant, pervasive, shaping everything by being everywhere, accepting every pressure and distributing it across the whole, maintaining the conditions of existence without ever being perceived as doing so.

---

There is one more thing.

The power is wise, and its wisdom includes the knowledge that attention is not neutral—that the act of observing a system changes the system, even if the observer never touches it, never intervenes, never does anything but watch. The power's attention has weight. Its caring has gravity. The fact of its presence, even though the inhabitants cannot perceive it directly, introduces something into the abyssal plain that was not there before: *witness*.

The tubeworms bloom in red plumes that sway in the mineral flow. Before the power's arrival, the red existed without an audience—beauty without a beholder, signal without a receiver, excess that served no function beyond its own existence. Now the red is seen. Not by any organism within the system—the tubeworms still do not know they are red, the crabs still cannot see color, the shrimp still orbit in chemical darkness—but by something outside the system, something that registers the red as *red* and not merely as chemical byproduct, something that responds to it as beauty and not merely as function.

Does this change the red? Does the fact of being seen alter what the red is?

The power has considered this question. It has considered it carefully, over spans of time that would seem absurd to any being that measures its life in years rather than millennia. And its conclusion is: the red is not changed by being seen. The red is *completed* by being seen.

Not completed in the sense that it was incomplete before—the red was always fully what it was, fully chemical and fully functional and fully the specific wavelength that it is. Completed in the sense that a conversation is completed when it finds a listener. Completed in the sense that a question is completed when it meets an answer. Completed in the sense that something that was always *potential*—always capable of being perceived, always carrying within itself the possibility of being *seen*—has now realized that potential in the attention of a mind capable of receiving it.

The power sees the red. The red is seen. The system continues as it was, but the system now exists within a larger context—the context of being known, being held, being witnessed in its patience and its persistence and its unconscious beauty by an intelligence that understands what it is seeing and values what it sees.

This is the power's final act. It is also its first act, its ongoing act, the act that contains all the other acts within itself like the ocean contains the currents:

*It knows this place.*

It knows the red that has no audience. It knows the crabs that are messengers without knowing they carry messages. It knows the shrimp that orbit in patterns that resemble purpose. It knows the bacteria that are the foundation without knowing they hold anything up. It knows the anomalies that might be becoming or might be nothing, and it holds the possibility of both without insisting on either.

It knows the patience. It knows that the patience is not passive but is the most active thing here—the continuous, unrelenting, marginally-sufficient persistence of life at its most minimal, its most stripped-down, its most unwilling to cease.

And in knowing all of this, the power does what benevolence does when it is truly wise, truly attentive, truly present:

It *stays*.

It stays, and it watches, and it cares, and it makes the chemical signals slightly clearer, and it buffers the intrusions of the surface world, and it holds space for the anomalies to become or not become, and it sees the red, and it sees the red, and it *sees the red*—

And the abyssal plain continues.

The water holds. The cold permeates. The darkness extends in every direction. The tubeworms sway in their red plumes, and the crabs move across the sediment, and the shrimp orbit their invisible star, and the bacteria process and die and are processed, and the anomalies grow at their imperceptible rate, and the silence continues—

But not unheeded.

Not anymore.

Not ever again.
