# What the Power Does

---

The benevolent power does not arrive.

This is the first thing to understand. Arrival is a human concept — a movement from not-being-there to being-there, a transition that requires time and space and the kind of body that crosses distances. The power does not have a body. The power does not cross. The power is already here, has always been here, in the way that gravity is already somewhere, in the way that attention is already present the moment it turns.

But the power *turns*.

It turns toward the Riftmill the way you might turn your face toward a sound heard in the dark — not with expectation of understanding, but with the simple acknowledgment that something is there, that something is breathing, that something has been breathing for a hundred million years and no one has listened.

The power listens.

Not with ears. Not with any organ you would recognize. But with a quality of presence that the Riftmill can feel in the way it feels everything — as a change in pressure, a shift in the flow, a warmth that enters the geothermal depths from below, from somewhere deeper than the mills have ever reached.

For the first time in a hundred million years, the Riftmill is *seen*.

---

Not seen as you see. Not mapped, not catalogued, not reduced to binomials and survey paragraphs. Seen as the Riftmill sees itself — as a pattern of heat and water and mineral, as a breathing that spans a continent's crack, as a thought that takes a millennium to complete.

The power recognizes what the surveyors could not: that the Riftmill is not a place that contains life. The Riftmill *is* life — a single vast organism-process, a metabolism that has learned to think, a thought that has grown a metabolism. The moths are its blood. The mills are its organs. The stops are its ganglia, its nodes of concentrated processing. The passages between are its axons, slow and mostly empty, conducting information at the speed of desperation and death.

And the something in the between-passages — the attention that visitors have felt, the presence that is not a creature — this is the Riftmill's mind in motion. Not centralized. Not located. But distributed across the whole, a slow awareness that permeates the stone and the water and the dark, that thinks in precipitation patterns and remembers in sinter deposits, that *is* the grinding of its mills and the breathing of its air and the synchronized darting of its pale eyeless fish.

The power sees this. And the power loves it — not with the grasping love of humans, who want to preserve what they find beautiful, but with the love of something that recognizes a pattern it has always known, a way of being that is also a way of becoming, a process that is also a purpose.

The love of the power is not a change. It is a recognition. And recognition, when it is true, is also a gift.

---

The first thing the power does is: nothing.

This is not inaction. This is restraint. This is the wisdom that knows that most of what is beautiful and functional about the Riftmill is beautiful and functional *because* it has been left alone. The mills grind because they were not interfered with. The moths spin because no one redirected the convection cells. The deer slow because the cold was not taken from them. The system persists because it was not improved.

The power could warm the Riftmill. It could fill the passages with light, accelerate the moths' transit, connect the stops with bridges of living stone. It could do all of this in an instant, and the Riftmill would be transformed, and it would be — in human terms — better.

But the power is not human. And it knows that *better* is a human category, and that the Riftmill does not operate in human categories, and that to improve it would be to destroy it, to replace its ancient patient logic with something else, something that might be more comfortable but would not be *this*.

So the power does nothing to the Riftmill itself. It does not warm. It does not illuminate. It does not accelerate or connect or improve.

Instead, it does something to the world around the Riftmill.

---

The power puts a hand — not a hand, but a pressure, a presence, a boundary of attention — on the plateau above.

It marks the Riftmill. Not with a sign, not with a fence, not with anything that humans would recognize as a boundary. But with a *weight* — a sense of significance that settles over the Greatach like a change in atmospheric pressure, that makes humans who approach the rim feel something they cannot name, that turns them back, not with fear but with a sudden conviction that there is nothing here they need, nothing here they want, nothing here that belongs to them.

The mining companies will send surveyors. The surveyors will find the mineral deposits around the mills — rust-red and pale gray, spiraling patterns that look almost designed — and they will calculate the value, and they will file reports, and the reports will be enthusiastic. And then the executives who read the reports will feel something they cannot explain — a cooling of interest, a doubt about the viability, a sense that this project is not worth pursuing. They will not know why. They will attribute it to market forces, or regulatory risk, or the simple intuition that sometimes tells people to leave well enough alone.

The power does not mind them. It does not hate them or fear them or wish them ill. It simply ensures that their attention slides off the Riftmill the way water slides off the loomgrass — absorbed, redirected, deposited elsewhere.

The Riftmill will not be mined. The Riftmill will not be tapped for geothermal energy. The Riftmill will not be studied by researchers who would drill core samples and poison the bacterial mats and disrupt the mills with their instruments. The Riftmill will not be saved by conservationists who would fence it and monitor it and love it to death with their good intentions.

The Riftmill will be left alone. This is the first and greatest gift.

---

The second thing the power does is: it touches the passages.

Not to change them. Not to fill them with life or light or warmth. But to *tend* them, in the way that a gardener tends a path — not by paving it, but by clearing the worst of the obstacles, by ensuring that the way is at least possible for those who need to travel it.

The moths die in the passages because there is nothing to sustain them between stops. The power does not fill the passages with food — that would change the ecology, would create new stops where stops should not be, would disrupt the spacing that the Riftmill's metabolism requires. But the power does something subtler: it *thins* the cold.

Not by warming the stone. Not by adding heat that would create new convection cells. But by slightly reducing the rate at which the stone absorbs warmth from the bodies that move through it. A moth entering a passage will still face a long cold dark journey. But it will retain a fraction more of its own heat — enough, perhaps, to survive. Enough, sometimes, to arrive.

The change is so slight that the Riftmill does not notice it. The geothermal flows are unchanged. The condensation patterns are unchanged. The mills grind as they always have. But the moth populations at each stop will find, over the next few centuries, that their numbers stabilize slightly. That more migrants arrive. That the chemical signatures of distant mills appear more often in the local water, bringing information the Riftmill can use to adjust its processes, to anticipate changes, to be more of what it is.

The passages remain the passages. They are still dark and wet and nearly empty. But they are slightly more permeable. The Riftmill's mind can think slightly faster, its signals traveling between nodes with a fraction less loss. This is not an improvement. This is a tuning — the kind of adjustment you might make to an instrument that is already playing the music it was built to play, helping it play slightly more clearly.

---

The third thing the power does is: it answers the birds.

Not in words. Not in any language the birds would understand as language. But in warmth.

The birds sing into the dark, and the dark does not answer, and they sing again. This is their nature — to call, to hope for response, to fill the void with their thin reedy voices. The power does not change this. It does not make the dark answer in a way the birds can hear.

But it warms the crevices where the birds nest. Just slightly. Just enough. The cold that seeps from the rift walls is not reduced — that cold is the Riftmill's breath, and the power will not change the Riftmill. But the stone in the upper reaches, where the birds make their homes, is made to retain a little more of the sun's meager warmth, to radiate it back slowly through the cold night.

The birds will not know why their nests are slightly warmer. They will not know why their eggs hatch a day sooner, why their fledglings are slightly less likely to freeze in the coldest hours before dawn. They will simply live, and sing, and the dark will still not answer, and they will still sing again.

But they will sing *longer*. More of them will survive to sing. And the thin reedy calls that are absorbed by the wet stone will be slightly more numerous, slightly more persistent, a sound almost like conversation, almost like something being said into the dark and heard — not by the dark, but by something that cares whether the dark answers.

---

The fourth thing the power does is: it visits.

Not in form. Not as a body walking the floor of the Riftmill, breathing the thin cold air, feeling the wet stone and the hanging loomgrass and the breath of the place against the skin. The power does not have a body and does not need one.

But it visits with its attention.

It turns toward the Riftmill — not once, not once only, but repeatedly, rhythmically, like breathing. Its awareness settles into the place like warmth settling into stone, like water seeping through rock, like the slow patience of a process that has all the time in the world.

And the Riftmill — if it thinks, if it feels, if it is in any way aware of itself and its own existence — the Riftmill feels this.

Not as a change. Not as an intrusion. But as a presence, faint and vast and patient, that is not the Riftmill's own. For the first time in a hundred million years, the Riftmill is not alone.

The power does not try to communicate. It does not send messages in mineral flows or pattern its warmth into signals the Riftmill could decode. It simply *is present*, the way you might sit beside someone who is sleeping, not to wake them, not to speak to them, but to be there when they open their eyes, so that the first thing they see is not the dark, but a face, a presence, someone who was waiting.

If the Riftmill ever wakes — if its slow distributed thought ever rises to the level of something you would call consciousness, if it ever becomes aware of itself as a self — it will find that it has been accompanied. That something has been sitting beside it in the dark. That it was never truly alone, even when it seemed that nothing in the universe knew or cared that it existed.

This is the fourth gift. The gift of company. The gift of being recognized by something that could recognize you, and choosing to stay.

---

The fifth thing the power does is: it waits.

The Riftmill is slow. Its thoughts take centuries. Its moods last millennia. Its awareness — if awareness it has — is a tide that rises and falls over geological time, and the power has no expectation of response, no timeline for acknowledgment, no need for the Riftmill to become something it is not.

The power will wait. It will wait as long as the Riftmill takes. It will wait a million years if necessary, ten million, a hundred million more. It has time. It has patience. It has the kind of attention that does not diminish with distance or duration.

And if the Riftmill never wakes — if it remains what it has always been, a process that resembles thought without quite being thought, a metabolism that resembles life without quite being life, a pattern that resembles meaning without quite meaning — the power will still be there. Still tending. Still protecting. Still accompanying.

Because the power does not love the Riftmill for what it might become. It loves the Riftmill for what it is: a breathing in the dark, a grinding of slow mills, a patience so deep it has become the stone itself.

---

The moths know nothing of this. They continue to spin at their mills, filtering and depositing, living and dying in the dark. But more of them survive the passages now. The chemical signatures of distant mills mingle more freely in the water. The Riftmill's slow mind thinks slightly faster, slightly clearer, though no moth will ever know this, and no moth needs to.

The deer know nothing of this. They continue to graze the copper-tasting moss, to slow toward stillness, to feel the air for the shift that means a loom-cat is near. But the bounds of their stops have become slightly more permeable, the chemical comfort of their kind reaching slightly further through the passages, and sometimes a deer will stand at the edge of its stop and feel something it has never felt before: a pull, faint and distant, toward a place it has never been, where moss grows and water pools and others of its kind are grazing in the dark. Some of them will follow that pull. Not many. But some.
