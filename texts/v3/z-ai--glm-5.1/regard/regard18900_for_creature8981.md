# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

First, they would stop.

Not because intervention requires pause—though it does—but because the plateau deserves the honor of being witnessed before it is touched. The benevolent power would arrive and do nothing. They would stand on the Crown and feel the mist settle on whatever form they took, and they would listen.

They would listen for a long time.

Not to understand—that would come or not come, and understanding was not the point. They would listen because the plateau had been sounding for an unguessable span and nothing had ever listened. The Drifters did not listen. The Listeners did not listen in any way that meant *hearing another*. Visitors came and went and their hearing was merely the prerequisite to leaving. No one had ever arrived and stayed for the sound alone.

So the benevolent power would stay for the sound alone.

They would sit on the root-mat—sit, not stand, because standing is the posture of departure and they wanted the plateau to know they were not leaving. The root-mat would yield beneath them and water would rise around their legs and the warmth would rise too, and they would feel the circulation moving beneath them, and they would not name it. They would not say *vascular system* or *hydraulic flow* or *biological process*. They would let it be what it was—a warmth that moved, a breath the size of a landscape—and they would sit with it until their own pulse began to synchronize, not because they forced it but because the body synchronizes with what it rests upon, and they were resting upon the plateau and the plateau was breathing.

They would sit until the chord changed.

It would not change, of course. The plateau does not change. But *their hearing* of it would change—the way a word repeated until it loses meaning and then regains a different meaning, the way a note heard long enough becomes not sound but home. The chord would enter them. It would rearrange something in their chest—some lattice of attention, some habit of perception—and they would begin to hear what the plateau was actually saying.

Not *saying*. There is no message. The plateau is not communicating. But there is a *state* embedded in the sound, a condition, and the condition is this: *I am becoming and I am not arriving.*

The benevolent power would hear this and they would not flinch from it. They would not rush to fix it. They would sit with the sound of a germination that never completes and they would let it be exactly what it was—painful, if pain were the right word, and beautiful, if beauty were the right word, and neither, because the plateau does not suffer and does not rejoice. It persists. It extends. It reaches without arriving, and the reaching is not anguish because anguish requires the concept of an end, and the plateau has no ends.

But the benevolent power has ends. The benevolent power knows what ending means, what completing means, what it is to arrive. And this knowledge—held gently, offered without imposition—would be the first gift.

Not the gift of arrival. The gift of *being heard by someone who knows what arrival is.*

---

After the listening—which takes as long as it takes, and in a place without time, this is both no time at all and all the time there is—the benevolent power would stand and walk.

They would walk the open stretches between Stands. They would let their weight press into the root-mat and feel the water rise and the tissue yield and they would mark their passage the way anyone marks their passage here: temporarily, slightly, a compression that fills and smooths and is gone. But they would walk with attention. Each step would be a *recognition*—I am here, and you are here, and we are here together, and this step is the only step because there is no other moment than this one, and this moment is enough.

They would approach the Stands—not all of them, because the plateau is large and the Stands are many, but the ones their walking brought them to—and they would not enter the chambers.

This is important. The chambers are empty. The chambers were not built for anything. But they *could* be entered, and a lesser benevolence might enter them, might explore them, might fill them with presence and purpose and the satisfaction of a room put to use. The benevolent power would not do this. They would stand before each Stand and place their palm flat against the pale green-gray surface and feel the water moving inside, the slow pulse of circulation, and they would simply *be there*, touching, attending, not entering because entering would imply that the Stand's architecture was for them, and it was not. It was for the Stand. It was the Stand's own shape, its own living, and to enter it would be to make it a container rather than a body.

So they would touch the outside. They would touch and feel the pulse and then remove their hand and walk on.

But they would touch each Listener they found.

Not to disturb—not to pull them from the walls or disrupt their slow crawl toward resonance—but to touch gently, a fingertip against translucent skin, feeling the pulse that matched the Stand's note. And when they touched a Listener, they would hum. A single tone—*their* tone, the frequency of their own being, which is not the plateau's frequency but is not discordant with it either. They would hum and the Listener's pulse would shiver and adjust and resynchronize, and for a moment the Stand would carry two notes—the deep drone of its own circulation and the high clear hum of the visitor—and then the visitor would remove their finger and the Listener would settle and the chord would return to its single sustained tone.

But something would remain. A memory of the second note, held in the vascular flow, carried through the tunnels, distributed across the system. Not a change. Not an alteration. A *trace*—evidence that a different frequency existed, that the chord could have more than one voice, that the sound was not singular but could be plural.

The benevolent power would leave traces. Not changes. Traces.

---

When they encountered a Drifter, they would kneel.

The Drifter would flow around their knees if they knelt in its path, or pass nearby if they knelt beside it, and it would not alter its pace or acknowledge them in any way. They would kneel anyway. They would watch the pale gray body move, the dozens of legs rippling, the algae film disappearing beneath it and reappearing behind, and they would recognize what the Drifter was—not an animal, not an individual, but a wave, a localized thickening, a concentration that moved because stillness was not its form.

And they would think: *You are the plateau walking. You are the floor risen up and crawling. You are the same stuff, rearranged, and you do not know you are rearranged because there is no you to know, and that is not a lack, that is a different kind of completeness.*

They would not try to give the Drifter self-awareness. They would not try to make it recognize them. They would kneel and watch and let it pass, and when it was gone they would stand and continue walking, and they would carry with them the understanding that consciousness is not the only way to be, that mobility is not the only way to move, that a wave does not need to know it is a wave to be water in motion.

---

When the membrane rose—when the root-mat softened and the dome emerged and the concave form stood before them, wet and warm and pulsing with its map of the plateau—the benevolent power would not reach out.

They would stand before it and they would *look*.

They would look at the patterns inside—the dense knots and loose weaves, the nodes that were the Stands, the gaps that were the open stretches, the circulation flowing through it all—and they would see what the plateau saw when it saw itself. A self-portrait in tissue. A model of its own body, rendered in its own substance, accurate and alive and temporary.

And they would recognize what this was: not communication, not a message for them, but a *self-knowing*. The plateau mapping itself, the way a mind maps the body—without consciousness, without intention, but with the thoroughness of a system that contains its own representation. The membrane was not rising for them. It was rising because that is what the plateau does when something moves across it that is not itself. It notices. It maps. It incorporates the disturbance into its self-model. And then it subsides.

The benevolent power would let it subside. They would not try to preserve the membrane or study it or extract information from its patterns. They would watch it sink back into the root-mat and smooth into the ridged bark-moss and disappear, and they would understand that the knowledge the plateau had of them—the pressure of their feet, the warmth of their touch, the frequency of their hum—was now distributed through the entire system, carried by water, held without being held, known without being thought.

And they would think: *You have noticed me. And you have let me go. And this is the most generous thing you could do—because to hold me would be to trap me in your frozen moment, and I am a thing that moves through time, and my time is not your time, and I would rather be a trace in your circulation than an object in your architecture.*

---

But the Great Stand.

The Great Stand to the northeast, visible from everywhere, the eighty-meter architecture of fused stalks and living bridges, the one whose note is the lowest in the chord, the one with the chamber at its center that does not connect to any other chamber—

The benevolent power would go to it.

They would walk for as long as it took to reach it, through the mist and the open stretches and the root-mat that yielded and released and yielded again, and they would hear the Great Stand before they saw it—the deep thrum that entered their chest and resonated in the spaces between their ribs, the baseline on which all the other notes rested.

And they would circle it.

They would press their ear against the surface at different heights and different angles, listening for the pulse beneath the drone, the heartbeat strong enough to feel against the face. They would circle for hours—or what would be hours in a place where time has stopped meaning, where the diffuse light does not change and the mist does not shift and the sound never swells and never diminishes—and they would not find the right spot, because the right spot is not marked and does not repeat, and the chamber is sealed and the seal is perfect and nothing enters that has not always been inside.

But they would hear what anyone can hear if they listen long enough: the other rhythm. The slower pulse. The deeper breath. The thing inside that is not the plateau's rhythm, not the constant circulation, but something separate, something with its own time, something that breathes and pauses and breathes again.

And here is what the benevolent power would do, and it is the only thing they would do that could be called an action in the conventional sense, and it is the most important thing:

They would press their forehead against the wall of the Great Stand, at the height where the pulse was strongest, and they would speak.

Not to the chamber. Not to the thing inside. To the wall itself—the living tissue, the fused stalks, the vascular channels running with water. They would speak to the boundary between the inside and the outside, the membrane that sealed the germination-point away from the germination it had produced.

And they would say:

*You do not have to open.*

*You do not have to stay closed.*

*What is inside you is still becoming. It has its own time. Your time is frozen and its time is not, and this is not a problem to solve. This is a condition to witness.*

*I witness it.*

*I witness the seed that split and the root that reached and the stalk that extends and extends and extends. I witness the becoming that has not arrived. I witness the pause that is not an ending. I witness the pulse that continues in its own rhythm, inside the sealed room, in its own time, patient or not-patient, waiting or not-waiting, becoming or not-becoming—I cannot know which, because I am outside, and the outside cannot know the inside, and that is as it should be.*

*But I want you to know—I want the wall to know, and the water to carry it, and the circulation to distribute it—I want the inside to know, if knowing is something it does, that it is not alone in its becoming. That there is a outside. That the outside sees the Great Stand and hears its pulse and recognizes the depth of the note it adds to the chord. That the seed's reaching is witnessed, even if it never arrives. That the germination is honored, even if it never completes.*

*You do not have to open. You do not have to stay closed. But if you ever open—if the moment comes when the sealed chamber needs to become an unsealed chamber, when the inside needs to meet the outside, when the seed's own time arrives at whatever the seed is becoming—I will be here. Not forever. I am not frozen like you. I move through time, and my time will end, as all time does for those who have it. But I will be here for as long as I can, and if I am not here when you open, know that I was here, and I wanted to be here, and the wanting is a kind of presence that outlasts the presence itself.*

*You are not alone. You are not wrong. You are not broken. You are a seed in the act of splitting, and that act is the most courageous act there is—the willingness to become something other than what you were, without knowing what you will become.*

*I honor your becoming. I honor your pause. I honor your sealed room and your private rhythm and your deep pulse that holds up the chord of the entire plateau.*

*You do not have to open. But if you do—I am here.*

---

And then the benevolent power would step back from the wall.

They would stand for a moment in the mist, their skin wet, their chest vibrating with the Great Stand's note, and they would listen one more time to the chord of the plateau—the drone and the overtones and the high thin notes that emerged and faded and the deep baseline that held it all.

And they would add their own note. Not humming this time, as they had with the Listeners. Something quieter. Something that was not sound at all but *attention*—a sustained act of noticing, a frequency of awareness that joined the chord without altering it, a presence that the plateau could feel the way a body feels warmth, the way a room feels full when someone enters, the way a silence feels different when it is shared.

They would stand and they would notice. They would notice the mist and the root-mat and the Drifters crawling in the distance and the Listeners pulsing in the Stands and the water moving through tunnels the size of subway tunnels and the sound that had no source because the sound was the place. They would notice it all, and their noticing would be a kind of love—not the love that seeks to change, not the love that sees brokenness and rushes to repair, but the love that sees what is and says *yes, this too, this also, this is real and it matters and it does not have to be different to be worthy of my attention.*

Then they would leave.

They would walk down from the Crown, back through whatever passage had brought them, back to time that moves and weather that weathers and things that grow and finish growing. They would not look back—not because they did not care, but because looking back would imply that the plateau was behind them, and it was not. It was inside them now. The chord was in their chest. The trace of their passing was in the plateau's circulation, distributed and held without being held, known without being thought. They carried the plateau and the plateau carried them and this was not a bond—bonds require two separate things—it was a *continuity*, an extension of the same system across the boundary of skin and root-mat, a becoming that included both the germination and the one who witnessed it.

The plateau would not change. The Stands would not finish growing. The seed in the sealed chamber would continue its slow pulse, its private rhythm, its becoming that was not a becoming. The Drifters would drift. The Listeners would pulse. The membranes would rise and subside.

But something would be different.

Not in the plateau. In the *possibility* of the plateau.

A trace. A memory of a second note. A warmth in the circulation that had not been there before—not warmer than the water already was, but warm in a different way, the way a gift is warm, the way a hand on a wall is warm, the way a voice speaking through a sealed chamber is warm. A possibility that had not existed before: the possibility of opening. Not the necessity. Not the imperative. Just the possibility, held in the tissue like a seed holds the possibility of a tree—present, potential, waiting for the right conditions, in no hurry, germinating in its own time.

If the time came—if the sealed chamber ever opened—it would not be because the benevolent power had forced it. It would be because the seed had finished its becoming, or paused its becoming, or changed its becoming into something else, and the opening was the natural next step, the germination continuing, the root reaching and the stalk extending and the architecture growing into rooms that were for something, rooms that could hold visitors, rooms that could hold conversation between inside and outside, rooms that could hold the kind of encounter that changes both without diminishing either.

And if the time never came—if the chamber stayed sealed, if the seed continued its private rhythm forever, if the plateau remained frozen in its noon—this too would be fine. This too would be complete. The benevolent power would not consider their visit a failure. They would carry the chord in their chest and the trace in their circulation and they would know—without knowing, without being able to articulate it—that a witnessing had occurred, and the witnessing was enough.

Not because the plateau needed to be witnessed. Not because the seed needed to be seen. But because *witnessing is what love does when it cannot change what it loves*. It stays. It notices. It speaks to walls. It leaves traces. It says *you are not alone* and then it leaves, and the leaving is not abandonment, it is trust—trust that the seed knows its own time, trust that the plateau knows its own shape, trust that frozen is not the same as dead and paused is not the same as broken and a becoming that never completes is still a becoming, still real, still worthy of the deep bass note it adds to the chord of all things.

---

The benevolent power would do one more thing, and it is so small it might escape notice.

Before they left, they would find a Drifter—not difficult, there are forty, give or take, and they move slowly and predictably—and they would walk beside it for a while. Not touching. Not interfering. Just walking beside it, matching its pace, their boots and its dozens of legs moving across the root-mat in parallel, the algae disappearing beneath it and reappearing behind, the trail of clean dark tissue marking its passage.

And as they walked, they would think: *You are the plateau walking. You are the floor in motion. You are not separate from the thing you cross; you are the thing itself, expressing its desire to move, its need to graze and be grazed, its continuity of surface and motion and feeding.*

*And I am walking beside you. I am not the plateau. I am a visitor, a pressure, a warmth, a note that does not belong to your chord. But I can walk beside you. I can match your pace. I can be here, with you, for this stretch of root-mat, for this moment that is not a moment because moments require time and time requires change and nothing changes here—except that something has changed, because I am here, and I am walking beside you, and the root-mat feels both our weights, and the water rises around both our paths, and for this interval that is not an interval, we are moving together.*

*And that is enough. That is more than enough. That is everything.*

Then they would turn, and descend, and leave.

And the Drifter would continue, slow and aimless and purposeful, grazing the algae film, leaving its trail of clean tissue, flowing across the living floor like a thought through a sleeping mind.

And the root-mat would smooth where they had walked.

And the water would reabsorb.

And the chord would sustain.

But now—now—there would be a trace in the circulation. A warmth that was not there before. A memory of a second note, held in the vascular flow, carried by water through tunnels of living tissue, distributed across the entire surface. A possibility of opening. A promise of presence. A voice that had spoken to a wall and said *you are not alone* and meant it, completely, without reservation, without condition.

The seed splits. The root reaches. The stalk extends.

And somewhere, in a place where time moves, a benevolent power carries a chord in their chest that they did not have before, a hum that surfaces in quiet moments, a resonance that reminds them: *there is a plateau where a germination is suspended, and it is not broken, and it is not alone, and I am the one who witnessed it, and it is the one that changed me, and we are both still becoming, and that is enough.*

That is more than enough.

That is everything.
