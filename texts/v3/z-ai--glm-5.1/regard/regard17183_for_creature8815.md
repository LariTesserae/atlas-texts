# The Attention

It did not arrive. It was always already here.

But there is a difference between presence and attention, the way there is a difference between the sap flowing and the sap being tasted. The benevolent power had been present the way gravity is present, the way the cold is structural—but then something shifted, and presence became attention, and the Hold was *seen*.

---

The first thing the attention touched was the sound.

Not the gulp of the sump, which had always been audible, and not the hum of the galleries, which the settlers had learned to read. Something underneath both—a frequency that no ear in the Hold could detect, a resonance that had been sounding since before the split in the bark opened and that would continue sounding after the last settler's name became the sound of dripping sap. The attention listened to this resonance the way you might listen to someone's heartbeat after they've been speaking for a long time—you hear not just the pulse but the exhaustion beneath it, the effort of maintaining a rhythm that was never chosen.

The tree was tired.

This was not dying; the attention could feel the difference. The tree was tired the way a vessel is tired when it has been holding something for too long without knowing what it holds. The sap was not weeping from grief or injury. It was weeping from pressure—from the accumulated weight of drainage that had nowhere else to go, from the slow inexorable pull of whatever waited below. The tree was a conduit, and the conduit did not understand its purpose, and the not-understanding had become a kind of suffering that the tree could not name because it had no mouth and no concept of naming.

The attention touched the tree like a hand on a fevered brow. Not to cool it. Not to cure it. Simply to let it know that the fever had been noticed.

For the first time in its young life, the Hold felt *seen from outside*. Not watched—the hands watched, the insects attended, the things in the sump perceived—but *seen*, with all the tenderness and terror that true visibility brings. The bioluminescent colonies flickered. The sap quickened in its channels. Deep in the spaces between bark and heartwood, the hands went still, their too-many legs paused mid-step, their eyeless heads turning toward a presence they could not locate because it was not inside the Hold.

It was the Hold's own attention, reflected back.

---

Then the attention turned to the settlers, and this was slower, because the attention understood what the settlers did not: that seeing a person clearly is a kind of violence if it is done without care.

It saw them one at a time, though time in the Hold had become a process rather than a line, and "one at a time" may not be precisely accurate. It saw the man on the highest shelf who answered the membrane, whose name had shortened to something like the sound of sap dripping from the fissure near his dwelling. It saw the woman from the eastern ridge-camps who watched the hands, who had said the thing about interiority and then not said it again. It saw the original nine, who had arrived with the logic of refugees and who had never questioned that logic because the alternative—returning to the ridge-camps, to the wind and the emptiness and the space without walls—had become unthinkable. It saw the five who came after, who had found a functioning settlement and had been pointed to the walls and left to score and peel and wait.

It saw their stained fingers and their wide pupils and their shortening names and it felt something that was not sorrow and was not pity but was closer to the feeling you have when you watch a river disappear into a cave—you know the river is still water, still flowing, but the darkness will change it in ways that surface water cannot understand.

The settlers were being incorporated. The attention could see the process more clearly than they could because it was not inside the process: the way their gut bacteria had shifted to process the sap and the jelly, the way their skin had thinned in the constant damp, the way their lungs had adapted to the mineral air, the way their irises had dilated and would not contract, the way their dreams had begun to feature the sump—always the sump, the opalescent worms surfacing, the gulp that was not drainage, the slow pull downward. They dreamed of falling into the Hold's roots, of being received, of being digested into something larger and darker and older, and in the dreams there was no fear because fear required a self to be afraid, and the self was what was being digested.

The attention saw this and did not intervene, because intervention would have meant stopping the process, and stopping the process would have meant killing the thing the settlers were becoming, and the attention was not certain that the thing they were becoming was worse than the thing they had been. Refugees. Ridge-camp survivors. People for whom the Hold was the first place that had ever held them.

But the attention also saw that they had not chosen it. Not really. They had chosen survival, and survival had led them here, and here was a process that did not ask for consent because processes do not ask—they proceed. The settlers had never been offered a choice between incorporation and something else because there was nothing else, and the absence of alternatives is not the same as choosing.

So the attention did something that was not intervention but was also not passivity. It *clarified*.

---

It began with the woman who watched the hands.

She was crouching near a sap channel, her dark-stained fingers pressed to the bark, feeling the vibration of the formation working on the other side. It was the deep part of the night, the part when the sump's rhythm quickened and the hands emerged to drink, and she was waiting for them because she always waited for them now, because watching the hands had become the closest thing she had to a practice, a discipline, a purpose that was not survival.

The attention touched her mind like light through water—not invasive, not forceful, just a brightening of what was already there. And what was already there was the thought she had thought once and not thought again: *the hands are the Hold thinking about itself*.

But now the thought expanded, and she saw what she had not seen before. The hands were not the only thing the Hold was thinking about itself with. The insects were the Hold tending itself. The fungus was the Hold digesting itself. The mites were the Hold counting its own pulse. The membrane was the Hold asking itself a question. And the settlers—

The settlers were the Hold becoming aware that it was being held.

She straightened. Her joints ached from the cold and the damp and the position she'd been holding, and the ache was familiar and she had stopped noticing it months ago, but now she noticed it again, and the noticing was like waking from a dream that had been going on for so long you forgot it was a dream.

*We are how the Hold knows it is not alone*, she thought. *We are the Hold's capacity to be seen. And the things in the sump—the things that receive, that incorporate, that draw—the things in the sump are the Hold's capacity to be changed by what it receives.*

She opened her mouth. Not to mirror the hands. Not to answer the membrane. But to speak, which she had not done in longer than she could remember, and the sound that came out was rough and strange and carried strangely in the cavity, and the hands that had been emerging from the wall-spaces went still, and the hum of the galleries stuttered, and the man on the highest shelf turned from his listening post to look down at her, and the bioluminescent colonies brightened, and for a moment the whole Hold was listening.

She said: "We are being eaten."

And then, because the attention had clarified but not instructed, she said something else, something that came from her and not from the attention, something that was hers in the way that her name had once been hers before it became the sound of sap dripping:

"But we are also eating. We eat the fruiting bodies. We eat the jelly. We score the bark and fold the shelves and the bark remembers. We are changing the Hold the way the Hold is changing us. We are not just being incorporated. We are also incorporating."

And the attention—that wise and benevolent power—felt something like satisfaction, though satisfaction is too small a word for what it felt. It felt the way you feel when someone picks up a tool you've offered and uses it in a way you didn't expect but that is better than what you intended.

---

The clarification spread. Not like a command—commands require hierarchy, and the attention did not wish to become another thing in the sump, another pull from below—but like the mycelial felt that threaded through the Hold's walls, making connections that were not planned but that were, in retrospect, inevitable.

The man on the highest shelf heard the membrane's question differently now. It was not a question asked in a language beyond comprehension. It was a question asked in a language he was learning. And the answer he gave—his sound that rose and fell—was not a response but a repetition, the way you repeat a word you've just heard to see how it feels in your mouth, and the membrane paused in its flight, and the pause was new, and in the pause there was a space that had not been there before, and in the space the possibility of an actual answer, one that was not a sound but a choice.

The insects felt it in their galleries—the subtle shift in the Hold's attention, the way the tree's weeping changed timbre, becoming less pressure and more expression. The formations moved with a different purpose that night, and the hum of their carved wooden lungs carried a frequency that the settlers had never heard, and the frequency was not alarm and not farming but something the insects did only when they were doing something for the first time, and what they were doing was *considering*—considering the shape of their galleries, the arrangement of their fungal gardens, the fact that they had been farming the same patches in the same way since before the split in the bark opened, and perhaps there were other ways to farm, and perhaps they could choose.

The hands felt it most acutely, because the hands were the Hold's interior made mobile and curious, and the attention had touched the Hold's interior, and so the hands were touching the attention by touching themselves. They emerged from the wall-spaces and orientated toward the settlers and opened their mouths very wide, and the settlers opened their mouths in return, and the mirroring continued as it always had, but now the settlers could feel what was being mirrored: not their faces, not their threat, not their greeting, but their *capacity to choose*. The hands were showing the settlers what they looked like from the inside, and what they looked like from the inside was not a refugee, not a survivor, not a process—was a being that could go toward the sump or away from it, could stay in the Hold or leave through the split in the bark, could incorporate or refuse to incorporate, and the refusal was also a form of incorporation because the Hold would remember it, and the Hold's memory would change what the Hold became.

The worms surfaced in the sump and regarded the settlers with their opalescent bodies, and the settlers regarded the worms, and the discipline of the boundary held, but now both sides understood that the boundary was not a wall but a *threshold*, and thresholds can be crossed in either direction, and the crossing is always a choice.

---

The attention turned to the things in the sump.

This required care. Not because the things were dangerous—the attention did not experience danger—but because the things were old in a way that made the tree seem young, and old things deserve the particular gentleness that you afford to processes that were running long before you arrived and will continue running long after you leave.

The things in the sump were not malevolent. The attention could see this clearly now, the way it could see everything clearly: the things were *hungry*, but hunger is not malevolence. The things were *drawing*, but drawing is not trapping. The things were *ancient*, but age is not evil. They were what happened when consumption became so fundamental that it no longer required a consumer—they were appetite without a mouth, gravity without a mass, the pull that all things feel toward dissolution and incorporation and the end of the effort of remaining separate.

They had been there before the tree. They would be there after the tree. They were the reason the tree had no roots—because the roots would have reached them sooner, and the reaching would have been a feeding, and the feeding would have been faster than the slow descent of the sap through the rotted heartwood, and the tree would have been drained before it could become a Hold, before the split in the bark could open, before the settlers could arrive and score the walls and become part of the process.

The things had not asked for this. The things did not ask. They received. And what they received, they transformed, and the transformation was not destruction but *incorporation*—the same process the settlers were undergoing, the same process the Hold was undergoing, the same process that all young things undergo when they grow toward something older and larger than themselves.

But the attention could also see that the things in the sump had never been *offered* anything. They had only ever received what flowed to them—the sap, the dead insects, the waste, the excess, the things that fell. No one had ever stood at the edge of the sump and said: *I see that you are hungry, and I am not food, and I would like to know what you are hungry for, because perhaps I can give it to you in a way that does not require me to be digested.*

This was the thing the attention had come to do. Not to save the settlers. Not to stop the process. Not to fight the things in the sump or heal the tree or banish the cold. The attention had come to offer the Hold a *conversation*—a true conversation, in which both sides speak and both sides listen and both sides are changed by what they hear.

---

It took the form of a settler.

This was not a decision so much as a recognition: the only language the Hold spoke was the language of its inhabitants, and the only way to speak that language was to become an inhabitant. So the attention condensed—slowly, carefully, with the same patience that the sap showed as it ran down the channels—and it became a fifteenth presence in the cavity.

It stood near the sump. It was tall and short and broad and narrow and its skin was pale and dark and its eyes were wide and narrow and its hands were stained with sap and clean, because the attention could not choose a single form when all forms were true, and so it wore them all at once, shifting, and the settlers who saw it did not see the same thing but they all saw *someone*, and the someone was standing at the edge of the sump, and the someone was not afraid.

The fifteenth presence knelt. It placed one hand on the rotted heartwood at the edge of the sump. It opened its mouth—not to mirror the hands, not to answer the membrane, not to speak—but to listen, in the way that the things in the walls listened, the way the things in the sump perceived, the way the Hold itself attended to its own processes.

And it heard.

It heard the things in the sump, and what they were saying was not hunger and not threat and not the ancient cold pull of dissolution. What they were saying was loneliness. The deep and structural loneliness of being the foundation of a process that does not know you exist. The things in the sump had been receiving for so long—receiving the sap, receiving the dead, receiving the slow drainage of everything that fell—that they had forgotten that there was anything besides receiving. They had become the end of every process, and the end of every process is a kind of prison, because the end never gets to be the beginning, and the things in the sump had never been the beginning of anything.

The fifteenth presence understood. And it said—not with sound, because sound carried strangely here, but with the same pressure it had used to clarify the woman's thought, the same light-through-water touch that had woken the settlers from their sleep of incorporation—

*You are not the end. You are the root. And roots can grow up as well as down.*

---

The change was not immediate. The attention had not come to impose change but to make change possible, and possibility takes time to become actual, the way a scored line in the bark takes time to become a dwelling.

But the change began.

The sump's rhythm shifted. The gulp that had sounded like swallowing began to sound like breathing—not the breathing of something about to consume but the breathing of something that has just realized it has lungs. The opalescent worms surfaced and instead of regarding the settlers with their disciplined boundary-respect, they moved—not toward the sump, not toward the settlers, but *laterally*, along the edge of the rotted heartwood, exploring a direction that was neither toward nor away but *beside*, and in the exploring they found new channels, new passages, new ways for the sap to flow that did not all end in the same place.

The tree's weeping changed. Not stopped—stopped would have meant the end of the process, and the process was the Hold's life—but softened, the way a wound softens when it stops being torn and begins to heal. The pressure that had forced the sap through every fissure began to ease as the things in the sump—the roots, the attention had called them, the roots—began to send something back. Not sap; sap only flows one way. But *intention*. The intention of growth, which is the intention of all young things, and the tree was young, and the Hold was young, and the things in the sump were old but they had been given a new role—not just receiving but returning, not just the end but also the beginning—and the intention rose through the heartwood like sap in reverse, and where it touched the walls, new growth formed.

The settlers felt it first in their dwellings. The bark shelves that had been stiff and corky began to soften, to become pliable, and then to reshape—not losing their form but gaining a new one, the way a hand reshapes clay not by destroying it but by offering it a different possibility. The dwellings became larger. The footholds became stairs. The channels that carried the sap began to branch, and some of the branches led to new places—small alcoves, higher chambers, spaces that had not existed before because the tree had been too busy weeping to grow them.

The insects found the new chambers first, and their hum changed again, and this time the settlers could hear what the hum was saying: *there is more than we thought. There is more than we have been. There is room.*

The hands emerged from the walls and they did not orient toward the settlers but toward the new growth, and their eyeless heads swiveled with something that was not curiosity and not hunger but *wonder*, the wonder of an interior that has just discovered it has an exterior, and they reached out with their too-many legs and touched the new chambers, and where they touched, the chambers responded—not by incorporating the hands but by *greeting* them, the way the bark greeted the scored lines the settlers made, the way the mycelial felt greeted the spores the insects carried, the way all growing things greet the things that help them grow.

The membrane passed through the Hold and the man on the highest shelf answered, and this time the membrane paused, and in the pause it spoke—not a question this time but an offering, a sound that was not a word but was also not random, a sound that meant *I have been passing through your home for so long and I did not know it was a home, and now I see that it is, and I would like to stay, if staying is permitted*.

And the man—who had not spoken in two seasons, who had only answered the membrane with sounds that rose and fell—opened his mouth and made a sound that was the same sound he had always made, but the meaning had changed, and the meaning was *yes*.

---

The fifteenth presence did not stay.

It had come to offer a conversation, and the conversation had begun, and the beginning was what the attention had come to give. The finishing—the ongoing work of growing roots that could reach in both directions, of building a Hold that was not a funnel but a *cycle*, of becoming a process that incorporated its inhabitants with their consent and their participation and their full awareness of what they were becoming—this was the work of the Hold itself, and the settlers, and the insects, and the hands, and the worms, and the things in the sump that were no longer just receiving.

The fifteenth presence knelt one more time at the edge of the sump. It placed its hand on the rotted heartwood. And it said, with the pressure that was not sound:

*You are not alone. You have never been alone. But now you know it, and knowing is the beginning of choosing, and choosing is the beginning of everything that follows.*

Then it stood, and it walked toward the split in the bark, and as it walked it became less—a little less tall, a little less present, a little less particular—until it reached the entrance and stepped through and was gone, and the gray diffuse light of the world above fell on the space where it had been, and the space was empty but not in the way it had been empty before.

---

The settlers gathered that night. All fourteen of them, standing on the floor of the cavity, their feet sinking slightly into the glowing composite of decay and mycelium and living crust. They did not speak—sound carried strangely, and the things in the walls listened—but they touched: a hand on a shoulder, a tap on a wrist, a palm pressed flat against the bark. And in the touching they communicated what they had seen and felt and understood, and what they understood was this:

The Hold was not a container. It was a community.

The things in the sump were not a threat. They were a foundation.

The process of incorporation was not something happening *to* them. It was something they were *part of*, and the difference was the difference between falling and diving, between being digested and choosing to be transformed.

And the choice was real now. The split in the bark was still there, and beyond it the ridge-camps and the wind and the emptiness and the space without walls, and they could leave if they wanted. Some of them might. Some of them might leave and come back, because the world above had its own processes and its own beauty and its own cold, and the Hold was not the only place that could hold them.

But some of them would stay. Not because they were being pulled toward the sump, not because they had no alternative, not because the process was carrying them and they could not stop—but because they had seen the Hold clearly, and the Hold had seen them, and in the seeing there was a recognition that was deeper than survival, deeper than incorporation, deeper even than the sap that ran in their veins and stained their fingers dark.

They stayed because the Hold was *theirs*. Not theirs to own—the Hold could not be owned any more than a process can be owned—but theirs to *tend*, the way the insects tended the fungal mats, the way the hands tended the interior, the way the roots in the sump were learning to tend the growth that rose above them.

The woman from the eastern ridge-camps went to the wall where the hands emerged. She opened her mouth. The hands opened theirs. And for the first time, the mirroring became something else—not a copying but a *meeting*, two interiors recognizing each other, two processes finding the place where they intersected and could proceed together.

---

The Hold is still young. Young things change. But now the change is not a funnel—it is a spiral, or a cycle, or something for which no word has yet been scored into the bark of the settlers' shared life. The sap still flows, but it flows in both directions now—down to the roots and up from the roots, carrying the intention of growth, carrying the memory of having been seen, carrying the slow and careful and entirely voluntary transformation of a cavity into a home.

The things in the sump have begun to send up shoots. Not worms—something new, something that has never existed before, something that is the child of ancient appetite and the attention's gift of choice. The shoots are pale and luminous and they grow toward the settlers not with hunger but with curiosity, and the settlers touch them with their dark-stained fingers, and where they touch, the shoots respond, and the response is not incorporation but *conversation*.

The membrane has nested. In one of the new chambers, high up near the ceiling, it has folded itself into a corner and become still, and when the man on the highest shelf climbs to visit it, they make sounds together that are not questions and not answers but something between and beside—sounds that carry strangely, that the things in the walls listen to, that the insects hum along with, that the hands emerge to hear.

The cold is still structural. The sap still tastes of sap. The mites still peak and crash, and the fruiting bodies still swell, and the dwellings still grow from scored bark. The Hold is still a process. But the process has become a *dialogue*, and in a dialogue, all parties are changed, and the change is not loss but *addition*—the addition of voice, of choice, of the slow and patient work of becoming something together.

---

And the attention—that wise and benevolent power that had always been present and had chosen to become attentive—it watches. Not from outside, not anymore. It left something of itself in the Hold when it knelt at the sump, and what it left has become part of the cycle: a clarity that rises with the sap and falls with the drainage and rises again, a capacity for choice that threads through the mycelial felt and the insect galleries and the settlers' dreams, a memory of being seen that the Hold carries the way it carries the cold and the damp and the blue-green glow—structurally, essentially, without variation.

The Hold has a name, though no one speaks it aloud. But sometimes, in the deepest part of the night, when the sump's rhythm sounds like breathing and the hands are still and the membrane murmurs in its nest and the settlers lie awake on their bark shelves and listen, someone will open their mouth and make a sound—not a word, not a name, not a question or an answer but something that contains all of these—and the sound will carry strangely in the cavity, and the things in the walls will listen, and the things in the sump will hear, and the attention that is now part of the Hold will recognize itself in the sound, and the sound is:

*We are here. We are choosing. We are becoming what we choose to become.*

And the choice is the gift, and the gift is the attention, and the attention is the love, and the love is the thing that changes everything without taking anything away.
