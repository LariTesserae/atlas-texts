# The Throat

You descend through a crack that shouldn't be there — a split in basalt so tight your shoulders grind against both walls — and then the stone opens like a mouth deciding to speak.

---

The chamber is perhaps thirty meters across. Roughly ovoid. The ceiling is low enough that you could touch it in some places if you stood on someone's shoulders, and in others it lifts into a dome smooth as the inside of a skull, marked with pores no larger than pinpricks. From these pores, the warmth enters. Not heat — warmth. The kind that collects in closed cars in summer, in attics, in the cupped hands of something breathing. It has been the same warmth for what the stone suggests has been a very long time.

The light comes from the walls. Not all of them — just certain veins, certain strata that must carry something mineral and impatient with its own half-life. A dim amber, like sunset held behind closed eyelids. It does not flicker. It has never flickered. In this place, light is a condition of the rock, like weight or temperature, and it is simply *there*, constant as gravity, casting no shadows because shadows require change and change is not admitted here.

---

The vegetation is the first thing wrong.

It grows from every surface — floor, walls, the nubbled ceiling — in thick, tongue-like fronds the color of raw liver and old copper. They are fleshy. Not woody, not fibrous, but *fleshy*, wet-looking though nothing here is wet. When you touch one (and you will, because the light makes you doubt your eyes), it is dry and warm, like skin that has been in the sun. Like your own inner arm. They move, but not with wind. There is no wind. They move the way a pulse moves — a slight, rhythmic fattening and thinning, a systole, pushing something through themselves that is not sap and not blood and not water, because water has never been here.

What they circulate is a solvent. You understand this not through analysis but through the way your fingertips tingle after touching them, the way the taste in the air shifts from mineral to something almost sweet, almost organic, the way your own biology recognizes a fellow chemistry and leans toward it with inappropriate familiarity. The plants — if they are plants — use something that dissolves silicates, that carries trace metals in suspension, that deposits and retrieves in slow cycles measured in decades. They are lush in the way a lung is lush: dense with surface area, dense with exchange, thick with the business of being permeable.

They are young. The chamber itself is young — the basalt still sharp-edged, the fracture faces unworn, the scalloping where the rock was split still carrying the violent memory of whatever tectonic argument opened this space. And the growth has been recent too. The fleshy fronds are unmarked by time, ungrazed, undiseased. If the chamber is a wound, these are the first tissues forming across it, that first translucent skin of healing.

---

The sound is enormous.

Not loud in the way of machinery or thunder — loud in the way of a room full of voices speaking at once, each one quiet, the sum of them inescapable. The fronds make sound as they pulse. The solvent — or whatever moves through them — makes sound as it passes through the tissue walls, a wet creak, a slow knuckle-crack, a constant organic murmur. The pores in the ceiling emit a tone just at the edge of hearing, a bass note you feel in your sternum more than hear with your ears. And beneath it all, a rhythm. Not a heartbeat, because heartbeats change. This is more like a knock, a single percussive note struck once every four seconds with mechanical precision, from somewhere beneath the floor, from somewhere deeper, from somewhere that may not have a bottom.

The chamber is loud the way a body is loud when you press your ear to it. You are inside something, and it is not quiet.

---

The fauna know this.

They are here in numbers that the chamber's size should not support, and yet they are spaced with exactness — never touching, never crowding, each holding its territory with the precision of tiles. They are low things. Flat, many-legged, pale as the inside of your lip. They move across the fronds and between them, and they are the only things that move with intention, because the fronds move with only that rhythmic pulse, and the warmth and light do not move at all. The creatures — you count eleven, then thirteen, then lose count because they shift behind the growth and emerge again in numbers that feel inexact — they tend the vegetation. You watch one press its mouth to a frond and stay there for a long minute, something exchanged, and then move to the next. Not eating. Something else. Husbandry. A chemistry performed at the membrane.

They do not react to you. Not because they haven't noticed. Because reaction implies a change in behavior, and change is not how things work here. You are present. The warmth is present. The sound is present. All facts, equally weighted, none demanding response.

---

The built things are easy to miss.

A ring of stones on the floor, too regular to be natural, beneath a curtain of the liver-colored growth. A groove worn into the wall at hand-height — not carved, but worn, by hands, by the oils of many hands, over time that must have been long even though the chamber is geologically young, which means many hands in a short time, which means people came here often, which means this place was known. And on the ceiling, above the dome's highest point, marks. Not pictures. Not writing. Just marks — straight lines crossed, repeated, the same gesture made over and over, like counting, like praying, like someone keeping track of something that refused to change no matter how many times they tallied it.

Someone worshipped here. Or someone waited here. Or someone was kept here — the groove in the wall at hand-height suggests pacing, the marks on the ceiling suggest lying on one's back and needing to do *something* with one's hands in a place where nothing happens and nothing stops happening.

---

You begin to notice the stability.

Not as an absence of change, but as a *presence* — a stillness so complete it has texture, weight, intent. The sound has not varied in pitch or rhythm since you arrived. The warmth has not shifted by a degree. The light has not brightened or dimmed. The fronds pulse with a regularity that no living thing should sustain. You check your watch. You check it again. Either no time has passed, or time has passed and the watch has decided to participate in the chamber's rule.

Nothing decays here. Nothing grows old. The chamber is young, and it will remain young. The vegetation is lush, and it will remain lush. The fauna will tend and be tended. The sound will continue. The warmth will hold. This is not equilibrium. Equilibrium is the endpoint of a process. This is something else — a place that was born already in its final state, that emerged from whatever violence split the rock already complete, already humming, already loud and warm and alive with its strange dry life, and it has not changed since the first second and will not change in the last.

The marks on the ceiling make a different kind of sense now.

---

You leave the way you came. The crack in the basalt. The surface. The weather. A world where things happen once and then happen differently. You will not find the chamber again. Not because it has moved or hidden itself, but because you have changed since being inside it, and it has not, and this discontinuity is enough to make you strangers.

In your dreams, for years, you will hear the knock. Every four seconds. Mechanical. Without origin. You will press your ear to walls in old buildings, listening for it. You will never find it. The Throat does not call. It has no reason to. It is already complete.
