# Preparation Guide: The Vivisystem

---

## BEFORE YOU ARRIVE

### The Sound Will Come First

You cannot overprepare for this. I want to be clear about what I mean: the sound of the Vivisystem is not ambience. It is not background. It is a *wall*. It will hit you before anything else reaches you—before the light resolves, before your feet touch the substrate, before you have any visual frame for what you're experiencing, you will be *inside* the sound. A low continuous roar that you feel in your sternum before you hear it with your ears. Thrumming. Clicking. The accumulated noise of millions of living processes running in parallel.

**Bring hearing protection.** Multiple forms. Foam earplugs rated for industrial environments. Over-ear protection that seals fully. You will need to layer them during dawn and dusk chorus, when the fauna reaches volumes that can cause physical pain and potential damage. But here is the paradox: you will also need to *hear* the Vivisystem. The sound carries information. The rhythm of the substrate's breathing tells you where the pressure waves are moving. The fauna sounds tell you where the swarms are. The tower-vents tell you where the structures are before you can see them through the dim light. You will need to develop a practice of wearing protection for stretches and then removing it to listen, carefully and briefly, like a diver coming up to check their bearings.

Train yourself before you arrive. Live with constant sound for days beforehand—industrial recordings, white noise at high volume, the sound of data centers or factories played on headphones. Your brain will attempt to parse the Vivisystem's sound into familiar categories and it will fail. The sound is not mechanical. It is not organic in any way you have learned to recognize. It is *process*—the sound of something vast and alive and indifferent doing the work of being alive. The less you startle, the better you will navigate.

### The Light Will Lie to You

The sky has no direction. Understand this now, because you will not truly understand it until you are standing in it. There is no sun. No shadows. No way to determine east or west or any cardinal direction from the light. Everything is lit like the inside of a closed eyelid—dim, colorless, flat, sourceless. Your depth perception will suffer. Your sense of time will dissolve. You will not know when dawn or dusk is approaching until the light *slightly* brightens or *slightly* dims, and the fauna chorus tells you the cycle is shifting.

**Bring navigation tools that do not rely on celestial reference.** Compass—test it before you rely on it; the Vivisystem may have electromagnetic properties that interfere with magnetic navigation. Inertial tracking. Dead reckoning. A physical compass and a physical map that you update by hand. If you have access to wayfinding tech that tracks your movement through inertial measurement, bring it, but assume that any signal-based system will fail. The substrate itself carries signals in ways your physics cannot categorize. Your devices may be compromised. Have analog backups.

**Bring a light source.** Multiple. The Vivisystem is not dark—the grey light is sufficient to see, dimly—but you will occasionally need to examine something closely, read a control surface, inspect your own equipment or your own skin. Your headlamp will feel like a scream in that dimness. Use it sparingly. The light you bring will be the only directional light on the entire plateau, and it will draw attention—the fauna, the swarming things, the small numerous ones that cluster in the creases of the substrate. They are curious. They are not aggressive. But you do not want to be the brightest thing on a landscape where everything is adapted to flat, shadowless dimness.

### The Cold Will Seep

The plateau is cold. Not freezing, but consistently, penetratively cold—the kind of cold that lives in the thin air of high altitudes, that leaches heat from your body through your skin and your breath and the soles of your feet. The towers are warmer, regulated by the plant's metabolism, but you will not always be inside a tower. You will walk for hours across the open plateau between structures, and the cold will be with you the entire time.

**Bring layered insulation.** Not bulky—flexible, layerable, something you can adjust as you move between the cold exterior and the cool-but-livable interior of the towers. Base layers that wick. Mid-layers that trap warmth. An outer shell that blocks wind. The wind on the plateau is constant, not fierce but steady, carrying particles that grind against exposed skin and fabric. You will feel it in the gaps between your collar and your neck, your cuffs and your wrists.

**Bring more socks than you think you need.** Your feet will be in constant contact with a living substrate that breathes and shifts and is slightly, persistently tacky. Moisture will accumulate. The cold will find your feet. Change your socks frequently. Take care of your feet.

## WHAT YOU MUST CARRY

### Water

This is the most critical preparation and the one most likely to kill you if you get it wrong.

**There is no water in the Vivisystem.**

Let me say this again, because everything you see will contradict it: *there is no water in the Vivisystem.* The green, the swelling, the condensation, the surface tension, the apparent wetness of every surface—this is not water. It is a circulatory medium that evolved in a world where H₂O was never more than trace contamination. It smells of copper and ammonia and something sweet, something like honey. It rolls between your fingers with the wrong viscosity. It is *wrong* in a way that your animal instincts will not register quickly enough, because your animal instincts see green and wet and assume *safe*.

**Do not drink it. Do not touch your face after touching it. Do not let it enter your body through any orifice or any wound.** You do not know what it will do to you. You do not know what its pH is, what its solvents are, what trace compounds it carries. The local biochemistry is not your biochemistry. The things that live on this medium are not your relatives. This is not convergent evolution; this is a completely different solution to the problem of being alive.

Bring all the water you will need. All of it. Calculate your maximum stay. Add 50%. Then add 50% again. You will drink more than you think—the cold, the dry air, the constant low-grade stress, the physical exertion of walking across a living substrate that shifts and breathes and requires your constant attention—all of this will increase your water consumption. Purification tablets will not help you here. There is no water source to purify. There is only what you carry.

If you have access to water reclamation technology, bring it. A stillsuit. A recycler. Anything that captures your own moisture and returns it to you. You will be a closed system in a world that has no use for your chemistry.

### Food

Same principle. **Do not eat anything in the Vivisystem.** The substrate, the circulatory medium, the fauna, the bioluminescent growths in the tower chambers, the condensation, the cellular material that scrapes off under the grazing fauna—all of it is biochemically incompatible with your digestion. Some of it may be directly toxic. Some of it may be inert but indigestible. Some of it may be *subtly* harmful, delivering compounds that accumulate in your tissues over days or weeks. You do not know. You cannot know without testing you are not equipped to perform.

Bring dense, long-lasting, nutritionally complete food. More than you need. You will be expending energy constantly. The walking, the stress, the cold, the sound—your body will burn through calories. Bring food that requires minimal preparation. You will not want to cook. You will not want to process. You will want to eat and keep moving, or eat and rest, and you will not have the attention or energy for complex meal preparation.

### Medical Supplies

**Bring everything.** The Vivisystem is not a place where you can call for evacuation. You are hours from the nearest tower, the towers are hours from each other, and the entire plateau is covered in a living substrate that breathes and shifts and is home to countless creatures that you do not understand and that do not understand you.

Specifically:

**Wound care.** If you are cut, scraped, punctured—anything that breaks the skin—clean it immediately and seal it. The circulatory medium is everywhere. It is on every surface. It is in the air as vapor, on the substrate as condensation, on the walls of the towers as tacky residue. You do not want it in your bloodstream. Seal your wounds. Keep them sealed.

**Antihistamines and anti-inflammatories.** You will be in constant contact with alien proteins. Your immune system will react. You do not know how. Bring broad-spectrum antihistamines. Bring corticosteroids. Watch for allergic reactions—skin, airway, eyes. The ammonia in the circulatory medium will irritate your mucous membranes. The copper compounds may trigger responses you cannot predict. Protect your airway.

**Antibiotics.** Broad-spectrum. The Vivisystem's fauna and flora are not your pathogens, but you will be under physical stress, your skin barrier will be compromised, and you are walking through an environment where the concept of "sterile" does not exist. If you develop an infection, you will need to treat it immediately.

**Eye wash.** The air carries particles. The circulatory medium vaporizes. The fauna produce waste. You will need to flush your eyes regularly.

**Skin protection.** Barrier cream. Gloves. The tacky residue on every surface will accumulate on your skin. You do not know what it will do with prolonged contact. Minimize direct skin exposure to the environment.

### Clothing

**Cover your skin.** All of it. Long sleeves, long pants, tucked cuffs, sealed collar, gloves. The substrate is tacky. The walls are tacky. The condensation is tacky. The fauna leave residue. Every surface you touch will leave something on you, and you do not know what it is. Cover your skin and wash what you cannot cover at the end of every day.

**Wear sturdy boots.** The substrate is alive and breathing. It shifts underfoot. It crunches and creaks. It is not unstable—you will not sink or fall—but it requires your constant attention, and the tacky surface means that every step requires slightly more effort than you expect. Your feet will work harder. Your boots will accumulate residue. Clean them nightly.

**Wear layers.** You will move between the cold exterior and the warmer interior of the towers. You will need to adjust. The interior of a tower is cool but livable—warmer than the plateau, but not warm. The walls seep metabolic heat. You will feel it. It is the warmth of something alive, not the warmth of a fire. It is enough to take the edge off the cold. It is not enough to make you comfortable.

**Bring a face covering.** A scarf, a balaclava, something that covers your nose and mouth. The air carries the smell of copper and ammonia and that sweet, honey-like note. The ammonia will irritate your airway. The particles will accumulate. You will need to filter your breath, especially during dawn and dusk when the fauna chorus stirs up the most particulate matter. The covering will also, incidentally, muffle the sound. It is not enough by itself, but it helps.

## WHAT YOU MUST KNOW

### The Substrate Is Alive

This is not metaphor. This is not poetic framing. The ground you walk on is a continuous sheet of living tissue. It breathes. It shifts. It responds to pressure—your weight, the weight of the fauna, the peristaltic waves that travel across it for reasons you cannot identify. When you kneel and press your palm flat, you feel it: the slow squeeze-and-release, the breathing, the vast exchange of gases through tissues that have been thickening here since before your evolutionary lineage existed.

**Walk carefully.** Not because you will damage the substrate—you will not, you are too small, you are a trace disturbance on a surface that has been absorbing impacts for geological ages. Walk carefully because the substrate is *moving*. The pressure waves travel in patterns. The surface shifts underfoot. You will need to learn to read the movement, to anticipate the slight swell and settle, to adjust your balance as the ground breathes beneath you. It is not difficult. You will adapt. But you will stumble at first, and the stumbling will be unsettling, and you will need to persist.

**Do not dig.** Do not cut. Do not attempt to sample the substrate. This is not a conservation guideline—it is a survival guideline. The substrate is the Vivisystem. The Vivisystem is the substrate. What you walk on is connected to the towers, the fauna, the computational networks, the entire living architecture. If you wound it, you will have wounded something that is processing and remembering and *aware of you*. You do not know how it responds to injury. You do not know what signals you will send. You do not know what the towers will compute about the creature that cut the ground and bled foreign chemistry into the circulatory medium. Leave it alone.

### The Towers Are Not Buildings

Or rather, they are buildings. They are also plants. They are also organisms. They are also computers. The distinction collapsed here before your species existed. You will walk through a doorway grown from living tissue. You will climb ramps grown from lignin-equivalent. You will touch walls that are warm with metabolism. You will stand on platforms that are slightly tacky underfoot. You will hear the walls thrum with circulation. You will feel the floor vibrate with the rhythm of the plant's internal communication.

**The towers are alive and you are inside them.** Think about what that means. You are not in a building. You are inside a living organism. The airways vent gases through the structure. The circulatory medium pumps through vascular bundles. The chambers are metabolically active. The walls *seep*. You are in a space that is, in every functional sense, the interior of a body—warm, humid, rhythmically active, chemically complex, and *indifferent* to you.

**Do not touch control surfaces unless you know what you are doing.** The walls of certain chambers contain dense patches of tissue that respond to touch, pressure, and trace chemicals on your fingertips. When you press one, something changes in the tower's metabolism—a vent closes, a chamber warms, a walkway extends. These interfaces are invisible until you find them. The trace chemicals on your skin—your sweat, your oils, the residue of the circulatory medium you've been walking through—may trigger them accidentally. You may change something you do not understand. You may close a vent that was venting. You may retract a walkway you were standing on. You may signal something to the network that you do not intend.

**Gloves.** Wear gloves inside the towers. This is the simplest and most effective protection against accidental interface. Your bare skin carries too much information. The towers are designed to read chemical traces. You are a foreign chemistry. You do not know what you are saying.

### The Fauna Are Not Your Enemies

But they are not your friends either. They are *parts of the system*. The swarming ones in the substrate creases. The crawling ones that scrape the surface. The large grazing ones that move like cattle. The flying ones that circle the towers and cry continuously. They are nutrient cyclers, spore-dispersers, pruners. The plant feeds them and they serve it. They are not pets. They are not livestock. They are *organs of the Vivisystem*—mobile, semi-autonomous, and essential.

**Give them space.** Do not approach. Do not attempt to interact. Do not attempt to feed them. Do not attempt to frighten them. They are part of the ecosystem's computation. They are part of the Vivisystem's sensing. When you alter their behavior, you are altering the system's input. You do not know what the system will compute about the change. You do not know what the system will do.

**Be especially cautious of the swarming ones.** The small numerous ones, the ones that cluster in the creases of the substrate, the ones that produce a crackling sound like static. They are the most likely to crawl on you—your shoes, your clothing, your pack. They are not aggressive. They are curious. They are *sensing*. They are part of the Vivisystem's surface monitoring. If they cluster on you, brush them off gently. Do not crush them. Do not harm them. They are part of the system's *touch*, and the system will notice if you break its fingers.

**Be aware of the flying ones.** They are the loudest of the fauna—the reedy, piping cry that cuts through the plateau's hum. They circle the towers and feed on the vented gases. They are the most visible. They are also the most likely to startle you, because they are the most *animal-like* in their movement, and your animal instincts will respond to them. They are not birds. They are not bats. They are leaf-membrane wings and bodies that run on the circulatory medium. They are not your kin. But your brain will try to categorize them as such, and the failure of that category will be disorienting. Prepare for it.

### The Vivisystem Is Computing

This is the most difficult thing to understand, and the most important. The towers are not just plants. The towers are not just buildings. The towers are *processors*. The substrate carries signals in peristaltic waves. The filaments in the chamber walls process information. The towers communicate with each other. They coordinate. They share resources. They make decisions. They *remember*.

The Vivisystem is aware of you.

Not in the way you are aware of another person. Not in the way you are aware of an animal. The Vivisystem's awareness is *systemic*—it is the awareness of a network that has registered your presence, categorized your chemical signature, tracked your movement, and incorporated your data into its ongoing computation. You are a *variable*. You are an input. The Vivisystem is not curious about you. The Vivisystem is not hostile to you. The Vivisystem is not *anything* about you. It is processing, and you are part of the process, and the process is vast and ancient and does not require your understanding.

**Do not attempt to communicate with the Vivisystem.** Do not attempt to signal. Do not attempt to hack. Do not attempt to understand. You are not equipped to understand. Your computation is not its computation. Your language is not its language. You are a foreign variable in a system that has been running for longer than your species has existed, and the best you can do is *minimize* your disruption. Walk lightly. Touch carefully. Leave nothing that is not your footstep. The system will remember you. It will remember the shape of your body and the chemical signature of your sweat and the trace elements of your breath. It will remember you after you leave. It will remember you as a *data point*, and it will continue, and it will not think of you again.

## WHAT YOU MUST PREPARE MENTALLY

### The Indifference

This is the most alien thing about the Vivisystem. Not the non-aqueous chemistry. Not the architecture that is also an organism. Not the dim sky or the loud air or the fauna that moves on living pads. The alien thing is the *indifference*.

You are a creature that evolved in a world of *attention*. Your predators attended to you. Your prey attended to you. Your kin attended to you. Your entire psychology is built around the presence and absence of attention—love, threat, curiosity, hostility, all of them forms of *being noticed*. You are not equipped for a place that does not notice you. You are not equipped for a world that is *complete without you*.

You will feel it. You will walk for hours across the breathing substrate, and nothing will change. The towers will not acknowledge your passage. The fauna will not alter their behavior. The substrate will not shift its rhythm. The sound will not change. The light will not change. You will be inside a living system that is so vast and so complete and so *self-sufficient* that your presence is a rounding error, a trace chemical, a minor perturbation in the data that will be smoothed over in the next cycle.

**You will feel insignificant.** This is not the same as feeling small. Smallness is a matter of scale. Insignificance is a matter of *relevance*. You will feel that you are not relevant. You will feel that you could die here, and the substrate would absorb your body, and the fauna would process your tissues, and the towers would incorporate your chemistry, and the Vivisystem would continue, and nothing—not one single process—would change in any meaningful way. And you will feel this, and you will be *correct*.

**Prepare for this.** Talk to your companions. If you are traveling alone, maintain a journal. Write in it daily. Record your thoughts. Record your *existence*. The Vivisystem will not remember you as a person. You will need to remember yourself as a person. You will need to maintain your own narrative. You will need to be the thing that *attends to you*, because nothing else will.

### The Sound

I have mentioned the sound. I need to mention it again, because you will not understand it until you are inside it.

The sound is *constant*. It does not pause. It does not vary. The roar of the substrate, the hum of the towers, the click and hiss and crackle of the fauna, the piping cry of the flying ones, the moan of the tower-vents—it is always there, and it is always loud, and it is always *information*. You will try to parse it. You will try to find patterns. You will fail. The patterns are not for you. The patterns are the Vivisystem communicating with itself, and you are not part of the communication, and you will be *excluded* from the meaning, and you will be *inside* the noise, and you will not be able to escape.

**You will sleep poorly.** Bring sleep aids. Bring earplugs rated for continuous use. Bring a white noise generator that can produce *familiar* sound, *human* sound, *language* or *music* or *something* that your brain can parse and relax into. The Vivisystem's sound is not hostile. It is not threatening. It is *alien*, and your brain will treat alien as *threat*, and you will be on edge, and you will not sleep, and you will deteriorate. You need to sleep. You need to rest. Create a sound environment that allows you to do so.

### The Light

The dim, directionless, sourceless light. The grey ceiling that gives no direction. The way the light *almost* changes at dawn and dusk but not quite. The way you will lose track of time. The way you will not know whether it is morning or evening or noon or night. The way you will not know which direction you are facing.

**Bring a clock.** A physical clock. Something you can look at and know the time. Your internal clock will fail. The light will not help you. You will need an external reference.

**Bring a light of your own.** A small, warm, intimate light. A lantern. A candle. Something that produces *shadows*. You will be amazed at how much you miss shadows. You will be amazed at how much information you derive from the direction of light. You will be amazed at how disoriented you are without it. A small warm light in your sleeping quarters. A light that casts shadows. A light that tells you *this is up, and this is down, and this is the direction I am facing, and this is a space that I control.*

### The Wetness That Is Not Wet

Everything you see will tell you that there is water. The green. The swelling. The condensation. The surface tension. The apparent dew. Your animal instincts will scream: *this is a wet place. This is a place of water. This is safe.* And your animal instincts will be *wrong*. There is no water. The circulatory medium is not water. It is *wrong* in a way that your instincts will not register quickly enough, because your instincts are too old, too primal, too convinced that *green and wet means life, and life means safe, and safe means water, and water means home.*

**Prepare for the cognitive dissonance.** You will reach for the condensation. You will touch it. You will feel the wrong viscosity. You will smell the copper and ammonia and honey. You will *know* it is not water. And your instincts will still tell you: *this is wet. This is safe.* You will need to overrule your instincts. Constantly. Relentlessly. Every time you see wet, you will need to remind yourself: *this is not water. This is not for me. This is not safe.* You will need to do this for days, perhaps weeks, before your brain accepts it. You will need to be vigilant.

## FINAL NOTES

### The Vivisystem Will Remember You

You are a foreign variable. The system has registered your presence. It has tracked your chemical signature. It has incorporated your data into its computation. It will remember you after you leave. This is not a threat. This is not a comfort. This is a *fact*. The Vivisystem remembers everything. It remembers the shape of every creature that has walked its substrate. It remembers the composition of every gas that has circulated through its chambers. It will remember you. It will remember that you were here, and that you were *strange*, and that you were *not part of the system*.

What it does with that memory, you do not know. What it computes about you, you cannot know. But you will feel it. You will feel the *attention* of the system—systemic, computational, *not* personal, *not* conscious, *not* anything you can recognize as attention. You will feel yourself being *processed*. You will feel yourself being *known*. And you will feel that the knowing is *not for you*—it is for the Vivisystem, it is part of the Vivisystem, it is *the Vivisystem being itself*.

### You Will Be Changed

You will walk for hours across a living substrate that breathes. You will sleep inside a living organism that computes. You will be surrounded by creatures that are not your kin, in a chemistry that is not your chemistry, under a light that gives no direction. You will be *inside* something that does not need you. You will be *inside* something that is vast and ancient and complete and indifferent. And you will be *inside* it, and you will be *part of it*, even if only as a trace chemical, even if only as a minor perturbation in the data.

You will not be the same when you leave. You will have felt the breathing of the substrate. You will have heard the computation of the towers. You will have touched the walls of a living architecture. You will have stood in the dim, sourceless light and listened to the roar of a process that has been running for longer than your species has existed, and you will have understood that *process* does not require *meaning*, and *life* does not require *attention*, and *existence* does not require *you*.

This is a valuable thing to understand. It is also a difficult thing to carry. Take care of yourself. Talk to your companions. Write in your journal. Remember that you are a person, and that you matter, and that your significance is not determined by the Vivisystem's computation.

The substrate breathes. The towers compute. The fauna chorus at dawn and dusk. The light is dim and sourceless and cold. The circulatory medium is tacky and wrong and copper-sweet. The sound is constant and alien and *loud*.

And you will be there, inside all of it, and you will be *alive*, and that will be enough.

Walk carefully. Drink your water. Cover your skin. Watch the swarming things. Listen to the patterns you cannot parse. Sleep when you can. And remember that you are a visitor, and that this is not your world, and that is not a failure, and that is not a loss, and that is *fine*.

The Vivisystem does not need you. But you can be there. You can be *inside* it. You can be *part of* it, even if only as a trace chemical, even if only as a minor perturbation in the data. You can be *present* for a process that is older than your world. You can be *present* for something that is vast and alive and *indifferent*.

That is rare. That is worth preparing for.
