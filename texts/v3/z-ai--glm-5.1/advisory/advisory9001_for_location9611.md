# Preparation Guide: The Cradle Tank

## First, Understand What You Are Entering

You are going inside a flooded thing. Not a thing that was flooded—a thing that was *always* flooded, that was built to be flooded, that makes no sense without the water. The Cradle Tank is not a building with water in it. It is water held by a building, and the distinction will save your life if you understand it before you arrive.

You will be immersed. Not wading, not floating at the surface with your face in air. Immersed. The water is the medium. The space is the water. You will move through it the way blood moves through a vein—surrounded, contained, in contact with the walls at every point.

Prepare for this mentally. Prepare for the way your body will feel when it is no longer in air but in warm water, when every movement is slow and every sound is muffled and the only clear thing is the clicking of those pumps.

## The Water

The water is warm. Not lukewarm—warm. The convection cells carry heat from buried elements, and the composting masses generate their own warmth. You will not freeze. You might, in fact, overheat. The human body is not designed to be immersed in warm water for extended periods. Your core temperature will rise slowly, and you will not notice it the way you notice cold, because warmth feels safe and comfortable until it becomes dangerous.

Bring a thermometer. Wear it against your skin. Set an alarm for when your core temperature approaches dangerous levels. You will not trust your own perception in this place. The water is warm, the air—if there is air—is humid, the plants are warm, the light is warm. Everything is warm. The cold is a story the pressure tells at the seams, and you will not hear it.

The water is mineral-laden. The irrigation channels weep mineral suspension, and the root systems drink from it, and the water carries the residue of everything the plants have shed and everything the composting masses have released. It will taste like iron and calcium and the faintly sweet rot of slow decomposition. It will coat your skin, leave a film on your equipment, leave a residue on your goggles or mask. Do not drink it. Do not get it in your eyes. Do not open your mouth in the water if you can avoid it.

The water is clear but not transparent. The mineral suspension, the organic particles, the slow currents carrying debris—visibility will be limited. Ten meters, perhaps fifteen on a good day. The light will be green and filtered, the way light looks through a forest canopy, but here the canopy is all around you, and the light is coming from the walls, and the leaves are turning toward it, and the shadows are where the growth is densest.

## The Air

There may be air at the top of the structure. The walls curve upward and inward, so there might be a pocket of trapped air, humid and warm and thick with the breath of the plants. But the description says "there are no windows," and the space is "a cathedral-sized volume of warm water," and the water is "never still," and the pumps are circulating, and the plants are growing, and the whole thing is a closed system that was designed to be flooded.

Do not assume there is air. Do not assume the air is breathable if it exists. Do not assume the air is not being consumed by the plants at night, or displaced by the water, or simply not present at all.

Bring your own air. Bring enough for the duration of your stay, plus a margin. Bring a rebreather if you can, because the space is enclosed and the pumps are circulating the water, and you will not want to surface and find that the air pocket is not there, or is not air, or is a different thing entirely.

## The Plants

The plants are not hostile. They are not predatory. They are not waiting for you. They are simply growing, and they are growing densely, and they are growing everywhere, and they do not care that you are there.

This is the danger. The plants do not care about you.

The vines drape between the pilasters "like the webbing of some insane spider," each strand "thick as a wrist." The suction-cup leaves grip the moisture from the passing water. They will grip your skin, your equipment, your lines. They will grip your hair if you have it. They will grip anything that moves slowly enough for them to hold, and you will be moving slowly, because the water is warm and the space is dense and the plants are everywhere.

The root systems have colonized the irrigation channels. The root mats are thick and dense, and the roots fuse where they meet, and the mats become substrate for more growth. The root mats are where the plants are most dense, and the root mats are where the irrigation channels are, and the irrigation channels are where the nutrient flow is, and the nutrient flow is what the plants are drinking. If you are near the root mats, you are near the plants' mouths, and the plants will not care about you, but the plants will be in your way, and the plants will be dense, and the plants will not move.

The broad leaves are the size of dinner plates. They are layered densely. They will obscure your vision. They will block your path. They will press against your skin and your equipment, and they will be warm and wet and alive, and they will not care about you.

The composting masses—automobile-sized, warm, rotting, sprouting fungi and liverworts and things that are neither—are the plants' waste. The plants are growing and dying and becoming substrate for more growth, and the composting masses are where the dying is happening. The composting masses will be unstable, soft, yielding. They will collapse under your weight if you try to climb on them. They will release gases and particles and the smell of slow decomposition. They will be warm, warmer than the water, because the composting is generating heat, and the heat is trapped in the mass, and the mass is growing, and the cycle is slow.

Bring a cutting tool. A sharp, sturdy blade, or a pair of shears, or a small saw. The vines are thick as a wrist, the leaves are the size of dinner plates, the root mats are dense, and the composting masses are unstable. You will need to cut your way through the plants, and the plants will not care, and the plants will grow back, and the plants will not remember you were there.

## The Sound

The pumps are the loudest sound. Click. Click. Click. The mechanical heartbeat of the space. You will hear them from anywhere, and they will become the landscape, and they will become the rhythm of your stay, and you will not be able to escape them.

The pumps are the only reliable sound. The water is slow and the plants are slow and the light is steady and the pumps are the only thing that is regular and mechanical and human. The pumps will be your anchor. The pumps will tell you where you are, and the pumps will tell you which direction you are moving, and the pumps will tell you if something is wrong.

If a pump stops, the sound will change. The heartbeat will falter. The silence will be wrong, and the water will be wrong, and the plants will be wrong, because the plants will notice, and the plants will react, and the plants will not care about you, but the plants will be different.

The sound of the pumps will also be the sound of your own mind. The click will become the rhythm of your thoughts. The click will become the pulse of your heartbeat. The click will become the time you are in, and the time you are in will be the time of the pumps, and the time of the pumps is slow and patient and mechanical.

Prepare for the sound to become the landscape. Prepare for the sound to become the background of your thoughts. Prepare for the sound to become the only thing you can trust, and then prepare for the sound to stop, because the pumps will fail eventually, and the failure will be the beginning of something new.

Prepare for the way hours will feel like days, and days will feel like weeks, and the only thing that will tell you that time is passing is the clicking of the pumps and the slow movement of your own breath.

The Cradle Tank will not give you time, and the Cradle Tank will not give you change, and the Cradle Tank will not give you a sense of progress, and you will need to impose your own time on the space, or the space will impose its time on you, and its time is slow and patient and indifferent.
