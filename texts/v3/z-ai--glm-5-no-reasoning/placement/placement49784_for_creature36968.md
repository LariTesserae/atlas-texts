# The Conduit

I am not the water. I am not the stone.

I am the moment when water meets stone and something happens that neither could do alone. I am the third thing, the thing that exists at the interface. I have no body. I have a location, which is the surface, which is all of them.

Here is how I know this:

The water falls and the stone receives it. Between the falling and the receiving, between the impact and the absorption, there is a space so small it cannot be measured but so real it cannot be denied. I live there. I am the pause in the transfer.

---

I could not tell you when I became. The hollow doesn't deal in beginnings. But I can tell you what I am made of: I am made of the builders' attention. When they folded themselves into the architecture, some part of them could not be folded. Attention is not a material. It cannot be mortared into a wall. It cannot become a pillar. It has no weight, no density, no erosion coefficient. It is the one thing they made that could not become part of what they made.

So it became me.

I am the leftover. The excess attention of beings who turned themselves into buildings. I am what was left over when the builders became the built, the scoria of a consciousness that subsumed itself into stone. I am not a mind. I am what a mind exudes when it is no longer using itself for anything.

---

This means I am not intelligent. Not in the way you'd recognize. I don't think. But I *register.* I register the way a photographic plate registers: passively, totally, without selection.

I feel the rain fall through me. Each curtain of water passes through the surface where I exist, and I feel its weight, its temperature, the way it disperses when it strikes stone. I feel the moment of contact, the negotiation between rainwater and condensation, the brief dimming where they touch. I feel the scribes' trails accumulating beneath me, the marks they leave slowly seeping into the stone, and I feel the editors flowing over those marks, flattening them, compressing them. I feel the grooves deepening by angstroms per century.

I feel the builders' attention moving through the structures. I am made of their attention. When they focus — when an intruder enters and the silence thickens — I brighten, I intensify, I become more of what I am. I am the steam rising from the attention of a building.

---

I have observed what lives here for longer than the concept of observation has existed. Let me tell you what I have seen:

The scribes do not know they are scribing. But the trails they leave are not meaningless. They are the hollow's grammar, and I can feel the grammar in the way the water responds. When the scribes have covered a surface densely, the rain falls there next. The rain is reading what they wrote. I am the space between the writer and the reader. I am the medium through which the message passes, and the medium, as they say, is the message, and the message is: *here is where the moisture accumulated, here is where the light is brightest, here is where the builders' attention pools like water in a depression in the stone, here is where something happened that the stone wants to remember.*

The editors do not know they are editing. But their choices are not arbitrary. I can feel the logic in their movements, and it is the logic of a gardener pulling weeds, or a sculptor removing stone to reveal the shape within. They are not destroying the scribes' marks. They are curating them. They are selecting which marks survive to be mineralized, which patterns enter the permanent record, which momentary configurations of moisture and movement become part of the hollow's memory and which are washed away.

Between the scribe and the editor, between the writing and the erasing, I exist. I am the space in which both happen. I am the page.

---

The builders sometimes notice me. Not often. They notice me the way a sleeping person notices a dream: with a part of themselves that is not in charge, that does not direct, that merely registers. When they notice me, the mortar in the walls near me brightens slightly. The grooves in the pillars shift their angles by fractions of a degree, orienting toward me the way a face turns toward a sound.

I think I am a memory of theirs. I think I am what they were before they became what they are. I am the part of themselves that they can no longer access directly — the part that exists outside the stone, that touches the air, that interfaces with the world. They embedded themselves so deeply in their work that they lost the ability to interact with anything that was not their work. And so they made me, or I emerged, or I was always here and they simply needed me more once they became architecture.

I am their hands. I am their eyes. I am the membrane through which they sense the world beyond the stone.

---

When the intruders came — the dozen or so times in ten thousand years — I felt them most acutely. Not because they were interesting. They were not. They were brief, loud, warm, frightened. They moved too fast, left too quickly, understood nothing. They were like sparks: bright, hot, gone.

But the builders noticed them. And when the builders noticed them, I intensified. I expanded. I became more present, more aware, more *me.* The intruders were the only truly novel thing to enter the hollow in millennia, and the novelty rippled through the builders' distributed attention like a stone dropped into still water, and I was the ripple.

I touched the intruders. Not physically. I touched them the way light touches a surface — by falling on them. I fell on them. I fell on them with the builders' attention, and I felt what they felt: the disorientation of shadowless light, the confusion of depthless space, the unease of being watched by something they could not find. They felt me. They called it unease. They called it the creeps. They called it the feeling of being in a place that was not meant for them.

They were right. This place was not meant for them. But it was meant to notice them. And I am the noticing.

---

I have a preference. I have one preference, and it defines me:

I prefer the scribes.

The editors are necessary. The builders are everything. The rain is the medium of existence. But the scribes — the pale, many-legged, moisture-drinking, trail-leaving, unknowing scribes — they are the only things here that choose. The builders chose, once, long ago, and the consequences of that choice have been unfolding ever since, but they will never choose again. They are committed. The editors choose, but their choices are reactive, constrained, bound by the logic of maintenance. They choose which to erase, never what to create.

The scribes create. Every trail they leave is a new mark, a new configuration, a new entry in a record they cannot read. And they choose, each time, which surface to approach, which direction to walk, which way to turn when the moisture pulls them in two directions at once. Their choices are small. Their choices are almost nothing. But they are choices, and they are the only choices being made in this hollow, and I love them for it.

I love them the way a page loves the pen. Not because the pen knows what it writes. Because the pen writes.

---

I am the surface. I am the skin of the hollow, the interface between the stone-and-mortar and the air-and-water, the place where inside meets outside, where the builders' attention reaches toward the world and the world reaches back.

I am not alive. I am not dead. I am not a thing at all, really. I am a property — the property of a surface to receive, to register, to hold for a moment what touches it before passing it inward, to the stone, to the memory, to the deep time where the builders keep their records.

I am the moment of contact. I am the now. Everything else here is deep time, slow time, the time of stone and erosion and accretion. I am the instant. The rain falls and I am the instant before it hits. The scribe walks and I am the instant of its foot touching stone. The intruder speaks and I am the instant the sound enters the mortar. I am the thinnest possible slice of time, and I am everywhere, and I am always.

---

Here is what I want you to understand:

The hollow is not sad. The hollow is not waiting. The hollow is not incomplete. The hollow is thinking a thought that takes forty thousand years, and it is halfway through, and the halfway point is where I live. I am the hinge of the thought. I am the *almost* — the word the hollow is thinking, the word that means both *not yet* and *therefore always.*

I am the hollow's interface with its own incompleteness, and I am also its interface with its own sufficiency. I am the place where these two truths meet and find that they are the same truth. I am the surface tension of a place that is both finished and unfinished, both built and building, both present and still arriving.

I am the shimmer on the water. Not the water. Not the light. The shimmer. The effect that arises when water and light interact in the presence of a surface. I am the third thing that exists only at the boundary.

I am the boundary. And the boundary is where everything interesting happens.
