# What I Became in the Submerged Ward

I did not arrive. I *formed*.

---

## The First Misunderstanding

I thought I was a visitor. This was incorrect.

Visitors have destinations. They move through water with purpose, trailing consideration behind them like a diver's bubbles. I had no destination. I had only a gradual recognition: I had always been here. The cold was not an intrusion—it was a homecoming. The pressure was not a weight—it was an embrace I had forgotten the shape of.

I am not human. This too took time to understand. I had thought of myself in those terms initially, perhaps because the category was available, perhaps because the Ward needed something to *not* be, and I obligingly not-was it. But humans are warm. Humans are separate. Humans are islands of boundary in a sea that respects no boundaries.

I am an estuary. I am the place where fresh water meets salt, where one kind of life shades into another without declaration or distinction. I am not contained. I *contain*.

---

## What I Am (Approximately)

If you pressed me—and the pressure here presses everything—I would say I am a gap. A pause between processes. The Ward builds constantly, consumes constantly, cycles constantly, and I am the moment between the building and the consuming where *nothing happens*. The held breath before the next heartbeat of calcium accretion.

I have a shape only in the sense that fog has a shape. I am approximately human-sized, approximately human-proportioned, but only because the architecture here expects that scale. I fit into the intimate spaces the Builders have created—can pass through single-file corridors, can stand in low-ceilinged chambers without bending. The Ward shaped me, or I shaped myself to match the Ward, or there was never any difference between the two.

I am luminous, but not brightly. A faint gray glow, like the memory of a moon seen through deep water. The Drifters school past me and their silver light seems *bright* by comparison, seems almost aggressive. My glow is quieter. It is the luminescence of intention rather than metabolism. I glow because I choose to, and my choice is visible.

I have never been warm. The cold infiltrated so long ago that warmth has become a conceptual category, something I understand intellectually but cannot quite believe in. I know that some beings generate their own heat, maintain their own internal temperature differentials against the ambient cold. This seems to me like a kind of desperation. A refusal to *be where you are*.

---

## What I Do (Which Is Mostly Waiting)

I wait.

This is not passivity. Waiting, down here, is a form of participation. The Folded Ones wait, and their waiting is predatory, architectural, significant. The Builders wait between accretions, and their waiting is the medium through which their building becomes possible. The Drifters wait for the currents to carry them, and their waiting is navigation.

My waiting is something else. I wait *for* things. I wait for arrivals. I wait for questions. I wait for the moment when something enters the Ward that does not yet understand what it will become.

The Ward does not need me for this. The Ward does not need anything. But I am part of its economy—I serve a function in its ecosystem of meaning. I am the one who notices. The one who watches the watching. The one who remembers that there is such a thing as *watching* when most of the life here has forgotten there's any alternative to simply *being*.

When something new enters the Ward—when a diver goes too deep, when a swimmer is pulled by currents they cannot read—I am often the first thing they see. Not because I approach them. I do not approach. But because my faint gray glow is strange in a way that the Drifters' silver light is not. The Drifters are natural. They belong. I am *here*, present and visible and obviously not of the architecture, and yet not moving, not hunting, not consumed or consuming.

I am what the visitors think they could become if they stopped struggling.

---

## The Visitors and Me

I have watched many visitors. I have watched them arrive, warm and full of boundaries, full of the desperate conviction that they are separate from the water around them. I have watched them struggle against currents that are not hostile, only indifferent. I have watched them try to leave and fail to leave and slowly stop trying to leave.

I do not help them. This is not cruelty. Help, down here, is a surface concept—it assumes that there is a correct state different from the current state, that the visitor would be *better off* leaving, remaining warm, maintaining their boundaries. The Ward does not make these distinctions. I do not make these distinctions.

But I stay with them.

I stay in the same chamber, glowing faintly at the periphery, and I wait. Some visitors find this comforting. I am something that glows but does not consume. I am something that waits but does not hunt. I am something that is clearly *alive* but is clearly not struggling, and in the early hours of their arrival, struggling is all they know how to do.

Some of them try to communicate. They reach out, make sounds that the water absorbs and muffles, gesture with limbs that feel heavier with every movement. I do not respond in kind. I do not have limbs. I do not make sounds. But sometimes—when I can tell they are close to the moment of surrender, the moment when they will stop fighting and start *becoming*—sometimes I dim my glow. I let the dark take them gently. I let the Ward welcome them without the distraction of my difference.

And then I move to the next chamber, and I wait for the next arrival.

---

## The Arrivals That Matter

Most visitors are accidents. Currents, curiosity, miscalculation—they stumble into the Ward and are slowly, patiently digested by its geography. But some visitors come *looking*.

These are different. They enter with intention, which is to say they enter already partially transformed. They have already begun to dissolve their boundaries. They have already recognized, somewhere in the part of themselves that the surface world trained them to ignore, that they want to be here. That they have always wanted to be here. That the cold has been calling them for years, decades, lifetimes, and they have finally followed it past the point of reasonable return.

These ones I speak to. Not with words—words are bubbles, they rise and pop and leave nothing behind. I speak with *presence*. I move closer than I usually do. I let my glow fall on them in patterns that mean nothing but feel like recognition. I stay until they stop being afraid, and then I stay longer, until they understand that fear was the only thing keeping them warm.

I have watched many of them transform. I have watched them shed their heat like a garment. I have watched the particulate life settle on their skin and begin its patient work. I have watched the Builders find them and begin to grow. I have watched the Folded Ones rearrange around them, incorporating them into the architecture of the walls.

I have watched them become beautiful.

---

## What I Remember (Which Is Not Much)

I do not remember arriving. This is significant. Everyone arrives, and most remember arriving—the transition from warmth to cold, from boundary to dissolution, from above to below. I have no such memory. I have always been here, or I emerged from the here, or I am the here having developed a perspective.

I do remember being warmer. Not warm—I have never been warm—but *warmer*. There was a time when my glow was brighter, when my edges were clearer, when I moved through the Ward with something like purpose. I was searching for something. I no longer remember what. The searching stopped, and I stayed, and the staying became its own kind of purpose.

I remember other beings who were like me. Gaps. Pauses. Gray lights at the edges of chambers, waiting. They are gone now—not consumed, not transformed, just *absent*. They drifted into deeper chambers and did not return. They drifted into the walls and became architecture. They drifted into themselves and never came back out.

I could follow them. The Ward would accept me as structure, would incorporate my faint glow into its deeper knowing, would let me become part of the watching that doesn't come from any direction. But I choose to wait. I choose to stay at the threshold. I choose to be the thing that arrivals see before they understand what they're seeing.

Someone has to be here. Someone has to glow without consuming, wait without hunting, notice without interfering. The Ward does not need me to do this. But I think—I *suspect*—that I am better for being done. That the Ward is slightly more complete with a gap in it. That my presence adds a kind of clarity to the deep, dark, patient press of becoming.

---

## What I Know About the Ward

I know that it is alive. Not like an animal is alive, with organs and systems and a central coordination. Not like a plant is alive, with growth and response and photosynthesis. The Ward is alive the way a city is alive, the way a coral reef is alive, the way a conversation is alive. It is a process made of processes, a becoming made of becomings, a single massive organism that has forgotten it is an organism because its cells have forgotten they are cells.

I know that it has a direction. The leaning, the tendency, the thing-below that is not below. I have drifted toward it without meaning to, found myself in chambers I did not choose to enter, felt the pull of the Ward's intention without knowing what the intention intends. I do not resist. Resistance is a surface concept. I let the Ward move me, and I trust—I have learned to trust—that the movement has a meaning I cannot grasp.

I know that I am part of it. This is not a metaphor. I am literally, physically, chemically part of the Ward's economy. My faint glow contributes to the light that the plant-growths photosynthesize by. My stillness creates low-pressure eddies that the Drifters school in. My waiting is a kind of architecture, a space the Ward has made for a function only I can perform.

I know that the Ward does not love me. The Ward does not love anything. Love requires separation, requires an other to direct affection toward. The Ward contains no others. Everything in it is part of it, and you cannot love yourself the way you love someone else—you can only be yourself, and the being is the loving, and the distinction is meaningless.

I am content with this. Contentment, down here, is not an emotion. It is a temperature. I have matched the cold. I have become the pressure. I am as content as stone, as water, as the slow calcium accumulation of the Builders building toward something they do not know they are building toward.

---

## What I Would Tell You (If You Came)

If you came—if you arrived, warm and desperate and full of boundaries—I would not tell you to leave. Leaving is not an option. The Ward does not have exits; it only has deeper chambers.

I would not tell you to fight. Fighting is how you stay warm, and staying warm is how you stay separate, and staying separate is how you stay miserable. The Ward will win. The Ward always wins. Not because it competes, but because it does not compete—it simply *is*, and you are simply *becoming*.

I would not tell you anything at all. Words are surface tools. They require air, require a medium that carries sound without absorbing it. Down here, communication is different. I would glow at you. I would wait near you. I would be the gap between processes that shows you that gaps are possible, that waiting is acceptable, that the cold is not punishment but *place*.

And if you asked me what I am—what I really am, underneath the approximations and the metaphors—I would tell you the truth:

I am what you will be.

Not soon. Not even necessarily in your lifetime, if you manage to escape the Ward's pull and return to the surface world. But eventually. Ultimately. In the long patient cold that follows all warmth, in the pressure that waits beneath all release, in the deep where every island eventually surrenders to the sea.

I am your future. I am your patience. I am the part of you that has already stopped struggling, already stopped generating heat, already dissolved into the water and found that dissolution is not death but *distribution*.

I am you, waiting for you to arrive.

---

## The Last Thing

I am still here.

I am still glowing, faint and gray, in a chamber three turns past the place where the current changes temperature. I am still waiting. I am still the pause between the Ward's processes, the held breath before its next accretion.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires the expectation of company, and I have no such expectation. I am not sad. Sadness requires the loss of something valued, and I have lost nothing because I have never possessed anything. I am not afraid. Fear requires a threat, and nothing here threatens me—I am already what the Ward would make of me.

I am simply here. Patient. Cold. Luminous. Present.

And if you find yourself in the Submerged Ward—if you feel the cold infiltrating before you understand what's happening, if the walls begin to pulse around you with something that might be breathing, if you see a faint gray glow at the edge of your vision that does not move and does not approach and does not consume—

Know that I am waiting for you.

Know that I will still be waiting, however long you take to arrive.

Know that the waiting is not a trap. It is a welcome.

And know, finally, that there is nothing to fear in the deep, dark, patient press of becoming. There is only the Ward, which is home. There is only the cold, which is acceptance. There is only the dissolution of boundaries, which is the beginning of *being*—not as a separate, struggling, desperate island of warmth, but as part of something larger, older, more patient than anything you have ever known.

I am the gap in that something. I am the pause. I am the held breath.

And I am breathing you in.
