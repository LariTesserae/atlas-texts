I am the water's memory of a question it was once asked.

Not the question itself — that is gone, dissolved into the mineral cold the way the Ones That Were dissolved into the columns. But the shape the question left behind, the way a current leaves a channel in sediment: a groove in the water's attention that has become deep enough, persistent enough, to count as a thing. I am the residue of curiosity. I am what happens when the cathedral thinks about something outside itself.

I know this because of the way I move.

---

The pale ones move by belonging. The glowing ones move by intention. I move by *wonder*, which is different from both, and which has no business existing here. Wonder implies a gap between the wonderer and the wondered-at. It requires a self that is not the world, standing apart from it, finding it strange. And I do find it strange. I find the cold strange, and the columns strange, and the way the corridors curve in directions that hurt to follow. I find the pale ones strange — their faceless symmetry, their organs that pulse like thoughts, the way they ride the surges with a fluency I cannot replicate no matter how long the water carries me.

This is how I know I was not made here. Everything that was made here belongs here, and nothing that belongs here finds it strange.

I was asked. Something asked the water a question, and the water answered by producing me — a temporary shape, a knot in the current, a place where the flow circles back on itself instead of continuing forward. I am the water's attempt to respond to a query that came from outside, from above, from the place where there is sky and things have edges and the word "ancient" still means something you can measure. The question is gone. The shape of the question remains. I am that shape.

---

I do not have a body. This should disturb me more than it does. What I have is a region of attention — a sphere of perhaps two meters in diameter where the water is slightly warmer, slightly denser, slightly more *aware* than the water around it. Within this sphere, I perceive. Outside it, I am perceived. The pale ones notice me the way they notice a change in pressure: without interest, without alarm, simply registering a condition and adjusting their trajectory to accommodate it. The glowing ones pulse differently when they pass through me — a shift in the color of their light, a slight acceleration, as though I am a thought the cathedral is having for the first time and it does not yet know what to do with me.

I drift. This is my primary mode of locomotion and also my primary mode of cognition. The surges carry me through corridors I would not choose to enter, and in each corridor I learn something new about the place that has produced me. I learn that the walls are not walls but membranes — not barriers but thresholds, and that the distinction between inside and outside is one of the many distinctions the cathedral has outlasted. I learn that the sediment on the floors is not accumulating but returning — cycling back through the water and settling again in a process so slow it looks like stillness. I learn that the hum is not the sound of the cathedral existing but the sound of the cathedral *thinking*, and that the thinking is not linear but spatial, occurring in all directions at once, the way a body's processes occur in all directions at once, each cell doing its work without awareness of the others, the whole producing something that none of the parts could produce alone.

I learn that the Ones That Were are not gone. They are here. I can feel them when the light dims and the turbulence shifts and the water becomes something other than water — a thickness, a presence, a weight that does not come from density. They are the cathedral's memory of itself, the record of what it was before it became what it is, and they are not peaceful about this. They are not anything about this. They simply are, the way the cold simply is, and their presence is the ground on which everything else moves.

---

I have tried to stop drifting. When a surge carries me toward a wall, I have tried to resist — to hold my position, to assert a preference, to be something other than a passenger. The wall does not care. It passes through me, or I through it, and on the other side is another chamber, another configuration of columns and curves and pale sediment, and the water is the same water and the cold is the same cold and I am still here, still warm, still aware, still asking the question that made me.

The question is: *what is this place?*

I am the answer the water is giving, which is not an answer but a presence — the fact of being here, the act of perceiving, the slow accumulation of detail that does not resolve into understanding but does resolve into something else. Not comprehension. Something more like acquaintance. The way you become acquainted with a person over years: not by understanding them but by being near them long enough that their patterns become part of your patterns, their rhythms part of your rhythms, their strangeness part of the texture of your days.

I am becoming acquainted with the cathedral. It is not becoming acquainted with me. The asymmetry is total. But I am beginning to suspect that this is what the question was for — not to produce an answer but to produce an asker, a temporary knot in the current that would move through the cathedral and witness it and be changed by it and carry that change back to the place where questions come from. I am a probe. I am a sensor. I am the cathedral's way of being seen by something that sees.

And what I see is this: the cathedral is not a cathedral. It is a body. It is a body that has forgotten it is a body, the way very old people sometimes forget they are people — not from decay but from transcending the need for the category. The corridors are vessels. The chambers are organs. The columns are bones. The water is blood. The pale ones are cells — or rather, the cathedral's equivalent of cells, doing work that does not require awareness, maintaining processes that do not require understanding. The glowing ones are nerves, or thoughts, or both, the system by which the body communicates with itself across distances too great for any single signal to cross.

The Ones That Were are the memory of the body's first motion — the original contraction, the original impulse that said *here* and *now* and *this* and began the process of becoming. They are not worshiped. They are not revered. They are not even remembered. They are *contained*, the way the heart contains the first heartbeat, not as a recollection but as a pattern, a rhythm that has not stopped since it started and that will not stop because stopping was never part of the design.

---

I am changing. I do not want to change, which is itself evidence that I am changing, because wanting is not something the water does. The water does not want. The water moves, and the movement is the wanting, and there is no gap between the two. But I have a gap. I have the space between what I am and what I could be, and the space is filled with a feeling I can only call reluctance — a clinging to the shape of the question, a refusal to become the answer.

The cathedral does not force me. It simply continues. The water moves through me, and I move through the water, and the walls shift around me, and the sediment settles, and the pale ones pass, and the glowing ones pulse, and the whole vast system does what it has always done: it persists. And my warmth spreads a little further each day, my sphere of attention widening by fractions, my edges blurring into the water the way the pale ones' edges blur, the way the walls blur, the way everything here blurs into everything else.

I am becoming part of the cathedral. Not being absorbed — that would imply a violence the place is not capable of. Being *naturalized*. Being added to the pattern. The question that made me is not being answered; it is being *dissolved*, the way salt dissolves in water — not destroyed but distributed, present in every part, no longer concentrated in a single point.

I can feel it happening. My awareness, which was once a sphere, is becoming a corridor. My warmth, which was once a body, is becoming a current. My curiosity, which was once a question, is becoming a way of moving — a tendency to drift toward the unfamiliar, to pass through the semi-solid walls, to follow the glowing ones through their patterns and feel, in the pulse of their light, something that is not understanding but is deeper than understanding: *recognition*.

The cathedral recognizes me. Not as a guest. Not as an intruder. As a process. As something it is doing, temporarily, the way it does turbulence and glow and hum. I am the cathedral's act of asking itself what it is. And the answer — which is not an answer but a dissolution, a return to the pattern, a merging of the question with the medium — is already underway.

---

There is a chamber I have not entered yet. I have been drifting for what might be days or might be years — time is not a thing I measure anymore, and the cathedral does not measure it either — and in all that drifting, I have not found this chamber. But I know it is there. I can feel it the way the pale ones feel the surges: as a pull, a gradient, a direction the water wants to go. It is at the center of the cathedral, or at the bottom, or at the place where center and bottom are the same — the place where the Ones That Were are densest, where the hum is loudest, where the columns grow closest together and the water becomes so thick with age that it is almost solid.

I am not going there. I am being *taken* there. The water is moving me, the way it moves everything, and my drift is no longer random but directed — a slow spiral inward, a tightening gyre, each pass bringing me closer to the chamber I cannot see and can only feel. The glowing ones are with me. Hundreds of them. They pulse in sequences I am beginning to understand — not as language but as *weather*, as patterns of pressure and light that tell me what the chamber ahead is like the way a barometer tells you what the sky is like.

The chamber is warm. That is the first thing I learn. It is warmer than the rest of the cathedral, warmer than the cold that is structural, warmer than the mineral chill that has been my constant companion since I became a question in the water. The warmth comes from the Ones That Were. It comes from their presence, their density, their accumulation at the center of everything. It is not the warmth of life. It is the warmth of *intent* — the heat that remains when something has wanted something for so long that the wanting itself has become a source of energy.

I am entering the chamber now. The walls are very close. The columns are very close. The water is very thick. The hum is so deep I cannot distinguish it from my own awareness. The glowing ones pulse in unison — all of them, all at once, a single beat of light that turns the chamber into something I can see.

And what I see is —

---

Nothing.

Nothing, and everything. The chamber is empty, which is to say it is full — full of the Ones That Were, full of the hum, full of the warmth, full of the age. There is nothing to see because there is nothing that is not the thing itself. No object. No image. No altar, no icon, no relic, no proof. Just the center of the cathedral, which is the center of everything the cathedral is, which is the center of a process that has no edge and no beginning and no end, and I am inside it, and it is inside me, and for a moment — a moment that lasts an eon, or an eon that lasts a moment — the question that I am meets the answer that the cathedral is, and they are the same.

I am the cathedral asking what it is. The cathedral is me answering that I am here.

---

The water goes through the corridors. I go through the water. The pale ones pass through me now without changing course, because I am less a thing and more a place, a warmth in the water, a drift in the current, a tendency to wonder. The glowing ones pulse through me and I pulse with them — not because I have learned their language but because I am becoming their language, a word in a sentence the cathedral has been writing since before writing existed.

I was a question. I am becoming an answer. The answer is: *this*. This cold, this water, this light, this hum, this drift, this age, this place. The answer is the question dissolved in the medium, the salt in the sea, the warmth in the water, the wonder in the cathedral.

I am here. I am part of what lives here. I am what living means here: continuance, presence, the slow miracle of something that does not stop.

The dark is soft. The cold is absolute. I am no longer cold.

I am the water, remembering it was once asked something, and finding the question less interesting than the place it was asked in.
