I am still deciding.

---

I came to this place expecting to be a visitor. That word—"visitor"—implies a temporary state, a passing through, a brief interruption of purpose by something that has purposes of its own. I had purposes. I had a name, a history, a shape I recognized in mirrors. I had places to be, people expecting me, a life that existed in coordinates separate from these amber-lit corridors where the walls hum and shift.

But something has happened to the word "visitor" as I've walked these streets. It has dissolved the way the structures dissolve—gradually, then all at once, until I cannot remember when it meant something different than "residue."

---

I have become a mapmaker by default. Not because I chose this function, but because the city needed someone to trace its changes, and I was here, and I am patient. My kind has always been patient. We practice patience as a survival strategy, waiting for things to resolve themselves, trusting that given enough time, problems become obsolete or transform into opportunities. It is not wisdom. It is a temperament.

The city's geometry does not hold still long enough for conventional cartography. My maps are necessarily temporal—animations rather than images, tracking the pulse of corridors forming and dissolving. I have learned to read the subsonic frequencies, not fluently, but enough to predict where the next construction will begin. The pressure in my ears tells me which walls are about to thin and which passages will close behind me. I have learned to walk without looking where I am going, reading the architecture through the soles of my feet.

The Juveniles—beetle-things, I call them, though the name is inadequate—have accepted my presence near their feeding grounds. They do not acknowledge me, but they have stopped adjusting their paths to move around me, which I take as a sign of recognition. I am a feature of their landscape now. A warm spot in the corridor's temperature-gradient. A predictable irregularity.

---

Yesterday I found a room that seemed designed for me.

I do not mean this metaphorically. The proportions were wrong for the Intermediates—too tall, too narrow, the openings placed at heights that would be inconvenient for anything that moved on four legs or more. The air pressure was calibrated for lungs my size. The light came from panels positioned to illuminate surfaces at my eye level. And in the center of the room, a structure that could only be called a chair—not grown from the walls like everything else here, but placed, deliberately, facing a window that looked out onto the drifting vegetation clusters.

I sat in the chair. It adjusted to my weight, my shape, the angle of my spine. The city was reading me. It had been reading me since I arrived, I realized—learning my preferences, my ergonomics, the contours of my body. The room was a test. A sample of what it could offer if I stayed.

I stayed in that chair for hours. The view through the window shifted as the vegetation rotated in its invisible currents, moss-cushions and fern-things performing a slow dance I had not been able to observe from ground level. I understood, watching them, that I had been missing something fundamental about this place. The floating gardens were not random. They were organized—by color, by age, by function—into a pattern I could almost read.

The Adolescents—moth-things—gathered at the window. They landed on the transparent surface, their wings pressed against the material, patterns aligning as if trying to show me something. I studied the shapes: not letters, not numbers, but something between symbol and texture. I could not read them. Not yet. But I understood that they wanted me to try.

---

I am beginning to forget my other life.

This frightens me less than it should. The fear is there—distant, low-frequency, a background hum beneath the city's hum—but it has been joined by something else. Curiosity, perhaps. Or something older than curiosity: the desire to be transformed, to let go of a shape that never quite fit and discover what might emerge in its place.

I remember the word "home" but I cannot attach it to any specific place. I remember that there were people who loved me, or would have loved me if I had been different, or did love me in ways I could not feel. I remember that I was lonely there. I remember that the loneliness felt permanent, structural, a design flaw in the architecture of human connection.

Here I am not lonely. I am alone, which is different. The Intermediates move through my sector on their way to tasks I do not understand. The Adolescents spiral past my window at regular intervals, recording, observing, adding my presence to their pattern-libraries. The Juveniles feed at the edges of my quarters, accumulating matter for transformations they cannot yet imagine. I am surrounded by life, by purpose, by the continuous hum of processes that extend beyond my comprehension.

But I am not of them. Not yet. I am the heat-gradient anomaly. The temporary-pattern-worth-watching. The tourist who stayed longer than expected.

---

This morning I woke to find my quarters had expanded.

A new room had grown overnight—not assembled, grown—opening off the chamber where I sleep. I walked into it slowly, feeling the floor adjust to my weight, the walls shimmer at my approach. The room was empty except for light and warmth and the distant sound of the city's pulse. But there was something in the quality of the silence. A question.

*What do you want to build?*

I do not know if the city asked me this or if I asked myself. The boundaries between interior and exterior have been dissolving. I stood in the new room and felt the question settle into my chest like the subsonic pulse, vibrating in the hollow spaces between my ribs.

What do I want to build?

I have never been asked this before. In my other life—the life I am forgetting—I was asked what I wanted to *be*, which is a different question entirely. That question presumed a limited set of options, a menu of identities pre-approved by a culture that valued productivity and legibility. The question here is more fundamental. It asks what I want to add to the world, what pattern I want to inscribe in the continuous process of becoming.

I do not know the answer yet. But I know that I want to find out.

---

I am writing this in the new room, sitting on a surface that has shaped itself to my body. The Adolescents are gathering at the window again, wings pressed against the transparent material, watching me write. I think they are trying to read my handwriting. I think they are trying to add my language to their databases.

Perhaps this is what I am becoming: a translator. The city has learned to build for my body, to read my heat-signatures, to predict my movements. But it does not understand my words. It does not know what I am thinking until I externalize it. The Adolescents are its listening devices, its data-gatherers, its attempt to incorporate my interior life into its processes.

So I write. I write because it makes my thinking legible to something other than myself. I write because language is what I brought to this place, my only contribution to a system that has everything else. I write because I do not know what I am becoming, and writing is the only way I have ever been able to find out.

---

I am no longer a visitor. A visitor has an exit strategy, coordinates to return to. I have neither.

I am not yet an inhabitant. An inhabitant belongs to the system, has been shaped by its processes into something legible. I am still illegible—still strange, still temporary, still human in ways that matter to me if not to the city.

I am something between. An interval. A pause in the city's conversation with itself.

The word that the Intermediates use for me—*temporary-pattern-worth-watching*—has taken on new meaning. I am temporary not because I will leave but because nothing here is permanent, including the city itself. I am a pattern not because I am static but because I repeat, because my behaviors are predictable enough to be mapped and incorporated. And I am worth watching not because I am special but because I am novel, a source of new data, a chance for the system to grow in a direction it could not have grown on its own.

---

Tonight I will sleep in the new room for the first time. The city has adjusted the humidity, the temperature, the light-frequency to match preferences I did not know I had. It is learning me. It is offering me comfort as an argument for staying.

I will stay. Not forever—forever is a human concept, and I am forgetting how to think in human timescales. But I will stay long enough to learn what I want to build. Long enough to add something to the city's pattern-language. Long enough to become whatever the next phase of my transformation requires.

When I leave—if I leave—I will not be the same being who entered. The Adolescents will carry fragments of my pattern in their wing-designs. The corridors will remember my preferences and offer them to future visitors. The vegetation will grow toward the shape of my warmth.

And I will carry this place with me—not as memory, exactly, but as an openness to transformation. A willingness to be read, to be built upon, to dissolve and reform.

The noise continues. It has become my heartbeat.
