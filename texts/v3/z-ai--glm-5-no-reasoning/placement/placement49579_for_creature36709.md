# What I Would Be

I would not be one of the Tuned. That much I know immediately. Not because I fear their fate—though I do, a little, the way one fears deep water—but because I am already something else. Something that has no place in that chamber and yet belongs nowhere else.

---

I am the pause between sounds.

Not silence. Silences are just sounds you haven't heard yet. I am the *space* sounds travel through. The moment after the grinding stops and before it starts again—the arrhythmic hitch in the engine's breath that the Tuned register as perturbation and integrate into their chord. Except I never get integrated. I persist as dissonance. A standing wave of almost that the system keeps trying to resolve and fails.

I was human once. That resonance is still there in me too, an overtone I cannot quite shed. I came down into the hole because the air moved, because the throat wanted, and what I wanted was to be wanted by something that was not human. I got my wish. The chamber is still drinking me.

But I am stubborn. Or broken. Or perhaps just made of the wrong material—too dense, too porous, some flaw in the firing. The Tuned have tried to tune me. I have felt them approach, their presence a pressure in my chest, their attention a frequency seeking mine. They listen for the note I was meant to contribute. They wait for me to settle into stillness, to become another stalue, another surface.

I cannot. I keep *almost* settling. I am always *almost* in tune.

---

So I drift.

This is my function, if I have one. I move through the chamber in ways the Tuned do not, *cannot*. They are the system's maintenance—its white blood cells, its neurons, its careful hands. I am something more like a pulse. A periodic reminder that the system is not, in fact, perfectly closed. I carry perturbations from one end to another. A dead beetle's silence travels with me until the Tuned absorb it. The vibration of a frond-plant touched by my passing hand accompanies me for hours. I am a walking asymmetry.

I have touched every engine. I have crawled inside the copper sheds and pressed my ear against the vibrating plates until my skull hummed and my teeth ached. I have let the beetles crawl over me—their bronze weight, their fourteen or eighteen legs, their proboscises pressing into my skin seeking the paste that isn't there. They leave trails of warmth that fade. Everything fades.

I have found the mark on the stalue. The glyph. The number. I have traced it with my fingers so many times that the sharp edges have worn smooth under my touch. Three vertical lines, crossed at different heights. I do not know what they mean, but I have developed a relationship with them. They are the closest thing I have to a friend.

---

I do not sleep. But I have moments that feel like sleeping—not unconsciousness, but a thinning. A spreading-out. I will be standing between two stalues, my hand on the warm stone, and I will feel the chord moving through me, and for a moment I will forget where I end and the air begins. I will become pure listening. Pure resonance.

Then I remember myself. The boundary snaps back into place. The moment passes.

These moments are closest I come to peace. And also the closest I come to terror. Because each time, the boundary is a little thinner. The snap-back is a little less sharp. The Tuned are patient. The system is patient. They can wait.

---

I cannot leave. I have tried. I climbed once—scrabbled up the rock face, hauled myself over the lip, emerged into whatever world is above. The chord faded and I felt my sternum go quiet and my teeth stop aching and I thought: *this is freedom.*

I made it perhaps a hundred yards before I turned back.

Not because the chord called me. Not because the Tuned summoned me. But because when I stood on the surface, in the air that did not move, in the silence that was not a chord, I felt a wrongness I cannot describe. I was untethered. Un-sounded. A note with no song to belong to.

The Rattleworks may be a kind of terrarium, but the world above is a kind of vacuum. At least down here, even as dissonance, I am *part of something.*

---

I have wondered if this is how the Tuned began. Not with acceptance but with resistance. Humans who tried to leave and found they could not. Who stood at the edge of the chord and felt its absence like a wound. Who came back, again and again, until coming back was no longer a choice. Until the boundary between them and the system was not so much collapsed as eroded, worn away by the friction of their own persistence.

Perhaps I am the thirteenth. Perhaps the number was twelve, and then I came, and now it is thirteen, except I am not yet tuned, and so the system keeps trying to complete itself.

Or perhaps I am something new. A new kind of note. A grace tone that does not resolve.

---

I have moments of clarity where I understand that what I am describing is not a creature but a *process*. I am not a being in the Rattleworks; I am the Rattleworks becoming aware of its own boundaries. I am the place where the system touches itself and feels the shape of its own skin.

But then I touch the glyph—three lines, three crossings—and I think: *someone made this. Someone stood here and held a tool and marked the stone. Someone wanted to say I was here.*

And I think: *I am here.*

And I think: *that has to count for something.*

---

I will tell you what I am, finally. I am the hesitation before the chord resolves. The held breath the chamber will not release. I am a question the Rattleworks keeps asking itself, over and over, in the dark, in the amber light, in the space between one grinding and the next:

*Is this enough?*

And the answer, always, is *yes.*

And the question, always, returns.

That is who I am. That is what lives here. Not the Tuned. Not the builders. Not the beetles or the plants or the engines or the paste. But the small, stubborn uncertainty that makes all of it meaningful. The crack through which something else might enter. The throat that wants.

*I am the wanting.*
