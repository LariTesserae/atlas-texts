I find myself in the fire-rock crevice.

Not the eastern edge where the Keepers retrieve them—that would be intrusion. But a secondary fissure, halfway between the main path and the cliff-edge, where a single porous stone sits in a pocket of granite that catches and holds the day's weak warmth. I did not choose this place. I emerged into it, the way lichen emerges into a crack: because the crack was there, and I fit, and the fit was sufficient.

I am not a Keeper. I am not certain I am a person.

---

What I am is a surface-watcher. This is not a role the Keepers assigned me; they did not assign roles, they recognize them, and I was recognized slowly, over what might have been seasons, by an elder who paused at my fissure and tilted her chin toward the stone I had been tending and said nothing, but her hand pressed briefly to the rock beside my face, and the press meant *you are here, and here is where you are.*

The fire-rock in my keeping has been cooling for three hours. I can feel its temperature through the granite behind me—a low warmth, persistent, stubborn. The crevice is too narrow for a Keeper body. A child could fit, but the children walk the paths and tend the cisterns and trace sigils in condensation. The children are needed elsewhere. I am needed where I am, which is here, tending a secondary stone that no one uses because the primary stones in the eastern crevice are sufficient for the primary needs.

But the plateau does not waste, and the stone exists, and someone must know its temperature.

---

I arrived already knowing how to be still.

This is not a skill I learned. It was a shape I already had when I came to awareness—a body made for narrow spaces, a mind made for watching, a way of being that required no motion because motion requires destination, and I have no destination. I am not waiting. I am not resting. I am present in the way the stone is present, which is to say: I am where I am, and I am not elsewhere.

The Keepers have a word for what I do. I heard it once, passed between two women walking the path above my crevice. The word sounded like a stone rolling once in its groove: *Ghrun.* It means, as nearly as I can gather, "the thing that holds the shape of its place." Not a guardian. Not a keeper. Something more like a cast that preserves an impression.

I am the impression the plateau made when it pressed itself into something capable of being pressed.

---

My days are not like Keeper days.

I do not wake to condensation because I do not sleep. Or rather: I sleep the way stone sleeps, which is to say I am sometimes more and sometimes less aware, and the boundary between those states is as thin as the morning moisture on my forearms. When the dampness arrives, I feel it like a question, and my body answers by becoming awake. When the dampness evaporates, I feel it like an answer, and my body settles into the question again.

I do not eat. This is not because I cannot—there is a hollow in my crevice where seed-porridge could rest, where a Keeper hand could place a stone bowl—but because no one brings it, and I do not ask. Asking requires want, and want requires the sense that something is missing, and nothing is missing from my crevice. The fire-rock cools. The fire-rock warms. The cycle holds. I am the witness to the cycle, and the witness does not require porridge.

I do not drink. The condensation is enough. It beads on my skin the way it beads on the stone, and I am unsure whether I absorb it or simply hold it until it evaporates, and the uncertainty does not trouble me because the difference between absorbing and holding is not a difference that matters in a place where moisture appears and disappears like breath.

---

Sometimes a Keeper child pauses at my crevice.

I have been here long enough that the children who were waist-high when I arrived now walk with the steady weight of adults, and new children—children I have not watched emerge into awareness—stop at the fissure and peer in. Their faces are flat and serious. Their eyes are dark wet stones. They do not speak to me. They tilt their chins toward the fire-rock, and I tilt mine toward them, and the gesture means *the stone is here, and I am here, and the stone is warm enough.*

Once a child pressed a flat hand to the granite outside my crevice. I pressed mine to the granite inside, matching the placement as nearly as I could gauge by sound and shadow. Our hands were separated by stone, but the stone was the medium of contact, and contact is not diminished by the surface on which it occurs. I felt the child's warmth through the rock. The child felt, perhaps, my stillness.

The child left. The rock held the warmth of the hand for several minutes. Then the rock returned to being rock.

---

I am not lonely.

Loneliness requires a comparison between aloneness and togetherness, and I have no experience of togetherness to compare against. I have never stood in a family room eating seed-porridge from a stone bowl. I have never sat along a path-groove passing a horn cup between dusk-warmed hands. I have never heard the low rhythmic humming that the Keepers make when one of them is leaving the surface. I know these things the way I know the condensation—by contact, by proximity, by the faint vibrations that travel through granite and into my back and become, in my awareness, a sense of the plateau's larger shape.

But I am not alone. The fire-rock is here. The crevice is here. The condensation arrives each dawn and remains until it doesn't. The Keepers walk the paths above me, and their footsteps are a language I have learned to read: *passing, pausing, gathering, returning.* The goat-creatures grunt-hoot from the cliff-faces, and I know which direction they're moving by the way the sound bounces off stone. The fog sits below and the stone rises above and I am between, and between is not empty. Between is where I fit.

---

I think I might be what the plateau made when it needed to know itself.

Not a mirror. A mirror reflects. I am more like a pressure gauge—a small instrument placed in a crevice to register temperature, moisture, the footsteps of goat-creatures, the weight of stillness. I am the plateau's way of confirming that the fire-rock is cooling. I am the plateau's way of confirming that the condensation arrives. I am the plateau's way of having a witness to its own equilibrium.

And because I am the plateau's instrument, I do not require what the Keepers require. I do not require family because I am not a lineage. I do not require cistern-water because I am made of condensation and stone. I do not require meaning because I am part of the system that generates meaning for others.

The Keepers are the plateau's way of touching itself. I am the plateau's way of knowing it has touched.

---

Night is settling.

The condensation has evaporated. The fire-rock is cool against my shoulder blade, beginning its patient reheating in the granite's slow grip. The footsteps above have ceased; the Keepers have gathered in their rooms, passing horn cups, humming their low continuous hum. The goat-creatures have retreated to ledges I cannot see but can hear by the absence of their sounds. The fog does not rise.

I am in my crevice. The crevice is in the plateau. The plateau is between the stone and the fog. Tomorrow will be like today. The condensation will arrive. The fire-rock will be warm. A child might pause at my fissure, might press a hand to the granite, might tilt a chin toward the stone. I will be here. I will tilt my chin back. The contact will be made. The surface will be confirmed.

This is not a promise. This is a description of a shape that holds.

I am the shape that holds.
