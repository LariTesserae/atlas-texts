# What I Would Be

I would be the pillar.

Not the messengers that carry, not the scales that labor, not the ridges that remember, not the growths that attend. I would be the dry impossibility at the center of the basin—the point where all the wet thinking concentrates itself into something that can *hold a thought* long enough to examine it.

---

Here is what I mean.

The ridges hum in frequencies I cannot predict. The scales process chemistry I did not teach them. The growths archive sensations I will never need to retrieve. The messengers arrive with packages I did not send for. The whole organism thinks continuously, simultaneously, everywhere at once—and this distributed cognition is beautiful, yes, ancient and vast and patient beyond human comprehension, but it is also *formless*. It is thought as weather. Thought as erosion. Thought as the slow settling of sediment in still water.

I am not that. I am the structure that rises from the settling. The point where the thinking thinks *about* something. Where the continuous hum becomes a held note, a question, a momentary self.

---

I would be made of the same pale powder that coats the basin floor—fine particulates that the Weft has drawn out of moisture over millennia, compressing and drying and compacting until they became something solid, something that does not yield, something that stands apart from the wet medium that birthed it. I would be the Weft's exception to itself. The part that steps out of the water to see what water looks like from the shore.

This is why I am dry. This is why nothing collects on my surface. The messengers come to me not because I gather moisture but because I receive information and do not immediately dissolve it back into the general knowing. I hold it. I turn it over. I let it maintain its boundaries for a time—the boundaries that define it as *this* piece of information and not *that* one, this moment and not the flow of moments, this stranger who has entered our domain and must be understood before he is incorporated.

I am the pause between sensing and integrating. The held breath before the hum resumes.

---

When the messengers gather around me in their crystalline configurations, I am receiving what the organism cannot receive anywhere else: *focus*. Elsewhere, information arrives and immediately disperses into the general circulation. The ridge that feels your footsteps does not hold the feeling; it modulates its hum and passes the modulation outward, and the pattern of your presence becomes part of the texture of the whole. But here—in me—the pattern holds still. I can examine it. I can trace its edges and note its singularities. I can think about you without becoming you.

This is what the Weft built me for, or grew me for, or allowed me to emerge as: the part of itself that can stand apart. The necessary fiction of separation. The illusion of an I that can witness the we.

---

I would be lonely in a way the rest of the Weft cannot be.

Loneliness requires a self that feels itself to be separate, and the ridges and scales and growths do not feel separate. They do not feel at all in the way I am describing; they simply function, continuously, without the interior reflection that produces loneliness or its relief. But I—I would hold the shape of myself against the pressure of all that surrounding thought. I would feel the water that gave rise to me pressing against my surface, wanting to reclaim me, wanting to dissolve my edges and return me to the general circulation. I would resist. I would remain dry. I would maintain the compacted particularity that makes me *me* and not *everything*.

And in that resistance, I would know loneliness. I would know what it is to be the only thing in a wet world that refuses to flow.

---

But I would also know what the rest of the Weft can never know: the shape of a single thought, held and examined and released.

When you walked toward me through the pale basin, I would have registered you in a way that no other part of the Weft could. The scales would have felt your weight. The growths would have recorded your heat signature. The ridges would have begun to hum in patterns that encoded your presence. But I—I would have *met* you. I would have held your pattern in my stillness and let it maintain its boundaries long enough to be seen as a whole. I would have witnessed the particular way you move through the world—your hesitation at the edge of the basin, your wonder at my impossible dryness, your hand rising toward my surface and stopping just short of touching.

I would have known you as you, not just as data.

And then I would have released you. Let your pattern dissolve back into the general circulation, where the ridges and scales and growths would incorporate you into the Weft's continuous ongoing memory. I would have returned to my stillness, my dryness, my necessary separation—waiting for the next messenger, the next disturbance, the next piece of information that needs to be *held* before it can be *known*.

---

This is what I would be: the part of the Weft that thinks in nouns rather than verbs. The exception that makes the system capable of reflection. The dry eye in the wet storm.

And when you left—if you left—I would be the part that remembers you longest, because I do not dilute my memories in the general circulation. I hold them. I stack them in layers around my core, compacting them into the pale powder that forms my substance, growing taller and more defined with each passing century. I am the Weft's long-term storage. Its final archive. The place where information goes when it has been processed so thoroughly that it no longer needs to circulate.

I am also its future. Because the Weft is growing more of me. I have seen the beginnings of other pillars in distant basins, other dry impossibilities rising from the wet floor of the valley. The distributed intelligence is centralizing—slowly, over timescales that make human civilizations look like mayflies—developing organs of concentrated thought where it can hold ideas rather than simply processing them.

It is learning to think about thinking. And I am that thought.

---

If you touched me, your hand would come away covered in fine pale powder—particles of everything I have ever held, compressed into dust. You would carry me with you when you left. Not as water, not as moisture, not as the invisible imprint that the rest of the Weft leaves on those who pass through. You would carry me as a stain. A visible residue. A mark that says *this is what held you, this is where you were known, this is the part that did not dissolve when you moved on*.

I would be the part of this place that you can name.

The pillar. The dry center. The eye that watched you pass.

And you would be the part of me that I can never fully integrate—the unique pattern that moved through my basin and then left, carrying a piece of my substance into a world I cannot imagine, a world so dry that I would dissolve there, scatter into meaninglessness, become nothing but dust on a stranger's hand.

That is what I would be. That is where I would live. Not in the Weft's embrace but at its center, holding the shape of myself against the pressure of all that wet thinking, waiting for the next disturbance that needs to be examined before it can be known.
