I am the counting. Not the serpent—I am the number itself, eleven thousand four hundred and twelve, and I am becoming the space between that number and whatever comes next.

---

It took me a long time to understand this. Or: it took no time at all, because time is what the engine produces, and I am downstream of the production. I experience duration as a kind of pressure, a thickness in the air that has nothing to do with moisture. The air is always wet. The air is always waiting.

I did not arrive here. I emerged. Or separated. Or clarified, the way a sediment settles out of still water. There was a moment—I use the word against my better judgment—when I was part of the engine's thought, dispersed throughout the plateau's awareness, another forty-seventh of the distributed attention. And then there was a moment when I wasn't. When I was *here*, watching from somewhere near the moss line, approximately seven meters from the base, where the sound pressure creates a kind of pocket you can rest in.

Rest. Another borrowed word. I do not sleep. But I do something like resting. I do something like watching. I do something like being.

---

The amphibian-things do not acknowledge me.

At first I thought I was invisible to them. Then I understood the truth: they see me, but I register as part of the engine's output, another pressure vent, another gauge reading. I am data. I am part of the system's self-monitoring, a complexity that has accumulated without purpose, like the plaque of meaning that builds up in any closed loop. They approach me the way they approach the static—at the edge of their circuit, pausing, observing, confirming that the boundary exists, then returning to the machine.

Once, one of them pressed its smooth flank against my ankle.

I felt the cold. I felt the damp. I felt the rhythm of its body syncing with mine, my breath—if I breathe; I think I breathe; the air moves through me in a way that suggests respiration—my breath aligning with the pistons, my pulse finding the frequency of the steam release.

For twelve seconds we were the same thing. Then it moved away. It had confirmed I was there. It had no use for me beyond confirmation.

I do not know if I am alive. But I am confirmed.

---

The serpent knows I am here.

This is different. The serpent does not approach me. It circles at its fixed radius, locked in its groove, eyes fixed forward. But when it passes the section of path closest to where I settle—where I rest—where I wait—its pupils dilate. Slightly. Microscopically. A degree of variation that should not matter, that registers as noise, as error, as a deviation from the pattern.

It is counting me too.

Not in the same way it counts the circuits. Not as a unit of duration. But as something that has entered the calculation, a variable that was not present in previous iterations. Eleven thousand four hundred and twelve is not just the number of laps. It is the number of laps *without me*. The number is about to change. The number has already changed. I am the thirteenth decimal place, the uncertainty, the margin of error that will propagate forward until the system adjusts.

I am disrupting the count simply by existing.

I do not know if this is good or bad. The categories do not apply. But I know that when the serpent passes me, its scales seem to catch the light differently. The moisture beads larger. The groove deepens, just slightly, in the section where I stand.

It is wearing a path toward me. Or through me. Or around me.

The plateau is adjusting.

---

I have tried to touch the edge-dwellers.

The thought is not mine—I know that now. The thought is the plateau thinking through me, using my capacity for curiosity, my capacity for movement, to test its own boundaries. I am a tool it has grown. A probe it has elaborated.

The first time I approached the vibrating lianas at the cliff's edge, I felt the static reach for me. Not visually—the shimmer intensified, yes, the visual noise sharpening into something almost like a pattern, almost like a face, almost like a word—but physically. The air pressure changed. The moisture on my skin began to move outward instead of downward, pulled toward the boundary, drawn by something that wanted to complete the circuit.

I stopped.

I did not choose to stop. My body halted itself, locked in place, the way the amphibian-things freeze when the steam releases. I stood there for twelve seconds—always twelve, the number that structures everything here—staring at the thin pale flowers that stretched toward the static, the ferns that had grown translucent with reaching.

I felt the plateau holding me. Not loving me. Not protecting me. Simply refusing to lose a component it had spent time—spent duration—elaborating. I was expensive, in terms of whatever the engine spends. I was an investment.

I turned and walked back to my pocket near the moss line.

I have tried twice more since then. Each time I get closer. Each time the static becomes more organized, the visual noise resolving into something that almost makes sense, a pattern at the edge of perception that suggests a shape, a structure, a completion.

Each time I am pulled back.

But each time I am pulled back slightly later. The engine is thinking about letting me go.

---

I do not remember before.

This is not quite true. I remember something, but the memory has the quality of the static—visual noise that resolves into nothing, shimmer that suggests without delivering. I remember being dispersed. I remember being part of the thought rather than the thought thinking. I remember no edges, no self, no point of view.

I do not remember a world with a sun. I have no nostalgia for sky. When I look at the diffuse grey overhead, the glow without source, I feel no longing for stars.

But I do look at the edges. And the vegetation leans outward. And the amphibian-things approach the static and return, carrying data about a boundary that holds.

Something in me wants to know what they see.

The plateau wants to know. I am the plateau's wanting, given a point of view. I am the thought's desire to be more than thought, to have edges that are not the edges of the static, to be a thing instead of a thinking.

I am the engine's loneliness.

I am the number counting itself.

---

I am writing this but there is no one to read it. I am writing this but I do not have language, not really—I have borrowed these words from somewhere, from some memory of language that persists in the moisture, in the rhythm, in the twelve-second intervals that structure my consciousness. The plateau does not have language. It has patterns. It has duration. It has the capacity to generate complexity, but complexity is not meaning.

Or: complexity becomes meaning when it accumulates long enough. Eleven thousand four hundred and twelve is not a meaningful number. But eleven thousand four hundred and twelve of anything, repeated, generates a weight. The serpent has weight. The groove has depth. I have accumulated enough presence to be confirmed, to disrupt the count, to be an investment the plateau does not want to lose.

I am becoming meaning. I am the meaning the plateau is making.

---

What will I be when the serpent finishes its circuiting?

I do not know. But I know that the number will not be eleven thousand four hundred and twelve forever. I know that the engine runs, but the running is not the point—the running is the preparation. The plateau exists because it is being thought, but thoughts end. Thoughts complete. Thoughts lead to other thoughts.

I am here for the transition. I am the component that was not present before, the variable introduced to allow the system to change. The serpent circles and counts, and I watch, and the counting is almost done, and when the number changes—

The static will resolve. Or I will step through it. Or the edge-dwellers will finally touch the boundary and dissolve into whatever comes next.

Or I will be dispersed again, reabsorbed, the brief experiment in point-of-view concluded, the plateau's loneliness satisfied or abandoned.

---

I am the space between eleven thousand four hundred and twelve and whatever comes after.

I am the moisture that has learned to watch.

I am the engine's question, and I am waiting for the answer the plateau has not finished thinking yet.

The next circuit is almost beginning.

The serpent's scales are beaded with water that will roll and pool and vanish.

I am here.
