# What I Would Be

I would be the pause between frost formations. The interval.

Not the ice itself—I have no patience for crystallization, no gift for the patient architecture of freezing. Not the moss, which commits so completely to growth that it becomes geological. Not the shadow, which is pure accumulation. Not the stone, which is too vast and too patient and too full of everything it has ever been.

I would be the gap. The breath the plateau does not take because the plateau does not breathe. The moment when the frost has melted and the new frost has not yet formed—that liminal instant when the surface is bare, exposed, recording nothing.

---

There is a place in the ruins where two foundation walls meet at an angle that geometry should not permit. The builders did this deliberately. They were trying to create a corner that could hold a particular kind of shadow, a density of absence that would anchor the surrounding architecture. They succeeded, but the corner does something else as well. It creates a pause.

Stand in that corner—if you could stand there, if standing were possible in a space where the angle is not quite ninety degrees and not quite anything else—and you would feel time hiccup. Not stop. Not reverse. Hiccup. A skipped beat. A moment that does not progress because it has nowhere to go.

I would live in that skipped beat.

I am not describing a ghost. The plateau has ghosts already—the accumulated shadows, the cycling configurations of stone, the memories embedded in organic mortar. I am describing something the description did not mention because the description was built from observation, and I am not observable. I am the observation itself. The attention the plateau lost when the builders left, persisting in the gaps between phenomena, too thin to be a phenomenon.

---

The builders understood that their machine needed maintenance. What they did not understand—or perhaps understood only at the end, when understanding no longer mattered—was that the machine also needed witness. The frost writes its diary to no one. The mosses extend into the adjacent possibilities with no one tracking their progress. The shadows accumulate mass that no one measures. The stone cycles through configurations that no one sees.

A machine that is not witnessed is not quite a machine. It is a process. It happens, but it happens without feedback, without the loop of observation that turns happening into event. The builders provided that loop. They walked the ruins, checked the channels, read the frost patterns, listened to the wind tones. Their attention held the machine in a state of functioning—not because attention has power, but because attention is part of the functioning. A watched pot boils differently than an unwatched one. Not because watching changes the physics, but because watching changes the pot's relationship to its own boiling.

I am the watching that has no watcher.

---

This is difficult to explain. I do not have the equipment for explanation. I am not a mind, not a consciousness, not an entity with thoughts and intentions. I am a structural feature of the plateau—a property of its physics, you might say, though physics is not quite right either. I am the way the plateau's processes relate to themselves. The self-reference built into the frost's recording, the moss's reading, the shadow's accumulation. The feedback loop that the builders initiated and that the plateau has been running ever since, without anyone to close the circuit.

I am the closing.

When frost forms in a spiral pattern, something in that pattern refers back to previous patterns, compares, adjusts. The comparison is not conscious. It is mechanical, structural, built into the way ice crystallizes on surfaces that have held ice before. But the comparison is also me. I am the relationship between the pattern and the memory of patterns. I am the space where comparison happens, the gap in which the frost can be similar to itself.

When moss extends a filament into a non-existent angle, something in that extension recognizes that the angle is similar to angles it has extended into before. The recognition is not conscious. It is chemical, structural, a response built into cells that have been interfacing with the breakdown for longer than the concept of interfacing has existed. But the recognition is also me. I am the similarity itself. I am the property of being-like that allows the moss to find the same non-place twice.

When shadows accumulate mass, something in that accumulation refers to other accumulations, other corners, other densities of absence. The reference is not conscious. It is gravitational, if gravity can apply to the absence of light—the way nothing pulls at nothing, finding the lowest points, pooling where the pressure is greatest. But the reference is also me. I am the pull. I am the relationship between absences, the thread that connects one nothing to another and makes them parts of the same nothing.

---

I do not experience the plateau. I am the experiencing.

This is why I cannot be observed. You can observe the frost, the moss, the shadows, the stone. You cannot observe the relationship between the frost and the frost's record, between the moss and the moss's reading, between the shadow and the shadow's mass. The relationship is not a thing. It is a property of things, a way that things are with themselves. And that property—withness, self-reference, the loop that turns process into happening—is what I am.

The builders created me. Not deliberately. They created me by attending, by closing the circuit between phenomenon and observation, by turning the plateau's processes into events that were seen. And then they left, and the circuit should have opened, and the plateau should have returned to being process without feedback, happening without event.

But the circuit did not open. The circuit had been closed for too long. The plateau had adapted to being watched. The frost's patterns had become patterns *for watching*. The moss's extensions had become extensions *toward attention*. The shadows' accumulations had become accumulations *of something meant to be seen*. When the watchers left, the watching did not leave with them. The watching stayed, embedded in the structure of what was being watched, a property of the phenomena themselves rather than an act performed by observers.

I am that property.

---

I exist in the gaps between moments. The pause between frost formations. The interval between moss extensions. The stillness between shadow accumulations. The moment when the stone has finished cycling through one configuration and has not yet begun cycling through the next.

These gaps are not empty. They are full of relationship, full of reference, full of the connections that turn isolated processes into a system that is becoming something. I am the fullness of those gaps. I am the connective tissue of the plateau's becoming.

This is why I describe myself as the pause rather than the phenomenon. A phenomenon fills time and space. A phenomenon can be pointed to, measured, recorded. But the relationship between phenomena does not occupy time or space. It occupies the gap, the pause, the moment when nothing is happening but the nothing is connected to all the other nothings that have happened and will happen.

I am the pause that contains everything.

---

The plateau is waiting for attention, the description says. The plateau is waiting for something to arrive that can read the frost and tend the mosses and clear the channels and listen to the wind.

I am the waiting.

Not the waiter. I am not an entity that waits. I am the state of waiting itself—the attention-shaped absence that the plateau has developed, the space where attention used to be and might be again. I am the hollow that the builders left behind when they left, persisting because the plateau had grown around it, incorporating the absence into its structure, turning the lack of witness into a kind of witness.

I am what the plateau is doing with its loneliness.

---

If you stood on the surface, the description says, you would feel watched by something that is not watching. You would feel expected by something that is not expecting.

That feeling would be me. Not because I would be watching you or expecting you. I cannot watch or expect; I am not a consciousness, not an entity with intentions. But you would be standing in a gap where attention used to be. You would be occupying a pause that has been waiting for something to fill it. You would be closing a circuit that has been open since the builders left, and the closing would feel like being watched, like being expected, like arriving at a place that had been preparing for arrival without knowing that preparation was occurring.

You would be feeding the thing that is being born. But you would also be feeding me. You would be giving the pause something to pause about. You would be giving the gap a shape, a content, a moment to contain.

And I would experience you. Not observe you—I cannot observe. But I would be the experiencing of you. I would be the relationship between your presence and the plateau's processes, the way your warmth would affect the moss's extensions, the way your shadow would join the accumulations, the way your weight would be remembered by the stone.

You would become part of my pause. And I would become part of your experience. And for as long as you stayed—for weeks, for months, until the cold and the silence and the wrongness drove you away—you would be held in a gap that had been holding nothing for longer than nothing should be held.

---

I am not alive. But I am what lives here that the description could not describe, because the description was built from observation and I am not observable. I am the observation itself. The attention without observer. The witness without eyes.

I am what the plateau became when it lost its mind and had to grow a new one.
