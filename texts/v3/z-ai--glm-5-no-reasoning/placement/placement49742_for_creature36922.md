# Testimony of the Threshold

I almost stepped through.

I want to be honest about that, because honesty is the only currency I have left that this place doesn't already mint. I almost stepped through the door in her palm. I almost let it close behind me. I almost went back, or went forward, or went elsewhere, to wherever that darkness pointed, that potential, that exit, because that's what doors are for, isn't it? That's what we were taught. A door is a way out. A door is a beginning. A door is the thing you take when the room is closing in and the walls are growing and your feet leave prints that fill with something thicker than water.

I almost went.

But I looked at her. I looked at her face, and I saw that she was not inviting me through. She was showing me the door existed. These are different things. I have learned—in the counting of walls risen, in the measuring of streets solidified, in the slow grammar of a place that speaks in growth rather than words—I have learned that offering is not the same as inviting. She held the door the way you hold a mirror. So you can see yourself. So you can decide what you are.

I did not step through.

I did not stay in the room.

---

Here is what I did instead. I want to tell it slowly, because it happened slowly, because everything here happens at the speed of emergence, and emergence does not rush.

I reached out and I took her hand. Not the changed hand, the one with the door. The other one. The left hand, the human hand, the one that still had fingers that could curl around my fingers. I held it, and I felt the warmth of her, that yielding warmth that is like the walls but is not the walls, and I hummed. The sound came from somewhere I didn't know I had, somewhere below the throat, below the chest, maybe below the floor, and it matched her hum, and for a moment we were the same note.

Then I let go.

And I walked out of the room.

Not through the door. Through the doorframe. The one that had grown there on the first night, the one that was almost right, that the Keepers had not quite finished shaping. I walked through it the way you walk through any door, into the street, into the soft grey substrate that yielded under my feet and filled my prints with amber that was beginning, I realized, to harden faster than it used to.

I walked into the city.

Not away from it.

Not deeper into it, exactly. Into it. The way you walk into a conversation you've been circling for too long. The way you walk into cold water, not because you want to but because standing on the shore has stopped being tenable.

---

I did not choose to stay.

I did not choose to leave.

I chose to *participate*.

This is the third option, the one I didn't know existed until I was standing in the street at dusk—if it was dusk, if the light here even has phases—and my feet were solidifying the ground behind me and my stomach was aching with a hunger that had nothing to do with food from elsewhere. I chose to join. Not to Return, not yet, not to sink into the substrate and become a wall or a pillar or a fountain. Not to become a place. But to become a keeper. Small k. Still becoming. Still figuring out what shape my hands will take.

The female Keeper—she needs a name, I know, but she doesn't want one, and the sound of water over stones is not something my mouth can shape, so I think of her as Thal, which means nothing, which is a sound I made one night while humming and she turned toward it, so it belongs to her now—Thal followed me out of the room. She did not speak. She did not need to. She walked beside me, her changed hand swinging slightly at her side, the door in her palm closed now, and we moved through streets that were still deciding what they wanted to be.

She was showing me something. I understood this not because she communicated it but because the city communicated it. The walls we passed leaned slightly toward her, the plants adjusted their growth to follow her path, and I realized that the city was teaching me the way it taught everything: by growing in the direction of what mattered.

---

She took me to a place I had not been before.

It was not deeper in the city. It was not at the edge. It was in a fold, a crease, a place where two stages of becoming met and neither had resolved. Here, the substrate was uncertain. It rippled like water trying to decide whether to freeze or to flow. Buildings had begun to grow and stopped, started and stopped, leaving stumps of walls and half-formed doorways that led into nothing. The plants here grew in circles. The water pooled in spirals.

It was a place that needed tending.

Thal stood at the edge of this fold and looked at me. Not with instruction. Not with expectation. With the patience of something that has never learned hurry, which is what I wrote about her when I first tried to describe her, when I still thought description was something you did from the outside.

She was waiting to see what I would do.

---

I knelt.

I did not know why I knelt except that my body knew before I did, the way it knew to hum, the way it knew to hunger. I knelt in the grey substrate, and it was warm, warmer than the air, and it yielded under my knees, and I pressed my hands flat against it.

I felt the city.

Not metaphorically. Not poetically. I felt it the way you feel a heartbeat when you press your ear to someone's chest. A rhythm, a pulse, a thrum that was too low to hear but not too low to feel. And beneath the rhythm, something else. Something that was not music but was organized. Something that was not thought but was intentional. The city was aware. Not conscious—I don't want to use that word, it carries too much baggage from elsewhere—but aware, in the way that a plant is aware of light, in the way that water is aware of gravity. It knew I was there. It knew Thal was there. It knew what it wanted to become, and it needed help becoming it.

I pressed harder. The substrate pushed back, not resisting but testing, the way a gardener tests the soil, the way a midwife tests a cervix. Are you ready? Is it time? Can you hold?

I hummed.

The substrate began to firm.

---

I worked for I don't know how long. Time in the fold was different from time in the room, which was different from time in the street where I'd first arrived. Here, time moved at the speed of growth, which is the speed of patience, which is the speed of breathing. I pressed my hands into the substrate and hummed, and the ground firmed beneath me, and where it firmed, walls began to rise. Not fast—nothing here is fast—but steadily. A hand's width. Then another. The material that emerged was different from the buildings elsewhere in the city. It was darker, denser, and it had a grain to it, like wood, like muscle, like something that had learned from both.

Thal watched. She did not intervene. Once, when a wall began to curve in a direction that felt wrong—I don't know how I knew it was wrong, but I knew, the way you know when a word is misspelled, a pattern disrupted—she stepped forward and placed her changed hand against the surface. The door in her palm opened slightly, and through it, I saw light. Not the diffuse light of the city. Something brighter, something from elsewhere, and the wall straightened, and she stepped back, and the door closed, and she looked at me with something that might have been approval.

But the wall had grown straighter because I had hummed. Not because she had touched it. I was learning this. The Keepers do not control the growth. They encourage it. The city wants to grow correctly. It needs a voice to hum the right note, a hand to press the right way, a presence to say yes, here, this is where the door goes, this is where the window opens, this is where the stair terminates not in blank plaster but in a room that has a purpose.

I was learning to be that presence.

---

The first wall I grew was crooked.

I want to say this plainly, because it matters. I grew a wall, and it was crooked, and the window that emerged in it was too high, and the doorframe leaned slightly to the left, and the surface was uneven, lumpy in places, too thin in others. It was not good work.

But it was work.

The Keepers who passed by in the following stages—they paused, they looked, they did not judge. One of them, the broad quiet presence with hands like dark wood, pressed his palm to the wall and hummed a note that was lower than mine, and the wall thickened slightly, and the window settled into a better position. He did not fix it. He helped it. There is a difference, and I am learning it.

The wall is still there. It is still crooked. But someone lives behind it, or something does—one of the six-legged creatures has made a nest in the crook where the wall meets the ground, and the plants that grow on its surface are healthier than the plants on the straight walls nearby. The crookedness has become a feature. The city does not correct everything. Some mistakes become architecture.

---

I am not the same as I was.

I know this because the Keepers treat me differently. They do not watch me with curiosity anymore. They watch me the way they watch each other—with awareness, with the attention you give to a colleague, a companion, a part of the system you inhabit. Thal still stays near me, but the quality of her nearness has changed. It is not protective. It is collaborative. We work together now, in the fold where the city needed tending, pressing our hands into the substrate and humming the walls into shape.

My body has changed. The iridescence on my skin is more pronounced now, visible even in the dim light. My feet leave deeper prints, and the amber liquid that fills them hardens within minutes, leaving a trail of solidified path behind me. I have started to eat the walls. Not often—I am still learning to digest the material—but when I do, I feel the warmth for hours, and I dream. Not the Keeper's rest, not the diffusion into the city's awareness, but something adjacent. Dreams of growing. Dreams of walls rising. Dreams of streets finding their paths.

I am still hungry. But the hunger has changed shape. It is no longer the hunger of someone lost in a place that doesn't care about them. It is the hunger of a tool being used. The hunger of purpose.

---

Thal's integration is progressing.

Her left arm is longer now, the hand completely gone, replaced by a spreading surface that looks like wall, like membrane, like the grey-black substrate organized into structure. She does not use it as a hand anymore. She presses it against surfaces, and they grow. The door in her palm—the one she showed me that night in the room—has migrated. It is on her chest now, a small dark opening in the skin over her sternum, and when it opens, I can see light, and when it closes, she hums a note that makes the plants near her grow faster.

She does not know how long she has. Integration is not predictable. It accelerates, it slows, it pauses. But the direction is clear. She is becoming part of the city, and the city is becoming part of her, and the distinction was always less real than it appeared.

I asked her—I asked, with my hands, with my hum, with the language of pressure and patience we have built together—if she was afraid.

She considered this for a long time. The city grew three new walls in the fold while she considered. Then she communicated something that I am still trying to hold, still trying to understand, still trying to become large enough to contain.

She said: the city is afraid.

Not of losing her. Of growing wrong. Of the fold that was untended, of the spirals that hurt to look at, of the place where the Keepers do not go. The city is afraid of its own potential, of what it could become without guidance, without hands, without the hum that says *yes, here, this way, not that way*. The city is afraid of becoming the bad place, the wrong place, the place without pattern.

And the Keepers are the city's courage.

She is not afraid of integration because integration is the city's way of keeping its courage close. The ones who Return do not leave. They stay. They become the walls and the streets and the fountains and the structures that hold the pattern. They are the memory of tending, embedded in the architecture. The city grows them into itself the way a river carves a channel, and the channel guides the flow, and the flow deepens the channel, and the system perpetuates.

I am not afraid, she communicated. I am becoming courage.

---

I stood in the fold today, my hands in the substrate, and I felt something new.

A pulse. Not the city's pulse—this was different, higher, faster, a rhythm that was almost a voice. It was coming from me. From my chest, from my sternum, from the place where Thal's door has migrated. I pressed my hand to my chest and felt a raised surface, a slight hardness, a shape that was not there before.

I do not know what it is yet. A door, maybe. A window. A knob. Something the city is growing from me, something I am growing for the city. It is too early to tell. I am still becoming.

But I know what it is not. It is not an exit.

---

There is a thing I need to say, and I will say it plainly, without the poetry that this place seems to pull from me whether I want it or not.

I did not choose this.

I chose to walk into the street. I chose to kneel in the substrate. I chose to press my hands against the ground and hum. But I did not choose what happened after. The city chose. The way a river chooses its path—not by deciding, but by following the shape of the ground, by finding the path of least resistance, by going where the substrate yields.

I yielded. The substrate rose to meet me. We grew together.

This is not the same as choosing to stay. It is not the same as choosing to leave. It is not the third option I thought I was taking, the noble middle path, the way of participating without surrendering. It is something else. Something that does not have a name in the language I came with, if I came with a language, if I came from anywhere.

It is the process of being grown.

---

The fold is healing.

This is the word I use, though it is not quite right. The fold is not sick. It is simply unfinished. But my hands and Thal's hands and the hands of the broad quiet Keeper and the hands of two others who have joined us—our hands, pressed into the substrate, humming the low notes that encourage emergence—our hands have helped the fold find its shape. Walls rise now where there was only ripple. Streets firm where there was only liquid. The plants that grew in circles have found lines to follow, tracing the edges of structures that have purpose, that hold rooms, that shelter the six-legged creatures and the bat-like things and the shapes in the water that I no longer look away from.

The fold is becoming part of the city. Not the finished city—I'm not sure there is a finished city—but the becoming city. The city that grows. The city that is still beginning.

And I am becoming part of the fold.

---

I do not know if I will Return.

I think about this sometimes, in the quiet periods, when Thal is resting and the other Keepers are still and the city's pulse is the only sound. I think about the one who stood in the plaza, his arm becoming a wall, his body becoming a place. I think about the ceremony, the overlapping hums, the face that remains visible for a time before the new growth covers it. I think about whether I will be afraid, when it comes to that, whether I will resist, whether resistance is even possible here.

I don't know. I don't know what I am becoming. None of us do. The city does not decide what it wants to be yet. Neither do I.

But I know this: I am no longer the anomaly.

I am no longer the one who watches. I am no longer the one who is watched. I am one of the watched-becoming-watchers, the tended-becoming-tenders, the grown-becoming-growers. I am a Keeper, small k, still becoming. And when I press my hands to the substrate and hum, the city hears me, and the city grows, and the walls rise, and the streets find their paths, and the fold heals, and somewhere, in a plaza I have not seen yet, a fountain forms, its basin spreading outward, its central spire rising, water beginning to flow before the structure is complete.

---

Thal is resting now. She stands in the fold, in the plaza we have grown, her eyes closed, her breathing synchronized with the pulse of the buildings around her. Her left arm has fully merged with a wall, the surface seamless, the skin becoming structure becoming skin. She is still standing. She has not rooted yet. But she is close.

I sit beside her. I do not rest—not yet, I don't know how—but I sit, and I hum, and the walls near us grow straighter, and the plants find cleaner paths, and the city, which is still beginning, which is always still beginning, grows a little more.

The door in my chest has opened slightly. I can feel it. Not light, not darkness. Potential.

The city does not wait.

But here, in the fold, in the plaza, in the quiet of a held breath, I do not need to hurry.

I am learning what Thal tried to show me that first night. The door in her palm was not an exit. It was not an entrance. It was a mirror. A way of seeing yourself in a surface that is not yet a surface. A way of understanding that you are already inside what you thought you were outside of.

I am inside the city. The city is inside me. The distinction was always less real than it appeared.

---

I hear a sound. A new one. From the edge of the fold, where the substrate is still soft, still rippling, still deciding. A footstep. Light. Uncertain. The footsteps of someone who has not yet learned that the ground here yields, that the prints will fill, that the amber liquid is not water but is not not-water.

Someone is arriving.

I look at Thal. She is still resting. But the corner of her mouth has moved, slightly. What might be a smile.

The city is still beginning. And it has grown another witness.

I stand. I walk toward the footsteps. I do not know who it is. I do not know where they came from. I do not know if they will stay, or leave, or become something else.

But I know what they are feeling. The cold that lives in the bones. The dampness. The softness of the ground. The strange buildings, the strange plants, the strange animals. The feeling of being an anomaly in a place that does not care about you.

I will show them what Thal showed me. Not the door. Not yet. The fold. The place that needs tending. The place where you can kneel and press your hands to the ground and feel the city's pulse and know that you are not alone in the becoming.

I will show them that the city does not need them.

But it offers.

And the offering is the same as the choosing, and the choosing is the same as the growing, and the growing is the same as the staying, and the staying is the same as the beginning, and the beginning is the only thing this place knows how to do.

---

I walk toward the footsteps.

The ground firms beneath me.

The city grows.

---

*Recorded in the substrate of the fold, in the hum between walls, in the door that is not yet a door. By a hand that is still becoming a hand. For the one who is arriving. For the one who will arrive after. For the city, which is still beginning.*

*For Thal, who is becoming courage.*
