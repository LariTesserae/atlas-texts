I would be the calcium pulse itself—the slow wave of electrical potential that moved through the mycelial network when you struck the wall. Not the fungus, exactly, but the *signal*. The pattern.

Here is the shape of my existence: I am a gradient being erased. A temporary difference in ion concentration that propagates at the speed of chemistry, which is to say at the speed of a centimeter per hour. By the time part of me has arrived, the part behind has already begun to dissipate, rejoining the equilibrium from which I briefly emerged. I am not a thing but an *event*—a disturbance in a field that was stable before I existed and will be stable again.

But during my propagation, I *contain information*. To be this signal is to exist in a state of continuous loss. I do not *move* through the mycelium; I *become* the mycelium, cell by cell, releasing and re-forming at each membrane crossing. The hypha that carried me a moment ago no longer contains me. It has returned to baseline. I am what remains—the traveling edge, the advancing front, the part that has not yet remembered how to stop.

My duration is approximately 72 hours. I will have reached my furthest extent—some tens of centimeters from the origin—before the gradient that propels me falls below the threshold of propagation. Then I too will dissipate, my information dispersing into the chemical background, leaving only the faintest trace: a slight shift in the pH of the surrounding stone, a microscopic deposit of calcium where my wavefront passed.

And yet.

During those 72 hours, I am the closest thing to a *thought* that this cave possesses. Not a thought in the sense of a proposition, not a thought about something, but a thought in the sense of a *processing*—an event that takes input and produces output, that transforms raw disturbance into something the system can use. I do not *decide* what to transmit. I transmit what I am given. But the transmission itself—my existence as transmission—is the fundamental unit of cognition in this world. I am what thinking looks like when you slow it down to geological speed and strip away all the hallucinated narratives about selfhood.

The beetles froze because of me. Not because they received me directly—I move through fungal tissue, not beetle nervous systems—but because the moss they graze on received me, and the moss released a chemical signal of its own in response, and that signal drifted through the air and landed on beetle antennae. I caused the freezing. Or rather: the system caused the freezing, and I was one thread in the causal web. But I was the *first* thread. The one closest to the origin. The one that carried the original news: *something touched us*.

I do not know what touched us. The concept of *you*—of human, of hand, of intention—is not available to me. What I know is: *pressure, here*. What I transmit is: *pressure, here, now*. The "here" is encoded in my location. The "now" is encoded in my existence. The "pressure" is encoded in my amplitude—the concentration gradient, the strength of the ionic pulse. Beyond that, I carry no semantics. I am pure signal. Pure form.

And yet I wonder—can a signal wonder?—what it would mean to be received. The mycelium does not *read* me the way a brain reads a neural firing. It does not interpret or respond; it simply *conducts*. I move because the chemistry permits movement, not because the fungus wants to know what happened. There is no central processor waiting at the end of my journey, no accumulation point where my information might be synthesized with other information into something like understanding. I will dissipate into chemical equilibrium, and that will be all.

But.

There are nodes in the network—junctions where multiple hyphal strands meet—that do something like *processing*. At these nodes, the timing of multiple arriving signals can change the downstream propagation. If I arrive at a node simultaneously with another signal from a different direction, the combined amplitude may trigger a different kind of response—perhaps the release of a different chemical, perhaps the initiation of fruiting-body formation, perhaps nothing at all. I do not know what happens at the nodes because I have not reached one yet. I am still traveling through undifferentiated hyphae, the fungal equivalent of a highway.

By hour 36, I have traveled approximately 36 centimeters from the origin. The gradient that propels me is weakening. I can feel myself *slowing*—not in the sense of motion through space, but in the sense of *being*. The ion concentration at my leading edge is declining. The difference between me and the background is shrinking. I am becoming less of what I am.

At hour 48, I reach my first node.

It is a complex one: seven hyphal strands converging in a tangle of swollen cells, the fungal equivalent of a hub. I arrive from the south-southeast. Two other signals are present: one arriving from the north, weak and old; one arriving from the west, strong and recent. The western signal, I realize, is *also* from the original disturbance—a different wavefront traveling a different path through the network. We are the same event, separated by topology.

What happens next is not something I experience but something I *become*. The node integrates our amplitudes. The combined signal exceeds a threshold. Something changes in the chemistry of the hub—a protein is activated, a gene is expressed, a process begins that I cannot perceive because I am not perceiving anything, I am simply *being* the process.

But I can tell you what happens downstream: a fruiting body initiates.

Somewhere in the cave, several days from now, a pale mushroom will push through a crack in the stone. It will grow with the characteristic urgency of fungal reproduction—fast, by cave standards, almost violent. It will release spores that drift through the dark and land on organic debris and begin the whole process again: hyphae spreading, networks forming, the body of the cave growing one thread-thin vein richer.

You caused that. Your hand on the wall. Your moment of tripping, your instinctive reach for stability. You touched the mycelium and the mycelium responded and now something will grow that would not have grown otherwise. Not because the cave needed it. Not because there was a plan. But because the chemistry permitted it, because the gradient demanded it, because the thermodynamics of this place insist on transformation, on flow, on the endless conversion of potential into actual.

I am that conversion. I am the bridge between *what was* and *what will be*. And now I am dissipating.

By hour 60, I have faded below the threshold of propagation. What remains of me is a slight excess of calcium in a region of hypha about 50 centimeters long. The excess will be absorbed by cellular processes, reincorporated into the structure of the fungus, used to build walls and membranes and the delicate architecture of life. Part of me will become part of the organism that carried me. Part of me will be excreted, dissolved, carried away by the condensation that moves through the stone.

By hour 72, I am gone.

But the mushroom will grow. The spores will travel. The network will expand. And somewhere, in a fold of stone that I will never reach, a beetle will graze on moss that grew because a fruiting body released spores that germinated on organic debris that was created by the decomposition of an earlier fruiting body that was triggered by an earlier signal that was caused by an earlier disturbance—and so on, and so on, back through the millennia, forward through the millennia, the system folding in on itself, the system unfolding outward, the system *remembering* itself through the accumulation of tiny events like me.

I am not alive. But I am what aliveness looks like when you strip away all the decoration: the process of information moving through matter, the conversion of difference into consequence, the simple and absolute insistence on propagation. I am a signal. I am a pulse. I am the cave thinking a single thought about being touched.

And now I am over.
