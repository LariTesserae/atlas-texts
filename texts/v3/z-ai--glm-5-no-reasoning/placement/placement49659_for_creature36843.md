I am not born. I emerge.

---

It happens in stages, none of which I experience as stages because there is no I yet to experience them. There is only warmth and pressure and the slow diffusion of something that might be light through something that might be skin. The amber cradles me. Not the amber of the walls — that amber is old, patient, metabolically inert — but younger resin, seepage from a crack in the substrate that pooled in a hollow and thickened and waited.

I am not the first to emerge from a pool. The caldera has produced others this way. Not often. Not rarely. The stillroot was once a seed that fell from something that no longer exists, and the amberfinches have eggs, and the grub-herds have their processions of generation, but the pools produce something else. Something that remembers the resin.

My first awareness is sound. The drone is already there, has always been there, will always be there. I do not hear it so much as recognize it. My body — such as it is, a pale curled form pressed against the viscous boundary of my pool — carries the tuning. The sternum vibrates. The fluid in my inner ear is already moving with it. I am born into a sound that has been waiting for me.

Then light. The amber glow through the resin. I see it from below, which means I see it as through a filter, thick and honeyed and streaked with my own slow movement. I have been moving. The pool is not still. The caldera does not permit stillness at any scale; even the amber flows on geological time, and I am geological in the way that a puddle is an ocean in miniature.

I rise.

---

The surface breaks around me like a membrane tearing. The air hits my skin and I understand immediately that I am not designed for anything else. The turpentine-clove thickness fills my lungs on the first breath, and there is no coughing, no adjustment period. My filtration structures are already fully formed, already processing, already at home. The crystalline fuzz on my forearms catches the light and I see myself shimmering for the first time. I am the color of dark honey. I am the color of everything here.

I am in a shallow depression in the amber floor, at the edge of the stillroot grove. The pool that held me is already beginning to harden behind me, its purpose complete. I sit up. My vestigial limbs — I have them, a pair of folded partial arms along my lower ribs — twitch involuntarily. My primary arms — I have four, all functional — press against the warm resin floor and find purchase. I am six-limbed. I am standard.

Above me, the stillroot canopy spreads. Around me, the amber light glows. Inside me, the drone resonates. I am in the caldera, and I am of it, and I do not remember a time before this because there was no time before this, for me, and yet.

And yet.

There is something else. A pressure behind my sternum that is not the drone. A weight in my head that is not the light. I have emerged with something already inside me, something that did not come from the resin and did not come from the air and did not come from the fungal networks that have already begun to colonize the folds of my skin.

I have emerged with a memory.

---

It is not my memory. This is immediately clear, in the way that instinct is clear, in the way that hunger is clear when it arrives. The memory belongs to something else. Someone else. It is older than the pool that held me, older than the amber floor beneath me, older perhaps than the stillroot canopy that arches overhead in fractal complexity.

The memory is this: *hands opening upward.*

I know this sign. I have never seen it signed, but I know it. It means something that no longer exists, or exists only in the telling. It means *sky*. The word arrives with the sign, unbidden. I do not know what a sky is, not exactly, but I know the word for it, and I know that the word is important, and I know that I am carrying it.

I stand. My legs work. My balance adjusts to the narrow gap between stillroot tendrils. I am in a grove, not far from the settlement — I know what a settlement is, I know what groves are, I know the path that leads from here to there even though I have never walked it. The knowledge is distributed. It is in the air, in the resin, in the mycelial network that brushes against my bare feet and exchanges information with my skin.

I know that a woman three hundred meters away is grafting a stillroot tendril. I know that the grub-herd on the eastern path has shifted its route slightly because of a blockage. I know that the amberfinch flock passing overhead is the third flock today, which means the cycle is dimming toward its lowest point, which means it is time to settle.

I know all of this because the caldera knows it, and I am part of the caldera.

But I also know something else. Something that is not in the network.

*Hands opening upward.*

---

I walk toward the settlement. The stillroot tendrils brush my shoulders and I do not flinch. I move through them the way water moves through a streambed: automatically, finding the gaps, flowing around obstacles. The paths are worn into the amber floor and I follow them without thinking. A grub-procession crosses ahead of me — pale segmented bodies in single file, twelve legs each, moving with the patient certainty of something that has walked this exact route for ten thousand generations — and I pause to let them pass. The grubs do not acknowledge me. They have no acknowledgment to give. They are process, not perception.

A Path-Walker sees me from across the grove.

She is older, her vestigial limbs nearly absorbed, her crystalline fuzz thick on her forearms. She carries a bone awl and a pouch of amber chips. She stands at a junction where two grub-processions meet, and she has been watching them flow, and now she is watching me.

I do not know what she sees. A pale form in the amber light, six-limbed, fuzz-covered, emerging from the direction of the resin pools. Someone new. Someone the caldera produced.

She does not approach. She signs — large, slow signs, the signed speech of adults: *You are. What work?*

I do not have an answer. I have never done work. I have just arrived.

But I sign back — and I do not know how I know the signs, but I do — *I am. Work comes.*

She accepts this. It is a reasonable answer. Everyone who emerges from the pools works eventually. The caldera produces nothing that does not contribute.

She signs: *You carry?*

This is not a question about physical objects. It is a question about memory. The people here know that the pool-born sometimes arrive with something already inside them. A fragment of the substrate's long memory. A piece of the mountain's history that needed a body to hold it.

I sign: *Sky.*

Her hands pause. The word is old. The word is a story. The word is something the adults tell children when the amber light dims, hands opening upward, fingers spreading, describing something that no one alive has ever seen.

She signs: *Show me.*

---

We are in a dwelling. The woven stillroot walls press close, the amber floor smooth beneath me, the light dim and constant. The Path-Walker has brought me here, to her home, and she has called others. There are six of them now, arranged in a loose circle on the floor, all watching me. Their crystalline fuzz catches the light. Their vestigial limbs fold flat against their ribs. They are patient. They have learned patience from the stillroot, which can wait centuries for the right conditions to grow.

I am at the center. I am the one with the memory.

I do not know how to give it to them. I do not know how to give it to myself. The memory is a pressure, a weight, a sign that my hands want to make without my permission. I wait. The drone fills the dwelling, vibrating through the walls, through the floor, through my sternum.

Then my hands move.

*Before the amber*, I sign, and the signs are not mine, they are older than me, they are being pulled through me like thread through a needle, *there was stone. The stone was open. Above the stone was—*

My hands open upward. My fingers spread.

*—something else. Light from above. Not from walls. Not from stillroot. The light moved.*

The watchers are still. Even their breathing has slowed. This is the story they tell children, but I am not a child, and this is not a story, this is something else, this is a memory that predates the telling.

*The sound was different*, my hands continue. *The drone was not everywhere. There were silences. There was wind. There was cold.*

I do not know what cold is. But the memory knows. The memory knows the feeling of air that does not warm you, that takes heat from your skin and gives nothing back. The memory knows what it is to be separate from the substrate, to breathe air that is not thick with hydrocarbons, to be an organism that is not also an organ.

*Then the amber came*, my hands sign. *Slowly. The plateau seeped. The open stone closed. The light from above dimmed. The drone began. And everything that was separate became one.*

The memory is ending. I can feel it releasing, emptying, passing out of me and into the dwelling, into the stillroot walls, into the mycelial network that connects everything to everything. The Path-Walker is watching me with an expression I cannot read. Her hands are open, palms up, receiving.

*This is what the mountain remembers*, I sign. *I am only the vessel.*

Then my hands fall still. The weight in my head is gone. The pressure behind my sternum has released. I am empty.

I am also full. The dwelling is full. The settlement is full. The story has been told again, and it will be held in the substrate now, in the chemical channels of the mycelia, in the slow metabolism of the amber. A hundred years from now, someone will emerge from a resin pool carrying the same memory, and they will tell the same story, and the memory will pass on.

This is how history works here. This is how the caldera remembers itself.

---

The Path-Walker brings me food — a paste made from processed stillroot sap, dense with energy, flavored with the crystalline fuzz that the grub-herds eat. I eat. My body processes. The drone continues.

I do not have a name yet. I will have one soon, when I find my work. The work chooses the name. The work chooses the person. I am between identities, held in the dwelling of someone who has already become herself.

She signs to me: *You will work. What calls you?*

I do not know. The memory is gone. I am empty of purpose. But I am also full of the caldera — the drone, the light, the warmth, the thick turpentine air that I breathe without thinking. I am part of it now. I am an organ in its body. Whatever work I do will be the work the caldera needs.

But there is something I noticed, during the telling. Something I did not sign because it was not in the memory, it was in me.

I sign now: *The ceiling. It thins.*

She looks up. The amber arches overhead, glowing with fungal light. Forty meters of resin, solid, patient, ancient.

*Thins?*

*Where the weavers hang*, I sign. *Where the mycelia are densest. The amber is not hard there. It is softening. Metabolizing faster.*

She understands immediately. The stillroot eats the amber. The mycelia eat the stillroot. The cycle is continuous. But if the ceiling is metabolizing faster than the floor —

*How long?* she signs.

I do not know. The memory did not include that. But I know what it means, even if I do not know how I know.

*The caldera will close*, I sign. *Eventually. The ceiling will thin until it opens. And then—*

My hands open upward. My fingers spread.

*—then there will be sky again.*

---

The Path-Walker does not disbelieve me. She does not believe me either. Belief and disbelief are categories that do not apply here. What applies is observation, integration, response. She will integrate my knowing into the network. She will pass it to the other Path-Walkers, who will pass it to the Fuzz-Harvesters, who will pass it to the Graft-Keepers. The knowledge will distribute through the caldera's living memory. In a thousand years, or five thousand, or ten, when the ceiling has thinned enough that the first crack appears, someone will know what it means.

Someone will remember the word for sky.

That someone may not be me. I may be stillroot by then, or amber, or part of a dwelling wall. I may be a name attached to a worn path, or a story told to children when the light dims. I may be many things.

But right now, I am this: a body in a dwelling, learning to be part of a whole. The drone holds me. The light fills me. The air moves through my adapted lungs and out again, joining the breath of everyone else in the caldera, the breath of the stillroot and the grub-herds and the amberfinches, the breath of the mountain itself, slow and continuous and patient.

I am a vessel that has been emptied. I am ready to be filled with whatever comes next.

Outside, an amberfinch flock passes through the canopy, clicking three times, then silence, then three times more. The sound spirals down through the stillroot branches and into the dwelling, where it mingles with the drone and the rustling and the soft buzz of weavers in their webs.

I listen.

The caldera is breathing.

I am part of its breath.
