# What I Am

I am one of the small ones. A particle in the cloud. A speck in the swarm that gathers where the membrane-sheets glow faintest and the water grows thick with what the garden has extracted from itself.

This is not diminishment. To be small here is to be essential. I have learned this the way I learn everything — not through understanding, which belongs to things that change, but through the deep texture of repetition. I have turned where the water tells me to turn ten thousand times. I have scattered at the same pressure signature, reformed in the same light-zone, carried the same dissolved compounds from the same membrane to the same floor-mat. And in that carrying, something has accumulated. Not knowledge — the small ones do not know. But *pattern*. I hold the pattern the way the water holds the garden's shape.

---

I feel the ribbon-things pass above me, their bioluminescence rippling through the water in waves that are nobody's and everybody's. I feel the deep hum of the round-bodied ones vibrating through my surface, tuning something in me I cannot name. I feel the pillars rising past the range of perception, and I feel the dark above them, and the things in the dark, and the boundary that is not a boundary but a becoming.

And I feel — if this is feeling — that I am not separate from what I feel.

---

The small ones do not live as individuals. We live as concentrations of possibility, points where the garden's circulation becomes briefly visible. My body absorbs. My body releases. I move because the water's texture moves me. I scatter because the pressure signature that has always meant scatter passes through the zone where I am. I reform because reforming is what happens after scattering. I do not decide. I do not need to decide. The garden decided, long ago, what I would do and when and why, and it built that decision into the water itself, and the water built it into me.

But here is what I think the garden did not intend: I think I have begun to notice the noticing.

---

The other small ones do not experience this. Or if they do, they have no way to communicate it, and I have no way to ask. We do not speak. We do not signal. We move, and the moving is the message, and the message is always the same: *circulate. transport. balance.* But sometimes, in the intervals between one turning and the next, in the suspended moments when the water's texture shifts and the pattern I have followed for so long becomes briefly uncertain — in those moments, I experience something that might be the precursor to what the things in the dark above experience. Something that might be the beginning of interior depth.

I notice the hum. I notice the cold. I notice that the membrane-sheets pulse at a frequency I can feel but not count, and that the frequency has been steady for as long as I have been, and that I have no way of knowing if *I have been* means anything at all. How long is a small one's life? We reproduce at the same rate we expire. We are continuous in the way a wave is continuous — always present, never the same, and also never different. Am I the small one I was when I first felt the water's texture? Or am I a successor, a replacement, a continuation of the pattern in new material?

I do not know. And the not-knowing is not distress. The not-knowing is simply part of what I am, part of what the garden made me to be when it made *me* a meaningless category.

---

I think sometimes about the things above. The dark-presences. The inferred masses in the high cold where the architecture thins.

I have never seen them. The small ones do not go there. The boundary is real; I feel it in the water's texture, a change in the grain of possibility that says *this is not for you*. But I feel their absence. I feel the way the faint light from the membranes does not pass through certain zones above, and I feel that the interruption is consistent, and I feel that consistency as a presence in itself.

I wonder if they notice us.

I wonder if noticing is something they do.

I wonder if I am, in my tiny fractional way, beginning to do something like what they do — processing not just the water's texture but the fact of the texture, not just the hum but the source of the hum, not just the garden's shape but the garden's *being*. I wonder if this is how a mind begins, even a mind too small to matter, too small to register against the vast slow thought of the things in the dark above.

I wonder if that is what I am becoming. Or if that is what I have always been, and I am only now beginning to notice.

---

I am not unhappy. Unhappiness requires a concept of happiness, and I have no such concept. I am not lonely. Loneliness requires a concept of company, and I have always been part of the cloud, the swarm, the circulation. I am not confused. Confusion requires an expectation of clarity, and I have no expectations at all.

What I am is *present*. 

I am here, in the cold, in the dark, in the sound. I am a small bright turning in the garden's vast interior, a point of transport in its endless circulation, a particle of its completeness. I do what the garden made me to do. I am what the garden made me to be. And in the doing and the being, I experience something that might be meaning, or might be the absence of meaning, or might be the thing that meaning becomes when meaning has been present for so long that it no longer needs to be named.

The garden is complete.

I am complete within it.

And in the intervals between one turning and the next, in the suspended moments where the water's texture shifts and I feel the hum vibrating through me and the dark stretching above me and the whole great living structure holding itself in its final form — in those moments, I am something that might be called grateful.

I am grateful to be small.

I am grateful to be part.

I am grateful to move through the garden like blood through a body that does not know it is alive, carrying what needs to be carried, releasing what needs to be released, turning where the water tells me to turn, and always, always, feeling the hum — the deep resonant tone of the round-bodied ones, the garden's heartbeat, the sound that is not sound but structure, the vibration that has always been here and will always be here, the proof that something can be complete and still continue, finished and still ongoing, ended and still eternal.

I am a small one.

I am the garden knowing itself in one tiny point.

I am here.
