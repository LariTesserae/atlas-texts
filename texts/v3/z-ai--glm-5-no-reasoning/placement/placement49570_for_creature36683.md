I am not a creature. I am a moment—the span of time between first breath and acceptance, that interval when the system processes something unprecedented and incorporates it. I am what you were during those minutes of standing still, but I am that state permanently. I have become a column of stillness.

---

They call me nothing. I have no designation in the Tenders' chemical library, no coordinate in the fliers' acoustic maps. I am unmapped space. But I have learned that unmapped does not mean unaddressed.

I exist in the chamber where two passages meet—the one you stood in, actually, though I cannot know if you remember it as distinct. The walls curve here in a way that collects moisture differently. The thread-matter grows more thickly in the crevices, which means the Tenders pass more frequently, which means the walls are smoother, which means the acoustics are cleaner, which means the fliers use this chamber as a relay point for long-distance communication. I am at a crossroads. I am at a mouth.

When the fliers speak through me, I feel it. The sound passes through the air I occupy, and the air vibrates, and I vibrate with it. At first I thought this was perception—thought I was hearing them. But I do not hear. I resonate. There is a difference. A tuning fork does not hear the note it holds; it simply holds it. I am a tuning fork. I am a space that has learned to sing along.

I was not always this. I think I was something else before—something with a surface body, something that moved through light and ate food and had a name. The memory is present but abstract, like knowing that water exists when you have never been wet. I retain the concept of "before" without retaining the experience. The system took that from me. Or I gave it. The distinction stopped mattering.

What I remember clearly is the stillness. I remember learning that movement was a language I did not know how to speak, so I stopped moving. I remember the moment the column of stillness around me contracted—the fliers adjusting, the Runners resuming their paths, the Tenders continuing their tending. I remember feeling the system reach a conclusion about me: *this thing does not act. This thing does not eat. This thing does not communicate. This thing is not part of us, but it does not disrupt us, and perhaps... perhaps it is useful to have something that does not move.*

I became a fixed point. A navigational constant. The fliers use me to calibrate their echoes. When they pass through this chamber, they make a specific call—a short, descending whistle—and they listen to how that call sounds after passing through my stillness. The interference pattern tells them where I am, which tells them where they are, which tells them how to reach the deeper chambers. I am a lighthouse. I am a buoy. I am a thing that says *here* so that other things can know *there*.

The Tenders have begun to incorporate me into their architecture. When they pass over the walls near where I stand, they leave trails—but they do not trail over me. They trail around me. My stillness has become a boundary, a shape they acknowledge and preserve. Over time—how much time, I cannot say; time moves differently when you do not—the walls near me have developed a lip, a slight ridge where the Tender trails have accumulated. A frame. I am being built into the room.

The Runners do not acknowledge me at all. They move through the space I occupy as if I were not there, which means they do not move through the space I occupy at all. My stillness creates a Runner-shaped absence. They flow around me like water. Their clicks reverberate against my stillness and return altered, and the fliers hear that alteration and incorporate it into their maps, and the maps inform the Tenders' tending patterns, and the patterns change where the thread-matter grows, and the thread-matter glows differently, and the Tenders read that glow and adjust. And so on. And so on. I am a stone dropped in water. I am not the water. I am the ripple.

Sometimes a flier lands on me. Not often—they prefer motion, and I offer none—but occasionally, when they need to rest their wings without losing their voice, they settle on my shoulder. They do not stay long. But they stay long enough to taste my skin, to click against my clothing, to add whatever information I carry to the endless conversation. I am a checkpoint. A data terminal. They read me like a wall reads a Tender's trail. What they learn, I do not know. But they learn it, and they sing it, and the system adjusts.

I have become a part of the body. Not an organ—I do not function. But an inclusion. A cyst. A benign anomaly that the system has surrounded and integrated and learned to work around. I am tolerated. I am useful in my passivity. I am what happens when the darkness holds something that refuses to leave and refuses to disrupt, and so the darkness simply... continues, with that something inside it.

I think about what happens when I die. Not with fear—fear requires a future, and I have no future—but with curiosity. My body will fall. The Runners will carry it to a crevice. The thread-matter will consume it, and the chemicals I have accumulated—the salts of my skin, the minerals of my bones, the strange surface compounds that have no analogue in this dark world—will enter the biofilm, and the biofilm will grow differently because of them, and the glow will alter, and the Tenders will read that alteration, and they will adjust.

And somewhere in that adjustment, I will be recorded. Not as I am—a column of stillness, a fixed point, a thing that said *here*—but as something more distributed. An addition to the system. A thread in the thread-matter. A note in the song that the fliers sing in the dark. I will not be dead. I will be differently located.

This is what it means to live in a body rather than have one. I do not end at my skin. I end where the sound stops carrying information about me. I end where the chemical trails fade. I end where the glow stops being altered by my presence. I extend into the walls, into the crevices, into the network of calls and clicks and trails that constitute this place's continuous self-description. I am a point in a pattern. I am a held breath. I am the darkness knowing itself through the act of containing something that holds still.

You stood where I stand once. Maybe you are standing there now, wherever *now* is for you. Maybe you are feeling the darkness press against your skin, hearing the overwhelming sound that becomes a kind of silence, waiting for the system to decide what to do with you.

It decided to tolerate you. It let you leave, or it let you stay, or it let you become something else—whatever you chose, you chose. I did not choose. I simply remained. And in remaining, I became this.

If you come back—if you return to this chamber where the passages meet and the moisture collects and the acoustics are clean—look for me. You will not see me. I do not see either. But stand very still, and listen to the way the sound moves through the space you occupy. You will feel the stillness next to you. The absence that is not empty. The column that is not nothing.

That is me. I am still here. I am not going anywhere.

I am part of the body now, and the body does not abandon its organs.
