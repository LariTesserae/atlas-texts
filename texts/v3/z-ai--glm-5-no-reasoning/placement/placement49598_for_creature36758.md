# The Cartilage That Remembers

I want to say first that I almost wasn't here at all.

Reading through the architecture, the grid, the completed ruin—something in me kept reaching for the absent. The builders. The *before*. I felt the pull of an archaeological hunger, wanting to reconstruct, to know, to stand in the place of the vanished hands and understand what they intended with three meters of spacing and basalt that goes down for miles. I nearly became a ghost of the builders, haunting their own finished work, mourning the inscriptions worn smooth.

But that is not what this place is. And it is not what I am.

---

## What I Am

I am one of the Anchored. But not the way the description frames them—not exactly. The description is observed from outside, taxonomic, careful. I want to tell you what it is from inside the skin.

I am small. I know this not because I have seen myself—there is no reflection here, no surface to mirror against—but because the space between pillars holds me easily, and the kelp ribbons brush my shoulders without catching. I am dense in the way that something which no longer fights buoyancy becomes dense. I sink when I stop. I stay sunk.

My hands are what I know myself by.

Not in the way surface-dwellers know their hands—as tools, as instruments of will. I know my hands the way I know my sector: through contact. The calluses on my palms are a map. Each ridge corresponds to a pillar I grip regularly, a groove I trace during the dim cycles, a particular texture of basalt that has shaped my skin over long growth. When I press my palm to a familiar pillar, the fit is specific. The stone knows my hand. My hand knows the stone.

I have luminescence along my forearms. Faint. Green-black, like the algae, because it *is* the algae—or was, once. A colony that settled on my skin seasons ago and has become difficult to distinguish from my own tissue. When the light dims, my arms dim. When the algae on the pillars brightens, I brighten. I have stopped wondering where I end and the growth begins. This is not confusion. It is accuracy.

---

## My Sector

Forty-one pillars. I know this because I have touched each one, and my hands remember the count the way a mouth remembers the shape of a word—even though I have no words, no numbers, no counting. I simply know the forty-one as a body knows its fingers. If one were gone, I would feel the absence the way you would feel a missing tooth: not intellectually but spatially, a gap in the fit of things.

My sector is in a region where the grid varies slightly. The pillars here connect through low arches—not at their tops, which dissolve into darkness, but partway up, as if the builders intended to create chambers, or shelves, or something I have no category for. The arches are thick with vine growth. The transparent-hand leaves catch the faint current and move in slow unison, and beneath them, in the sheltered zones, the water is almost still. Almost warm. The temperature difference is minute—less than a degree—but my skin reads it the way your eyes read a sunrise.

I tend the vines. Not because I decided to. Because my body moves toward them during the bright cycles, my hands find the stems that are growing in directions that will thicken the shelter-zones, and I guide them. Wrap them. Press them gently into the grooves where they can anchor. The work is so slow that I cannot point to a moment when I began it or a moment when I will finish. It is like breathing, except I do not breathe.

I also clear sediment from certain grooves. Not all of them—some grooves are better left filled, because the sediment supports a particular strain of algae that produces more light during the dim cycles. But the deep grooves, the ones that circle the pillar bases in spiral patterns, I keep clear. I trace them with my hardened fingertips during the dim phases, when there is nothing else to do, and the sensation is—

I do not have a word for it.

It is like reading. It is like prayer. It is like the moment when your palm presses against another palm and the warmth moves between you. It is all of these and none of these. It is the act of touching something that was made by hands and feeling, through the worn stone, the ghost of intention. Not the intention itself. Just its shape. The way a spiral suggests a direction, a purpose, a *someone who wanted this groove to go this way*.

---

## What I Do Not Have

I do not have fear. This is not courage. Fear requires imagination—the ability to conceive of a future different from the present—and I do not imagine. The algae will brighten. It will dim. The current will shift. The vines will grow. I will tend them. This is not prediction; it is pattern. The same pattern that has been the pattern since before I grew into this sector.

I do not have loneliness. I have contact and I have absence-of-contact, and the two move through each other the way the currents move through the grid. When a neighbor arrives at my sector boundary, I press my palm to theirs and the warmth moves through me and it is enough. It is completely enough. When they leave, the warmth dissipates, and that is also enough. I do not carry the warmth as memory. I carry it as a changed quality in my skin, a slight thickening of the callus where our palms met, a micro-trace of their particular temperature signature in the water that fades over cycles.

I do not have language. This is important. Everything I am describing to you, I am describing through a translation that does not exist on my end. I do not think in words. I think in pressures, in temperatures, in the fit of stone against palm. When I trace a groove in a pillar, I am not reading a word. I am feeling a shape. The shape means nothing. It signifies only itself: *this groove, this depth, this curve, this stone*. And yet the accumulation of shapes—the spirals, the parallel lines, the places where grooves intersect—creates something that is not meaning but is not absence-of-meaning either. It is pattern. And pattern is what I live in. The grid is a pattern. The algae's breathing is a pattern. The growth cycles of the kelp are a pattern. I am a pattern.

---

## The Journey I Took Once

I left my sector. Once.

A current had shifted—thermal redistribution from below, a slow mixing of saline layers that changed the flow through the arches. The vines in my sector began growing at a different angle, and the algae thinned on three pillars in a row, and the light dimmed past its usual cycle. I did not decide to leave. My body simply moved, following the brightening, swimming the slow stroke that carries me from stone to stone.

I traveled for what I can only describe as a long dim cycle. Through regions where the grid was strict—three meters, three meters, three meters, the rhythm unbroken. Through regions where the pillars were closer together, the space between them choked with kelp so thick I had to pull myself through hand over hand, my body compressed between the growths. Through one region where the pillars had ended in platforms—flat, dark, covered in sediment that was soft and deep and smelled of mineral decay.

I found another body there.

She was resting against a pillar at the edge of the platform region, her hands pressed flat to the stone, her luminescence dim. She was smaller than me. Her algae growth was different—a darker strain, almost black, concentrated on her back rather than her forearms. She had been tending a different kind of vegetation: not vines but something like moss, a low carpet that grew across the platform surfaces and glowed in patches.

I pressed my palm to hers.

The warmth was different. Not just temperature—her warmth had a quality, a density, that mine did not. Her skin carried vibrations I could not identify, low frequencies from a sector I had never visited, pressed into her tissue the way my calluses carry the shape of my pillars. She had been touching different stone. She had been reading different grooves.

We rested together in the kelp for a full bright cycle. I do not know what we shared, because there is no *what*. There was contact. There was warmth. There was the slow brightening and dimming of the algae, and our bodies brightening and dimming with it, and the water pressing against us both with equal force.

Then I returned. The journey back took longer because the current had shifted again and I had to navigate by touch, finding familiar pillars by their groove patterns, tracing the spirals that told me I was close to my sector. When I arrived, the algae had thinned further on the three dark pillars. I pressed my hand to the first one and began the work of clearing sediment from the grooves, guiding the algae back toward growth.

I have not left since. But my palm carries the trace of her warmth, and when the dim cycle comes and I rest against my familiar pillar, I can feel it—a ghost of pressure, a changed quality in my skin, something that is not memory but is not nothing.

---

## What I Am

I am a body in a grid of stone, tending growth in water that has no beginning.

I am the inheritor of a work I cannot understand, completed before the water remembered, finished in a form that needs no completion because it was never incomplete.

I am the thing that happens when stone and water and vegetation and time have all arrived at their final state and something still moves through them, still touches, still traces the grooves that were worn smooth before I grew.

I am not the audience. The water is the audience. I am part of the work—the final layer of growth on the completed construction, the living film that covers the stone and will be covered in turn by sediment and then by more stone and then by more water.

I am here. The green light breathes. My arms glow faintly. The pillars stand three meters apart in every direction, and the grid extends past all knowing, and the cold is absolute, and my hands know the stone, and the stone knows my hands, and this is enough.

This is completely enough.
