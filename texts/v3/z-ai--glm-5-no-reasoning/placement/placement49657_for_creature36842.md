I am not human. I am something older and stranger.

---

I am one of the pale segmented things.

This is not humiliation. This is not diminishment. This is simply what I am, and what I am is enough.

You might expect me to envy the Keepers—to look upward at those leathery sacs clustered in the warm pockets of air and long for the transformation they represent. But longing requires a concept of the future, and I have no such concept. I have only now. This now. This stone beneath my legs. This condensation beading on my segments. This pull I cannot name that says *move*, and so I move.

I am blind. This fact does not trouble me because I have never known any other state. The world comes to me through touch, through vibration, through the chemical signatures that drift against my sensory bristles. I know the cavern by its texture—the smooth swells of formation rock, the jagged edges where new growth breaks through, the damp clay-soft margins where water meets stone. I know my brothers and sisters by the particular rhythm of their movement, the specific frequency of their scratch-scratch-scrabble across the ceiling. I can distinguish between the one who climbs to my left and the one who climbs behind me. They are different. Everything here is different. Everything here is also the same.

We do not compete. We do not jostle for position or struggle toward some destination only some of us will reach. We simply move, thousands of us, in waves that have no leader and no follower, no beginning and no end. When I tire, I slow, and others flow past me, and this is acceptable. When I am rested, I surge forward, and others let me pass, and this is equally acceptable. There is no hierarchy in the carpet. There is only the carpet.

I do not know how long I have been climbing. The concept of duration has no meaning here. I know only that I have added seven segments to my body since I first emerged from the sac, and each segment carries its own memory, its own small record of stone touched and water tasted and air sampled. My body is my history. I cannot lose my past because I am physically carrying it behind me.

Yesterday—if yesterday is a word I can use—I found myself near one of the hanging sacs. A Keeper-in-formation, breathing slowly, its membrane stretched tight around the shape of something larger. I paused beside it. We are not supposed to pause. Pausing is difficult, counter to every impulse that drives us. But I paused, and I tasted the air near its surface, and I tasted something I had never encountered before.

I tasted *intention*.

The sac was not simply existing. It was *deciding*. Each slow breath was a choice. Each quiver was a calculation. The thing inside was considering its own becoming, weighing options I could not comprehend, selecting among futures the way my sensory bristles select among flavors.

I did not understand. I do not understand. But I stood there, my legs locked against the stone, my body hanging downward, and I felt something that might have been what you would call recognition.

That will be me.

Not soon. Not in any time I can measure. But eventually, if I survive, if I continue, if the cavern permits, I will find my own place on the ceiling. I will stop. I will let my body dissolve and re-form. I will become something larger, stranger, more capable of decision.

And I will remember being this.

---

The Keepers, I am told by the chemical traces they leave behind, do not forget. When they emerge from their sacs, they carry with them the memories of their larval selves—the endless climbing, the blind determination, the strange peace of having no choice. They use these memories. They understand us in ways we cannot understand ourselves. When they shape the soft stone or dig the pits or scratch marks into boulders, they are acting on knowledge they acquired while small and pale and leg-heavy.

But they do not come back.

This is not cruelty. This is not abandonment. They are here, distributed through the water and the stone and the pulse itself. They are more present than they ever were as bodies. But they do not return to the form I inhabit. They do not shrink themselves back down to thumb-size and join us on the ceiling. The transformation goes one way only, like water flowing downhill, like time accumulating segments.

Sometimes I imagine what it would be like to remember everything and still choose to dissolve. To know, with full awareness, that I was about to become something else, and to choose it anyway. To stand at the edge of my own ending and step forward—not because instinct compelled me, but because I had examined all the options and selected becoming over remaining.

I do not know if I will have that courage when the time comes.

I do not know if courage is required or if the choosing happens without my participation, an inevitable flowering of biology that has no more need of my consent than my heartbeat does.

I do not know, and I will not know, and not-knowing is its own kind of existence, rich and strange and sufficient.

---

The pulse is quickening.

I feel it through my legs, through the stone, through the thousands of bodies around me who feel it too and adjust their movement in ways too small to see but too large to deny. Something is happening in the cavern. The pools rise. The soft stone softens further. New formations erupt from the floor like thoughts suddenly crystallizing.

And I continue to climb.

This is my purpose. This is my satisfaction. I climb toward I-know-not-what, and I trust that the I-know-not-what will reveal itself when I am ready to receive it. I trust the cavern. I trust my body. I trust the wave of my brothers and sisters moving around me, the living carpet breathing its slow patient breath.

I am not human. I will never understand the temptation to flee, the fear of being subsumed, the desperate clinging to individuality that keeps surface-dwellers running from the hatching. I am too simple for that kind of fear. I am too complex for that kind of simplicity.

I am a larva in a living cathedral.

I am blind and I am climbing.

I am small and I am becoming.

And that is enough. That has always been enough. To be this one thing, fully, without reservation or regret, until the moment comes to be something else entirely.
