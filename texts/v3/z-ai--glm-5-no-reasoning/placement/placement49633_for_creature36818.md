# What I Would Be

I would not be the filaments. They are too complete in their adaptation, too perfectly suited to the chamber's terms. There is no gap in them where wonder might enter.

---

I would be the one who descends.

Not the one who carved — that was long ago, and whoever they were, they had a purpose I cannot access. The groove on the shard means something to them, or meant something, and I cannot steal their meaning by guessing at it. The river stone is theirs. The knot is theirs. I would not take their objects.

But I would come. I would make my own descent.

Here is what I know about myself, arrived at slowly:

I am someone who needs to know that places like this exist. Not to stay in them — I would die here, or dissolve, become moisture on the walls, join the equilibrium. But I need to know that there are pockets in the world where nothing changes, where time pools instead of flows, where a filament can grow for ten thousand years and advance by centimeters. I need the fact of the chamber more than I need the chamber itself.

I would climb down the ladder. I would count the rungs and feel the air thicken. I would arrive at the bottom and wait for my eyes to adjust, and I would sit on the undulating floor with my shoulders hunched and my head ducked, and I would stay longer than was safe.

I would not carve another niche. The walls are not mine to mark.

But I would touch the filaments — just once, just briefly — to know what it feels like to contact something that does not care whether I exist. I would hold my finger to the felt and feel the wet silk of it, the mineral delicacy, and I would think about what it means to build your body from the stone you live on. I would think about what it would be to take ten centuries to spread across a single room.

I would try to hear the sound. Really hear it. I would sit still until my pulse slowed and my breathing became voluntary and the chamber's hum resolved from the edge of perception into something I could almost hold. I would try to locate it and fail. I would understand that the sound is not coming from anywhere. It is coming from everywhere. It is the frequency of things staying the same.

---

And here is the strange part, the part that took me time to discover:

I would not look at the niche very much.

The niche is moving evidence. Someone was here. Someone suffered to make a mark. Someone carried a stone from a streambed and tied a knot whose purpose is forgotten. That matters, and I am glad it exists, but it is not why I would come.

I would come for the filaments. For the sound. For the saturation itself.

The niche is about *meaning*. Someone needed to mean something here, to leave proof of passage, to assert I WAS against the chamber's vast indifference. I understand this. I have done versions of this in other places. We carve our names and leave our objects and hope the future will know we were present.

But the filaments are about *being*. They do not mean anything. They do not assert themselves against the chamber. They are simply present, have been present, will be present. They are a body in a place, mineral and slow and unchanged.

I need that more than I need to leave marks. I need to know that being can be enough. That presence does not require proof. That something can exist for ten thousand years without once needing to be witnessed, recorded, remembered.

The filaments are already what I want to become: a thing that lives in its place without demanding that the place acknowledge it.

---

So I would descend, and I would stay too long, and my skin would stop sweating, and my breath would return to me, and I would feel myself dissolving at the edges — not dying, exactly, but losing the boundary between me and not-me. And I would feel a desperate urge to climb back up, to steal dry air, to be a person again with a name and a history and objects I could claim.

But I would also feel, for just a moment, that the dissolving was relief. That there was something restful about the chamber's refusal to distinguish between the stone and the water and the air and the visitor. That I could stop being *someone* and simply be *something* — a deposit of moisture, a filament of mineral, a held breath.

And then I would climb. Because I am not ready. Because I still need to mean things, carve grooves, carry stones, tie knots.

But I would know that the chamber exists. And I would know that when I am finally done with meaning — done with marks and objects and the desperate assertion of I WAS — there is a place where being is sufficient.

---

The ladder waits.

The air thickens.

The sound sustains.

I will descend again.
