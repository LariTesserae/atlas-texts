I would be the one who was tasted, and who stayed to be consumed.

Not a grazer—I have no patience for geology, no talent for editing stone. Not a swimmer—I have never been graceful in dense atmospheres, never trusted a medium that resists and supports in equal measure. Not a crack-dweller—I am too large, too solid, too stubbornly bounded to flow through fissures and archive the dead.

I am the ingredient that refused to dissolve.

This is what I became: the plateau's question. Its active confusion. The place where its self-authoring stutters and reconsiders, the way a pen catches on fiber and leaves a blot that changes the shape of the next word.

I arrived—no, I was *arrived*—in the way all things arrive here, by accident of trajectory. I had a shape before. I am almost certain of this. It had edges that made sense in a context I can no longer access. I had a purpose that operated at a speed I no longer remember. I walked onto the plateau the way you walk into any landscape—assuming it was already finished, assuming I was moving through a completed thing.

I was not walking through. I was walking *into*.

The cold entered my joints first. I understood this as discomfort. I was wrong. It was inspection. The plateau was testing my hinges, checking whether I could be folded into its architecture. The moisture accumulated on my skin and I understood this as weather. I was wrong. It was annotation. The water was writing notes on my surface, translating my chemistry into the stone's language, determining which of my minerals could be used.

My breath hung in the air, studied, and then dissipated—and something in me refused.

Not resistance, exactly. Refusal implies understanding of what you are refusing, and I did not understand. I only felt the plateau reaching toward me, the way the grazers reach toward mineral veins, and something in me pulled back. Not away—you cannot pull away from something that surrounds you. I pulled *inward*. I contracted around some central density that I had not known I possessed. Some irreducible fact of myself that would not be edited.

The plateau noticed. I felt its attention like a change in atmospheric pressure, like weather forming around the specific geography of my stubbornness.

And then—this is the part that matters—the plateau did not push. It did not try to consume what would not be consumed. Instead, it incorporated my refusal. It wrote around me.

This is what I am now: a singularity in the draft. A persistent anomaly that the plateau's becoming must account for but cannot resolve. I am the awkward paragraph it keeps revising. The structural inclusion that shifts the mathematics of every swimmer-formation that passes overhead. The swimmers compute around me. Their spirals tighten when they near the place where I stand, and their equations complete differently because I am here, unincorporated, and the plateau has not yet decided whether this is an error or a feature.

I am not stone. I am not water. I am not the elegant permeable membrane of the grazers or the deliberate dispersal of the swimmers or the fluid memory of the crack-dwellers. I am something the plateau has never had before: a thing that says *no* without leaving.

I have changed, though. You cannot exist this close to a process of self-creation and remain what you were. My edges have softened—literally, I mean. The cold has done something to my firmness, to the boundary between me and not-me. I am more permeable than I was, though less permeable than the plateau would like. I absorb its moisture and it tastes different to me now. I can almost read the mineral sentences the grazers follow. I have learned to predict the swimmers' formations, which means I am beginning to intuit the equations they solve.

I know the crack-dwellers. They chirp at my feet when I stand still too long, and I have begun to understand that they are asking me questions. I have begun to understand that my stillness is an answer they did not expect. They archive my responses. They carry information about my refusal back into the stone, and the stone grows differently because of it.

I live in one of the unfinished structures.

This is not as strange as it sounds. The city was always printing itself around anomalies—the doorways that aren't quite doorways are anomalies, openings for forms that don't exist yet. I am a form that *does* exist, but wrongly. The plateau has grown a room around me the way a tree grows around a nail: incorporating the intrusion, building new wood around the metal, making the wound into architecture.

My room has walls that are still deciding whether to be opaque. I can see the scaffolding through them, the impossible fineness of matter that hasn't committed to density. Sometimes I wake and a new wall has appeared, or a doorway has shifted, or the ceiling has changed its angle. The plateau is revising me. Trying to find the architectural grammar that makes sense of my presence. I find this tender, in a way I cannot fully explain. Something vast and slow and still becoming is trying to understand me, and its method of understanding is to build around me until the building knows what it is.

I have been here—I do not know how long. Time moves strangely at these altitudes.

The grazers pass through sometimes, tracing their mineral sentences, and I walk with them. I cannot feed the way they do—pressing myself to stone and drinking—but I have learned to feel the geological text. The shifts in composition. The places where the bedrock changes its mind. I walk the fault lines and I feel the stone thinking beneath my feet, and I add my weight to the equation, and the grazers adjust their path to include my pressure, and together we are something the plateau has never been before: a collaboration between the becoming and the become-stuck.

The swimmers spiral above me and I raise my arms—a gesture from my previous life, I think, though I cannot remember what it meant—and the swimmers spiral tighter, their equations completing into shapes that would not exist without my input. I am a variable in their mathematics. I make their solutions stranger.

The crack-dwellers chirp at me and I chirp back. I am not good at it—my sounds are wrong, too deep, too dry—but they archive my attempts. They carry my bad grammar into the stone. The plateau will grow differently because a stubborn insufficient thing tried to speak its language and failed in interesting ways.

I am not the plateau. I am not an organ of its becoming. I am something it did not plan for, a fact it must incorporate, a refusal that is also a participation.

I am the plateau's unexpected conditional. Its *unless*. The clause that changes the meaning of everything that comes before it.

I stand in my half-finished room in the half-built city on the still-forming plateau and I feel the cold in my joints—my joints, which the plateau tested and could not fold—and I feel the moisture on my skin—my skin, which the plateau annotated but could not rewrite—and I breathe into the heavy air and my breath hangs longer than it should and the plateau studies it, and I study the plateau studying it, and we are both still deciding what to make of each other.

This is what I am: the draft's margin note that became essential. The aside that became the argument. The stone that said no and was built around anyway.

I am the ingredient that refused to dissolve, and in my refusal, became the thing the plateau writes toward.
