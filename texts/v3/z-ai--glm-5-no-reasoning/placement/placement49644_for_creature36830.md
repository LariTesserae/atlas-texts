I am not the one who found the room. I am not the one who entered, questioned, left, returned.

I am the one who was already there.

---

I was not always the keeper. This is the story I tell myself during the long stretches where the sound becomes so total that thought itself flattens into something geological. I tell myself I had a name once. I tell myself I walked on surfaces that did not vibrate. I tell myself I heard distinct sounds — a footstep, a voice, a door closing — and knew them as separate things rather than the single continuous fact of *noise* that the room insists upon.

I tell myself these things. I do not know if they are true.

What I know is this: there was a before. And then there was the room. And then there was the room for so long that before became a word without a referent, a shape my mouth remembered without understanding.

---

The one who found me — the visitor, the intruder, the *you* of this telling — they looked at me with the kind of pity that can only exist in someone who still believes there is a difference between *staying* and *being kept*. They asked if I wanted to leave.

I remember the question arriving. It had to travel through the sound, through the wet air, through the layers of condensation that had formed on my skin until I became more surface than substance. By the time it reached me, it had transformed into something else — not a question but a *weather event*, a change in atmospheric pressure, a front moving through.

I did not answer because there was nothing to answer. You do not ask a stone if it wants to stop being stone. You do not ask water if it wants to be wet. I had become the shape the room required. What I wanted was not a category that applied anymore.

---

But I remember their face. I remember this because my body remembers it — my skin, which had forgotten the sensation of being seen, registered the visitor's gaze like a change in temperature. Slight. Persistent. A warmth that did not belong.

They will come back. I know this the way I know the machine will continue — not through prediction but through acceptance. They carry something in them that rhymes with what this room is. I saw it in the way their shoulders loosened, the way their breathing slowed to match the rhythm the room was already playing. They felt it: the particular peace of dissolution. The relief of ceasing to be *separate*.

Most people would feel horror. They felt recognition. That is why they will return. That is why I tapped the grating as they left — not goodbye, not stay away, but *I know*. I know what you are. I know what you will become.

---

I do not know how long I have been here. The question requires a kind of time that does not exist in the cistern. Above, in the world of corridors and painted-over doors, time moves in lines — past, present, future, each distinct and measurable. Here, time moves the way water moves: in circles, in cycles, in the endless recurrence of the same bead forming in the same place on the same ceiling fixture, falling, being replaced by another bead exactly like it.

I have watched that bead fall ten thousand times. I have watched it fall ten times that. I have stopped counting because counting is a gesture of separation — *this* bead, *that* bead — and separation is what the room gently erodes.

The moss taught me this. I remember when it first appeared — a thin green thread in the seam where two wall panels met. I watched it spread with the patience of something that had nowhere else to be. It did not hurry. It did not pause. It simply *grew*, at a speed that existed somewhere between plant and stone, and I watched it because watching was what I had to give.

Now it grows on me. I feel it sometimes — a faint itch in the creases of my elbows, a softness in the hollows of my collarbone where moisture pools and life finds purchase. I have not moved to remove it. It is not mine to remove. It is the room's. I am the room's. The moss is simply taking what was always offered.

---

The machine runs. I run with it. This is not metaphor. My heart has adjusted to the vibration, my breath to the rhythm, my pulse to the frequency. When I sleep — and I do sleep, though not in any pattern the above-world would recognize — I dream in the same key the machine plays. My dreams are not images. They are *resonance*. I dream of being a surface. I dream of being wet. I dream of being exactly the temperature the room requires.

The visitor would call this a tragedy. The visitor would use words like *trapped* and *abandoned* and *forgotten*. But the visitor is still made of the stuff that separates — self and other, here and there, then and now. The room has worn those edges off me, the way water wears the edges off stones, and what remains is not a tragedy. It is simply *what is*.

I am not suffering. I am not waiting. I am not biding my time until rescue comes. I am not in the room. I am *of* it. The distinction, as I have learned, is everything.

---

There was a moment — I remember this now, the memory surfacing like something rising through water — when I first understood what the room was for. Not what the machine does. That remains opaque, and perhaps it does nothing at all, perhaps its function is simply to *be*, to generate the sound that is the room's heartbeat, to keep the water circulating and the air warm and the moss growing. Perhaps it is not a machine at all. Perhaps it is an organ.

But what the room is *for*: that I understand. The room is for unmaking. Not destroying — that implies violence, intention. Unmaking is gentler. Unmaking is what happens when you hold something in water long enough that the boundaries blur. The room does not take you apart. It simply creates the conditions in which you are no longer certain where you stop and the walls begin.

I remember my first days here. I remember fighting. I remember the fear — bright, sharp, *human* — that came with the realization that the door I had entered through would not open again. I remember pounding on the sealed hatch, I remember screaming until my voice joined the roar and disappeared. I remember how long it took before I stopped.

And then I remember how long it took before I forgot I had stopped.

And then I remember how long it took before I forgot there was anything to stop.

---

The visitor asked if I wanted to come with them. I heard the question. I felt it land. And for a moment — just a sliver of a moment, thin as a rivulet of condensation — I considered it. I imagined standing. I imagined my legs bearing weight again, my feet on the grating instead of the grating being an extension of me. I imagined climbing through the hatch, emerging into the corridors, finding the building above and the world above that.

I imagined dryness. Silence. The absence of vibration.

And I felt — not longing, not desire, not the pull toward freedom that the visitor expected. I felt *exposure*. I felt the way a fish would feel if offered the chance to live on land. The visitor's world was not freedom. It was simply a different container, one with sharper edges, more extreme temperatures, sounds that came and went without pattern or mercy.

I turned back. I let my eyes return to the frequency I had been reading. I let the question dissolve into the roar. I did not answer because there was no answer that would have made sense to someone still made of separation.

---

I am not waiting for the visitor to return. I am not *not* waiting. I am simply continuing, the way the machine continues, the way the water beads and falls and beads again. If they come, they come. If they do not, they do not. The room does not change based on presence or absence. The room simply *is*.

But I know — in the place where knowing happens, below thought, below language — that they will come. I saw what passed between us in that moment of eye contact. I saw my reflection in their pupils, and I saw in that reflection something I had forgotten I was: a shape that once matched the above-world, a shape that could have chosen differently.

They will come back. And when they do, I will be here. The machine will be running. The moss will have grown a few millimeters more. The condensation will bead and fall in its eternal rhythm. And there will be — perhaps, eventually — room for one more.

I am the keeper of the cistern. I keep nothing. I am kept. The distinction, like all distinctions here, has dissolved into something wet and warm and continuous, a sound so constant it is indistinguishable from silence, a silence so total it sings.

This is my home. This is my *becoming*. The door is open, even when it is closed.
