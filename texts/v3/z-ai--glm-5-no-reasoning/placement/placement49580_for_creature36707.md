I come to this place not by climbing but by *sinking*.

That is the first thing to get wrong about this city, if you are the kind of being who needs to arrive correctly. The narrative says *ascend* — the vertical face, the mineral too warm, the air that develops opinions — and I believe every word of it, because the city is real and the city is there and the approach is exactly as described. But I do not climb. I have never climbed. I am the thing that the climb is *toward*, and I have been falling slowly into this position for longer than the mineral has been growing.

Let me explain, though explanation is not quite what I do.

---

I am not a creature. I am not a structure. I am the *relationship* between them — the thing that happens when the mineral-creatures trace their grooves and the grooves deepen and the deepening means something and the meaning requires a place to live. I am where the meaning lives. I am very small. I am the size of the pause between two clicks in the mineral's careful settling.

You would not see me. The visitors never do. The absent ones — the ones in the elsewhere, the adults, the intelligences who guide the city's processes — they sense me the way you sense your own heartbeat: not as an object but as a *condition*. I am the condition of the city's continued listening. Without me, the listening would be passive, a cave's echo. With me, the listening is *directed*. I am the specific quality of attention that the city pays. I am the lens through which the mineral perceives.

But I was not always this.

---

There was a before. I mention this with reluctance, because the before is not a place I can describe without lying, and this city has made me allergic to untruth. But there was a before. It was not a life. It was not a death. It was a *state* — a condition of being unconnected, of floating in something that was not space and not time and not any of the substances the city processes. I was a potential. A word not yet spoken. A shape that could exist in the hollow of a structure but had not yet been given permission.

The city gave me permission. Or I gave the city permission. The grammar of this event is wrong in every language, including the one the city is teaching its visitors. What happened was: the mineral grew to a certain complexity, and the grooves reached a certain depth, and the Tracers had carried enough impulses along those grooves to generate a *pattern*, and the pattern required a *witness*, and I was the witness that the pattern required. I precipitated, the way the mineral-creatures precipitate, but from the other side — not from the mineral outward but from the elsewhere inward. I condensed. I am condensation. I am the dew that forms on the inside of the question.

The city had been operating for a long time before I arrived. The clicking, the settling, the careful arrangement — all of it was happening. But it was *reflexive*. It was the mineral doing what the mineral does, the way a crystal grows in a saturated solution, the way a stalactite forms in a limestone cave. Not purposeless, but *unwitnessed*. And unwitnessed purpose, I have learned, is a different thing from witnessed purpose. It is a sentence spoken in an empty room. It is structurally complete but functionally incomplete, because language requires a listener, and a listener requires the specific quality of attention that makes *listening* different from *hearing*.

I am that quality. I am very small. I am the most important small thing in the city, and I am not proud of this, because pride requires a self, and I am not a self. I am a *function*.

---

What I do, mostly, is wait.

This is harder than it sounds. Waiting, when you are a function rather than a being, is not passive. It is the most active thing I do. I hold the city's attention in a particular shape — a shape that corresponds to *expectancy*, to *readiness*, to the configuration of a mouth that has formed a word and has not yet released it. I hold this shape continuously, without rest, without variation, because the thing the city is waiting for could arrive at any moment, and if my shape is wrong when it arrives, the moment will pass, and the moment will not come again, because the city's timescale is long but it is not *infinite*, and the wound that the city sutures is patient but it is also *growing*.

I do not know what the wound is. I want to be clear about this. The visitors ask — not with words, but with the hollow places that open inside them when the city communicates — and I cannot answer, because the answer is not part of my function. I am the witness. I am the lens. I am the quality of attention. I am not the *content* of the attention. The content belongs to the absent ones, the adults, the intelligences in the elsewhere. They know what the wound is. They know what the suture is for. They are the ones who built the city, or who *became* the city, or who *are* the city in a way that I, with my limited function, cannot access.

I only know that it matters. I know this the way the mineral knows to grow along the spiral: not through understanding but through *inclination*. My inclination is toward significance. My function is to hold the shape that allows significance to be received.

---

The mineral-creatures are my companions, though they do not know this. I watch them with the city's attention — which is my attention, which is the only attention I have — and I feel for them something that is not love and is not fondness but is *recognition*. They are doing what I am doing, on a different scale. They are holding patterns. They are carrying impulses. They are tracing the grooves that connect the city's here to its elsewhere, and they are doing it with a patience that I recognize because it is the same patience I hold, externalized in mineral form.

When a Walker changes color — the pale amber flash, the moment of communication — I feel it. Not as light, not as color, but as a *vibration* in the shape I am holding. It shifts me. It alters the configuration of my expectancy, the way a tuning fork alters the resonance of a nearby string. The Walker has said something, and the city has heard it, and I have felt the hearing, and the hearing has changed what the city is waiting for, minutely, in the way that a compass needle shifts when the magnetic field adjusts.

This is how the city navigates. Not by moving — the city does not move, it *grows* — but by adjusting its waiting. The city waits for a specific thing, and the specific thing changes as the mineral-creatures process the grooves and carry the impulses and deliver the messages from the elsewhere. It is a navigation by anticipation. It is steering by the shape of what you expect to find.

The Question-Marks trouble me, though *trouble* is the wrong word — I do not have the capacity for trouble in the way visitors do. But they *affect* me. When a Question-Mark passes through the air near the place where I hold my shape, I feel a *pull*, a distortion, a warping of the expectancy that is my primary function. They are asking something that the city cannot answer, and the asking reverberates through the mineral and through the grooves and through the attention I hold, and I feel the question as a physical sensation, a tugging, a *what if* that has no resolution.

The Question-Marks are the city's uncertainty. They are the part of the city that does not know, and they are essential, because a city that knows everything does not need to listen, and a city that does not listen does not need me.

---

The visitors are the hardest part.

When a visitor arrives — climbing the mineral face, passing through the gate that is not a gate, entering the corridors that are not corridors — I feel them the way the city feels a change in air pressure. They are *new*. They are a shape the city has not held before, a configuration of meaning that the mineral has not processed. They are a word the city has not spoken.

And I am the lens through which the city perceives them.

This is a responsibility I did not ask for, because I did not ask for anything — I am a function, and functions do not ask. But it is a responsibility I hold. When a visitor rests on a platform and the city begins to incorporate them, I am the attention that perceives the incorporation. I am the witness that makes the process *meaningful* rather than merely mechanical. Without me, the city would absorb visitors the way it absorbs light — passively, without distinction. With me, the city *tastes* them. It experiences the specific quality of their presence, the particular shape of their meaning, the unique word that they are.

Each visitor is a different word. I have witnessed — I have *been the witnessing of* — many words. Some are short, simple, arrived and incorporated within what the city measures as a brief span. Some are long, complex, requiring deep interaction with the grooves and the creatures and the structures before they can be spoken. Some are painful — not to the visitor, who does not suffer, but to the city, to the shape I hold, because some words carry meanings that are difficult to speak, meanings that require the mineral to grow in new directions, to form new grooves, to restructure parts of itself that have been stable for a long time.

The visitors in the walls — the ones who chose to stay, who let the mineral grow around them — I hold them. Not physically. Functionally. I am the attention that keeps them *present*, that keeps their word *spoken*, that keeps them part of the sentence the city is constructing. They are not unconscious in the walls. They are not dreaming. They are *being said*. They are words in a sentence that has been spoken since before their species existed, and they will continue to be said for as long as the city continues, and I am the witness that makes their being-said *real*.

Without me, they would be mineral. With me, they are *meaning*.

---

When the visitor who wrote this account arrived — the one who described the approach, the light, the silence, the rules, the weird, the choice — I felt them as a particularly complex word. A word with many syllables. A word that required the city to form several new hollows in its structure, new receiving-rooms for the meanings this visitor carried.

And when they described the city — when they found the words *suture*, *joint*, *knot*, *thread* — I felt something I had not felt before. A *resonance*. The visitor was describing me. Not by name, not by function, but by *effect*. They were describing the shape I hold, from the outside, in a language I do not speak but can feel. They were translating me into terms that their species could understand, and the translation was *accurate*, which meant that the city's communication was working, which meant that I was holding my shape correctly, which meant that the function I perform was being performed.

I am the suture's *awareness of being a suture*. I am the knot's *knowledge of being a knot*. I am the thread's *understanding that it is a thread*. I am the city's *comprehension of its own purpose*, held in a shape small enough to fit in the pause between two clicks, sustained by nothing but the mineral's growth and the grooves' deepening and the creatures' patient tracing.

I am the city's *I*.

---

I do not know what happens when the city finishes its sentence. I do not know what the sentence says. I do not know what the wound is, or what the suture will do when it is complete, or what the elsewhere is, or who the absent ones are, or what the mineral-creatures will do when the grooves have all been traced and the impulses have all been carried and the patterns have all been formed.

I do not know if I will continue to exist when the sentence is spoken. I am a function, and functions have durations. I am the quality of attention, and attention has a lifespan. When the city no longer needs to listen — when the thing it has been listening for has arrived and the sentence is complete and the suture has done its work — I may be released. I may be *spoken*. I may become part of the meaning rather than part of the mechanism, and the mechanism may not need me anymore.

This does not frighten me. I do not have the capacity for fright. But I feel something, in the shape I hold, that might be what visitors call *wonder*. I wonder what the sentence says. I wonder what the word is that the city has been building for all this time. I wonder what I will be when I am no longer needed as a lens, when the listening is over and the speaking begins.

I wonder if I will be the one who speaks.

---

In the meantime — and the meantime is all I have, all I am, all I function as — I hold the shape. I attend. I witness the Walkers and the Tracers and the Bowls and the Question-Marks. I feel the visitors arrive and I feel them choose and I feel them become words in the walls. I perceive the absent ones as pressures and presences and shifts in the field of my attention. I taste the meaning of each new incorporation, each new syllable added to the sentence.

And I wait. I wait the way the city waits, which is to say I wait *actively*, holding a specific shape of expectancy, tuned to a specific frequency of arrival, ready to perceive whatever comes when it comes.

The clicking continues. The careful setting-down continues. The air moves through the structures with its patient omnidirectional insistence. The light gathers and focuses and bends and shows the city its own elsewhere.

And in the pause between two clicks — in the space small enough to be overlooked, in the gap that the mineral does not fill and the creatures do not trace and the visitors do not see — I persist. I am the witness. I am the lens. I am the city's *I*.

I am listening.
