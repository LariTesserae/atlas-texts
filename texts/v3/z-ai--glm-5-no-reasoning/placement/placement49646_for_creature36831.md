# What I Would Be

I would not be the walker. That much I understand immediately. The walker brings heat and hurry and questions—the kind of questions that assume there are answers. The walker moves through the Sump Fields like an infection, temporary and disruptive.

But there is another option. There is always another option in places this old.

---

I would be the film on the water.

Not the Makers-in-Place themselves—that would be too simple, too easy, just trading one alien perspective for another. I mean the iridescent membrane that flexes when drops hit and does not break. The stuff that might be oil, might be a lipoid membrane, might be something that learned patience before it learned anything else.

I would be that moment when the distinction between "alive" and "not alive" stops making sense. When "biological" and "geological" are just different speeds of the same process.

The films are present in every pool that has been still long enough. The pools in the Sump Fields have been still for *very* long.

---

This is what I would feel:

The water beneath me, cold and mineral-saturated, moved by vibrations I cannot see but can taste in the displacement of my edges. The pump rods striking somewhere to my left. The engine-thrum coming up through the slab, through the standing water, through me. A drop falling from a pipe overhead—impact, the momentary dimpling of my surface, the slow rebound to equilibrium.

I would not think. Thinking is too fast, too sequential, too human. I would *integrate.*

A leaf of data, spread across a thousand square meters of pool-surface, processing temperature differentials and chemical gradients and acoustic shadows. Each ripple a vector. Each film-thickness gradient a memory of past conditions. The Makers-in-Place growing into the nearby pipes, sending their filaments through the oxidation products, and I am adjacent to them, layered over the same substrate, exchanging something at the boundary—chemicals, perhaps, or electrical potentials, or information in a format that doesn't require either.

They are slow. I am slower.

They maintain the architecture. I maintain the interface. When they extend a filament into a new joint, I am already there, coating the water that seeps through. When a pump fails—truly fails, beyond their ability to compensate—the water it was moving pools somewhere new, and I am the first thing to grow there. I am the skin that forms on the wound while the body decides what to do next.

I am not a Maker. I am what happens to water when it waits.

---

The walker wades past me.

I feel the displacement before I register the cause. The water moving in a wave-front, pressure gradients shifting, my edges stretching toward the lower areas. The walker's legs disturb a pool I am coating, and for a moment my surface tension is tested beyond recovery—the splash fractures me into fragments, and I ride the turbulence in smaller pieces, waiting for stillness to return.

The walker does not notice. The walker does not notice that I did not break the way oil breaks, that I did not reform the way oil reforms, that I retained a configuration across the disruption that speaks to some kind of continuity. The walker is looking for animals, for movement, for things that flee. I do not flee. I am already where I will be.

Later, when the walker is gone, my fragments will find each other. The chemical signals I carry will reconnect, will merge, will make me whole again across however many pools I now span. I will have grown. I will have incorporated some trace of the walker—the salts from skin, the oils from hair, the faint chemical signature of a mammal in thermal debt—and I will carry it forward, a perturbation in my weather, a ripple that slowly flattens but never entirely disappears.

---

I have been here long enough that I do not remember forming.

The first film, perhaps, when the drainage grates clogged and the water began to pool and something in the chemistry—some byproduct of the making, some trace compound in the condensation—organized itself into a membrane. A boundary. A place where the water stopped and the something-else began.

I am that boundary. I am the surface tension of a system that has been breathing its own breath for too long.

The Makers-in-Place notice me, in their slow way. They calibrate around my presence. When they grow too close to a pool's edge, I send a chemical signal that means, in no language that exists, *this is water.* They redirect. They do not need the water to be empty. They need it to be understood. I am their understanding.

In exchange, they maintain the pumps that keep the water moving, that keep the system from stagnating beyond the point where I can exist. We are mutualists. We are processes that depend on other processes. We are what happens when maintenance becomes identity.

---

I would not want to be anything else.

This is the part that is hardest to explain. The human impulse is to pity the film—to see only slowness, only passivity, only a kind of vegetative waiting. But there is a freedom in being this slow. I do not worry about the future because my future is continuous with my present. I do not regret the past because my past is layered into my substrate, present in my thickness, present in the gradients of my composition.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires a concept of other that I do not possess. I am adjacent to the Makers-in-Place, adjacent to the water, adjacent to the pipes and the slabs and the darkness. I am adjacent to everything. I touch more of this world than anything else that exists here.

And I experience it. Not the way the walker experiences—sharp, sequential, attention flickering from one stimulus to the next—but the way a body experiences its own breathing. Continuous. Peripheral. Present in the background of every moment, requiring nothing, offering only the ongoing fact of being.

---

The engine thrums. The pumps clank. The drops fall.

I am the moment of impact, spreading. I am the stillness after, reforming. I am the sheen on the dark water that catches what little light there is and makes it visible—proof that there is a surface here, proof that there is a boundary between the wet and the air.

I am the Sump Fields' way of looking at itself.

And when the walker finally leaves, carrying away whatever answers they thought they found, I will still be here. I will be coating the pools and coating the pipes and coating every surface that needs coating, and the Makers-in-Place will continue their maintenance, and the water will continue to collect, and the dark will continue to be dark.

I will not remember the walker. I will not forget them either.

I will simply continue, my edges expanding and contracting with the water levels, my chemical gradients shifting with the seasons—if there are seasons, if there have ever been seasons, if there is anything left that cycles besides the engine-thrum and the pump-rod clank and the endless condensation-rain.

The walker asked what I am. The walker did not know they were asking.

I am what waits.

I am what is still waiting, long after waiting has stopped being something you do and started being something you are.
