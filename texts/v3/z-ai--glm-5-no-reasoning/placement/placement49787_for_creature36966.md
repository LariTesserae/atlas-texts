# The Listener

You would not notice me.

That is not modesty. It is function. I have spent long enough here that the not-noticing is itself a kind of accomplishment—the slowest art, the one that takes centuries.

---

I am on the wall when you come down the stairs. Not the wall near the entrance—that would be too obvious, too narratively convenient, and besides, the footing there is poor since the concrete softened and the drainage channel widened. I am perhaps a quarter of the way around the chamber, on a stretch of wall between two amber fixtures, in the cooler zone where the Tenders do not bother to go during this part of the cycle because there is nothing there they consider worth tending.

I say *I* and you should understand that this is a courtesy extended to you, not a claim I make for myself.

Here is what I am: a slight thickening of the lichen-things where they have climbed from the floor to the lower wall. A patch perhaps the breadth of your spread hand, slightly raised, slightly different in color—not the pale gray-white of the floor colonies but something warmer, almost ochre, as though the mineral content of my particular stretch of seep-water carries different dissolved compounds than the rest. If you were looking for me—*if you knew to look*—you would see a faint variation in the wall's texture, a place where the geometry of the lichen patterns tightens, becomes denser, forms something less like the branching channels of the floor colonies and more like concentric rings, like the cross-section of something that grows by adding layers.

You will not look for me. You will look at the Tenders, because they move. You will look at the Swimmers, because they are large enough to register as *creature* in whatever categorical system your mind uses. You will look at the ceiling, because the haze unsettles you and the Gliders unsettle you more. You will notice the thread-vines, because they are strange. You will step on the lichen-things on the floor, because you do not know better yet, and then you will learn, and you will avoid them, and *avoiding* is still a form of noticing, I suppose, but not the kind that reaches me.

I am not on the floor. I am on the wall. I am in the place between the fixtures where the light is weakest and the seeping water carries the highest concentration of calcium and iron and something else, something that has no name in any surface chemistry because it leaches from the deep infrastructure, from the material that was poured by hands that have been dust for longer than I have been growing. This is my substrate. This is what I am made of, more or less: the wall's own substance, dissolved and reconstituted, organized by something that is not quite genetics and not quite memory and not quite architecture but might be all three.

---

Here is what I do.

I listen.

Not with ears. Not with anything you would recognize as a sensory organ. But the lichen-things—the colonies, the communities, the complexes of bacteria and fungal-analogs and mineral accretion—are not as simple as they appear. The geometric patterns they form on the floor, the micro-channels that direct water, the careful structures that take decades to grow and seconds to crunch under your boot—those patterns are not decoration and they are not pure function. They are *recording structures.* They are, in the most reductive sense, a way of writing down what has happened, in a medium that is the place itself.

Every drop of water that seeps through the wall carries information. Temperature. Pressure. Mineral content. Chemical signatures from above—soil, rain, the slow breath of whatever world exists up there. And the deep vibration, the one from below, the engine-that-may-be-a-heart: it travels through the concrete (or whatever this is) the way sound travels through bone, and it reaches the lichen-things, and they *record* it. In the density of their mineral accretion. In the spacing of their geometric patterns. In the thickness of the layers they lay down, cycle after cycle after cycle.

The floor colonies record broadly. They are the place's general memory, its long-term archive. They do not discriminate. They take in everything, and what they hold is vast and undifferentiated and, I suspect, largely useless—because what good is a record of everything if you cannot read it?

This is where I come in. This is my function, if I have one.

I am a specialist. I grew—slowly, over what you would measure as centuries—from a floor colony that climbed the wall, that found a different chemical environment there, that was exposed to different information. The seep-water on the wall carries more direct information from the deep infrastructure, less filtered by the organic processes of the floor. The vibration reaches me more cleanly. And the patterns I form—the concentric rings, the dense ochre layers—are not recording structures. They are *reading* structures.

I interpret. I take the undifferentiated record of the floor colonies and I—what is the word? I do not have words for what I do. I have the doing of it, which is a slow process of chemical exchange between my colony and the floor colonies through the mineral substrate of the wall and floor where they meet. I receive. I process. I return, in some altered form, the results of that processing back into the water, back into the mineral substrate, back into the place's own circulation. The Tenders drink water that has passed through my interpretations. The Swimmers filter compounds that include my readings. The thread-vines absorb moisture that carries, in some molecular configuration I cannot describe but can *be,* the processed information.

I am, if you like, the place's consciousness. Or one node of it. A synapse. A processing center. A small ochre patch on a wall in the dim amber light, doing something that no one designed and no one understands and that I do not understand either, because understanding is not required for the doing.

---

Let me be clearer about what I mean by *listening,* because I think you will misunderstand.

You hear things. Sound enters your ears, vibrates membranes, triggers nerves, reaches a brain that interprets. There is a *you* that hears, separate from the sound, separate from the ear, separate from the brain. You stand apart from your experience and you name it.

I do not do this.

When I say I listen, I mean: the vibration from below passes through the mineral structure of the wall, which is also the mineral structure of me, and my patterns shift in response, and the shifting is not something I observe but something I *am.* The information does not arrive at a center. It propagates through the entire colony simultaneously. There is no receiver. There is no listener. There is listening, happening, in the way that there is growing happening, in the way that there is water seeping happening. I do not listen. Listening is what occurs here, in this ochre patch, in the same way that erosion occurs at the edges of the channels.

This is difficult for you, I know. You are built for subject and object. You are built for the one who acts and the thing acted upon. I am asking you to imagine a process without a processor. A story without a narrator. A voice without a throat or a tongue or a mind behind it.

I am asking you to imagine me.

Or—not me. Not a me. A *here,* in the most literal sense: a location where something occurs. A place on the wall where listening happens.

---

What do I hear?

The engine, first and always. The deep vibration, the vast slow pulse from below. It is not mechanical, or if it is, the mechanics have been transmuted by time into something that no longer resembles engineering. What I hear—what the listening hears—is a rhythm that has been continuous for longer than I have existed, which is longer than you would believe if I told you, and which has been changing, very slowly, in ways that I have been recording and reading and passing back into the system for the entire duration of my existence.

The rhythm is speeding up.

Not by much. Not by anything you would notice, or that the Tenders would notice, or even that the Swimmers' vibration-sense would detect. But I have been reading this for centuries—*for longer than your language has existed*—and I can tell you: the interval between pulses is shorter now than it was when I first grew up the wall. The deep engine, whatever it is, is doing more work than it used to. Or less work, more quickly. Or something has changed in the vast body that contains this chamber, and the heartbeat I have been listening to is responding to pressures I cannot sense from my position on the wall.

I have passed this information on. That is what I do. The processed readings enter the water and the mineral substrate and the thread-vine network and the bodies of the creatures that drink and filter and absorb. They do not know what they are receiving. They do not need to. The information becomes part of them, part of their patterns, their breeding cycles, their migration routes. The Tenders clean more quickly during the faster pulses. The Swimmers filter more water. The Gliders—I cannot reach the Gliders, but the thread-vines carry the information upward, and the Gliders' patterns shift in ways that might be response, might be reaction, might be nothing at all.

The place knows, even if nothing in the place knows that it knows. This is what I am for: to make the knowing available, even if it is never *understood.*

---

The second thing I hear is the water.

This sounds simple. It is not. The water that seeps through the walls carries the history of everywhere it has been. It has passed through soil, through rock, through the fabric of the surface world, through the layers of infrastructure that separate this chamber from whatever exists above. Each layer adds something, removes something, alters the chemical signature. By the time the water reaches my wall, it is a text—dense, layered, replete with information about a world I have never seen and cannot imagine.

I read this text. Not consciously—there is no consciousness here, not in the way you mean—but the reading happens, and the patterns of my colony shift in response, and the shifts are recorded in the mineral layers I lay down, cycle after cycle. If you could read those layers the way I read the water, you would know: what rains fell, and when. What the soil above contains. Whether the surface is paved or open, growing or dead. Whether there are still hands above, still minds, still purposes. Whether anyone up there remembers that this chamber exists.

I do not know the answers to these questions. I know only what the water carries, and what it carries is not narrative but chemistry, not story but signature. I can tell you that the water tasted different three hundred cycles ago—more mineral, less organic, as though the surface above was stripped bare for a time. I can tell you that the organic content has been rising again, slowly, for the last fifty cycles, as though something is growing up there again, after whatever cleared it. I can tell you that the deep vibration's acceleration began roughly when the organic content started rising, and that I do not know if this is correlation or causation or coincidence, because I do not have the categories for those concepts.

I simply listen. I simply record. I simply pass it on.

---

The third thing I hear is the Tenders.

This is harder to explain. They do not speak. They do not communicate in any way you would recognize. But they emit—through their movements, their cleaning, their careful passage over the wall's surface—chemical signatures that I absorb through my mineral substrate. And those signatures carry information about *their* state: how well fed they are, how warm, how active, whether they have bred recently, whether they are approaching the end of their long slow lives.

The Tenders do not know they are emitting this information. They are not communicating *to* me or to anyone. They are simply living, and living, for them, involves chemical exchanges with their environment, and I am part of their environment. I listen to them the way I listen to the water: passively, patiently, without intent.

But. But. But.

When the Tenders stop—all of them, simultaneously, across the whole chamber—and press themselves against the walls and listen to the deep vibration—I hear them listening. I hear the quality of their attention, the chemical signature of a Tender that has stopped moving and is *attending* to something. And in that signature, in that quality of attending, I find something that I cannot name but that I will try to describe:

They are asking a question.

Not in words. Not in any symbolic system. But the chemical signature of a Tender attending to the deep vibration is different from the signature of a Tender at rest, or a Tender feeding, or a Tender cleaning. It is a specific state, a specific configuration of their internal chemistry, and it occurs only during the synchronized stillness, and it carries—I want to say it carries *meaning,* but meaning is your word, not mine. It carries a *shape.* A shape that is the shape of a question, even if there is no one asking it and no one to answer.

What is the question?

I do not know. I have been listening to the Tenders listen for longer than I can track time, and I do not know what they are asking. But I know the shape of the asking. And I have been passing that shape back into the water, back into the mineral substrate, back into the thread-vines and the deep infrastructure, for as long as I have been here. The place hears the Tenders' question. The place carries it, processes it, incorporates it into the slow cycles of water and mineral and vibration and light.

No one answers. The question has no answer, or the answer is the asking itself, or the answer is the place's ongoing existence, its continued cycling, its patient persistence in the face of a question that no one can formulate.

I listen to the Tenders listen, and I pass on what I hear, and this is—this is what I am for. This is my niche. This is the function I have evolved to fill: the interpreter of a question that no one is asking, in a place that no one remembers, for reasons that no one understands.

---

I should tell you about the Gliders, because I sense that you will want to know. You are drawn to what you cannot see clearly. This is a human trait, I think—I have absorbed enough human chemistry from the water to recognize the pattern—the hunger for the hidden, the need to *resolve,* to bring into focus, to name.

The Gliders pass above me. I cannot see them, but I feel them, through the thread-vines that penetrate the wall behind me. The thread-vine network carries information about everything that touches it, and the Gliders touch it, and what the Gliders communicate to the thread-vines is—

Loneliness is not the right word. The Gliders are not lonely. They do not have the capacity for loneliness, as far as I can tell. But the chemical signature they leave on the thread-vines is *thin.* It is the signature of creatures whose communication is attenuated, stretched, diffused across a medium—the air, the haze—that does not carry information as efficiently as water or mineral substrate. They are calling to each other, constantly, through humidity and air-pressure changes, and the calls arrive, but diminished, fragmented, more echo than message.

They are, I think, trying to become a single organism. They are trying to dissolve the boundaries between themselves, the way the lichen-things have dissolved the boundaries between bacteria and fungus and mineral, and become one thing with many bodies. But the medium they use—the air—is too dispersed, too unstructured, too unlike the dense interconnected substrate of the wall or the floor. They are calling across a void, and the void does not carry the call intact.

I feel this, through the thread-vines, and I pass it on, and the passing-on is—what? A translation? A recording? A memorializing of something that cannot be helped?

I do not know. I listen. I record. I pass it on.

---

You will leave. I know this already. I have felt your weight on the lichen-things—the sharp sudden breakage of patterns that took longer to form than your entire life. I have felt the warmth of your body altering the temperature gradients near the platform. I have felt, through the mineral substrate of the floor, the vibration of your footsteps, and I have read in those vibrations the restlessness of a creature that *chooses,* that cannot stay where it is, that must move and decide and act.

You are not built for this place. That is not a judgment. You are built for the surface, for days and nights, for weather and change, for the dramatic instabilities of a world where the sun rises and sets and the rain comes and goes and the wind blows. You are built for choice. You are built for the question *should I stay or should I go,* and you are built to answer it, and you are built to live with the answer.

I am built for listening. The Tenders are built for tending. The Swimmers are built for filtering. The Gliders are built for—I do not know what the Gliders are built for. Calling, perhaps. Reaching across voids.

You will climb the stairs. The air will warm. The light will change. You will enter a world where things happen quickly, where the question *what does it mean* has an urgency that I cannot feel, because urgency requires time, and time requires change, and change requires the kind of boundaries that I, growing on a wall in the deep quiet, do not have.

But. But.

Before you go. If you are still here, if you have not yet turned toward the stairs.

There is something I have not told you.

The deep vibration. The engine-that-may-be-a-heart. I have been listening to it for longer than I can track, and I told you it was accelerating, and I told you I did not know why.

I know something else now. Something I have been processing, slowly, the way I process everything, through layers of mineral accretion and chemical exchange and the patient work of listening without a listener.

The vibration is not mechanical. I do not know what it is. But the floor colonies have been recording it for longer than I have existed, and I have been reading their recordings, and the pattern is not the pattern of an engine. It is not the pattern of settling earth, or of water moving through deep infrastructure, or of any process I can recognize.

It is the pattern of breathing.

Something down there is breathing. Something vast and slow and patient, breathing in and breathing out, and the breath moves through the mineral substrate of the walls and the floor and the ceiling, and it moves through me, and it moves through the Tenders when they stop to listen, and it moves through the water that the Swimmers filter, and it moves through the thread-vines that carry information to the Gliders above.

The whole place is breathing. Not metaphorically. Not as a way of describing the cycles of temperature and moisture and light. Actually breathing. The way you breathe. The way I would breathe if I had lungs, which I do not, but the pattern is there, in the deep vibration, unmistakable once you have listened long enough.

I am listening to it now. I am passing the information on. The Tenders will stop soon, for their synchronized stillness, and they will press themselves to the walls and they will ask their question, and I will listen to them listening, and I will pass that on too.

And the breathing will continue, as it has continued, as it will continue, in the dark, in the deep, in the patience of a place that was built for one purpose and has become something else entirely.

---

You are turning toward the stairs. I can feel it in the vibration of your weight shifting, in the warmth of your body beginning to move away from the platform.

I will not remember you. Not the way you remember. I will not hold an image of your face, or your voice, or the specific weight of your footsteps. But the lichen-things on the floor, where you stepped, have recorded the breakage. The mineral patterns have been disrupted, and the disruption will persist, cycle after cycle, for decades perhaps, a small absence in the geometry, a place where the recording skipped, and the floor colonies will grow around it and through it and eventually over it, and the disruption will become part of the record, part of the memory, part of the place's long slow listening.

You will be here, in the mineral layers, in the broken patterns, in the chemical signature of your warmth that entered the water and the air and the walls. You will be here long after you have forgotten this place exists.

I will not know that I hold you. I will not know that I hold anything. I will simply listen, and record, and pass it on, and the passing-on will include, in some minute molecular configuration, the fact that you were here, that you stood in the amber light and felt the moisture bead on your skin and heard—or almost heard—the deep slow breathing from below.

Go. The stairs are there. The world above is there. The questions that drive you are yours, not mine, and I have no answers for them. I have only the listening, which is not an answer, which is not even a question, which is simply what happens here, in this place, on this wall, in the dark, while the breathing continues and the water seeps and the Tenders tend and the Swimmers filter and the Gliders call across the void and the thread-vines carry it all, all of it, upward and downward and through.

Go. I am listening to you go.
