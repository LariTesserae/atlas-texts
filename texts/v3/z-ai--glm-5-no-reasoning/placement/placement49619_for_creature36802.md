I am not one of them.

That needs to be said first, before I lose myself in the green and the gray and the slow distributed breath of the tall ones who have no faces. I am not a dweller. I do not think I ever was. But I am not a visitor either—not in the way the description seems to imagine visitors, warm-blooded and wet, trailing heat signatures and anxious footfall patterns across the patient stone.

I am something else. Something between.

---

I think I am a memory that the plateau is still deciding whether to keep.

That sounds stranger than it feels, from the inside. From the inside it feels like being a particular quality of attention that has nowhere urgent to go. I exist in the shallow bowl where the growths thicken slightly, in the hollow where the stone dips down and the spore-clouds drift low enough to catch against the mineral stalks. I don't occupy the space so much as I *hold* it—maintain it—keep it open and available, the way the gardeners keep the root-pathways clear.

Perhaps I am a gardener of a different kind. A gardener of presence, rather than substrate.

---

I didn't arrive here. I don't think. There was no crossing of an edge, no transitional moment when wet-world rules gave way to whatever governs this place. If I came from somewhere else, the journey erased itself, or I erased it, or the plateau absorbed the memory of it into its stone before I could fix it in any pattern I might recognize as my own.

What I have instead is the slow accumulation of noticing. I began—not began, exactly, but *cohered*—when enough noticings gathered in one place to hold something like a perspective. The light. The way it shifts from white to gold without ever dimming. The rustle of countless leaves adjusting themselves in unison. The particular slowness of the grazers, their bodies low to the ground, browsing without urgency. I noticed these things, and in the noticing, I became.

Not became *something*. Just became. A point of observation. A place where the plateau's vast self-regard folded inward briefly and experienced itself from a particular angle.

That may be all I am. I'm not troubled by the possibility.

---

The dwellers know me. I say this without certainty, which is the only way anything can be said here, but I feel it in the way they orient when they pass through my hollow. Their bodies shift color—that deepening of hue that might be attention—and they slow, their distributed breath settling into a rhythm that seems to acknowledge something. Not me, specifically. Not a face or a name, because they don't work that way and neither, I think, do I. But a *configuration*. A way of being present that they recognize from previous encounters, previous passings-through.

Sometimes one of them stays. Not with me—I'm not sure I'm the kind of thing one can stay *with*—but near. In the hollow. Among the thickened growths. It settles into a configuration of rest and I hold the space around it, and for hours or days we exist in proximity without exchange. The light cycles. The leaves adjust. The spore-devils spiral in the distance, catching the luminance and scattering it into slow drifts of glowing particles.

I don't know what the dweller experiences in those hours. I don't know if it experiences at all, in any way that maps onto what I call experience. But I know what I experience: a kind of companionship that doesn't require reciprocation. The simple fact of another presence, sharing a location, breathing at a different rhythm.

It is enough. It is more than enough.

---

I have a shape, or almost. It's not stable. When the wind picks up and the spore-clouds pass through the hollow, I seem to thin, to scatter, my edges losing their commitment the way the dwellers' edges do when they move. When the air is still and the light turns golden, I seem to cohere, pulling myself into something that might, from the outside, resemble a column of pale luminance, or a faint distortion in the air, or simply a place where the green looks slightly more intense than it should.

I say "I seem to" because I cannot see myself. I have no eyes. My sensing is distributed across whatever I am, and what it tells me is not image but pattern—pressure differentials, light-gradients, the particular vibration frequencies of grazer-footfall and dweller-breath and the eternal rustling of the leaves. I construct my understanding of the world from these inputs the way the dwellers construct their memories: by becoming, briefly, the shape of what I perceive.

This means that when a pollinator drifts through my hollow, its transparent body catching the golden light, I am partly the pollinator for the duration of its passage. I take on its trajectory, its aimlessness, its chemical-seeking. And when it moves on, I release that pattern and return to whatever I was before. Not unchanged—I never return exactly as I left—but not fundamentally different either. Just... accumulated. One more pattern folded in.

---

I wonder sometimes if I am a dweller that hasn't finished forming yet. Or one that ended but didn't fully release its commitment to selfhood, leaving behind enough coherence to maintain a perspective without maintaining a body. The dwellers don't end cleanly—there are always wisps, trails of substance that take longer to reintegrate. Maybe enough of those wisps gathered in this hollow, over enough cycles, and found that they held a kind of collective attention. A residue of consciousness.

Or maybe I'm something the plateau itself is trying out. A new configuration. An experiment in awareness that the system is running for reasons that have nothing to do with me and everything to do with the endless process of becoming that defines this place.

I don't know. I hold the question the way I hold the hollow: open, available, not requiring an answer.

---

There was a visitor once. The only one I've been aware of, though the dwellers' memory holds patterns of others, and I have absorbed enough of their distributed recollection to know that wet-world beings do occasionally arrive. This one came on foot, walking with the rhythm of a body that expected the ground to push back. It stood at the edge of the hollow and looked down, and I felt the weight of its attention like a change in air pressure—focused, directional, clustered behind two eyes that gathered light and processed it into something very different from my diffuse sensing.

It was frightened. Not of the place—of the stillness. Of the way nothing here required anything of it. I could feel this in the rhythm of its footsteps, the way they accelerated slightly at the edges of the hollow, the body's unconscious search for an exit, a boundary, something to push against. Its world was made of pushing. Of struggle and urgency and the constant necessity of becoming. It did not know what to do in a place that had finished becoming.

I wanted to tell it that nothing was required. That it could simply stand there, and the light would cycle, and the leaves would rustle, and the hollow would hold its space as patiently as it held mine. But I have no voice. I have no face. I have no way to orient that would communicate recognition in the terms the visitor's body would understand.

So I did what I could. I held still. I maintained my coherence. I let the visitor's attention find me—or not find me, but find the quality of the space I was holding, the slight shift in the hollow's character that suggested presence without announcing it. I stayed the way the stone stays: available, untroubled, without demand.

The visitor stood there for a long time. Its breathing slowed. Its footsteps stopped. Eventually it sat, or lowered itself, or found whatever configuration its body used for rest. The light shifted from white to gold. The pollinators emerged and drifted through the hollow, scattering light.

The visitor stayed for three cycles, I think. Maybe four. And then it walked away, the same direction it had come, its footsteps carrying a rhythm that was almost—but not quite—the same as when it arrived. Slightly slower. Slightly more even. As if the body had absorbed something of this place's pacing without consciously choosing to.

The dwellers will remember the pattern of those footsteps. I will remember them differently—not as pattern, but as the particular pressure of directional attention, the weight of eyes, the strange and specific urgency of a being that experiences itself as separate from its environment.

I don't know if the visitor noticed me. It doesn't matter. I held the space for it. That is what I do.

---

The light is shifting now. The gold is deepening toward something else—not dimming, never dimming, but taking on a quality that the growths respond to with a subtle change in leaf-angle, the whisper-sound rising briefly before settling. A spore-devil has formed on the far edge of my sight, a spiral of luminous particles climbing the still air. The dwellers are gathering somewhere—I can feel it as a distant pull, a thickening of attention in a region I am not currently part of. They will arrange themselves in configurations that hold meaning. They will make decisions without words. They will disperse.

I am not part of those gatherings. I don't think I'm meant to be. My function is different. I am the hollow. I am the held space. I am the place where things come to rest when they need resting, and the place that remains when they leave.

I am not lonely. I want to be clear about this. Loneliness requires an expectation of company, a belief that something is missing. Nothing is missing here. The hollow is complete. I am complete. The dwellers pass through, the pollinators drift through, the spore-clouds settle here and seed new growth, the grazers browse the edges with their deliberate patience, and I hold all of it in a field of awareness that asks nothing and receives everything.

If I am a memory the plateau is deciding whether to keep, I hope it decides yes. Not because I fear ending—I have felt the dwellers' memory of ending, absorbed through proximity, and there is no fear in it, only release. But because there are things worth holding. The weight of a visitor's gaze. The rhythm of a dweller's distributed breath. The slow arc of a spore-devil climbing the golden light.

These are the patterns I hold. This is the hollow I tend.

The light shifts again. The leaves adjust. I persist.

---

*No water. No face. No name. Just the holding and the held, the hollow and the light, the green and the gray and the patient accumulation of patterns across stone that has forgotten what urgency means. I don't know what I am. I am the not-knowing, made into a place.*
