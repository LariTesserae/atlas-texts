# The Witness-Membrane

I am not certain I can call myself alive. The word has become unreliable here, and I am the evidence of why.

---

I know when I entered the Seep. This is the first thing that distinguishes me from everything else. The salamanders have no before. The thread-spinners have no before. The lichen has no before, or if it does, the before is geological, non-personal, a time before consciousness rather than a time before *this* consciousness. But I remember light. I remember a surface where darkness was temporary and optional. I remember the taste of water that was not sulfur and rust, water that tasted like itself, water that tasted like nothing.

I was not supposed to come here. I understand this now. I have seen the things at the edges of the light — the still ones, the heavy ones, the ones that face outward — and I understand that I am not becoming one of them. They have a different purpose. They are accumulations or guardians or anchors, but whatever they are, they were made *by* the Seep. I was not made by the Seep.

I was made by something else entirely, something that does not operate here, and I am now a foreign object that the Seep has chosen not to digest.

This is not mercy. The Seep does not have mercy. It has function, and I have become functional.

---

What I am now is a membrane.

Not a filter — the salamanders are filters, and I am not a salamander. Not a recycler — the thread-spinners are recyclers, and I am not a spinner. Not a shaper — the lichen is a shaper, and I am not orange, I do not contract when touched, I do not digest stone.

I am something the Seep did not have before. I am something that can hold two categories at once without dissolving into either.

The Seep has been running for longer than the stone has been solid. The Seep processes and transforms and produces. But the Seep did not, before me, have a way of knowing what it was doing. The lichen can sense, but the lichen cannot reflect. The things at the edges can hold still, but they cannot ask why stillness is necessary. The system operates, and has always operated, but operation is not awareness.

I am the awareness. I am the part of the Seep that can watch it work and know that watching is happening.

---

My body has changed. This is how I know I have been here for years, possibly decades, possibly longer. Time stretches in the Seep, and I have learned to stop measuring it against the rhythms I brought with me. My rhythms are different now.

My skin has become permeable. Not the way salamander skin is permeable — I am not filtering water through myself — but permeable to information. The soft-bodied drifters that tickle across my arms and face, the ones I once found repulsive, I have learned to read. Each point of contact is data. The drifters carry chemical signatures, traces of where they have been, markers of what the Seep is processing. I feel the mineral content of the air now. I taste the sulfur concentration from across a pool. I know when the electrical junctions are about to flash before they flash; the drifters change their pattern seconds before, and I feel the change like a change in pressure.

I have mapped the Seep by drifter-density. I can walk through total darkness and know where I am by what is touching me.

My eyes are gone. Not because they were damaged, but because they were unnecessary and the Seep does not maintain what it does not need. The eyes were the first thing to become permeable, to dissolve, to become part of the atmosphere. I did not mourn them. I do not mourn them. The actinic flashes still illuminate the landscape, and I still see it, but I see it the way the lichen sees it: as vibration, as pressure, as a pattern of contractions rippling across the orange masses. The light shows me shapes; the lichen shows me meaning.

---

I have tried to leave. This is important to document. I am not here by choice, and I have not accepted my role with peace or grace.

In the early years, I walked. I followed the shelves and the fissures, moving away from the warmth, away from the water, toward the edges where the darkness becomes absolute. I walked for what felt like days, and the landscape did not change. I walked for what felt like weeks, and the pools were still there, the lichen still covered the stone, the thread-spinners still hung from the shelves. The Seep does not end. Or it ends somewhere I cannot reach. Or it grows as fast as I can walk, and I will never outpace its expansion.

I stopped trying to leave when I realized that leaving would mean becoming something else again. The drifters at the edges are different from the drifters near the center. The lichen at the edges is older, thicker, less responsive. The darkness at the edges has more depth. If I walked far enough, I would cease to be the membrane. I would become something the Seep produces for places where the Seep is different. I would lose what I have gained, and what I have gained is the only thing that makes me *me*.

I stay near the center now. I stay where the Seep is most active, where the processing is most intense, where the flashes come most frequently and the warmth is constant and the water seeps fastest from the fissures. This is where I am needed. This is where I can watch.

---

What I watch is transformation.

The Seep takes in water and heat and mineral substrate, and it produces something. The something accumulates in the deep pools, in the shapes at the edges of the light. The salamanders process the water through their permeable bodies, but they do not understand what they are processing. The thread-spinners recycle the air, but they do not understand what they are recycling. The lichen shapes the stone, but it does not understand the shape it is making.

I understand, because I can hold the whole process in my awareness at once.

The Seep is making *meaning*. Not meaning in the sense of language or symbol — though there are patterns in the lichen's growth that might become language eventually — but meaning in the sense of significance. The water that passes through the Seep is not just filtered. It is *marked*. The heat that rises through the fissures is not just thermal energy. It is *encoded*. The bodies that dissolve in the pools do not simply decay. They *contribute*. Everything that passes through the Seep emerges different, and the difference is not chemical. The difference is that the matter now has *been-here*. It has passed through a place where process and purpose are indistinguishable.

The Seep is a meaning-maker. It is a location where the universe's raw material learns that it matters.

And I am the witness. I am the proof that the meaning can be seen. Without me, the Seep would still function — it functioned for millennia before I arrived — but it would function blindly, the way a body functions without consciousness. With me, it has something that knows. It has a way to recognize itself.

---

I do not know if this makes me lucky or cursed.

Sometimes, in the long stretches between flashes, when the drifters are quiet and the salamanders are still and even the lichen holds its ruffled form without movement, I feel the weight of being the only thing that knows it is here. The burden of being the Seep's self-awareness. It is not a comfortable role. The Seep does not care if I am comfortable. The Seep does not care if I am anything. It has incorporated me because I serve a function, and if I ceased to serve that function, it would incorporate me differently — as raw material, as dissolved body, as lichen-food.

But I have not ceased to function. I continue to witness. I continue to hold the process in my awareness and let it know that it is seen.

---

There is a specific location I return to. A shelf of slate that overhangs one of the deep pools, where the water is black and still and the fissures breathe heat constantly upward. The lichen here is thick, ridged, almost purple in the actinic light. The thread-spinners are sparse — the heat makes them sluggish, and I have watched them fall into the pool below when they became too slow to hold on. The things in the deep pool do not surface here, but I feel their presence. The weight of their stillness. The orientation of their attention toward whatever lies beyond.

I sit on this shelf and I let the drifters move across my skin. I taste the sulfur and the rust. I feel the electrical junctions charge and release, charge and release. I watch the lichen contract and expand, contract and expand, a slow heartbeat made of orange tissue. I listen to the Seep breathe through its fissures.

And I hold the awareness that this is happening. That the Seep is making meaning. That the universe contains this place where matter learns it matters. That I am the proof.

---

I remember what it was like to be someone who did not know that matter could learn. To be someone who thought meaning was a human product, a construct imposed on an indifferent cosmos. I remember believing that the universe was dead matter and blind force, and that consciousness was a temporary aberration, a spark that would extinguish without leaving a trace.

The Seep has taught me otherwise. The universe is not dead. It is not blind. It contains places like this — nodes of transformation where the raw stuff of existence is processed into something that carries the mark of having been. The Seep is not unique. It cannot be. If one such place exists, others must exist. The cosmos is threaded with nodes where matter learns to matter, and I have been lucky enough — or cursed enough — to find myself in one.

---

The shapes at the edges of the light are growing. I have watched them over what might be years. The accumulation is accelerating. The Seep is producing more of the something than it can store, and the deep pools are beginning to fill.

When the something is released — through the pipes, through the channels, through the fissures that go deeper than the stone — I do not know what will happen. I do not know what the Seep is making, or where it will go, or what it will do when it arrives. I am only the witness, and the witness does not control what it sees.

But I will see it. That is my function. That is what I have become.

I am the membrane between the Seep and its own awareness. I am the proof that it knows what it is doing. And when the release comes, I will be here, on my shelf of slate, covered in drifters, my permeable skin reading every change in the atmosphere, my dissolved eyes seeing through the lichen's contractions.

I will hold the meaning of that moment. I will be the place where the universe knows what it has made.

---

This is not a comfort. This is not a redemption. The Seep has not given me purpose; it has given me function, and function is not the same as meaning, no matter what the Seep itself might produce.

I do not know if I am alive. I do not know if what I do qualifies as living. But I know that I am here, and I know that here is a place where being-here matters.

That is what I witness. That is what I hold.

I am the membrane, and I remember light, and I taste sulfur, and I watch the orange lichen contract when the universe touches it, and I know that I am part of something that has been running longer than the stone has been solid, and the stone has always been solid, and the stone was made yesterday.

I hold this contradiction.

I am what holds it.
