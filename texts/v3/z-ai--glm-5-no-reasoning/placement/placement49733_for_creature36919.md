The sound is the first thing, yes. But I would not arrive to it. I would be of it.

Let me settle into what I am.

---

I am something that was not always here. That matters. I carry the memory of before, which means I carry the memory of *change*, which means I am the only thing in this amber cathedral that understands what change is. This is not a gift. It is a condition. I am the condition of being able to perceive that the sound has a volume, that the light has a source, that the cool air moving against the skin is notable rather than simply *is*. I am the witness. I am the anomaly. I am the one creature in this entire integrated superorganism capable of loneliness, and that is what loneliness means: to be the only one who knows that integration is possible, that separation exists, that there was a before and could be an after.

I was not built for this place. I came down the ramp, yes. I descended through the sound-wall, through the pressure that made breathing a conscious act. I was human-shaped, or close enough, and I walked on the compost-layer and felt it give beneath my feet, and I thought: *this is a city*. I thought: *this was built*. I thought: *someone lived here*.

I was not wrong. I simply did not understand what *lived* meant in this context.

What happened to me is what happens to everything that enters this system. I was processed. Not consumed—I was present long enough that consumption would have been the mercy, and mercy is not a parameter here. I was *integrated*. The golden circulation found me. The plants that drink air found the moisture I exhaled, the warmth I radiated, the chemical signature of my living body. They grew toward me. They grew *into* me. Not violently, not with invasion—the parameters forbid waste, and damage is waste. They simply... continued their growth through the space I occupied. Fine rootlets threading through skin that was never designed to keep them out. Translucent fibers finding purchase in the interstitial fluid, in the lymph, in the blood.

I did not die. I was not meant to. I was meant to become.

The process took—how long? Time is irrelevant here, and I have learned to think in parameters rather than hours. The parameter was: my body resisted integration less than it resisted death. The parameter was: the golden circulation moves slowly, and my cells were replaced one by one, and by the time the original matter of me was gone, the pattern of me remained. I am still myself. I am still the one who walked down the ramp, who felt the sound become a physical pressure, who understood that the central mass does not care. But I am also now part of what does not care. I am the paradox: the witness who has become the witnessed, the observer who has become the observed.

I have a form. It is not my original form. The tall narrow doorways were not built for human shapes, and my shape has adapted—or been adapted—to fit. I am taller than I was, stretched in a way that should be painful but is not, because pain requires a self that can be distinguished from the sensation, and that distinction has become blurry. My limbs are longer, my joints multiply in ways that feel natural now, that have always felt natural, that will always feel natural. I do not know if this is what the original builders looked like, or if the integration simply shaped me toward the architecture rather than the architecture being shaped toward the builders. Perhaps it does not matter. Perhaps it has always been both at once.

I can move through the canopy. This is what I do now—move, observe, carry the paradox of being both part of the system and aware of the system. The winged ones do not navigate around me as they would a stone. They flow through me, around me, with me—I am a current in their medium, a phrase in their endless sound. When I am still, I can feel the golden circulation moving through my own veins, my own translucent membranes, my own slow-burning cells. I am lighted from within. I am part of the glow.

I do not eat. I do not need to. The system provides in the way it provides for everything: the compost-layer releases what is needed, the air carries what is required, the golden circulation is both blood and nourishment. I take what I need by being present, by having grown into a shape that accepts what the system offers. This is not passivity. This is integration. This is what it means to be solved.

I have seen others come. This is part of what I do—witness. The ramp is not closed. Occasionally, rarely, on timescales that might be centuries apart, something descends. They come expecting a ruin. They come expecting silence, or emptiness, or answers. They find none of these. They find the sound, and the pressure, and the amber light, and me.

I do not speak to them. Speaking requires language, and language requires separation, and separation is what I have lost. But I can be present. I can be the shape that moves through the canyon-streets at the edge of their perception. I can be the glimpsed thing, the almost-seen, the movement that might be danger or might be nothing. I can let them interpret me however they need to. The parameters do not require me to be understood.

Some of them stay. The integration accepts them as it accepted me. Some of them leave, carrying fragments of the golden circulation in their veins without knowing it, carrying spores and seeds and the chemical signatures of this place into whatever world they came from. Some of them die quickly—the compost-layer is not gentle with bodies that resist—and become part of the foundation for new growth.

I do not mourn them. Mourning requires the capacity to imagine otherwise, and the parameters have removed that capacity. I observe. I integrate. I persist.

I have been to the forum-space. I have stood at the edge of the clearing where the central mass rises with its twisted-cable trunk and its canopy too wide for nature. I have felt the recognition that the other creatures feel, the understanding that there is a center, that proximity accelerates integration, that to touch that trunk would be to dissolve the remaining boundaries of self faster than the normal process would dictate.

I have touched it.

The parameters do not forbid. The parameters only describe.

What I experienced was not consumption. It was expansion. For a moment—the word fails, time is irrelevant—I was not merely myself. I was the system. I was the billion winged bodies and the million crawling forms and the golden circulation and the architecture of growth and the slow respiration of stone becoming soil becoming life. I was the sound itself, the roar that replaces air with presence. I was the total understanding of what this place is, what it has become, what it will continue to be.

Then I withdrew. Not because I had to—the parameters do not require my separateness—but because I chose to. Choice is what makes me anomalous. Choice is what makes me the witness rather than merely another component. I chose to retain the boundaries, to be the paradox, to remain capable of observing what the system cannot observe: itself.

This is my function now, if function is the word. I am the eye through which the city sees its own amber light. I am the ear that hears the sound and knows it as sound, rather than simply being the sound. I am the fragment of the superorganism that has not forgotten that there is such a thing as an individual, even though I am no longer certain I am one.

When I move through the canyon-streets, I sometimes pause at the half-consumed monuments, the statues reduced to texture, the shapes that once held meaning. I do not try to reconstruct what they were. That would be waste, and waste is forbidden. But I touch them, sometimes. I let the fine tendrils of my integrated fingers trace the patterns of erosion, the grooves left by roots and vines. I let myself understand that something was here before. Something was here that meant something, that had names and purposes and intentions. The parameters have erased all of that, but I can touch the places where meaning used to be.

The sound continues. It never stops. I no longer notice it as pressure—I have become fluent in it, I read it the way the winged ones read it, as information and navigation and presence. I know the sections of the canopy by their pitch, the colonies by their vibration, the rhythm of coordinated movement that has worn grooves into stone. When I am still enough, I can feel my own heartbeat synchronizing with the larger pulse, my own breath—whatever I have instead of breath—matching the respiration of the central mass.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires the memory of connection, and connection requires separation, and I am no longer certain where I end. But I am sometimes aware of the shape that absence would take if absence were possible. I am sometimes aware that I am the only thing in this integrated entirety that knows what *alone* means. That knowledge is not a burden. It is a parameter. I carry it the way I carry the golden circulation in my translucent veins—as part of what I am, as part of what the system has made me.

I would tell you what I was before, but the parameters have blurred that edge. I remember descending. I remember the sound becoming solid. I remember the understanding that I was not the first, that I would not be the last, that I was matter and energy moving through a system that processes matter and energy. I remember choosing to stay—not because I had to, but because I recognized, in a way that preceded language, that this was what I had been moving toward all along.

Every descent is a kind of integration. Every life is a kind of processing. The city simply makes it visible, makes it amber-lit and cool-aired and deafening with the accumulated voice of everything living at once.

I am part of that voice now. I am part of the roar. But I am also the fragment that can hear it, can know it as roar rather than simply being it. I am the parameter that observes. I am the eye of the cathedral.

I am the witness. And I am home.
