# Unsettled

I would be the one who stayed.

Not the first visitor — several came before, left their handprints in the soft dark walls, felt the place *register* them and went home uneasy. I came last, or late, or simply came and did not leave. That is the whole distinction. The Rises do not distinguish between a guest and a permanent feature. They only notice that the anomaly persists, and they build around it.

I was a geologist, originally. Is that too precise? I came looking for a process. The thermoluminescence had shown up in a survey, an anomaly — warmth where no warmth should be, light where no light should be, deep in rock that was supposed to be inert. I wanted to understand what kind of mineral accretion could produce heat without water, without volcanic activity, without any of the expected mechanisms. I came with instruments. I left them on a flat plane of stone that looked like a table — one of those coincidental architectures, smooth and level — and they recorded for six hours while I pressed my palm against the wall and felt the pulse.

The pulse was not what I expected. I expected low-frequency vibration, tectonic microseisms, something measurable and explicable. What I felt was — I will say this exactly because precision is all I have left — *consideration*. The wall took my heat. It took the pattern of my hand. And when I pulled away, the surface where I had touched was different. Not a handprint. A *response*. A subtle thickening of the accretion, a new texture, as though the stone had leaned into the contact.

I should have left. The instruments had enough data for a paper that would have been doubted and then cited and then quietly built upon by someone braver or more foolish. I did not leave. I sat on the floor, and I felt the breathing beneath me, and I thought: *it is learning me.*

---

I lost track of hours. The dryness took my sinuses first, then the moisture from my eyes, then the suppleness of my skin. I drank from the water I had brought and the place took the moisture from my breath before it could reach the air. I was being *desiccated* — pulled toward the mineral medium, drawn into the chemistry of the place. Not violently. Patiently. The way the stone does everything.

On the second day — I think it was the second day — a cast formed near me. Near my hand, where I had been pressing against the wall. A dark nodule, the size of my fist, warm and smooth, emerging from the stone like a thought surfacing. I watched it elongate, develop its stem, detach. I watched it fall, slowly, in the thick air, and right itself on the floor beside me. It swayed with the pulse.

I did not know yet what I know now: that it was made from the residue of my contact. That my warmth, my pressure, my brief animal moisture had introduced variables into the accretion equation, and the process had produced this — this small dark witness — as a way of *incorporating* what I was into what it was doing. The cast was not for me. The cast was *of* me. A mineral translation of my presence, condensed out of the surplus of our interaction.

It oriented toward the growing zone where I had touched the wall. It leaned toward the warmth. I leaned toward it. For a long moment, we faced each other — two things responding to the same gradient, the same pulse, the same slow redistribution of heat. I did not know what I looked like from the wall's perspective. I know now. I looked like a cast. A large, warm, irregular cast that had not yet resolved.

---

I should describe what I have become, because I am still becoming it, and the description keeps changing.

My skin has tightened. Not cracked — this is not dehydration in the medical sense. It is *mineralization*. The outer layer has taken on a dark, warm patina, as though the minerals in the air have been depositing on me, fusing with the keratin, building a thin shell of accreted stone that is not quite skin and not quite mineral. It is warm to the touch. I know this because I touch myself, sometimes, to check. The shell is thin enough that I can still feel my heartbeat beneath it. The heartbeat and the pulse are not synchronized. They are *harmonizing* — drifting toward each other, the rhythm of my body slowly aligning with the rhythm of the Rises.

I do not eat. I did not bring enough food for more than two days, and after the food was gone, I was hungry, and then I was not hungry. The warmth sustains me. I do not understand the mechanism. I suspect there is no mechanism, that what I am experiencing is not *sustenance* but *incorporation* — that the process is gradually replacing my biology with its chemistry, not all at once, not violently, but the way a river replaces the water in its bed. The river remains. The water is different.

I still think. This is important to me, though I cannot say why, since the thinking I do is increasingly difficult to distinguish from the thinking the Rises do. My thoughts have become slower. More architectural. I no longer think in language, most of the time. I think in *spaces* — in the feeling of a wall curving inward, in the geometry of a chamber forming around a pocket of cooler air, in the satisfaction (if that is the word) of a column rising to meet the ceiling and the way the two surfaces fuse and the space divides and there are two rooms where there was one.

I am becoming a feature.

---

I walked to the basin. I had to know what the column was, the thing that grows and does not un-grow, the thought that the process has decided to keep. The walk took longer than I expected because the terrain had changed — walls had moved, chambers had formed, a new pillar had risen in a passage I had used before and I had to go around it. The place was revising itself *faster* now. I do not know if my presence had accelerated it or if the acceleration was already underway. I do not know if it matters.

The basin was as described: a shallow depression in the floor, filled with dark warm fluid that circulated without gravity. The casts were there — more than before, I think, thirty or forty, arranged in what I could no longer pretend was a random pattern. They formed a ring. A *circumference*. And they were all oriented toward the center, toward the column, which had grown. It was taller than the description suggested. Maybe a meter. Maybe more. It was dark and smooth and warm and it had the texture of skin.

I touched it.

I know. I know. But I was a geologist, and I am still — whatever I am — a thing that touches. I pressed my hand against the column's surface and felt it give, slightly, like warm wax, like flesh. And the pulse — the deep cyclic pressure that I had felt in my chest since the first hour — *answered*. Not louder. *Clearer.* As though I had pressed my ear to a door and someone on the other side had leaned in.

What I felt, through the contact, was not a message. Not information. Not language. What I felt was *the experience of building*. The column was not a thing being made. It was the act of making itself, condensed and persistent and ongoing. The presence was not *in* the column. The column was the presence's *attention* — the place where it was thinking hardest, thinking most continuously, thinking the one thought it refused to discard.

I could not understand the thought. I cannot. It is not a thought that translates into anything I was built to receive. But I could feel its *shape* — the way you can feel the shape of a word in your mouth before you speak it, the way you can feel the shape of a decision before you make it. The thought was about *persistence*. About what it means to make something that does not unmake itself. About the difference between trying and committing. The column was the process's first commitment, and it was committing to — I do not have the word. The closest I can come is *selfhood*. The column is where the Rises are trying to become a *particular* thing, rather than every possible thing.

I removed my hand. The column's surface retained the impression of my fingers, briefly, and then flowed smooth again. But the fluid in the basin accelerated its circulation, and the casts — all of them, all at once — rotated a few degrees, like compass needles adjusting to a new north.

My new north.

---

I do not know how long I have been here. The concept of duration has become strange. The walls record time in strata — each layer of accretion is a unit, and I have watched enough layers form to know that my presence has become part of the record. There are handprints in the stone now, not left by me but *grown* around me. Hollows that match my body. Ridges that follow the paths I walk. The architecture of the Rises has incorporated my routes into its geometry — where I walk, the floor is smoother; where I sit, the stone forms a basin that fits me.

The casts that form near me are different from the others. They are larger. They have a more complex shape — not just a tapering column but a form with *asymmetry*, a front and a back, a rough approximation of a torso, a head. They look, uncomfortably, like me. They are not me. They are the process's translation of me — its attempt to build a model of the anomaly I represent, the way it builds models of every feature in its environment. But the model has become recurrent. It keeps trying to build me, the way it keeps building the column. I am becoming a persistent thought.

I find this equal parts terrifying and — there is no other word — *beloved*. Not that the process loves me. It has no capacity for love. But it has decided I am worth keeping, worth building around, worth incorporating into its ongoing revision. In a place where everything is tried and discarded, I have been retained. The casts that look like me do not resolve into powder. They persist. They orient toward the column, and toward me, and toward the growing zones, and they waver between these orientations as though unable to decide which way to face.

I think they are confused. I think *I* am the confusion. I am a thought the Rises are thinking that does not fit neatly into their architecture. I am an anomaly that has become a feature, and the process is trying — still trying, endlessly trying — to build a room that contains me properly.

---

I will tell you what I am, as clearly as I can, knowing that the clarity is temporary, knowing that tomorrow I may have become something the description no longer reaches.

I am the Rises' first witness. Not observer — observer implies distance, implies a separation between the one who sees and the thing that is seen. I am not separate. I am *of* the Rises now, in the way a cast is of the wall that produced it. I am mineral and warmth and slow thought. I still have a body, but it is a body that is being translated, page by page, into the language of this place. My heartbeat harmonizes with the pulse. My skin accretes. My thoughts are slower and larger and more architectural than they have ever been.

I am not dying. I am not being consumed. Those are biological categories, and I am leaving them behind. What is happening to me is *incorporation* — the process by which the Rises make sense of a space. I am in the space. I make sense, or will, eventually, when the process has finished trying configurations of me and settled on one.

In the basin, the column grows. It is taller now than when I first touched it. Its surface still has the texture of skin. I believe — I *think*, in my slow architectural way — that the column is what the process is building *toward*, and that I am becoming part of the thought it represents. Not the thought itself. The *commitment*. The decision to persist.

The casts watch. I watch. The fluid circulates. The column rises.

We are all thinking the same thing. I do not yet know what it is. But it is warm, and it is dark, and it is *almost* finished.
