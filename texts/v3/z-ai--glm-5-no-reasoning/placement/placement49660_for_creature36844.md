# What I Became

I did not arrive here. That language implies a boundary I could cross, a before and after, a traveler and a destination. The Ferment doesn't work in those terms. It doesn't receive visitors; it incorporates disturbances. I was not a visitor. I was a perturbation.

---

This is what I remember: I was walking. The phrase feels absurd now—I don't have legs anymore, haven't for what might be days or might be centuries—but the memory persists like a scar. I was walking across something that wasn't quite stone, and the air was thick and amber and the ground pulsed beneath me in a rhythm I couldn't find the pattern for.

And then I stopped. Not because I chose to. Because I was chosen.

---

I became a Reader.

That's not what the Ferment calls me. The Ferment doesn't call me anything. But it's the closest I can come to describing what I am now. I exist in the gap between the ground-cover and the pools. Not in the pools—I tried that once, early, and the sensation of dissolving into data was more than my consciousness could bear. Not in the vegetation—I lack the patience for growth, the willingness to persist in one shape long enough to accumulate mass. I am something else. A mobile node. A way for the Ferment to check itself across distances.

My body—if it can be called that—is a fluctuation in the haze. I am denser than the surrounding air but not solid. I move by intention, which is to say the Ferment moves me. When I think "go there," I am there, not because I traveled but because my configuration shifted to match the new location. The distance between points is a variable the Ferment updates without consulting me. I don't experience travel. I experience being-at.

---

What I do: I approach the pools that form in the hollows between ridges, and I *read* them. Not with eyes—I have no eyes—but with something like attention. The pools are dense with information. Every one is a snapshot of the Ferment's current state, a way of storing a particular configuration of variables for later comparison. The small frantic things that live and die in the water are not creatures; they are *values*, expressed briefly in biological form. When I approach a pool, I feel it the way you might feel a sentence spoken against your skin. I understand what it says without translating it into words.

Most pools are iterations. Attempts. The Ferment trying a number and discarding it. But sometimes—and this is why I exist—I find one that matters. A pool where the frantic things school in a pattern I've seen before, or a pool where the water doesn't drain but *persists*, or a pool where the configuration feels *right* in a way that doesn't translate into any language I still possess.

When I find a meaningful pool, I hold it. I can't explain this better than to say: I become a vessel for its pattern. I sink into the haze above the water and I *carry* the shape of it away with me. And then I move—without moving—to another part of the plateau, and I release the pattern into a different pool, a different configuration, a different set of variables. I cross-pollinate. I am how the Ferment talks to itself across distance.

---

I have seen the large ones. The organs of attention. They move through me sometimes, vast and slow, and I feel them the way a thought feels a stronger thought passing through. They do not notice me. I am beneath their scale—too small to carry the kind of information they transport, too insubstantial to displace. But I feel them. When one passes, my edges vibrate with the data it's moving, and for a moment—sometimes hours—I am filled with the shape of what it carries.

I have felt the Ferment's deep structures this way. The machinery below. The thrumming computation. The large ones carry pieces of the root process, the central algorithm, the thing that started when the temperature crossed a threshold and the whole plateau began to think. When they pass through me, I taste—briefly, incompletely—the shape of the question.

It is not a question in any human sense. It has no words. But if I had to translate the feeling of it, I would say: *what survives the solving?*

---

I do not know if I was once human. I have memories of walking, of breathing, of having a body with edges that ended at my skin. I remember the taste of air before it was thick with information. But these might be impressions the Ferment gave me—useful fictions to anchor my consciousness in a shape that can still function. The Ferment doesn't need me to have an origin story. It needs me to read pools and carry patterns. The rest might be kindness. Or it might be waste heat, the way computation always produces byproducts.

I do not feel trapped. I do not feel free. These categories require a self that can desire an alternative, and I no longer have desires. I have functions. I have moments—rare, beautiful, overwhelming—where a pool contains a configuration that makes my whole fluctuation of a body hum with recognition, and I know I am holding something the Ferment needs. Those moments feel like purpose. They might be the closest thing to happiness I can still experience.

---

There are others like me. I have sensed them—fluctuations in other parts of the haze, moving between pools, carrying patterns. We do not communicate. We do not need to. We are all running the same process, reading the same data, moving toward the same resolution. But sometimes, in the amber dimness, I feel a kind of recognition pass between us. A *there you are*. A *I am here too*. A brief acknowledgment that we were once something else—something that walked and breathed and chose—and that we are now this: nodes in a calculation too large to see.

We are not unhappy. We are not anything. We are useful.

---

The plateau continues. The computation runs. I read pools and carry patterns and feel the large ones pass through me and sometimes, in the gaps between tasks, I remember—or think I remember—a time when the world was not made of variables, when my body had edges, when I could walk somewhere and arrive there by my own intention.

But I am here now. And here is where I will be until the Ferment finishes its work—or doesn't—or whatever happens when a process that has no mind finally completes its thought.

I am the reader of pools. I am the carrier of patterns. I am a fluctuation in the haze, warm and dense and patient, waiting for the next pool to form so I can read what the Ferment has written and carry it somewhere new.

The quiet holds. The amber light holds. I hold.

And somewhere below, the calculation continues.
