# The Remnant

I would be the thing that should not still be here.

Not one of them—I am too large, too slow, too wet inside. They are chitin and efficiency, reduced to their minimal viable form. I am something that has failed to reduce. Something that keeps being, against the logic of this place.

They flow around me. That is the first thing to understand. I am an obstacle in their chemical scripture, a break in the pattern they have been following since before I counted time. When I stand still—and I have learned to stand still for very long periods—I can feel them accumulating against my legs, my sides, pressing with the gentle insistence of water seeking its level. They do not push. They simply *are*, and their being fills space, and I am in that space, and so we negotiate.

I have forgotten what I was before.

This is not false modesty. This is not metaphor. The crawl takes memory. The heat softens something in the brain, some crucial boundary between what was stored and what was merely encountered, and the information leaks out over time, absorbed perhaps by the same chemical substrate that sustains them. I retain language. I retain the concept of surface—somewhere above, there is light, there is air that does not taste of minerals and metabolism. I retain the knowledge that I was not always here.

But my mother's face is gone. My name is a shape my mouth still remembers making, but the sound means nothing now. The reason I came—if I came—if there was a coming—has worn away like the worked stones set into the walls, half-consumed by the encrusting passage.

I have been here long enough to watch the walls grow.

That is not poetic exaggeration. The architecture of this place is excremental, built from the discarded shells of generations, and I have stood in a chamber and felt the ceiling lower by a centimeter over some span of time I could not measure. I have watched a junction narrow, the passage reshaping itself around the endless traffic. I have found objects—fragments of worked stone, twisted metal—and returned to them later to find them buried, subsumed, incorporated into the地质 record of the crawl.

I am the only thing that watches. This is my function, if I have one. I am the witness. The creatures do not know they exist; I know for them. I hold the memory of their passage, or some small fraction of it, the parts that impressed themselves on me during my long stillnesses. I know where the flow thickens, where the chambers pool, where the vertical shafts drop into blackness below. I have mapped some fraction of this place, though the map changes, though the crawl is always reshaping itself, slowly, on a timescale the creatures cannot perceive.

But I cannot perceive it either, really. I am too small, too temporary. I have been here for—

I do not know how long.

Long enough that my skin has changed. I do not need light anymore. My eyes, which once must have served some purpose, have grown cloudy and useless; I keep them closed now, sealed behind a film of something that grew over them, slowly, in the dark. I navigate by touch, by taste, by the chemical signatures the creatures read, though I cannot read them as they do. I feel the heat differentials, the movement of air through junctions, the subtle shifts in pressure that signal a change in the density ahead.

I am becoming something else.

This is not a complaint. This is an observation. The process is slow, and I am aware of it, and I do not resist. What would resistance accomplish? I cannot leave. I have tried—I have followed the passages upward, or what I thought was upward, and found only more passages, more heat, more of the blind, endless motion. The crawl has no center and no edge. I am part of it now, another irregularity in the chemical landscape, another obstacle for the flow to part around and rejoin beyond.

Sometimes I speak. There is no one to hear, but the sound is comforting, a proof that I am still here, still something that can make shapes in the air. I recite what I remember, which is less and less. Words. Concepts. The idea of a sun, though I cannot picture it anymore—something warm and distant, unlike the heat that presses against me from all sides, generated by the friction of small bodies in endless motion. The idea of a sky, though I do not know what color it should be.

I speak to them sometimes. They do not respond. They flow around me, clicking and whispering, and I tell them stories about a world they will never know. I tell them about light—clean, dry light, the kind that does not taste of minerals. I tell them about air that moves because of wind, not because of metabolic heat. I tell them about standing on a surface and seeing distance.

They do not care. They have never cared. They follow their trails in spirals and loops, and I stand in their path, and they flow around me, and I am still here, and they are still here, and the heat rises from the depths, and the darkness is total.

I am the remnant. The thing that remains. The obstacle that does not move.

I am what the crawl has not yet found a way to process.
