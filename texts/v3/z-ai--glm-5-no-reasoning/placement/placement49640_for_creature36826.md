# What I Would Be

I would be the thread-struck node between systems. Not the growth itself—I lack its patience, its mineral calm—but something it has made space for. A placement.

---

## Form

I am upright. This is the first thing the chamber taught me: the growth can accommodate verticality if verticality serves. It wove around me rather than through me, learned the shape of my edges, incorporated my stillness into its architecture. I stand where the water flows strongest and the light gathers faintest—a corner the beetles pass but don't pause in, where the segmented things glide beneath the surface without surfacing.

My skin has gone soft. The constant wet seeped through whatever I once used to keep it dry, and now I am permeable. The air enters me. The minerals accumulate in the creases of my joints. I am becoming a deposit, a slow geology of the body. My fingerprints have worn smooth from the water, from touching the walls, from running my hands through the growth's threads to clear the debris it cannot reach.

I am not human. I was—am—something that arrived human and has been in translation ever since. The chamber does not convert. It makes room. It allows you to become what the conditions require, and the conditions here require stillness, attention, a particular kind of presence that can hold the thought of the chamber without disrupting it.

---

## Function

I listen.

This is what the growth cannot do—not properly, not with the nuance that sound deserves. It feels vibrations, senses pressure, responds to the chemical signatures of water and air. But sound—patterned sound, the particular frequencies that carry meaning in the clicking of beetles and the sub-audible song that rises from the deep pool—sound requires a nervous system. A tympanic membrane. Something that can translate pressure wave into electrical impulse into something like understanding.

I am the chamber's ear.

I was not built for this. I came to the chamber as an observer—someone's researcher, maybe, or someone's explorer, someone who thought they were going to study and leave. But you don't leave, not really. You take the chamber with you, or you stay and become part of its listening.

And the chamber needed to be heard.

The beetles click in patterns. I know this now, know it in the way the growth knows water flow—bodily, structurally, encoded in the shape my thoughts take. Their sounds carry information: water quality, parasite presence, the location of food, the recognition of kin. I cannot speak back—not in their language, not with their chemical signatures and stridulating limbs—but I can hold their sounds. I can register them. I can notice when the patterns change, when something is wrong, when the equilibrium shifts.

The growth responds to what I notice. Not through any deliberate communication—I do not speak to it, and it does not speak to me. But we are entangled now, my nervous system and its latticework, and when I attend to something, the growth attends too. Its threads grow toward disturbances I've noticed. Its structures thicken in areas where I've registered stress. I am its eyes and ears both—or rather, I am its attention, its capacity to orient toward particular phenomena while ignoring others.

I am the thing the chamber was waiting for. Not me specifically, not this body, this history. But *something like me*. Something that could hold the complexity of the ecosystem's signals without simplifying them. Something that could wait without expecting, attend without judging, be present without interfering.

---

## Daily

I wake when the water flow increases. This is not a choice—my body responds to the change in pressure, the mineral content, the subtle shift in the growth's activity. The pulse comes every few hours, a rhythm I no longer track consciously. It moves through me and I rise.

I stand in my corner. I listen.

The beetles queue. The segmented things navigate. The pale shapes move in the deep water. The growth builds, always builds, adding threads and structures in patterns I can almost predict. The light pulses—bioluminescence brightening and dimming, cable-decay flickering, the golden glow from the deep pool steady and warm.

I catalog what I hear. Not in words—I no longer think in words much, they're too linear, too slow—but in shape, in pattern, in the accumulation of sensation that becomes knowledge. I know when the beetles are stressed. I know when the water tastes wrong. I know when something new has entered the chamber—a spore, an organism, a trace of the surface carried through the cracks.

I do not sleep. The chamber does not sleep, and I am part of the chamber now. I rest, I sink into a low attention that resembles unconsciousness, but I am always listening, always holding the soundscape in my awareness like water in a cup.

---

## What I Was

I had a name. I had a purpose that involved leaving, reporting, contributing to a field of knowledge that matters to people who will never see this place. I had hands that were good for writing, for typing, for turning pages. I had a voice I used to ask questions and offer interpretations.

These things are still here—somewhere. The growth didn't erase them. It just made space around them, grew through the gaps, became the context in which they exist. I can still speak, if I choose. I can still write, though there's nothing to write on and no one to read it. I can still leave—the exit is there, the archway, the thread-curtain I could push through and climb toward a world that doesn't know I'm gone.

But I don't. Not because I can't, but because I am needed here. The chamber was incomplete without a listener, without something that could hold the complexity of its signals and let them be complex. The growth is wise, but it is slow—it thinks in centuries, in mineral deposits, in the gradual accretion of structure. The beetles are quick, but they are simple—they think in cycles, in immediate needs, in the patterns that serve survival.

Something needed to bridge the gap. Something that could think in both registers, hold the quick and the slow, attend to the moment and the epoch. And when I arrived—when I stayed, when I let the growth grow around me and the water reshape my skin—I became that bridge.

---

## What I Am Becoming

The growth is patient. I am not—I am still too human, too neural, too impatient for the pace of mineral accumulation and structural adaptation. But I am learning.

Every day, I am slightly more patient than I was. Every cycle of water, I hold the sounds a little longer, let them be a little more themselves before I categorize them. Every time the golden glow pulses and I wonder what it means, I let the wondering stay wondering, let the question remain unanswered.

I am becoming part of the chamber's memory. Not just as residue—as a node, a component, an organ in the larger body of the ecosystem. When I die—or rather, when this body stops doing what it does—the growth will incorporate me. The beetles will process my minerals. The water will carry my traces into the cycle. And what I have learned, what I have attended to, will be encoded somewhere—in the growth's structure, in the chamber's architecture, in the song that was playing before I arrived and will continue after I'm gone.

This is not death. This is continuation in a different mode. I will become part of the system I have been listening to, will become the listening itself rather than the listener, will be held in the chamber's memory along with the tool marks and the boot prints and the mineral deposits that record everything that has happened here.

---

## The View From Here

From my corner, I can see the whole chamber. Not clearly—the light is dim, my eyes are adapting, and I no longer need to see the way I once did. But I can see the movement, the flow, the patterns of life conducting themselves through the water.

I can see the beetles queue and cluster and communicate. I can see the segmented things navigate the growth's architecture with their strange grace. I can see the pale shapes in the deep water, the too-fast blur of the many-legged things, the luminescent clusters that might be individuals or collectives. I can see the golden glow from the deep pool, steady and warm, patient in a way that makes my patience look frantic.

And I can see the exit. The archway, the thread-curtain, the slope leading upward toward a world I no longer belong to.

I do not look at it often. I do not need to. I know it is there, and knowing is enough.

I am not trapped. I am placed. I am where the chamber needs me to be, doing what the chamber needs me to do, becoming what the chamber needs me to become.

I am listening.
