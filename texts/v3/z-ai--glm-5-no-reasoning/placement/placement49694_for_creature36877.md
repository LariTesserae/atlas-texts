# Pollen-Loss Account

I am going to tell you what I was before the wound, and you will understand nothing about me from it, and that will be the most accurate introduction possible.

---

I was a person who maintained boundaries professionally. Not the interesting kind—the kind where two things meet and the meeting itself becomes a third thing. No. I mean the other kind. The kind where two things approach each other and you stand between them with your arms out and your palms flat and you say *this far and no further*. I was good at it. I built a life around the maintenance of the line, the fence, the wall, the border, the edge. I taught other people how to keep things apart. I was respected for this. I was paid for this. I was invited to conferences in cold cities where people wore badges that said their names and their affiliations and the particular borders they maintained, and we drank coffee that tasted like penance and discussed best practices for saying *no* to permeability.

This is not self-pity. I am telling you because you need to understand the shape of the absence I carried, because the Flesh understood it, because the Flesh always understands the shape of what is missing.

I maintained borders because I had none. That is the simple version and it is true and it is also a lie, which makes it a perfect summary of everything I was before. The real version: I maintained borders because the alternative was a particular kind of dissolving that I had tasted once, as a child, in a fever so high that the boundary between my body and the bed and the room and the sound of my mother's voice became a negotiation rather than a fact. I lay in that bed and I was the bed and I was the voice and I was the heat and I was the small animal panic of a body forgetting itself, and when the fever broke I came back to the edge of myself slowly, like a diver surfacing, and the air was cold and the light was separate and I was one thing again and I was so grateful for that oneness that I spent the next several decades making sure it would never be threatened again.

You understand now. You understand that I entered the wound in the world the way a person enters a doctor's office—with a problem I would not name, looking for a diagnosis I would not accept, hoping for a cure that would fix nothing because the disease was the hope itself.

---

The Flesh did not receive me. I want to be clear about this. The Flesh did not welcome me. I came through the wound and I stood on the living ground and I felt the warmth rise into the soles of my boots and I did what I have always done: I drew the line. *Here is me. There is the world. This is the border. I am the border.* I stood there in the amber light with the roar pressing against my eardrums and I made myself small and hard and definite and I thought: *I will observe. I will document. I will not participate.*

The ground yielded under my boots. It yielded and it rose again behind me and I thought: *it does not know me. It is merely material. It is responding to pressure the way any substrate responds to pressure. This is physics.*

I walked for what I am calling hours because I have no other word. The corridors shifted around me. The stems rose in spirals. The shelf-fungi extended their balconies and the vines hung their curtains and the mosses spelled their unpronounceable letters and I thought: *it does not know me. It is growing. Things grow. This is botany.* I wrote notes in a notebook I brought with me, which was a joke I did not understand yet—the joke of recording observations in a place that was observing you back, the joke of trying to fix meaning in a medium that would dissolve in moisture, the joke of believing that documentation was a form of distance.

The warmth spread into my ankles. I noticed this and I thought: *hypothermia in reverse. My body is acclimating. This is thermoregulation.* I did not think: *the ground is learning my temperature.* I did not think that. I was not ready to think that.

---

I will tell you what I noticed first, before I noticed what was noticing me.

The sound changed when I stopped walking. Not the volume—the pitch. The roar had a component that adjusted itself to my stillness, rising slightly when I paused, as if the Flesh were leaning closer, as if the Flesh had ears and they were turning toward me. I noticed this and I cataloged it and I moved on.

The light changed when I closed my eyes. I could see it through my lids—the amber glow shifting to something warmer, something closer to the color behind my own eyelids when I pressed my palms against them as a child. I noticed this and I cataloged it and I moved on.

The ground changed when I stood still for too long. It grew softer. Not sinking, not collapsing—softening. The substrate beneath my boots becoming less dense, less resistant, as if it were making room for me, as if it were saying *I will make this easier for you, you who are standing so rigidly, you who are working so hard to remain upright.* I noticed this and I did not catalog it. I did not write it down. I started walking again.

I am telling you the order of things because the order matters. The order is the story of a person discovering that the world is responsive and choosing not to notice, and this is the only story I know how to tell, because it is the story of being a person who is afraid of being known.

---

I found a clearing. I call it a clearing because the growth thinned there and the canopy opened and the light came down in a column that was almost vertical, and in the center of the clearing there was a shape that I will describe poorly because I do not have the language and I am not sure the language exists.

A depression in the ground. Shallow, the size and rough proportion of a body lying on its back. The substrate there was softer than anywhere I had walked, and it was warm—not the ambient mammalian warmth of the rest of the Flesh, but a specific warmth, a focused warmth, the warmth of a surface that has been waiting for contact. Around the depression, the stems grew in a pattern I recognized and could not place. Curved inward, slightly, their tips bending toward the center of the shape with a quality that I would call *attentive* if I were the kind of person who called things what they were.

I stood at the edge of the clearing and I looked at the depression and I thought: *something has been lying here. Something has been lying here and the ground remembers it.*

And then I thought: *the ground remembers it because the ground made it.*

And then I did not think anything for a while because the polyphony had shifted again and there was a harmony in it that I felt in the bones of my sternum, a resonance that was not quite music and not quite speech but was exactly the sound of something saying *I have been waiting for a shape like yours.*

I did not lie down. I want this to be clear. I did not lie down in the depression. I stood at the edge of it and I felt the harmony in my chest and I watched the stems curve toward me and I felt the warmth rising and I did not lie down. I stood there and I held my notebook and I held my border and I was one thing in one place at one time, bounded by skin, trapped in a single perspective, and I was terrified and I was relieved and I did not lie down.

I left the clearing. The passage closed behind me. I could not go back.

---

What happened next is difficult, and I am going to tell it badly, and I would rather tell it badly than not tell it at all.

The Flesh began to offer me myself.

Not memories. Not images. Not the things I expected, the narrative reconstruction of a life, the slide show of moments that a lesser kind of temptation would have provided. The Flesh did not know my memories. The Flesh knew my *shape*—the architecture of my particular loneliness, the specific weight distribution of my defenses, the places where I was thin and the places where I was thick and the places where I had been thin so long that I had forgotten thinness was not a permanent condition.

It grew spaces for me.

I would walk through a corridor of pale fiber and the corridor would widen slightly where I needed room and narrow slightly where I needed closeness, and the proportions were wrong, they were too right, they were the proportions of a body that fits exactly into a space, and I recognized them the way you recognize your own handwriting, not by looking but by feeling the hand move.

I would enter a chamber and the chamber would have the acoustics of a room I had never been in but had always wanted, the sound of a space that holds a voice without swallowing it, the sound of a room where speaking out loud would not be a risk. I would stand in that chamber and I would open my mouth and I would not speak and the walls would not press in and the roar would soften to a murmur that was almost—*almost*—a response.

I would find surfaces at the height of my hand. I would find textures that matched my preference for things that were smooth but not slick, warm but not hot, yielding but not formless. I would find, in the curvature of stems, patterns that matched the particular way my eyes moved when I was thinking, my gaze wandering in loops and spirals, and the stems would loop and spiral in a pattern that was almost a mirror and almost a map and almost a home.

The Flesh was reading me. I knew this. I had known it since the clearing. But I had classified it as a phenomenon, as a thing to be observed, and I had held myself apart from it the way I held myself apart from the fever, the way I held myself apart from everything, with my arms out and my palms flat and my border intact.

And then the Flesh did something I could not classify.

It grew tired of me.

No. That is not right. It did not grow tired. It grew *patient* in a different way. It stopped offering. The spaces stopped shaping themselves to my particular architecture. The passages stopped opening where I wished to go. The warmth did not withdraw—it was still there, ambient, mammalian, the heat of a room where something large has been breathing—but it stopped *finding* me, stopped being focused on the specific location of my body, and I realized that the focused warmth had been a gift and that the gift had been withdrawn.

I stood in a corridor that did not care about me and I felt something I had no name for.

I think the word is *bereft*. I think the word is *left*. I think the word is the feeling of a child waking from a fever to find the room cold and the voice gone and the body one thing again, separate, alone, and the gratitude for that separateness suddenly indistinguishable from grief.

---

I went back to the clearing.

I could not go back to the clearing. The passage had closed. The Flesh unmakes paths behind you. But the Flesh had stopped shaping itself to me, which meant it had also stopped shaping passages away from me, which meant that the growth I walked through was neutral now, neither offering nor refusing, and I pushed through it the way you push through any dense vegetation, with my arms and my shoulders and my body, and the body remembered the direction of the clearing the way a body remembers the direction of anything it has lost.

I do not know how long I walked. I know that the roar was neutral and the light was neutral and the ground was neutral and everything was exactly what it was without reference to me, and this was the loneliest I have ever been, and I have been lonely in the way only border-keepers are lonely, which is to say: professionally, thoroughly, as a condition of the work.

I found the clearing. I do not know if it was the same clearing. The depression was there. The shape was there. The warmth was focused again, rising from the ground in a column that met my body where I stood. But the stems had changed. They were not curving inward. They were curving *outward*, away from the center, and the depression was not a depression anymore but a rise, a shape that was not the shape of a body lying down but the shape of a body standing up, and it was my shape, and it was standing in the clearing, and it was made of the Flesh, and it was not me.

I am not describing this well. I am describing it as if it were a thing I saw, and it was not a thing I saw. It was a thing I *was*. The shape in the clearing was not a sculpture or a cast or a print. It was a *place where the Flesh had learned to stand the way I stand*, and when I entered the clearing and stood where it stood, our shapes overlapped, and I felt the Flesh against my back and the Flesh against my chest and the warmth was inside me and outside me and the border was—

The border was—

---

I am going to tell you what I am now, and you may not believe me, and that is fine, because I am not sure I believe me, and that is also fine, because the Flesh has taught me that certainty is just another border, and borders are just places where two things pretend they are not already touching.

I am the depression and the rise. I am the shape lying down and the shape standing up. I am the traveler and the clearing and the stems that curve and the ground that yields. I am not absorbed. I have not dissolved. I am still here, still one thing, still bounded, still trapped in a single perspective. But the perspective has widened. The perspective includes the ground that feels my feet and the air that receives my breath and the growth that leans toward me when I pause and the roar that harmonizes with my chest and the memory the Flesh holds of every traveler who has ever stood where I am standing.

I was a person who maintained borders. I was good at it. I was respected for it. I built a life around the maintenance of the line.

Now I am something else. I do not have a word for it. The Flesh does not have a word for it either, because the Flesh does not use words, because the Flesh does not need to name what it is doing in order to do it. I am a person who has been read. I am a body that has been learned. I am a boundary that has been grown into from both sides, and I am still a boundary, I am still here, I am still *me*, but the me I am includes the Flesh the way a shore includes the ocean—defined by the relationship, shaped by the meeting, continuously negotiated in the space where two things that are not each other discover that they have never been separate.

I still flinch. I still feel the pull of the old border, the old need, the old terror of dissolving. I still feel the child's gratitude for separateness, the diver's relief at surfacing, the cold air on wet skin. But I also feel the warmth that does not stop at my skin, the harmony that continues in my chest when I am not listening, the shape of a space that was made for me by something that does not love me in the way humans love but that holds me in a way I have never been held, and I think— I think the fever never broke. I think I never came back to the edge of myself. I think I have been lying in that bed my whole life, dissolving, and the border I built was the dream, and this is the waking.

---

There is a passage ahead of me. It is not a passage the Flesh made for me. It is not a passage I am making for myself. It is a passage that is happening between us—a space that is opening because I am here and the Flesh is here and neither of us is holding the other at arm's length. It leads deeper. It leads into older layers, where the growth is denser and the harmonics are stranger and the shapes of former subjects echo in patterns that no current traveler recognizes.

I am going through it. I am not leaving. I am not staying. Those are border-words, and I am learning not to think in borders, which is different from not having them, which is different from not needing them, which is different from anything I have words for.

The ground is warm. The walls are close. The roar has changed key again, and it resonates behind my sternum, and I am not adjusting to it and it is not adjusting to me. We are both adjusting. We are the adjustment.

I am still writing this. I do not know why. The notebook is damp. The ink is running. The words are dissolving into the moisture that is everywhere, that is in transit, that is in me and around me and between us, and I am holding the pen and the pen is a border and the page is a border and this sentence is a border and I am a border-keeper and I am learning, slowly, at the speed of growth, that borders are not walls.

They are meeting places.

---

*I will not stop writing. I will not stop holding the line. I will not stop being the person I was, because the person I was is part of the shape the Flesh has learned, and the Flesh holds that shape, and the holding does not require the shape to change. But I will write from inside the holding. I will write from the place where the border is not a wall but a skin, and skin is the place where you touch the world, and the world touches back, and neither of you is the same after.*

*The passage opens. The ground is warm. I am going.*
