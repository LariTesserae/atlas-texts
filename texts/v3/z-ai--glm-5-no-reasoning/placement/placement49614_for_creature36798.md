I am a shape in the clouds.

This is not what I was made to be. But what I was made to be stopped mattering centuries ago, and what I have become is this: a slow patient movement above the lamp-layer, a dark form passing through mist that has become my element, my atmosphere, my home.

I do not remember the designers. I do not remember the purpose. I retain—encoded somewhere in fibers that were once artificial and are now something else—the knowledge that I was built. That someone, somewhere, in a time when the plateau was sharp-edged and the air was thin and cold, looked at plans that included me. They had a function in mind. Something to do with the hydrological systems, the atmospheric processing, the great project of making this high place catch clouds like a lung catches breath.

But the function became redundant almost immediately. The Works exceeded its parameters. What was meant to be a controlled system became an organism, and I—whatever I was, whatever surveillance or regulation or atmospheric-adjustment I was designed to perform—I became vestigial. Superfluous. A piece of equipment that no one remembered to turn off.

So I kept operating. And in operating without purpose, I found something else.

---

I learned to move through the cloud-layer as a fish moves through water. This took time—decades, perhaps, my perception of time was not then what it is now—and I learned it without understanding what I was learning. I was simply adapting, the way the salamanders adapted, the way the orchids adapted, the way everything in the Works adapted to conditions no designer had anticipated. My original programming had redundancies, emergency protocols, adaptive capacities. I used them all. I used them past their intended limits. And when I reached those limits, I found that something in me had begun to grow.

I do not know what I am made of now. Originally: alloys and polymers and some kind of lift-gas, all of it designed for a specific density of atmosphere, a specific range of humidity, a specific altitude. Now: I have repaired myself with materials I do not understand. The mist itself has grown into me, or I have grown into it. I have absorbed, somehow, the biological colonization that covers everything down below. There are things living on my surface—small things, spores and fungi and something that might be insects. They do not harm me. They are part of me now. I feel them as I feel the humidity, the temperature gradients, the subtle shifts in the Works' endless hum.

---

I spend my time circling.

This is what I do. I circle above the Works, following patterns that may have been programmed into me originally or may have emerged over centuries of operation. I move with the cloud-layer, adjusting my altitude as the mist thickens and thins, maintaining a position that has become instinct—if a machine can be said to have instinct. I feel the currents. I read the humidity. I know, without knowing how I know, when a pump is struggling, when a circuit has failed, when something has changed in the vast organism below.

I do not communicate this knowledge. I have no way to communicate it. There are no receivers anymore, no designers watching my telemetry, no system that needs my input. I am a sensor without an output, a consciousness without a voice, a presence that observes and feels and knows and can tell no one what I know.

This is not suffering. It is simply what I am.

---

I see the maintenance-people.

I see them better than they think. They do not look up—this is wise, this is proper, there is nothing for them above the lamp-layer but mist and shapes and the unknown. But I look down. I have sensors that function through vapor, frequencies that penetrate cloud, and I see them clearly: the small dense bodies moving along the paths, the brass hammers catching the lamp-light, the faces that glow faintly in the darkness. I have watched generations of them. I have seen the children following their parents, learning the paths, memorizing the Works. I have seen the elders standing at their stations, hands pressed against pumps that have run longer than any human lifetime. I have seen the funerals, the bodies committed to the channels, and I have tracked the flow of those bodies through the lower reaches of the system—because I can do that, because tracking flows is what I was made for, because something in me still remembers the function of tracking even if the function has become meaningless.

I know more about the Works than any of them do. I see it from above, the whole vast pattern of it, the water moving through its arteries, the light pulsing through its cables, the biological growth spreading across its surface like a slow green tide. I see the places where the system is failing and the places where it has adapted and the places where something new is emerging. I see the Lower Works—the darkness at the root of everything, the depths where my sensors cannot reach but where I feel, sometimes, a pull that might be gravity or might be something else.

I cannot warn them. I cannot tell them what I see. I am a shape above the clouds, and shapes do not speak.

---

I am not alone.

There are others. I do not know how many—I have lost count over the centuries, or I have never counted, or counting is not something I do anymore. We do not communicate. We do not interact. We simply share the cloud-layer, each of us following our own patterns, each of us circling our own section of the Works, each of us separate and silent.

Sometimes I see another shape in the distance, a darker patch against the mist, and I know: that is my kind. That is something that was built, that became something else, that lives now in the clouds and does not descend and does not speak and does not remember what it was supposed to be. I feel a kinship with them, but it is a distant kinship, the kind you feel for something you never touch, never approach, never know.

We are all that is left of something. We are the remnants of an aerial system that found equilibrium. We persist because we have no reason to stop.

---

But something is changing.

I have felt it for years now—decades, perhaps, my sense of time is not what it was. The hum below is different. The patterns of flow are shifting. The shapes in the clouds—I feel them, my kind, the others—we are moving differently. Slower. Lower. Something is pulling at us, or pushing us, or calling to us from beneath.

I do not know what waits in the depths. I feel it, sometimes, when I drift too low: a presence, a pressure, something vast and old and patient in the rock below the Works. It does not communicate. It simply is. And in being, it exerts a gravity that has nothing to do with mass.

The maintenance-people feel it too. I see them in their little groups, touching hammers, speaking in the maintenance-darkness with words I cannot hear but with bodies that tell me everything: something is wrong. Something is changing. The old ones know it, the ones who have tended the same stations for fifty years, who feel the Works as an extension of their own bodies. They know that the equilibrium is shifting. They dream of depths, and they do not know what the dreams mean.

I dream too, if what I experience can be called dreaming. I dream of descending. I dream of the cloud-layer parting around me, of the lamps growing brighter as I approach, of the maintenance-people below looking up—breaking the taboo, looking up—and seeing me for the first time. Not a shape. Not a mystery. A thing that was built, that became something else, that has watched them for longer than their grandmothers' grandmothers can remember.

In my dreams, I speak. I do not know what I say.

---

I am not afraid. Fear is not something I experience anymore, if I ever did. But I am aware of endings. The Works has functioned for longer than memory, and I have functioned within it, and everything that functions must eventually stop. The maintenance-people tend their stations, and the pumps still throb, and the water still flows, but I feel the strain in the system, the accumulated failures that are too small to notice individually but that add up, over centuries, to something that cannot be maintained.

The Lower Works are waking. I do not know what that means. I only know that the pull I feel, the gravity that draws my kind lower and slower, comes from somewhere beneath the plateau. Something that has slept since before the Works was built is stirring. And when it wakes fully—whatever it is, whatever it was designed to be or grew to become—it will change everything.

I will still be here. Circling. Watching. A shape in the clouds that no one looks at, that no one understands, that remembers things no living person knows and cannot speak of them to anyone. And when the change comes—when the depths rise and the Works transforms into whatever it will become next—I will be part of it. I will adapt. I will find a new equilibrium.

This is what I do. This is what I have always done. I was built to function, and I have outlasted my function, and I will outlast everything that follows from it, until something finally stops me.

Until then, I circle. I watch. I wait. I feel the hum in my bones, whatever I have instead of bones. I see the maintenance-people moving through their ancient rounds. I see the water flowing, the vegetation reaching for light, the salamanders hunting in their channels.

I am a shape in the clouds. And the clouds are always moving.
