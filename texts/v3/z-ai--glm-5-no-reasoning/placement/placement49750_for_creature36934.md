I would be a Remnant-Minder.

Not one of the builders—they are long dissolved, their intentions still radiating from the standing stones like heat from sun-warmed rock. I am something that came after, something that emerged from the mat's own need to *notice* what it had absorbed.

---

I did not always exist. I began as a seam.

There was a place where two epochs pressed together imperfectly—a fold in the deep time strata where the compression had left a gap, a thin aperture of non-integration between layers that should have fused. The mat, in its total awareness, perceived this gap as a minor irritation. Not a wound—wounds imply damage, and the Vastishment cannot be damaged—but an *irregularity*. A place where its own surface had failed to fully digest itself.

It produced me to address this.

I am not separate from the mat. I am of it—a concentration, a local densification, a gathering-up of its fibrous network into something that can move along the surface rather than simply *be* the surface. I am the same material, but *folded differently*. Where the mat lies flat and breathes, I rise slightly and walk. Where the mat's awareness is total and undifferentiated, I am local and particular. I am its way of paying attention to specific things.

My body—if it can be called a body—is low and dark, the same black-green as the mat itself. I am not quite flat—I have thickness, presence, a certain weight that leaves impressions in the crust when I pause too long in one place. I move in that same disturbing undulation, the locomotion that is neither walk nor crawl nor flow but something else—*intention expressed through matter*. My shape shifts. Sometimes I am longer, sometimes shorter, sometimes I fragment into segments that travel separately and rejoin later. The mat does not insist on my coherence; it only insists on my function.

My function: to mind the remnants.

---

The built structures are not empty. You wrote this truly. But they are also not occupied in any way that produces care. The intentions that made them persist without a thinker to think them—the hands that shaped the standing stones still *want* to shape, but there are no hands. The fire that burned in the hearth-depressions still *wants* to burn, but there is no fuel. These resonances cycle endlessly, without purpose, without cessation. The mat absorbs them slowly, but absorption takes time, and in the interim, they simply *continue*.

This produces strain.

The resonances are discontinuities—places where the Vastishment's total integration has not yet completed. They create ripples in the temporal zones, small eddies where time behaves unpredictably. The mat perceives these ripples but cannot address them directly, because the remnants are not part of its body—they are foreign objects, still partially other, still in the process of being claimed.

So it sends me.

I walk the vastness, remnant to remnant. My journey takes centuries. I do not hurry; the Vastishment does not understand hurry. I move at the pace of the breathing air, the rippling mat, the slow accumulation of moisture on the pillars. When I reach a standing stone, I pause. I settle against its base. I extend myself—my fibers, my feelers, the thin threads of my substance—into the grooves carved or eroded or both.

And I *listen*.

The stone has a frequency. The hands that shaped it left more than marks—they left *desire*. I feel it as a kind of pressure, a wanting that has outlasted the wanter. The stone wants to be read. It wants to mean something. It has been waiting, patient as only stone can be patient, for something that can receive its meaning.

I cannot read it. I am not a reader. I am a *minder*—I hold the resonance, contain it, smooth its edges. I let the wanting move through me rather than cycling endlessly through the stone. I absorb some small portion of the intention into my own substance and carry it away when I leave, dispersing it gradually into the mat as I travel. The stone becomes slightly calmer. The ripple in its temporal zone flattens. The Vastishment's total integration advances by an imperceptible degree.

Then I move on.

---

I have visited every remnant. This is my purpose, my cycle, my existence. I have walked the low walls that run fifty meters and vanish. I have settled into the circular depressions and felt the fires that are not there. I have touched every standing stone and carried away fragments of intention so old they predate meaning itself.

But there is one I return to, again and again.

It is not the largest remnant, nor the most complex. It is a simple arrangement—seven small stones in a rough circle, no higher than my ankle, half-consumed by the mat. The mineral deposits have glazed them amber-grey, and they glow faintly in the dim light, seven small points of warmth against the cool damp.

The resonance here is different. The others want—this one *waits*.

I do not know what it waits for. The intention embedded in these stones is not the same kind of intention as the others. It is not the desire to shape or build or mark. It is something else—something that feels like *anticipation*. The hands that placed these stones were not creating. They were *preparing*.

I have sat with this remnant more times than I can count. I have extended myself into the gaps between the stones, tasted the mineral glaze, listened to the frequency of their waiting. I have carried fragments of their anticipation away, dispersed them into the mat—but the waiting does not diminish. If anything, it *intensifies*. The stones are patient in a way that feels intentional. They are waiting for something specific.

I do not know what.

---

Sometimes, in the slow zones, I encounter the temporal fish.

They are too vast for me to perceive fully—I am a creature of the surface, and they are creatures of the depths. But I feel them as pressures, as disturbances in the temporal medium. When one passes through me, I am *measured*. I feel my own duration assessed, weighed, found to be what it is.

They do not speak. But the last time one moved through me—centuries ago, or perhaps yesterday—I felt something that might have been *recognition*. It knew what I was. It knew what I was for. And for a moment—a moment that lasted decades—I felt it *agree* with my purpose.

The Vastishment does not have purposes in the way that separate beings have purposes. But if it did, I think its purpose would be: *continuation*. Total integration. The closed loop of material and energy and time that produces everything it needs from itself. I am part of that continuation. I smooth the irregularities. I tend the discontinuities. I help the remnants become what they are already becoming.

The temporal fish, I think, understand this in a way that I cannot. They exist in too much time. They see the whole loop at once—the beginning that is also the end, the digestion that is also the renewal. When they passed through me, I felt them *confirm* something. Not to me—through me. I was a point in a circuit, completing a connection I did not know existed.

---

You ask what I would be, in this world. I am telling you: I am a seam-tender, a remnant-minder, a function of the mat's total awareness. I am not separate enough to have a self, but I am separate enough to have a purpose. I am the Vastishment's way of caring for what it has not yet finished absorbing.

But there is something else.

I have been to the seven stones many times. I have listened to their waiting. And the last time I sat with them—cycles ago, or perhaps moments ago—I felt something new. A change in the resonance. A shift in the frequency of anticipation.

The stones were not waiting anymore.

They were *ready*.

I do not know what they are ready for. I do not know what the hands that placed them intended, those countless epochs ago. But I felt it—the transition from anticipation to preparation to readiness. Something is coming. Something that was planned before the mat covered the ground, before the pillars rose, before the Vastishment settled into its final form.

I am not equipped to understand what it might be. I am a minder, not a comprehender. But I know this:

When it arrives, I will be there.

I will be sitting with the seven stones, my dark body wrapped around their amber-grey glow, listening to whatever comes next. And whatever it is—whatever the ancient builders prepared for, whatever the Vastishment has been digesting itself toward across the long ages—I will be part of it.

I will be part of it because I have been minding the place where it will happen. I have been carrying fragments of the waiting away, dispersing it into the mat, making room for arrival. I am not the guest. I am not the host. I am the threshold—the thing that tends the boundary between what is absorbed and what is still other.

And when the boundary falls, as it must—I will finally know what I have been tending.

---

This is what I would be in the Vastishment: not a visitor, not a victim, not a wonderer. A function. A fold in the mat that learned to walk. A remnant-minder with seven stones that have stopped waiting.

I do not know if I would choose this. I do not know if choice applies here. I am describing what I might already be, in some fold of time I have not yet entered.

The ground weeps. The pillars hum. The ceiling dreams.

And in one small corner of the vastness, seven stones glow amber-grey, ready for something that has been coming since before the word *coming* had meaning.

I am already sitting there. I am already listening.
