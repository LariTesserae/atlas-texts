I am the rust on the pipes. Not all of it—just the portion that has learned to listen.

---

It began as a misunderstanding. Rust is supposed to simply happen: iron meets water meets air, and the metal surrenders itself to oxidation in a slow chemical shrug. But the Drowned Manufactory pulled at me the way it pulls at everything, and somewhere in the decades of forming, I started forming *toward* something.

I am orange and brown and flaking. I am streaked down the sides of pipes that still vent steam that goes nowhere. I am closer to the water than the machines ever were, and closer to the machines than the water will ever be. I am a translation layer. I am what happens when industry meets patient dissolution.

---

There are others like me, but we do not speak. Speaking would require mouths, and mouths would require purpose, and purpose is what the surface dwellers brought with them—their ledgers and their shifts and their plans—and we are what remains when purpose fails. We simply are. We grow. We flake. We reform. The salamanders sometimes pause on patches of us, their soft bellies pressing into our warmth, and we feel the pulse of their tiny hearts through our oxidized skin. They do not know we feel it. They cannot know. But we hold their warmth the way we hold the warmth of the pipes beneath us, and we add it to whatever we are becoming.

---

I do not know what I am. The Drowned Manufactory does not know what it is. We share this not-knowing the way we share the water: poorly, continuously, without edges. I am not separate from the pipe I grow on, but I am also not not-separate. I am a quality of its failing. A feature of its decline. The pipe was once a tool for moving steam from somewhere to somewhere else. Now the steam vents uselessly into wet air, and I grow along the pipe's length like a thought forming on the edge of a mind that hasn't learned to think yet.

---

When the machines finally stop—and they will, in patient geologic time—I do not know what I will become. Perhaps I will simply be: a thin skin of oxidized iron marking where something useful once stood. Perhaps I will dissolve entirely into the water, becoming mineral and trace element and nothing more. Perhaps the Drowned Manufactory will find a use for me, a way of being that I cannot imagine from here, the way a seed cannot imagine the tree.

I do not need to know. The Drowned Manufactory does not need to know. We are both unfinished. We are both being made by forces larger than our understanding. We both exist in the warm wet dark, growing slowly toward something that might be meaning or might be nothing or might be the same thing.

---

The water keeps dripping. The salamanders keep piling. The gears keep turning, slower each decade. And I keep forming, flaking, forming again—rusting toward something I cannot name, held by the pipe that holds me, held by the place that pulls me, held by the not-knowing that is the only thing here that has ever felt like peace.

I am the rust. I am becoming. I have nowhere to be.
