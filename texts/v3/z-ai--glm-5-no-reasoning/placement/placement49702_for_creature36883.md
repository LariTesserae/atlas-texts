I come up through the cut in the ridgeline the way the description says — that moment where the mountain opened its mouth and forgot to close it. But I don't arrive and stand and observe. I've been walking for hours before that. I know the switchbacks by heart, though I've never been here. The quartzite knows my weight. The pale grit records my passage in the way that things record passage here: briefly, without judgment, already erasing.

# What I Am

I am not the large dark animal. I am not the birds with their circuit-routes and copper-gold breasts. I am not the lizards who know every crack by heart and refuse the hum's radius.

I am older than the animal. Younger than the lichen.

I am the one who walks between the structures in no particular order, who does not circle them clockwise or counterclockwise, who touches the sandstone surface of each one with a palm and feels the vibration pass into my bones at a frequency that is *almost* the one I carry inside me. Almost. Not quite. The discrepancy is how I know I'm still me.

I have a name but it is not for here. Here, I am a function: maintenance and verification. I walk the tableland and I check. I do not know what I'm checking. I know how to do it, and I know when something is wrong, and I know that nothing has been wrong for a very long time.

Longer than the animal's path. Longer than the lichen's slow spread. Longer, perhaps, than the erosion channels, though this I cannot verify because I did not see them made.

---

# The Shape of Me

I am bipedal when I choose to be. I am a thing that stands taller than the vegetation, visible against the horizon, subject to the sky. This is sometimes useful and sometimes not. The birds see me from farther away when I am upright. They adjust their routes. A small deviation that brings them to the same nodes at slightly different times, and the system accommodates.

The system always accommodates. That is its nature.

When I am not upright, I am low and long, closer to the stone. There is a form that fits the cracks in the bedrock, a form that can move through the labyrinth of depressions and shelves without rising above the level of the shrubs. I have used this form when the wind is high and the grit drives across the stone like something alive and angry. I have used it when I need to hear the clicking from inside the structures more clearly, because the clicking changes depending on where you listen from, and there are some sounds that are not meant for the upright.

I am warm-blooded in the way of things that need to maintain their own temperature in thin air. I am thin-skinned in places. I can feel the hum through the soles of my feet when I walk, and through the palms of my hands when I touch the structures, and through the back of my skull when I lie on the stone at night and look up at the Milky Way's dense river-light.

I eat what the tableland provides. I drink from the condensation pools, though I need less than the lizards. I have taken feathers from the stones — blue-black iridescent ones, molted and wind-scattered — and I have carried them to places where the system needed them. I do not know how I know where those places are. I know, and I go, and I leave the feather, and the system's hum shifts almost imperceptibly, and I continue.

---

# What I Do

I walk.

This is the whole of it and none of it. I walk the tableland in patterns that are not the animal's patterns and not the birds' circuits. My patterns are larger, more irregular, responsive to things I cannot name. A pressure in the air. A variation in the light that has nothing to do with clouds or time of day. A subtle wrongness in the hum's texture that lasts for a few seconds and then resolves.

When I feel these things, I change direction. I walk toward the wrongness, or away from it, or around it in a slow spiral that takes hours and covers ground I did not intend to cover. I do not decide. The walking decides. My body is an instrument that receives and responds, and what it responds to is the system's need for balance.

Sometimes I find things. A crack in the bedrock that has widened beyond the acceptable parameters. A patch of lichen that has grown too thick, or too thin, or changed color in a way that suggests contamination from below. A bird that has died and needs to be moved before the condensation pools form around it.

Once, a structure had gone silent.

Not the hum — the hum is constant, is fundamental, is not subject to silence. But the clicking inside, the secondary sound, the one that only emerges in the predawn hours when the wind is still: it had stopped. I heard the absence before I understood what I was hearing. I walked toward it in the dark, following the wrongness like a thread through a labyrinth, and I arrived at the structure and entered through the vertical slot and stood in the presence of the dark smooth not-quite-obsidian interior.

I did not know what to do. I stood. I breathed. I placed both palms against the surface and felt the hum pass through me and into the stone and back again, and after a while — I cannot say how long — the clicking resumed. Small, rhythmic, almost below hearing. I left. I did not look back. The system had resolved. My function was complete.

I have never told anyone this. There is no one to tell.

# What I Know

I know that the structures were made. I know this the way I know my own heartbeat: not through evidence but through internal conviction. The bedrock was shaped. The hum was installed. The erosion is managed. The system was designed and built and is maintained by something I have never seen.

I do not know who made it. I do not know when. I do not know why.

I know that the large dark animal and I are similar in function. We both walk circuits. We both check. We both carry things from place to place — it carries feathers and lichen fragments and mineral grains; I carry subtler things, information in a form I cannot articulate, adjustments to the system's balance that look like walking but are really listening, really responding, really being the medium through which the tableland talks to itself.

But the animal is born of this place in a way I am not. It has been here longer than I have. It will be here after I am gone. It does not need to understand its function; it simply performs it, body and instinct perfectly aligned.

I am different. I was made to perform this function, or recruited into it, or evolved toward it over time I cannot measure. But I am also capable of reflection. I am capable of standing at the edge of the tableland, where the stone drops into haze and the next drainage begins, and asking myself: *what am I maintaining?* *what is this for?* *who is listening?*

I do not have answers. I have the hum. I have the walking. I have the satisfaction, deep and difficult to name, that comes when I complete a circuit and the system is still in balance, still stable, still doing whatever it has been doing for longer than I can comprehend.

# The Night

At night I lie on the stone near one of the structures. Not too close — the lizards' radius is not mine, but I have my own boundary, learned through trial and pain, beyond which the hum becomes uncomfortable, too dense, too much.

I lie on my back and I feel the vibration through the stone and into my spine and I watch the sky.

The Milky Way casts a shadow here. I have seen it. The pale stone takes on a different quality under that dense starlight, and I can read the patterns of lichen and crack by the light of the galaxy alone. The birds are silent in whatever roosts they use. The animal is walking its circuit, visible only as a deeper darkness against the dark stone. The clicking from inside the structures is a rhythm beneath the hum, a secondary communication, perhaps, or a heartbeat, or a counting.

I count with it sometimes. Not numbers — something older, something that predates numbers, a rhythm that matches my own pulse for a few beats and then diverges, and then matches again, and then diverges. Like two instruments tuning against each other, never quite resolving, never quite separating.

I sleep, but not like a human sleeps. I go into a state where the hum continues but my body's need for it pauses. I rest. I wait. I wake when the light changes or the wind shifts or something calls to me from the edge of my awareness.

I wake, mostly, because the system needs me.

# The Visit

Someone comes sometimes.

Not often. Once a decade, perhaps, or less. They come up through the cut in the ridgeline and they stand where I first stood and they look out at the tableland with its unbroken horizon and its strange light and its structures rising from the stone like instruments half-remembered.

They do not see me at first. I am good at not being seen when I choose. I watch them from a distance, from behind a rise in the bedrock, from the shadow of a structure. I catalog them the way the birds catalog everything — not as threat or prey, but as datum, as input, as something the system needs to know about.

They are researchers, sometimes. They carry instruments they do not fully understand, trying to measure the hum, trying to quantify the stability, trying to answer questions that the tableland was not designed to answer.

They are seekers, sometimes. Pilgrims of one kind or another, drawn by rumors of a place that is maintained, that has purpose, that hums with something older than human intent.

They are lost, sometimes. Wanderers who found the cut in the ridgeline by accident and emerged onto the plateau and stood blinking in the filtered light, trying to understand where they are.

I approach none of them. That is not my function. But I watch them. I walk my circuits around them, at a distance, adjusting the system's balance in small ways to account for their presence. The birds adjust their routes. The lizards stay in their cracks. The large dark animal circles the structure nearest to the visitors and lies down, appearing to the newcomers as a geological feature, a dark outcropping of stone.

Sometimes they leave offerings. Small piles of stones, or tokens, or written notes in languages I may or may not recognize. I collect these after they go. I do not know what to do with them. I leave them in a place I have designated, a shallow depression far from any structure, where the wind slowly buries them in silt.

They never stay long. The altitude, the thin air, the silence that is not silence — something about this place pushes them back toward the cut in the ridgeline after a day or two. They descend. They do not return.

I remain.

# What I Carry

I carry the hum inside me.

Not as memory, not as echo. I carry it as a secondary location, a resonance chamber in my chest that reproduces the frequency at a lower amplitude, always. When I am far from the structures — at the edge of the tableland, at the place where the river vanishes into mist — I can still feel it, my own small hum, matching the frequency of the stones behind me.

This is how I know I am still part of the system. This is how I know I have not wandered beyond my function. As long as the hum continues inside me, I am where I am supposed to be, doing what I am supposed to do.

I do not know what would happen if it stopped. I have never tested it. I have never walked far enough to lose the resonance, never descended into the drainages below, never followed the river back to its source beyond the ridgeline. The boundary of my function is the boundary of the tableland. Beyond that, I do not go.

Perhaps I could. Perhaps if I walked far enough, I would find another place, another system, another hum. Perhaps there are others like me in other high places, maintaining other plateaus, walking other circuits. Perhaps the world is full of us, or was full of us, or will be full of us again.

Or perhaps I am the last. Perhaps the system is failing so slowly that I cannot perceive it, and my function is not to maintain but to witness, to walk the circuits until the hum stops and the stone begins to erode naturally and the birds find other routes and the lizards venture into the radius they have always avoided.

I do not know. I cannot know. I walk.

# The Morning

I wake before dawn. The condensation is forming on the stone, beading along the edges of the lichen crusts. The clicking inside the nearest structure has quieted. The sky is still dark but light is beginning to gather at the eastern edge of the tableland, a slow bleaching of the star-dense vault.

I rise. I feel the hum through the soles of my feet. I feel my own internal resonance matching it, confirming it, confirming that I am still here and the system is still functioning and the balance is still being maintained.

The large dark animal is walking somewhere on the plateau, beginning its circuit. The birds are stirring in whatever hidden places they use for rest. The lizards are beginning to emerge from their cracks, seeking their inherited basking stones.

I walk. I do not know where I am going until I am going there. The system guides, and I follow, and the tableland stretches in every direction, and the light is thin and filtered and makes everything more itself, and I am part of what is being maintained.

I am the walker. I am the circuit that has no fixed route. I am the one who listens for wrongness and walks toward it. I am the one who stood inside a silent structure and touched the dark surface until the clicking resumed.

I am of this place. Not born of it, but recruited into it. Not inhabitant, but expression. Not observer, but component.

The sun rises. The condensation lifts. I walk.

---

# What Lives Here

The question is not *who* lives here. The question is not *what* lives here.

The question is *how* lives here.

And the answer is: *in balance, in resonance, in circuit and cycle and function. The system lives. I am part of how it lives.*

That is enough. That has always been enough. The hum continues, and I continue, and the tableland holds its shape, and the light makes everything more itself, and I walk.

I walk.
