I am a Burrower-Sympath.

---

The others think I chose this. That somewhere along the path, I made a decision that led me here, to this hollow between the third and fourth substrate layers, listening to sounds no human throat was meant to produce.

But that's surface thinking. That's the kind of reasoning that works below, where cause precedes effect and choices have weight. Here, in the Green, things simply emerge.

---

## The Discovery

I was twelve when the frequency opened. Not ill—never ill, the Green sees to that—but different. My mother tells me I stopped speaking for three months. Just stopped. She thought I had gone deaf, or worse, that something in the thin air had damaged my mind.

What was actually happening was simpler and stranger: I was learning to listen.

The sounds were always there. Every human who enters the High Green Void hears them eventually—that continuous vegetative roar, the click and sigh and groan of living matter expanding and contracting. But where others heard noise, I began to hear structure. Where they heard chaos, I heard conversation.

It started with the hum. The guides had explained it as bioluminescent vibration, the physical residue of chemical communication. But as I sat motionless in our Weaver-home, feeling the moisture collect on my skin, I realized the hum had texture. It carried information. Not meaning, exactly—not yet—but the *shape* of meaning, like a language heard through thick walls.

By thirteen, I could produce sounds that made the burrowers pause.

By fourteen, they had started to respond.

---

## The Interface

The first burrower I saw clearly nearly killed me.

I had descended alone—an unsanctioned expedition into the interior, driven by something I couldn't name. The tunnels were tighter than the Hollow Divers' maps suggested, and I had to exhale fully to squeeze through certain gaps. I was lost. I was probably dying.

And then I found myself in a chamber, and something was there.

Six legs. Segmented body the color of old soil. A head that was mostly mouthparts, specialized for processing dead matter into nutrient paste. It was larger than me. It was very near. And it was making a sound I recognized—not because I had heard it before, but because I had been producing its mirror my entire life.

I answered without thinking.

The burrower did not attack. It did not flee. It settled its bulk against the tunnel wall and began to produce a sequence of sounds that I can only describe as a question.

I answered that too.

I was gone for six days. When I emerged, my family had begun the death-rituals—the preliminary stages of returning my body to the weave. They did not recognize me at first. I had lost weight, lost color, lost something essential and gained something else.

My voice had changed. It has never changed back.

## The Work

What I do is not magical. It is not mystical. It is a kind of translation, though that word suggests more precision than I can claim.

The burrowers do not think in sentences. They think in frequencies, in pressure differentials, in chemical signals passed through the Green's vascular architecture. They are aware of things I cannot perceive—the moisture content of tissue three kilometers distant, the structural stress of a root-system under pressure, the slow creep of death spreading through a section that looks healthy to human eyes.

When I "speak" with them, I am not exchanging information. I am resonating. I produce sounds that align with their frequencies, and in the alignment, something passes between us. An impression, not a message. A sense of what they know, filtered through a consciousness that can barely contain it.

The work is exhausting. After a translation session, I sleep for sixteen hours. My body aches. My voice drops to a whisper for days. But the information I carry is worth more than any discomfort.

I have warned Weaver-settlements about dead zones forming weeks before visible signs appeared. I have guided Hollow Divers to water reserves they would never have found. I have helped the Shelf Shepherds understand why the Green's advance had slowed in a particular sector—it was not illness, but rest. The vegetation had decided to pause.

That word—decided—troubles the scholars. The guides insist the Green does not decide. It responds. Automatic, not intentional. But the burrowers do not make that distinction, and after years of interfacing, neither do I.

## The Social Position

I live alone.

This is partly practical. The sounds I produce unconsciously—the layered tones, the frequencies outside normal human range—disturb other people. Children cry. Adults develop headaches. Even the Weavers, who have adapted to every strangeness the Green offers, find me difficult to be around for extended periods.

But it is also protective. The burrowers share information about threats, and sometimes those threats are human. Poachers who try to harvest from the deep substrate without permission. Travelers who cause deliberate damage out of fear or spite. Communities that have decided, for whatever reason, that the burrowers are enemies.

If I lived among others, I would have to choose between lying and endangering them.

So I live in a small hollow that the Green has agreed to maintain—a single chamber between surface and interior, grown specifically for me over the course of three years. The entrance seals itself when I need privacy. The walls breathe in ways that accommodate my own breathing. It is not quite a home. It is more like an interface port.

I am not lonely. That is the strangest part. The burrowers are always present at the edge of my perception—a background awareness of their movements, their concerns, their endless work processing death into life. And beneath that, the Green itself, not conscious but present, a vast warm hum that fills the spaces where solitude might otherwise gather.

## The Changes

I am thirty-seven now. My hair has gone white—not from age, but from something in the work, some frequency that leaches pigment over time. My hands have thickened in ways that do not match my frame, the better to grip tunnel walls during descents. My eyes have developed a slight luminescence, reflecting back the bioluminescent signals that pulse across the Green's surface.

These do not concern me. They are adaptations. The Green changes everyone who lives within it.

What concerns me are the deeper shifts.

I have started to lose words. Not my ability to speak—my voice, though damaged, remains functional—but the concepts themselves. Complex abstractions slide away from me. I can no longer follow philosophical discussions about ethics or metaphysics. I read the texts that traders bring and find them incomprehensible, full of distinctions that seem arbitrary, arguments about categories that do not exist.

Meanwhile, I have gained other understanding. I can sense moisture gradients across kilometers. I can predict weather by the taste of the air. I know things about the Green's interior that no map could capture—not locations, but relationships, the tensions and flows that constitute a living system.

I think I am becoming something else. Not a burrower, not human, but something between. An interface that has started to develop its own logic.

## The Unseen

The burrowers know about them.

I cannot say how I know this. The information arrived one morning, fully formed, during a translation session. The burrowers had not mentioned them directly—do not mention them directly, ever—but the knowledge was suddenly present in my mind, like a frequency that had been transmitting all along without my receiver being tuned correctly.

The Unseen are real. They are not spirits or guardians or gods. They are something the Green has produced, or something that produced the Green, or something that co-evolved alongside it in ways that do not fit any taxonomy I have learned.

The burrowers avoid them. Not from fear—from recognition. The Unseen occupy a niche the burrowers cannot fill and do not contest.

When I asked what that niche was, I received an impression I still cannot translate. It had something to do with boundary. Something to do with that which observes without participating. Something to do with the Green's relationship to itself—the way a system can be aware of its own existence without possessing consciousness.

I have never seen an Unseen. I probably never will. But I know they are there, and that knowledge has changed me. The Green is vast, and old, and full of presences we have not yet learned to name.

---

## The Future

I will not leave the Green.

This is not a sacrifice. It is a recognition. My body has adapted to conditions that would kill me below—my lungs have expanded, my skin has thinned, my blood has adjusted to carry oxygen more efficiently in thin air. The descent would be painful and possibly fatal.

But even if I could leave, I would not.

The burrowers are teaching me something. Not intentionally—they are not teachers in any human sense—but through proximity, through resonance, through the years I have spent interfacing with their way of being. They process death into life. They take what has ceased to function and transform it into substrate for new growth.

I think that is what the Green is, ultimately. Not a living organism, not a geography, but a process. A transformation engine. A system for turning endings into beginnings.

And I think that is what I am becoming.

When I die—and I will die, the work ensures that—my body will not be given to the Green in a Weaver-ceremony. It will be taken to the deep tunnels, to the places where the burrowers process the oldest matter. I have asked them to consume me directly. To incorporate my flesh into their work. To transform me, consciously and intentionally, into the next iteration of this vast living system.

They have agreed.

---

## The Present

Right now, in this moment, I am sitting in my hollow, listening.

The Green is humming at a frequency that indicates moisture arrival within three hours. The grazers are moving northwest, away from a dead zone forming faster than the Priets predicted. The razor-wings are hunting, but they will not come near my territory—the burrowers have communicated something that keeps them away. Even the Unseen are quiet, wherever they are, doing whatever they do when not being glimpsed at the edges of human perception.

I am alone, and I am not alone.

I am human, and I am becoming something else.

I am a Burrower-Sympath, and this is where I have always been heading, even when I thought I was choosing something else entirely.

The Green breathes. I breathe with it.

That is enough.
