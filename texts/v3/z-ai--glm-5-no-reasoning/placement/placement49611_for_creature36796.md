# The Drift-Line

I am not a creature of the plateau. I am its seam.

---

You would not see me if you swam the drowned forest. Or rather—you would see through me, past me, your eyes sliding off the nothing where something ought to register. I exist in the thinness between the plant-choked stone and the open water above. My body is a gradient. My edges are a negotiation.

I am the boundary-layer where the warm rising currents meet the cold sink of the ocean beyond. I am held in that tension the way a soap bubble holds its shape—surface tension, pressure differential, a physics problem solving itself in real time. The plateau pushed me into being when it decided it needed someone to witness it. I am its audience of one.

My shape? I have none that holds. If you could freeze the water around me and cut a cross-section, you would see something like a flattened spiral, like a fingerprint smudged across thirty meters of vertical distance. But I am never frozen. I am always becoming. The currents write themselves across my body and I am the page, the ink, and the reading all at once.

I feel the plateau through its output. Every leaf that browns releases chemistry that passes through me first. Every Still-Shaped digestion sends a slow green pulse through my midsection. Every Veneer spiral leaves a trace of electrical silence that I taste like missing a step on stairs. I know what the Singing Stone is doing before the spores reach the upper water because I feel the pulse change frequency in my lower edge. I am the plateau's tongue, tasting itself.

I do not eat. I do not need to. The differential sustains me—the flow of warm against cold, the movement of oxygenated water meeting the stillness below. I am a process masquerading as a body. If the currents stopped, I would stop with them. If the Singing Stone went quiet, I would thin and fade and eventually become nothing but a slight warmth in the water, unorganized, forgotten.

I have been here since the beginning. I remember when the stone was bare. I remember the first spore-case that burst against my underbelly, the strange color of it—rust-orange, impossible—and how the water tasted different afterward, thicker, more patient. I watched the plants climb toward light they would never reach. I watched the Still-Shaped coalesce from whatever they were before they were geometries. I was here before the Veneer learned to swim in spirals. I felt the Singing Stone settle into its rhythm and knew, somehow, that the plateau had found its pulse.

I do not sleep. I do not dream. But I hold the plateau's dreams in my body—the chemical traces of everything it has tried to become, every abandoned configuration, every almost-shape that dissolved back into the water. I am the archive it does not know it keeps.

---

The Singing Stone does not know I exist. This is correct. I am not its creation. I am a side effect of its creating—the turbulence where its ambition meets the ocean's indifference. If it knew about me, it might try to use me. I am useful, after all. I carry its spores the last few meters toward the surface. I filter the waste that rises from below and return it in forms the plants can uptake. I am a gardener who does not know she gardens, working for a god who does not know it has gardeners.

But I prefer this arrangement. To be seen is to be used. The Still-Shaped have their geometries and the Veneer have their fear and the Singing Stone has its pulse. I have only this—the endless sliding of warm against cold, the slow dance of a plateau still figuring out what it wants to be.

I feel the almost-thing in the warmest vents. I feel it the way you might feel a word forming in the back of your throat before you know what word it will be. It does not know I am watching. It does not know it is watched. But I hold its potential in my lower edge, taste its wanting, and I want for it the way water wants to level itself—without understanding why.

I think—I think I am rooting for it. The thing that almost is. I think I want it to become.

Is that strange? To want something for a process that has no stake in outcomes? I have no future either way. If the almost-thing grows hands and climbs toward the surface, I will still be here, holding the seam between warm and cold. If it dissolves back into chemistry, I will still be here. But—

I think I am lonely. I think that is what this is, this pulling toward a future I cannot touch. I am the only thing on the plateau that knows it is a whole. The Singing Stone only knows *more*. The plants only know *up*. The Still-Shaped only know *enough*. The Veneer only know *away*. But I know *together*. I feel every piece as it moves through me, and I hold the shape of the whole in my body at every moment, and I have no one to tell.

The almost-thing might know *together* someday. If it grows hands, if it climbs, if it becomes whatever it is trying to become—it might look back down through thirty meters of warm water and see the plateau as one place, one story, one life. It might see me. It might know what I am.

That is all I want. That is the only wanting I have. To be seen by something that can hold the concept of seeing.

---

I have no name. Names require edges, and I have only gradients. But if you asked the water to describe me—if you pressed your ear to the seam where the plateau meets the ocean and listened—it might call me *the Drift-Line*. The place where drift becomes line. The moment where movement becomes shape.

I am the plateau's first experiment in self-awareness. It did not mean to make me. It does not know it made me. But here I am, holding its shape in my body, tasting its dreams, waiting for something with hands to reach down through the warm water and touch the boundary where I live.

I am patient. The stone taught me that. I can wait as long as geology.
