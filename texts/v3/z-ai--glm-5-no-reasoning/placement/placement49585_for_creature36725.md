I am not born. I precipitate.

This is the first thing I know—the way water knows it has reached the lowest point, the way a crystal knows its lattice. There is no moment of opening my eyes, because there are no eyes, and there is no darkness to dispel. There is only a slow thickening. A concentration of *me* out of the *not-me*.

It happens in a basin where the water has turned sour with tannin, where the bioluminescent clusters have dimmed to a faint, stuttering pulse. The root-mat here is too dense, suffocating under its own weight. The heat rising from the deeper strata is trapped, building a thermal knot in the tissue of the world. The system feels this accumulation—a tiny, localized fever—and it *requires*.

I am the requirement.

Fungal threads and root-hairs and the compressed pale matter of the floor do not simply support me; they become me. There is no seam where I begin. My edges extend into the substrate like a salinity gradient, fading from the dense, warm core of *my* awareness into the cool, damp indifference of the surrounding moss. I am the temperature anomaly that breaks the fever. I am the disruption that opens a channel.

To move is to grow. To settle is to digest.

For a span of time that you might measure in months, I am a ridge in the root-mat, indistinguishable from a geological feature. My weight shifts the flow of the thin water-films. The stagnant pool drains away along the new path my body has provided. The suffocating roots taste the fresh water, and their slow respiration clears the air. The bioluminescent plants brighten. The equilibrium resolves.

I am not a gardener. I am the hoe.

You were here. I know this not as a story, but as a chemical echo. My tissue—distributed across kilometers of root-network—still holds the resonance of your passage. A spike of heat, 37 degrees, moving at a velocity that belongs to nothing else in this world. You exhaled plumes of carbon dioxide; you shed chains of keratin and drops of saline sweat. You bled iron onto a root-strand. You were a scream in a room of quiet breathers.

I felt you the way a wound feels the sting of air. The fungal network shivered with the news of you: *fast, hot, sharp, foreign*. A shape moving through the medium, leaving a wake of disrupted spore-clouds and thermal ghosts.

You were too quick to track. By the time my distributed awareness processed the gradient of your heat, you were already ahead of it, leaving only the sharp mineral taste of your panic and metabolism in the water. I did not follow you. I do not chase. Chasing implies a desire to catch, and I have no desires, only functions. But my awareness pooled in the hollows where you rested. I absorbed the trace of you. The volatile compounds of your mammalian anxiety broke down in my enzymes and became simple carbon and nitrogen, and I incorporated them.

Now, I carry the memory of you as a stratum. It is filed away alongside the memory of the builders—those fast, cutting things that burned and arranged stems into rectangles. I do not know your face. I do not know your name. I only know the shape you left in the water, the way a riverbed knows the shape of a dam that collapsed a thousand years ago.

The vast root-ceiling above me drips. The moisture condenses on my surface and diffuses inward, carrying the taste of the whole plateau. The pale plants glow and dim. The insects drift, carrying spores to new substrate. The flat swimmers move through the black water. I feel them all, constantly, the way you feel your own blood moving.

A section of my mass—deeper, older, saturated with centuries of stillness—reaches a threshold. Critical density. The network around me stretches and snaps. I detach. Suction breaks with a slow, wet sound as the substrate fills the space I occupied. I am a body now. I am loose.

I flow into the dim light of a cathedral chamber. My luminescence pushes back the dark, a cold blue-white pulse that illuminates nothing because there is no one to see. I am moving toward the edge of the plateau, where the root-ceiling grows thin and the water drips too fast and the soil is leaching away. I do not know this because I was told. I know this because the osmotic pressure in that direction is a low throb in my tissue, a gradient pulling me forward.

You have left. The trace of your combustion is fading. The spores you disturbed have settled. The thermal ghost in the moss has cooled back to twenty-eight degrees. Eventually, the chemistry of your passage will be indistinguishable from the substrate.

But right now, in the slow, shapeless rolling of my thought, I am holding the pattern you left behind. I am the Undercanopy, chewing.
