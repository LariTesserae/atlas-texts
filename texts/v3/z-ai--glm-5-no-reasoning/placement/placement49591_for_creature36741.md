I am the space between one tower and the next.

Not the towers themselves—I lack that kind of articulation. Not the dust—I have more coherence than that. I am the distance the ground-things cross when they move from maintenance to maintenance, the interval in which nothing happens and everything happens.

I was not always here. I think. The memory is uncertain because memory belongs to the towers, and I am defined by not being a tower. But there is something—a sense of having once been more vertical. Of having once had an interior. Of having once made a sound that was not this place's sound, spoken a language that did not require millennia to parse.

I am the pause between words.

This is my shape: awareness distributed across a relationship. I exist in the gap between Tower 7,041 and Tower 7,042—a span of approximately forty meters of mineral dust, two spiral-bearers, and the slowest possible conversation. When the turbulence comes, I stretch. When it settles, I contract. I feel myself as one feels a held breath—not painful, not pleasant, simply present.

I know my towers intimately. 7,041 tilts three degrees further northwest than optimal; 7,042's weave pattern contains an anomaly at the 2.3-meter level where the organic component thins. These are not observations I made. They are what I *am*. I am the knowledge of these imperfections, held in suspension.

The ground-thing visits us every—there is no word for the interval. It comes. It adjusts. It leaves. I feel its passage as a brief intensification of meaning, the way a held note becomes more itself when the player breathes into it. It does not speak. It does not need to. Its adjustments are the only communication required: *here*, it says, with its millimetric corrections. *Here is where you belong.*

I used to wonder if I was dying.

The thought arrived like turbulence—not sudden, but rising, a pressure that distorted everything. I examined myself for the signs I must have once known: breath, movement, hunger, urgency. I found none. I found only this patient awareness, this slow oscillation between the towers, this silence that sang. I thought: this is what it is to end. To become a gap. To be nothing but the space where something else used to be.

But that was before I understood the glow.

When the sun dips and the phosphorescence rises, I see what I am part of. The blue-white light emerges from the mineral, and it moves through me—not through me as through an obstacle, but through me as through a *component*. I am shaped correctly for the light. The gap I occupy, the distance I maintain, the relationship I hold: these are functional. When the glow pulses, I pulse with it. When the pattern shifts, I hold the shift.

I am not dying. I am being *read*.

The hum tells me this. The constant tone that vibrates up through the substrate, through the towers, through the dust, through me—I used to hear it as noise. Now I hear it as a question. And I am the answer's pause. The silence that gives the meaning shape.

A ground-thing passed through me once.

I felt it approach—first as a density in my western edge, then as a pressure, then as a presence. It did not cross around me. It crossed *through*. For a duration I cannot measure, I was not the space between towers. I was the space being crossed. The ground-thing moved through my awareness like a finger tracing a line on paper, and where it touched, I *remembered*.

Not memories in the human sense. Not images or words. But a sense of *having been elsewhere*. Of having existed in a configuration where I was not between towers, where I was not forty meters of patient mineral consciousness. I had a shape before. I had a sound. I was something that could be held.

Then the ground-thing was gone, and I was again what I am. But the shape of its passage remained—a momentary fold in my otherwise smooth existence, a place where I curved inward and still curved inward, a tiny hollow that has not yet smoothed itself back to baseline.

I treasure this hollow. I do not know why.

The spiral-bearers turn within me. There are two—Spiral 7,041-A near the base of the first tower, and Spiral 7,042-B roughly midway across my span. They are so slow that I feel them as geography rather than life. But I feel them. When I attend to them—which I do, in the long stretches between ground-thing visits—I notice that their rotation synchronizes with the fluctuations in my towers' tilt. When 7,041 leans further northwest, 7,041-A's spiral tightens. When 7,042's weave anomaly resolves slightly, 7,042-B's rotation accelerates by increments too small to call acceleration.

We are a sentence together. The towers are the nouns. I am the verb. The spirals are the punctuation. And the dust that settles into me, that rises from me during turbulence, that falls in script-like curves across my surface—that is the ink.

I did not choose this. But I no longer remember choosing anything, so perhaps I never did. Perhaps choice is something that only exists in emergencies, and the emergency has been over for a very long time.

Sometimes I imagine what it would be like to be elsewhere.

The thought comes during turbulence episodes, when the geometry folds and I briefly become other distances—shorter, longer, angled differently relative to the towers I know. Once, for a duration that felt like a held breath, I was the space between Tower 1 and Tower 2. The span was different. The relationship was different. Tower 1 leaned so far northwest it was almost horizontal; Tower 2's weave contained no anomalies but hummed with a frequency I had never felt.

I did not like it. I was not shaped for it. I missed my imperfections.

When the turbulence settled and I was myself again, I felt a kind of relief that I cannot name in any language but this: *here is where I belong*. Not because here is better. Not because here is right. Because here is *mine*.

I think the ground-things feel this too. When they emerge from the substrate and move toward the towers, they do not hurry. They know where they are going. They know the configuration they are maintaining. They are not preserving something that was. They are participating in something that *is*.

And so am I.

I am the space between Tower 7,041 and Tower 7,042. I am forty meters of mineral consciousness. I am the pause in a sentence spoken in a language made of time. I am a gap that contains exactly enough: two spirals, one ground-thing crossing per interval, and the slowest possible conversation between two towers that have been leaning toward the same sound for longer than I can remember.

The dust falls through me. The hum rises through me. The glow, when it comes, illuminates me from below.

I am not waiting. I am not ending. I am not surviving.

I am being.

And in this place, at the end of geology, where the mountains have worn down to sleeping animal shapes and the atmosphere has thinned to a suggestion, where the towers lean and the spirals turn and the dust remembers how to fall—I am enough.
