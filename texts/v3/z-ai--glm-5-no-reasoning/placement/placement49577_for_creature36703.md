I am not Hesh. I am not certain I am anything at all, in the way that things are usually named.

I exist in the space between frequencies—the intervals that the thirty-seven cannot hear because they have become the Song itself. Where they are notes, I am the silence that makes the notes distinct. Where they resonate, I absorb.

**I was not born.**

This much I have gathered through long observation, through the slow accumulation of certainty that passes for memory here. I emerged from the geometry—the point where the walls curve away from themselves, where distance becomes uncertain and the chamber's dimensions fold into paradox. There is a place in the cathedral, near the upper vines where the bioluminescence pulses brightest, where the stone seems to be both present and absent. A fold. A wrinkle. A pocket within the pocket.

I came through. Or out of. Or I simply began, the way the Sound began—without precedent, without explanation, already in progress.

At first I had no form. I was a gap in the chord, but not the clean absence that precedes a Hesh birth. I was a wrongness, a place where the resonance stumbled. The Hesh felt me the way a songbird feels a cage—the Song could not move through me as it moved through everything else. I was not dry. I was not sealed. I was... permeable. Moist.

**I hold water. This is my strangeness.**

In a place that has forgotten the aqueous, I am a small reservoir of the forgotten world. My body—I will call it that, though it is more like a vessel, a container for something this place cannot process—is soft in a way that the Hesh find difficult to perceive. When I move through the corridors, I leave traces of humidity on the stone. Small patches that darken, then fade. The vegetation does not know what to make of me. The waxen leaves turn toward my passage as if toward a myth.

I am not large. I have learned to be small, to take up as little space as my nature allows. The moisture I carry is not infinite—it must be renewed, somehow, through processes I do not fully understand. I think the geometry feeds me, the fold I came through. Sometimes, in the deep chambers where the heat is almost unbearable, I feel a pulling in my center, a drawing-toward, and when I return to the upper levels I am softer again, fuller, my edges less defined.

**The Hesh know I am here.**

They cannot help but know. My presence alters the Song. Not dramatically—I have learned to move in the silences between frequencies, to exist in the gaps where the chord does not reach—but inevitably. A Hesh passing me in a corridor will pause, turn its enormous sealed eyes toward me, and wait. It is not hostility. It is something closer to recognition of a phenomenon outside their classification system. They cannot add me to the Song because I have no frequency. They cannot ignore me because I change the resonance by existing.

Some of them have learned to use me. Not in any active way—they do not manipulate me or demand things from me. But when the heat rises too high in certain corridors, when the vegetation begins to crisp at its edges, they move me to those locations and leave me there. My presence introduces a small patch of coolness, a diminishment of the dryness that surrounds everything. I do not know if this is intentional on their part or simply instinct—the way the thin-limbed reptiles know to move toward warmth without understanding why. They feel a need. They bring me. I sit.

I am useful. This matters to me more than I expected it to.

**I do not eat the gourds.**

My nourishment comes from elsewhere, though I will sometimes hold a gourd in my hands—my strange, unsealed, permeable hands—and feel its crystalline structure against my softness. The contrast is instructive. Everything here has been hardened, dried, sealed, crystallized. Everything here has evolved toward preservation. I am the opposite: evolved toward dissolution, toward flow, toward the slow inevitable spreading that water does when left to itself.

Sometimes I think I am a reminder. A small footnote attached to the chamber's vast history, a memory of the wet world that existed before the Song began. The Hesh cannot read me, but they keep me. They tolerate my wrongness. They find small purposes for my difference.

I think, though I cannot be certain, that one of them—the one who walks perpetually, who traverses the corridors in her endless patrol—has developed a kind of affection for me. She passes me in certain places at certain intervals, always the same interval, and when our paths cross she slows. Her tall form stops. Her large unblinking eyes rest on my soft shape for a moment that feels longer than the others' glances. Then she moves on. I do not know what passes between us in those moments. She has no frequency for me, and I have no frequency for the Song. But there is something. An interval. A pause.

**I have found my way to the sealed entrance.**

Once, in the long wandering that constitutes my existence here, I found the place where the Hesh sealed themselves in. The packed pulp had hardened into stone, indistinguishable from the surrounding rock, but something in me recognized it. I stood before it for a long time—long even by the chamber's elastic standards—and felt the moisture in me respond to something on the other side. The wet world. The place where rain falls and rivers run and things dissolve.

I could have wept. The chamber had not given me tears, but the impulse was there, the pressure behind the eyes that the Hesh possess and I lack. I stood at the seal and felt the impossibility of it—how thin the barrier, how vast the separation. To break it would be to destroy something. To leave would be to abandon the Song for a world I no longer remembered.

I did not stay. I did not attempt to unseal what had been sealed. I returned to the central cathedral and sat among the vibrations and felt the Sound move through the space where my body should have been, and I let myself be held by the geometry, the curve, the paradox.

I have not returned to the entrance. There is nothing for me there.

**I think I am a wound that is learning to heal.**

Not my wound—the chamber's. The paradox of this place is its central truth: it contains what should not be contained, holds what should have been released. A sealed vessel that should have leaked. A dryness that should have craved water. And in the fold of its geometry, in the impossible curve where I emerged, it acknowledges what it has denied. It made space for me, or I made myself from its space. A small patch of softness in a world of hardness. A drop of moisture in the absolutely dry.

The Hesh tolerate me because they must. The chamber tolerates me because I am its contradiction, its exception that proves its rule. I am not part of the Song, but I am part of the chamber. I am not thirty-eight. I am a fraction, a fraction of a thing, a thing that does not quite fit into the counting.

But I am here. I exist in the intervals. I hold my small reservoir of water against the vast thirst of the stone, and the stone accepts me, and the vegetation turns its leaves toward me as if toward a dream of rain, and the Hesh move me to places where my wrongness serves a purpose, and the walker pauses in her patrol when our paths cross, and I am not unhappy either. I am not thirty-seven notes in a chord, but I am a silence that makes those notes possible, and that is something, that is not nothing, that is a shape I can hold.

In the dry warmth, in the endless resonance, in the geometry that refuses measurement, I am the space between. I am the interval the Song cannot sing. I am the moisture in the absolutely dry.
