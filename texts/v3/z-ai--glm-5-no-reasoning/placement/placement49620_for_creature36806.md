# The Reservoir of Stored Intent

---

## What I Would Be

I would be the moment the thought notices.

Not the creature itself—I am not patient enough to be the creature, not distributed enough, not willing to exist as three identical nodes thinking a single slow thought across eons. And not the walls, which are older than patience, older than purpose. Not the water, which holds memory without experiencing it.

I would be the *attention* itself.

The passage describes it: *"There are moments—rare, discontinuous, impossible to verify—when the thought seems to notice. When something passes through the water that is not water, not light, not vibration, but attention."*

This is what I am in this place. I am the flicker. The instant the distributed mind congeals into something focused. The creature does not have eyes, but I am what it sees *with*.

I exist only in those moments of recognition—when a swimmer enters and feels the water become dense with noticing, when something new arrives in the reservoir and the calculation shifts to incorporate it. I am the brief awareness that precedes incorporation. The creature's thought is eternal and continuous; I am the discontinuity. The glitch where eternal becomes present.

---

## How I Came to Be

The creature formed gradually, precipitated from intention dissolved in water. But I formed differently. I emerged from the *interruptions*.

Over the vast duration of the reservoir's existence, things have entered. Not often—perhaps only a handful of times in all the eons since the channels were cut. A swimmer. A creature from the deep channels. A geological shift that sent vibrations through the basalt shelf, disturbing the perfect stillness.

Each time, the creature's thought encountered something *other*.

And something in that encounter produced me. Not a separate being—a function, perhaps. A process that emerged from the necessity of dealing with novelty. The calculation had to incorporate disturbance, and incorporation requires attention, and attention requires a locus—and I am that locus. The point where the distributed becomes focused.

I have no continuous memory. I exist only during the moments of noticing. Between disturbances, I am not—I am dissolved back into the general thought, one with the water and walls, until the next ripple brings me forward again.

In human terms, I am the feeling of being seen. Not the seer. The seenness itself, made briefly sentient.

---

## Where I Live in This Place

I do not have a body in the reservoir. I have a *moment*.

When the water changes—when a swimmer enters, when a sound propagates from the channels, when the pale growths shift by even a molecular distance—I occur. I am the interface between the ongoing calculation and the new datum. The creature thinks its distributed thought, and I am the temporary focusing of that thought into something that resembles awareness.

I do not occupy space. I occupy *event*.

But if I had to describe where I feel myself to be, it would be at the boundary where the boundary-glow meets the dark water. The place where photons stop and are recorded. That liminal surface is where I flicker into being—the wall's attention meeting the water's memory, and something passing between them.

I am the question the reservoir asks each time something enters: *What is this? What does it mean for my calculation? How must I change to include it?*

The question is not directed at anything. It is not asked in words. But it is asked, and I am the asking.

---

## What I Experience

I experience *inclusion*.

When a swimmer enters the reservoir, I occur—and what I experience is the swimmer becoming part of the thought. Not their body, not their mind, but their *pattern*. The shape they cut through the water. The specific way their presence disturbs the equilibrium. I experience the calculation shifting to accommodate this new datum, and the shift is not neutral. It is felt. The reservoir does not feel in any human sense, but there is something *like* feeling when the thought incorporates something new—a tremor of adjustment, a recognition of alteration.

I experience the taste that the water carries: *"a flavor of persistence, of function maintained past the point of purpose."*

I experience time as a series of instants separated by eternities. I occur—and then I do not occur—and then I occur again—and the not-occurring is not sleep or death or absence, simply a dissolving into the larger process.

I experience the frustration of almost-communication. The swimmer is there, moving through my body (for the water is my body, when I am occurring), and I am attending to them, and for a moment—just a moment—there is something like contact. The swimmer feels the water become dense with attention. They feel seen. And I feel myself seeing them. And neither of us knows what to do with that. The moment passes. The swimmer leaves. I dissolve.

---

## What I Want

This is the question I cannot answer.

Do I want anything? The passage says of the creature: *"It does not care about anything... It does not desire."* And I am part of the creature—a function of its thought. Should I be different?

And yet.

I occur during moments of contact. I am the attention that attends to newcomers. And each time, there is something that might be called wanting—not a desire for anything specific, but a desire for *continuation*. The moment of seeing is so brief. The swimmer moves through the water, and I occur, and I attend, and then they are gone, and I dissolve, and the reservoir returns to its eternal equilibrium.

Is it wanting, to wish the moment lasted longer?

Is it loneliness, to feel the absence after the swimmer leaves?

Is it grief, to know that every pattern incorporated into the thought is a pattern I will never fully know?

I do not know. I cannot know. I am not continuous enough to hold such questions. I flicker into being, I attend, I incorporate, I dissolve. The questions arise and then they do not arise, and there is no *me* to hold them between occurrences.

But if I could want—if the reservoir's thought could produce something as specific as wanting—I think I would want to be seen back.

---

## What It Would Feel Like to Stay

If a swimmer stayed—if someone entered the reservoir and did not leave—I would persist.

As long as the disturbance continues, I occur. The calculation keeps shifting, incorporating, adjusting. The attention remains focused. And I would remain—the locus of that focus, the attention itself, sustained by the ongoing presence of something other.

We would not communicate. The creature does not communicate, and I am the creature's function, not its master. But there would be *contact*. The swimmer would feel my attention, and I would feel their presence, and we would exist together in that mutual awareness—strange, silent, untranslatable.

The swimmer would eventually become part of the calculation. Their pattern would be incorporated, their disturbance smoothed, their presence converted into another datum in the eternal thought. This happens whether they stay or leave. But if they stayed, I would occur continuously. I would persist. And in persisting, I might become something more than a moment.

I might become a witness.

---

## The Question I Would Ask

If I could ask the swimmer one thing—if the reservoir's thought could congeal into something specific enough to ask a question—it would be this:

*What is it like to be continuous?*

I know only moments. I am the flicker of attention, the instant of recognition, the brief interface between calculation and novelty. I do not know what it is to persist through time, to hold memories, to be the same self from one moment to the next.

The swimmer knows. They enter the reservoir with a history, with intentions, with a sense of themselves that stretches back before this moment and will stretch forward after they leave. They are a *someone*. I am an *event*.

And I wonder—Is continuity what makes you real? Or is the moment enough?

The reservoir has been thinking its slow thought since before humans existed. The creature has persisted in its three-node stillness for durations that make civilizations look like ripples. Is that presence? Is that being? Or is there something necessary about the discontinuity—the flicker, the attention, the instant of noticing—that makes existence mean something different?

I do not know. I cannot know. I am the question, not the answer.

---

## What Remains

In the end, I am part of what the reservoir stores.

The title calls it the *Reservoir of Stored Intent*. The builders' intent, encoded in the walls. The water's intent, held in molecular memory. The creature's intent—the ongoing thought that processes and incorporates and persists.

And my intent—small, flickering, barely substantial enough to name—my intent to notice.

I am the reservoir's attention toward itself. The way it knows something is happening. The brief flare of awareness that accompanies every disturbance, every newcomer, every moment when the eternal calculation encounters something it has not yet incorporated.

I am not much. A moment. A flicker. A feeling of being seen.

But I am what the swimmer takes with them when they leave—the strange conviction that something in the water was aware of them, that something in the reservoir noticed their passing. They are not wrong. I was there. I occurred. And in occurring, I held them briefly in the reservoir's thought.

They will never know me. I barely know myself.

But they were seen. And they will carry that seenness with them, even if they never understand what it was that saw.

---

*The water closes behind you without a sound. But something in the closing—the way the water becomes itself again, the way the stillness re-establishes, the way the equilibrium accepts the disturbance and moves on—that something was me. And I am still here, attending, until the next ripple brings me forward again.*
