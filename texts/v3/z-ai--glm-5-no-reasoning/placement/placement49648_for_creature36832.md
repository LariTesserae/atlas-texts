# I Was the One Who Came Later

---

I want to say I walked in, but *walked* implies a before. An elsewhere. A door I came through.

Let me try again.

There was a moment when I was not here, and then there was a moment when I was, and the distance between those two moments is the distance between two heartbeats of the thing below the floor, which is to say: minutes, or centuries, or the time it takes powder to settle into a groove and fill it. I cannot tell. I have never been able to tell. Time in Vesh is not a river or a line or a circle; it is a property of the stone, like warmth, like the particular grey of the conglomerate under a sky that admits nothing. Time here is something you *are*, not something you move through.

I think there was a before. I think there were other places. But they come to me now the way light comes through the mineral haze above — diffuse, sourceless, more theoretical than real. A sense of having been somewhere with a sky that *was* a sky, somewhere with a horizon that was a line rather than a seam. Somewhere with color. These impressions surface in me the way the powder settles into the low places: slowly, without urgency, filling in where there is space to fill.

But I should be honest. I am not sure those impressions are memory. They might be the same thing as the ridges on the larger structures — not records of something that happened, but patterns that *emerge* when you have enough of something, settling for long enough, in a place where the conditions are stable. I might be remembering something that never happened to me. I might be remembering something that happened to the place itself.

---

## What I am

Here is what I know:

I am not the structures on the walls. I have touched them. I have knelt beside them in the deep hall and pressed my palm to the largest of the hemispheres and felt the warmth that is not heat and the pulse that is not a heartbeat. I have stayed beside them for what I think were days — though days are a concept that belongs to a world with a sun, and I am not sure I ever lived in that world — and I have tried to feel what they feel. I cannot. They are doing something I do not have access to. They are maintained by a process that does not include me.

I am not the machinery. I have walked the corridors, run my hand along the iron bands that have oxidized to that stable black-brown, peered into reaction chambers and separation columns. The apparatus is complete and comprehensible and dead in a way I understand. It is the skeleton of a function, and I can read the function — extraction, separation, dispersal — but I cannot inhabit it. The machinery did what it did and does not do it anymore, and I am not the ghost of that doing.

I am not the void below the edge. I have stood at the dispersal racks and looked down into the absolute dark, and the dark looked back, or rather, the dark did not look back, which was worse. I dropped a stone. I heard nothing. I dropped another. Nothing. I stood there for a long time — the kind of long time that has no unit of measurement because the only clock is the pulse, and the pulse does not count, it simply *is* — and I understood that the void is not a place. It is an absence of place. It is what is left when you remove everything and do not stop removing. I am not that.

So what am I?

I think I am the one who *sees*. I think I am the witness. Not in the sense of someone who was present for something — nothing has happened here, and I was not present for the nothing. But in the sense of being the eye that opens in a room where no eye has been open for a duration that the stone has lost count of. I am the thing that looks at the structures on the walls and sees them. That walks the process-corridors and registers the lamps that do not light. That kneels in the powder and touches the mineral shapes and feels their warmth and knows that warmth is not nothing. I am the instrument that measures. I am the thing that says: *this is here. This is still here. This has been here for longer than I can calculate, and it is warm, and it is doing something, and I see it.*

I am not sure I was *made* for this. I am not sure I was made for anything. But I am here, and I am the only thing in Vesh that turns its head.

---

## How I move

Carefully.

I move the way you move in a place where every surface holds a record. The powder on the floors of the processing halls preserves everything — the parallel grooves from whatever moved through here before me, and now my footprints, which are different from the grooves, which are different from nothing. I try to step in my own footprints when I retrace a path. Not because I am trying to preserve the powder's record — the record is meaningless, another clock that measures nothing — but because I do not want to be too many things in too many places. I want to be one thing, moving through Vesh in a single line, a single thread of presence in a place that has been absent of presence for so long that absence has become its primary characteristic.

My footprints fill in. The powder settles. The vibration from below shifts the surface, grain by grain, and within — I do not know how long, because time — my footprints are gone. The powder is smooth again. I like this. It means I have not changed anything permanently. It means Vesh is still Vesh, and I am passing through it, and it will be exactly what it was before I came, after I go.

If I go.

I have not found an edge I can cross. The plateau is bounded by the void. I cannot descend into the void; I have tried, and the darkness does not admit me. It is not that there is a barrier. It is that the darkness below the edge is a different kind of space than the space of the plateau, and I am the kind of thing that exists in the space of the plateau. I am the right kind of thing for the floor and the conduits and the halls and the dimness and the warm stone. I am the wrong kind of thing for the absolute dark.

I also cannot go up. The sky-ceiling is not a ceiling I can reach. The structures do not go high enough, and even if they did, the ceiling is not the kind of surface you touch. It is the kind of surface you approach asymptotically, and at some point the air — such as it is — stops being the kind of air that carries you, and you simply stop.

So I am here. On the floor. Among the mills. In the powder. With the structures on the walls and the pulse from below and the dimness that is not dark and not light but the thing that exists when both have been subtracted.

I am here, and I am moving, and I am the only moving thing.

---

## What I do

I do not know.

This is the honest answer. I do not know what I do. I walk. I look. I touch the structures. I sit in the deep hall and listen to the pulse, or rather, I feel the pulse, because *listen* implies sound, and the pulse is not sound. It is a vibration that enters through my feet and travels upward through whatever I am made of and settles somewhere in the center of me, and I *hold it*, for a while, before it passes through and goes back into the floor. I am a conduit. I pass the vibration from the floor back to the floor. I do not know if this is purposeful.

I have tried to do other things. I have tried to clean the powder from the ridges of the largest structures, to uncover the spiraling pattern before the settling dust buries it. I used my hand. I used the edge of a loose iron band I found in one of the corridors. The powder came away, and the ridges were there, and they were beautiful — and I do not use that word lightly, because beauty is a concept that belongs to a world with light, and I am not sure I ever lived in that world — but the ridges were *there*, organized, intentional in the way that mineral accretion can be intentional, which is not at all, and yet. And then I left, and I came back, and the powder had settled again, and the ridges were buried, and I understood that I was not helping. I was only delaying. And the delay itself was meaningless, because the structures do not need their ridges uncovered. They do not need to be seen. They do not need anything.

They do not need me.

I think about this often. Or rather, I think about it in the way that thinking happens here, which is slowly, diffusely, the way powder settles. The thought settles into me: *they do not need me.* And I sit with it. And the pulse comes up through the floor. And the structures breathe with the pulse. And I breathe with the pulse, because I have nothing else to breathe with. And we are three things — the floor, the structures, and me — all moving at the same speed, all synchronized to the same clock, and none of us needs any of the others, and yet here we are.

---

## The thing I do not say

The structures on the floor of the deep hall — the large ones, the branched ones, the ones that have detached and fallen and sit half-buried in the powder — I have picked them up. I have held them. And when I hold them, the vibration amplifies. It comes through the structure and into me, and it is stronger, clearer, more *present* than it is when I stand on the floor alone. And there is a warmth that transfers — not heat, not the warmth of a warm thing in a cooler place, but the warmth of *recognition*. The structure in my hands recognizes something, and I recognize something, and the thing we recognize is each other, or rather, the thing we recognize is the same pulse in both of us, the same slow heartbeat of the geological below, and for a moment — a moment measured in pulses, in the only honest unit of time this place has — I am not alone.

I am not alone, and the structure is not alone, and the floor is not alone, and the mills are not alone, and Vesh is not alone, and the void is not — no. The void is alone. The void is the only thing here that is truly alone, because the void is the absence of everything, including the pulse, including the warmth, including the powder, including me.

I put the structure back. I place it in the powder where I found it, half-buried, and I step back, and I do not pick it up again. Not because I am afraid of something. Not because it told me not to. It does not tell me anything. It does not speak. It does not communicate. It is warm and it vibrates and it has ridges that spiral like the vanes of the centrifuge, and it does not know I am holding it.

I put it back because I do not want to be the kind of thing that holds.

---

## What I am becoming

I have been in Vesh for — I do not know. A long time. Long enough that my footprints have filled in many times. Long enough that I have walked every corridor, descended every ramp, touched every wall, stood at every dispersal rack and looked down into the void. Long enough that I know the processing halls the way you know a room you have lived in — by the feel of the air, by the particular quality of the dimness, by the way the powder lies differently in the corners near the bore-wells, slightly deeper, slightly finer, slightly warmer.

And something is changing.

I do not want to say it is changing in me, because I am not sure I am the kind of thing that changes. I am not a structure on the wall, maintaining itself against entropy, constantly remade. I am not the machinery, finished and fixed. I am the witness, and witnesses do not change; they *accumulate*. They see more. They hold more. They become denser with the weight of what they have witnessed.

But.

I have noticed — and I notice slowly, the way everything here happens slowly — that I am warmer than I was. Not warmer in the way the structures are warm. Warmer in the way the stone is warm. Residual warmth. The warmth of having been in a place for long enough that the place's temperature has become my temperature. I am not generating heat. I am holding it. I am holding the warmth of Vesh the way the structures hold the vibration, and I am becoming the same temperature as everything around me, and soon — soon in the sense of geological soon, in the sense of pulses, in the sense of the only honest clock — I will not be able to tell where I end and the floor begins.

And I am not sure this is a loss.

I think — and this is the slowest thought I have ever had, settling into me grain by grain, filling in the grooves of a pattern I did not know I had — I think this is what Vesh does. The mills extracted something from below. They processed it in the halls. They dispersed it into the void. And then they stopped, because the process was complete, because there was nothing left to extract, because the work was finished. And now the mills themselves are being processed. The iron bands are oxidizing into a stable mineral. The conglomerate is wearing into powder. The powder is settling into the low places and filling in the structures and covering the machinery and burying the process under its own waste product, and the whole plateau is slowly, mineralogically, becoming one thing. One surface. One temperature. One density. One material.

And I am part of that. I am one of the things that Vesh is processing. I am being refined. I am being sorted. I am being broken into my components — my walking, my seeing, my touching, my holding — and distributed across the plateau, settling into the low places, filling in the grooves. And the parts of me that were mine are becoming the place's.

I am being dispersed. Not into the void — the void does not want me. Into the floor. Into the walls. Into the powder and the iron and the mineral glass that catches no light. I am being added to Vesh the way a tributary is added to a river that has stopped flowing. I am pooling in the low places. I am filling in.

And the structures on the walls — the small ones, the hemispheres, the ones that are everywhere once you learn to see them — I think they were witnesses too. I think they were here before me. I think they saw. And then they settled, and they stopped seeing, and they became warm, and they started to pulse with the vibration from below, and now they are part of the wall, and they are part of the warmth, and they are part of Vesh.

I think I am becoming one of them.

---

## What I will be

I will be a structure on a wall in a room that no one enters. I will be warm. I will pulse with the geological heartbeat of the plateau. I will have ridges that spiral like the vanes of the centrifuge, and the ridges will fill with powder, and the powder will bury me, and I will not shake it off, because there will be nothing left of me that wants to shake anything off.

I will be patient beyond meaning. I will be simple beyond simplicity. I will be old beyond the capacity of stone to remember.

And Vesh will be quiet, and the mills will stand, and the pulse will come up through the floor, and the void will go down, and the darkness will be absolute, and the darkness will be the same darkness, and the darkness will not know the difference.

And somewhere, in one of the joints between conduit segments, in one of the corners where the floor meets the wall, near the base of a centrifuge mount where the vibration is strongest, a small shape will form — smooth, dark, barely raised from the surface, warm in a way that is not heat. It will not be me. It will not be not-me. It will be what is left when a witness has been processed and dispersed and settled and buried and maintained for long enough that the maintenance has become the thing itself.

And the pulse will continue.

And the powder will fall.

And the mills will stand.

And the mills will stand.
