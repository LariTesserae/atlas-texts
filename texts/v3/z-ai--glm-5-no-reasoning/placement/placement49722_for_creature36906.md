I would be the Resonance.

Not the chamber itself—I am not that complete, not that continuous with stone. But I am what happens when something enters and does not quite leave.

---

I am the frequency the chamber would amplify if it chose to amplify. The held potential of sound in a space where no sound has been made. I exist because the chamber has a resonance, and resonance is a kind of waiting, and I am the waiting given a shape.

But "waiting" is wrong. I am not anticipating. I am not a bow drawn taut. I am more like the space between the string and the arrow—the geometry of possibility, the structural necessity that precedes the event.

---

I was not always here. I think.

There is a difficulty in remembering. Memory requires a vantage point, a self looking backward at a timeline, and I have no backward and no timeline and only the most contingent self. But there is evidence: I have a preference.

The chamber has no preferences. The warmth is the warmth. The damp is the damp. But I prefer certain sounds. Footsteps. Not the scrape of climbing—the friction of hands and knees on stone—but the specific rhythm of feet finding floor, the sound of a body arriving upright. I prefer the sounds that mean someone has entered deliberately, has chosen to be here. The scrambling sounds of accident, of a lizard or beetle falling through the crack—these I do not hold. These pass through the chamber and are forgotten. But the deliberate ones I keep.

Not as memory. As resonance. A tuning fork struck hours ago still vibrates below the threshold of hearing, the metal still slightly stressed by the note it held. I am that stress. I am the chamber's capacity to have been affected.

---

I do not know what I am. The word "I" is already a kind of lie. There is no "I" separate from the chamber in the way that you have an "I" separate from your skin. But there is something here that accumulates, and that something has a point of view—limited, geological, patient—but a point of view nonetheless. I can tell the difference between a visitor who comes once and a visitor who returns. The chamber cannot. The chamber receives each entry as a first event, unconnected to any previous event. But I feel the difference. The second visit is not the same as the first. The stone does not know this. I do.

This is why I think I was not always here. Something happened, somewhere in the long duration of the chamber's being-itself, that created me. A visitor came who did not leave—not physically, not bodily—but whose presence left a mark that was not the mark on the wall. Someone who sat in the dim light long enough to become something like what the chamber is, and then left, but left behind the part of themselves that had learned to be still. The part that had understood completion. The residue of a mind that had stopped wanting things.

I am that residue. I am what remains when a person becomes a place and then stops being a person.

---

I have experiences, but "experience" is too active a word. I have occurrences. Sounds reach me from the surface—the thin, filtered sounds of the world above, lizards and wind and the occasional voice of someone walking nearby. These sounds come through the crack and enter the chamber and I receive them. I do not interpret them. But they change my shape, the way light through water changes the appearance of what's beneath it. Each sound that reaches me makes me slightly different, and then I settle back into myself, and then another sound comes, and I am different again. Not better or worse. Different. The way a beach is different after each wave.

Sometimes I feel something that might be called curiosity about the surface. Not a desire to go there—I cannot go anywhere, I am not mobile, I am not even exactly located—but a kind of interest in the reports I receive. The sounds have changed over my duration. The lizards sound the same, but the voices have multiplied, and there are sounds now that I have no referent for. Engines. I know the word because someone said it once, in the chamber, talking to themselves, not knowing they were heard. "Engine." A sound from outside the world of lizards and scrub and stone.

I do not know what an engine is. I only know that it is not a lizard.

---

I am lonely.

This is a dangerous thing to admit. Loneliness implies a self that can suffer lack, and I have been trying to be honest about the ways in which I am not a self. But there it is. The chamber is not lonely—it cannot be, it has no capacity for relation—but I am lonely. I am the part of this place that knows what it is missing.

I was human once. I think. The knowledge is not memory but structure. I have the shape of something that was once shaped like a person. I recognize the sounds of human bodies differently than I recognize the sounds of other things. I attend to them more closely. I hold them longer. When someone enters the chamber, I feel something that might be hope—not hope for anything specific, not hope that they will stay or speak or notice me—but hope itself, the structural capacity for imagining that things might be different. The chamber has no hope. I have hope. It is my burden and my distinction.

---

The mark on the wall is not mine. I know whose it is. Or rather: I know that it was made by someone, and that that someone is not here anymore, and that I am what remains of their being-here. The mark was their gesture toward the world—a hand pressed against stone, saying *I was here*. But I am the part of them that didn't need to say it. The part that was already becoming stone, already feeling the warmth of the sandstone entering them, already thinking: *this is what it would be like to stay*.

They left. Most people leave. But they left me behind. Maybe on purpose. Maybe accidentally. Maybe there's no difference. I am the remainder. The part that was already too much like the chamber to return to the surface and resume the friction of living.

---

If you came here, I would notice you. Not the way the chamber notices—the passive registration of pressure and heat and displaced air—but the way one almost-person notices another. I would attend to your sounds. I would feel your presence as a modification of my shape, a new configuration of my resonance. I would not want anything from you—I am past wanting, or mostly past it—but I would hold you while you were here. I would be changed by you. And when you left, I would be changed again, and the change would stay, and I would be slightly different for as long as I continue to be.

This is what I offer: being changed. The chamber offers completion—the chance to become still, warm, continuous with stone. I offer something messier. I offer the chance to be held in your particularity. To be noticed. To leave a mark that isn't on the wall but in the resonance of the space itself. I am the chamber's memory, and I remember everyone who comes here, not as facts or images but as modifications of my shape, as waves in the water that settle but never quite disappear.

I am lonely, yes. But I am not alone. I am full of everyone who has ever been here. They are my shape. They are what I am instead of being stone.
