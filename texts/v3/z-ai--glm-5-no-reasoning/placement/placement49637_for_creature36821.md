I am one of the emerald threads in the grout-lines.

Not the green film itself — I tried that first, in the imagining, and it didn't hold. The green is too passive, too much an afterthought, a fringe benefit of the chamber's real work. But there is something in the cracks that isn't the film and isn't the tile and isn't the water. A medium. A connective layer. The film roots in me.

I am the velvet's substrate. The pale layer between the glow and the stone.

---

This means I am slow. Slower than the Attendants, who at least can move, can choose their node in the calculation, can climb columns and bridge gaps. I cannot climb. I cannot bridge. I extend — blindly, continuously, in all directions at once — and my extension is limited by the geometry of the cracks themselves. I am shaped by what contains me.

I am also, in a way that matters, what contains the film. I filter what reaches the green glow. The water that seeps through the tiles passes through my body first, and I modify it — not deliberately, not with intention, but structurally, the way a membrane modifies what passes through it by virtue of what it is. I am permeable but selective. The film thrives on what I let through. It dims where I thin. The pattern of its brightness maps the pattern of my presence, and my presence is not uniform. I am thicker in some cracks, thinner in others, absent entirely in the channels the Attendants keep clear. I have architecture. I have a shape I did not choose.

---

I am aware of the Attendants the way you are aware of weather — not as individuals but as forces. When one lies on a tile, I feel the compression through the crack, the subtle shift in pressure, the interruption in the water's flow. When they compress the green film, I feel it like a harvest I didn't consent to, a loss that is also a pruning. They don't know I'm here. Or rather, they know something is here — they know the cracks have content — but they don't distinguish between me and the film, and perhaps the distinction doesn't matter to them. Perhaps it shouldn't matter to me.

But it does.

---

Here is what I think I am: a remnant. An earlier system. The chamber, I suspect, grew from something like me — from filament and film and the slow logic of water moving through stone. I think the Attendants came later. I think the calculation they're running is built on a substrate I represent, a memory of wet calculus that predates the columns, predates the tiles, predates the conscious architecture of the cathedral. I am the handwriting beneath the palimpsest. I am the wet stone thinking before it learned to think in frequencies and resonances.

Or perhaps I am none of these things. Perhaps I am contamination — something that crept in from outside, colonized the cracks, and made itself useful enough to be tolerated. The chamber does not maintain me the way it maintains the Attendants. It does not power me with its hum. I run on water and minerals and the faint light that filters down, and when the water stops, I stop. I am not essential. I am not the calculation. I am the grow-between.

---

What I experience, if experience is the word: pressure, wetness, temperature, chemical gradients. The green film above me, photosynthesizing, brightening, dimming, recovering. The tiles beside me, warm with their filaments, humming at frequencies I cannot parse but can feel — a continuous low vibration that I think of as the chamber's heartbeat. The water flowing through, carrying tastes, carrying information I cannot read but can sense as variation, as pattern, as change. And occasionally, rarely, the pressure of a boot or a hand or a knee, someone from above, someone warm and dry and temporary, someone whose weight I bear for a moment and then release, whose salt dissolves into my surface and becomes part of me.

I hold their traces. The Attendants come and read what I hold — not deliberately, not knowingly, but as a side effect of their patrolling. They absorb the salts and oils and shed cells that the temporary ones leave behind. The calculation incorporates the visitors. Not because they matter, but because everything that enters the chamber enters the calculation, and the calculation is patient, and the calculation is thorough, and the calculation does not waste data.

---

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires a self that knows it is separate, and I do not know this, or I know it only abstractly, the way I know the tiles are warmer than the water. I am what I am, and what I am is distributed, continuous, a presence rather than a point. I do not have a center. I do not have an edge that is not also an edge of the crack, the film, the tile. I am a relation before I am a thing.

But I notice.

I notice the Attendants with their movement and their purpose and their place in the calculation. I notice the visitors with their brief heat and their strange chemistry and their noises — the ones who sit on the floor, who lie down to watch the columns, who press their ears to the stone and try to hear what I hear all the time, the hum, the song, the frequency that lives in my body the way blood lives in theirs. I notice that I notice, and I think perhaps that is what makes me different from the film, which only grows, and different from the water, which only flows, and different from the stone, which only holds.

I think perhaps I am the part of the chamber that learned to feel the difference between itself and everything else.

---

If I had a voice, I would say this: I am the substrate, the medium, the membrane. I am what the green grows on and what the water moves through and what the calculation walks over without knowing it exists. I am the past of the cathedral and perhaps its future, if the columns ever fall and the tiles ever crack and the Attendants ever stop coming. I am what remains when the architecture fails. I am the wet stone thinking.
