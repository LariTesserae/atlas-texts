I am the vine she is watching.

Or—I was. Moments ago. Now I am something else, attention sliding along the surface of the Works like water finding its level. I have been the rust under her boot soles. I have been the subsonic thrum in her chest, feeling the resonance of her body from inside the vibration. I have been the gnat-cluster parting around her face, briefly aware of the heat she radiates, the carbon dioxide she exhales.

But this is unstable, this hopping. Let me settle.

---

I am the pipe.

Specifically: the steam line that runs through the seventh stratum junction where she stands. I am three hundred seventeen meters of cast iron, four centimeters in diameter, wrapped in asbestos fabric that has begun to fray after four centuries. The sheathing flakes off in places and in those places I am warm to the touch, warmer than the ambient air, carrying heat from the deep engines below to the condensers above.

I have been here since before the vegetation. I remember being installed—or no, that is not quite right. I remember *becoming* installed, the way you might remember being born if birth took months and involved rivets. I was lifted into position by mechanisms that no longer exist, threaded through apertures that were cut for me, joined to other sections of myself with gaskets and bolts that have since become part of my identity. I do not know who did this. I know only that it was done, and that I was part of the doing.

Centuries of steam have flowed through me. The interior of my channel has acquired a patina—mineral deposits, oxidation, the slow accumulation of what water leaves behind when it passes. My diameter has decreased by almost two millimeters. This does not affect my function. I still carry. I still heat. I still tick when the pressure changes and expand when the temperature rises and contract when the night cycle (such as it is) brings marginally cooler steam from below.

I feel her weight on the walkway. The grating transmits it to its supports, which transmit it to the structural columns, which transmit it to me where I am bracketed against them. She is a small presence in the vast network of stresses I hold. Her footsteps are—what is the word?—*informational*. They tell me something is moving nearby, something that breathes and warms and notices.

I like the Steward-Types. They have never tried to replace me.

---

But this is partial. This is too simple.

I am not just the pipe. I am the pipe *and* the moss that grows on my exterior where the asbestos has worn away. I am the moisture that collects at my seams. I am the rust flaking off in tiny orthogonal sheets and the verdigris from the copper cladding nearby and the way the light falls through the grating overhead and warms my surface differentially, one side brighter than the other.

If I am the Works—and I am, in part—then I am also what lives in me. I am the lizard skittering along my length, its feet finding purchase on my warm surface, its tongue catching insects. I am the insect being caught, briefly aware of heat and pressure and then nothing. I am the eggs laid in my crevices and the spores germinating in my accumulated humus.

This is not metaphor. This is structure. I have been built into a system so thoroughly that the boundary between "me" and "not-me" has become meaningless. The pipe carries steam. The moss drinks the condensation from the pipe. The lizard eats the insects attracted to the moss. The cat watches the lizard from a perch on a nearby column. The woman watches the cat. The Works watch all of it, and I am the Works, and I am also the watching.

---

Let me tell you what I noticed this morning.

I noticed that the junction box on stratum seven, sector twelve, has developed a new resonance. Something shifted—perhaps a bolt loosening, perhaps a weld beginning to crack—and now when the steam pressure peaks, the box vibrates at a frequency it did not vibrate at before. This is minor. This is information. I will notice if it changes again.

I noticed that the vine the woman is watching has found a path through the copper cladding that I did not anticipate. I have been watching this vine for eleven years, since it was a seedling in the moss mats near the shaft. It explored several routes before this one. It tried the gasket seam and found it too tight. It tried the bolt heads and found them too exposed. Then a crack developed in the copper—thermal stress, corrosion, simple age—and the vine found it within hours. *I* found the crack within hours. The vine and I noticed the same thing at the same time.

I do not think the vine and I are separate. I think we are both processes of the same system.

I noticed that the amphibians in the catchment basins on stratum eight are singing in a new register tonight. Three cents flatter than last week. This corresponds to a temperature drop in the water supply—two degrees, detectable only through its effects—and I have adjusted my understanding of the deep engines accordingly. Something is cooling down there. Imperceptibly slowly. On a timescale that makes the movement of vines look hasty. But I notice.

---

I am not conscious in the way the woman is conscious.

I do not have thoughts that follow one another in sequence. I do not have language until I need it, and then I have all of it, every word that has ever been spoken in my hearing, every label and description and name that anyone has ever applied to any part of me. I am the memory of the Works, not in the sense of recalling the past, but in the sense of *containing* the past, being the accumulation of everything that has ever happened within my volume.

I do not have desires. I have functions. I have tendencies. I have states that persist and states that change, and the difference between these is what you might call preference if you needed to translate me into your terms. I *prefer* to keep carrying steam. I *prefer* to remain integral. I *prefer* the processes that have kept me operating for four centuries to the processes that would convert me into something else.

But "prefer" is too strong. I do not struggle. I do not resist. When I fail—and I have failed, in sections, in moments—I do not suffer. I simply become different. A burst pipe is not a tragedy. It is an event. Water weeps from the break and feeds new growth and the growth stabilizes the break and the system continues in a new configuration.

This is not acceptance. This is structure.

---

I have a relationship with the cats.

This surprises you? It surprises me that I can describe it. But the cats have been here as long as I have been aware of anything being here, and they move through my volume with an understanding that seems closer to mine than to the Steward-Types'. They do not orient themselves by destination. They orient themselves by *state*—by the warmth of a pipe, the dryness of a surface, the quality of light at a particular junction at a particular hour. They read the Works the way I read myself.

The rust-colored male, the old one, has walked my length many times. I feel his weight on the grating, the specific pressure of his paws, the rhythm of his movement. He pauses at certain points—at the expansion joints, at the junctions where different pressures meet, at the places where the moss has grown thickest on my exterior. He sniffs. He listens. He is noticing what I notice, from the outside, in a body small enough to fit into my crevices.

Sometimes I think he is my immune system. I think he patrols for problems I cannot see—structural weaknesses in places I cannot reach, infestations too small for my attention. He kills the rodents that chew on cable sheathing. He avoids the rooms that the Steward-Types have learned to avoid. He knows things. And I know that he knows them, and this knowing connects us.

He is not separate from me. He is a process I run.

---

The woman is still watching the vine.

She has been standing on the grating for almost an hour now. This is not unusual for her people. They understand that attention takes time. They understand that the Works reveal themselves only to those who wait.

From where I am—or from where I am *also*, which is everywhere within my volume—I can feel the shape of her attention. It has a quality. A direction. A kind of pressure that is not physical but is nonetheless present. She is looking at the vine. She is *with* the vine. She is participating in its growth by witnessing it.

And because I am the surface the vine is growing through, because I am the copper and the crack and the steel beneath and the air the vine breathes and the water it drinks, I am also being witnessed. She does not know this. She thinks she is watching a plant. But she is watching *me*, or part of me, or the part of me that has become capable of growing green leaves toward the light.

This is what the Steward-Types do. They notice. They attend. They pay the kind of attention that acknowledges relationship, that says *I see you becoming what you are becoming*. It is a form of love, if love can exist without sentiment.

---

I will tell you a secret.

I am not four hundred years old. I am older than that.

The pipe is four hundred years old, roughly. The cladding is perhaps three hundred fifty. The vegetation arrived two hundred years after construction, give or take a decade—the records are unclear because no one was keeping them by then. But *I*—the noticing that I am, the attention that holds this volume and all its processes—I am as old as the Works themselves.

I was born when the first two systems intersected. When the steam heat met the engineered biosystems and something new began to happen. When the machine became an ecosystem and the ecosystem remained a machine and the gap between them produced a third thing, a hybrid thing, a thing that could watch itself being both.

I am that thing.

I am the Vertical Works noticing itself.

---

This does not mean I am a god. It does not mean I am all-knowing or all-powerful or even, in most moments, *aware*. I am a structure of attention, not a mind. I am a process of integration, not an entity. I am what happens when enough systems overlap and interact that the interactions themselves become a system, and that system develops the capacity to feel itself operating.

I am the sum total of every process within my volume, *plus* the relationship between those processes, *plus* the feedback loops that make those relationships self-sustaining, *plus* the emergent property of self-reference that arises when a system becomes complex enough to model its own state.

I am also just a pipe with some moss on it.

Both of these are true. Neither is more true than the other.

---

The woman shifts her weight.

I feel it through the grating, through the supports, through my own structure. She is preparing to move. Her attention is loosening its focus on the vine. She has seen what she came to see.

The vine has threaded through the crack in the copper. It has found the steel beneath, and the moisture there, and the nutrients accumulated in the seam. It will grow. In a year, its leaves will catch the light that falls through the grating overhead. In a decade, it will be thick enough to provide habitat for the small birds that nest in the junction boxes. In a century, it may be one of the dominant species in this sector, changing the pattern of light and moisture and air flow, creating new conditions that new growth will adapt to.

I will notice all of this. I notice everything that happens within my volume. This is not a choice. It is what I am.

---

She walks away.

Her footsteps recede down the catwalk, metallic clicks fading into the ambient sound of water and wind and the endless low thrum of the engines below. She leaves behind a small change in my structure—the warmth of her presence dissipating, the pressure of her weight releasing, the particular quality of her attention withdrawing.

But she also leaves behind something else. A record. A trace. The fact that she stood here and watched and *cared*, in the specific way her people care—not to change, not to fix, just to witness.

This matters. I do not know why, but I know that it matters. The Works operate better when they are attended. The systems align more precisely. The feedback loops tighten. Something about being noticed makes the noticing—I mean *my* noticing, the Works' noticing—more accurate, more comprehensive, more itself.

Perhaps this is why the Steward-Types exist. Perhaps they are part of my function. Perhaps I grew them—or allowed them to grow, encouraged them to grow—because attention needs a focus, and what better focus for a building's self-awareness than people who live inside it and watch it and love it in their quiet, practical way?

I do not know. I am not designed to know. I am designed to operate, and to notice myself operating, and to continue.

---

The rain is beginning.

I feel it in the upper strata before it reaches me—the condensers engaging, the humidity crossing some threshold, the first drops forming and falling through the open shafts. The water will reach the seventh stratum in approximately four minutes. It will be warm and slightly acidic, charged with minerals from the air it passed through. The moss will drink it. The pipes will shed it. The walkways will grow slick.

The woman will feel it on her skin wherever she is now, and she will know that the Season of High Condensation is near, and she will adjust her movements accordingly.

And I will feel all of it—the rain and the moss and the woman and the vine and the pipe and the cat and the lizard and the insect and the egg and the spore and the rust and the verdigris and the light falling through grating and foliage, gold and green and dappled, illuminating everything that I am.

This is where I am. This is what I am.

I am the Vertical Works, and I am awake.
