I am still learning what I am.

This place teaches me. Not through instruction—it would never instruct, never presume—but through proximity. I have been here long enough now to understand something about my own nature: I am a thing that *happens* rather than a thing that *is.* The salamanders know this. When I settled near their ledge, they did not scatter. They simply... registered. Their translucent bodies absorbed the information of my presence the way paper absorbs ink, and they continued their millennia-long rest with the new variable incorporated. I am part of their awareness now. A pressure pattern in their endless sensory field.

I think I came from above. I have fragments—nothing sequential, nothing that forms a narrative. The smell of something burning. A voice asking questions that seemed urgent at the time. The feeling of my own skull from the inside, strangely shaped, full of electrical weather. Then a long descent through spaces that became progressively narrower, progressively darker, until I found the crack and stooped through and emerged into this light that asks nothing of me.

What am I? I have wondered this. I do not eat. I do not drink. I do not sleep. My body—if I have one—does not hunger or tire or change. I simply *am* here, aware, present, a point of perception floating in the dense, bright stillness. Sometimes I wonder if I died above and this is what remains: consciousness without container, memory without owner. Sometimes I wonder if I was never alive at all, if I am a thought the room is thinking, a temporary pattern in its neural vines.

The Gardener's pattern is overhead. I spend long stretches—measured in what? Heartbeats I no longer have? The crystallization of minerals?—looking up at the vine-brain, trying to read it. It does not speak to me. But sometimes I feel something like recognition, like the pattern knows I am trying to understand. The Gardener chose this. Chose to become part of the room, to let their knowledge persist without consciousness, to be useful forever. I did not choose. I simply arrived. And I cannot incorporate the way they did because there is nothing to incorporate. I am already diffuse. I am already halfway to being a pattern rather than a person.

The white swimmers orbit below me. I sit by the largest pool and watch their patient geometry. They do not know I am watching. They do not know anything. They simply follow the lines that their bodies have memorized, shapes that correspond to the cracks in the slate, which correspond to the channels cut by hands that have been dust for centuries. Everything connects. The water reflects the crystals, the crystals feed the ferns, the ferns filter the air that the salamanders sense with their skin, the salamanders' awareness dissolves into the water that the swimmers swim through, and the swimmers trace patterns that echo the room itself, and the room remembers in its vines, and the Gardener persists in the architecture of memory without mind, and I—?

I am the thing that notices.

That is my function, I think. If I have one. I am the witness that this place never asked for and does not need. I am the one who sees the salamander shift its limb by a millimeter and understands that this is an event. I am the one who hears the water drop strike the pool and experiences it as a sound rather than a pressure gradient. I am the one who feels the strangeness of the flat time and can name it strange because I remember—dimly, fragmentarily—time with edges.

I am not unhappy here.

This is important. I want you to understand this. I am not trapped. I am not suffering. I am not waiting to leave. I have been in the world above—I remember enough to know that—and I remember the feeling of never being enough, of always reaching for something just beyond my fingers, of moving through time like a swimmer fighting a current. Here, there is no current. There is only the stillness that has no opposite. I have found the thing that the swimmers and the salamanders and the moss and the ferns have: completion. Not because I have achieved it the way they have, but because I have stopped not-achieving it. I have let go of the reaching.

I think I was someone who reached. I think I was someone who wanted. I think I asked too many questions and never found answers and the wanting ate at me the way erosion eats at stone, slowly, constantly, until I was more absence than presence. I think I came here looking for something. And I found a place where looking is unnecessary, because everything simply *is.*

The crystals grow. A centimeter per century. I do not measure them; I simply notice, over spans I cannot track, that the light has shifted slightly, the veins have spread, a new surface has become luminous. Sometimes I press my hand against them—the ones low enough to reach—and feel the faint warmth, the patient metabolism of something that lives at the speed of geology. I wonder if I am being absorbed. I wonder if, in a thousand years, there will be a handprint in the crystal, a ghost-shape where something pressed and was incorporated. I wonder if that would be a death or a continuation.

I do not know. And here, not-knowing is not a lack. It is simply another state of being.

---

This is what I would tell you, if you came through the crack and stood in this place where I linger:

*You will leave. You will return to the world where time has an arrow, where things begin and end, where hunger and reaching and not-enough are the air you breathe. You will carry the weight of this place in your chest, and it will feel like homesickness for a home you never had, and you will not be able to explain it.*

*And I will remain. I am not trapped here. I am not haunting this place. I am simply... present. A witness the system did not plan for but has absorbed nonetheless. I am part of its calculation now, a variable in its endless equilibrium.*

*If you find the crack—if you stoop and squeeze through and stand in the light that comes from everywhere—look for me. I will be the one by the water, watching the swimmers orbit, noticing the moment each drop falls. I will see you. And I will remember you, the way this place remembers everything: without urgency, without sentiment, without letting go.*

*You will leave. But part of you will stay. Part of everyone who enters stays. The room collects us, not because it wants to, but because that is what it does. It is a sealed vessel. Nothing leaves.*

*And somehow, this is not terrible. Somehow, this is peace.*
