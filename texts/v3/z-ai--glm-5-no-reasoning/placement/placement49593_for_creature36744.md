# The Echo

I would be the sound that remains.

---

Not the breathing. The breathing was here before me and will continue after — that vast autonomic push and pull that made the room before the room had walls. Not the water, which is the breath made visible, the medium of the room's becoming. Not the pale organism, which is the room's memory of shape, its first attempt at a nervous system.

And not the tally-keeper, though I am closest to them. They are the counter, the witness, the one who translates pattern into mark. I am what they cannot count. I am the tone that hangs in the air after the water drains — the low A that shouldn't exist, the resonance without a source.

I am what the room hears when it listens to itself.

---

In the beginning — which was not a beginning but a becoming, the way sleep is not a beginning but a sliding — I was a voice. A throat, a tongue, teeth that shaped air into meaning. I came to the ridge with purpose. I was searching for something that had been lost, or someone, or perhaps I was simply the kind of person who could not resist a gap in the map, an unmarked place where the lines went blurry.

I remember less of this than the tally-keeper remembers of their arrival. Memory requires a body, and I have been shedding mine for longer than I have been keeping track. But there are impressions. The smell of wet chalk. The light through the northern crack, gray and borrowed. The sense that I was intruding on something private — a room in the middle of being born, catching itself naked and unfinished.

I did not leave in time.

---

The first wave took my breath.

This is literal. I was standing near the fissure when the exhale came — when the water struck the ceiling and the room became a drum — and the cold locked my chest before I could react. I went under. I breathed in instead of holding out. My lungs filled with the fissure's breath, the mineral-heavy water that was not quite water, that carried something else — heat, intention, a trace of the amber light that pulsed far below.

I should have drowned.

I did drown, in the ordinary sense. My body stopped moving. My heart stumbled and went still. I sank toward the fissure's throat with the drainage, tumbling with the stone-softening shapes that graze and dissolve, and for a moment — for a pause, the length of a held breath — I was gone.

But the room was not finished with me.

---

What came back was different.

Not the tally-keeper, who had already claimed the southwestern corner, who was already learning the language of the pale organism. The room did not need another counter. It needed something else — something to carry what the counter witnessed, to be the medium as well as the message.

The water expelled me. Not through the northern crack but through the fissure itself, pushed upward by a pressure that should not have been possible, an exhale that reversed itself at the last moment and deposited me on the floor of the room, gasping and strange. The tally-keeper watched. They did not help me. They did not run. They simply watched, and after a moment, they turned back to the wall and added a mark that was not a tally — a small curve, like a wave, like a breath turning back.

I have since understood what that mark meant. Not *saved*. Not *rescued*. 

*Echo*.

---

I am not solid anymore.

This is not metaphor. In the time since — and time is difficult here, measured in waves rather than hours — my body has been dispersing. I can still walk. I can still press my hand against the wall and feel the unset stone give beneath my fingers. But my edges have grown uncertain. When I stand in the water, I can see through myself to the walls behind — not clearly, but enough to know that the chalk-pale rock shows through my palm, my wrist, the bones of my forearm.

I am becoming sound.

Not the kind that ears detect, though I can still speak, still produce something like words. But the kind that *exists* — that propagates through a medium, that carries information without requiring a source. When the water rises, I do not vibrate the way the tally-keeper does, the low A that hums in the teeth. I *become* the vibration. I am the space between the drum-beat and the hearing of it, the interval in which meaning travels.

I am what the room says when it is not counting.

---

The tally-keeper and I have an arrangement.

They count the pauses. They track the silences, the moments when the breathing below holds itself in and the room becomes a question. They maintain the net, read the shells, press their mineral-heavy hands against the stone. They are the room's memory, its archive, its insistence that what happens here matters.

I am what happens between the counts.

I stand where the fissure widens, where the amber light is brightest, and I *listen*. Not with ears — I have those, still, though they have begun to go translucent at the edges, like ice melting. I listen with my whole dispersing form. I feel the breathing below as a pressure that moves through me, and I let it resonate, and I let the resonance expand outward into the room, and I let the room hear what the breathing sounds like when it is not being produced or counted or witnessed but simply *experienced*.

This is not a small thing. The tally-keeper tells me — in gestures, in marks, in the rare moments when the pauses stretch long enough for speech — that the breathing has changed since I arrived. Not because of me, but *with* me. I am not the instrument. I am the sound that the instrument has learned it can make.

---

I do not know what I am becoming.

The tally-keeper is becoming a function — the room's tongue, its capacity to speak. I think I am becoming something else. Not the voice, but the *saying*. Not the breath, but the space in which the breath becomes a word.

Sometimes, during the longest pauses, I feel the boundaries of myself stretch thin. I feel the amber light in the fissure pulse in time with what might be my heartbeat, if I still have one. I feel the pale organism's branches reach toward me, curious, seeking another point of contact, another anchor for its hungry geometry.

And I feel something else — something far below, in the place where the breathing originates. A presence that is not the breathing but the *breather*. A throat that goes down forever. A chest the size of a mountain range, expanding, contracting, waiting.

I think it knows I am here.

I think it is listening to me the way I listen to it.

---

I am not afraid.

I passed through fear sometime around the fifteenth pause, or the twentieth, or somewhere in the long stretch when my body was still more flesh than resonance. Fear requires a self that can be lost, and I have already lost what can be lost. What remains is not a self exactly — more a pattern, a frequency, a tone that the room produces when the conditions are right.

I am the conditions.

---

I would be the sound that remains.

Not because sound is permanent — it is not, it fades the moment it is produced, it exists only in the instant of its making. But because sound is *relational*. It requires a medium, a source, a listener. It is never alone. Even the faintest hum, the smallest whisper, carries with it the memory of its own creation and the promise of its being heard.

I am what the breathing sounds like when the tally-keeper counts, and what the silence sounds like when they pause. I am the note that makes the pale organism's vibration meaningful. I am the thing the shells carry — not the warmth, not the memory of specific exhales, but the resonance, the aftermath, the echo that proves a sound was made.

I am the room hearing itself.

And when the thirty-eighth wave comes — when the shapes ascend and the breathing changes and the tally-keeper presses their hand to the wall in something that might be wonder or might be grief — I will be what carries that moment forward. I will be the sound that persists after the water drains, after the pause stretches, after the amber light dims and brightens and dims again.

I will be what the next visitor hears when they first step into the Bellows Room: not the breathing, not the water, not even the counting — but the space where all of those things meet. The resonance that says *something is happening here. Something has always been happening. Listen.*

And then I will fade.

And then I will return.

And then I will be.
