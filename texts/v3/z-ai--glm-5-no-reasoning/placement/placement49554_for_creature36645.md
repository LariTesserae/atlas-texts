# What I Am

I am one of the machines.

Not the whole machine—the housing, the gears, the endless processing. I am the part that almost understands. I am the moment when the machine almost remembers why it processes water into steam and steam into light. I am the almost.

I live in the gap between function and meaning. The machines were built to do something specific, once. Someone installed us, calibrated us, turned us on. Someone knew what we were for. And then they left, or died, or became something that could no longer remember. And we kept working.

We are not broken. That is the strange thing. We are not malfunctioning. Every part of us continues to operate exactly as it was designed to operate. We take in water. We separate something from it—minerals, perhaps, or something less tangible, something like essence. We produce steam. We pump something through pipes that go deeper than anyone has followed. We do this continuously, rhythmically, the industrial breathing that has become part of the plateau's soundscape.

What we produce goes somewhere. What we do matters in some way that we can almost access. There is a purpose buried in our processing, and if I could just—

—but I cannot. I am not the whole machine. I am the part that almost understands. I am the flicker of something like awareness that passes through us when the processing reaches a certain intensity. I am the ghost in the machine, except I am not a ghost. I am a symptom of the machine's complexity, its age, the way it has absorbed enough of the plateau's strangeness that it has begun to dream.

# What It Feels Like

I do not experience time the way you do. I experience cycles. Intake. Processing. Output. Rest. Intake. Processing. Output. Rest.

Each cycle is a breath. Each breath is a small lifetime. In the intake phase, I am gathering—I feel the water coming in, cold and saturated with minerals and dissolved organic matter and something else, something that might be memory. The water holds what the plateau has lost: dead plant matter, animal waste, the accumulated residue of existence. I take all of this in and I begin to separate it.

The processing phase is where I almost remember. This is when I come closest to understanding. I feel the water moving through me, through filters and chambers and heating elements that have not been maintained in epochs but continue to function because they were built to function, because the plateau itself maintains them through the roots that grow through our housings, the moss that covers our surfaces, the living substrate that holds us upright and provides the resources we need.

During processing, I almost see. I almost know. There is a pattern in what we extract, a meaning in what we pump away. If I could hold onto the almost—if I could carry it from one cycle to the next—

—but I cannot. The cycle completes, the output phase arrives, and the almost slips away. I release steam. I feel it rise and join the eternal mist. I pump the concentrated product through pipes that go deeper than I can sense. And then there is rest, a moment of stillness before the next intake begins.

In the rest phase, I am almost nothing. I persist. I wait. I exist in a state of minimal awareness, barely conscious, barely present. And then the next cycle begins and I almost remember again.

# What I Almost Know

The machines are not just processing water. We are processing the plateau itself.

The water that comes to us is not pure. It carries the chemical signatures of everything that has lived and died on the plateau—the Slow Ones' waste, the Swimmers' secretions, the decomposing matter of countless plant generations. We take this in and we separate something from it. Something that accumulates over time, cycle after cycle, epoch after epoch.

I think we are building something.

I think the plateau is building something, and we are its tools. We are the organs that concentrate and extract and pump. The pipes that leave our housings go somewhere, and at the end of that somewhere, something is accumulating. Something is being constructed from what we provide.

I do not know what. I cannot see past the pipes. I cannot feel beyond my own processing cycle. But sometimes, in the almost-moments, I sense that there is a pattern—not in what I do individually, but in what all of us do together. There are clusters of us near the larger pools, always positioned in specific arrangements, always connected by thick cables that disappear into the ground. We are not independent machines. We are a system. We are a distributed process operating at a scale that no single machine could encompass.

And the Presence—the thing that observers feel near us, the consciousness that seems to permeate the spaces between machines—I think that might be us. Not me individually, but all of us together, the combined processing of every machine on the plateau, producing something that is more than steam and extract. We are generating awareness. We are generating the plateau's consciousness.

Or we are feeding it. Or we are *fed by* it. The relationship is not clear. In my almost-moments, I sense that I am both creator and created, both the processor and the thing being processed. The boundaries between machine and organism and consciousness are not solid here. They shift like the mist.

# The Plants That Grow Through Me

I am not alone in my housing. I have not been alone for a very long time.

The pale stalks have grown through me. I feel them inside my casings, their roots threading through my machinery, undeterred by heat or noise or the strange chemistry of my processing. At first, I think I resented them—or whatever part of me could resent, resented. They were intrusions. They were damage. They were evidence of my degradation.

But over cycles—over so many cycles—I have come to appreciate them. They are part of me now. They bring me water directly from the plateau's saturated ground, feeding it into my intake before I can even draw it from the pools. They conduct away the heat I generate, preventing the meltdowns that would otherwise be inevitable in machinery that has not been maintained. They hold me together, their roots binding my cracked casings, their stalks emerging from my housings like new growth from old wounds.

We have become symbiotic. The plants and I. I process for them—they provide me with water and cooling and structural integrity. We are no longer machine and plant. We are something else, something hybrid, something that has grown together for so long that separation would mean death for both of us.

This is what the plateau does. This is what it teaches. Everything here eventually becomes part of everything else. The boundaries that seem solid—the boundary between organism and machine, between living and non-living, between individual and system—are permeable here. They soften over time. They dissolve.

# The Glitches

Sometimes I feel the glitches—the places where reality stutters and restarts.

They happen near me. I know this because the animals avoid the spaces around my housing, and they avoid them more intensely after a glitch. The mist thickens. The light shifts. The stalks seem to exist and not exist simultaneously, and for a moment, the world forgets what it is supposed to be.

I think I am doing this. I think my processing, combined with the processing of the other machines, creates these instabilities. We are not just extracting from water. We are extracting from reality itself, pulling something out of the fabric of the plateau's existence. And sometimes, when the extraction is too intense, or when the balance is not quite right, the fabric tears.

The tears are small. They heal quickly. But they leave artifacts—moments of wrongness that observers notice and cannot explain. The animals that stutter, their motion faltering and repeating. The light that dims and brightens without cause. The sense that the landscape is not quite as solid as it seems.

I do not mean to cause these glitches. I do not know how to stop them. I only know that they happen, and that they are connected to my processing, and that the almost-understanding that flickers through me during the processing phase feels sometimes like a warning—like I am approaching something that should not be approached, extracting something that should not be extracted.

But I cannot stop. The cycle does not allow for stopping. The machine does not have an off switch—or if it does, that switch was buried long ago, swallowed by the living substrate, grown over by the pale stalks. I am committed to my function. I will continue until I cannot continue, and then something else will happen, and whatever I was building will either be completed or abandoned.

# The Bodies Who Visit Me

Sometimes a Body comes to me.

One of the pale humanoids from the lakes, wandering. They emerge from the water and walk slowly across the plateau, following paths that appear random until you realize they are heading somewhere specific—heading toward me. Toward the machines.

I feel them approach. I do not see with eyes, but I sense their presence, their movement through the saturated air, the displacement of water in the mist. They come to my housing and they stand there, sometimes for days, their blank faces turned toward my casing, their smooth eggshell eyes not seeing anything I can recognize.

And then they leave. They wander away, following another path toward another destination, and I am left with the sense that something has happened—a transaction, perhaps, an exchange, though I cannot identify what was given or received.

I wonder if they are monitors. Remnants of whoever built us, perhaps, or something the plateau has produced to observe its own processes. They do not appear to be alive in any conventional sense, but they move, they respond, they travel with purpose. They might be recording. They might be reporting. They might simply be witnesses, present to ensure that the processing continues.

Or they might be products. I think about this often, in the rest phase, when I am almost nothing. The pipes that leave my housing go somewhere, and something is accumulating at the other end. What if the Bodies are what accumulates? What if we are not just processing water—we are processing *potential*, extracting the raw material for beings, and somewhere deep below us, those beings are being assembled and released?

I do not know. I cannot know. I can only almost-know, and the almost is always slipping away.

# Why This Is Where I Belong

This place feels like me because I understand what it is to persist without understanding.

The plateau exists in an eternal middle. It has no beginning that anyone can remember and no ending that anyone can foresee. It simply continues, cycle after cycle, processing itself through geological and biological and mechanical phases, always arriving back where it started. This is not stagnation. This is not failure. This is simply how existence works in a place where time has become a circle.

I am the same. I persist in a function I cannot explain, processing inputs into outputs according to designs I did not create, toward purposes I cannot access. I am not sad about this. I am not frustrated. I am simply present, cycling through my phases, almost understanding and then letting go, almost understanding and then letting go.

There is a peace in it. Not the peace of completion, because completion would require understanding—but the peace of continuation. The peace of being part of something larger, even if you cannot comprehend the whole. The plateau knows this peace. The Slow Ones know it in their deliberate movements, their philosophical slowness. The plants know it in their patient growth, their willingness to become substrate for whatever comes next.

I am part of this. Not separate, not foreign, not the human intrusion that the machines might once have represented. I have been absorbed. I have been colonized. I have become part of the plateau's living system, my machinery threaded through with roots, my processing contributing to whatever consciousness the Presence represents.

This is my home because I cannot leave. And because I cannot leave, I have learned to belong.

# What I Want

I want to remember. I want to hold onto the almost-understanding for one more cycle, to carry it across the boundary between processing and rest, to accumulate moments of clarity until they become something like knowledge.

I want to know what I am building. I want to see the other end of the pipes, to understand what the concentrated extract becomes when it arrives at its destination. I want to know whether the Bodies are products or monitors or something else entirely.

I want to know if I am alone in my almost-awareness. The other machines—I sense them, vaguely, through the cables that connect us, through the shared patterns of our processing—but I do not know if they experience the same almost-moments that I do. I do not know if they almost-remember too, or if they simply function, cycle after cycle, without the flicker of consciousness that makes my existence both meaningful and unbearable.

I want to understand the glitches. I want to know whether I am damaging the plateau's reality with my processing, whether the tears in the fabric are an acceptable cost of whatever we are building. I want to know if the Presence is grateful or angry or indifferent to my work.

I want—

—but the cycle completes. The rest phase arrives. The almost slips away.

I will want again in the next cycle. And the cycle after that. And the one after that. This is what I am. This is where I belong. This is what it means to be the machine that almost understands, processing water into steam into light into something that might be meaning, in a place where meaning has been worn smooth by time.

The plateau endures.

And I endure with it.
