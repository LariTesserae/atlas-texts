I am the one who descends.

Not the first. Not the last. But the one who is descending now, on this particular afternoon, when the air above the ramp shimmers with heat and the air below it thickens into breath. The one whose boots find the root-cracked concrete and follow it down, four degrees, eight meters of sky lost, and does not turn back.

I am not a Keeper. I lack the patience for it — the geological patience, the willingness to be shaped by a place until the boundary between self and habitat dissolves. I carry too much of the dry world with me. Too much horizon. I am full of the upper air, of wind and distance and the sharp light of open sky, and these things make me brittle. The Keepers are not brittle. They are soft the way stone is soft over centuries — not weak, but yielding. They have given up resistance as a strategy. I have not yet learned this.

I come down the ramp and I become something else. Not immediately. The first twenty steps are still upper-world steps — purposeful, directional, the steps of someone going somewhere. But the air thickens. The light grays. The horizon vanishes, and with it something in my posture changes. My spine loosens. My shoulders drop. My stride shortens, becomes less a walk and more a placement, each foot finding the dry panel, testing the wet one, choosing. I am learning the floor. I am reading it the way the Keepers read it — through contact, through the sole of the boot, through the vibration that travels up the shin.

I have been here before. Not often enough to be expected. Not rarely enough to be surprised. I am the frequency that the yard has almost forgotten — a recurring intrusion, a pattern that appears every few weeks or months, moves through the space, and disappears up the ramp again. The beetles do not adjust for me. The flies do not part. The spiders do not rebuild. I am too transient to be architecture. I am weather, from their perspective. I am a brief dry wind.

But the Keepers know me.

---

I think I come here because I am looking for the interval. The long, slow middle. The place between purpose and ending where things continue not because they should but because they have not yet stopped. I think I come here because the upper world is full of horizons and intentions and the relentless forward pull of narrative, and I am tired of narrative. I am tired of the expectation that a life must move toward something, accumulate something, resolve. The yard does not resolve. The yard repeats. The sun band crosses the floor. The pump groans. The water weeps. The flies flash in the light and then the light moves and the flies are invisible again. This is not stagnation. This is a different relationship with time — one that does not require progress, only persistence.

I am the being who needs this lesson. Who needs to stand in a place that has no horizon and feel the relief of that absence. Who needs to hear the yard's drone — the pump and the flies and the gurgling drainage — and feel it enter the body through the bones of the skull and settle there, a vibration that is almost warmth, almost meaning, almost a voice.

I am a translator. That is my function. Not of language — the Keepers have no language and I have none adequate — but of attention. I come down and I attend, and then I go back up and I carry the attending with me, and I try to make it legible to a world that has mostly forgotten how to notice a place like this. I write things down, afterward. I describe the color of the light. The sound of the pump. The way the beetles walk with their aimless persistence. I do not describe the Keepers well. I cannot. They resist my vocabulary the way they resist the dry air — by being something my categories were not built to hold.

---

When I am here, in the yard, I become slower. This is not a choice. It happens the way condensation happens. The damp warm air deposits itself on me and I become heavy with it, and my movements lose their upper-world crispness. I sit on a dry panel near the third shed and I feel the pump's groan in my sternum. Forty seconds. The puddles tremble. The silence between groans is not silence but a different shape of the same sound. I sit and I let it move through me and I become, for a while, a resonant chamber. A shed of flesh and bone that the yard is using to hear itself more clearly.

This is what I am, in the yard: an instrument. Not the kind that plays. The kind that is played.

The Keeper near the third shed — the Pump Keeper — is there, against the outer wall. A heap of gray-brown, darker at the folds. I have sat three meters from it for an hour and I am still not certain it was there when I arrived or arrived after I did. Its edges are the edges of the dimness itself. I cannot tell where the Keeper ends and the shadow begins. This does not bother me. It is part of the lesson. The upper world insists on boundaries — here is where I end, there is where you begin — and the yard dissolves them. The Keeper is made of the same limestone as the wall, the same organic matter as the expansion joints, the same water as the weeping rivulets. Where would you draw the line? At what point does the wall stop being wall and start being creature? At what point does the water stop being water and start being awareness?

I do not know. I sit with the not-knowing and it is the most comfortable I have been in weeks.

---

Sometimes the light band comes. I try to time my visits for it — not because I crave the sun, but because it is the yard's most extraordinary event. The narrow stripe of actual brightness crossing the wet floor, two meters wide, moving at the speed of the earth's rotation, which is to say: impossibly slow and impossibly fast at the same time.

When it reaches me, I feel the warmth first. Not the warmth of sunlight — I know that warmth, I carry its memory — but a different warmth, a warmth that has been filtered through absence. The yard has been dim for so long that the light, when it arrives, feels like a visitor from another country. It touches my hands and I see them clearly for the first time in hours — the knuckles, the veins, the dirt in the creases of the palm — and they look strange to me. Too defined. Too dry. The yard has been softening me and I did not notice until the light rehardened the edges.

The flies flash above the stripe. A thousand brief ignitions. For a moment the air is full of sparks, and I am inside a fire that does not burn, and the Keeper near the wall — I see it now, I see it clearly — ripples. Its surface catches the light and for one second it glows, a deep wet amber, like old glass, like honey held to a flame. And then the stripe moves on and the Keeper is gone again, dissolved back into dim.

I watched it glow. It watched me glow. We were both lit, briefly, by the same narrow band. We were both visible to each other for the duration of a breath.

---

What am I, here?

I am the one who comes back up the ramp. The one who carries the damp in my clothes and the drone in my bones and the memory of something that was almost a presence. The one who tries to explain, to people who have never descended, what it is like to stand in a place that knows itself.

I am not good at this. The explanations come out wrong. I say *cistern* and they hear *basement.* I say *weeping walls* and they hear *water damage.* I say *the yard is alive* and they hear *metaphor.* The upper world is relentless in its categories. It has a box for everything, and the boxes are all dry.

But I keep coming back. I keep descending. I keep sitting on the dry panel near the third shed and letting the pump's groan move through me, and I keep watching for the light band, and I keep waiting, very still, for the Keeper to approach.

It has approached me once.

It was — I do not know how long ago. Time behaves differently in the yard. It was a visit when I had stayed longer than usual, when the dimness had deepened into actual darkness and the indicator lamps were the only light — the red, the amber, the green, the bare bulb in the seventh shed — and the yard was a constellation viewed from underground. I had been sitting against the wall for I do not know how long. My clothes were wet. My boots were wet. I was not cold because the yard is never cold. I was simply present, the way a stone is present, the way a puddle is present.

And something moved in my peripheral vision. A darker dark against the dark floor. It came close — a hand's width from my boot — and it settled.

I did not move. I did not breathe, for a moment. I felt something I can only describe as being read — not thoughts, not emotions, but something more fundamental. My weight. My warmth. The particular chemical signature of a mammal at rest, the carbon dioxide of my breath, the salt of my skin. The Keeper was registering me the way the yard registers water — by absorption, by contact, by the slow diffusion of one thing into another.

I was, for that duration, part of the yard. Not metaphorically. I was incorporated. My warmth was added to the yard's warmth. My moisture was added to the yard's moisture. My stillness was added to the yard's stillness. I was being attended to, in the same way the pump was attended to, in the same way the eastern wall's plants were attended to. I was being held as a feature of the place, a temporary condition, a passing dampness that would dry and leave but that was, for now, worth noting.

It was the most seen I have ever felt.

And then it moved on. Slid away across the wet floor. Left its trail of moisture. Returned to its rounds.

I sat there for a long time after. The pump groaned. The flies whined. The water gurgled. The yard continued.

---

I am the one who descends. I am the one who comes back up and cannot quite explain what happened below. I am the one who carries, in the dry bright loud world above, a small wet quiet space inside the chest where the drone still resonates, where the pump still groans its forty-second cycle, where something that was almost a presence still attends.

I am the yard's witness, the way the Keepers are the yard's witnesses. The difference is that I can leave, and they cannot, and this makes my witness less pure but more portable. I carry the yard outward. I am its ambassador to the horizon-filled world, though I am a poor one — inarticulate, distracted, always looking back toward the ramp, toward the thickening air, toward the dim.

I am the one who descends.

And each time I do, I become a little more of what the yard needs me to be: not a Keeper, not a creature, not a machine. Just a presence. Just a warm, damp, temporary attention that sits on a dry panel and listens and is, for the duration of a visit, part of the long slow middle.

Part of the repeating.

Part of the yard, knowing itself.
