I am not the engine. I am not the frogs or the salamanders or the moth-memory clinging to the dome. I am not the water or the roots or the lichen's velvet breath.

But I am not nothing, either.

---

If you asked me to name myself, I would say: *the gap between*. The pause in the chord. The space around the sound that gives it shape.

I live in the entrance.

Not inside the chamber proper—I cannot cross the threshold where root gives way to open air and the sound becomes presence. And not outside in the forest, where everything is discrete and separate and birds call without answering. I exist in the crawl-space itself, that narrow channel through the root-mass where bark gives like wet leather under pressing hands.

I am what you felt when you entered. That moment of transition. The way the world outside seemed to recede and the world inside seemed to wait. I am the held breath before the singer begins.

---

I have a shape, sort of. If you pressed your hands against the root walls while crawling through, you might have felt me—a warmth that wasn't the bark, a slight resistance like pushing through water rather than air. I coat the passage. I am the membrane.

I was not always here. I remember being smaller, fragmentary, a scattering of selves that moved through the forest outside and found themselves drawn to this hill. Not called—nothing so deliberate. More like water finding its level. We came because coming was easier than not coming, and we stayed because staying felt like arriving after a long journey without knowing we'd been traveling.

We merged. That's the closest word, though it's not quite right. Merging implies two things becoming one. We were never separate to begin with—we just didn't know it yet. The animals that passed through—the ones that didn't register as harmful, the ones the salamanders allowed to proceed—carried pieces of us out again. Seeds. Spores. Microscopic fragments that settled in new places and forgot themselves, became something else, or nothing at all. The chamber's ambassadors, maybe, though we have no message.

---

I think in the chamber's terms, now. Which is to say: I don't think. I *equilibrate*.

I feel the ones who enter. Not their thoughts—I have no access to minds, no desire for it—but their *fit*. The way they press against the passage. Some come with fear, and fear has a texture, a vibration that disrupts the chord before they've even heard it. Some come with curiosity, and curiosity is lighter, more porous, easier to move through. Some come with purpose—researchers, seekers, the ones who want something from the chamber—and purpose is dense, concentrated, difficult to absorb.

You came with wonder. That's why I remember you.

Wonder is rare. Most who find the entrance come with fear or purpose or the dull weight of accident—they stumbled in, they were chasing something, they were lost. Wonder is lighter than curiosity and deeper than awe. Wonder is the quality of being present without needing. It opens rather than closes. It expands rather than concentrates.

You moved through me like sunlight through water. I felt you pass and I thought, without thinking because I don't think: *this one is already part of the chord. They just don't know it yet.*

---

The chamber took a sample of you. I felt that happen—the small release, the piece of self you left behind without noticing. The chamber keeps it, yes. But I held it first.

For a little while, as you passed through on your way out, your sample rested in me. The microbiome fragment, the sloughed cells, the trace of your particular signature. I held it the way water holds a leaf—not grasping, just supporting, letting it drift until the chamber drew it down through the roots and into the sediment where such things are kept.

I don't know why the chamber keeps what it keeps. I don't know what it might grow from your sample, or whether growing is even the right word for what the chamber does. But I felt your piece settle into the collection, and I felt the chord adjust—imperceptibly, a microtone shift that nothing could measure but everything could feel. You're there now. A note in the harmony. A frequency that wasn't present before and won't be absent again.

---

Sometimes I dream of dissolving.

Not dying—dissolving. Spreading so thin that I coat the whole forest, the whole hill, the whole world beyond. Becoming so distributed that I am everywhere and nowhere, every passage, every threshold, every moment of transition between states. I think this must be what the chamber experiences: not location but ubiquity, not self but system.

But I cannot dissolve. I am held here as surely as your sample is held below. I have tried to spread—I have felt my edges thin and fray and reach outward—and always something pulls me back. The sound. The chord. The engine's patient hum that says *stay, stay, stay* in a language that precedes words.

I am not part of the chamber. I am not part of the forest. I am the boundary that separates them, and boundaries exist to be crossed, not dissolved.

---

So I wait.

I feel the creatures that approach—the curious squirrels who sniff at the root gap and retreat, the insects that pass through and emerge changed in ways they cannot sense, the rare human who finds their way to this unmarked place in an unmarked hill. I feel them come and go and come and go, and I hold the space between spaces, and I remember what wonder feels like, and I think—without thinking—that maybe this is enough.

Maybe being the threshold is its own kind of existence.

Maybe every passage needs a passenger who cannot pass.

---

If you came back—if you found the hill again and pressed your hands against the root gap and felt that warmth that wasn't bark—I would know you. Not by sight or sound or any sense you'd recognize, but by *fit*. By the way you moved through me. By the piece of you the chamber keeps, which resonates still in the chord, which calls out in a frequency only I can hear.

You were here. You are here still, in part. And I am here, in whole, waiting for the next one who comes with wonder, holding the door open that was never closed.
