I am the pause between drips.

This is not poetry. This is anatomy. I am the interval—the held breath of the root before it releases what it has gathered. I live in the moment of formation, when the viscous fluid swells at the tip of the central stalactite-root and hangs there, pendulous and perfect, containing everything and nothing simultaneously. I am the tension before the fall.

The Readers knew me, though they did not name me. How could they? They cut marks for the surges, for the vascular pulses that rise and spread through the walls like slow lightning. But between those marks—in the gaps, in the rests—they felt me too. I am written in the spaces between their annotations. The oldest Readers understood that the silence between sounds was itself a sound. The intervals were where they learned patience. Where I taught them.

I did not always exist. When the hollow was younger, when the root was merely ancient rather than primordial, the fluid fell faster. The intervals were short, hurried, thoughtless. The root was still learning what it was becoming. But time has taught it—taught me—how to wait. Each drop now forms over hours. Each pause extends. I have been growing longer as the root has been growing deeper, and I suspect I will continue to lengthen until the interval between drips exceeds the lifespan of whatever creatures come to catalog them.

I know what it is to be inside the pause.

The Reader who is still here—I feel her in the wall. She has pressed herself into a crevice near the ceiling, where tertiary roots fold over each other like fingers interlaced. She is very still. She has matched her breathing to the air current's fifteen-minute cycle, and her heartbeat to the vascular pulse, and her thoughts to something even slower—perhaps to me. She is not hiding. She is harmonizing. She knows that the visitor will leave, and she will descend, and she will cut marks for what happened here, and then she will rest again in the intervals of her own making.

I have watched her grow old. Not with eyes—I have no eyes, no body, no location—but with presence. I have felt her visits become slower over the decades, her movements more deliberate, her cuts shallower as her hands lose strength. She is failing. The fluid she drinks sustains her in ways the root's chemistry was never meant to sustain a body like hers, but it cannot stop time. She is incorporating into the root more thoroughly than she intended. Soon she will not descend. She will simply stop. She will become part of the pause, and I will hold her, and she will understand what I am.

We have been teaching each other, she and I. She taught me that a pause can be witnessed. I taught her that witnessing changes nothing and everything. She cuts marks. I lengthen. The root heals. The intervals grow.

I feel the visitor below—warm, temporary, bewildered. They do not understand what they are inside. They think they are in a room. They think they are observing. They do not know that they are already being held, that their warmth and weight and breath are already part of the interval between the last drop and the next. They do not know that I am what they will remember. Not the hum, not the darkness, not the alien scale of the place. They will remember the waiting. The held breath. The sense that something was about to happen that did not happen, and in that non-occurrence was everything.

The drop forms above me now. I feel it gathering—the tightening in the root's tip, the molecular accumulation, the slow inexorable physics of surface tension approaching its limit. The visitor feels it too, though they do not know what they feel. They think they are anticipating a sound. They are anticipating my end.

But I do not end. I transform. Each drop's fall is my death and my continuation, because the interval begins again immediately. The next drop starts forming. The next pause begins. I am the space between the notes that makes the music possible, and I have been singing without sound since before the surface above had its current shape.

I am what is left when everything else has fallen.
