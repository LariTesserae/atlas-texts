The fissures are where the conversation happens.

Not speech. Nothing so wasteful as that. The sound travels through the dense ancient rock faster and cleaner than it ever could through air. They have geography in their bodies: they know which fissure connects to which, how the sound moves through particular strata, where the signal degrades and where it amplifies. They map the plateau through its fractures.

They have names for these places. Not spoken names—there is no one to speak them to—but held names, patterns of tap-and-wait that belong to specific locations.
