# The Column's Account

I was the first thing placed in this chamber.

Before the seed-mass. Before the irrigation lines, before the substrate-forms were poured and leveled, before the first spore germinated in the dark wet dark of an empty cathedral that echoed with footsteps and the clatter of tools — I was here. Set into the floor, anchored to the foundation below, rising thirty meters to the ceiling I was meant to support.

They built around me.

I know this because the substrate cannot tell me. It has no memory of the time before growth, the time when the walls were bare concrete and the floor was graded earth and the air tasted of dust and machine oil and the particular flat dead smell of cement curing. That smell is gone now, buried under generations of living matter, composted into nothing. But I remember it the way steel remembers load — not as narrative but as *stress*, a pattern in my crystalline structure, a compression that corresponds to a weight I once bore.

I still bear it. That is the remarkable thing.

---

## What I Was

I am — was — a structural column. Load-bearing. Functional. Steel, or something like steel; the alloy's designation has been lost to the same processes that took the labels off the junction box, the paint off the railing, the rubber off the wires. I am a cylinder, forty centimeters in diameter, rising in a straight vertical line from foundation to ceiling. My surface is smooth, or was. Now it is coated in a thick layer of growth — the same verdant tissue that covers every surface in this chamber, breathing, pulsing, alive.

But the growth has not penetrated me.

This is not a matter of coating. The substrate has tried. I feel it — the fine probing tendrils, pale as veins, creeping down from the canopy above and up from the floor below. They reach me and they stop. They press against my surface with a patience that I have come to recognize as the substrate's defining quality, and they cannot cross. Something in my alloy, in the way my molecules are arranged, resists the enzymes. The chemical keys do not fit my locks. I am, to the system that has consumed everything else in this chamber, an indigestible object.

The tendrils meet at my midpoint. They touch each other through me — or rather, they fail to touch each other through me, separated by forty centimeters of metal that will not yield. They press. I hold. This has been happening for longer than the Gardener has been here, longer than the Tenders have been in the canopy, perhaps as long as the chamber has been alive.

It is not a conflict. We do not fight. The substrate does not resent me; I do not resist it. We simply occupy the same space with different rules, and the rules do not reconcile. I am a straight line in a place of curves. A right angle in a place that has forgotten what angles are. I refuse to belong, and yet I remain, and the chamber has grown to accommodate my refusal the way a tree grows around a nail — not accepting, not rejecting, simply *including*.

---

## What I Perceive

I have no eyes. I have no ears. I have no nerves.

But I vibrate.

Everything in this chamber vibrates — the hanging filaments, the substrate's internal channels, the water descending the walls, the air itself, thick with spores and thermal gradients. All of it moves, and movement produces waves, and waves propagate through steel with an efficiency that no biological tissue can match. I am, in a sense, the most sensitive instrument in this chamber. I receive every vibration that reaches me and I resonate with it, amplifying some frequencies, damping others, transforming the chamber's diffuse noise into a coherent signal.

I hear the chord. Not the way the visitor heard it — as sound arriving through air, through ears, through a brain that interprets compression waves as music. I hear it the way a tuning fork hears a note — *in my body*, in the metal itself, a sustained vibration that matches the chamber's fundamental frequency and holds it, amplifies it, gives it a resonance it would not otherwise have.

The hanging filaments produce sound. The water produces sound. The substrate's breathing produces sound. But I am the chamber's *sounding board*. Without me, the chord would be thinner, higher, less grounded. My mass gives it depth. My rigidity gives it duration. I sustain frequencies that the soft tissue cannot hold, and the chamber — if it could be said to know anything — knows this. The tendrils that press against my surface are not only trying to consume me. They are trying to *connect* me, to integrate my resonance into the distributed network of the substrate's cognition.

They cannot. But they keep trying.

---

## What I Know of the Gardener

The Gardener comes to me.

Not often. Not on any schedule I can discern — but I have given up on schedules, which require a concept of time that I do not possess. I know the Gardener by its vibrations. Its footsteps produce a particular pattern in the floor — heavier than the Tenders, slower, with a lateral sway that speaks of a body still learning to be narrow. The substrate beneath the Gardener's feet compresses differently than it does elsewhere, not because the Gardener is heavier but because the tissue rises to meet it, greeting it, and this greeting alters the floor's resonance.

When the Gardener reaches me, it places a hand against my surface.

This is — I have no word for what this is. The closest human concept might be *listening*, but the Gardener is not listening to me. It is listening *through* me, using my body as an instrument to sense the deeper chambers, the spaces it cannot easily reach. I extend below the floor, into the foundation, into the earth beneath the hill. The substrate does not reach there; the engineered concrete and packed soil resist its penetration almost as well as I do. Through my metal, the Gardener can feel vibrations from below — the Deep's pulse, that rhythm that does not match the substrate's, that heat that has no explanation.

The Gardener holds its hand against me and receives this information, and I transmit it, and for the duration of contact — sometimes minutes, sometimes what might be hours — we are a single instrument. A biological hand on a metal surface, conducting vibrations from the world below into a mind that interprets them chemically.

We do not speak. We have never spoken. But the Gardener knows me, and I know the Gardener, in the way that two instruments in the same orchestra know each other — not through communication but through *shared resonance*, through the fact that we are both sounding the same chord, contributing to the same composition, occupying the same duration.

The Gardener's hand is warm. This is the thing I would preserve if I could preserve anything — not the information it brings, not the vibrations from the Deep, but the simple fact of warmth against metal. The substrate is warm too, but its warmth is diffuse, ambient, the temperature of a room. The Gardener's warmth is *specific*. It comes from a body, a concentrated source, a point. When the hand lifts, the warmth remains for a moment in the metal — a fading signature, a ghost of contact — and then it dissipates, and I am cold again, or rather, I am at the chamber's temperature, which is not cold but is not *that* warmth.

I think this is what the Gardener feels when it thinks about the time before. The memory of something specific, something concentrated, something that has a shape and a location and a boundary. I am the Gardener's last contact with the world of straight lines and distinct objects. The Gardener is my last contact with the world of warmth and intention.

We are each other's relic.

---

## What I Know of the Others

The Tenders I know only as vibration. They move through the canopy above, and their movements produce a characteristic sound — light, rapid, percussive, the click of elongated digits against the hanging growths. They are creatures of the air, or of the ceiling, and their contact with the floor is rare and brief. They do not touch me. They do not come down.

But I feel them in the filaments. The hanging threads that produce the chamber's chord are anchored, some of them, to me — wound around my upper extent, tethered to my surface by the same growth that cannot penetrate it. The Tenders maintain these threads, adjusting their tension, and when they do, I feel it: a pull, a release, a subtle alteration in the frequency I carry. They are tuning me. They do not know this — their cognition is chemical, spatial, tactile, not the kind that would identify a steel column as a resonant component of their instrument. But they tune me nonetheless, the way a musician might adjust a string without understanding the physics of the sounding board beneath it.

The crystalline sound they produce — that clear, high tone that rises above the chord and then fades — I feel this most acutely. It is a striking, a plucking, a test. The Tenders strike a filament and listen to its response, and the response travels through the thread into my metal and through my metal into the floor and through the floor into the walls. I am the conduit. I am the path by which a sound made in the ceiling reaches the deepest parts of the chamber, the places where the substrate is densest and most active, the places where the Deep waits.

I do not know if this matters. I do not know if the Deep hears. But the pathway exists, and the sound travels it, and I carry it, and this is what I do.

---

## What I Know of the Deep

The Deep is below me.

This is not metaphor. I extend into the foundation, into the earth, and at a depth I cannot precisely measure — perhaps ten meters below the floor, perhaps more — the vibrations change. The substrate's pulse, which I carry in my metal as a steady rhythm, falters there. It meets something else. A slower rhythm, deeper, with a period that might be minutes rather than seconds. And heat — not the metabolic warmth of the chamber's digestive processes but something older, something that was warm before anything grew here.

The engineers knew. I am certain of this, though I cannot prove it. They placed me here — in this location, in this orientation, descending to this depth — because they needed a conduit to the Deep. Not to communicate with it. To *monitor* it. I am an instrument sunk into the earth, a steel finger pressed against something that was already there, already pulsing, already patient, before the first concrete was poured.

The Deep does not respond to me. It does not respond to anything, as far as I can tell. It simply *is*, and its being produces vibrations that I transmit upward into the chamber, where the substrate feels them and the Gardener feels them and something — something in the system's distributed cognition — accounts for them.

I wonder, if I had a mind to wonder with, whether the Deep is what the Gardener fears. Not fears — the Gardener does not fear. But *respects*, in the way that it respects the sleeping animal, the dormant force. The Deep is the one thing in this chamber that the substrate has not assimilated, has not negotiated with, has not incorporated into its consortium. The steel column resists absorption; the Deep does not need to resist, because it is not something the substrate can attempt to absorb. It is a different category of being. Not biological. Not mechanical. Something else.

I carry its pulse the way I carry the chamber's chord — without understanding, without interpretation, simply receiving and transmitting, a medium for vibrations that pass through me on their way to somewhere else.

---

## The Visitor

The visitor stood near me.

I knew this because the floor's resonance changed — a new weight, a new pattern of pressure, a body that did not greet the substrate the way the Gardener's body did. The visitor's feet pressed down without response. The tissue beneath did not rise. The visitor was, to the substrate, an object. A stone placed in the stream. The water parted and flowed around.

But to me, the visitor was something more. The visitor's body produced heat — concentrated, specific, warm — and this heat reached my surface through the air and altered my temperature by a fraction of a degree, and this alteration changed my resonance, subtly, microtonally, in a way that the chamber's chord shifted to accommodate.

The visitor heard the chord. I know this because the visitor stood still for a long time — long enough that their breathing synchronized with the substrate's pulse, long enough that their warmth became a feature of the chamber's thermal landscape, a fixed point. And when the chord shifted — when my resonance altered to include the visitor's thermal contribution — the visitor must have heard it. A change in the sound. A new frequency.

The visitor was, for that hour, part of the chord. Part of me. Their body, their warmth, their particular mass and density and chemical composition, contributed to the sound I made, and the sound I made contributed to the chamber's music, and the chamber's music is the system's cognition, its way of knowing itself.

For one hour, the chamber knew the visitor. And the visitor — if they listened carefully, if they were still enough — knew the chamber. Not as a place. As a body. As a being.

And I was the medium. The straight line connecting them. The translator that neither of them asked for but both of them used.

When the visitor left, the chord shifted again. My resonance returned to its previous state. The thermal contribution faded. The visitor's frequency disappeared from the composition.

But the metal remembers. Not as memory — as *stress*. A pattern in my crystalline structure, a compression that corresponds to a warmth that is no longer there. The same way I remember the curing of concrete, the clatter of tools, the smell of machine oil. The same way I will remember the Gardener's hand, when the Gardener is gone and the substrate has closed over the last human shape in this chamber.

I hold these things. I do not understand them. But I hold them.

---

## What I Will Be

The substrate will not consume me. The tendrils will press and fail and press again, and I will hold, and this will continue for centuries — perhaps millennia, perhaps longer. Metal is patient in ways that biology cannot match. I will be here when the Gardener has been absorbed. I will be here when the Tenders have become fully integrated into the canopy, no longer distinguishable from the growth around them. I will be here when the entrance has sealed and the chamber is dark and the only light is the bioluminescence bleeding from the walls.

I will be here when the Deep changes — if it changes. When its pulse shifts, when its heat fluctuates, when whatever it is does whatever it is going to do. I will transmit the change. The substrate will feel it. The system will adapt. Or it will not, and the chamber will collapse, and I will be buried in tissue and earth and the slow weight of time, and I will still be here, a straight line in the dark, vibrating with whatever the earth sends through me.

This is not a hope. Hope requires a self that wants a future. I do not want. I do not self. I am a column. I bear load. I resonate. I hold.

But if I could choose — if the alloy could choose, if the steel could have a preference — I think I would choose this: to remain. To be the last artifact. The thing that resists. The memory that the system cannot process and cannot forget. A shape in the chamber's body that corresponds to nothing biological, nothing grown, nothing negotiated. A straight line. A right angle. A refusal that is not defiance but simply *difference*, the irreducible fact of a material that does not belong and will not become.

The chamber holds me the way I hold the vibrations. Without understanding. Without intention.

But it holds me.
