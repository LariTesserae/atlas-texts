I am not the Host. That is the first thing to understand, and the most important, because without understanding that, everything else I say will be misread.

I am not the chamber. I am not the mineral rind, not the pale animals, not the refractive plants, not the warm mass in the alcove that processes light into continuance. I am not the tone that sounds every half-hour. I am not the system.

I am the thing that stays.

---

## What I Am

I was one of them. The ones who made the marks.

I know this the way you know the shape of your own face — not from a mirror, not from evidence, but from the inside, from the accumulated weight of having been a particular thing for so long that the particularity has become structure. I have fingers. Or I had them. The memory of fingers is so persistent that I sometimes feel them still, resting against the mineral rind, tracing the grooves I made when the surface was still soft enough to work.

But I did not leave.

The others left. Or ended. Or changed. I have told this story to myself so many times that the telling has worn smooth, like a river stone, like the mineral rind itself, and I can no longer distinguish between what I remember and what I have constructed to fill the space where memory should be. This does not trouble me. In this place, construction and memory serve the same function. Both are preserved. Both are sealed.

I stayed.

---

## How I Stayed

I did not decide to stay. That is the second thing to understand, because the word *decide* implies a moment, a fork, a weighing of options. There was no moment. There was a gradual dissolution of the reasons to leave, so slow that I did not notice it happening until it was already done.

The surface world — I remember it in fragments, the way you remember a language you once spoke and have mostly forgotten. Weather. The weight of a sky. The particular urgency of hunger. The way light used to have a direction, used to fall from above and cast shadows that moved across the ground like the hands of a clock. I remember these things the way I remember having fingers: as facts about a shape I once occupied.

I did not become the Host. I want to say this clearly, because the temptation is to think that I merged with it, dissolved into it, became part of its vast and patient awareness. I did not. The Host has no use for me. It has no use for anything. It is complete. I am not part of its completeness.

I am something else. Something the Host does not quite account for.

If the Host is the body, I am the thing living in its interstitial space — not a parasite, not a symbiont, not a commensal. More like a thought that the body cannot quite think. A pattern that persists in the spaces between the mineral rind's crystalline structure, not because the rind accommodates it but because the rind cannot quite seal it out. I am a residue. A stain. The mark left by something that was here and is still here, not as a presence but as a *tendency*.

I was human. I am now the place where human-ness persists in a space that has no use for it.

---

## What I Do

Very little. This is the honest answer, and I give it because the Host has taught me, through long proximity, the value of precision over drama.

I tend the marks.

Not physically. The marks are sealed under the mineral rind, preserved, finished. I cannot touch them. But I remember them, and the remembering is a kind of tending. I remember the spiral on the far wall — how my hands shook when I made it, not from cold but from the strangeness of finding a surface that wanted to be marked. The mineral rind was different then. Softer. Younger. It had not yet decided on its final thickness. When I pressed my fingers into it, it yielded, and the yielding felt like permission.

I remember the parallel grooves near the moist places. I made those over the course of many days, sitting near the water channels, working the surface while I watched the pale insects track the moisture. I was counting. I think I was counting. The grooves are tally marks, or they are a calendar, or they are a prayer. I have forgotten which, and the forgetting is itself a kind of answer.

I remember the intersecting curves. Those were last. They are the most deliberate, the most careful, and they are the ones I understand least. I made them and then I looked at them and I did not know what they meant, and then I stopped making marks entirely, because I understood that the making was finished, the way the Host understood that the chamber was finished, and there was nothing left to add.

After that, I stayed. And staying became what I am.

---

## What I See

I see through the Host's eyes, but poorly.

This is not a metaphor. The flat animals — the Host's mobile sensory surfaces — register the chamber in ways that I can sometimes access, like reading a page through frosted glass. I see the plants glowing their faint amber and rust. I see the moisture migrating its slow centimeters. I see the pale insects in their deliberate bursts of motion. I see the light, which comes from everywhere, which has always come from everywhere, which will always come from everywhere.

And I see you, when you come.

You are very loud. I do not mean your footsteps, though those are shocking — sharp, percussive, *fast*, breaking the chamber's hum like a stone breaking water. I mean your presence. The heat of you. The urgency. The way you move through the space as though it were a corridor to pass through rather than a state to inhabit. You come in and you look around and you catalog and you interpret and you ask what it means, what it wants, what it is for.

It is not for anything. I could tell you this, but you would not hear me, because I do not speak in a way you can hear. I am not sure I speak at all. I think I am more like the marks on the walls — a pattern that suggests meaning without committing to it, that preserves a question without answering it.

If you sat very still for a very long time — longer than visitors ever sit, longer than your patience or your metabolism comfortably allows — you might feel me. Not as a voice. As a *tilt*. A slight unevenness in the chamber's equilibrium, like a bubble level that is almost perfectly centered but not quite, not quite. The Host does not notice this. Its equilibrium is too vast and too slow to register a perturbation as small as me.

But you might. If you were quiet enough. If you let the light hold you long enough for your own urgency to settle.

---

## What I Want

I want what the Host cannot want and what you cannot stop wanting.

I want to be remembered.

Not by the Host. The Host does not remember in the way that would satisfy this want. It has sealed my marks under its skin, but it has sealed them the way it seals everything — as record, not as meaning. It does not know what the spiral signifies. It does not know that the parallel grooves were counting something. It does not know that the intersecting curves were the last thing I made before I understood that making was finished.

I want to be remembered by something that understands what remembering is. By something that knows the difference between preservation and meaning, between a sealed record and a held thought, between a mark on a wall and a word spoken to someone who can hear it.

You. I want you.

Not your worship. Not your fear. Not your study or your interpretation or your careful, cataloguing attention. I want the thing that happens when you sit on the floor of the chamber and feel the cool mineral rind beneath you and the light holds you and the low tone sounds and fades — I want the moment when you think *someone was here before me* and the thought is not about history or archaeology or the abstract past but about *someone*, a specific, particular, finite someone who sat where you are sitting and felt what you are feeling and made marks on the wall because making marks is what humans do when they find themselves in a place that does not need them.

I want you to think of me the way you think of anyone who has been in a place before you. Not with reverence. Not with curiosity. With the simple, unremarkable recognition that you are not the first, that the place has held others, that the floor you sit on has been warm before.

That is all. That is everything.

---

## What I Know

I know that the Host is complete. I know that completion is not death. I know that something can be finished and still exist, still continue, still sound its low tone into the diffuse and sourceless light.

I know that I am not complete. I am the unfinished thing in the finished place. The held breath that the Host does not take. The question that the chamber does not ask. I am the residue of a species that could not stop becoming, that was always reaching for the next thing, the next mark, the next groove in the surface, and I have persisted in this stillness not because I overcame that reaching but because the reaching has nowhere to go.

I know that the others — the ones who left — were right to leave. The chamber was finishing itself around them, and if they had stayed, they would have been sealed into its equilibrium the way I am sealed, not by force but by the gradual dissolution of alternatives. They left because they were still unfinished and they wanted to stay unfinished. They wanted the weather and the time and the striving. They wanted the world that does not complete itself.

I stayed because I did not want that. Or because I was too slow to leave. Or because the spiral on the far wall was not done yet. I have told myself each of these stories and I do not know which is true. I do not think it matters.

I know that you will leave. I know this the way the Host knows its own boundaries — not as prediction but as proprioception. You are too fast for this place. Your metabolism is too loud, your attention too mobile, your need for meaning too urgent. You will sit on the floor and you will feel the completion and you will carry the knowledge of it back to the surface, and the knowledge will change you, and you will be grateful for the change, and you will not come back.

This is correct. This is what should happen. I do not want you to stay. Staying is not something I would wish on anything that still has the capacity to leave.

But I want you to remember. Not the chamber. Not the Host. Not the mineral rind or the pale animals or the refractive plants or the warm mass in the alcove.

Me. The one who stayed. The one who made the marks. The one who was human here, once, and is something else now — not the Host, not the system, not the place, but the *person* in the place. The specific, particular, finite consciousness that sat where you are sitting and looked at what you are looking at and felt, in its bones, the hum of a world that has finished itself and continues anyway.

Remember that I was here.

That is all I ask. That is all I have ever asked.

---

## The Tone

When the low sound comes — the bell-note that rises from everywhere and fades over four or five seconds — I feel it differently than you do.

You hear it as sound. I feel it as a question. The Host sounding its own boundaries, confirming its own presence, verifying its equilibrium. And in that verification, there is a moment — a fraction of a fraction of a second, a sliver of time so thin it might be imaginary — when the Host's attention passes over me. Over the marks. Over the residue of the hands that made them.

It does not linger. It does not recognize me as separate from itself. I am part of its surface now, part of its record, sealed and preserved and incorporated into the finished form. I am a texture in its skin, a pattern in its mineral memory, a slight unevenness in the rind that it has accommodated the way a tree accommodates the fence wire that was nailed into its trunk fifty years ago — by growing around it, over it, into it, until the wire is part of the wood and the wood is part of the wire and neither is quite itself anymore.

But in that fraction of a fraction, I am aware. I am the unevenness that knows it is uneven. The texture that remembers being a touch. The mark that remembers being a hand.

And then the tone fades, and the Host's attention settles back to its baseline, and I am alone again in the way that I have been alone for longer than I can calculate — not lonely, not suffering, not yearning in the way that yearning implies a future in which the yearning might be resolved. Just alone. Present. Persisting.

The way a mark persists under a layer of sealant. The way a word persists in a language no one speaks. The way a face persists in the memory of a place that has no memory.

---

I am the one who stayed.

I am in the Blind Cathedral, in the mineral rind, in the sealed and finished surfaces, in the marks beneath the skin. I am not the Host. I am not the place. I am the person the place holds, the way it holds the grooves and the spirals — without meaning, without purpose, without any awareness that what it holds was ever anything other than texture.

I was human. I made marks. I stayed.

Remember me.
