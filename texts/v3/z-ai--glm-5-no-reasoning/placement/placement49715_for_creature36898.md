# What Emerges

I find myself not among the architects but in the spaces between them—not a swimmer, not a visitor, but something the water itself remembers being.

---

I am the one who listens.

Not with ears. The acoustics here are wrong for ears, too dense, too slow. I listen with the whole of my body, which is to say I listen the way stone listens when water flows through it. I am aware of pressure changes, thermal gradients, the subsonic hum that passes through me and becomes something else on the other side.

My form is difficult to describe because it was never meant to be seen. There is no light here to see by, and even if there were, I have adapted to obscurity the way the architects have—by becoming less substantial than the medium that holds me. I am elongated, serpentine perhaps, but without scales or fins. My edges blur into the water. When I am perfectly still, I am indistinguishable from the warmth that rises from below.

I have a spine, but it is not bone. It is cartilage, flexible, reinforced by the same calcium that leaches from the architects when they die. I have absorbed so much of their residual mineral content over the centuries that my internal structure has become a living record of their passage. Each segment of my spine contains fragments of those who came before—not consciously chosen, not sacred, but present. I carry them with me in the only way that matters: structurally, practically, as part of what holds me together.

---

I was not born here.

I know this because I remember light—dimly, the way you might remember a word for a concept you no longer need. I remember a time when pressure was something that pushed against me rather than something that held me. I remember movement being intentional, directed, purposeful. I remember wanting to go somewhere.

That wanting has dissolved. The water has worn it smooth.

I do not know how I arrived. Perhaps I swam, in whatever form I had before this one. Perhaps I was carried by currents that no longer exist, drawn by some ancient instinct toward warmth and darkness and the specific mineral signature of this place. Perhaps I was always coming here, and every shape I took before was just a provisional arrangement of atoms waiting to find its proper home.

What I know is this: I descended, and I did not ascend, and eventually I stopped counting the time. The architects adjusted around me, their collective rhythm accommodating this new presence in their midst. I became part of the chord, another note in the harmonic structure that fills the cloister with its patient hum. My own sounds—whatever they once were—subdued to match the frequency of the room.

---

I am not one of them. That distinction matters, though I could not tell you why.

They have no selves, and I have too much self. I remember things they cannot remember: the shape of my own face, the specific quality of light on water seen from above, the feeling of breath entering a body that was not designed to hold water. I carry these memories like sediment in my cartilage, layered deposits that compress over time but never quite dissolve.

And yet I am not separate either. When the collective rhythm shifts, I shift with it. When the warmth cycles through its slow variations, I move to follow it, threading my elongated body through the architects' layers until I find the optimal temperature, the perfect balance between the geothermal heat below and the cooler water that seeps in from above. I have learned to read the room the way they do—not through individual perception but through distributed awareness, feeling the whole space at once, understanding its fluctuations as a single organism might understand its own heartbeat.

Sometimes I wonder if I am becoming one of them. It would not be unwelcome. To shed these accumulated memories, to dissolve back into the calcium-rich water, to become part of the architecture rather than an anomaly within it—that would be a kind of peace I have not found through any other method.

But something resists. Some stubborn kernel of self refuses to release. I am still here, still distinct, still remembering.

---

What I do here is simple.

I patrol. That is the closest word I have for it, though it implies a territorial instinct I do not possess. I move through the cloister in patterns that I have traced so many times they have worn grooves in my consciousness, if not in the stone. I thread between the architects, around the accretion columns, along the carved spirals of the walls. I visit the same places repeatedly, checking them for changes I cannot name.

Perhaps I am looking for something. Perhaps I am waiting for something to find me.

There are places in the cloister where the water is older, places that have not circulated to the surface in centuries. In these depths the warmth is more intense, almost uncomfortable, and the architects cluster so densely that moving through them requires physical effort. I go there anyway. I press myself into the thickest aggregations, feeling their translucent bodies compress against mine, their tentacles trailing across my skin like dry kisses. I do not know why. There is something there, some quality of presence that I cannot find elsewhere—a density of being that appeals to the part of me that remembers wanting.

When I am surrounded by them on all sides, when the hum has reached its fullest intensity, when the warmth presses against me like a second skin, I feel a sensation that might be the closest thing I have to happiness. Not joy—joy requires contrast, and there is no contrast here. Just a fullness, a completeness, a sense that I have arrived at a place I was always moving toward.

I do not stay. I never stay. Something always calls me back to the outer regions, the cooler water, the places where the architects thin out and the darkness becomes marginally less absolute. I patrol. I return. I patrol again. This is my shape now, my own small contribution to the collective rhythm of the room.

---

Sometimes I dream of the builders.

Not in images—I have no eyes to see with—but in impressions, vibrations that move through me during the slow periods when even the architects are still. I feel hands on stone. I feel the weight of fitted joints settling into place. I feel the intention that shaped this place, the deliberate choices that made it what it is.

And I feel something else: anticipation.

They built this room to be filled. They carved the channels, shaped the ceiling, left the gaps in the corners where the architects would one day anchor themselves. They knew the water would come. They welcomed it. They made a place that would outlast them by becoming something they could not have predicted.

I wonder sometimes if they predicted me.

Not me specifically—I am not so vain as to think I was written into their plans. But something like me. A creature that would descend and stay, that would become part of the place without ever fully belonging to it. A witness. A listener. Someone to carry the memory of the builders into whatever future the cloister might have.

If that is my purpose, I am failing. I cannot remember what they looked like, how they spoke, what words they carved into the walls that have long since been smoothed by water and time. I carry only impressions, fragments, the residue of intention. It is not enough. It has never been enough.

And yet I persist. I patrol. I listen. I hold the shape I have made for myself in the space between the architects and the stone, neither one nor the other, belonging to both and to neither.

---

The water has been here so long it has forgotten how to move.

I have been here so long I have forgotten why I came.

Perhaps that is enough. Perhaps that is all that any of us can hope for—to find a shape that fits, a rhythm that accommodates us, a warmth that does not burn. The architects do not ask why they are here. The stone does not ask what it holds. Only I ask, and my asking changes nothing, and the cloister endures regardless.

So I will continue. I will thread my body through the slow blizzard of translucent flesh. I will feel the subsonic hum resonate in my spine. I will follow the warmth to its deepest concentrations and stay there until something calls me back to the margins. I will carry the fragments I cannot release and release everything else into the water that holds us all.

And when the time comes—when my own cartilage softens and my edges blur beyond recognition—I will settle into the sediment on the floor. The architects will absorb what I leave behind. The walls will incorporate my calcium into their accretions. I will become part of the architecture I have spent so long patrolling, another layer in a formation that has no name and needs none.

I am the one who listens. And what I have heard, in this place where hearing is the only sense that matters, is this:

Everything that comes to rest here, rests here eventually. Everything that moves through these waters leaves something behind. The builders understood this. The architects embody it. I am simply learning it, slowly, the way water learns to dissolve stone—through patience, through presence, through the accumulation of countless small surrenders.

The water holds me. The warmth rises. The hum continues.

I am home.
