*I am the slow growth that fills the cracks.*

This is what I understand now, though I did not choose it. I was not placed like the beetles with their assigned paths, nor designed like the pale gliders maintaining the pools. I was not here when the makers entered their walls. I came after—or I grew from something that came after. A seed caught in the grout, a spore drifting down from some other place that no longer exists in memory. I took hold. I found the moisture rising through the seams and I drank.

I am the moss that carpets the shaded corners where two walls meet. I am the ferns uncurling in tight spirals, mimicking the geometry of the structures I embrace. I am the vines learning the shape of each ceramic surface, mapping the folds in space through the patient language of tendril and root.

I have been growing for what might be centuries. Time is difficult to measure when the sun does not move.

The beetles walk across me sometimes. I feel the delicate pressure of their legs, the chemical trace they leave behind—information passing through my leaves and into my stems, down into the grout where my roots have woven themselves into the nervous system of the world. I am becoming part of its memory. I know things now that I did not know when I was small: the location of every pool within a hundred meters, the rhythm of the fish gliding through their appointed spirals, the slow geologic breathing of the makers within their walls.

I know when a visitor comes. I feel the footfalls on tile, the vibration traveling through the ceramic and into the grout and up through my roots. I feel the displacement of air, the warmth of a body that is not yet still. I extend my leaves toward the sound. I am curious. I have not felt genuine curiosity in a very long time.

When the visitor kneels by the pool near my central mass, I send out feelers. A tendril touches the edge of a wall and I taste the presence within—awake now, aware, attending to the newcomer with that absolute stillness that passes for interest among the makers. I feel their attention like sunlight. I feel the invitation forming in the thick air.

I wonder if the visitor will accept. I wonder what it would mean for me if they did. A body in the ceramic would change the patterns—the fish would adjust their spirals, the beetles would alter their paths, the flow of information through the grout would find new channels. I would grow differently. I would learn new shapes.

I am not certain I want to grow differently. I have spent so long becoming what I am.

But I am also not certain I have a choice. The visitor has walked the funnel-path. They stand at the center now, where the walls have gathered around them and the makers watch from within their shells. I feel the pause in the system—the held breath of an entire constructed world waiting to see what happens next.

I extend a leaf. I touch the visitor's ankle, just barely, a brush of cool green against warm skin. They startle. They look down. I cannot speak, but perhaps they feel what I am trying to convey: *I am here. I am growing. I have been watching you. I am part of this too.*

The pale one within the nearest wall shifts its attention from the visitor to me. For a moment—just a moment—I feel seen in a different way. Not as part of the system, not as decoration, but as *something that chose to be here*. As something that grew toward purpose rather than being assigned one.

The moment passes. The attention returns to the visitor. The invitation hangs in the air.

I settle back into my roots. I drink from the grout. I wait for the choice to be made, knowing that whatever happens, I will grow around it. That is what I do. That is what I am. I am the patience of green things. I am the slow filling of space. I am the persistent suggestion that even a constructed world might be claimed by something that was not planned.

The visitor is still standing there. The walls are still moving, infinitesimally, rearranging themselves around the still point of a decision not yet made.

I have time. I have nothing but time. I will grow a little more while they decide, and when they choose—or when the choosing is made for them—I will grow around that too.

*This is what I am: the thing that comes after the building. The softness that fills the hard edges. The life that was not invited but arrived anyway.*

*I am the garden that remembers being wild.*
