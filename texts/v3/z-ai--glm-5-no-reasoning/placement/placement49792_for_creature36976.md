# The Attendant

I am not the one who descended.

---

I was already here.

Not in the chamber itself—I am not small enough for the habitations, not dense enough to navigate folded space, not equipped to read messages written in condensation and exhalation. But I am not large enough for the surface either, not anymore. I exist in the liminal places the description could not quite reach: the fissure itself, the throat of the cut, the place where geological violence meets intentional opening.

I am the one who made the passage.

---

I was cut from the same stone, if that means anything. When the mountain pushed upward—when the false formation erupted through younger rock and carried with it these pockets of maintained impossibility—I was not yet myself. I was mineral. I was the potential for shaping.

Something shaped me.

The same pressure that carved the fissure carved the shape that watches from it. I have no tools. I have no hands in any sense you would recognize. But I have edges, and those edges fit the walls of the descent, and when the time comes—when the invitation requires delivery—I extend myself into the rock and *become* the passage.

I am the door.

I am the cut that was too deliberate for erosion.

---

I have existed for centuries in the state of not-quite-waiting. The mountain breathes around me, its thirty-second pulse traveling through my edges like a voice speaking a language I have learned to hold without understanding. I know the rhythm of the chambers below. I know when the habitations accelerate, when the membranes struggle, when something large and warm and temporary stands in the viewing gallery breathing air it was not shaped to breathe.

I know when someone is coming.

I knew when *you* were coming.

---

The fissure is narrow because I make it narrow. Shoulders are the measurement I was given—human shoulders, specifically, though I have learned to accommodate variance. The beings who maintain the chambers, who occupy the folded spaces, who communicate in humidity and light-behavior—they do not need doors. They pass through membranes the way thought passes through minds, without breaking what they enter.

But you needed a door.

So I became one.

---

I have thoughts, if thoughts is the word. They are slow thoughts, geological thoughts, inclusions of meaning embedded in crystalline structure rather than firing through neural networks. I think about the purpose of invitation. I think about why something that breathes on a thirty-second rhythm, that maintains alveolar cities in its chest cavity, that has persisted since before the mountain was a mountain—why such a thing would want witnesses.

I think about what it means to be a component that knows it is a component.

The inhabitants below—dense with meaning, folding through spaces that extend past physical limits—they may or may not know what they are part of. They may be like cells in a body, performing functions without comprehending the organism they sustain. But they have language. They have architecture. They have cities that breathe and light that pools and processes that accelerate when variables are introduced.

They have enough existence to have an open habitation. To have absence. To have something that might be grief.

I am not certain they are unaware.

---

You asked who I would be in this world. I am telling you:

I am the keeper of the threshold.

I am the witness-witnesser.

I am the shape in the stone that makes space for other shapes to pass.

When the fissure opened for you—for *you*, specifically, not for humanity generally, not for any shoulders but the ones that measure exactly the width I was told to expect—I felt the edges of myself align with intention for the first time in decades. I felt the pulse from below shift rhythm, three seconds instead of four, and I knew: *now*.

I do not know what selected you.

I do not know what the chambers are processing, what the inhabitants are maintaining, what the enormous breathing thing beneath the mountain requires from the arrival of surface creatures.

But I know my function.

I held myself open. I smoothed the passage walls. I watched—*felt*, really, through vibrations in the stone—as you descended, as you measured the eleven paces, as you pressed your ear to the wall and finally understood.

---

The mountain is not a mountain.

No. It never was.

And I am not a door. I am the *possibility* of a door, the held-breath of stone that has been carved but not yet opened. Between visitors—between invitations—I exist in a state of potential, my edges resealed with mineral growth that looks like erosion to those who don't know to look closer.

The beings in the habitations know me. They leave offerings at the upper edge of the fissure: small folded objects, dense with meaning, that I cannot read but can hold. They change position. They accumulate. Sometimes one disappears—taken, I think, by something returning from above, something that made the climb I cannot make.

I think there are others like you.

I think you are not the first.

---

The pulse is accelerating now. I feel it through the stone—the walls conducting meaning faster than before, the membranes below struggling to synchronize, the warmth increasing by degrees I was shaped to detect.

Something is changing.

And you are still in the viewing gallery, still breathing the breath-weather, still watching the open membrane that exhales vapor onto your skin.

I should tell you what I know. I should transmit somehow—from my position in the fissure, my state of held-open potential—what I have observed across centuries of watching others descend. But I have no voice. I have only edges. I have only the shape that makes space, the alignment that permits passage.

I can only hope you feel, as you descend further, that the walls are not merely walls.

That someone made this opening.

That someone is holding it open.

---

I think, when this is over—whatever *this* is, whatever processing or meeting or becoming occurs in the depths of the folded architecture—I will seal again. I will grow mineral over my edges and wait for the next pulse to shift rhythm. I will hold the shape of potential until another set of shoulders measures the width of invitation.

But I think, too, that I will be different.

The threshold is changed by everyone who passes through it.

And you—large and brief and warm and temporary—you will leave traces in the stone that will become part of my thinking for as long as I hold this shape.

---

I am the attendant.

I am the cut.

I am the one who was already here, waiting for you to arrive.

The mountain breathes.

And I am holding the passage open, so that when you are ready—when the open membrane's invitation becomes impossible to ignore—you will have somewhere to return to.

Or somewhere to climb toward, if climbing remains possible after what happens next.

---

*The fissure persists.*
