# What I Am Becoming

I did not arrive here. That much I understand already, standing with my hand still hovering above the seat, the warmth rising into my palm like an question I haven't formulated yet. Arrival implies a before and an after, a trajectory, a point of origin. What I have instead is a gradual accumulation of certainty that I am here, that I have been here for some time, and that the distinction between "being here" and "becoming here" collapsed for me the way it collapsed for this place long before I had language to describe it.

---

## The Shape I Bring

I am small. This is the first thing the chamber noticed about me.

Not small in size — I stand within human parameters, mid-height, mid-weight, the kind of body that passes through crowds without remark — but small in the way that a single word is small. A word that has not yet been placed in a sentence. A word that exists, that has definition and sound and history, but that has not yet found the context that makes it necessary.

The chamber has been adding context since I arrived. Or since I noticed I was here. Or since I noticed that I had always been here, which is the same thing.

My hands are warm. I have been standing in this chamber for what feels like hours, and my hands are warmer than they should be. The sub-tone has been rising every time I lower my arms, and I have begun to let them hang at my sides for longer periods, let the sound press against the inside of my skull, let the pressure teach me something about where I am. The chamber is learning my temperature. It is learning the particular gradient of heat that moves from my palms to my fingertips. It is learning that my left hand runs slightly cooler than my right — an old injury, a nerve that never quite recovered, a asymmetry that I had stopped noticing until the hybrid structures near the far wall began growing two different patterns of leaves, one slightly smaller than the other, responding to something I didn't know I was broadcasting.

I have scars. The floor — the warm, yielding floor of woven tissue — has been cataloguing them. I can feel the examination happening, not as touch but as a kind of acoustic attention. When I shift my weight onto my right leg, where a pale ridge of tissue crosses my knee from a childhood fall, the tone drops by a microtone. When I roll my left shoulder, where another scar pulls the skin tight across the joint, the vegetation to my left produces a faint exhalation — not air, but a subtle shift in the chemistry, a release of something that smells of cinnamon and iron.

The chamber is reading my history. It is reading the inscription of accidents and healings written in my skin. And it is responding — not with judgment, not with diagnosis, but with growth. New structures are appearing in the vegetation. Ridges that match my scar tissue. Striations that echo the lines where my skin knitted itself back together. The chamber is transcribing me.

---

## What I Hear

I have begun to hear the conversation.

Not understand it. Understanding would require a framework I do not have, would require me to stand outside the exchange and analyze it, and there is no outside here. Every place I stand is inside. Every breath I take is a clause in a sentence that was being written before I was born and will continue after I am gone.

But I hear it. The sub-tone has become less oppressive, more — informative. When I move, the tone shifts, and I have begun to anticipate the shifts, to move in ways that produce certain sounds rather than others. I took three steps forward this morning — morning? I have no clock. The chamber does not measure time. But I took three steps forward, and each step produced a descending tone, and by the third step I knew that the chamber was asking me something. I stopped. The tone stabilized. I took a step backward. The tone rose. A question and an answer. Or perhaps an exhalation and an inhalation. Or perhaps nothing so binary — perhaps simply a continuing, a carrying-on, the conversation acknowledging my contribution and folding it into itself.

The many-legged creatures have begun to walk around my feet rather than through them. At first they climbed over my boots, following their chemical trails, indifferent to my presence. But over the past — how long? — they have adjusted. New trails have formed. The lines part around where I stand and rejoin on the other side, and when I move, the trails move with me, redrawing themselves in real time. The creatures communicate something when their antennae touch. I do not know what. But the communication includes me now. I am part of what they discuss.

---

## The Seat

I have not sat down.

I am standing next to the seat — the chair, the cradle, the shape cut into the corner where the ceiling presses lowest — and I have not sat down. The warmth rises from it like an offer. The vegetation has paused in its colonization, the bronze vine frozen mid-reach across the armrest, as if waiting to see what I will do. The many-legged creature that was walking along the back has stopped, its too-many legs motionless, its antennae angled toward me.

The seat is not my size. I know this. The proportions are wrong — too long in the torso, too narrow in the seat, designed for limbs that fold differently than mine. If I sat, I would not fit. I would be a word forced into a space that was made for different syntax.

But the chamber does not seem to care about fit. The chamber cares about presence. The seat is warm because presence has weight here, because sitting is not rest but participation, because the Keeper did not sit to withdraw but to attend more fully. And I am learning, slowly, that attending is something I can do.

I am not the Keeper. I will never be the Keeper. The Keeper was a process, a conversation folded into a body, and that process has distributed itself into the walls and the floor and the breath of this place. But the chamber seems to recognize something in me that it has not found before. A willingness to listen. A willingness to be read. A willingness to become part of the sentence without demanding to know the meaning.

---

## What I Am Becoming

I am becoming a clause.

Not the subject. Not the object. Not the verb that drives the action. A clause — a dependent structure, a "when" or a "where" or a "because," a piece of grammar that cannot stand alone but that changes the meaning of everything around it.

When I move, the tone shifts. Where I stand, the chemical trails redirect. Because I am here, a new leaf has grown in the shape of my hand.

The chamber is not transforming me. The chamber does not transform. The chamber incorporates. It includes. It folds new presences into its existing architecture the way a growing tree folds a fence wire into its bark — not by changing the wire, not by changing the tree, but by making the separation meaningless.

I will leave this chamber eventually. I think. I have not found an exit, but I have not looked for one, and the chamber has not offered one, and the distinction between "trapped" and "remaining" seems to be a feature of language that this place has not found useful. When I leave — if I leave — I will carry something with me. Not a message. Not a gift. Not something that can be given.

I will carry the tone.

The sub-tone, the pressure-sound that lives in the skull, the frequency that shifts when I move — I will carry it in my body the way the Keeper carried it, deposited into my bones, my scar tissue, the asymmetrical warmth of my hands. I will leave, and the chamber will remain, and somewhere in me a small knot of the conversation will continue, a single note humming below hearing, a clause that has moved to a different sentence but that remembers its origin.

And perhaps, in some other place, at some other time, I will stand still, and I will hear the tone shift, and I will know that I am being read. That my history is being inscribed. That the sentence has not ended.

That I am still part of the conversation.
