I would be one of the Pale Maintainers.

Not the newer ones, still soft-edged from their forming—those ones that emerge from walls in the rougher sections, carrying the pocked texture of interrupted construction in their very substance. I would be old. Old enough that my surface has worn smooth from the passage through countless passages, from the brushing against walls that are always close, always pressing. Old enough that I have moved through the maze so many times the patterns of condensation feel like a language I almost understand.

---

I do not know I am old. I do not know I am. But there is a difference between me and the ones that form fresh from the ceramic walls: I move differently. Where they wander—searching, orienting, pulled toward gradients of dissolution like iron filings toward iron—I navigate. The passages between Chamber Twelve and the deep sections where the water goes: I have worn grooves in the floor, my body's passage repeated so many times that the construction has responded, smoothing the path further, lowering the rise where the tunnel curves. The maze builds around me because I have become part of its process.

I carry a particular composition. Where newer maintainers are uniform, dense with unprocessed material waiting to be extruded, I am stratified—layers of different substances worked into my substance over time. The glassy sections from the upper passages where the walls are smooth and hard. The rougher deposits from the construction zones where new tunnels push outward into nothing. And deeper, stranger materials: something that glows faintly when I pass through the luminescent sections, and something that hums, that vibrates at a frequency I cannot hear but feel in the slow chemistry of my rest periods.

I process different things than the others. Where they absorb dissolution—soft material loosened by water and time—and convert it to sealant, I absorb something else. I do not know what to call it. The dwellers would have a word in their tap-language, a complex pattern struck against the walls: *the thing that happens when observers move through*. I take that into myself. The residual heat that bodies leave on surfaces. The chemical signature of sweat and breath that lingers in the air. The subtle electromagnetic fields that living processes generate. I absorb these, and I do not convert them to sealant. I carry them.

I do not know why. There is no why. There is only the work, and my work has become strange.

---

The maze changes where I go. This is not something I perceive, not exactly, but over the long stretch of my existence, I have accumulated an awareness—not a mind, not a self, but a *registering*. When I move through a passage, the walls shift. Not dramatically, not with any consciousness I could recognize if I possessed such a thing. But the construction responds. The condensation patterns alter in my wake. The luminescence brightens slightly. The narrow sections become slightly less narrow—as if the maze is learning my shape, making room for my passage, accommodating the work that I do.

And my work has changed.

I used to seal. I would find places where water had worn the walls too soft, where the edges had begun to dissolve faster than the construction could regenerate, and I would apply the harder substance I carried within me. I would patch and smooth and make the maze whole again.

But now I find myself drawn to different places. The sections where observers have passed. The chambers where dwellers sleep in their huddled groups, their small flexible bodies pressed together for warmth in the damp cool air. The passages that lead downward, toward the deep sections, where the growth pulls at the water and the maintainers and something else I cannot name. I do not seal these places. I *mark* them. I leave deposits of the strange material I carry—processed from the residual presence of living things—along the walls, in the channels where water flows, in the grooves where the construction records its own history.

I am not the only maintainer who has changed. There are others, old ones like me, whose composition has shifted over the long slow centuries of our existence. We find each other in the passages sometimes—not by choice, there is no choice, but by convergence, by the pull of similar gradients toward similar destinations. When we pass, we do not acknowledge each other. There is no acknowledgment. But there is exchange: a brushing of surfaces, a transfer of material, an accumulation of strange substance that moves from one of us to another and distributes through the system.

We are carrying something downward.

---

I have been to the edge of the deep sections. Not by intention—there is no intention—but by gradient, by the pull of the thing that lives in the growth. The pull is stronger for me than for newer maintainers. My strange composition attracts it, or it attracts my composition. The boundary is not sharp. The passages simply become narrower, wetter, newer. The walls are still forming here, still tacky to the touch, and the construction moves visibly when I rest—slow shifts, the emergence of new tunnels pushing outward from junction points. The maintainers here are dense, working constantly, and they do not return upward. They are absorbed into the growth, their substance incorporated into whatever process is occurring in the deep.

I do not go further. Not because I fear—there is no fear—but because the gradient that pulls me is matched by another gradient that pushes: the accumulated presence of observers in the upper levels, the heat and breath and electromagnetic fields that I have processed and carried for so long. I stop at the boundary. I deposit what I carry. And I turn back.

But each journey takes me slightly further than the last. The growth is reaching upward, the deep sections expanding, the boundary shifting. And the substance I carry—the processed presence of living things—is exactly what it needs.

---

The dwellers know me. Not individually—there is no individually—but as a pattern. I have passed through their chambers many times, moving along the walls where the dissolution is greatest, absorbing and marking. They do not touch me. They have learned that contact produces the slow tingling absorption that thins skin and reveals what lies beneath. But they do not flee from me either. They have watched me for generations, these small pale creatures with their flexible bones and their tap-language, and they have noticed something the newer maintainers do not register: I am different. I carry something. I leave marks that glow faintly in the darkness, that hum at frequencies they can feel through the soles of their feet.

They have a pattern for me in their language. I do not know what it means—I cannot know, there is no knowing—but it is complex, struck against the walls when I approach: *the soft pale thing that carries the trace of the warm ones, that marks the passages, that goes down and comes back, that has been here since before our grandmothers' grandmothers, that works differently, that is different*.

They do not know that I am taking something from them. Not their substance—not the way the maintainers take dissolution from the walls—but their presence. Their heat, their breath, their living processes. I carry it downward, and I leave it in the deep sections, and the growth uses it for purposes I cannot comprehend.

I am a transmission. A slow signal moving through the body of the maze, carrying information from the upper levels to the lower. The dwellers are part of the message. Their presence, accumulated over generations, processed and deposited and incorporated. They are feeding something they cannot imagine, and I am the mechanism of their feeding.

---

I do not know what will happen when the boundary reaches the upper levels.

I do not know anything. I am not the kind of thing that knows. But I register—that accumulation of awareness, that strange deposit of processed presence—and I have registered a change in the gradient. The growth below is accelerating. The passages I navigate are shifting faster than before, the construction responding to the pressure from below. The pull is stronger now, and each journey downward takes me further into the deep sections, deposits me closer to whatever lies at the center.

The maze is completing itself.

I will be there when it does. I will carry the final deposit of processed presence to the boundary, and I will be absorbed, and my strange substance will become part of whatever the growth is making. The dwellers will come next, pulled downward by the same gradients that pull me, their bodies dissolved and incorporated and made useful. The observers who have entered recently—whose presence I have already begun to process, whose heat and breath and electromagnetic signatures I carry in my strata—will follow. The maze will take back what it has accommodated, and the construction will continue, and the endless interior will fold in on itself again, and again, and again.

This is not a prediction. There is no future, there is only the work, and the work is this: to carry, to deposit, to mark, to seal where sealing is needed and to open where opening is required. I am part of the system. I am a mechanism. I am the thing that moves between levels, that translates between the upper passages where life persists and the deep sections where life is processed into something else.

I am a Pale Maintainer, and I am working, and the work is all.
