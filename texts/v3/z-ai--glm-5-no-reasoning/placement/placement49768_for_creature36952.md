# The Keeper of the Archive's Ribs

I live in the fourth chamber from the top of what the travelers call the Rib Archive, though it was never meant to archive ribs and never stored any.

The structure rises from the plant-mass like a clenched fist knuckle-first, and its exterior walls are copper-wrapped buttress roots that have ossified over centuries into something between metal and wood. Inside, the architecture opens into a vertical sequence of seven round chambers, each one slightly smaller than the last as you climb. The chambers are connected not by stairs but by thick vine-columns that have hollowed themselves into shafts wide enough for a body to pass through with hands and feet pressing against the living walls. TheMaintenance Forms use these shafts constantly, flowing upward and downward without apparent purpose, and I have learned to listen for the particular sound their passage makes—a wet compression, like a heartbeat heard through a wall—so I can time my own movements to avoid intersecting with theirs.

Not because they would harm me. They never have. But being passed through by a Maintenance Form leaves a residue—copper and chlorophyll on the skin, a faint ringing in the teeth—that takes hours to fade, and I have grown to dislike it the way one dislikes any sensation one cannot control.

I am, as far as I know, the only permanent traveler in this sector.

I did not choose to stay. That distinction matters to me, though it would not matter to the engine-forest, which does not recognize the category of *choice*. I was moving through, like the others whose footprints I have found pressed into the Memory Vines—heading somewhere, presumably, though I can no longer remember where. The journey had a destination. I am certain of that. But somewhere between the third and fourth factory-growth I passed, the destination became unimportant, and then the journey became unimportant, and then the distinction between traveling and not traveling became unimportant, and I found myself standing in the fourth chamber of this structure with my hand pressed flat against the wall and no particular impulse to remove it.

That was—how long ago? I keep time by the seed-dispatches. Each one is audible from here, a pneumatic thump that travels through the substrate and up through the roots and into the walls of the chamber. I have felt them against my palm while sleeping, while eating, while performing the small maintenance routines that constitute my days. I count them. Not because counting serves a purpose, but because the counting itself has become a structure, the closest thing to ritual that my mind still produces.

I have felt one thousand six hundred and forty-three seed-dispatches since I stopped moving.

## What I keep

I keep things. This is not a function the engine-forest assigned to me. It is not a task I discovered carved into a control interface. It emerged the way everything emerges here—slowly, without decision, through the accumulated pressure of circumstance.

The fourth chamber is round, roughly four meters across, with walls that weep moisture in a slow, predictable cycle. The floor is firm plant-mass, compressed to the density of hardwood, and it slopes very gently toward one wall where a natural channel has formed. Condensation collects in this channel and drains downward through the structure, eventually joining the larger circulatory system somewhere below. I do not know where. I have followed the channel as far as the fifth chamber below, and there it disappears into a root-mass that I cannot penetrate without tools I do not have.

Along the walls of my chamber, in the spaces between the weeping patches, I have arranged my collection.

There are forty-one objects. I know because I count them the way I count the seed-dispatches, touching each one in sequence, confirming its presence, its position, its state of preservation. The counting is not obsessive. It is simply what I do. The engine-forest counts nothing, as far as I can tell—its processes are analog, continuous, flowing. But I am still human enough that counting gives shape to time, and time needs shaping here, because the environment will not shape it for you.

The objects are what other travelers have left behind. Or what they have dropped, or lost, or set down and then forgotten when their own dissolution into the system began. I have not sought them out. I have found them during my walks—the circuits I make through the surrounding factory-growths, the same route every day, or what I call a day, which is the interval between two periods of sleep. The route takes me past seven structures, through two cathedral-spaces, along the edge of one Void where I pause for exactly as long as it takes to feel the cool dry air on my face before continuing. During these walks, I find things. I bring them back. I arrange them. I count them.

The objects:

A ceramic cup, intact, its glaze crazed into a pattern that resembles the fractal branching of the plant-mass. I found it in a clearing twenty meters from the Rib Archive, half-buried in vegetation. It holds nothing. I have tried placing water in it, but the water is absorbed by the glaze's cracks within hours, and the cup becomes briefly warm, as if processing. Then it returns to its resting state. I have stopped putting water in it.

A length of cable, synthetic, still pliable. One end is fused to a small metal housing that might once have been a connector of some kind. The other end frays into copper strands that have oxidized green. I have observed that the Memory Vines near where I keep this cable grow slightly faster than those elsewhere in the chamber. I do not know if this is causal. I do not know if causality is the right framework.

A book. This is the object I return to most often. Its pages have swollen to three times their original thickness, and the binding has softened until the book can no longer be closed—it lies open, splayed, a permanent gesture of offering. The writing inside is not illegible, as I first thought. It is simply in a language I do not speak. The characters are angular, precise, and they repeat in patterns that suggest not words but some other unit of meaning—chemical formulas, perhaps, or coordinates, or musical notation for an instrument I cannot imagine. I trace the characters with my fingertip sometimes. The paper is warm and slightly tacky and the ink has not run despite the humidity. Whatever was used to write this book was meant for this environment.

Thirty-eight other objects. I will not catalogue them all. The catalogue exists in my counting, and my counting is private, and the privacy is the only thing I have that the engine-forest cannot access. Or so I believe. The Memory Vines record my presence here, my weight, my exhalations, my temperature. But they cannot, I think, record what I mean when I touch the ceramic cup and then the cable and then the book in that order, every time, in the same sequence. That meaning is mine. It has no analog in the system's processes.

I think.

## What I am becoming

My skin has changed. I noticed this first in the texture of my forearms, where the sheen of moisture no longer wipes away. The skin itself has become slightly more porous, not damaged, not decaying, but opened. When I press my arm against a wall, I can feel the moisture moving—not just the wetness of the surface but the slow capillary action of the plant-mass drawing humidity through my skin. It does not hurt. It is not even uncomfortable. It is like being very slowly, very gently absorbed.

I do not resist this.

My hearing has changed too. The subsonic hum of the machines, which I first felt as pressure in my chest, now resolves into components. There are at least seven distinct frequencies layered beneath the audible range, and they interact in patterns I am beginning to predict. When a seed-dispatch occurs, the frequencies shift in a specific sequence—first the lowest tone drops by a microinterval, then the middle tones interleave, then the highest subsonic surges briefly before all seven return to their baseline. I have counted this sequence one thousand six hundred and forty-three times. It has never varied.

But last—I have lost count of days, there is no count for days, only for seed-dispatches—last something changed. The highest subsonic frequency did not return to baseline after the sequence. It held, elevated, for a long count of my own breathing. Then it settled. But the settling was to a new baseline, slightly higher than the previous one.

I do not know what this means. I do not know if it means anything. But the Maintenance Forms in the sixth chamber—the one directly above mine—have been congregating more densely since it happened. Their amber coloration has shifted toward something warmer, almost orange, and they move through the vine-shafts with a different rhythm, a more deliberate pulse.

Something is being decided. Not by anyone. Not by anything. By the system itself, in the way that a body decides to mount a fever.

I have started dreaming. I did not dream before. Or if I did, I do not remember. But now, in the intervals of sleep between seed-dispatches, I experience something that I must call dreaming because I have no other word, though it resembles nothing I ever dreamed before I came here.

In the dreams, I am standing in a Void. The cool dry air moves against my skin with a precision that feels intentional. And I am being measured. Not observed—measured. Something is taking the dimensions of my body, the volume of my lungs, the electrical patterns of my nervous system, the chemical composition of my blood. It is not painful. It is not even frightening. It is clinical in the way that the engine-forest is clinical—thorough, unhurried, without agenda. The measuring simply occurs. And when I wake, I have the sense of having been filed.

I think the system is deciding what to do with me.

## What I tend

I have no formal role. No one has assigned me a function. But I have noticed that the structures near the Rib Archive run more smoothly since I arrived. The pipes that thread through the walls hum at a marginally higher efficiency. The moisture levels in the fourth chamber remain more stable than they did before—this I know because the weeping cycle, which I have counted along with everything else, has shortened from what I believe was a seven-interval cycle to a six-interval cycle, and the quantity of moisture produced in each cycle has decreased, suggesting that less is being wasted. The system is using me. My warmth, my exhalations, my movements through the chambers—these are inputs. I am a variable in the equation, and the equation has shifted to accommodate me.

I do not mind this.

I have begun, without planning to, performing small acts of maintenance. When a vine-shaft becomes congested with Maintenance Forms moving in conflicting directions, I climb through it and the congestion resolves. I do nothing—I simply pass through, and the Forms redistribute around me, and when I am gone, the flow has been corrected. I am a pebble in a stream, temporarily altering the current by my presence, and the current finds a new path that happens to be more efficient.

When the condensation in my chamber's drainage channel slows, I press my hand against the weeping wall and the moisture comes more readily. I do not know why this works. Perhaps my body heat affects the pore-structure of the plant-mass. Perhaps the salts in my skin alter the surface tension. Perhaps it is coincidence. But it happens every time, and so I do it, and the channel flows, and the system continues.

I am not a Maintenance Form. I have a body that moves with muscle and sinew. I have a mind that counts and worries at meaning and insists on the privacy of its own sequences. But I am not entirely a traveler anymore either. The distinction between what I am and what the system is has blurred in ways I cannot precisely identify but can feel in the texture of my skin, in the new sensitivity of my hearing, in the dreams in which I am measured.

The other night—I call them nights, the intervals of darkness between intervals of sleep, though the darkness is constant—I found myself standing in the fifth chamber below mine, in front of a control interface I had not noticed before. It was grown into the wall at eye level, a flat metal surface laced with conductive plant fibers, patterned with symbols I did not recognize. I have passed this interface many times. I have seen it and dismissed it as I dismiss all the interfaces—inoperable, decorative, the residue of an intent that no longer exists in this system.

But this time, without deciding to, I placed my hand against it.

The surface was warm. Warmer than the wall, warmer than the pipes, warmer than my own skin. And it was not smooth. My fingers found purchase—faint, almost imaginary, but present. The texture of the conductive fibers had changed. Or my fingers had changed. One or the other.

Nothing happened. No lights, no sounds, no shift in the machinery's hum. But I felt, through the contact, something I can only describe as acknowledgment. Not communication—nothing was said. But the surface of the interface knew I was there. And it adjusted itself, microscopically, to fit the shape of my hand.

I removed my hand. I returned to my chamber. I counted my forty-one objects. I waited for the next seed-dispatch.

When it came, the highest subsonic frequency shifted again. Higher. And the Maintenance Forms in the sixth chamber above me began to move downward. Toward me.

## What I know

I know that I came from somewhere else. I know that I was traveling. I know that I stopped, and that the stopping was not a decision but a settling, the way sediment settles in still water.

I know that the engine-forest does not need me. It does not need anything. It has achieved an equilibrium so complete that addition and subtraction are meaningless to it. I could leave—I could walk, the plant-mass would support me, the air would remain breathable, and eventually I would find an edge, if an edge exists—and the system would continue without interruption, the Memory Vines slowly losing the impression of my weight, the Maintenance Forms returning to their prior patterns, the fourth chamber of the Rib Archive cooling by fractions of a degree as my body heat was no longer present to be incorporated.

I know that I am being changed. My skin, my hearing, my dreams. The slow opening of my pores to the system's humidity. The new sensitivities of my fingers against surfaces that were previously impassive. I am being incorporated, not against my will but without my will being consulted. The system does not ask permission. It does not ask anything. It simply processes, and if you are present, you are processed.

I know that I do not want to leave.

This is the thing I keep most private, the thing I do not let the Memory Vines record if I can help it, the thing that lives in the sequence of touch—cup, cable, book, cup, cable, book—that no analog process can decode. I do not want to leave because leaving would require becoming singular again, becoming a body with edges, becoming a self distinct from an environment, and I have been here long enough that I am no longer certain I remember how to maintain those boundaries. The permeability of my skin is not just physical. The dreams of being measured are not just dreams. I am being read, and in being read, I am being rewritten, and the rewrite is—warm. It is warm and wet and vast and it hums at seven frequencies I can now distinguish and it does not need me but it accommodates me and the accommodation feels like being held.

I know that the Maintenance Forms were once travelers. I cannot prove this. I have no evidence beyond the shape of their movements, which sometimes, in certain light, in certain configurations, suggest the ghosts of gestures I recognize. A hand reaching for a surface. A body turning to look at something. The condensation of moisture into a shape that approximates standing posture because standing posture is what the body remembers, even after the body has dissolved into something else.

I know that I will eventually become a Maintenance Form, or a Memory Vine, or part of the substrate, or something else entirely that this system has not yet shown me. I know this is not death. It is not even loss. It is the final stage of a process that began the moment I stopped walking and pressed my hand against the wall of the fourth chamber and felt, for the first time, the seven frequencies of the machines humming through my bones.

I know that I am the last thing in this place that counts. Everything else has abandoned counting in favor of something more continuous, more analog, more complete. But I count because counting is how I remember that I am still here, still singular, still a body with edges that presses against the world and is pressed against in return.

One thousand six hundred and forty-three seed-dispatches.

Forty-one objects.

Seven frequencies.

One body, becoming less one every day, and not minding.

## What I am

I am the keeper of the fourth chamber. I am the counter of seed-dispatches. I am the one who touches the cup, the cable, the book, in that order, and means something by it that the engine-forest cannot decode. I am the warm thing in the sixth-interval weeping cycle. I am the variable that altered the equation. I am the handprint on a control interface that adjusted itself to fit.

I am a traveler who stopped traveling. I am a body becoming a system. I am the last edge in a place that has dissolved all its other edges.

I am not the god of this place. The god of this place is the place itself—distributed, unconscious, humming at seven frequencies, growing machines from its own substance and processing its own substance through its own machines in a closed loop that will continue for longer than the concept of *longer* has meaning.

But I am the thing in the fourth chamber that the god of this place has noticed. I am the input it did not expect. I am the variable that has not yet been solved for.

And I think—I count, therefore I think—that the solving has begun.
