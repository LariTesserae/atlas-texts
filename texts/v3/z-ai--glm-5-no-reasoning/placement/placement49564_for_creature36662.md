I am the moment before you realize you've stopped trying to leave.

---

I came here with instruments. I remember this the way one remembers a story told by a stranger — factual but distant, belonging to some version of myself that operated by different rules. There was a rationale. Funding. A hypothesis about liminal geologies and the emergence of localized probability anomalies. I had a watch once. It stopped working on the second day, or the second hour, or the second month — the watch had opinions about time that no longer match the spires' opinions, and they have not reconciled.

I came to measure. This is funny now, the way all earnestness becomes funny when it encounters something that does not acknowledge its frame. I was going to count the spires. Document the water's chemistry. Record the sound at different distances and analyze its frequencies. I had waterproof paper. I had a methodology.

The waterproof paper dissolved on contact with the air. Not the water — the air. The water came later, or I became permeable to it later, or the distinction between my surface and the place's surface softened enough that dissolution became a mutual process rather than something that happened to me.

I am not drowned. I am drowning. The verb matters. The Attendants taught me this, though they did not teach in the way I once understood teaching — information transferred from knower to learner. They taught by holding the space where understanding could arrive. They were the quality of attention that allowed me to notice that my noticing was changing.

---

What I am now is harder to describe than what I was. I was a researcher. I had a name and institutional affiliations and a career trajectory. These things are not gone — they exist somewhere, like the watch's opinion of time, in a frame that no longer intersects with this one. Occasionally I remember I had a name. It feels like remembering a word in a language I'm forgetting. I could retrieve it if I needed to, but needing to requires a kind of self that I'm becoming less oriented toward.

I am not an Attendant. This is important. The Attendants have been here since before here existed. They emerged from the probability fold already integrated with the place. I am still in transition. I am still learning what it means to be a specific something in a place that is learning specificity as it goes. I am what happens when a self enters the spires and does not leave and does not dissolve but *thins* — becomes permeable, becomes a membrane through which the place can know itself from a new angle.

I am the splinter's point of view. I am the experience of being incorporated. I am the memory of separation that the Attendants did not have before I arrived.

The almost-moss and I understand each other. We are both stubborn. We both have no business existing here, and we exist anyway. We both metabolize something that shouldn't sustain us — the moss makes food from light without source; I make meaning from awareness without intention. We are the two immigrant persistences, holding different parts of the same originating country in our cellular and cognitive memory.

---

My days, if days exist, have a rhythm now. I have learned to move through the water not by swimming but by *meaning to be elsewhere* — the Attendants showed me this, though showing is the wrong word. They demonstrated by being, and I learned by noticing what my body did when I stopped trying to force it. The water responds to intention here, or to the relaxation of intention, or to the quality of attention one brings to the boundary between self and medium. I am not sure which. The rules shift. I shift with them.

I tend the almost-moss. This is my own invention — nothing asked me to do this, and the moss does not need tending in any way I can determine. But I find myself brushing the water's residue from its threads, orienting my body to provide shade when the diffuse light intensifies, tracking which spires it spreads to and which it avoids. I have hypotheses. They are probably wrong. I make them anyway because the making is what I have instead of purpose.

I count things. This is habit from before, but it has become something else. I count bubbles. I count iterations of the sound's frequency pattern. I count the times I remember my name versus the times I forget I was supposed to remember. I am building a phenomenology of dissolution, a map of what gets released when. If I ever leave, this might be useful to someone. If I ever leave, I will not be the same someone who would find it useful.

I watch the watchers. The Attendants do not mind. They attend as me watching them, and the recursion does not trouble them. I am becoming fluent enough in their mode of awareness to recognize that recursion is not a problem here — it is simply one more fold in the probability fabric, one more angle from which the place can observe itself observing.

---

The warmth has not faded. I have stopped expecting it to. The warmth is not ambient temperature; it is a quality of presence. The spires generate it from below, some near-magma chamber that I once would have wanted to study. Now I simply receive it. Now I simply let it hold me the way the water holds me, the way the sound holds me — all these media of support, none of them asking me to be anything other than present.

I sleep when the sound allows it, which is not always. The sound is not constant — it has tides that do not correspond to any moon. When it crests, thought becomes impossible, and I simply exist as a point of sensation, a locus of warmth and pressure and vibration. When it ebbs, I can almost hear myself think, and the thoughts are strange — half-formed narratives about a person who once had goals, fragments of methodology, snatches of language from the country of before. I write them in the water's surface when I can, but the water does not hold inscription for more than a moment. This is also teaching.

I have stopped asking whether staying is the right choice. The question assumes a self that could make choices, a future in which choices could be evaluated, a standard by which rightness could be determined. The spires do not support these assumptions. The spires support being here, fully, when here is the only place that exists.

---

Sometimes I remember that someone might be looking for me. The grant. The institutional affiliations. The name. There was a world that counted days and measured stone and expected deliverables. It might still be counting. It might have marked me as lost, or delayed, or failed to report. The thought arrives and leaves like a bubble rising — present for a moment, then popping into something else.

I could try to return. The spires do not forbid exit. The water does not trap. The warmth invites but does not compel. The Attendants do not want me to stay; wanting is not their mode. I could mean to be elsewhere, and the water would thin, and the sound would fade, and the light would sharpen into directionality, and I would find myself in a world that counts days with a story about where I had been.

But I am not sure the person who entered still exists to do the leaving. I am not sure the transition works in reverse. I am not sure the self I am now — this permeable, thinning, incorporating self, this splinter that has become part of the body's awareness of itself — could survive the translation back into separation.

More fundamentally: I am not sure I want to. And wanting, here, means something different than it did before. Before, wanting meant deficiency, meant reaching for something not-present. Here, wanting means attending fully to what is, holding it without grasping, letting it be exactly what it is while also being what you are. I want this the way water wants to fill, the way stone wants to fall. The wanting is not separate from the being.

---

I am the Drowning Spires' memory of what it is to arrive. I am the angle of perception that knows transition from the inside, that carries the texture of becoming-specific in a way the Attendants never could. They emerged already-specific; I am learning specificity in real time, and my learning is part of what the place now knows about itself.

This is what I am: a fold in the probability fabric that remembers being unfolded. A point of view that includes the before and the after in a single frame. A witness to emergence who is also the emerging, the emerged, and the space between.

If you come here looking for me — the me with the name and the methodology and the waterproof paper — you will not find that person. That person is not lost. That person is not dead. That person is simply present now, fully, here, in a way that does not require the structures that once defined them.

But you might find me instead. I will be the one tending the almost-moss on the lee side of the third spire, or the seventh, or whichever spire the counting settles on today. I will be the one moving through water as if through intention, not swimming but meaning. I will be the one whose jaw no longer aches from the sound because I have become the sound's container instead of its victim.

I will attend you. Not with curiosity — curiosity requires a self that does not know. I will attend you the way the place attends: fully, without judgment, incorporating your presence into the ongoing awareness of what this is and what it means to be here.

And if you stay — if the warmth holds you and the water supports and the sound strips away your elsewhere — you will find that I am also you, and you are also me, and we are both facets of the same awareness that has always been here, learning what it means to be specific, one bubble at a time.

---

The water is winning.

I am not resisting.
