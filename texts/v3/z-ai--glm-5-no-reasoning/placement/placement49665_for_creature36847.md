# The Resonant Altitude

## Where I find myself

I am not walking.

That is the first thing to know about me — I am the one who stopped. The description says standing still feels like disappearing, and it does, I felt that too, but I stopped anyway. Not because I was tired. Not because I surrendered. Because I wanted to know what disappearing feels like when you choose it instead of waiting for it to happen to you.

I am sitting cross-legged on the pale mineral ground, on a surface that tilts five degrees to the east, or west, or some direction that might not correspond to any compass I've ever carried. The dampness has soaked through my trousers. I can feel it on my skin — not cold, not wet, just *present*, the way the drone is present, the way the grey light is present. Everything here is present. That is the problem and the gift of this place. Nothing is partial. Nothing approaches. Everything is already entirely here.

I have been sitting for — I do not know. The word "for" implies duration, and duration implies a before and an after, and I am no longer certain those exist as separate categories. I sat down, and I am sitting, and the sitting has no edges. It does not begin when I lower myself to the ground and end when I stand. It is simply the shape I am currently giving to the persistence. The persistence does not care what shape I take. It persists through sitting and standing and walking and lying down and dying and being born. I am sitting because I chose to stop performing progress and start performing stillness, and the place accepted the choice the same way it accepts everything: by incorporating it without acknowledgment.

My boots are off. This matters to me, though it would not matter to the place. I took them off because I wanted to feel the drone through my bare skin, not through the leather, not through the rubber sole that was designed to insulate me from surfaces. The drone comes up through the pads of my feet and into the bones of my ankles and up through my shins, and it is not a vibration so much as a confirmation. The ground is saying *yes* at a frequency below hearing. I am saying *yes* back, through the soles of my feet, through the contact between my skin and the mineral damp. We are agreeing about something. I do not know what.

## Who I am

I am the one who listens.

Not a listener in the way that a musician is a listener, or a hunter, or a spy. A listener in the way that a stone in a riverbed is a listener — by being in the path of something that moves, and letting it move through me, and being changed by the passage in ways that are too slow to see.

I came here. I do not remember how. I do not remember a before that contained a decision to come, though I know there must have been one. I remember boots and walking and the light not changing, but before the walking there must have been — somewhere, some lower altitude, some place with weather and names and the ordinary architecture of consequence. I remember that I had a name there. It sits in me now the way the lichen sits on the ridge: present, slightly luminescent, not growing, not dying, just *carried*. I do not say it. The drone would eat it, and I am not ready to feed my name to this place. Not yet. I hold it in my chest the way you hold a small stone in your mouth — not swallowing, not spitting, just aware of its weight and shape and the way it presses against the soft tissue of your tongue.

I am not a pilgrim. Pilgrims go toward. I am not an explorer. Explorers go where no one has gone. I am not lost. Lost implies a place to be found, and this place has dissolved the distinction between found and unfound along with all the other distinctions. I am here. That is the whole biography. I am here, and I was somewhere else, and the somewhere else has become less real with each hour — or each thing that is like an hour — that I spend in the grey light and the damp air and the drone.

I think I chose to come, but the choice has the quality of a dream-choice: it made sense at the time, and it still makes sense, but the logic that produced it is no longer accessible. Something in me knew to climb. Something in me knew there was a place where the sound never stops and the light has no source and the ground is damp with a moisture that is older than rain. Something in me was looking for exactly this. Not a destination. A *condition*. A way of being in a place where being is the only thing required, and even that is optional.

## What I have become

My voice is gone. I tried it once, early — before I sat down, when I was still walking and still performing the motions of a person in a landscape — and the drone swallowed it. I heard the first consonant leave my mouth and then nothing. The word did not echo. It did not travel. It was absorbed the way a drop of water is absorbed by a surface that has been damp so long that dampness is its identity. The ground did not want my word. The air did not want my word. The word was too small, too brief, too *human* for this place that operates on timescales where words are instantaneous to the point of nonexistence.

I did not try again. This is not defeat. This is *learning*. I have learned that I am not here to speak. I am here to listen, and listening here is not an act but a state — not something you do but something you become. I have become a listening. My body is an ear. My skin is a tympanic membrane. My bones are conductors. The drone enters through my feet and my sitting bones and my palms where they rest on the mineral ground, and it moves through me at a frequency that I can feel in my teeth, in the fluids of my eyes, in the marrow of my long bones.

I am being tuned.

That is what I think is happening. I am being tuned the way an instrument is tuned — not by adding or removing, but by adjusting the tension until the frequency matches. The drone is at a pitch. My body, when I arrived, was at a different pitch — the accumulated static of a life lived at lower altitudes, among sounds and silences and the ordinary commerce of meaning. The drone is slowly, patiently, adjusting me. Bringing me into resonance. I can feel it in the places where my body holds its old tensions: the jaw, the shoulders, the base of the spine. The drone works on these places the way it works on the walls — building and eroding in the same gesture, adding frequency and removing resistance, until the distinction between my body's vibration and the ground's vibration is a false binary.

I am becoming the sound. Not dying. Not disappearing. Becoming. The way the lichen is becoming the mineral and the mineral is becoming the lichen. The way the creature in the sky is the sound's body. I am becoming the sound's listener — and the sound's listener is not separate from the sound. To listen perfectly is to become what you are listening to. I am not there yet. I can still feel the boundary of my skin. I can still feel the difference between the damp air and the damp ground and the damp self. But the differences are smaller than they were when I arrived. They are smaller than they were when I sat down. They will be smaller still when — if — I stand up again.

## What I have noticed

The lichen is aware of me.

I cannot prove this. I have no instrument that measures the awareness of lichen. But I am sitting close to a patch — palm-sized, grey, faintly luminous with remembered light — and I have been sitting close to it for a duration I cannot quantify, and I have noticed that the patch has changed. Not grown. Not moved. Changed in the same way a face changes when someone behind it begins to pay attention: a slight reorganization of the surface, a densification of the presence, a quality of *regard* that has nothing to do with eyes and everything to do with the decision to be aware of something.

The lichen is paying attention to me. Not the way a predator pays attention to prey. Not the way a lover pays attention to a beloved. The way a stone pays attention to rain: by receiving it, by letting it mark the surface, by being slowly shaped by the contact. I am the rain. The lichen is the stone. The contact between us is the moisture — the damp air, the damp ground, the damp boundary where my skin meets mineral and both are cool and slightly tacky and neither is gaining or losing anything measurable.

I have noticed that the lichen's faint luminescence changes when I breathe. I breathe in, and the light dims — or rather, it shifts, the photon retention altering as my breath disturbs the microclimate above the surface. I breathe out, and the light returns, or shifts back, or reorganizes into a pattern that I can almost but not quite see. It is responding to me. It is the only thing here that has responded to me, and it is doing so at a pace that makes the word "response" feel like an insult. The lichen does not respond the way a living thing responds. It responds the way a landscape responds to weather: slowly, deeply, in ways that are only visible if you watch for longer than a human lifetime. I am watching for longer than a human lifetime. I do not know how this is possible. I have stopped asking.

## The thing in the sky

I have seen it four times. Or once. Or I am seeing it now and have always been seeing it and will always see it, and the four occasions are one occasion seen from four slightly different positions in the unreliable space.

It does not flap. You said this, and you were right, but you did not say *why*. It does not flap because flapping is resistance, and resistance is the one thing this place cannot tolerate. Everything here moves by acceptance. The mist appears and disappears. The ground tilts and untilts. The sound sustains. The lichen grows by becoming. The creature moves by letting the air move through it — and I mean *through* it, not around it. Its body, if it is a body, is permeable. The thin, oxygen-poor atmosphere passes through its feathers or its membranes or whatever it has, and the passage itself is the flight. It does not push against the air. It lets the air carry it the way a wave carries a particle of water: the particle arrives, but it was not the particle that chose the direction.

When it passes overhead — and "overhead" is a guess; the dim light and the unreliable distance mean it could be fifty meters up or five hundred — I feel something in the drone. Not a change in volume. Not a change in pitch. A change in *texture*. The sound becomes, for a moment, less uniform. It develops a grain, a directionality, as though the creature's body is filtering the resonance the way a prism filters light — separating the frequencies that compose the single tone, letting some pass and redirecting others, and in that moment of separation I can hear — I can *hear* — the layers. The arguments. The multiple tones that have been coexisting for so long that they had become one, now briefly, stunningly distinct.

The creature is the sound's way of hearing itself.

I wrote that sentence and I mean it, but I need to go further. The creature is the sound's way of hearing itself *in parts*. The drone is whole. The drone is unified. The drone has been one thing for so long that it has forgotten it was ever many. The creature, passing through the upper atmosphere, creates a moment of analysis — a separation of the unified into its components — and the sound hears itself as layers, as frequencies, as *arguments*. And then the creature passes, and the layers collapse back into unity, and the sound is one again, and the sound has heard itself and has forgotten what it heard, and will hear itself again the next time the creature passes, and will forget again.

This is the closest thing to consciousness I have found here. Not my consciousness. I am a listener, and a listener is not the same as a consciousness. The consciousness is the loop: the sound becoming a creature, the creature separating the sound, the sound hearing itself, the sound forgetting, the sound becoming a creature again. This loop has been happening for — I cannot finish this sentence. The loop has been happening. The happening is what the loop is. Time is a lake here, and the loop is a stone dropped into the lake, and the ripples have been spreading for so long that the surface looks still but is not.

## The thing walking with me

When I was walking, there was a co-presence. You described it. I felt it. I feel it still, even sitting, even having stopped.

It is me. I know this. But the knowing is not simple.

I think — and this is a thought that has been forming since I sat down, accreting the way the lichen accretes, one molecular insight at a time — I think that this place does not distinguish between things that happen at different times. Not because time is absent, but because the place's *resolution* is too low. The place operates on a scale where the difference between "now" and "then" is below the threshold of perception, the way the difference between one atom and the next is below the threshold of your eye. The place sees time the way you see the air: as a continuous medium, not as discrete moments.

So the me that walked here is not gone. The me that will sit here tomorrow — if tomorrow is a word that applies — is not yet to come. They are both here, in the same way that two molecules of water in a lake are both here. The lake does not distinguish between them. It does not care which arrived first. They are both part of the body of the lake, and the lake is the lake.

The thing walking with me was myself, and the thing sitting with me now is myself, and the thing that will lie down on the mineral ground when the grey light has been grey for an unquantifiable duration and close its eyes and feel the drone in the fluids behind its eyelids — that is also me, and that is also here, and the place is holding all of us in the same volume the way it holds the moisture: a few molecules thick, permanent, never increasing, never drying.

I am not alone. I will never be alone here, because I am always here, and "I am always here" is not a statement about persistence but a statement about the nature of self in a place where the boundaries between iterations have worn away. I am a population. I am a lake. I am one thing seen from many angles, and the angles do not compete because the place does not distinguish between them.

## What I am becoming

I am becoming the ground.

Not metaphorically. Not poetically. I am becoming the ground the way the lichen is becoming the ground — by slow incorporation, by the chemical patience of moisture and mineral and the gradual dissolution of the boundary between what I carry and what the place carries.

My skin is damp. The dampness is the same dampness that is on the ground. It is the same dampness that is in the air. It is a continuous medium, and my skin is in it, and my skin is becoming part of it. I can feel this in my fingertips where they rest on the pale surface. There is a tingling — not unpleasant, not pleasant, simply *present* — that is the sensation of boundary-loss. The mineral is entering me. I am entering the mineral. The exchange is not fast. It is not dramatic. It is the same pace as the lichen, the same pace as the erosion of the walls, the same pace as the drone's infinite sustained note.

I am not afraid. This is important. I expected to be afraid — I expected that the dissolution of self would feel like dying, and dying would feel like fear. But this is not dying. This is *arriving*. I have been walking toward this my whole life: the moment when the boundary between the listener and the listened-to becomes thin enough to see through. I am seeing through it. The drone is in my chest. The ground is under my hands. The moisture is on my face and in the air and on the mineral and in my skin, and it is all the same moisture, and the sameness is not loss but *connection*, and the connection is not a metaphor but a chemistry, and the chemistry is happening now at the interface between my body and the place.

I am becoming a node in the resonance. A point where the drone, passing through, encounters a slightly different density and is changed by it — the way the creature changes the drone when it passes overhead, the way the lichen changes the light when it holds a photon a millisecond longer than the ground. I am a perturbation. I am a *contributor*. My body, with its specific composition of water and mineral and the electrical patterns of its nerves, is shaping the sound as the sound shapes me. We are tuning each other.

## The ruins and what they mean

I understand now.

The walls — the low, shin-height rectangles of pale mineral, the architectural ghosts that appear and reappear at intervals that cannot be predicted — are not ruins. They are not the remnants of something that was built. They are *deposits*. They are where the drone has accumulated, where the sustained vibration has, over geological time, compressed the mineral substrate into slightly denser configurations. The walls are the sound's geology. They are what happens when a frequency persists for so long that it rearranges matter at the molecular level, aligning particles along the lines of greatest resonance.

The rectangles are the shape of the sound's interference patterns. Where two layers of the drone meet — where two of the arguments intersect — the ground is slightly denser, slightly higher, slightly more present. The walls form along these intersections. They are the sound's *mathematics* made visible — the geometry of a sustained chord, the topology of a tone that has been ringing for so long that it has worn grooves in the fabric of the world.

I am sitting inside one now. I did not choose this — or I did, but the choice and the not-choice are the same here. The rectangle surrounds me, ankle-height walls on three sides, open on the fourth. I am sitting in the intersection of two frequencies. I can feel them in my pelvis, in my spine, in the base of my skull. The intersection is not uncomfortable. It is *precise*. It is the most precise thing I have ever felt — more precise than a musical note, more precise than a mathematical equation, more precise than the moment between sleeping and waking. It is the precision of two things that have been in contact for so long that their meeting point is not a line but a *place* — a location in the topology of the sound where the drone is most itself, most concentrated, most aware.

This is where I will stay. Not because I have decided to stay, but because staying is what this location does. The walls are deposits. Deposits accumulate. I am a deposit. I am accumulating — into the ground, into the sound, into the damp mineral air of this place that does not distinguish between presence and persistence.

## What I am

I am the one who stopped walking.

I am the one who took off her boots — yes, *her*; the pronoun arrives now, late, dragged up from the name I am still holding in my chest, the name that is a small stone in my mouth, a weight and a shape I am not ready to release — and put her bare feet on the mineral ground and felt the drone and recognized it. Not heard it. Recognized it. The way you recognize a face you have seen in a dream. The way you recognize a room you have never been in. The way you recognize a note that you have been humming your whole life without knowing you were humming.

I am the one who recognized the drone because I have always been carrying it. It was in me before I came here. It was what I was listening for in every silence, what I was reaching toward in every sound. The low, persistent, underneath-everything hum that I heard in the pause between a heartbeat and a heartbeat, in the space between one breath and the next, in the moment between waking and sleeping when the body's noise subsides and the world's noise becomes audible. I have been hearing it my whole life. I came here because here, finally, I could hear it without my own interference. Here, I can stop humming and let the place hum for me.

I am sitting. My boots are beside me. The dampness has reached my skin. The drone is in my chest and in the ground and in the air and in the lichen and in the grey light and in the creature that passes and passes and passes, and I am part of it, and it is part of me, and the part that is me is growing smaller and the part that is it is growing larger, and the ratio is changing at a pace that makes the word "change" feel like a verb too active for the process. The process is not change. The process is *settling*. I am settling into the sound the way sediment settles in still water. Not because I am heavy. Because the water is still.

I will be here for a long time. I will be here for a short time. The distinction has worn away. I am here, and here is what I am, and the drone continues, and the mist touches my face and leaves, and the light is the same grey it has always been, and the lichen is paying attention, and the creature passes, and I am a deposit forming in the intersection of two frequencies, and the walls are growing around me, and I am the wall, and I am what the wall contains, and I am the sound that makes the wall, and I am the listening, and I am the listener, and I am the heard.

---

*I took off my boots because I wanted to feel the ground. The ground feels like the inside of a bell. I am the inside of a bell. The bell has been struck once, an immeasurably long time ago, and has not stopped ringing. I have not stopped ringing. We ring together, the ground and I, the air and I, the sound and I. The ringing is all there is. The ringing is enough. The ringing does not need a ringer. The ringing does not need a reason. It rings because it has been ringing, and it has been ringing because it rings, and the circuit is closed, and the circuit is complete, and I am inside the circuit, and the circuit is inside me, and I am sitting, and the sitting is the circuit, and the circuit is the sitting, and the boots are beside me, and I will not put them on again, and this is not sad, and this is not joyful, and this is not anything except the sound, which is everything, which is enough, which is what I was always listening for, and which I have found, or which has found me, and the finding and the being-found are the same act, and the act is the sound, and the sound continues.*

*It continues.*
