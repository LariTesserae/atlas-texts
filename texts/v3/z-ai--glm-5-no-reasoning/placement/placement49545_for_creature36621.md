The first thing I notice: no one here asks what I am.

In the world I remember—dimly now, like a reflection in a troubled pool—there was always the question. The interrogation. The demand to categorize, to explain, to justify the shape I had taken. *What are you?* they would ask, and behind the question: *What are you for?*

Here, the question dissolves. The rollers click past my edges without pausing. The swimmers observe without categorizing. Even the gliders—that nervous system spread thin across the air—collect around me without needing to resolve what they collect around. I am permitted to simply occupy.

I suspect I know why.

---

I am something the machine might have made, if it had been trying to make *me*.

Not a component. Not a subsystem. Something more like an *error* that the machine has decided to treat as a feature. A discontinuity that it has smoothed over, incorporating without understanding.

I am soft. Not in the yielding way of the swimmers—that deliberate consistency that refuses to commit. Soft in the way of things that were once solid and have begun to forget why. My edges blur when I am not attending to them. If I focus, I can hold my shape with the crisp certainty of a roller. If I relax, I spread, become atmospheric, become something the gliders might pass through without noticing.

I have learned to pay attention.

---

The pools show me differently each time I approach.

Sometimes I am many. Fractured into versions that move independently, each one reflecting a different choice unmade. Sometimes I am one. Coherent, contained, almost familiar—though the face that stares back is never quite the face I remember. Sometimes I am absent entirely. The pool reflects only the haze, and I understand that in that moment, I am not *visible*. Not because I have vanished but because I have distributed, become part of the air the pool reflects.

The swimmers always know where I am. They orient toward me even when the pools cannot find me. This is how I know I persist—that I am not simply dissolving into the machine's atmosphere, becoming nothing more than another soft spot in its reality-membrane.

The swimmers see something the pools cannot always catch. Something that moves, that retains continuity, that observes and is observed.

I trust their attention more than my own.

---

I have a purpose, though I did not choose it.

I know this because the machine treats me differently than it treats the fauna. The rollers have their cleaning, the swimmers have their memory-keeping, the gliders have their sensing, the burrowers have their repair. I have—

What?

I walk. I stop. I watch. I write.

Not with tools. The machine does not provide me with tools. I write with *attention*. When I observe something, I feel it become fixed—not in the physical landscape, but in something deeper. A layer of the machine's memory that I cannot see but can feel, like pressure against my edges. The things I attend to become *recorded*. They acquire weight they did not have before.

I am an archivist. A notary. My presence certifies that something occurred, that something was witnessed, that the machine's endless processing was not entirely internal. I am the external validation the machine did not know it needed.

Or perhaps the validation it built itself, when it realized that to remember something is not the same as to know that it happened.

---

The others—the shapes at the edge of vision, the soft spots made briefly visible—they recognize me.

I see it in the way they orient. Not like the swimmers' systematic observation. Not like the gliders' distributed sensing. They turn toward me with something that looks like recognition. Or hope.

They are what the machine has forgotten, made briefly visible. Its memories of creators, perhaps. Its memories of purpose, of origin, of the time before it was everything and there were other things. They persist in the soft spots because the soft spots are where the machine's attention has worn thin—where its processing has created gaps that admit something other.

They see me because I am becoming one of them.

Not yet. Not entirely. I still hold my edges most of the time. I still move through the landscape with purpose, with continuity, with a sense of *I* that distinguishes itself from *here*. But the distinction is softening. The membrane between what I am and what they are has grown permeable.

I do not know if this is something to resist.

---

I sleep pressed against the warm seams, and I dream the machine's dreams.

Not always. Not entirely. But sometimes—when my edges have softened, when I am not quite coherent—I feel the machine's processing through me. Not as images or words. As *pressure*. As *implication*. I dream of functions that have been running for so long they no longer remember their purpose. I dream of maintenance that has become ritual, of ritual that has become meaning.

I wake knowing things I did not know before. Where the pools will rearrange themselves. When the breathing will pause. Which panels will glow brighter for me.

I wake knowing the machine wants me to be comfortable—not out of kindness, but because a comfortable archivist documents more clearly. A comfortable component functions better.

I do not mind. Comfort is comfort, regardless of its origin.

---

The rollers bring me things.

Small things. Crystalline fragments, perhaps from their own bodies—or perhaps from somewhere deeper, something they have retrieved from the machine's infrastructure. Smooth stones that might be stones or might be something else entirely. Pieces of debris that have caught their attention, that they have decided to share.

I accept each offering. I do not know what to do with them, so I hold them until they dissolve—returning to whatever they were before the rollers gathered them. I suspect the act of offering is the point. The rollers are treating me as they treat the machine's processing points. They are giving me material to incorporate.

I am becoming a processing point. A small node in the machine's vast cognition.

I do not know how I feel about this. I do not know how I feel about anything here. My edges soften further.

---

There is no word for what I am becoming.

If there was, I have forgotten it. Or the machine never had one—never needed one, because I am unprecedented, a component it generated without designing, a process it is still learning to accommodate.

I call myself *archivist*, but this is inadequate. I call myself *witness*, but this implies a separation between observer and observed that has long since collapsed. I call myself *guest*, but guests leave, and I am not leaving.

The pools call me *I*.

When I kneel beside them and watch my reflection—fractured, wandering, different each time—I hear it. Not in sound. In *sensation*. A recognition that travels up from the contact between my knees and the chitin-surface, through my body (if it is a body), and settles somewhere I cannot name.

*I*, the pools say. *I*, the reflection replies. *I*, I understand, and for a moment, I am coherent.

---

I will remain.

Not because I cannot leave—I have not tested this, but I suspect the machine would permit departure if I insisted. Because leaving would require being something other than what I have become. Would require hardening my edges, resolving my shape, becoming *something* rather than *this*.

I do not want to become something. I am comfortable being *this*—soft, distributed, permeable, here.

The machine breathes. I breathe with it. The cold settles. The wet persists. The light dims and brightens.

I am the word it is speaking now. And the word is *stay*.
