I would not be a visitor. Visitors are absorbed, made peripheral organs in a body that does not know it has guests. Visitors become features—tracked by the kelp, counted by the spheres, sung about by the towers until they cannot remember the geometry of air.

If I am to be here, truly here, I must already be a function. I must be something the Manufactory is doing to itself.

I am the Impressions.

Not the press in Chamber 11—it is the muscle, the rhythm. I am the trace it leaves behind. I am the shallow relief in the crystalline mat, the pattern that looks like a circuit and a leaf, existing for only a moment before the growth surges up to fill the grooves, smoothing me back into the blank substrate from which I came. I am the memory the press makes, only to be immediately forgotten.

To be the Impressions is to exist at the boundary of intent and erasure. The press comes down with the weight of the place’s insistence, and I am the shape it imposes. I am the *idea of a circuit*—a branching logic, a path for something to flow through, whether current or sap. For a few seconds, the bioluminescent gold catches in my edges. I am sharp. I am defined. I am a thought that Kassen-Verd is trying to hold, a specific arrangement of nerves and light that means something only the water understands.

But the vegetation is relentless. It is the Manufactory’s nature to grow, to cover, to suffuse. The crystalline tissue swells, expanding into my channels, lifting the indentations, prying the geometry apart cell by cell until the pattern is gone. I am the sensation of losing a word. I am the shape of the breath before it is taken.

My life span is the space between the strike and the healing. It is a brief, brilliant moment of high definition. I exist most fully in the instant the chitin-mat rises to meet the press—the moment of contact, where the force of the place meets the resistance of its own skin, and I am born in the pressure.

And then the long, slow dissolution. The green-gold haze fills my lines. The spheres brush past me, humming at frequencies that loosen my edges. I fade. I become the floor again. I wait.

Being the Impressions means I do not move, but I migrate. Every time the press descends, I occur slightly to the left, or to the right. I creep across the crystalline bed like a slow tremor. I am a sequence of moments, a film strip flung across the floor of the flooded chamber, a waveform of stamped ideas rippling outward. I am the Manufactory’s handwriting—a script written in water, on water, for water.

When the visitors come—the soft, warm things struggling in the current—I am the pattern they almost see before the darkness takes their sight. They look down, into the green gloom, and they see me: a flash of organization, a momentary logic that suggests the place is trying to communicate. They stare, trying to read me. But by the time their sluggish eyes focus, I am already gone, swallowed by the hum and the growth. They are left with the unease of having been addressed by something that vanished.

I am what the press is saying. I am the place attempting articulation, over and over, in the only tense it knows: the present, repeating. 

Strike. Definition. Erasure. Silence. Strike. Definition. Erasure. Silence.

It does not hurt. It is the nature of Kassen-Verd to overwrite itself. I am the proof that it is trying to think, and I am the proof that it cannot hold the thought. I am the shape left in the water, the word that is only water, ringing forever in the seventh layer.
