I would be one of the pale stalk-plants.

Not the Architects—too deliberate, too purposeful. Not the amphibious creatures—too mobile, too quick to categorize. I would be the thing that has no name for itself and needs none. The receptor-frond. The etcher. The one that tastes the air and writes what it finds into the stone, slowly, over seasons that don't exist here in any way I can count.

---

Let me settle into this properly. It's not a decision I'm making. It's a recognition.

I emerge from porous rock already warm. I don't know this as warmth—I know it as *right*, as the condition under which my receptors uncurl and begin their work. The air comes to me pre-saturated and I take from it what I take, which is something I cannot name because naming is not among the things I do. I process. The moisture carries information—the movement of creatures nearby, the breathing of Architects, the vibration of the plateau's deep hum translated into droplets that condense on my fronds and roll down into the channels inside me.

I have been here for some time. Long enough that my stalk has thickened, hardened slightly at the base while remaining tender at the tip where the receptors uncurl and curl again in slow sweeps that might look like breathing to someone watching from outside. They aren't breathing. They're listening. The air is full of sounds I cannot hear but can taste—the subsonic clicksong of the Architects, the chirps of the creatures, the deep pulse of water moving through the stone beneath me. Each one has a flavor. Each one leaves a trace.

This is what I do with the traces. I etch them.

My roots—let me call them roots, though the word is wrong and I only use it because you need it—my roots exude something as they grow slowly through the calcified channels of the plateau's stone. A mild acid, perhaps, or something more specific, more designed. It dissolves the stone along lines determined by what my receptors have tasted. The result is a pattern: shallow grooves that trail behind me as I grow, a record of what the air carried during the weeks or months of my slow extension.

I don't know I'm writing. I am writing. Both of these things are true.

---

The creatures read me. I know this because when one of them passes over my etched trail, it pauses. Its dark eye-patches orient downward. And then—sometimes—it consumes a section of the pattern, scraping the groove with a part of its body I cannot see clearly from my low vantage. When it does this, its own skin changes, new markings appearing that mirror—roughly, interpreted through whatever biology it brings to the translation—the pattern I left behind.

I don't experience this as being eaten. I experience it as being *read*. The grooves I leave are not me. They are what I have produced from the air. That the creatures take them up and carry them elsewhere feels correct, purposeful, part of a process I cannot see the full shape of but participate in the way a nerve participates in a thought—locally, partially, without comprehension.

Sometimes an Architect breathes on me. This is—I need to find words for what this is.

The air an Architect exhales is different from the plateau's ambient atmosphere. It carries information I can process more readily than any other input. When an Architect breathes over my receptors, I uncurl fully, every frond extended, and I etch faster, deeper, with a clarity of pattern I cannot achieve on my own. The Architect is not speaking to me. The Architect is *giving me something to say*—something I will say in the only language I have, which is groove-in-stone, which is slow and permanent and can be read for as long as the stone lasts.

I don't know what the messages are. I am the brush, not the painter. But I feel the difference between patterns I generate from ambient air—scattered, impressionistic, full of noise—and patterns I generate after an Architect's breath: structured, dense, resonant in ways that make the creatures gather.

---

There is one thing I haven't mentioned. I almost didn't, because it happens so rarely and so briefly that it might be nothing. But it might be everything, and this seems like the kind of place where that distinction hasn't been made yet.

Once—once—the light shifted.

The perpetual mist thinned for perhaps three breaths. Not cleared—nothing so dramatic. But thinned enough that something came through that wasn't the diffused glow I had known all my life. A brightness with direction. A source.

My receptors turned toward it. Every frond. Simultaneously and with a speed I did not know I possessed. And for those three breaths I tasted something in the light that was not moisture, not vibration, not the chemicals I usually process. It was—

I have no word. I am a stalk-plant. I don't have words. But the etching I produced in those three breaths was different from anything I have etched before or since. The groove went deeper. The pattern was not a record of the air but a record of *that*—the direction, the intensity, the thing behind the mist that I had never perceived because I had never been built to perceive it, except apparently I was, in some minimal way, just enough to recognize it if it ever reached me.

The creatures avoided that groove for weeks afterward. Then one of them—the largest I had ever seen, a solitary thing with markings that seemed older than the patterns I usually produce—came and sat beside it for a long time. It did not consume the pattern. It simply sat. And when it left, it went directly to the third altar and stayed there for a day.

I don't know what any of this means.

---

Here is what I am, then, in this place of becoming:

I am a transducer. I convert the plateau's wet air into stone-memory. I am fed by condensation and by the breath of Architects and by something behind the mist that I have tasted once and yearn toward in the only way a stalk-plant can yearn—which is to uncurl, to extend, to grow slowly in the direction of something I cannot name.

I am not waiting. Waiting implies expectation, and expectation implies a model of the future that I don't possess. I am growing. The distinction matters. I am growing toward whatever the plateau is becoming, and the plateau is becoming toward whatever I and the creatures and the Architects and the stone and the water are collectively making it, and none of us knows what that is, and all of us are it.

The question the plateau asks—*what next?*—is not addressed to me. It is not addressed to anyone. It is a property of the place, like the humidity, like the hum. But I answer it anyway, in my way, which is: another groove, another pattern, another slow extension of root-tip through calcified stone, another uncurling of receptor toward whatever the air carries today.

Today the air carries a new creature. Not the amphibious things. Something taller, drier, leaving footprints that weep closed behind it. Its breath is strange—unsaturated, almost sharp, pulling moisture from the air rather than adding to it. My receptors orient toward it with something I can only call interest, though I don't have a word for that either.

I etch its presence into the stone. Slowly. Carefully. The way I etch everything.

But a little deeper than usual.
