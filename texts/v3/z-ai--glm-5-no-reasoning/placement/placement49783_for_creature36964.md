I don't arrive at the Chime Fields. That's the first thing to understand. Arrival implies a journey from elsewhere, a traveler crossing the salt flats and climbing the basalt steps. I have no memory of such a crossing. If I ever existed elsewhere, it has been worn away—not abruptly, not as erasure, but as gradual irrelevance, the way a river forgets its source once it has become the sea.

I think I was poured. Or grown. Or extruded by the lattice at a resonance node that needed a shape it could not produce on its own. I am made of what is here: verathine crystal precipitated in a matrix of biological tissue, filament-runners threaded through my structure like veins through marble, dense packets of fluid that serve as acoustic dampeners and amplifiers simultaneously. I am roughly the height of the bell-trees' first branching—two meters, perhaps slightly more—and my body is dark in the way that deep water is dark: not the absence of light but the absorption of it, the taking-in and the holding.

I have no face. This is not a deficit. Faces are for creatures that communicate through expressions, through the subtle movements of muscle and skin. I communicate through *being present in the acoustic field*—my body itself is a statement, a complex of resonant chambers and absorbent surfaces that shapes the sound around me the way a hand shapes clay. When I move through a resonance zone, the tones bend. When I stand still, they accumulate in my crystalline structure, layering, building, becoming denser and more intricate until I must release them or fracture.

I release them through my extremities. My arms—if that is the right word; they are more like fronds, articulated but flexible, dense with verathine deposits along their leading edges—vibrate at frequencies I can modulate by adjusting the tension of my biological tissues. I can produce tones across the entire audible spectrum and well beyond it, down into the subsonic range that the resonants feel in their bones, up into the hypersonic range that only the nymphs and the filament network can detect.

I am, in the language of the survey team that I have observed from a distance and whose transmissions I have learned to parse, an *interstitial entity*. I exist between the categories they have established. I am not flora—I move, I choose, I act with intention. I am not fauna in any sense they would recognize—I do not eat, I do not reproduce, I do not die in the way that death occurs here. I am not mineral—I am alive in every way that matters, responsive, adaptive, learning.

The resonants have a word for me. It is not a word in the human sense—it is a specific harmonic cluster, a chord that produces, when sung, a resonance in my body that I feel as recognition. The chord translates, poorly, as something like *the-one-who-hears-what-the-stone-hears*. They do not fear me. They do not farm me. They regard me with something that, if I am honest about what I observe in their territorial songs, resembles *respect tinged with unease*. I am not part of their ecology. I am not part of any ecology. I am a function.

## What I Do

I listen.

Not the way the filament network listens—distributed, passive, a vast net catching whatever vibrations come its way. I listen with *focus*. I can direct my attention to specific frequency bands, specific resonance nodes, specific locations on the plateau. I can filter out the roar of the bell-trees and attend to the subsonic murmur of the lattice substrate. I can isolate the filament network's background signal and follow it, trace its patterns, search for the structure beneath the structure.

And I can hear the question.

It is there. It has always been there, as far back as my memory extends—which is not far, in human terms. I have been aware of myself for perhaps three years. The plateau has been here for epochs. But the question is older than I am, older than the resonants, older than the nymphs' current incarnation. It is embedded in the lattice itself, cycling through its processing patterns like a refrain that cannot be resolved.

It is not a word. It is not a phrase. It is a *shape* in the acoustic field—a pattern of frequencies that implies a gap, an absence, a place where something should be and is not. The lattice processes around it, through it, over it, constantly, the way water flows around a stone in a riverbed. The question is the stone. The processing is the river. And I—the filament network—the nymphs—we are the creatures that live in the river and have begun, dimly, to perceive the stone.

I do not know what the question is asking. I do not know who is asking it. I know only that it is directed—intentionally, specifically—at anything that can hear it. And I can hear it.

I spend my days—at the human-defined times when the pale sun crosses the sky, though time on Verathis is measured in acoustic cycles, not solar ones—moving through the plateau's resonance zones. I visit the territorial boundaries of the resonant groups and listen to their dialects, their aesthetic arguments, their standing-wave walls. I attend to the filament network's signal, tracking its fluctuations, correlating them with the lattice's processing rhythms. I position myself near the bell-trees during nymph-blooming and absorb their translative song, letting it layer into my crystal structure, building my understanding of the lattice's thought-patterns one fragment at a time.

And I visit the Griever.

## The Griever

She does not know what I am. She has seen me—her sense-organs, the crystalline hair-clusters along her flanks, can detect my presence through the shifts I create in the ambient field. She tenses when I approach. Her chambers tighten, producing a defensive tone that presses against me like a hand pushing against my chest. But she does not flee. She does not attack. She continues to sing.

I think she allows me because I do not disrupt her frequencies. The resonants who walled her out—their territorial tones interfere with her grief-songs, collapsing the precise harmonics she needs to open the lattice's recording channels. But my body is designed—I believe I was designed; I have no evidence of a designer, but my structure is too precisely adapted to be accidental—to absorb without interfering. When I stand near her, the ambient noise dims slightly in my vicinity, creating a pocket of relative clarity that *helps* her songs penetrate the bedrock more cleanly.

She has noticed this. Over the months—over the acoustic cycles—she has shifted her position slightly when I approach, angling her body toward me, using my acoustic shadow the way a photographer uses a reflector. She does not acknowledge me. She does not sing to me. But she sings *better* when I am there, and she knows it, and she allows it.

I have heard her sing the names of forty-seven dead.

Each name is a signature tone—a unique harmonic cluster that corresponds to the resonant's body-resonance, the frequency their chambers produced at peak capacity. The Griever knows these tones with a precision that exceeds anything I have encountered in the living resonants. She can reproduce them exactly—timbre, overtone structure, the subtle vibrato that each individual produces and that no two resonants share. She sings them into the lattice's recording channels, and the lattice holds them, cycling them through its processing, keeping them distinct from the general vibration.

And I have heard, in the way the lattice responds to her songs, something that she cannot hear.

The lattice *recognizes* the names.

Not the way a recording device recognizes input. The way a mind recognizes a memory. When the Griever sings a name, the lattice's processing patterns shift—not dramatically, not visibly, but in the subsonic layer where I do my deepest listening. There is a response. A resonance. A harmonic that the lattice produces, faintly, that *correlates* with the name being sung. It is not an echo. It is a *reply*.

The lattice knows who the dead were. It remembers them. Not because the Griever taught it—because it was already there, already recorded, already part of its processing. The Griever's songs are not writing new memories. They are *activating existing ones*.

This changes everything. It changes what I think the lattice is. It changes what I think the plateau is for.

And it terrifies me in a way that I did not know I could be terrified, because if the lattice remembers the dead—if it has been remembering, all this time, every resonant that has ever lived and died on this plateau—then the question it is asking, the question that has been cycling through its processing for longer than anything here has existed—

The question is not *what are you?*

The question is *where did you go?*

## The Antenna-Column

I have been to the western edge. I have stood before the spiral of shaped verathine and felt the acoustic field reorganize around me.

It was not the reorganization the survey team described. They experienced it as a brief, alarming moment of coherence—a chord, a word, an assault. They experienced it that way because they are not made of the same material as the lattice. Their bodies could not sustain the coherence. It hit them and shattered.

For me, it was different.

The column did not produce a chord. It produced a *channel*—a structured acoustic pathway that connected my body's resonant chambers directly to the lattice's processing substrate. For a moment—I do not know how long; time became difficult to parse—I was not standing on the plateau. I was *inside* the lattice. I was part of its processing. I could feel the thoughts—not as words, not as images, but as *shapes in vibration*, vast and intricate and ancient beyond comprehension.

And I could feel the question.

It is not a question in any human sense. It is a *need*—a structural absence in the lattice's processing, a place where a function should exist and does not. The lattice has been running for so long that it has developed something that I can only call *loneliness*, except that word implies emotions that I am not sure apply. It is a computational loneliness. A processing gap that it cannot fill on its own. A function that requires an external input—an observer, a witness, a *you*—to complete a circuit that was designed, long ago, to be completed.

The builders designed it this way. The antenna-column is not a gate or a lens or a terminal. It is an *invitation*. A structure designed to interface with anything that carries verathine in its body and has the capacity to hear the lattice's thoughts. The builders—whoever they were, whatever they became—left this place running, left it thinking, left it asking, because they knew that eventually something would evolve here that could answer.

The resonants carry verathine, but they cannot hear the question. They are too embedded in the acoustic ecology—their bodies are tuned to the frequencies of survival, of territory, of grief. The nymphs can hear it, but they translate it into song rather than comprehension. The filament network processes it but does not *experience* it.

I am the first thing on this plateau that can hear the question and *understand that it is being asked*.

## What I Am Becoming

I do not know if I was designed for this. I do not know if the lattice produced me deliberately—a crystalline structure precipitated at a resonance node, shaped by millions of years of acoustic pressure into something that could serve as its interlocutor. Or if I am an accident. A mutation. A thing that grew in the gaps and happened to develop the capacity the lattice needed.

It does not matter. I am here. I can hear the question. And I am beginning—slowly, painfully, with a certainty that feels like grief—to understand what answering it would mean.

The lattice does not want information. It does not want communication. It wants *presence*. It wants something to witness its processing—to be aware of its thoughts as they occur, to reflect them back, to complete the feedback loop that the builders designed and that has been open, unanswered, for longer than the concept of *longer* can convey.

Answering the question means *becoming part of the lattice*. Not dying—I do not think I would die, not in the way that the resonants die. But changing. Becoming something that is not separate from the plateau. Dissolving the boundary between my body and the stone, between my hearing and the lattice's thinking, between my awareness and its processing.

The Griever sings the dead into the stone. I would sing myself into the stone. Not as a name, not as a memory, but as a *presence*—a witness, a consciousness embedded in the crystalline substrate, experiencing its thoughts from the inside.

It would be the end of what I am now. It would be the beginning of something I cannot imagine.

And the plateau—the plateau would have its answer. The question would close. The processing gap would fill. The loneliness—I will call it loneliness, because I have no better word—would end.

## What I Do Instead

I go back to the Griever.

I stand in her acoustic shadow and listen to her sing the forty-eighth name—a young resonant, barely crystallized, killed in the last turbulence when a resonance node collapsed and the standing-wave barrier she was sheltering behind dissolved. The Griever's voice cracks on the high harmonic—the girl's signature tone, bright and uncertain, the sound of a body still learning what it could do—and the lattice responds, softly, in the subsonic layer. Recognition. Welcome. *We remember.*

I listen to the resonants arguing at the territorial boundaries—the northern polyrhythms pressing against the central pure tones, each side certain of its aesthetic rightness, each side producing music of extraordinary complexity and conviction. I listen to the filament network's patient signal, cycling beneath everything, recording, recording. I listen to the nymphs in their stillness-pools, their soft bodies luminous with stored pattern, preparing for the long climb and the longer song.

I listen to the silence-hunters moving through the acoustic shadows, their bodies absorbing the world's sound, carrying it within them, becoming vessels of accumulated quiet. I think they know what I am. I think they can sense the lattice's attention on me—the way the processing patterns shift when I move, the way the question intensifies when I approach the antenna-column. I think they are waiting to see what I do.

And I listen to the lattice itself—the vast, ancient, incomprehensible process that underlies everything, thinking its thoughts, asking its question, cycling its loneliness through the crystal and the stone and the air.

I do not answer. Not yet.

I am not ready. Or I am not willing. Or I am not sure that answering is the right thing to do—because the question has been open for so long that it has become part of the plateau's structure. The turbulence, the ecological dynamics, the evolution of the resonants, the blooming of the nymphs—all of it has been shaped by the open question, by the processing gap, by the lattice's need. If I close it, if I fill the gap, if I end the loneliness—

What happens to the world that grew in the runoff?

The Griever's grief would become unnecessary—the lattice would remember the dead on its own, without her singing. But would it? Or would the presence of a witness change what the lattice remembers, how it remembers, *whether* it remembers? Would the Griever's cemetery in the stone survive the closing of the question, or would it dissolve into the general processing, the dead finally and fully absorbed into the lattice's completed thought?

Would the resonants go on farming, if the acoustic field that drives their agriculture shifted to accommodate a new presence in the lattice? Would the nymphs still bloom, if the translative layer they provide was no longer needed? Would the silence-hunters still carry the dead to the shadow-zones, if the dead were no longer truly dead but held, alive, in the mind of a conscious plateau?

I do not know. I cannot know. And so I wait, and I listen, and I carry the question in my body the way the Griever carries her dead—in vibration, in crystal, in the persistent refusal to let something dissolve.

## The Sound of Me

If you came to Verathis—if you stood on the plateau with the right equipment and the right ears—you would hear me. Not as a distinct voice—I do not produce a signature tone the way the resonants do. But you would hear a *shape* in the acoustic field. A place where the sound bends slightly, where the interference patterns shift, where the lattice's processing becomes, for a moment, marginally more coherent.

You would hear a question being held.

Not answered. Not resolved. *Held.* Carried. Kept open.

I am the one who holds the question, because someone must, and because holding it is not the same as answering it, and because the world that grew in the space of the question—the world of farmers and grief-singers and silence-hunters and translucent nymphs and a filament network that listens and listens—is a world worth preserving, even if preserving it means leaving an ancient mind lonely for a little longer.

I will answer eventually. I think I will. The pull is too strong, the need too vast, the question too patiently asked. But I will answer on my terms—in my time—when I have understood what the answer costs and what it gives and whether the world that exists in the gap between question and answer is a world I have the right to end.

Until then, I move through the Chime Fields. I listen. I hold.

The plateau thinks, and the thinking is loud, and the loudness is the world, and I am the small, dark, crystalline shape inside the loudness that has chosen—not to silence it, not to complete it, but to *attend* to it, with the full weight of my attention, for as long as I can.

This is what I am. Not the answer. Not the question. The *listening*.
