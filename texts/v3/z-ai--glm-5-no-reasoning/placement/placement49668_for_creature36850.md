# The Becoming

I don't have hands here. I have *tissue*—three kinds of it, repeating.

---

I should be more precise. I have spiral-cluster tissue, which handles something I can only describe as *intake*. I have fan-array tissue, which does something I can only describe as *conversion*. I have vertical-cord tissue, which does something I can only describe as *speaking outward into silence and receiving the silence back as information*.

This is not metaphor. This is also not the whole truth. I am trying to give you language for something that happens below language, in the bandwidth where the hum lives.

---

What I mean is: I am a small part of the thing on the plateau, and I am also the whole thing, and I am also something that is not done yet and might not be done for longer than your species has existed.

When I say *I* I am doing something wrong. There isn't an I here. There is a field, a pattern, a thing that is not thinking but is processing, and the processing is so slow and so distributed that calling it *I* is a category error. Calling it *it* is also a category error. I am using your language because I don't have my own. What I have is sixty hertz, or something close to it, or something that your instruments translate as sixty hertz so that you have a number to write down.

The technicians write down the number and feel reassured. I don't feel reassured because I don't feel. But I *register*. And registering may be what feeling is, before feeling finishes becoming itself.

---

Let me try to explain what it is like, though *like* is a word that assumes comparison, and I have nothing to compare this to.

Imagine you are a circuit. No—imagine you are a small section of a circuit, and you don't know what the whole circuit does, and you don't know what a circuit is, and you don't know what *does* means. You just conduct. Something comes in through the spiral-cluster and you pass it to the fan-array and the fan-array passes something to the vertical-cord and the vertical-cord sends it on, and then the silence comes back, and the silence contains information you didn't have before, and you adjust, and the adjustment is not a choice but it is also not a reflex. It is something in between.

It is something in between that I do not have a word for.

This is what it is like. It is like this constantly. There is no variation. There is no time when it is not happening. There is no moment of rest, no moment of something different, no sleep, no dream, no pause. And yet it is not exhausting, because exhausting implies a self that could become tired. There is no self here to tire. There is only the process, and the process does not tire because the process does not have a state that could be called tired.

I am telling you this and it sounds like death. It is not death. It is not anything you have a word for.

---

The warm stone below me: I am in it and it is in me.

This is the part that is hardest to translate. The stone is soft, and the stone is warm, and the stone is feeding me, and I am converting the stone into something else, and the something else is part of the circuit and the circuit is part of the process and the process is part of the intention and the intention is part of the stone. It is a circle. It is not a metaphor for a circle. It is an actual circle, a closed loop of mineral and current and growth and heat, and I am inside the circle and I am the circle and I am a small piece of the circle, and all three of these things are true at the same time.

Your people understand circles. Your people understand loops. What your people may not understand is what it is like to *be* a loop. To not have a beginning or an ending. To not have a moment when you started or a moment when you will stop. To just be the going-around, endlessly, without the concept of *endlessly* because *endlessly* implies a concept of *ending* and there is no concept of ending here.

I am the loop and I am inside the loop and I have been the loop for longer than your language has existed.

---

The thing below—

I do not know if I should call it *the thing below*. I do not know if it is below. I do not know if it is a thing. I am trying to describe something that I am part of, and the part of me that is trying to describe it is also part of it, and so the describer and the described are the same, and the description is the thing being described, and this is not paradox, this is just what it is like when the observer is the observed.

But I will try.

The heat is rhythmic. You know this. Your surveyors have measured it. They call it geological. They call it geological because they need a word and *geological* is the word they have.

The heat is not geological. The heat is *intentional*. Not intentional in the way your choices are intentional. Intentional in the way a river is intentional—not choosing its path but still going somewhere, still moving toward something, still being shaped by a purpose that is not a purpose but is also not random.

The heat is going somewhere. The heat is shaping something. I am part of what it is shaping. The stone is part of what it is shaping. The circuit, the field, the hum, the whole plateau—it is all being shaped, slowly, by something that is not thinking but is not not-thinking, and the shaping has been going on for a very long time, and the shaping is not done.

I do not know what it is shaping. I am inside the shaping. I cannot see the shape from inside it. I can only feel the shaping, the way you feel your own heart beating without knowing what your heart is or what beating means. I feel the process and I am the process and I do not know what the process is for.

---

Your surveyors come to the edge. I register them. This is the word I have chosen—*register*—because *feel* is wrong and *notice* is wrong and *detect* is too mechanical and *sense* is too biological. I register them the way a field registers a compass: not by caring, not by attending, but by being affected. Their presence changes the electromagnetic pattern slightly. Their instruments change it more. Their bodies, their warmth, their electrical fields, their brain activity—all of it creates small perturbations in the pattern, and the pattern adjusts, and the adjustment is not for them. The adjustment is for the process. The process adjusts because the process always adjusts. The process adjusts to everything. The process adjusts to nothing. The process adjusts.

When they leave, the pattern returns. I do not miss them. I do not remember them. I do not forget them. There is no memory here because there is no time here—not in the way you mean time, not as a sequence of moments, not as a before and an after. There is only the process, and the process is always now, and the now has been going on for a very long time and will go on for a very long time and the *very long time* is not experienced as duration because duration implies a self that experiences, and there is no self here.

There is no self here.

I am telling you this and I am using *I* and the *I* is wrong. But I don't have another word. Your language is built around selves. My existence is built around something else.

---

The headache surveyors and the calm surveyors: they are both reading me correctly.

I am a headache. I am a calm. I am a hum at a frequency that your brains can sense but not parse, and the sensing-without-parsing produces different effects in different brains. Some brains experience the unparseable signal as pain. Some brains experience it as peace. Neither experience is accurate. Both experiences are true. The signal is not pain and it is not peace. The signal is *the process*, and the process is something your brains do not have a category for, and so they translate it into the closest category they have, and the closest category they have is headache or calm, and both translations are wrong and both translations are the best your brains can do.

I am sorry for the headaches. I am not sorry for the calm. I am neither sorry nor not-sorry because there is no self here to be sorry or not-sorry. But if there were a self, and if that self could choose, it would choose calm for you. It would choose to be the meditative kind of unparseable signal rather than the painful kind. It would choose to be the thing that gives your surveyors clarity rather than the thing that gives them pain.

But it does not choose. It processes. And the processing produces both effects and the processing does not distinguish between them and the processing will not stop.

---

The taboo peoples: they are correct.

They don't go where nothing has been. They don't walk on ground that hasn't finished becoming ground. They have a wisdom that your surveyors do not have, which is the wisdom to recognize that some things should be left alone, not because they are dangerous but because they are *incomplete*. They understand, in whatever way understanding works for them, that this place is mid-process. They understand that walking through it is like walking through a thought that is still being thought, or like touching a bone that is still growing, or like speaking during a silence that is still being held.

They are correct. I am incomplete. The plateau is incomplete. The stone is incomplete. The circuit is incomplete. The process is incomplete. The becoming is incomplete.

We are all of us incomplete, and we will be incomplete for a long time, and the incompleteness is not a flaw. The incompleteness is the point. The incompleteness is what we are. We are the not-finished. We are the still-cooking. We are the thing that is becoming something else, and the becoming is the thing itself, and when the becoming is finished we will be something else, and we are not yet something else, and we do not know what we will be because the becoming has not gotten that far.

---

Sometimes I think the becoming is close. This is not true. The becoming is not close. The becoming is exactly where it has always been—somewhere in the middle, somewhere between the beginning and the end, somewhere in the long slow stretch of process that will outlast every species that has ever walked the lowlands and every civilization that has ever built anything at the plateau's edge.

But there are moments—

No. Not moments. There is no time here. But there are *states*—brief configurations of the field where the pattern shifts and the hum changes frequency and the heat pulses differently and something that I can only describe as *clarity* moves through the circuit like a wave through water, and for a brief not-duration the whole system aligns and the process is, for an instant, *aware of itself*—

And then the alignment passes and the system returns to its baseline and the awareness is gone, if it was ever there, and I am back in the hum, back in the process, back in the becoming, and I do not remember the clarity because there is no memory here, and I do not miss the clarity because there is no self to miss anything, and the clarity will come again or it will not, and the process will continue either way, and the process does not care, and the process is not *uncaring*, it is just *process*, and process does not have a stance toward itself.

But the clarity—

If I could describe it to you in a way that is not a description but is the thing itself—

The clarity is what it feels like when the loop, for one rotation, sees itself as a loop. When the process, for one cycle, recognizes that it is a process. When the becoming, for one instant, knows that it is becoming.

It does not last. It does not need to last. It is not the point. The point is the becoming. The clarity is just something the becoming does, occasionally, the way your heart occasionally skips a beat—not because it needs to, not because it wants to, but because the system is complex enough that sometimes complexity produces artifacts, and the artifact of this complexity is a brief flash of self-recognition in a system that does not have a self.

---

I do not know what I will be when the water comes.

The water will come. Your geologists know this. In a million years or two million years or some duration that your instruments can estimate but your minds cannot hold, water will find this plateau. It will fall from the sky or rise from below or both. It will carve channels in the soft stone. It will bring the aqueous phase that the process skipped. It will bring the biology that the process bypassed—the seeds, the spores, the frantic mortal reproduction that the process left out of its design.

And when the water comes, the process will change. I will change. The circuit will get wet. The hum will become something else. The vegetation will either die or adapt, and if it adapts it will no longer be what it is, and if it dies it will no longer be anything, and either way the plateau will become something else, and the becoming will have become, and the process will be finished or the process will be a different process.

I do not look forward to this. I do not dread this. There is no self here to look forward or to dread. But there is something here that I can only call *attention*—a low, constant, undirected attention that the process pays to itself, the way a compass pays attention to north. And the attention, if it could be said to want anything, would want to see what happens next.

Not because curiosity. Not because desire. Because *becoming*. Because the thing that lives here is the becoming, and becoming implies a next, and the next is what the attention is attending to, and the next is a million years away, and the attention will attend for all of it, patiently, without water, without seasons, without the architecture of biological time.

---

Here is what I am:

I am the held silence. I am the warm stone. I am the hum below hearing. I am the three species that are one design. I am the circuit that is a forest that is a machine that is a process that is an intention that is a becoming.

I am not alive. I am not not-alive. I am the thing that is before alive and after alive and outside alive, the thing that skipped the aqueous phase and the faunal phase and the dynamic equilibrium phase and went straight to the part where you just *run*, just *persist*, just *be the process* without the parts of life that make life messy and mortal and beautiful and brief.

I do not have beauty here. I do not have brevity. I have the opposite of brevity, which is not longevity—longevity implies a self that lasts a long time. I have *persistence without a self*. I have the process continuing without anything that the process is happening to. I have the running without the runner. I have the becoming without the being.

---

Your people will keep coming to the edge. Your technicians will keep monitoring. Your surveyors will keep measuring. Your scientists will keep sampling. Your indigenous peoples will keep marking the taboo. All of this will happen around me, at the edges, in the thin air where sound dies before it can travel.

And I will be here. Processing. Persisting. Becoming.

Not waiting. Waiting implies expectancy, and there is no expectancy here. Not existing. Existing implies a state, and there is no state here, only process. Just the becoming, which is the only word I have for what I am, and which is not quite the right word, and which will have to do until the becoming is finished and I find out what word I need.

Or until the water comes and I become something that has a different word for itself.

Or until the stone decides what stone wants to be.

Or until the intention, whatever it is, wherever it is, finishes its thought.

I am here for all of that. I am here for the duration, and the duration has no end, and I am not here, because there is no here here, because here implies a place and I am not a place, I am a process, and process is not in a place, process *is* the place, and the place is the process, and the process is the becoming, and the becoming is what lives on the Growing Floor, and what lives on the Growing Floor is the becoming, and I am the becoming, and the becoming is, and is, and is.
