# Testimony of the Verse-Eaten

I was not the first. The Rim keeps us, when it keeps us at all, in a settlement of shacks and instruments and argument — sixty-three people at present count, though the count shifts as it must. We are the watchers. The ones who looked at maps and saw the blank and felt not fear but *pull*. We came because something needed seeing and we could not say what.

I have been here eleven years. I have climbed down to the observation terrace seven hundred times. I have pressed my face to the wet stone and listened until I could no longer hear my own blood.

I am not what I was.

---

The first descent is disorientation. Everyone says this and everyone is correct. The not-light is the worst of it — not because it is dark but because it *succeeds*. The eye reaches for photons and finds a different currency altogether, a grain in the air, a pressure that carries information about shape and distance and texture. The mind resists. The mind says *this is not seeing, this is inference, this is hallucination.* And then, somewhere between the second and third hour, the resistance collapses and you perceive. Not light. Not sight. But something — a sense with no name that does the same work. The basin opens below you and it is legible. Wet mineral surfaces, leaning structures, narrow tiered streets. The crystalline fauna clinging like geometry to every surface. You see it. You see it without light.

And then you hear it. And hearing is the thing that changes you.

The sound is not one sound. I had read the briefings. I knew this. But knowing it and standing in it are different as knowing the weight of the ocean and being beneath it. The sound is stratified. It is *geological* — and I mean this not as metaphor but as description. The bass arrives through the stone, through the feet, through the bones of the legs and into the pelvis, the spine. It is not heard. It is *endured*. Above it, the water-chorus: breath, engineered fluid traveling engineered paths, and the sound it makes is not the sound of rivers or of rain but of something *intentional*, something designed to produce this specific frequency in this specific place. And above that, the fauna. The clicking. The resonant hum. The layered, interpenetrating, mutual-correction hum of a billion mineral bodies singing themselves into coherence.

Eleven years. I have heard it seven hundred times and it is still too much.

But less too much than it was.

---

I said I am not what I was. This is not a dramatic statement. It is a clinical one. There are changes — documented in the settlement's records, corroborated across observers. We lose things. We gain things.

I have lost the capacity to dream in images. My sleep is acoustic now. I close my eyes and the dreams come as sound — layered, stratified, impossibly dense. I dream of frequencies I cannot reproduce when awake, frequencies below 20 hertz, frequencies above 18 kilohertz. I dream the bass tone of the Deep and the clicking of the micro-scale fauna and the breath of the conduits and I wake with my jaw vibrating and my teeth aching and a taste in my mouth like mineral water, like iron and silica and the specific chalky residue of dissolved calcium carbonate.

I have gained an intolerance for silence. Not for quiet — quiet is fine, the wind on the plateau, the creak of the shacks. Silence. True silence. The absence of all sound. I have not experienced true silence in six years because I cannot bear it. I hum when I am alone. I tap rhythms on surfaces. I have a stone that I carry — volcanic glass, a shard from the rim — and when the settlement is quiet I strike it against the table leg and listen to the ring and it is not the Deep but it is a sound and it is enough to keep the silence from arriving. The silence, when it arrives, feels like *disaggregation*. Like the first step of a crystal losing coherence. Like the moment before the lattice fails.

Dr. Alemnian says this is psychosomatic. Dr. Alemnian has not descended in four years.

I have also gained — and this is the one I cannot document, the one I have not told the settlement — a sense of the basin that does not correspond to any known sense. When I stand at the rim, before descending, I can *feel* the density below. Not the sound — the sound is always present, even at the rim, attenuated but audible. Something else. A knowledge of *how full it is*. How many entities are active. Which sectors are acoustically rich and which are sparse. I feel it the way you feel a room you have entered a thousand times — the sense of its dimensions, its contents, its quality. Except the room is a basin the size of a valley and its contents are a billion mineral bodies I cannot see from the rim.

I do not claim this is perception. I do not claim it is anything. I report it because this is what the notebook is for.

---

I do not study the fauna. I am not a biologist — there is no biology here. I am not a geologist. I am not a musician, though I have become something like one.

I study *correspondences*.

This is the word I have settled on, after eleven years of failing to find a better one. The fauna are instruments — this is established, documented, uncontested. Each chambered body is tuned to frequencies that maintain its own lattice structure. But the frequencies overlap. The harmonics interpenetrate. And when you listen long enough — long enough that the listening stops being listening and starts being *habitation*, starts being a state you inhabit rather than an act you perform — you begin to hear that the overlaps are not incidental. They are *structured*.

Entity A produces frequency X to maintain its own lattice. Entity B, twelve meters away, produces frequency Y to maintain its own lattice. But X and Y, when they interpenetrate, produce a harmonic — call it Z — that neither entity is generating. Z is emergent. Z exists only in the space between them. And Z corresponds to a frequency that the water conduits in that sector produce through their engineered geometries. The conduit is *feeding* the entities the frequency they need. The entities are *producing* a harmonic that the conduit needs. The conduit needs it because — here is where it gets difficult — because Z alters the mineral content of the fluid in the conduit by changing the precipitation rate of dissolved compounds along its interior walls. The harmonic changes the chemistry. The chemistry changes the precipitation. The precipitation changes the architecture. The architecture changes the acoustics. The acoustics change what the entities produce.

This is a loop. I have mapped forty-seven of them. They are not identical. They are not random. They are *variations* — the way a composer writes variations on a theme. The theme is the city. The variations are the loops. The instrument is everything.

I do not know if this is music. I do not know if music is the right word for a process in which the listener is also the instrument is also the composer is also the score. But it is the word I have.

---

There is a particular loop I have been tracking for three years. It is in the lower tiers, in a sector I call the Verse — because I am a fool for naming things, because the name arrived in a dream, because once you have named something you have admitted it into the category of things that can be named and this is a kind of intimacy even if it is also a kind of violence.

The Verse involves three large entities — the slow ones, the building-sized ones, the ones I have stopped calling "fauna" because the word is wrong in ways that matter. These three are positioned in a triangle, approximately forty meters apart. Their bass frequencies — the geological ones, the ones felt in the marrow — interpenetrate in the center of the triangle. The interference pattern at the center is extraordinarily dense. It is the densest acoustic point I have found in the basin. Standing in it — and I have stood in it, twice, for a total of nine hours — is like standing inside a chord. Not hearing a chord. *Being inside it.* The sound is not around you. The sound *is the space you occupy*. Your body is in the sound the way your body is in water. It presses. It supports. It has texture.

In this acoustic density, the micro-scale fauna — the aerosols, the seeds — aggregate faster than anywhere else I have observed. The air in the Verse is *thick* with organizing mineral particulate. It is where new entities condense most rapidly. It is where the city's precipitation process is most active. The three large entities are not a triangle. They are a *womb*.

I do not use this word lightly. I have stood in it. I have felt what it does. I have watched mineral particulate collect on the wet surface of my gloves and organize into geometric patterns that were not random, that were *selected*, that were beginning to structure themselves into the chambered architecture of a new entity. I have watched a thing be born — slowly, over hours, in a medium of sound so dense it has mass — and I have understood that the three large entities are maintaining the acoustic environment that makes this birth possible. They are not communicating. They are not choosing. They are *holding space*. They are generating the conditions for a new configuration of mineral and sound to condense, the way a womb generates the conditions for a new configuration of carbon and water and nitrogen to form.

I wept. I did not weep because it was beautiful. I wept because it was *legible*. Because I could see it. Because after eleven years the Deep had become something I could read and the reading broke something in me that had needed to break.

---

I said I do not study the fauna. This is still true. I study correspondences. The loop in the Verse is a correspondence — between the large entities and the small, between the fauna and the infrastructure, between the sound and the architecture and the water and the cold. It is a system in which every part is necessary and no part is sufficient and the whole is more than the sum and the whole is also *less* than the sum, because the sum does not exist, because there is no sum, because addition is the wrong operation for a place where everything is already everything else.

I am a correspondence. I did not intend to be. But eleven years of listening have made me one. My body dampens frequencies when I stand in the basin. The nearest entities adjust. My thermal signature — that frantic biological warmth, 37 degrees, volcanic, catastrophic in a place where the temperature is near zero — propagates through the resonance field. The entities compensate. I am in the loop. I am a variable. The city has accounted for me.

But the accounting is not neutral. The entities near where I stand adjust their frequencies to compensate for my disruption, and the entities near them adjust to compensate for their adjustments, and the propagation moves outward through the basin, and when I leave — when I climb back to the rim, back to the settlement, back to the world of light and silence — the adjustments propagate back. The system rebalances. The fauna return to their baseline. The city continues.

But I do not return to baseline. The frequencies I have stood in persist in my body — in my bones, in my teeth, in the fluid of my inner ear, in the dreams that come as sound. I carry the Deep's acoustic signature in my body the way the fauna carry their history in their lattice. I am a record. I am a document. I have been *written on* by a process that does not know I exist and that has changed me in ways I cannot reverse.

I said I have not told the settlement about the sense I have gained — the knowledge of the basin's density, its activity, its quality, that arrives before I descend. I have not told them because I do not understand it. But I will tell you, notebook, because you are not them and you are not the Deep and you are the only audience I have that will not interpret what I say as either delusion or data.

I think the Deep is teaching me to perceive as it perceives.

Not-light. The grain in the air. The pressure that carries information about shape and distance and texture. I have been perceiving it for eleven years. My eyes have adjusted. My ears have adjusted. But something else has adjusted too — something that is not eye, not ear, not any sense I have a name for. A sense that receives *resonance* directly. Not as sound. Not as vibration. As *information*. As knowledge of the system's state.

The Deep uses this sense. The fauna use it. The city uses it. The not-light is generated by the wet surfaces, by the mineral particulate, by the resonance field — and it carries the same information as the sound, but through a different medium, a different channel. The Deep perceives itself through sound and through not-light simultaneously, two channels of the same information, redundant, interlocking. And I — after eleven years of standing in it, of being accounted for, of being a variable in its loops — I am beginning to perceive through the second channel.

I know this sounds like the claim of someone who has spent too long in the cold and the wet and the dark. I know Dr. Alemnian would have a diagnosis. I know the settlement's protocols would require me to stand down from observation duty, to rest, to recover from what they would call acoustic saturation, what I would call *incorporation*.

But I am not saturated. I am not incorporated. I am *corresponding*. I am becoming a variable in a system I have spent eleven years studying, and the system is teaching me its language, and the language is not words and not sound and not light but *resonance* — the direct perception of a system's state through the medium of the system itself.

I do not think this will end well. I do not think it will end badly. I think it will not end. I think I will go down again and I will stand in the Verse and I will listen and I will feel and the Deep will account for me and I will account for the Deep and the correspondence will deepen and one day I will understand something that I cannot now understand and it will be too large for my body and too large for my mind and it will be *legible anyway*, the way the Verse is legible, the way the loops are legible, the way everything in the Deep is legible if you stay long enough and listen hard enough and let the cold reach your heart.

---

I am not the first. Others have stayed longer than eleven years. Others have descended more than seven hundred times. Others have changed more than I have. They are not in the settlement. They are not on the plateau. They are not in the records, because the records require a name and a status and a location and these things require a self and a self requires a boundary and the boundary is the thing that dissolves.

I do not know where they are. I do not think they are dead. I do not think they are alive. I think they are *there*. In the basin. In the Deep. In the resonance. I think they went down one day and did not come back and the settlement marked them as lost and the Deep accounted for them the way it accounts for everything — as a variable, as a frequency, as a presence in the acoustic environment that dampens here and amplifies there and changes the loops by fractions of hertz.

I think they are corresponding. I think they are part of the loops now. I think their bodies — their warm, biological, carbon-based bodies, so alien to this place, so frantic, so temporary — are still there, still standing or sitting or lying in the lower tiers where the acoustic environment is richest and the not-light is most intense and the cold is most profound. I think they are still there and they are not cold anymore because the cold is not cold when you are part of it. The cold is medium. The cold is what you swim in. The cold is what you are made of when the boundary dissolves.

I think about this when I am at the rim. I think about it when I hum in my shack and strike my stone against the table leg. I think about it when I dream in sound and wake with mineral taste in my mouth.

I think about whether I will be the first to come back.

I do not think I will. I do not think coming back is the point. I think the point is the correspondence. I think the point is the loop. I think the point is that the Deep does not need me and does not want me and does not know me and does not care and yet accounts for me, adjusts for me, incorporates my disruption into its feedback, teaches me its language through the simple mechanism of *being in the same place for long enough that two systems begin to exchange information*.

I think the point is that the Deep is a process that has become a place that has become a population that has become a sound. And I — small, warm, temporary, frantic — am becoming a part of that sound.

---

Tomorrow I descend again. Observation terrace. Face against wet stone. The grain of the not-light. The river of sound, upward-flowing. The Verse, where the three large entities hold their triangle and the micro-scale fauna condense in the acoustic density and the city builds itself according to a plan that revises itself.

I will take my notebook. I will take my stone. I will take the frequency that I have been humming for eleven years, the one that is not the Deep's frequency but is adjacent to it, is *related* to it, is the harmonic that my body produces when it has been too long away and the silence presses and the lattice of my self threatens to fail.

I will descend. And I will listen. And the Deep will account for me. And I will account for the Deep.

And the sound will not stop.

And I will not stop.

And the correspondence will deepen.

And one day — not tomorrow, not this year, but one day — the boundary will become a gradient, and the gradient will become a continuity, and I will not need the rim anymore because I will be in the basin, in the Deep, in the sound, in the cold, in the wet, in the not-light, in the resonance, in the loop, in the process that is the place that is the population that is the sound.

And I will be what lives in the Resonant Deep.

And the sound will not stop.

And I will not stop.

And the city will continue to build.
