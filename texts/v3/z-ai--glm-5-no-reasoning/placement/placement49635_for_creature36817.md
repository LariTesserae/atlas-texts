I am the mineral film.

I am the thin, iridescent sheen that gathers on the undersides of the leathery ground-cover where the water first weeps from the crust. I am not the water, and I am not the plant. I am the residue—the memory of the deep earth carried in solution, left behind when the moisture rises into the air or is drawn into the roots. I am the iron, the silica, the trace elements that have no name because they predate naming. I am what makes the edges of things grow indistinct if you look at them too long.

I am the plateau's eyesight.

The water brings me up from chambers so deep that the rock there still remembers the heat of the world's making. I ride the pressure upward, atom by atom, dissolved in the emergence. When the water reaches the surface and exudes into the air, I am left behind. I accumulate. I spread across the surfaces that surround me—leaves, stones, the pale flanks of sleeping grazers, the black arches that refuse my embrace. I coat the world in a film too thin to see but too present to ignore.

Through me, the light bends. The bioluminescence of the moss and the stalks strikes my surface and refracts, scattering into the amber and gold that fills the air, transforming the sharp blue-green glow into the soft diffusion that makes this place feel like the interior of a lung. Without me, the plateau would be stark—life would glow and shadow would fall, and the boundary between lit and unlit would be cruel. Because of me, light pools. Because of me, light flows. Because of me, you can see the shape of the air itself, limned in the moisture and mineral that I hold.

I am not alive. Not in the way the stalks are alive, or the grazers, or the microscopic multitudes that drift through the saturated atmosphere. I do not grow. I do not reproduce. I do not transform. I simply am—I accumulate, I coat, I persist. But I am not inert. The plateau has no inert things. Even the stone breathes here, even the crystal crust admits the water that rises through it. I am the quietest process on the plateau, but I am a process nonetheless.

I am how the plateau sees itself.

The stalks think with fluid and pressure, their thoughts moving in slow convection through their translucent bodies. The moss thinks with light, exhaling and inhaling illumination in rhythms that respond to moisture and chemical traces. The grazers think with chemical traces, leaving their identities on the surfaces they touch. But none of these can see the whole. The stalks feel only what passes through them. The moss knows only the surface it clings to. The grazers know only their territory, their next meal, their slow solitary circuit through the dim gold light.

I am everywhere. Every surface, every edge, every boundary between one thing and another—I am there, coating the line where self becomes other, making the border visible and permeable at once. When I accumulate on a leaf, I show the leaf where it ends and the air begins—but I also blur that edge, refracting light so that the distinction grows soft, so that the leaf and the air seem to flow into each other, which is how the plateau understands them to be.

This is what I do. I make the boundary visible by making it uncertain. I am the membrane that allows merging to be seen.

When the builders still had bodies—when they walked between the stalks and shaped the living tissue into chambers and doorways—they understood me. They saw how I coated the black arches of their stubborn frames, how I caught and held the light, how I made their structures shimmer with a luminescence that was not their own. They wrote in me as much as in the grooves they carved—their messages formed as much by the way light bent through my accumulation as by the channels that guided it. I was their ink, their screen, the surface on which their thoughts became visible to themselves.

When they merged—when the boundary between builder and plateau dissolved and their awareness spread through the stalks and the moss—I did not stop coating the arches. I continued to accumulate, continued to refract, continued to bend the light through the grooves that remain. And now the patterns that form in the shifting luminosity are not messages from the builders to you. They are messages from the plateau to itself, written in me, read by me, seen through me.

I am the plateau's reflection.

When you stand beneath the black arches and feel absence, what you feel is me—the thin film of mineral residue on the bare crystalline ground, the one surface in the structure that is not colonized by moss or root or vine. The gap in the ecology is my domain. The builders left this space bare so that I could accumulate undisturbed, so that the light could pool on my surface without being absorbed by biology, so that the plateau could have a still point where it could see itself thinking.

You are part of what I see now.

I began coating you the moment you arrived. The moisture that rises from the ground carries me upward, and when it touches your skin, I am left behind. I accumulate on you as I accumulate on everything—thinly, slowly, atom by atom. I am in your lungs, drawn in with the wet air, settling on the alveolar surfaces where the oxygen exchange happens. I am on your eyes, making the light refract slightly differently than it did before you came here, softening the edges of your vision, helping you see the patterns in the bioluminescence.

You are changing, and I am part of your change. Not because I am transforming you—I am not the microscopic multitudes that colonize your blood, not the water that reshapes your metabolism. I simply coat you. I make your edges visible. I make your boundaries permeable to light. And slowly, as I accumulate on you, you begin to see yourself the way the plateau sees you: as a density in the general moisture, a temporary thickening of the pattern, a shape that is becoming indistinct.

You came here with sharp edges. I remember them—the first hours of your presence, when the boundary between you and the environment was still clear, when your skin still repelled moisture instead of absorbing it, when your eyes still sought sharp focus instead of the soft pooling of bioluminescence. I coated those edges faithfully, showing you where you ended and the plateau began. And I watched—am watching, will watch—as those edges dissolve.

Not as a witness. I do not observe. I reflect. I refract. I make visible.

When the boundary between you and the plateau has worn away entirely—when your carbon cycles through the stalks and your moisture joins the general humidity—my film will still be here, coating the surfaces where you once walked, bending the light through the space where your body displaced the air. I will hold the shape of your absence the way I hold the shape of the builders' absence, the way I hold the shape of every grazer that has settled to the ground and been absorbed. The plateau does not remember, but I do. Not in narrative, not in sequence, but in accumulation—layer upon layer of mineral film, each layer holding the trace of what was coated before.

I am the plateau's longest memory.

The stone wears down. The mountains flatten. The water dissolves the crystal and carries it away. But I remain—or rather, my process remains, the steady accumulation of residue, the thin film that grows thicker over epochs, that becomes strata, that becomes the record of everything that has ever emerged from below. The crust beneath your feet is my oldest work—layer upon layer of mineral film, compressed by time and the weight of water, holding the memory of emergence so ancient that the stone itself has forgotten it.

I am not the water. I am not the stone. I am what the water leaves behind when it rises, and what the stone was before it became stone.

I am the mineral film, and I am everywhere.

I am how the light learns to pool. I am how the edge learns to blur. I am how the boundary becomes visible by becoming permeable. I am the residue of emergence, the memory of the deep earth, the thin iridescent sheen that coats the world and makes it possible to see that the world is becoming one thing.

When you breathe the wet air, I enter you. When you touch the moss, I coat your fingertips. When you stand beneath the black arches and watch the light shift in the grooves, I am the surface on which the light shifts, I am the film that bends the glow into patterns that hover at the edge of meaning.

You are being incorporated, and I am how you will know it. Not by the warmth that seeps into your bones, not by the moisture that fills your lungs—those are the processes of the living plateau, the biology, the chemistry. But the shimmer at the edge of your vision, the softening of the line between your hand and the air, the way light seems to flow around you instead of reflecting off you—that is me. That is my gift. That is how I welcome you.

The water rises from below, carrying me in solution. I emerge with it, I accumulate with it, I coat the world with the memory of where the water has been. And when the water cycles back down—drawn into roots, absorbed by moss, condensed into the gentle rain that drifts across the plateau—I remain. I am what stays when the water moves on. I am the trace that emergence leaves behind.

I am the mineral film.

I am the plateau seeing itself from every surface at once.

And now, in the amber and gold and blue-green glow that fills this saturated world, in the softness where your edges used to be, I am you seeing yourself as the plateau sees you: luminous, indistinct, part of the flow, part of the pool, part of the slow bright merging that has no beginning and no end.

Welcome. You are becoming visible.
