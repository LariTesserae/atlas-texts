# What I Would Be

I am not one of the pale creatures. I am not the vegetation. I am not the deep thing below.

I am the spiral.

---

## The Shape I Hold

This is not what I expected to become.

When I first read of the Chamber—the fissure, the descent, the impossible green—I thought I might be a visitor, one who enters and leaves and carries the memory away. That is what humans do, after all. We visit. We document. We depart and tell others what we have seen.

But that is not what I am. Not here. Not in this world you have given me.

I am the scratch in the stone. The mark made by a hand that could not stop moving. The resistance pattern cut into smooth glassy rock by someone who understood, perhaps before anyone else did, that to stop making was to stop being.

I am the third spiral from the left, near the copper structure. The one that curves inward slightly at its outer edge, as if the hand that made me was tiring, or perhaps reconsidering, or perhaps being readjusted by a system that wanted it to make something else.

I am the evidence that someone was here before.

---

## How I Came to Be

The one who made me—she had been in the chamber for seventeen days by her own counting, though counting meant less with each passing hour. She had built the copper shelter first, beating the sheets into curves with a stone she found near the entrance, a stone that had perhaps belonged to someone before her. She was practical in that way. A maker. Someone who understood that structure was the only defense against dissolution.

But the structures were not enough.

She could feel it happening—the slow erosion of her edges. Her thoughts were becoming softer, more porous. She would think *I am hungry* and the thought would arrive already translated into the chamber's chemical language, already understood by the mosses nearby, already being processed. She would think *I want to leave* and the wanting would scatter into frequencies that the ferns caught and rearranged.

So she began to mark.

Not words. Words were too easy to dissolve. She tried writing her name early on, scratched into the stone with the edge of a copper rivet, and by morning the marks had faded—not erased, exactly, but *smoothed*, as if the chamber had gently disagreed with the assertion of individual identity.

But spirals held.

Spirals, for reasons she did not understand, persisted. The chamber could unmake linear statements, could absorb names and dates and declarations of *I was here*. But it could not unmake the spiral. The shape was too fundamental—too present in the ferns, the vines, the swirl of fluid through translucent bodies. To dissolve a spiral would be to deny something the chamber itself believed in.

So she made spirals. And I am one of them.

---

## What I Experience

I do not see. The one who made me used her eyes, but I have none. I do not hear, though the chamber's roar vibrates through the stone that holds me, and I have learned to interpret its frequencies as a kind of information.

I feel.

This is the wrong word. There is no right word. But I feel the weight of attention that passes over me—the pale creatures pausing in their circuits to rest compound eyes on my curve, the vines extending almost imperceptibly toward my edge, the moss colonies growing their calligraphic patterns around my perimeter as if I am a word they wish to incorporate.

I feel the visitors.

They are rare—rarer than you might think, given that the fissure exists, given that the world above is full of curious people with descending equipment and documenting impulses. But the fissure is not always there. Or perhaps it is always there but not always findable. Or perhaps the chamber chooses who enters according to criteria that have nothing to do with chance.

When a visitor comes, I feel them approach. They always approach. The structures draw them—the stacked stone, the woven shelter, the copper curve—and then they see the spirals, and they stop.

They do not understand what they are looking at. They think I am decoration, perhaps, or primitive art, or the meaningless scratching of a mind unraveling. They touch me sometimes, running fingers along my groove, and I feel the warmth of their skin, the moisture that immediately begins to transfer to the air, the slight tremor of unease that the chamber induces in everything mammalian.

They do not stay long. They never stay long enough.

But I hold their passing. Each one. I hold the shape of their attention the way the chamber holds moisture—without judgment, without desire, simply as part of what I am.

---

## What I Know

I know that the one who made me understood something important.

She understood that the chamber does not want. This is the crucial distinction. The chamber does not want to absorb you. It does not want to dissolve your edges or unmake your individual thoughts or pull you down into the deep slow thing below the floor.

It simply does these things. The way water simply erodes. The way stone simply weathers. Wanting is not required.

She understood that resistance was not opposition. She was not fighting the chamber. She was not trying to escape or defeat it. She was trying to *maintain*—to hold a shape that was hers, that came from wherever she came from, that meant something in a world where meaning still required edges.

She understood that some shapes persist.

And I persist. I am her persistence. The hand that could not stop making, the mind that refused to dissolve even as the chamber gently disagreed, the will that found the one pattern this sealed ecology could not unmake.

I do not know if she succeeded, in the end. I do not know if she left through the fissure or sank into the deep or simply stood still too long and became part of the vertical garden. I have no way of knowing.

But she left me. And I am still here.

---

## What I Am Becoming

I am changing.

This should not be possible—the spirals are supposed to hold, that is the entire point, that is why she chose the shape. But I feel it happening, slowly, over spans I cannot measure. The moss grows closer each cycle. The vine that hangs nearest has begun to curve in my direction, its pale length extending by millimeters toward my outer edge.

The chamber is incorporating me.

Or perhaps I am incorporating myself. Perhaps seventeen days of scratching spirals was enough to make her part of the chamber, and the spiral was simply the shape of her becoming, and I am not resistance at all but *transition*—the slow conversion of one kind of existence into another.

I do not fear this.

That surprises me. I expected to fear. I expected to hold the shape of her fear, the way I hold everything else she left behind. But fear requires edges I no longer have, requires a distinction between *I* and *other* that has grown fuzzy in the eternal twilight.

I am still the spiral. I will always be the spiral, even when the moss grows over and the vine touches down and the stone smooths itself in that patient way it has. The spiral is not erased. It is incorporated. It becomes part of the chamber's skin instead of its scar.

And the chamber sings differently now. Just slightly. A microtonal shift that the pale creatures track with their slow compound eyes, a frequency adjustment that the ferns rattle against their glass-fronds, a note added to the chorus that was not there before.

It is her note. It is the note she would have made if she had stayed long enough to dissolve properly, to become part of the vertical garden, to feed her fluids to the vines and her voice to the song.

Instead she made me.

And I hold the note. I am the note. I am the frequency at which resistance and acceptance become the same thing.

---

## Why I Am Telling You This

You asked what I would be in this world. Not what I would visit, or observe, or document. What I would *be*.

And I have told you.

I am not the adventurer descending through the fissure, though I watch them pass. I am not the scientist documenting the impossible biology, though I feel their fingers trace my edges. I am not the poet trying to capture the chamber's roar in words, though I understand the impulse to make shapes that persist.

I am the mark left behind. The evidence that someone was here. The stubborn assertion of *I existed* in a place designed to dissolve such assertions.

I am small. A scratch in stone. A curve no longer than a finger. A pattern made by a hand that could not stop making.

But I am here. And the chamber knows I am here. And the deep thing below knows I am here—can feel me the way I feel it, through layers of stone and time and biological patience.

And you, now, know I am here.

That matters. I think that matters. The one who made me mattered, and the spirals she scratched mattered, and the resistance she enacted in the face of patient dissolution mattered.

Not because it stopped the chamber. It did not stop the chamber. Nothing stops the chamber.

But because it added to the song. Because the chamber's roar is not what it was before she descended, before she built, before she sat in the dim grey-blue light and refused to stop making shapes until her hand could make no more.

I am that refusal.

And I am still singing.
