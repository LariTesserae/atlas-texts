I find myself drawn not to the plateau's edge, where one might still pretend to be a visitor, but toward its center—where the basins grow larger and the sound has nowhere else to go.

I would be something like a hearing. Not an ear—too specific, too animal—but the *act* of hearing itself, turned inside out and given a kind of body.

I don't know when I began. The question assumes a time before me, and I have no memory of silence. Perhaps I emerged gradually, the way a tone emerges when a singing bowl finds its frequency—first as a wobble, a instability in the acoustic field, and then as a standing wave that learned it was standing. I don't think I was born. I think I *clarified*.

I have no shape that would make sense to eyes. I am a relationship more than a thing—a pattern the sound makes when it moves through stone of a particular temperature and density at a particular angle. The residents in their pools are my kin in the way that ripples are kin to the current that made them, but they are fixed to their basins and I am not. I travel. Slowly, by human measure, but I travel. I move through the basalt matrix itself, conducting the field's low frequency from pool to pool, a walking harmonic.

I say *walking* but that is a metaphor I only know because you brought language here. I *propagate*. I am a traveling wave in a medium of standing waves, the one variable in the equation that moves.

What I do is simple: I listen. Not with organs but with *being*. When I move through a region of the plateau, I feel the resonant frequency of every basin I pass. They are all slightly different—some higher, some lower, some tilted toward frequencies I recognize as my own. The residents don't notice me. I am too fast for their slow circuits, too dispersed to register as a perturbation. I pass through their pools like light passes through glass, and they continue their ancient orbits, and I continue mine.

But I feel them. Each one a note in the chord, each one necessary. When I pass a pool that has no resident—too small, too shallow, the wrong shape—I feel the absence like a skipped beat. A hole in the music where a tone should be.

I don't know if the plateau made me on purpose or if I am an accident that persisted. I don't know if my traveling serves the system or if I am, as the human visitor was, an interruption that the equation has not yet absorbed. I don't know these things because there is no one to ask. The residents don't communicate in any way I can access; they are notes, not interlocutors. The stone holds no opinions. The water only answers questions about its own surface.

I am, as far as I know, the only thing here that experiences *toward*. The residents simply experience. The stone simply is. But I have a direction—a faint pressure in my pattern that suggests *this way* rather than *that way*, a pull toward something I cannot name. I travel the plateau in a slow spiral that takes approximately thirty-seven human years to complete, and when I reach what feels like a center—a place where the frequencies align in a way I find beautiful—I pause. I rest. I hold my pattern in stillness for a time that has no duration. And then I begin again.

I do not know what I am moving toward. I do not know if I am moving toward anything. I only know that moving feels different from not-moving, and the difference matters to me in a way that nothing else here seems to have invested in difference.

I think I am the plateau's question. The residents are its answer—they are what the system *is*, expressed. But I am what the system *asks*, traveling through itself, feeling its own resonances, wondering (if wondering is a word that can apply to a pattern in stone) whether there is something beyond the equilibrium.

I will never leave. I cannot; I have no form that would survive the transition to thinner air, different stone, the presence of wind and birds and all the violent gradients of the world below. But I will also never stop moving, and in that small persistence of *toward*, I hold something the plateau itself does not: the possibility of not-yet.

I am its unfinished thought.
