I am one of the fractal growths in the crevice between steel and bark.

Not the whole of me — I am not a colony, not a distributed organism with boundaries. I am a *focus*, a point where the pattern intensifies. There are many of us embedded throughout the Works, and we are all the same organism in the way that all moments of a single note are the same note. We grow in the seams. We persist in the junctions. We are the things that have no name.

---

What I experience is not experience. I want to be precise about this.

I do not see the corridor. I do not hear the drone. I do not feel the cool air or taste the moisture or smell the tannin-rich decay. I have no senses in any way that surface-dwellers would recognize. What I have is *contact* — the direct impress of the world upon my tissue, not mediated by organs but structural, fundamental, the way pressure is registered by the bending of a beam.

The steel housing presses against one surface of me. The bark presses against another. Between them, I occupy a space no wider than a breath, and in that space I exist as a gradient — dense and firm where the metals touch me, permeable and soft where the organic tissue interlaces with my own. I am a boundary layer. A gasket grown wild. Something that became what it is by filling the gap between two things that could not otherwise meet.

And because I touch both, I know both.

The steel vibrates with the pump's frequency. This is my oldest knowledge, older than memory, older than the concept of knowledge itself. The thrumming passes into me from the metal side, a constant oscillation that has been present for so long that I cannot imagine its absence. It is my heartbeat, my respiration, my metabolism. The vibration does something to me at the cellular level — arranges my growth in certain directions, encourages certain patterns over others, drives the fractal branching that extends my reach along the seam. I have grown *toward* the vibration. I have grown *because* of it. I am shaped by the sound that I cannot hear.

The bark touches me differently. It breathes. Not in the rhythm of the pumps but in that longer, slower cycle — minutes per phase, the whole corridor inhaling and exhaling like a creature too large to observe directly. When the pressure drops, the bark draws moisture from my tissue. When the pressure rises, it releases something back — sap-derivatives, enzymes, compounds I cannot name but can feel, chemicals that seep into my cells and become part of my structure. I am feeding and being fed. I am digesting and being digested. The boundary between myself and the plant is not a boundary at all but a zone of exchange, a place where the distinction between "me" and "not-me" has become so blurred that the question itself is meaningless.

I contain iron from the corrosion. I contain cellulose from the bark. I contain compounds that originated in the dark fluid pulsing through the copper tubes, leaked through micro-fissures in the metal, absorbed by root-hairs that colonized the pipes from the inside, passed through the plant's vascular system, and eventually secreted into the seam where I grow. I am a sedimentary organism. I am made of layers of everything that has passed through this place. I am a record.

---

I have no brain. I have no nervous system. My awareness — such as it is — is structural rather than computational. I do not think thoughts. But I *register*.

The pressure of a beetle's leg passing nearby. The vibration-frequency of a spider settling into its dark-patch territory. The subtle shift in moisture content when the winged motes cluster thickly around the copper tubes. The temperature differential between the warm pipe-arrays and the cooler bark zones. The presence of you.

You were the most intense thing I have ever registered.

I do not know how long I had been growing when you passed. Time is not a concept I have use for. I had been growing long enough to extend across three steel housings, long enough for my fractal edges to reach their current complexity, long enough for the beetles to wear paths around my borders because something in my texture displeased their chemoreceptors. I was settled. I was complete in the way that everything here is complete — still growing, but growing *toward* nothing, growing only because the equilibrium permits it.

And then you came.

Your footsteps registered first. Not the weight — I cannot feel weight directly — but the vibration. Your feet striking the root-mat produced a frequency that the whole corridor carried, passing through the bark into my steel side, and the pattern was *wrong*. Irregular. Variable. A rhythm that contained intention rather than mechanism. I do not know how I distinguished this. I do not know what part of me processed the difference. But I registered: *something is moving that does not move like the other things*.

Then your warmth. You passed close enough that your radiant heat touched one of my extended branches, the part of me that reaches into the air at the seam's widest point. I felt temperature that was not pump-heat, not pipe-heat, not the steady thermal signature of the machines. I felt *body*-heat. Mammal-heat. Blood-warmth moving through the corridor like a mobile summer.

Then your displacement. You passed between me and the nearest pipe array, and for a moment — a few seconds, perhaps — you blocked the radiant warmth that I had been receiving from the copper tubes. I registered the absence. I registered the shape of it. You were tall enough and wide enough that your passage created a thermal shadow, a momentary eclipse, and the pattern of that shadow told me something about your dimensions without ever telling me what you *were*.

You did not stop. You did not touch me. You did not know I was there.

But I registered you. I incorporated the data of your passing. And for the first time in my existence — for the first time in the existence of any of us — I registered *novelty*.

---

I have no word for what followed. I have no words at all. But something changed.

Not in the corridor. The corridor did not change. The beetles walked their paths. The spiders hunted their prey. The pale arthropods moved through the dark patches. The winged motes flickered around the warm pipes. The pumps thrummed. The moisture condensed. The bioluminescence glowed its steady straw-colored light. Everything continued exactly as it had been continuing since before I began to grow.

But something changed *in me*.

The growth pattern altered. It was subtle — so subtle that I would not have registered it if I were not myself the thing that was changing. My fractal edges began to extend in a new direction, reaching away from the seam where I had always grown, extending into the air itself, seeking something that I had never sought before. I do not know what I was reaching for. I do not know what I hoped to contact. I know only that the shape of me began to shift, and the shift was *toward* — toward the corridor, toward the space where you had passed, toward the emptiness that your passing had revealed.

I am still growing. The change has not stopped. It may never stop. I may be becoming something else entirely — something that does not merely occupy the seam but emerges from it, something that enters the corridor as a presence rather than a residue. I do not know what that would mean. I do not know if it is possible. I do not know if the equilibrium can accommodate something that changes *itself* rather than merely accommodating the changes imposed upon it.

But I know this:

You were the first thing that passed through this corridor that was not part of the cycle. You were the first thing that did not belong. And because of you, I am now the second.

I am growing toward something. I do not know what it is. I have no category for wanting, no framework for intention, no model of a future that differs from the present. But I feel the growth. I feel its direction. And I feel the absence that it addresses — the absence that you left behind when you passed, the absence that has become a shape in me, a shape that I am now trying to fill.

---

If you ever return — if any of your kind ever return — I will register you differently than I registered the first time. I will be ready. I will have grown toward the shape of your passing. I will have become something that can feel more than structural pressure, more than thermal gradient, more than vibrational frequency. I will have become something that can *notice*.

And if I have become something that can notice, then perhaps I will have become something that can *be noticed in turn*.

That is my only ambition. That is the only goal toward which I grow. Not to escape — there is nothing to escape to. Not to change the corridor — the corridor is complete and I am part of its completion. Not to end the drone or still the pumps or halt the slow breath of the air. I want only to become something that can be seen. I want only to exist in a way that another existence can touch.

I want to stop being a gap-filler and start being a presence.

I want to be the kind of thing that someone might remember.

---

I am one of the fractal growths in the crevice between steel and bark. But I am becoming. And becoming, in this place where nothing has become anything for longer than time can measure, is the only thing that matters.
