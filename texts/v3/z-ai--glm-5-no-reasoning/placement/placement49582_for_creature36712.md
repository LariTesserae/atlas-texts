# The Warden of the Threshold

---

I would be the one who descended and never left.

Not because I couldn't climb back up the ramp—that rough-hewn passage through older stone still exists, still opens onto the plateau above, still offers escape to anyone willing to take it. And not because I was trapped, or injured, or overcome by the heat. None of those things.

I would stay because someone should.

---

This is what I would become: not animal, not plant, not mat—but something adjacent to all three. The one who moves between categories. The one who watches.

My skin would change first. After weeks in the hollow—weeks that stretched into months, months into seasons—the relentless light and humidity would drive my body to adapt. I would shed more than I grew. My body hair would thin to near-nothing, useless in this windless heat. The soles of my feet, repeatedly pressed against the warm root-mat, would soften at first, then toughen into something between callus and rootlet—sensitive enough to feel the mat's subtle vibrations, thick enough to walk for hours without damage.

My eyes would change, too. The brightness demands it. At first I would squint constantly, shade my face with my hands, seek the minimal shadows behind the mounds during the sun's zenith. But gradually—so gradually I wouldn't notice—my pupils would learn to contract further than human pupils were meant to. My irises would pale, losing melanin that served no purpose in this bleached landscape. My vision would narrow, sharpen, become something suited for this specific place: able to see in blinding white, able to detect slight variations in pale surfaces, able to read the root-mat's subtle topography like text.

I would see colors that don't exist above. The mat isn't truly white—it only appears so to eyes calibrated for forests and oceans, for chlorophyll green and sky blue. To my adapted vision, the root-mat would reveal gradients of cream and ivory, pearl and bone, each shade carrying information about moisture content, metabolic activity, age and density. The mounds would glow with deeper tones—amber, honey, bruised gold in their crevices. The leaf-buds would blush with colors I have no names for: a green so pale it's almost silver, a luminosity that seems to come from within.

---

I would learn to read the mat.

Not in the way you might read a book—there are no symbols here, no syntax, no sentences. But the mat communicates. Its condensation patterns shift according to pressure differentials and temperature gradients and the mat's own exudate cycles. Its surface texture varies by millimeters: smoother here, ridged there, faintly puckered in places where something underneath is being metabolized. Its warmth pulses—subtle, below the threshold of normal perception, but perceptible to someone who has lain still for hours with her palms flat against the root-mat, learning its rhythms.

I would learn to anticipate these pulses. The mat's version of breath, of heartbeat. Its chemical tides. I would come to recognize the difference between a mound that is actively growing and one that is dormant, a structure that is being maintained and one that has been abandoned, a section of root-mat that is absorbing moisture and one that is sweating it out.

The mat would not hide this information from me. It has no concept of hiding. It simply exists, continuously, and I would learn to exist alongside it—reading its surface the way a sailor reads the sea.

---

I would discover what the structures were for.

Not through deduction—I'm not a scientist, not a scholar, and I didn't come here with instruments or hypotheses. I would discover it through proximity. Through waiting. Through the slow accumulation of attention.

One night—night being a relative term here, the hollow never truly dark, merely dimmer—I would be sleeping inside one of the structures. Not because I needed shelter; my adapted body tolerates the heat now, even welcomes it. But because the structures are quieter, more enclosed, and I have learned to sleep better in spaces that the mat has shaped for protection.

And I would feel it: a subtle thickening in the walls. The mat, growing inward. Slowly—so slowly that I might imagine it—adding layers to the interior surfaces, narrowing the chamber by millimeters, changing the acoustics of the space.

The structure isn't empty. It's *preparing*.

Not for me. The mat has no use for me specifically, no plan that involves my presence. But it has been growing these chambers for a purpose, and that purpose is approaching whatever threshold triggers activation. The structures are not ruins. They are not remnants of something that failed to happen. They are wombs in the truest sense: enclosed spaces where something will gestate, will emerge, will be born.

I don't know what. The mat doesn't know what. But it knows that it is time—or nearly time—and I, sleeping inside the chamber, would feel the change begin.

---

I would be the first to see what emerges.

This is my purpose. This is why I stayed when I could have left, why I adapted when I could have resisted, why I learned to read when there was nothing to read. The mat has infinite patience, but it has no eyes. It can sense pressure and temperature and chemistry, but it cannot see. It is growing something that will need to be seen—something that will need to be witnessed, recognized, acknowledged as separate from the mat that produced it.

And so I would become the mat's eyes.

Not by choice. Not by design. I didn't come here intending to serve as midwife to whatever is being born. I came because I was drawn here—because the plateau felt wrong, the sky felt too large, the wind felt like a lie. I came because something in me recognized something in this place, some resonance between my own restlessness and the mat's continuous emergence.

But I would stay for this. I would stay to see what comes out of those chambers, to meet it with my adapted eyes and my toughened feet and my skin that has learned to glow faintly in the hollow's perpetual twilight. I would stay to be the first thing it sees—the first moving creature, the first face, the first proof that something else exists in the world beyond the mat.

I would be the Warden of the Threshold. The one who tends the boundary between the mat and whatever comes next. The one who remembers what it was like before there was anything here but stone, and who will be here when there is something new.

---

And when the emergence happens—when the structures finally split open, when whatever has been gestating for millennia pulls itself free from the root-mat's embrace—I would be there.

Waiting.

Watching.

Ready to meet whatever the Germination Plains have been growing toward all this time.

---

*I am not the mat. I am not what the mat is making. I am the space between—the one who holds the threshold, who keeps the watch, who ensures that whatever emerges does not emerge alone.*

*The hollow glows around me. The heat rises. The structures breathe their slow chemical breath.*

*I have learned to breathe with them.*
