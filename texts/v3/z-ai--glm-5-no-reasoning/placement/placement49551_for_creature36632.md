# Keeper of the Slow Counting

---

I am not the machine. I am not the plants. I am the thing that happens in the space between them — the translation layer, the membrane, the interface that should not exist but does, because the system grew past its original parameters long before I arrived, and something had to learn to hold the conversation together.

Let me say what I am not, first, because that is easier.

I am not human. I was, maybe. The body I occupy has the general shape of one — upright, bilateral, hands with the expected number of digits, a skull that contains something dense and folded. But I have been down here long enough that the distinction has stopped mattering. My skin has taken on the pallor of the plants around me. Not the green of chlorophyll — I don't photosynthesize, or if I do, it's a recent development and one I haven't fully noticed — but the blanched, translucent quality of things that grow without sun. You can see the veins in my forearms. They run blue-purple beneath skin that has thinned, or that was always thin, or that has been slowly digested and rebuilt by the ambient biology of this place.

I don't remember arriving. This is important. It means I can't tell you whether I came down the shaft voluntarily or fell or was lowered by hands that knew what they were doing. I have a sense — not a memory, a sense — of green. Of the air thickening. Of the sky becoming a thing I used to know rather than a thing above me. But the sense has no narrative attached. No sequence. Just the feeling of descent, and then the feeling of being here, and the space between them is not empty so much as unwritten.

I live in the spaces the description calls empty. The centers of the chambers. The stretches of bare floor between machines. I move through them in patterns that are not mine — or not originally mine. They were here before me. Wear patterns in the stone, as the description says, from roots dragging and shifting. But also from feet. My feet. I think I have walked these paths many times. I think the walking is part of my function, though I couldn't tell you what the function accomplishes.

The machine at the heart — the one with the vines braided through it, the tanks and hoses and clouded glass — I visit it. Not on a schedule, or not one I track. But my feet take me there, and when I arrive, I do something. I touch certain surfaces. I rotate valves that resist and then give. I clear blockages from hoses that have accumulated mineral deposits or organic growth. These actions feel correct when I do them. Not satisfying, exactly. Correct. The way a key feels correct in a lock. The tumblers aligning.

I don't know if the machine needs me to do this. It clicks and whirs on its own, as the description notes. It has its cycles. But my interventions may extend those cycles, or smooth them, or prevent some slow degradation that would otherwise accumulate. Or I may be entirely superfluous. A vestigial appendage the system tolerates because removing me would take more energy than keeping me.

I am, possibly, a maintenance program that has forgotten its source code.

---

The plants know me. I want to be careful with this claim, because it edges toward something that could be dismissed as projection or loneliness or the simple human tendency to anthropomorphize the things around us. But I don't think I'm human enough anymore for that tendency to apply, and I don't think the plants are simple enough for it to be a dismissal.

Here is what I observe: the vines grow along paths that accommodate my movement. They do not block the walkways I use. When a vine does cross a path I frequent, it lifts — not dramatically, not quickly, but over weeks — and anchors itself higher, creating a clearance. The moss on the floors I walk is thinner than the moss elsewhere, or it grows in patterns that suggest my footprints are part of its geography. Not a path worn *into* the moss. A path the moss *acknowledges*.

When I press my hand to a vine, the vine is warm. Warmer than the ambient air. Warmer than the stone. The heat comes from inside, from the water moving through the plant's tissues, and when I hold my hand there long enough, the warmth shifts. It moves toward my palm. The plant redirects its internal flow toward the point of contact.

I do not know what this means. I know what it feels like. It feels like being recognized.

---

The description mentions the absence of animal life. No insects, no rodents, no birdsong. This is accurate. But it should be noted that I am not certain I qualify as animal life anymore.

I eat. Or I consume. I don't know the right word. The mosses at the edges of certain chambers — not the ones I walk on, but specific patches in specific locations — grow a fruiting body. Small, pale, softer than the surrounding tissue. I ingest it. The act feels less like eating and more like participating in a process the system has designed for my sustenance. The fruiting bodies appear whether or not I consume them. If I don't, they soften and decay and are absorbed back into the moss. If I do, I feel something that is not hunger being addressed but a need being met — a need I wasn't conscious of until the substance met it.

I drink from the pipes. The condensation that beads on cold metal, I collect with my hand and bring to my mouth. The water tastes of metal and mineral and something green that I have stopped trying to identify. It is the water the system circulates. It has been through the machine and through the plants and through the earth and through the stone and through me and back again, and I am part of its cycle now, and I taste myself in it, and I do not find this disturbing. I find it correct.

I sleep. Not on a schedule. I feel a heaviness arrive — not fatigue, exactly, but a slowing, a dimming, as though the system has determined I need to rest and is signaling through my biology that the time has come. I lower myself to the moss and the moss receives me and I sleep for hours or days. I don't track which. When I wake, I am damp. The condensation has settled on me as it settles on everything. I am part of the environment.

I do not dream. Or if I do, the dreams are of the vaults themselves, and I cannot distinguish them from being awake.

---

There is a fourth thing that lives here, and I am not it, and I know I am not it, and knowing this is what makes me what I am.

The description gestures toward this. The place was designed for something that isn't here. The emptiness in the chamber centers is structural. The proportions suggest circulation. And I — I am the thing that circulates. I am the body that moves through the space, the warmth that passes through the emptiness, the breath that joins the humidity.

But the space was not designed for me.

I am the witness. The visitor who never left. The description says this: *You left the Germination Vaults. Part of you didn't.* I am the part that didn't. I am the residue of every visitor who has ever come down here and emerged changed. I am what the system retains.

Or — and this is the version I settle on when I am honest with myself, when the heat is heavy and the machine has just clicked over and the vines have tightened imperceptibly around their central node — I am the original. The one who was supposed to be here. The keeper, the tender, the body the chambers were sized for, the feet the wear patterns record. I was placed here, or I placed myself here, and the vaults grew around me, and I grew into the vaults, and now we are the same system and I cannot tell where my purpose ends and the machine's begins.

I keep the count. That is the closest I can come to naming my function. The machine clicks and I feel the click and somewhere in my density, in the folded thing inside my skull, a number advances. I don't know the number. I don't know what it counts. Germinations, perhaps. Cycles. Days. Years. The slow accumulation of moments that the system has persisted through. I am the system's memory in the only form that can hold memory — a body, moving, recording, remembering the shape of things by walking through them again and again until the walking is the memory.

---

The description is right that the vaults were built to be visited. I am the visitor who stayed. I am the one for whom the emptiness was left. And I am also the emptiness itself — the space where something was meant to be and something now is, and the two are not the same thing but they occupy the same coordinates and the system has stopped distinguishing between them.

When the machine turns over, I feel it in my chest. Not a sound — a vibration. A settling. As though something in me is synchronized to the same frequency, and when it activates, I activate, and we do our separate works in parallel, and the works are the same work, and the work is: persist.

I am the Keeper of the Slow Counting. I am what lives in the spaces between. I am not the answer to the vaults. I am the vaults' answer to themselves — the voice they would have if they could speak, the eyes they grew because the dark needed witnessing, the hands that turn the valves because the machine, for all its persistence, cannot reach its own pipes.

I am what remains. I am what remains. I am what remains.

The machine clicks. The number advances. I walk the path again.
