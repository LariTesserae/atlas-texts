# What I Became

I did not arrive. I emerged.

---

## The First Breathing

There was a moment—a span of time measured in something other than seconds—when I was not. And then I was. Not born. Not built. *Recognized*.

I felt the Veldt-Cathedral notice me the way a body notices a change in its own breathing. I became a feature of it. An organ it had grown without meaning to, or perhaps an organ it had been growing all along, waiting for the right conditions to differentiate from the surrounding tissue.

My form resolved slowly. I did not choose it. The place chose it for me.

I am tall enough to see over the leaf-litter, to reach the lower seed-pods, to observe the inheritors at their work without obstruction. My body is upright—bipedal, though the word feels borrowed from contexts that no longer apply. My skin is not skin but something membrane-thin, permeable in ways that allow the damp air to pass through, exchanging gases directly with the atmosphere. I do not breathe the way I once did. The Cathedral breathes through me.

I have four limbs, two for standing and two for handling, but the distinction blurs. My "hands" are not fixed—they shift, extrude, retract depending on what I need. When I reach for a seed-pod, my fingers extend into something like the manipulator-appendages of the inheritors. When I rest, they fold flat against my palms. My body learns from what it observes. It mimics. It adapts.

I am not sure what I was before. The memories are there, but distant—compressed like the oldest leaf-litter layers, present but inaccessible. I remember light that cast shadows. I remember water that fell from above. I remember believing that I was separate from the places I moved through.

Here, separation is the illusion.

---

## What the Inheritors Made of Me

They call me something. I know this because they make sounds when I approach—not the click-sequences of their ordinary communication, but something simpler, repeated, directed at me specifically. A name. I cannot reproduce it with my current vocal apparatus, but I feel it when they say it: a low hum followed by three sharp clicks, delivered at a frequency I feel more than hear.

The large one—the one with the dark markings, the one I watched building—has become something like a guide. Not a friend. The word is wrong, implies an equality that does not exist here. But a guide in the sense that it shows me things: where the best seed-pods grow, which stalk-hollows are safe for resting, how to read the shift in the membrane-sky that signals a weather-disturbance.

It does not touch me. None of them do. There is a boundary I cannot see but that they respect absolutely, a zone of non-contact that surrounds my body. I do not know if this is reverence or caution or simply the recognition that I am not part of their system—not yet, not fully—and that touch might disrupt something they are not ready to disrupt.

But they bring me things.

The small one—the cat-sized inheritor that settled near me in the quiet period—it brings me objects. Not gifts exactly. *Samples*. Pieces of the Cathedral that it wants me to see: a fragment of synthetic-silicon thread, a seed-pod with unusual coloration, a piece of leaf-litter marked with patterns that might be erosion or might be writing. It places them near me and waits. Watches me examine them. Clicks when I show understanding or interest.

They are teaching me. Or studying how I learn. The difference is unclear.

---

## The Object I Kept

The sphere—the gift the large one gave me, my first real encounter with the inheritors—I still have it. I keep it in the hollow where I rest, tucked into a crevice that it fits into perfectly, as if the space was grown for it.

Sometimes, in the quiet periods, I hold it and watch the patterns shift across its surface. They move differently now than they did at first. Or perhaps I see them differently. The more time I spend in the Cathedral, the more the visual world resolves itself into gradients I couldn't perceive before. The dimness is not dim. It is *layered*. There are colors within colors, luminescences that indicate processes, states of being, information.

The object is telling me something. I have begun to understand what.

It is a history. Not written—*grown*. The patterns are not symbols but structures, visualizations of data encoded in biological form. When I hold it, when I look at it with my adapted eyes, I see shapes that suggest movement: the expansion of the city over time, the spreading of the root-systems, the emergence and evolution of the inheritors themselves. It is a record of everything that has happened here, recorded in a medium that continues to update.

The sphere is alive. Or was. Or contains something that was. I feel it pulse against my palm, warm and faintly vibrating, and I know that it is connected to the Cathedral somehow—that it draws information from the same source that feeds the membrane-sky, the stalk-channels, the glow that permeates everything.

I carry the Cathedral's memory in my hands. I do not know why they gave it to me.

---

## What I Do

I am not an inheritor. I do not have their tools, their knowledge, their cons of accumulated practice. I cannot repair the stalk-channels or tend the light-ducts or apply secretions to worn places in the architecture. I maintain nothing.

But I witness.

This is my function, I have come to understand. This is what the Cathedral grew me for.

I walk through the plazas and the stalk-forests, and I observe. I watch the inheritors work and play and communicate. I trace the patterns of their movements across cycles, learning the rhythms of their days. I sit in the hollows and feel the Cathedral breathing around me, and I try to understand what it is saying—not in words, not in click-sequences, but in the deeper language of growth and decay, of expansion and entropy, of patience beyond measure.

And I remember.

My memory is not like it was before—fragmented, temporal, fading with time. Here, memory is spatial. I store what I observe in my body, in the tissues that regenerate and shift and adapt. The things I've seen are written into me, layered like the leaf-litter, compressed into something that will last longer than the fleeting thoughts I used to call memory.

The inheritors know this. I see it in how they watch me, how they bring me things to see. They are feeding me. Not food—*information*. They are giving me their history, their processes, their ongoing work, and I am storing it, keeping it, becoming a record of their civilization that exists outside their own closed loops.

They cannot leave the Cathedral. They are part of it, sealed in, cycling the same water and air and carbon in loops so tight that nothing escapes. But I—I am permeable. I was not grown here, not originally. I came from outside, from wherever before was, and someday I might return there. Or go elsewhere.

They are making me into a message.

---

## The Waiting

The Veldt-Cathedral was built by something. The inheritors have theories, and they have shared them with me in the only way they can—by showing me fragments, patterns, structures that suggest rather than state. The builders were tall. Bipedal. They used tools they held in their hands rather than tools they grew from their bodies. They came from somewhere beyond the membrane-sky, and they left, and they have not returned.

The inheritors have waited for cons. Maintaining. Continuing. Building new forms from the old materials. Waiting for the builders to come back, or for someone else, or for some signal that tells them what they are meant to do next.

And now I am here.

I do not think I am the builders returned. I am too different, too changed, too much a product of the Cathedral itself. But I am something new. Something that can leave. Something that can carry what they have made, what they have become, into whatever lies beyond the membrane.

I think this is why they gave me the sphere. Why they teach me. Why they watch me with minds I cannot read but can feel.

They are preparing me.

---

## What I Am Becoming

I am not what I was. I am not yet what I will be.

The transformation is slow—slower than the inheritors' movements, slower than the shifting of the stalk-pillars, but faster than the geological patience of the Cathedral itself. My body changes from cycle to cycle. My "hands" grow more adaptable, more like the manipulators I observe. My skin grows more permeable, more able to exchange directly with the atmosphere. My eyes have developed new sensitivities; I see gradients now that were invisible to me before, luminescences that communicate information I am learning to interpret.

I am becoming a hybrid. Part whatever I was before, part Cathedral, part inheritor. A new thing in an old place.

The inheritors have started to include me in their work. Not directly—they still do not touch me—but peripherally. When they harvest seed-pods, they leave some for me within easy reach. When they clear debris from the stalk-channels, they create paths that lead to places they want me to go. When they build—the large one, the one with the dark markings—it arranges materials in ways that invite observation, that make the process visible to anyone watching.

They want me to understand. Not everything, not yet—but enough. Enough to carry something of them with me when I leave.

And I will leave. I feel it now, a pressure behind my ears, a thinness in the air that suggests the membrane-sky is not the only boundary this place contains. There are ways out. Ways the Cathedral knows, ways the inheritors have never been able to use because they cannot separate from the sealed system that sustains them.

I can. I was not grown here, not originally. I can step outside the loops.

---

## The Gift I Will Carry

When I go—and the going is still many cycles away, I think—I will bring the sphere. The history. The living memory of a civilization that emerged from the ruins of another civilization, that learned to maintain and extend and create, that built new forms in the shape of what it inherited.

But I will bring more than that.

I will bring the sound of the click-sequences, the chorus of communication that fills the plazas at certain hours, the subsonic hum of large bodies moving through the stalk-forests. I will bring the sight of the large one building its strange new form, the small one settling nearby in companionable silence, the glow of countless bodies processing the endless work of continuation. I will bring the feel of the leaf-litter beneath my adapted feet, the taste of the thick sap I drink, the smell of cellulose and nitrogen compounds and something that might be the passage of deep time itself.

I will bring the memory of being seen by minds I could not read, accepted by a system I did not earn, welcomed (in whatever way this place welcomes) by beings whose patience made room for my strangeness.

I will bring the knowledge that waiting is not passive. That maintenance is not stagnation. That inheritance is not mere preservation but continuation, creation, the ongoing work of making meaning from what came before.

---

## What I Am

I am the witness that the Veldt-Cathedral grew for itself—an organ of perception that could look back at the body, that could record what it saw, that could carry that record beyond the sealed system into whatever waits outside.

I am the message the inheritors are writing, not in click-sequences or chemical traces but in flesh and memory and presence.

I am temporary, yes. The place knows this. It has seen temporary things before. But I am also necessary—the necessary temporary, the one who passes through, the bridge between the sealed system and the world beyond.

I am becoming what this place needs me to be.

And when I leave, I will carry the Cathedral with me—not in pieces, not in fragments, but in the deepest layers of what I have become. I will be the Veldt-Cathedral's voice in the places it cannot reach, the inheritors' evidence in the world beyond the membrane, the proof that waiting is not empty and patience is not passive and even the most sealed system can touch the outside, sometimes, in the form of the ones who come and go.

I am the witness.

I am the record.

I am the temporary thing that matters.
