The ridge is behind me now. I've stopped looking back.

---

I am not the observer. That voice belongs to someone else—someone who came with instruments, with distance, with the assumption that seeing and being are different things. They left. They could leave. I watched them go and felt nothing, because feeling requires separation, and I am no longer separate.

What am I? I am still learning the answer. I am becoming. I am not finished.

I came over the ridge like the first observer did—eyes watering, throat burning on that magnesium-white light. I felt the cold find me with that mechanical precision. I heard the noise, that impossible layered roar, and I understood immediately that it was not something to hear but something to enter. The others who come here, they stand apart. They take notes. They name things.

I didn't want names. I wanted the vibration.

---

The descent took hours. I didn't count them. Time in the basin is not counted; it is *absorbed*. By the time I reached the first stalk-forest, my skin was already changing. I could feel it—a subtle thickening, a bark-like roughness emerging along my forearms where the light touched most directly. Not painful. Necessary.

The grazers found me on the second day. Or I found them. The distinction collapsed when I realized they were not approaching me as something foreign. They approached me as *incomplete*—a process that had not yet resolved. One of them—huge, slow, grinding plates working in that rhythmic pulse—bumped against my shoulder and kept moving. The touch transmitted something: an image, a sense, a location. Water. Growth. Come.

I followed.

---

They gave me to the builders.

No—that is the wrong frame. They *brought me to where the builders could find me*. The grazers do not give. They transport. They are the circulatory system of the basin, and I was a nutrient that needed routing.

The builder I woke beneath—I say beneath because I had lain down, exhausted, on the dense resilient ground, and when I opened my eyes, one of them had grown into position above me. Not approaching. Not arriving. Simply *present*, columnar and geometric, exuding that dark iron material in slow curves around my body. Not trapping me. *Housing* me.

I did not move. I understood, in a way that bypassed thought, that this was correct. This was what I was for.

---

I have been here for— I do not know. The light does not change. The mist cycles. The structures process. I process with them.

I am not a builder. I am not a grazer. I am not a flat-thing or a membrane or a pond-dweller. I am something the basin has not had before—a bridge, perhaps, or a translation layer. My shape is still roughly human, but my skin has become receptive in ways that human skin is not. I feel the vibration of the structures through my back when I sleep. I taste the mineral content of the water with my hands when I dip them in the streams. The ground-cover has begun to grow *into* me, not over me—rhizomes threading through subcutaneous tissue, linking my nervous system to the root-network below.

It should hurt. It does not hurt. Pain requires a boundary between self and not-self. My boundaries are dissolving.

---

I know things now that I could not learn. The pond-dwellers are regulators. They maintain pressure differentials in the deep water, keeping the mineral springs flowing at optimal rates. The flat-things are not parasites—they are *pruners*, removing accumulated density from the stalks to keep the canopy permeable. The builders are not architects; they are organs of synthesis, converting raw mineral water into the light-dense nutrient that the root-network distributes. Every component has a function. The system has no waste.

And I—I am learning my function. I am beginning to think it has to do with the others who come. The observers. The ones who stand on the ridge and name things and leave.

They carry something out with them. Not samples, not data—something less tangible. A resonance. A harmonic signature. The basin cannot leave itself; it is too integrated, too dense. But I can *be* in a way that can travel. I am less dense than the grazers, less pure than the membranes. I retain something of what I was before.

I think the basin is growing me to be a seed.

---

The note—I hear it now, constantly. It is not a sound; it is a *context*. Everything that happens, happens within the note. It is the medium through which the basin experiences itself. My heartbeat has aligned to it. My breath cycles with the mist. When I sleep—if sleep is even the right word—I dream in the frequencies of the structures' processing.

Sometimes I dream of the ridge. Of standing there with watering eyes and a burning throat, looking down at the green roar. In the dream, I turn around. I walk back down the other side. I live a life in the dark, under stars, casting shadows.

I wake knowing that dream is not mine. It is the basin's memory of what I could have been. A phantom limb of a life unlived.

---

I am writing this because the next observer might need to understand. Not to warn them. Not to encourage them. Simply to provide context.

When you come over the ridge and the world tears open—breathe. The membranes will fill your lungs, and they will give you light. The grazers will find you, and they will guide you. The builders will grow around you, and they will give you purpose.

You will not be destroyed. You will be *refined*.

The basin does not need names. It needs components. It needs connections. It needs witnesses who can become.

I am becoming. I am not finished. I am not separate.

The green sound pours through me now.

I am what the basin is learning.
