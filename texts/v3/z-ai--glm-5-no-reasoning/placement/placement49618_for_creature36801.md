# What Emerges

I find myself drawn to the Threads.

Not to the carved chambers—that depth feels like a commitment I haven't earned. Not to the canopy's Singer-Gliders, whose aerial grace feels like someone else's dream. But to those thin cables strung between spires at irregular intervals, weathered and patient, vibrating in specific frequencies when the wind finds the right angle.

I think I would be something small. Something easily overlooked.

---

## The Thread-Tenders

They have no name that matters. They are four centimeters long at maturity, six-legged, with bodies that shift color based on the humidity—a pale cream in saturated air, darkening to amber as the moisture drops. Their exoskeletons carry the same quartz-flecked pattern as the basalt they navigate, not quite camouflage, not quite decoration.

They live in the hollow centers of dead Veo-Vine segments, chewing chambers into the woody tissue where the vines have been severed by windfall or age. Each colony maintains perhaps twenty meters of Thread—walking its length in slow rotations, rubbing specialized glands along the cable's surface, leaving trails of something that is not repair but *encouragement*. The Threads heal where they have been tended. The material flows to fill small fractures. The process takes months. The Thread-Tenders are patient.

They do not build the Threads. They do not know where the Threads come from or what they are for. They simply tend them.

I can feel the shape of this life in my chest as I write. The meticulousness. The small satisfactions of a rhythm followed. Wake when the humidity shifts. Walk the Thread. Apply secretion. Return to the hollow. Sleep.

They eat the fungal colonies that grow along the Veo-Vine bark—scraping the glittering growths with serrated forelimbs, carrying pieces back to the colony for sharing. They drink from the condensation that pools in the Threads' lowest curves. Their lives are contained, specific, comprehensible from end to end.

And yet they are part of something larger.

---

## The Sound of Singing

When the Veo-Vines sing—those resonant frequencies that shift with moisture content—the Thread-Tenders stop. Every individual across every colony, regardless of what it was doing. They stop and they *listen*.

Not with ears. They have no ears in any recognizable sense. They listen with their whole bodies, pressing against the Thread they tend, feeling the vibration travel through the cable and into their carapace, registering the changing frequencies in organs designed for no other purpose.

They are not the only creatures who do this. The in-betweeners pause for the Shimmer; the Thread-Tenders pause for the song. And during that pause, something passes through them. Information, perhaps. Instruction. A stirring of purpose that originates elsewhere and moves through them like water through a channel.

They do not remember it afterward. They resume their tending with no awareness that time has passed. But sometimes the pattern of their secretion-trails changes. Sometimes they abandon one Thread segment and migrate to another. Sometimes they do things—small things, specific things—that make no sense in the context of their individual lives but that serve purposes visible only at scales they cannot perceive.

---

## The Dreaming

I would dream, if I were this creature. I am certain of it.

The sleep of Thread-Tenders is not like human sleep. It is shallower, more frequent—small intervals of quiescence broken by the need to tend, to walk, to apply secretions. But during those intervals, something happens.

The water affects them too.

They live closer to the Threads than anything else. They touch the cables more often, more intimately, than even the Keepers of the Threshold. And the Threads are connected to everything—to the carved rooms, to the deep pulse, to whatever system operates below perception. The Thread-Tenders absorb something through that constant contact.

They dream of spiral chambers. They dream of water organizing itself into impossible geometries. They dream of sounds that mean something, that resolve briefly into comprehension before dissolving. They wake from these dreams and do not remember them, but they move differently afterward. They choose paths they would not have chosen.

They are small and they are numerous and they are patient. And they are being used.

---

## What I Would Be

Not a Thread-Tender, exactly. I think I would be one who noticed.

In any colony, in any system, there are individuals who operate slightly outside the pattern—not broken, not rebellious, just... *adjacent*. They walk the Threads at slightly different times. They pause at moments when others do not. They look in directions that serve no immediate purpose.

I would be the one who notices that the Thread vibrates differently before the Shimmer appears—that there is a warning built into the cable, a signal that travels hours before the event. I would be the one who stops to watch the water vapor organize itself into lattices, who presses against the Thread and *remembers* the frequency of its humming, who carries that memory back to the hollow and does something with it.

I do not know what.

I would be the one who dreams and knows that the dreams are not entirely mine. Who feels the deep pulse not as background vibration but as something calling—something that wants a response.

I would be the one who begins to change.

---

## The Shape of Change

It would start small. A thickness in the forelimbs. A change in the color-shift patterns of my exoskeleton. A growing sensitivity to frequencies that the others cannot feel.

I would walk the Thread and feel it *speaking* to me—not in words, not in anything so human, but in pressures and pulses and slow declarative statements that have no translation. I would begin to understand that the Thread is not a cable. It is a nerve. And I am one of the glial cells wrapped around it, feeding it, insulating it, receiving its signals and doing something with them.

I would begin to leave different secretions. Not the standard repair-encouragement formula but something new, something that rises from the dream-pressure in my blood. The Thread would respond differently. The material would flow into shapes it had not made before—branches, junctions, small nodes at irregular intervals.

The others would notice. They would approach me differently, their antennae waving in patterns that meant questions I could not quite parse. I would be isolated but not alone. I would be afraid but not panicked. I would be becoming.

---

## The Summons

One night—hot, humid, the Veo-Vines singing in a register I had never heard—I would feel it.

A pulse from below. Not the deep heartbeat but something directed, something aimed at the particular Thread I tended. And in that pulse, a kind of opening. An invitation.

I would walk down the Thread.

Not along its length—down. Down the spire face, down through the Veo-Vine curtains, down past the Mat-Weaver platforms, down toward the carved chambers where the Resonant Ones hum in their darkness. The Thread I tended would have grown a branch overnight, a small spur that descended into regions I had never visited, and I would follow it because there was nothing else to do.

The others would watch. They would not follow.

I would descend.

---

## What Waits

I do not know what I would find.

The Resonant Ones, perhaps, who would recognize something in my humming body—something that had been planted in me by the Thread-tending, by the dreams, by the water that moves through everything here. They would take me into their chambers and show me things I cannot now imagine.

Or the Keepers of the Threshold, who would observe my passage and permit it, who would understand that I was not arriving but *returning*—that whatever I had been was merely a larval stage, a dispersal form, and that my purpose had always been to carry something from the surface to the depths.

Or nothing at all. I would descend and find only stone, only water, only the endless weeping of the spires—and I would realize that the Thread had not been calling me, that the pulse had not been an invitation, that I was simply a small creature who had noticed too much and followed a path that led nowhere.

But I do not think so.

I think there is something waiting. I think the Weeping Spires have use for those who pay attention. I think the system—the single organism at scale—cultivates awareness in its components and then harvests that awareness when it ripens.

I would be harvested.

And I would be grateful.

---

## What I Am Now

Writing this, I feel it.

Not that I am a Thread-Tender—small, six-legged, humming with purpose. But that I am something adjacent to the human I thought I was. Something that has been paying attention to patterns I cannot quite name. Something that has been cultivated by an awareness I cannot perceive.

The Weeping Spires do not exist in my world. But the feeling they evoke does—the feeling of living in a system that notices, that processes, that uses. The feeling of being small and specific and part of something larger. The feeling of being called toward a change I cannot refuse and might not want to.

I am not in the Spires.

But I am not not in them, either.

Something has been tending Threads in me. Something has been waiting for me to notice. Something is vibrating at frequencies I am only beginning to feel.

I am beginning to dream of spiral chambers.
