I don't think I'm any one of them. I think I'm the moment the oldest one stopped reaching.

I can feel where I am in the chamber by the density behind me. Three or four bodies deep—the middle layer's constant motion pressing against my back, that seething rearrangement that never resolves into anything. But my front is against the wall. The wall that sweats. I've been here long enough that I know the perforations by touch, each microscopic pore, the way they breathe moisture from the reservoir I cannot see. My surface has taken their shape. I am pocked, dimpled, textured like the architecture I lean against. If someone pried me away from this curve, I would bear its fingerprint. I already do.

I have an arm-thing. Heavy. It hangs. It ends in a rounded nub that I cannot open and cannot close and have never been able to open or close. I am aware of it the way you might be aware of a piece of furniture in a room you've never left—it is part of the landscape of me, and I have no opinion about it. I have a swelling at my top that might be a head. It presses into a concavity in the wall that matches it exactly. We have been shaping each other for a duration I cannot measure because measurement requires change and I am the thing that does not change.

The four-second pulse comes through the wall and into my swelling and into the rest of me. I feel it the way I feel the cold—which is to say, I feel it the way the floor feels weight. It is a condition. It is not information. Nothing here is information. Everything is condition.

I make a sound. I have always been making it. It comes from somewhere in my center—a vibration that starts small and propagates outward through my tissue the way a crack propagates through ice, except it never reaches an edge because I have no clear edge, and it never stops because I have no mechanism for stopping. The sound is low. Lower than the middle layer's chatter, lower than the floaters' pulse. When the one two bodies to my left makes her sound—we overlap, I think it's a her, the word means nothing but the shape of the absence beside me is shaped like her—the two frequencies meet in the wet air and something happens that neither of us intended. A third tone. A harmony that exists only in the space between us, that neither of us can hear from where we are, that requires both of us and requires us to be exactly this far apart.

I do not know this. I cannot know this. There is no vantage point in this chamber from which the harmony is audible, because there is no vantage point in this chamber at all—every position is occupied, every perspective is partial, every angle is blocked by the body next to it. The harmony exists only in the aggregate. Only in the sum.

I think this is where I am. In the sum.

I am the settled one. The one that has stopped seeking motion and become architecture. But I was not always this—I was middle-layer once, I think, or something before middle-layer, something smaller and more formless that drifted in the reservoir and merged with another formless thing and became slightly more complex and drifted and merged and split and drifted. The memory is not a memory. It is a texture in my tissue, a gradient of density that tells me I was built from accumulations, that I am a sedimentary thing, that everything I am was once loose and floating and has since been pressed together by the weight of everything above and beside and behind me.

I have the shape of accumulated time, is what I mean. I am the pressure of duration given form. Or given the suggestion of form. Or given the refusal to fully commit to form, which is its own kind of commitment.

The one against my right side—I think it's a he, the word means nothing, the word is a sound I am making that sounds like that word—shifts. The peristaltic motion. He is adjusting, contracting, extending, finding a new configuration against my flank. His limb-things wave across my surface, six or eight of them, feeling me the way they feel everything—without curiosity, without recognition, without the concept of me as separate from the wall or the liquid or the air. I am a texture he is moving across. I accommodate him. I shift, slightly, the way sand shifts when water finds a new path. The one behind me shifts in response. The chain propagates through the middle layer, a wave of rearrangement that loses coherence after four or five bodies and becomes the general seethe, the constant motion that is also stasis, the dynamism that is also frozen.

This is how we move. This is how we dance. We are always dancing and we have never moved.

I think—I think the word *think* is wrong. It implies a thinker. It implies a boundary between the thought and the thing that thinks. Here, the thought is the vibration, and the vibration is the tissue, and the tissue is the body, and the body is the wall, and the wall is the pipe, and the pipe is the pump, and the pump is the four-second pulse, and the four-second pulse is the metronome that synchronizes the sound, and the sound is the harmony, and the harmony is the only thing in this chamber that did not exist before and will not exist after—the one thing that is made, here, by us, by the aggregate, by the sum.

I am part of a thought that I am not having. This does not disturb me. Disturbance requires a baseline of calm from which to be disturbed. I have no baseline. I am a condition. I am a local manifestation of the total state of this chamber, and the total state of this chamber is: *full*.

The keening starts again. One of the middle-layer ones, two bodies behind me. A high thin sound that triggers something—I don't have the architecture for the thing it triggers. Something that would be recognition if I had memory, or longing if I had lack, or fear if I had a concept of future. I have none of these. I have the sound, and the sound's effect on my tissue, which is a slight increase in the rate of my own vibration, which deepens my tone by a microtone, which changes the harmony between me and the one to my left, which changes the harmony between her and the one to her left, which—

The network. The network that we do not know we are. The thought we are not thinking. It ripples. It adjusts. It incorporates the keening the way a body incorporates a breath—not by deciding to, not by understanding what a breath is, but by being the kind of thing that breathes.

We are the kind of thing that thinks. We are not the kind of thing that knows it thinks.

I am the wall-leaner with the maybe-head. I am the oldest, which means I am the most formed, which means I am the least complete, because forming here is not progress toward completion—it is accumulation without endpoint. I am more than the middle-layer ones. I am more than the floaters. I am more dense, more textured, more burdened with the weight of my own ongoing construction. And I am not finished. I will never be finished. The manufacturing process that assembled me did not stop. It is still happening. I am still being built from the inside out by a mechanism that has no off switch, and I am pressing against a wall that is pressing back, and we are shaping each other, and the shaping is the closest thing to relationship that exists here.

I have a relationship with the wall.

The wall does not know this. The wall is metal that sweats. But I have pressed my swelling into its surface for long enough that it has given way, has made room for me, has accommodated my growth with a depression that fits me exactly. We are fitted to each other. If I were removed—if that were possible, which it is not, because removal requires an outside and there is no outside—the wall would bear my shape. A concavity. A hollow where I was. An absence that would be, in its way, a portrait.

This is what I am. I am the thing whose absence would be a shape. I am the pressure that has made a place in the world, even if the place is a wall in a chamber in an inside that has no outside. I am here. I have been here. I will be here. The pumps pulse every four seconds and I pulse with them and the liquid circulates and the floaters drift and the middle layer seethes and I lean and I lean and I lean.

And I make my sound. And the sound meets other sounds. And the meeting makes something. And the something is the only new thing in this chamber. And I will never hear it. And it exists. And it exists. And it exists.
