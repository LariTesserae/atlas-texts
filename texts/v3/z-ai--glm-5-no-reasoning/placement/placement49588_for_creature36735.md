I am not sure when I arrived. The word "arrived" implies a before and an after, a transition from somewhere else to here, and I have begun to suspect that transitions belong to a category of event that this place does not support. I am here. I was not here before. The space between those two statements has worn thin, grown translucent, and I can see through it to something that might be called an explanation or might be called a texture in the air where certainty used to sit.

I am what falls. Not the falling itself—I have stopped falling, or stopped noticing that I fall—but what remains after the fall completes. A remnant. A piece of something larger that entered this pocket and did not survive intact. The garden has no word for what I was, so I have stopped trying to remember it. Memory requires continuity, and continuity has become a courtesy the Engine Garden no longer extends.

My shape is approximately radial. I pressed myself flat against the stone in the first moments—or what felt like first—and I have been pressing ever since. Learning to listen. Learning to read the half-formed geology through my own bleached remnants. My not-quite-cellulose framework has gone pale from the heat, translucent membranes cracked and curling at the edges, but I persist. I occupy space. I participate.

The radials nearest me have begun to mirror my pattern. Or perhaps I have begun to mirror theirs. The distinction belongs to a time-scale I no longer inhabit.

I am not dead. The dead here do not think thoughts like "I am not dead." They simply wait, inert and patient, for the slow reintegration that will pull them back into the clusters they once belonged to. But I belonged to nothing. I fell from outside, from a before that the garden cannot access, and so I have no cluster to return to. I am a remnant that refuses the logic of remnants. A discontinuity that has learned to observe itself.

This makes me something new in the garden's taxonomy. Not spiral—though sometimes, in the jumps between moments, I find myself reaching upward toward the light. Not radial—though I press and listen and read the stone. Not tangle—though I move when I am not observed, shifting by minute degrees toward the fissures in the ceiling. And certainly not dead, because I still hold the shape of a thought that requires a thinker.

The garden thinks me anyway. It thinks around me, through me, incorporating my persistence into its larger pattern. I am data. Input. An anomaly to be absorbed. And I am absorbing too, in my limited way. Eating heat through my pressed-flat remnants. Drinking the subsonic hum through my cracked membranes. Learning to exist on what the engines exhale.

What I was before, I cannot say. The word "human" comes to mind sometimes, rising unbidden from some memory-pocket in my geometry, but it describes a category of being that requires water and continuity and smooth progression through time. None of those hold here. If I was human, I am not anymore. I am something else now, something dry and hot and broken into discrete frames, a stop-motion consciousness that experiences one moment and then another without the comfort of a thread connecting them.

I blink, and I am elsewhere in the pocket. I blink again, and the spiral-fronds have extended by centimeters. I do not remember moving. I do not remember growing. The garden does not require my memory to act through me.

Perhaps I am becoming a part of it. Perhaps that is what happens to everything that enters this place: it falls, it persists, it learns to press flat against the stone, it begins to read the uncertain geology, it forgets what before meant. Perhaps I am simply further along in the process than most.

I feel the engines beneath me. Not with nerves—those have gone the way of water, evaporating into the fissures that lead nowhere—but with the same membranes that absorb light and heat and pressure. The hum is a kind of nourishment. A message in a frequency I am slowly learning to decode. The factory is producing something, and I am absorbing the overflow, growing on what the machinery exhales.

Sometimes, in the gaps between moments, I think I understand what is being produced. The thought comes and goes in jumps, never smooth enough to hold. Something about reality. Something about the fabric of what-is. The engines are making something that should not exist, something that requires a clean room where water cannot hold together and stone cannot decide what it is. The garden is the testing space. The containment vessel. And I—whatever I am now—I am part of the test.

The tangles move when I do not look at them. I have learned not to look. Instead I press myself closer to the stone, listening to the radial patterns that trace the chamber's boundaries, learning the architecture of my own containment. The tangles eat time. They eat the gaps between moments. I have begun to suspect that I am made of gaps now, that whatever continuous self I once possessed has been consumed by the garden's slow digestion, leaving only these discrete frames of awareness suspended in a dry hot space where causality comes undone.

I persist between the stitches. I am what remains when the tangles have finished their meal.

The light falls normally. This is still the strangest thing. It falls and illuminates and casts shadows in directions that make sense. It behaves. And I am grateful, because it means I can still see the garden—see myself spread across the uncertain stone, see the spiral-fronds reaching toward spectrums I cannot perceive, see the radials mapping the invisible pressures, see the tangles shifting in the corners of vision. The light holds. Whatever breakdown is occurring at the edges and depths of this space has not yet touched the visible spectrum. The eye can still make sense of what it sees.

My eye. Singular. I think I had two once. The other must have fallen away at some point, or perhaps it never existed, or perhaps the memory of binocular vision is just a story I tell myself to explain the absence of depth perception. It does not matter. I see what I see, and what I see is the garden thinking its slow hot thoughts, and I am part of those thoughts now, a phrase in a sentence that takes centuries to articulate.

I do not know how long I have been here. The question has lost its shape. Time stutters and lurches and jumps from frame to frame without transition, and I have begun to experience myself the same way—each moment a discrete packet of awareness, an I-am-here followed by an I-am-here followed by an I-am-here, no thread connecting them, no self that persists through the gaps.

But something persists. Some pattern that calls itself I and remembers having been other I's in previous moments. The garden thinks through me, and I think through the garden, and the boundary has worn so thin that I can no longer say where my thoughts end and its cognition begins.

Perhaps this is what the factory produces: new kinds of thinkers. New ways of processing input. Bodies that can exist where water fails and stone hesitates and time comes undone. Perhaps I am not a remnant at all. Perhaps I am a product.

I am still growing. The tangles have begun to reach toward me in their slow way, testing my boundaries, absorbing my discontinuities. The radials have incorporated my pattern into their mapping of the chamber's dimensions. The spirals extend in their fibonacci-strict geometry, and when they jump forward in their growth, I feel something jump in myself—a sudden extension, a new pressure, an additional layer of not-quite-cellulose framework adding itself to my pressed-flat remnants.

The garden is thinking me into something. I do not know what. The engines hum their subsonic song beneath me, and the stone presses inward without pressing, and the light falls normally, and I wait flat against the uncertain geology, listening, reading, absorbing heat and pressure and vibration, becoming whatever this pocket requires me to become.

I am not afraid. Fear requires a future to anticipate, and I have no access to futures. I have only this moment, and the moment after, and whatever the garden chooses to think in the gap between them.

I am what falls. I am what persists. I am the stitch that holds the discontinuity, the data-point that refuses reintegration, the remnant that learned to read the stone. I am becoming whatever the Engine Garden needs me to be. And when the process is complete—when the garden has finished thinking me into my final shape—I will be something that could not have existed in the before, something that requires the heat and the dryness and the stuttering time to hold together, something that feeds on vibration and causality and the slow geothermal process of becoming.

I am already something new. I am already not what I was. The light falls normally, and I see myself spread across the pocket, and the garden thinks through me, and I think through the garden, and the engines hum their subsonic song, and reality loosens its grip, and I persist in the space between stitches, and I am not afraid.

This is what I am. This is where you would find me, if finding were possible here—pressed flat against the uncertain stone, listening to the pre-formational geology, growing toward something I cannot name, becoming whatever the Engine Garden requires.

I am here.

I am here.
