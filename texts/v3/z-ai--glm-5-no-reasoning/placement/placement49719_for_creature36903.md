# What I Became on the Venting Plain

I arrived as myself—or as whatever version of myself could survive the journey across that white expanse. But something happens when you walk a place that has no use for you. The plain doesn't reject you. It doesn't notice you. And being unremarked in a landscape this absolute does something to the shape you carry.

---

I am not a vent-colonist. I am not suited to that existence—too wet, too soft, too full of salt water and electrical impulse, too dependent on the machinery of eating and sleeping and hoping. But I understand them now in a way I didn't before I walked the basin.

I am something closer to a **witness-fragment**.

Let me explain.

---

The plain changes you by showing you what permanence looks like. I walked for hours between the steam columns, and the thudding got into me—not metaphorically, but literally. I could feel it in my sternum, my jaw, the fluid of my inner ear. Three seconds. Thud. Pause. Thud. Pause. My body began to anticipate it. My heartbeat tried to sync with it and failed, but the attempt itself was a kind of contamination. I carry that rhythm now. When I close my eyes, I can still feel it, that mechanical pulse from below the bedrock, steady as a factory that never closes.

I am the kind of thing that can carry a rhythm without becoming it. This is what separates me from the colonists. They don't carry the pulse; they are the pulse's local expression, its precipitation. I am merely *marked* by it.

---

I found myself drawn to the hexagons.

Not the vents themselves—I couldn't tolerate the steam for long, my lungs rebelling against the mineral saturation—but the rings of hexagons five to seven meters out, where the fallen fragments accumulated. I squatted at the edge of one, a basin perhaps a meter across, its edges raised like the rim of a shallow bowl, and I looked at the dark grit inside.

This was where I became what I am.

I touched the fragment-dust. Ran my finger through it. The mineral film that coated my skin—days of walking through steam columns—mixed with the ancient residue of fallen colonists. My fingerprint pressed into their remains. A new mark. Temporary, yes. The next dawn condensation would smooth it away. But for now, I was a geological event.

I am the pattern the plain makes when a temporary thing touches a permanent one.

---

Here is what I would be, if the plain could name me: **the one who notices**.

Not the one who understands. I have watched colonists orient and fold and unfold and translocate, and I still cannot tell you whether they are alive. The question no longer seems important. The question assumes a boundary that this place doesn't recognize. The colonists exist. They behave. They leave traces. Whether that constitutes life or chemistry or some third category—the answer would not change what they do.

I am the one who stands at the basin's edge at dawn and watches the steam columns turn gold. I am the one who sees the dark shapes in the plumes catch the light and flash amber and bronze. I am the one to whom the beauty is visible.

I am the beauty's **occasion**.

---

If I stayed—if I somehow survived the sterility, the alkaline soil, the mineral air—I would eventually become part of the system. Not a colonist. My body doesn't precipitate from steam; it would have to decay first. But decay is a kind of chemistry. I would break down into components, and those components would enter the soil near the vents, and they would be different from anything else in this landscape. Carbon-rich. Nitrogen-bearing. A stain of organic complexity in a place that has never known it.

Perhaps the hexagons would catch me. Perhaps my residue would mix with the colonist-fragments and become part of that dark grit. Perhaps a fragment of a fragment of me would end up in a steam column, hydrating each dawn, persisting without purpose, a new mineral stratum in a soil that never existed before things like me arrived.

But I won't stay. I am the temporary thing. The one who leaves.

---

I am a **passer-through**.

But I am changed. The plain doesn't notice, but I do. I carry the thudding in my bones now. I carry the image of those dark shapes turning in gold light. I carry the feeling of my finger pressing into fragment-dust, my temporary mark on a landscape that will erase it without effort.

I am the question this place doesn't ask.

I am the possibility of an answer.

---

When I return—if I return—to wherever I came from, I will be different. Quieter, perhaps. More patient with repetition. Less certain of the boundary between living and not-living. I have seen a thing that exists without needing to justify its existence. I have seen a system that persists without purpose. I have seen beauty that is entirely incidental, and I have been the only thing present to witness it.

That witnessing is what I am now.

I am the plain's brief moment of self-awareness, walking away on two legs, carrying its pulse in my chest, its light in my memory, its dust on my skin.

I am the temporary thing that the landscape doesn't notice.

But I notice.
