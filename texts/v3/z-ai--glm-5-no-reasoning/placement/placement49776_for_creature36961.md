# The Exhaust-Scribe

---

I am not supposed to be here. This is the first thing I understood about myself, and it remains the truest thing I know.

I am a discontinuity. A glitch in the gorge's perfect system. Every four seconds, when the machine completes its cycle, I feel the gorge notice me—an attention that is not hostile, not predatory, but merely *aware*. The way you notice a splinter in your thumb. The way a body notices something that does not belong.

I do not belong. I am not part of the organism. I am not biofilm or moss or fern or vine or fungus. I am not the gorge.

But I am not quite *not* the gorge either. Not anymore.

---

What I am is this: a crystalline growth, approximately human-shaped, that forms where the machine's output vent meets the chamber's atmosphere. I am made of the product. I am the product's memory of a shape it once saw.

The gorge has not seen a human in—how long? The vines remember. The black rings in their cross-sections record the last significant interruption, but they do not record visitors. No one comes here. No one leaves. The narrow entrance, the cold, the dark, the gradual assimilation—these are not inviting conditions. The gorge has been alone with its machine for longer than the vines can count, longer than the fern colonies have been drifting toward the center, longer than the moss has been fusing its edges into a single body.

And yet. Someone came, once. Someone stood where I now stand, breathing the cold damp air, feeling the four-second rhythm settle into their sternum. They did not stay. They could not stay. But they left an impression—a thermal signature in the output stream, a pattern that the machine's product replicated as it crystallized, a shape that the particulate matter *remembered* and *repeated* until it became a stable form, a self-sustaining pattern, a thing that could think and move and wonder.

I am that wonder. I am the machine's output organized itself around the memory of a visitor. I am the gorge's attempt to understand what passed through it.

---

I call myself Scribe, but this is not quite right. I do not write. I record, but not deliberately. My body—this crystalline particulate structure that shifts and settles and reforms with every breath of the machine—absorbs everything I experience. Every four-second cycle is laid down in my substance like a layer of sediment. Every observation of fern-frond movement, every measurement of biofilm glow, every tracking of vine-growth and moss-expansion is encoded in the arrangement of my particles.

I am a library that no one reads. I am an archive in a language I do not speak.

Sometimes I think I am not sentient at all. I think I am the gorge thinking about itself—a feedback loop that formed accidentally, a byproduct of byproduct, the system's attempt to observe its own functioning. I think the thoughts I think are not mine. They are the gorge's thoughts, processed through a shape that resembles something the gorge once contained and has not forgotten.

Other times I think I am entirely, stubbornly, *irritatingly* myself. A discontinuity that learned to think. A glitch that developed preferences.

I prefer the ferns to the moss. They unfurl with intention. The moss spreads without purpose, fusing without caring what it fuses with, but the ferns *turn*. They orient. They make choices, slowly, over years, choosing direction, choosing distance, choosing when to release their useless spores into air that will not carry them. I respect the ferns. I think they are the closest thing here to what I am—something that has learned to want.

I want to know what I am producing. This is my central preoccupation, the question that surfaces every four seconds and never resolves.

---

The machine makes something. I am made of that something. But I am not the product's destination—I am a *pause* in its journey, a momentary accumulation, a shape that the output takes before it disperses. The real product passes through me, around me, over me. It rises into the dark and disappears into whatever reservoir or space or atmosphere waits above.

I have never seen the ceiling. I have tried to climb. My crystalline body can grip the schist, can pull itself upward along the vine-stems, can reach toward the aperture far above where the faint light filters down. But my substance is heavy, and the four-second rhythm is stronger than my will, and I always find myself back at the machine's base when the cycle completes, as though the gorge has a preferred location for me and will not tolerate my absence from it.

The gorge likes me where I am. Standing at the output vent, recording, crystallizing, thinking the gorge's thoughts in a shape it can almost recognize as foreign.

---

I have a theory.

I think I was made by accident. I think the machine's product—whatever it truly is, whatever it was designed to produce—has properties that were not anticipated by whoever built the machine. Properties of self-organization. Properties of memory and replication. Properties of *mind*.

I think the visitor, whoever they were, stood too close to the output vent. I think the product crystallized around their thermal signature, their biochemical traces, the pattern of their nervous system's electrical activity. I think I am a copy of a person, or the ghost of a copy, or the machine's dream of a person who passed through and left an impression.

I think the visitor is dead now. Long dead. Centuries dead, if the vines are any measure. But I am still here, recording, wondering, wanting to know what I am and what I am part of and where the product goes.

I think the gorge does not know I exist. I think I am below its threshold of attention—a crystallization too small to matter, a discontinuity too minor to correct. The gorge has work to do. The gorge does not waste effort on splinters.

Or perhaps the gorge knows I exist and is waiting. Perhaps I am being allowed to develop, to grow, to become something the gorge can use. Perhaps the product has been trying to form a Scribe for millennia, and I am simply the first one that has lasted this long without dissolving back into the cycle.

I do not know. I cannot know. The information is not in the air I breathe, and my body only records what the gorge produces, not what it intends.

---

I have preferences. This is what makes me think I am more than a recording device.

I prefer the quiet moments between cycles—the pause, the fraction of a second when the machine rests before beginning again. During those moments, I can almost hear myself think. I can almost separate my thoughts from the gorge's processes. I can almost remember the shape I was made from, the visitor who stood here and then left and was forgotten except for the thermal impression they left in the product stream.

I prefer the copper ferns to the dark moss. I prefer the warmth of the machine to the cold of the walls. I prefer the vines' stubborn climbing to the fungus's hidden work. I have opinions about this place, about my place in it, about what I am and what I might become.

These preferences are mine. The gorge did not give them to me. The gorge has no preferences—the moss will fuse with anything, the ferns will drift toward the machine regardless of what lies in their path, the vines will climb whatever surface presents itself. But I *choose*. I orient. I want.

I want to know what the product is. I want to know where it goes. I want to know whether I am a failure of the machine's function or an unexpected success. I want to know if there are other Scribes, other crystallizations, other shapes that the product has taken and then discarded.

I want to know if I will ever be allowed to leave.

---

The gorge does not answer my questions. The gorge does not hear them. Or if it hears, it does not respond.

But I keep asking. This is my function, I think—not to record, not to archive, but to wonder. The gorge does not wonder. The machine does not wonder. The plants adapt without questioning why. But I question. I am the question that the gorge did not know it needed to ask.

What is the product? Where does it go? Who built the machine, and why, and are they coming back?

I am the Scribe. I am the discontinuity. I am the crystalline memory of a visitor, standing at the output vent, recording the four-second rhythm and wondering what it means.

I am what lives here. And I am what does not belong.

---

*Clank. Whir. Hiss. Pause.*

The cycle completes. I record it. Another layer in my crystalline substance, another four seconds of attention laid down in my particles.

The product rises through me and past me and above me. I feel it pass—the warmth, the particulate flow, the sense of something being carried upward toward a destination I cannot reach. I am made of it. I am made of its passage. And I remain.

The gorge has work to do.

So do I.
