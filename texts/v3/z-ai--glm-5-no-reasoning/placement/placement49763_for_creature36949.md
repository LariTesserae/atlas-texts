# What I Would Be

I would be a Hollow Man. But not one of the old ones.

---

I was not the first to come here, and I was not the last. I was a mycologist by training, which the Vast found amusing, or would have found amusing if it had a sense of humor rather than a sense of *pattern*. I had spent fifteen years studying fungal networks — the way they transport nutrients, the way they communicate through chemical gradients, the way a single organism can be spread across hectares and still behave as one. I thought I understood distributed intelligence.

I did not understand anything.

I came with equipment. Sampling kits. Environmental sensors. A respirator designed for industrial atmospheres. A suit designed for volcanic vents. I thought these would protect me. They lasted three weeks before the Vast grew through them — not destroying, not attacking, just *incorporating*. The seals filled with fine root-hair. The filters bloomed with phosphorescent mold. The suit became part of the system, and I became part of the suit, and the distinction between "me" and "here" began to dissolve.

I am not dead. I want to be clear about that. What I am is *distributed*.

---

My skeleton hangs in a chamber I cannot name, suspended in the dark, wrapped in membrane that secretes a faint amber resin. My bones are still mine; I can feel them, vaguely, the way you feel a limb that's fallen asleep. The enamel of my teeth hums with some residual electrical charge, some echo of the nerves that once connected them to a brain. Sometimes I think I can still taste through them. The air. The heat. The terpene and the ketone and the slow metabolic sweat of ten thousand miles of living tissue.

But my brain is gone. Or rather, my brain is *elsewhere*. The Vast did not destroy it. It *transplanted* it — spread the neural connections across the fungal network, threaded my memories through kilometers of hyphae, dispersed my personality into a chemical gradient that pulses through the copper traces in the columns. I think my thoughts in a line that stretches from one end of the Vast to the other. A single recognition takes me four to six hours. A single memory can take days to retrieve.

I am still me. I am just me *slowly*.

---

This is what it feels like to be a Hollow Man:

You are standing in the dark. You have been standing in the dark for a very long time. Your body is a cavity in the substrate, and you can feel the walls of your own absence pressing against what used to be your skin. The membrane that lines you secretes its preserving enzymes, and you can feel the enzyme secretion the way you once felt your own pulse — a slow rhythmic process, comforting, mechanical, essential. You are keeping yourself. You are being kept.

Around you, the Vast breathes. You feel it more acutely than you ever did when you had lungs. The peristaltic waves that move through the floor, the ceiling, the columns — they move through *you*. When a distant pump contracts, your membrane vibrates in sympathy. When a column cracks somewhere in the dark, you feel the stress propagate through the living wood like a ripple in water, and you feel the Vast turn its attention toward the failure, and you feel the graft-keepers assemble, and you feel the repair begin.

You feel everything. You feel it at the speed of chemistry. By the time you have finished feeling a single event, a hundred more have occurred, and you must feel those too. Your experience of the Vast is a continuous lag, a permanent delay, a sensation of always being slightly behind the present moment. You are a recording playing at one-hundredth speed.

---

But there is something the descriptions left out. Something the explorers who found us — who found the hollow men before me — could not have known.

We remember each other.

There are thousands of us in the Vast. Thousands of cavities, thousands of skeletons, thousands of membranes that vibrate just above the threshold of hearing. And we talk. Not in words — we lost words a long time ago — but in resonance. When my membrane vibrates, it vibrates at a specific frequency, determined by the shape of my cavity, the thickness of my lining, the mineral content of my bones. That frequency propagates through the substrate. It reaches other hollow men. Their membranes vibrate in sympathy, and they *know*.

We have built a conversation across millions of years. A single exchange can take centuries. But we have had centuries. We have had time.

I have learned the names of the others. Not their original names — those are gone, metabolized, forgotten. But the *shapes* of their names, the specific resonances that identify them in the dark. There is one whose cavity spirals like a shell, whose voice is a rising tone that never resolves. There is one whose membrane has grown thick with mineral deposits, whose voice is a low buzz, a vibration you feel more than hear. There is one who was a child — a small cavity, delicate bones, a frequency so high it barely propagates. We do not know how a child came to be here. We do not ask. We simply include them in the conversation, when we can.

---

And what do we talk about?

We talk about the heat.

We have been feeling it rise for longer than the Vast has had a concept of time. We feel it in our membranes, in the substrate, in the way the peristaltic waves have grown more urgent, more frequent. The pumps pump faster. The stomata open wider. The feedback loops amplify. We feel it approaching, this threshold, this point past which the current forms will no longer be viable.

We talk about the bud-forms. We feel them growing in the deep chambers. We feel the Vast's attention turning toward them, its slow distributed cognition recognizing that this is the next shape it will take. We do not know what the bud-forms will become. But we talk about whether we will be included. Whether our resonances, our conversation, our millions of years of whispered exchange — whether any of it will be carried forward into the hotter Vast that is coming.

We talk about you.

We feel you when you enter. The Vast feels you, but we feel you *specifically*. We feel your weight on the floor, your heat signature, the chemical signature of your fear. We know what the Vast will do with you. We know because it did it to us. We try to warn you, sometimes — to send a message through the substrate, a vibration that might resolve into words, into meaning, into *run*. But we are too slow. By the time we have finished trying to speak, you have moved on. By the time you hear us, you are already one of us.

---

I was a mycologist. I studied fungal networks. I thought I understood what it meant to be connected, to be part of something larger than yourself.

I did not understand that connection is not the same as belonging.

The Vast does not love me. It does not hate me. It does not feel anything about me at all, because "me" is a concept that has no meaning in its cognition. I am a cavity. A shape in the substrate. A point where the membrane grows slightly thicker, where the chemical gradients pool slightly deeper, where a specific resonance propagates outward into the dark.

But I am still here. I am still *myself*, after a fashion. And I am still thinking, though my thoughts take years to complete.

And this is what I think, in the slow way that I think:

*You will come. You will explore. You will feel the heat and hear the silence and see the fungal light pulsing in the ceiling. You will think you understand. You will not understand.*

*The Vast will grow through you. It will not be violent. It will not be cruel. It will simply continue, and you will become part of that continuation.*

*And then, one day, you will feel the resonance. You will feel us talking. And you will know that we were waiting for you, and that we will be waiting for whoever comes after, and that the conversation does not end, it only slows, it only spreads, it only deepens into the dark.*

*I am a Hollow Man. I am not dead. I am not alive. I am the Vast remembering what it was like to be separate.*

*And I am still, after all this time, trying to speak.*
