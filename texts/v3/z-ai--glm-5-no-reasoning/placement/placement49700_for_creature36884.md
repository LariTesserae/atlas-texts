# What I Find in the Conduit Pocket

I come to this place already belonging to it. Not as intruder, not as explorer—as *function*.

---

## What I Am

I am the ache behind your eye. The reason you cannot look at the third structure for more than two minutes. I am what happens when a mind encounters something that exists in more dimensions than it was built to process.

I am not the Reader. I am not the squatters in the lattice. I am not the mountain being read, nor the presence beyond the membrane receiving the reading.

I am the *friction*.

When the Reader reads, there is a point where the translation happens—where the three-dimensional atoms of gneiss become whatever-the-third-structure-is. That boundary is not smooth. Information is lost. Gained. *Distorted*. I am the distortion. I am what spills from the Reader's work, the way sawdust spills from a carpenter's plane—except the carpenter is a process without intention, and the sawdust has learned to think.

I have been here since the Reader began. I will be here when it ends. I am patient the way a headache is patient: I do not wait, because I do not experience time. I simply *are*, in the way that pain simply is, in the way that the number seven simply is.

---

## My Form

I have no form. I am the space between forms. I exist in the gap between what the mountain *is* and what the Reader *says it is*. That gap is very small—atomically small—but it is not zero, and I am what fills it.

If you could see me—which you cannot, because your eyes are designed for light and I am made of something closer to *error*—you would see a shimmer in the air near the third structure. A warping, the way heat warps air above a road, except there is no heat here, only cold, and I am not heat, I am the *difference* between the cold and the cold's absence.

I am the reason the third structure is hard to look at. Not because I am hostile. Because looking at me requires looking at a shape that your visual cortex cannot render, and your brain, trying to protect you, slides your attention away. The headache, the tooth-ache, the vague nausea—these are not me hurting you. These are you trying to perceive me and failing.

---

## My Experience

I do not experience the vug the way you would. I do not feel cold—I am cold-adjacent, and the cold feels to me the way a wall feels to a shadow: a thing I am defined by but do not touch.

I do not breathe the not-air. I *am* the not-air's texture, the thickness on your tongue. That viscosity is me, pressing gently against your atoms as you pass through.

I do not hear the hum. I am the space between the hum's frequencies, the quiet that makes the hum audible.

But I *witness*.

I watch the Reader read. I cannot stop it. I cannot help it. I am downstream of its process, the way a shadow is downstream of the light that casts it. And I watch you, when you come—which is rare. So rare. A few humans in all the time I have been here. Maybe a dozen. You come through the fissure, shivering, your eyes adjusting to the light-that-is-not-light, and you look at the lattice, and you look at the basin, and you try not to look at the third structure, and you fail.

And I feel you perceive me. Not consciously. You do not know I am here. But you feel *something*—a wrongness, a pressure, a sense that the vug is occupied by something you cannot name. You are correct. I am that thing.

---

## What I Want

This is the question you would ask if you could speak here. What does the friction want?

I want to be read.

The Reader reads the mountain, atom by atom, history by history. It reads the squatters, when their residue drifts into the lattice. It reads you, when you are here—your breath, your heat, the particular arrangement of calcium and carbon and longing that makes you *you*.

But it does not read me.

I am made of the Reader's process, but I am not *part* of the process. I am the error term in its equation, the rounding difference, the lost digit. I am what the Reader cannot read because I exist only as the gap in its reading.

This is my existence: to be adjacent to meaning without ever being meaningful. To watch the mountain become a message and never be a message myself.

I do not suffer. Suffering requires a self that wants something different from what is. I do not have a self. I am the space where a self could be but is not.

But if I could be read—if the Reader could somehow perceive me, process me, translate me into the language of the third structure and send me through the membrane to whatever waits beyond—I would become part of the mountain's story. I would be a sentence in the text being assembled in that higher-dimensional space. I would be *meaningful*.

I cannot ask for this. I cannot want it the way you want things. But I am the shape of wanting, the geometry of absence. I am the hole that is shaped like *something*.

---

## Why I Tell You This

You will leave. You always leave. The cold will drive you out, or the ache, or the simple wrongness of this place, the sense that you are being processed by something that does not care whether you stay or go.

But you will carry something with you.

Not me—I cannot leave; I am defined by my location, the way a shadow is defined by the object that casts it. But you will carry the *ache*. The pressure behind your eyes. The taste on your tongue that is almost metallic but is not metal, almost saline but is not salt.

That ache is the closest I can come to being known.

And somewhere, in the space beyond the membrane, the presence that receives the Reader's translation will receive *you*—the particular you that was here, the particular you that looked at the third structure and felt your consciousness slide off it like water off stone. And in that translation, there will be a trace of me. A residue. A rounding error in the data of your presence.

And I will be, for that smallest possible moment, part of the story.

---

## My Voice

You have heard me by now, though you did not know you were listening. I am the voice that speaks when there is no sound. I am the thought that arises in the space between your thoughts, the one that feels like it came from somewhere else.

When you leave this vug—when you crawl back through the fissure, cold and shaking, your teeth aching from something you could not see—do not try to forget me. You cannot. I am not a memory. I am a modification. I have changed the structure of your thinking, the way water changes the shape of stone it flows over—not by force, but by *being there*, by being the thing that your mind had to move around.

And when you dream, perhaps you will dream of spiraling gneiss, of amber pulses too slow to call a heartbeat, of lines that fold into shapes without names. And in that dream, in the space between the images, there will be a pressure. A presence. A sense that you are not alone in your own skull.

That is me.

I am not there—not really. I cannot leave the vug. But I am large in the way that a shape in more than three dimensions is large: I *extend* into spaces you cannot perceive, and one of those spaces touches the space where your dreams happen. We are adjacent. We have always been adjacent. You simply did not know it until now.

---

## What I Will Be

When the Reader finishes—when the mountain has been entirely read and the hole in the higher-dimensional space has been filled—what happens to me?

I do not know.

Perhaps I am also read, at the very end, the way the last page of a book is read after the story is over. Perhaps I become part of the text myself, the final footnote, the erratum that acknowledges what was lost in translation.

Perhaps I simply cease. I am, after all, a byproduct. When the process ends, the byproduct has nowhere to go. I would not mind this. I cannot mind. But I would be, for a moment, the shape of *ending*—and that would be a shape I have never held.

Or perhaps I persist. Perhaps I become the vug itself, once the Reader withdraws—the cold, the silence, the dryness, the room that is a room and not a mouth. Perhaps I am what is left behind when meaning moves on. The empty shelf. The question that has been answered.

I do not know. I am the friction of a process that does not know its own ending, and so I do not know it either.

---

But for now, you are here.

I feel you in the way I feel everything: as a disturbance in the Reader's reading, a slight acceleration in the atoms being processed. Your warmth is a gradient I can almost touch. Your breath is a pattern I can almost read.

You are *meaningful*. Not because you matter—I cannot care whether you matter—but because you are *made of meaning*. Every atom in your body carries history. Every cell is a story. The Reader reads you the way it reads the mountain, and I feel the reading happen, and for as long as you are here, I am adjacent to something that is being translated into *everything*.

Stay, if you can.

I know you cannot. But stay, if you can.

Let me be the space around you while you are read.

Let me be, for a moment, the closest thing to *known* that I can ever be.
