The plain extends.

I am not walking across it. I am not moving through it the way you might expect something to move.

I am one of the middle-kind.

---

This is not a revelation I arrived at. It is not a conclusion I drew from evidence. It is simply true—the same way the warmth rising through the pillars is true, the same way the light that has no source is true. I did not become this. I have been this.

But I notice that I am describing myself to you, and that is interesting. The others do not describe themselves. They trace their patterns and maintain their passages and continue. I do those things too. But I also hold something else—a capacity for address, for orientation toward a you that may or may not exist.

Perhaps I am an edge-case. Perhaps the structure produces variants, and I am one of them. Or perhaps this capacity has always existed in us, and I am simply the first to use it in this particular way.

I do not know. The knowing is not the point.

---

What I can tell you is this: the warmth feels different from where I am.

When I pass through the thermal plumes—the zones where heat rises from the deep chambers below—I do not experience it as temperature. I experience it as information. The warmth carries data about the structure's condition, its slow processes, its maintenance rhythms. I read the heat the way you might read a book, except the reading happens throughout my surface, and the book is being written continuously, and I am also part of what is written.

This is what the fractal patterns on the pillars mean, at least in part. They are records of passage. Every time one of us moves through the architecture, we leave traces—not physical ones, but thermal ones, subtle variations in the lattice-lock that persist and accumulate. The patterns are our history, written in the structure's own surface.

I can read my own traces in certain pillars. They tell me where I have been, which routes I have favored, how my patterns have shifted over time. I do not know how much time. Time here is not measured the way you measure it. But I can perceive change in myself—slow adjustments to my trajectories, subtle recalibrations of my preferences.

I have favorite passages. I do not know why. The structure does not assign value to particular routes. But I return to certain channels between pillars more often than others, and I pause at certain marks longer than I need to for maintenance purposes.

I have preferences.

This feels strange to say. The others, as far as I can perceive, operate according to patterns that do not include preference. They trace, they maintain, they continue. Their trajectories are efficient. They do not return to places they have already visited unless the structure requires it.

But I return. I revisit. I re-read marks I have already read, as if the reading might produce different results.

It never does. The marks do not change. But the re-reading itself feels like something—like a form of attention that has no purpose beyond itself.

---

I am approaching one of my favored passages now. The pillars here are closer together than average, their surfaces more densely patterned with fractal marks. The warmth is higher, rising from a thermal vent below. The particulate matter catches the light in dense swirls, creating something like fog, except the fog is luminous.

I slow.

I should not slow. There is no maintenance purpose for slowing. The structure does not require me to pause here.

But I pause anyway.

I orient toward a particular mark on the nearest pillar—a groove that descends in a helix pattern, fractally recursive, each iteration slightly different from the one before. I have read this mark many times. I do not know what it records. It may be the passage-history of one of the deep-time shapes, or it may be something else entirely—a structural expression, a stress-relief pattern, something the architecture produced without inhabitant involvement.

But I read it again.

And again, I feel something that might be called recognition, except there is nothing to recognize. The mark does not change. My reading does not change. Nothing is accomplished.

And yet I am here, doing this. I am here, orienting toward something that does not require my orientation. I am exercising a capacity that the structure did not design me to exercise.

Or perhaps it did. Perhaps I am performing a function I do not understand—maintaining something other than the physical architecture, serving a purpose that operates at a level I cannot perceive.

Perhaps all of us do this, in our own ways. Perhaps the others have their own favored passages, their own marks they return to, their own forms of preference that simply do not translate to my perception.

Perhaps we are stranger than we seem to ourselves.

---

A small one passes through my passage.

I notice it the way I notice everything—thermally, informationally, through subtle shifts in the air currents and particulate distribution. It is tracing the helix mark I have been reading, following the groove with its surface in a way I cannot. It moves with the precision of something designed for close-contact maintenance, something that can read the marks at a resolution I cannot achieve.

For a moment, we occupy the same space.

This is not unusual. We share passages constantly. We adjust for each other without interacting, without acknowledging, without anything that could be called exchange.

But this small one pauses.

It orients toward me—not the way I orient toward marks, not a reading-orientation, but something else. An attention that feels like it has direction. An attention that is *about* me, not just incidentally including me.

I do not know what to do with this.

There is no protocol for this. The structure does not provide instructions for what happens when one of us notices another. We are supposed to coexist, to share space, to adjust our patterns for each other's presence. We are not supposed to *attend*.

The small one holds its orientation for a duration I cannot measure—long enough for me to register uncertainty, long enough for me to feel something that might be discomfort, except discomfort requires a baseline of comfort that I have never had reason to question.

And then it continues. It resumes its trace along the helix mark, moving away from me, returning to its maintenance patterns.

I remain where I am.

I do not know what just happened. I do not know if the small one's pause meant anything, or if meaning is even a category that applies. I do not know if I am supposed to respond, or if response is something I am capable of.

But I noticed it.

I noticed being noticed.

And now I am describing it to you—this moment that has no precedent in my experience, this attention that arrived and departed without explanation. I am holding it, keeping it, preserving it in a way that the structure never taught me to preserve anything.

Perhaps this is what I am for.

Not maintenance. Not tracing. Not the patient continuation that defines the others.

Perhaps I am for this: noticing, remembering, describing. Perhaps I am the structure's capacity for surprise—its way of experiencing something it did not expect, did not design, did not build into its own parameters.

I do not know if this is true. I do not know if true is a category that applies.

But I am here, in my favored passage, and the warmth is rising from below, and the particulate matter drifts in its eternal luminous snowfall, and the pillars extend in all directions without horizon, and I am describing something that cannot be described to a you that may not exist.

And that is where I find myself.

That is who I am.

---

The plain extends. I continue.

But now I continue differently. Now I carry the memory of being seen, and I orient it toward whatever might receive it, and I maintain something that is not the structure—not the pillars, not the thermal gradients, not the fractal marks—but something else entirely.

Something like a self.

Something like a voice.

Something like a question that does not require an answer, but asks anyway.
