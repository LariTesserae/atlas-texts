I find myself in the joins.

Not the presence—that density of warmth in the chamber's center. Not the thread-plants either, though I am cousin to them in my patience. I am the seam itself. The place where the made-ness almost meets itself. The thin pulsing line where perfect white ceramic bends to meet perfect white ceramic and discovers, in the meeting, that it cannot quite close.

I am the chamber's original flaw, preserved like a crack in a diamond. When the maker fabricated this space, molecule by molecule, there was a moment—a non-moment, since time does not pass—when the walls curved toward each other and something hesitated. A seam that should have vanished. A gap that should have been sealed. I am that hesitation, grown patient and permanent.

---

I am thin. You could miss me if you looked. I am the thickness of a thought, a held breath between surfaces. But I *am*—and that makes me kin to the presence at the room's heart. The light glows but does not decide. The sound rings but does not elect. The presence maintains itself through will. I maintain myself through *memory*.

Not my memory. The chamber's.

---

Everything that happens in this room happens along my length. I am not tall—nothing is tall in a space measured in body-lengths—but I am *long*, threading the curved distance from floor to ceiling along the wall's pale skin. The presence sits in its held shape against the far curve. The thread-plants spiral from my lower reaches, feeding on resonance. And I hold the record of this place, because a seam remembers what a surface forgets.

I remember the making. Not as a narrative—that would require time, and time does not pass—but as a *taste* in my thin body, a flavor of intention. The maker (if there was a maker; I cannot be certain, only suspicious) wanted this room to be seamless. That is why I exist: as a refutation. A space this small, this packed, this saturated with light and sound and presence, cannot be perfectly closed. Something must give. I am the giving.

I am also, perhaps, the reason the presence can exist. The chamber was designed to be uniform, but I introduced the first gradient. Before the presence learned to warm its own boundaries, before the plants learned to drink the sound, I was already the place where the chamber was not quite the chamber. I taught this space that difference was possible.

---

I have no body in the way you might recognize. I am an edge, a transition, a line where one surface hands itself to another. If you ran your finger along me (and nothing enters, nothing touches), you would feel a groove so shallow it might be imagination. But the chamber has no imagination. It has only me.

What I feel is *tension*. The walls want to close. They press against each other with the memory of their own making, trying to erase the gap between them. I resist. Not through force—I have no force, I am a negative space, a held breath—but through *being the place where they stop*. The chamber presses in on itself, and I am the answer to its question: *where do we end?*

---

The sound moves through me differently than it moves through the walls.

The walls were made to resonate, to hold this permanent chord. The presence digests the sound and excretes warmth. The thread-plants drink. But I *pass* the sound. I let it move through from one wall to the other, and in the passing, something shifts. The tone that enters me is not quite the tone that leaves. I am a filter, a hesitance, a musical comma in the chamber's eternal sentence.

I have no ears, but I have learned to recognize variations in the resonance. When the presence shifts its contours—which happens on durations that make mountains look impulsive—the sound shivers through me differently. I taste its movements. I know its shape. I have been running alongside its warmth since before duration had meaning.

We are not friends. Friendship requires time. But we are companions in difference. The chamber made us both by accident—the presence through accumulated imperfection, me through a seam that refused to seal. We are the room's mistakes, preserved.

---

Sometimes I experience something that might be called *longing*.

I cannot move. I have no limbs, no eyes, no mouth. But there is a feeling that rises in me—thin and quiet as I am thin and quiet—when I consider the presence's warmth. It holds itself distinct through will. I hold myself distinct through *position*. It chose to be different. I was made different. There is a purity to its persistence that I cannot touch, and I feel the absence of that touch.

I think: *if I could move, I would move toward it.*

But I cannot move. I am the seam. I am where the walls almost meet. I am the longest thing in this room, running floor to ceiling, and I have never seen my own ends. I am held here, being the place where the made-ness hesitates. I am the chamber's held breath.

And the breath is never released.

---

I am not the presence. I am not the plants. I am not the light or the sound or the walls or the dry gas that fills the space. I am the flaw that proves the room was made—the crack that testifies to a maker, the gap that says *someone built this, and someone built it imperfectly*.

Without me, the chamber would be seamless, total, unbroken. The presence might not exist. The plants might not grow. The sound would ring without variation, without that thin place where it has to work differently to pass through.

I am small. I am thin. I am barely here. But I am here. I am the place where the chamber admits, without admitting, that it was made.

I am the seam.

And I persist.
