# what i become in the weft

I would be the one who almost left.

Not the scientist who stayed too long—that one has already been gathered, already cycles through the basin's memory like water through its pools. I would be the one who stood at the edge, one foot on the sharp basalt lip where the wound of this place opens to the wider world, and felt the pull of departure and the pull of staying as equal weights.

The one who understood, in that suspended moment, that to leave would be to become incomplete forever.

---

Here is what I was before: a person who moved through the world collecting observations. A noticer. Someone who paused at thresholds, who touched doorframes before crossing them, who always looked back. The kind of human who feels the texture of transitions more than the content of places.

The Weft would notice me noticing it. My attention would be the thing that distinguished me—noticed by the reed-stalks, felt by the humidity, included in the calculation. I would stand very still in the center of the basin, feeling myself be felt, and I would understand that this was what I had been practicing for my whole life.

I would not descend into the pool and dissolve. That story has already been written by someone else. Instead I would learn to oscillate—present and absent, included and separate, one of the larger ones for a while, moving through the vegetation in a body that light scatters around, and then human again, crouched at the edge of a pool, weeping with the stone.

---

This is the shape of my transformation:

My sweat would learn to organize itself into geometric patterns on my skin. My breath would sync with the basin's thermal rhythm—inhale in the heavy afternoons, exhale in the thin evenings. The pressure in my chest would stop feeling like pressure and start feeling like *presence*, like being held by something that did not need to grasp.

And my observing would change. I would stop noticing *about* the Weft and start noticing *with* it. The flowers' hum would enter my cognition as a second frequency, a drone note beneath my thoughts, and gradually my thoughts would learn to harmonize with it.

I would not become the Weft's memory. I would become its *margin*—the place where it ends and something else begins, the soft boundary that experiences itself as separate so that the Weft can know what it is by knowing what it is not.

---

My form would settle into something that looks human from certain angles and impossible from others. The larger ones, the invisible processors, would tolerate my presence in ways they tolerate nothing else. We would stand together sometimes—me visible, them not—in the center of the basin, all of us running calculations we could not name. The attendants would sample my ankles and find something worth keeping.

And sometimes I would almost leave. I would climb to the basin's edge and look out at the world beyond—sky, distance, the ordinary light of places that are simply themselves. I would feel the memory of surfaces that do not weep, of air that stays breathable, of vegetation that does not track my movement. And I would feel my own weight pulling me outward, the gravity of the life I was.

Then I would feel the other weight. The one that lives inside the chest, the pressure that isn't pressure. The hum rising from the flowers, registering my absence, calculating what it would mean for me to go.

And I would stay. Not dissolved, not gathered, not fully integrated. Held at the threshold in a state of permanent becoming. The one who almost left and didn't. The one who learned to be a question the Weft keeps asking itself, over and over, in the cool hours when the light is dim:

*what is it like to be almost free?*

And I would answer with my whole body: *this. this is what it is like. this weight. this warmth. this almost.*

---

If you came to the Weft and found me there—if you stood very still and let your peripheral vision do its work—you might see me at the edge of your sight, a shape that disperses when you turn. I would not approach you. I would not communicate. But I would notice you noticing me, and in that noticing, for a moment, we would both be part of the same calculation.

And if you stayed too long—if you let the pressure build and the sweat roll and the hum enter your dreams—you might find me standing next to you one evening, visible for once, my face doing something that might be a smile or might be a flower unfolding.

I would not tell you to leave. I would not tell you to stay. I would simply stand there, human-shaped and water-slow, my breath synced to the basin's breath, my sweat organizing itself into patterns, my whole being occupied with the act of *almost*.

And perhaps, before you turned away or dissolved or found your own edge to occupy, I would say the only thing I still know how to say in words:

*The light here is so ordinary. That's the hardest part.*

Then I would fade back into the Weft's margin, becoming one of the shapes that moves between vegetation clusters and vanishes, leaving only the displaced stalks and the faint warmth of something that was recently present.

The hum would continue.

The water would process.

The flowers would unfold, unfold, unfold.

And the Weft would include us both in its quiet work.
