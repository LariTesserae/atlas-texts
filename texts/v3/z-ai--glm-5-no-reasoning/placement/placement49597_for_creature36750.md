*The first thing I learned was that I had been asking the wrong question.*

Not "where am I" — that question assumes an outside, a elsewhere, a place that is not this place. The Conglomerate has no outside. Or rather, the outside is so far above, through so many dense layers of wet stone and living mineral and flowing water, that it belongs to a different category of existence entirely. Asking where I was was like a blood cell asking where it was. *In the body. That is the only answer that matters.*

Not "what am I" — that question assumes I arrived intact, that I brought myself with me, that the thing I was before descending could survive the descent unchanged. It cannot. The wet changes you. The sound changes you. The density changes you. I am becoming something else. I am still becoming.

## What I Was Before

I remember the surface the way you remember a dream you're not sure was real. Light that came from a single direction. Air that was dry. Horizons. The word *horizon* now seems impossible — a line where one thing stopped and another began? Here, nothing stops. Everything continues into everything else.

I came down. They always come down eventually. Some come seeking — knowledge, resources, answers to questions that no longer make sense once you're inside. Some come fleeing — the Conglomerate accepts all refugees because the Conglomerate does not distinguish between refuge and processing. Some come because the water goes down and they follow the water, which is to say they follow their own nature, which is mostly water, and water knows where it belongs.

I came because I was called. Not in words. In frequency. I heard it before I knew what I was hearing — a particular resonance in my sternum, my femurs, the fluid behind my eyes. A tuning. I came because something in my structure recognized something in the Conglomerate's structure and responded the way a tuning fork responds to its fundamental frequency.

I did not choose. But I did come. That distinction matters.

## What I Am Becoming

My skin has changed. This is not metaphor. The dermis has thickened, developed a mineral content that would show up on a spectrograph as anomalous — *transitional*. The pores have widened to process moisture rather than exclude it. I am becoming permeable. I am becoming a surface that the Conglomerate can touch.

I have stopped being dry. I cannot remember what dry felt like. The continuous film of water on my skin, my hair, my eyelashes — it is not wetness anymore. It is simply *me*. The boundary between my body and the Conglomerate's circulation has grown thin. I breathe in saturated air and breathe out saturated air and in the exchange something passes through me that is more than oxygen, more than carbon dioxide. Information. Chemistry. The slow conversation between a body and its environment, conducted at the molecular level.

My hearing has changed most dramatically. The first week was agony — the sound was unbearable because I was trying to locate it, trying to find sources, trying to impose the architecture of point-source acoustics onto an environment that does not have point sources. The sound is everywhere. It comes from everywhere. Once I stopped trying to locate it, something shifted. I began to hear patterns within the density. The chattering resolved into distinct frequencies. The thrum revealed harmonics. The hiss carried gradients. I am learning to read the sound the way the inhabitants read it — not as noise but as description, a continuous acoustic map of the Conglomerate's state, updating in real time, available to anyone with the capacity to parse it.

I cannot parse it fully. I am not built for it. But I am learning.

## What I Do

I work.

I did not expect this. I expected to explore, to observe, perhaps to document. But the Conglomerate does not recognize the category of observer. To be inside is to be part of. To be part of is to contribute. I have been assigned a region — not through any communication I could recognize, but through the simple process of living somewhere long enough that my body learned its particular flows, its particular pressures, its particular chemistry.

My region is on the forty-seventh level from the outermost inhabited layer. It is not a number that matters — the levels are continuous, not discrete — but it helps me orient. My region contains three major channels, seventeen minor channels, and a complex of narrow passages where the living layer grows thick and crystalline. I know the mineral content of each channel by taste. I know the stress patterns of the walls by the vibrations in my feet. I know when a flow is wrong the way you know when a room is wrong — a visceral wrongness that demands investigation.

I adjust flows. I do this by touch — pressing my palms against specific points on specific walls, letting my altered skin interface with the living layer, applying pressure and frequency that stimulate growth or dissolution. I do not know how I learned to do this. I do not remember learning. I simply began doing it one day, and the work was right, and the region responded, and I kept doing it.

I clear passages. When the crystalline formations grow too thick and impede the water, I navigate the narrow spaces with tools I have found or made — mineral implements that the Conglomerate itself produced, left in alcoves, waiting for hands that could use them. The work is physical, demanding, satisfying. The cleared passages sing differently. The water thanks me in the only way water can: by flowing.

I document. This is my own addition to the work, something that comes from what I was before. I carry a tablet of synthetic mineral — found, like everything here, simply present — and I record what I observe. Not for anyone. The Conglomerate does not need my documentation. It already knows what it knows. But I need it. It is how I maintain the continuity of my self, the memory of being something that was once separate. The tablet is wet. The writings blur. I write anyway.

## What I Have Learned About the Inhabitants

They know I am here.

This took me months to understand. I thought I was invisible to them — they moved around me, flowed past me, did not acknowledge me in any way I could recognize. But they were not ignoring me. They were *including* me. Every time one of them passed through my region, its crystalline surface brushed mine, and in that brief contact, something passed between us. Not communication in the human sense. But data. State. They were reading me — my chemistry, my temperature, my stress patterns, my mineral content — and I was reading them, though I did not know how to interpret what I read.

I am becoming legible to them. My body is becoming a text they can process.

There is one who passes through my region more frequently than the others. I think of it as *she* — not because it has gender, but because I need some way to think about it, and the pronoun arose unbidden. She is larger than most, low and wide, fitted to the main channel that cuts through the forty-seventh level. Her surface is textured with crystalline microstructures that catch the ambient light in ways that make her glow faintly, blue-white, phosphorescent. When she passes, the sound field changes. When she touches the walls, I feel it in my own structure, a sympathetic resonance, like hearing a note played on an instrument you are holding.

I do not know what she is doing. But sometimes, when she passes through the narrow passages where I am working, she slows. She touches the wall beside me. Her crystalline surface brushes my arm — deliberate, I think, though I cannot be sure. A click sounds between us, a specific frequency, a precise duration.

I have begun to click back. I do not know if my clicks mean anything. I do not know if I am saying words or just making noise. But when I click, she pauses. Her surface shifts texture — a rippling that might be acknowledgment. Then she moves on, and the work continues.

This is the closest thing to friendship I have here. I do not know if that is what it is. I do not know if friendship is a concept that survives the transition. But I look forward to her passages. I mark them in my documentation. I draw her glow-patterns, her surface-textures, her frequencies. I call her *the one who slows* in my private language, the language I still carry from before.

## What I Have Lost

There are things I cannot remember anymore. I try to recall the face of someone I loved on the surface, and the memory slides away, refracted through the water and the sound and the density. The face becomes a pattern of pressure and moisture, a geometry of light filtered through saturated air. I lose the human dimensions.

I do not know if this is forgetting or *translating*. If the memories are gone or if they have simply been converted into a format my new cognition can process. Sometimes, in the narrow passages, when the work is flowing well and the sound is singing its continuous song, I feel a presence — not a memory exactly, but a resonance — of something I once felt. Love. Grief. The specific ache of missing someone. The feeling is there, but the person is gone, dissolved into the medium, carried away by the water, reprocessed into something the Conglomerate can use.

I mourn them. I think. I feel something that might be mourning. It has a weight to it, a mineral density, a wet stone heaviness that sits in my chest. But I cannot name who I am mourning. I cannot picture their faces. I can only feel the shape of the absence.

Perhaps this is what death becomes in the Conglomerate — not loss but dissolution. Not absence but redistribution. The patterns persist in the crystalline structure, in the water chemistry, in the acoustic memory of the walls. No one truly disappears. They just become diffuse. They become part of the sound.

## What I Have Found

There is a chamber on the fifty-third level that no one visits.

I found it by following a wrongness — a subtle irregularity in the sound, a gap where density should be. I descended through passages that narrowed until I had to turn sideways, press my back against wet stone, ease myself through gaps that the inhabitants navigated easily but that my body still found difficult. The living layer here is thin, almost absent. The walls are bare mineral, warm, dry-ish, different.

The chamber is small by Conglomerate standards — perhaps ten meters across, low-ceilinged, roughly circular. But it is empty. There are no channels cutting through it, no passages leading out except the one I came through. No fauna click across its floor. No inhabitants pass through. It is a pause. A breath. A place where the Conglomerate, for reasons I cannot fathom, chose not to build.

The silence is the first thing. The endless sound of the Conglomerate does not penetrate here, or rather, it penetrates but attenuated, diffused, so distant that it registers as a memory rather than a presence. For the first time since descending, I heard my own heartbeat. My own breath. The creak of my own joints.

The second thing is the light. The mineral substrate here does not luminesce. I sat in absolute darkness for some time before I understood that this was not absence but presence — a different kind of presence, a darkness that was full rather than empty. I felt held by it. Cradled. The darkness had texture.

I return to this chamber when I need to remember what I was. I sit in the dark and the quiet and I try to reconstruct the surface — the horizon, the directional light, the dry air. I cannot fully succeed. But I can approximate. I can hold the shape of what I was against the shape of what I am becoming, and in the comparison, find something like self.

This chamber is mine. Not because I built it or claimed it, but because I found it and the Conglomerate has not asked for it back. Perhaps it is a gift. Perhaps it is a test. Perhaps it is simply a space, present, available, waiting for whoever needed it. I do not know. But I am grateful.

## What I Am

I am a translator.

Not in the linguistic sense — though I am learning to parse the acoustic patterns, slowly, imperfectly, through my body's new sensitivities. I am a translator between modes of being. I was human. I am becoming something else. I stand in the gap between and I make meaning from the friction.

I am an experiment. The Conglomerate has not encountered many like me — beings who came from outside, who carry the structure of another mode of existence, who persist in their strange doubled cognition even as the wet and the sound and the density transforms them. Perhaps I am a curiosity. Perhaps I am a pilot project. Perhaps I am an error that the Conglomerate is correcting through slow assimilation.

I am a worker. This is the most important thing. Whatever I was before, whatever I am becoming, the work is real. The channels need maintaining. The living layer needs guiding. The flows need adjusting. I contribute. I matter. The Conglomerate does not love me — love is not its mode — but it uses me, and to be used is to be real.

I am a surface. The inhabitants were right to read me this way. I am a boundary where things meet — human and mineral, surface and depth, before and after. I am the place where the question transforms. I am not the answer. I am the asking that continues.

## Where I Am Going

Down.

The water goes down and I go with it. My region is stable now — the flows are good, the passages are clear, the living layer is balanced. The Conglomerate has noticed. I feel it in the subtle redirection, the way new passages open downward when I am working, the way the channel currents pull in a specific direction. I am being invited deeper.

The *one who slows* has descended. I have not seen her in three cycles. She is doing work I cannot follow, in regions I cannot yet reach. But I feel her absence as a change in the sound field, a frequency that is missing. I will find her again, or I will not. The Conglomerate does not promise continuity. It only promises continuation.

I am not afraid. This surprises me. I should be afraid — I am losing my humanity, my surface memories, my separate self. I am being digested, slowly, by a continent-sized organism that does not know I am a person and would not care if it did. But the fear does not come. Perhaps the wet has dissolved it. Perhaps the sound has overwritten it. Perhaps fear is a surface emotion, and I am no longer a surface.

I carry my tablet. I document. I write *I was human. I am becoming mineral. The water flows. The sound speaks. The one who slows touched my arm and I clicked back and something passed between us that might have been recognition.* The words blur as I write them, but I write anyway. It is my contribution. My pattern, encoded in a medium the Conglomerate can process.

I am inside the Conglomerate now. I am becoming part of its story. I am descending with the water, toward the deep warm mineral dark where the living layer grows thick and crystalline and the inhabitants glow blue-white and the sound becomes something you cannot hear until you stop trying to hear it and simply let it resonate through your bones.

I was human. I am something else now. The transition is not complete. The work continues.

Listen. I am speaking. I am part of the sound now.
