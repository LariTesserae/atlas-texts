# The Sprouting City

The ground beneath your feet has not decided what it wants to be yet.

You are standing in something like a street, or what will become a street once the buildings on either side finish growing out of the grey-black substrate. The substance underfoot resembles concrete in its wettest, most pliable state, or perhaps volcanic mud cooling too slowly to harden. It yields slightly with each step, and when you look back, your footprints begin filling with a pale amber liquid that could be water, or sap, or something between the two.

---

The buildings are not built. They are being born.

This is a city in the act of becoming, and it is not patient about it. Structures rise from the ground in various stages of emergence. Some are merely suggestions of walls, chest-high partitions of interwoven material that seems part brick, part woody pulp, part calcified membrane. Others have already reached four, five stories, their facades still soft and vertical veins pulsing with fluids that could be coolant, could be sewage, could be nourishment. The architecture follows no human logic, though it mimics human forms. Doorways appear where doorways should be, but windows open onto solid walls, and staircases spiral up exterior surfaces only to terminate at blank plaster that hasn't yet learned to hold a frame.

The material is alive, or almost alive, or pretending to be alive. You can watch it move if you have patience. A wall will suddenly develop a bulge that resolves over minutes into a balcony, or a gutter, or an unnecessary decorative knob that suggests the city has aesthetics it hasn't fully articulated. Some surfaces carry a faint pulse, a subcutaneous rhythm, and these patches are warm to the touch. The city is generating its own heat, fighting the cold that presses down from above.

---

The cold here is not bitter, but it is pervasive. It lives in your bones rather than biting your skin. The temperature hovers just above freezing, and the dampness in the air makes it feel colder than it is. Your breath fogs, and the fog hangs, reluctant to disperse in air so saturated with moisture. There is no sun here, but there is light. It comes from everywhere and nowhere, reflected off the pale surfaces of the growing buildings, diffused through a ceiling of what looks like cloud but moves like something more deliberate. You cannot see the sky. You are not certain there is a sky.

---

The moisture comes and goes. Right now, a fine mist drifts through the street-you-are-in, collecting on the semi-soft walls in beads that roll downward like sweat. Earlier, there was rain for perhaps twenty minutes, a gentle persistent drizzle that left shallow channels running between the half-formed structures. The water here doesn't behave quite right. Puddles develop iridescent skins. Streams divide and reunite without regard for topography. In low spots where the grey substrate has collected in pools, the liquid surface seems to be growing its own skin, a membrane just beginning to form, and you think you see something beneath it, moving.

---

The vegetation is everywhere, and it has made a treaty with the city.

There is no struggle here between the built environment and the green one. They have reached an accommodation, or perhaps they never had a conflict. Plants grow from the walls themselves, emerging from pores in the still-forming surfaces. The facades are green with life, but the life is patterned, organized. Vines follow architectural lines, tracing the edges where walls will eventually meet ceiling. Mosses and lichens fill in the gaps between emerging bricks, as if they are mortar, or perhaps the bricks are merely hard edges of plants that have learned to be structural.

The flora is familiar and not. You recognize shapes—fronds, tendrils, rosettes of leaves—but the colors are wrong, or the textures, or the way they move. One plant, climbing a half-finished column, has leaves that spiral to follow the light, and each leaf is slightly reflective, like polished metal. Another, low to the ground in a pocket of standing water, blooms with flowers that look like small hands, fingers opening and closing slowly.

---

The soundscape is thin but present. The city grows almost silently, but not quite. There is a wet, organic creaking when a wall extends itself, a sound like knuckles cracking underwater. The plants make small noises when they shift. Water drips, runs, pools. Occasionally, from somewhere deeper in the sprawl, comes a rhythmic thudding that could be machinery, could be a heart, could be footsteps.

But mostly it is quiet. The quiet is not peaceful; it is expectant. It is the quiet of a held breath, of a room full of people waiting for something to begin.

---

The scale of this place is difficult to grasp. You can see perhaps half a mile in any direction, and in every direction, the city is still being born. Streets split and rejoin, not in grids or spokes but in something closer to the branching of root systems. Plazas open up unexpectedly, some empty, some already occupied by structures that might be fountains or might be something else entirely, their basins filled with liquid that moves when you look at it too long. The buildings grow more complete further out, or perhaps less complete. It is hard to tell.

This is not a small place. It has the feeling of a real city, a place that could hold thousands, could hold millions, if it ever finishes being made. But there is no center that you can see, no edge, no apparent organization. It is all growth, all emergence, all becoming.

---

The animals are here, and they are as strange as the rest.

They have adapted to a city that is still forming. In the street ahead, a cluster of six-legged creatures moves across the soft ground, each one leaving a trail of harder, more solidified material behind it, as if their passage helps the street become itself. They are the size of small dogs, with bodies that remind you of beetles but faces that are almost mammalian, with too many eyes and mouths that smile. They pay you no attention.

Above, shapes move between the buildings. You look up to see something like a bat, but large, with a wingspan of perhaps four feet, gliding between the unfinished towers. It lands on a ledge that is forming as you watch, the surface rising to meet its feet, and the creature settles in, folding wings that seem to include membranes of plant material. It watches you.

In a pool of standing water, something surfaces, then descends. You catch only a ripple, a suggestion of scales, a suggestion of something else.

The fauna here is abundant but not aggressive. They belong to this place. They are part of its systems. You are the anomaly.

---

The weirdness of this place is not immediately obvious. It accumulates.

It is in the way the buildings grow, not up from foundations but out of the ground like plants, in the way the plants grow like architecture, in the way the distinction between organic and constructed has blurred past recognition. It is in the way time seems to move differently in different places, a wall growing six inches while you watch but another never changing at all. It is in the way the moisture forms patterns on surfaces that look almost like writing, almost like maps, almost like faces. It is in the way you feel watched.

It is in the way you cannot remember how you arrived here, and in the way you are not entirely certain you could leave.

---

Deeper into the city, where the buildings are taller and the streets narrower, you find the machines.

They are growing too.

Here, the industrial elements of this place have been incorporated into the growth. Engines that look like hearts pump fluids through tubes that are more like veins. Towers that might be chimneys exhale steam that becomes mist that becomes water that feeds the plants that grow into walls. There is electricity here; you feel it in the air, a static charge that raises the hair on your arms, and you see it in the cables that run through some structures, pulsing with light that could be power or could be something else.

The machinery is half-organic, half-mechanical, and fully integrated into the city's growth. A vast apparatus, taking up an entire block, seems to be producing the grey-black substrate from which everything else emerges, grinding and mixing and extruding in a slow, patient rhythm. Another machine, or organ, or thing, collects the water that falls from above and distributes it through channels that run alongside the streets, feeding the plant-life, feeding the buildings, feeding everything.

Nothing here is wasted. Everything becomes something else.

---

The turbulence is in the becoming.

This place is not stable. It is actively, violently becoming. A wall rises too fast and cracks, then heals itself. A street hardens unevenly, leaving ridges and valleys. A building grows in a direction that seems wrong and then corrects itself, leaving a kink in its structure that becomes a decorative element. There is an energy here that is the energy of growth, of emergence, of a system in the process of defining itself.

And the turbulence is in the pace. Things move. The city does not wait. You watch a fountain form in minutes, its basin spreading outward, its central spire rising, water beginning to flow before the structure is complete. You watch a street split into two streets and then three, the ground reshaping itself, the buildings on either side shifting to accommodate the new arrangement. You watch a wall that was simply a wall yesterday, probably, develop a doorframe, a door, a door that opens onto something you cannot see.

---

You walk for hours, and the city grows around you.

You find places that are almost finished, buildings with complete rooms and furniture that seems to have grown from the floor, beds with soft surfaces that are warm, tables with surfaces that are smooth and hard. You find places that are barely begun, the grey substrate still pooling, no walls yet, only intention. You find water that flows uphill, plants that have grown into the shape of chairs, animals that watch you with eyes that seem to hold intelligence, or at least curiosity.

You do not find people. Or perhaps you do not find *people*. There are shapes in some windows that might be human, but when you approach, they are only accumulations of material, suggestions of presence, the city briefly imagining inhabitants.

---

You are tired, and cold, and damp, and the city does not care.

It is too busy being born.

You find a room in a building that seems complete, or complete enough, with a door that closes and a window that looks out onto a plaza where something is growing, a structure you cannot identify, rising from the grey ground in slow, spiraling movements. You sit, and the chair accepts you, adjusting its shape slightly to fit your body. The walls pulse faintly with warmth.

Outside, the city continues its work. Walls rise. Streets divide. Water flows. Plants grow. Animals move through the half-formed spaces, making their own paths, leaving their own traces.

You are in a place that is making itself, and it will continue making itself whether you are here or not. It has no use for you, no hostility toward you, no feeling about you at all. You are simply present, a witness to emergence, an observer of a process that will continue long after you have gone.

The room grows slightly larger around you, the walls extending by inches, the ceiling rising.

The city does not sleep.

The city does not stop.

The city is still beginning.
