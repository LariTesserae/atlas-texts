This is a shallow basin, a bowl-shaped depression in ancient stone. Perhaps three hundred paces across at its widest, ringed by low ridges that rise no higher than a standing person. The floor lies nearly flat, tilted almost imperceptibly toward the center where a crack widens into a narrow fissure, the basin's single drain.

The stone is the thing. Everything here is stone. A pale, mineral surface spreads in all directions, the color of old bone or sun-bleached driftwood. It is not one unified mass but rather layers upon layers compressed and hardened over erasures of time. In places the surface has cracked into polygonal plates, fitted together like a vast, irregular floor. The cracks between them are narrow, barely wide enough to insert a finger. Nothing moves through them.

There is no water here. There hasn't been water here for a very long time. The stone holds a memory of wetness, though, in its chemistry and structure. Certain pockets of the basin floor are stained with mineral deposits in faint rings, orange and white and pale green, where moisture once evaporated, leaving behind its dissolved passengers. Salt crystals glitter in the darkness when the faint light catches them correctly. When touched, they feel glassy and cold.

The darkness is not complete. A dim luminescence permeates this place, though its source is difficult to identify. The stone itself might hold something phosphorescent in its composition, or perhaps the faint glow bleeds from somewhere beyond the ridges. The light is enough to see by, but only just. Shapes emerge from the gloom as you approach them, never before. Distance blurs into uniform shadow.

The warmth is also difficult to source. The stone feels pleasantly warm to the touch, warmer than the air above it, which itself holds a steady, comfortable heat. Not hot enough to cause discomfort. Hot enough to make stillness appealing. The temperature does not vary from one part of the basin to another. It does not fluctuate between day and night, if those concepts even apply here. The warmth simply is, held in the stone and radiating upward, constant.

Vegetation grows in sparse colonies, but only in certain bands along the basin floor. They are not plants as we would recognize them. Lichen-like growths spread across the stone in patches, some angular and cracked like dried mud, others raised and warty like coral formations. Their colors are muted and earth-toned: dusty greens, browns, and grays that blend with the stone beneath them. They do not move. They do not flutter or respond to touch.

A few larger growths stand alone, scattered at wide intervals across the basin floor. They resemble stools or low tables, rounded masses of harder material anchored to the stone below. Some have begun to accumulate mineral deposits on their upper surfaces, forming crusts that catch the faint light. The distance between each growth is considerable; you could walk for a full minute between any two without encountering another living thing.

There are no animals. Nothing walks, crawls, flies, or swims here. Nothing has left tracks or marks upon the stone. The stillness is absolute.

The silence is equally absolute. The air is thick enough to carry sound, but nothing produces any. Your own footsteps make small scraping sounds against the stone, startling in their intrusion. Your breathing is a thunderous mechanical rhythm. If you stand still and hold your breath, the silence returns, pressing against your ears.

This place has existed in its current form for an incalculable span of time. The erosion that shaped it is ancient, completed long ago. The basin floor is settled and stable. No forces are currently at work reshaping it. The mineral deposits will continue their slow accumulation. The lichen-like growths will expand fractionally over centuries. The cracks may widen by millimeters over millennia. But these changes are so slow as to be imperceptible, operating on timescales that make human lifespans look like the blink of an eye.

This is a place in perfect equilibrium. It does not need anything. It does not want anything. It simply persists, warm and dark and silent, holding its quiet shape against the darkness beyond the ridges.

Standing here, in the center of the basin where the faint drain crack splits the stone, you feel your own temporality acutely. Your warmth will leave this place when you do. Your sounds will vanish. The stone will hold no memory of your presence. The mineral rings and lichen colonies will continue their patient processes, unchanged and unaware.

There is something peaceful in this. Something that feels like rest.
