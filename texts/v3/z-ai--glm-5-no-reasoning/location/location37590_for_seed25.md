The darkness here is not an absence. It has weight and texture. You feel it press against your skin like cloth, like being inside of something vast and breathing.

Before you understand what you're hearing, you understand that sound has become a physical environment. The noise arrives in layers that you learn to separate only slowly. There is a bass frequency—a thrumming, chest-deep vibration that comes from somewhere below the ground itself, or perhaps from the movement of too many bodies over too much earth. Above this, a mid-range wash: clicking, scraping, the sound of millions of jointed legs testing surfaces, mandibles working against mandible, the friction of chitin on stone. And threading through it all, high and sharp, come the calls—whistles, clicks that resolve into patterns, a language or something like one that moves through the darkness in waves, answered from distances you cannot measure.

The terrain beneath your feet is smooth and dips gently. Not rock, exactly. Something harder, denser—a mineral surface that has been worn by passage. You crouch and touch it: cool, slightly damp, with a texture like very fine sandpaper. The moisture is condensation, you realize. The place sweats. There is warmth rising from below, from the bodies, meeting the cooler air that flows from somewhere else, and the collision of temperatures leaves everything glazed with a thin wetness that makes the darkness slick.

You smell loam, minerals, something ammoniac and sharp.

This is a cavity in the earth. A system of cavities. The scale is difficult to judge in the dark, but the way the sound moves tells you that the space extends far beyond your immediate perception—opens into chambers you cannot see, connects to passages that lead to places you will never reach. The density of sound comes from the density of life. Everything here is occupied.

And then your eyes begin to adjust, and you understand the darkness differently.

It is not black. It is dark blue, dark green, darkness with color in it. And in that darkness, you begin to see movement.

The fauna are everywhere.

They cover the walls first—great sheets of them, organisms the size of your hand, flat and pale, with too many legs and long sensory antennae that wave in the air, testing, testing. They move slowly over the mineral surfaces, and where they pass, they leave trails—scraped clean, slightly polished. They are tending this place. You do not know what they eat. Each other, perhaps. Or something that grows in the cracks that you cannot see.

On the ground, between your feet, smaller creatures scatter. You feel rather than see them—clicking things, shelled things, moving with speed and purpose. They do not collide with you. They move around. The awareness of your presence ripples outward through them, communicated by sound or by vibration or by something else entirely, and the space around your body becomes a column of stillness in a room of motion.

Above, in the darkness that could be ceiling or could be infinite vertical space, things move with a different kind of rhythm. Fluttering. Gliding. You hear the snap and flex of wing-membranes, the sudden rush of displaced air. These are larger. The calls come from them—the patterns, the whistles, the language that you almost recognize as communication. They navigate by sound. The noise is their sight.

You stand very still, and you watch, and you listen.

The overwhelming impression is of being inside a system. A functioning. This is not a place of chaos; it is a place of processes. The moisture condenses and drips and is absorbed. The creatures tend surfaces, eat, communicate, breed, die. Their waste feeds something else, perhaps microscopic, perhaps fungal—there is a faint bioluminescence in patches, a pale gray-green glow that illuminates nothing but reveals itself, moss-like crusts in the deeper crevices. Not vegetation. Simpler. Spawn-matter. Thread-matter. The damp architecture that remains when plants never evolved.

This world is old. The erosion parameter told you that, and now you feel it in your bones. The smooth stone, the established paths, the way the sound moves—this is a place that has been what it is for a very long time. The balance is not fragile. It is robust, settled into itself, patient. You are the anomaly. You are the thing that does not belong.

And they know it.

The communication has changed. You hear it—the patterns shifting, the calls modifying, information moving through the darkness away from you and returning transformed. The column of stillness around your body is widening. The nearest creatures are pulling back, not in fear but in reorganization. Making room for something.

Or making space to observe.

You realize you cannot tell how many there are. The darkness makes counting impossible, and the sound makes counting meaningless—one voice, ten thousand voices, it's all the same roar. But you feel the attention. The weight of being perceived by something that perceives in a way you cannot access. They read you through vibrations in the floor, through the displacement of air, through the sound your body makes simply by existing—your breath, your heartbeat, the electrical activity of your nervous system perhaps, if they sense such things.

You are being measured.

The moisture continues to bead on every surface. The coolness settles into your clothing, your skin. The darkness presses. The sound continues, deafening, immersive, becoming a kind of silence through its opposite—when everything is noise, nothing is noise, and you find you can exist within it, let it wash over you like water.

A creature lands on your shoulder.

It is light. The weight of a small bird, perhaps, but the texture is wrong—too many legs, too dry, the faint scrape of claw-points through fabric. You do not move. It sits there, and you feel its antennae move, touching your ear, your cheek, tasting the strange chemistry of your skin. It makes a sound—a low clicking, almost a purr. And then it lifts off, wings snapping, and disappears into the dark.

Something has been communicated.

You do not know if you have passed a test or failed one. But the column of stillness around you begins to contract again. The creatures return to their previous patterns. The overwhelming sound resumes its previous composition, the modification that was you settling back into the broader composition of them.

You are still an anomaly. But you are a tolerated anomaly. For now.

The landscape extends around you, dense with life, dense with process, dense with the magnificent roaring industry of creatures who have built a world in the dark. There is no horizon here. There is only the next wall, the next chamber, the next passage—mineral and moisture and movement, extending beyond your perception into places where the sound comes from, where the water flows from, where the warmth rises from, where the logic of this place was established long before anything like you existed and will continue long after you leave.

If you leave.

You breathe. You listen. The darkness holds you.
