# The Cistern of Wings

The place sits very high. You feel it in the pressure behind your eyes—that thinner air, that altitude where breath comes reluctantly. Elevation 2.8. The world below is abstraction, a rumor. Clouds move beneath you more often than above.

---

It begins as a crack in the mountain's face. A wound that has healed wrong. The entrance is narrow—the width of two bodies pressed sideways—and the darkness inside exhales as you approach. Warm. Damp. Alive.

You descend. Not down into the mountain, but *in*. The passageway corkscrews, and the stone under your palms is slick with something that isn't quite water. Condensation breeds here. The warmth (temperature 1.9) seeps from the rock itself—thermal veins in the earth's body, a slow fever that has burned for epochs.

---

The passage opens into a chamber.

Cathedral-sized. Grove-sized. A space you could walk the perimeter of in three minutes, but that you cannot take in with a single glance. The scale is small in the way a heart is small—contained, but impossibly deep.

The darkness here has weight. Light 0.3. Your torch or flame or phosphorescent fungus catches only the nearest surfaces; the rest retreats into implication. What you can see: walls that are not walls. A ceiling that breathes.

---

This is where they live.

Fauna 2.8. Overwhelming. The density of presence hits you before you understand what you're seeing. The walls *move*. Every surface—ledges, crags, the bulges of stone like frozen boils—every surface is occupied. They cling in layers. Bodies pressed against bodies. Near-touching. Layered. High-saturation, density 2.3.

Wings.

Hundreds of thousands of wings. Leather membranes. Fur slicked with the chamber's dampness. The sound (sound 2.0) is a living thing—a roar made of a million small sounds: the scratch of claws, the click of echolocation, the wet slap of wings against stone, against each other. A river of noise. A waterfall of noise. A noise that fills the chamber and pours out through the crack in the mountain's face.

They are not bats. Not quite. The shape is bat-like, but wrong. Too large, some of them. Too small, others. The proportions are nearly correct, but not correct enough. Weirdness 0.7. Unusual. The torchlight glints off eyes that reflect wrong colors. The fur patterns are almost symmetrical but drift into asymmetry at the edges. One of them turns its head and you see teeth—too many teeth, in rows that continue back into the dark.

---

They are not aggressive. They are not afraid. They have lived here longer than the mountain has been called by any name.

Erosion 2.4. The chamber is weathered by them—the stone worn smooth where their bodies have pressed for generations. Grooves carved by claws over centuries. The air itself is an erosion, thick with the particulate of their existence: guano in layers (shallow piles, not deep—the chamber is too warm and damp for accumulation, the material breaks down into something else, something that feeds the thin vegetation). There are no constructed structures here. Built 0.1. Nothing made. Nothing fabricated. Only what the stone and the creatures have made of each other.

---

The vegetation is sparse (vegetation 0.8), but present. Patches of something pale and thread-like cling to the walls where moisture collects. Not moss. Not fungus. Something between—a genus that only exists in this place, in this specific combination of warmth and wet and waste. It feeds on what the creatures leave behind. The creatures feed on what flies in from outside—moths, beetles, things that are drawn to the mountain's warmth. A closed loop. A patient cycle.

The patches glow. Faintly. Enough that when your torch dies, you will not be in total darkness. The creatures do not need the glow—they navigate by sound—but the glow is there. The chamber's concession to visitors. A warning, perhaps. A welcome, perhaps.

---

You stand at the edge of the space. The creatures swirl above you—movement that is somehow both chaotic and organized. They do not fly *at* you. They fly *around* you, adjusting their patterns to accommodate your presence, a slow dynamic (dynamic 1.2) that unfolds over minutes, not seconds. They acknowledge you and they move on.

The water here is not standing water. There is no pool, no underground lake. The moisture is atmospheric, condensing on the walls, on the wings, on your skin. Water 1.2. Damp. Intermittent. You can taste it in the air—mineral and organic and ancient.

---

This place has rules.

**You must move slowly.** Sudden motions trigger something in the swarm—a tightening, a compression, a shift in the sound-field that you do not want to hear.

**You must not make loud noise.** The chamber is already loud; the creatures are adapted to a specific frequency range. To introduce something outside that range is to announce yourself as *other*. To announce yourself as *other* is to be investigated. To be investigated by thousands of small bodies with too many teeth is—unwise.

**You must not take.** The stone, the pale vegetation, the small bones on the floor—these are not yours. They belong to the cycle. The creatures have a relationship with this place that predates your species' understanding of relationship.

**You must not stay.** The warmth and the damp and the concentration of biological matter creates an atmosphere that humans can tolerate for a time, but not indefinitely. The creatures carry microbes that they are immune to and you are not.

---

The chamber has a shape that is difficult to remember afterward. Researchers—who have found this place, will find this place—draw maps that do not match. The dimensions shift depending on where you stand. The darkness plays tricks. Or perhaps the space itself resists being known.

There are deeper passages. Smaller. The creatures flow into them and disappear. Where they go, what they do there, whether the chamber connects to other chambers, other mountains, other worlds—this is unknown. The passages are too small for human bodies.

The creatures are the only technology here. Tech 1.0. Primitive but effective. They have evolved to navigate absolute darkness, to find food in a landscape of stone and sky, to live in densities that would destroy other species. Their bodies are tools. Their wings are hands. Their voices are eyes.

---

The sound never stops.

Even when the swarm is still—those moments of collective rest when the bodies hang like fruit in the darkness—there is sound. The drip of condensation. The settling of stone. The processes of digestion and respiration from ten thousand small bodies. The chamber murmurs. It speaks in frequencies below human hearing, vibrations felt in the bones.

If you stay long enough, you begin to hear patterns. Not language. Not song. Something like weather. The sound of a system in balance, erosion 2.4, a place that has worn itself into a shape it can maintain.

---

The creatures do not have a name for this place. They do not have names. They have frequencies, positions, relationships. This chamber is not *home*—it is *where the warmth is*. It is *where the others are*. It is *where we go when we are not elsewhere*.

You, standing at the edge of the swarm, feel the warmth and the noise and the weight of all those small bodies pressing against the boundaries of your understanding. This is not a place made for you. It is not made at all. It has evolved, settled, eroded into its current state.

You are permitted to see it. You are not permitted to stay.

---

The entrance, when you turn to leave, is a rectangle of gray light. Outside, the mountain's face. The thin air. The clouds moving beneath you like slow cattle.

Inside, the sound follows you for a moment, then fades. The warmth bleeds away. The dampness evaporates from your skin.

You emerge with nothing to show for your journey. No artifact. No photograph can capture what that chamber *is*. No recording can hold the weight of that sound. You have seen something that has existed since before seeing was invented, and it has not changed for you, will not change for you, will not change for anything less than the mountain itself changing.

The crack in the rock exhales one last time. Warm. Damp. Patient.

Then the wind takes it.
