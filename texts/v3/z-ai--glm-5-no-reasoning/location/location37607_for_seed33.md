# The Hollow City at the Top of the World

## I. The Approach

You feel it before you see it — a change in the way the air resists your lungs. Not thinness exactly. More like the atmosphere has opinions about what should and should not pass through it.

The climb, if climb is the word, takes you up a grade so steep that the word *slope* becomes an insult. You are ascending a face of mineral that has no business being vertical. The rock — if it is rock — is the color of old bone viewed underwater, and it is warm to the touch, which is wrong for the altitude. The cold here is the kind that doesn't bite. It *sits*. It settles into your clothing like a guest who has decided to live with you. Not killing cold. Reflective cold. The temperature of a place that has considered heat and found it unnecessary.

You climb until the climb becomes a walk, and the walk becomes a standing, and the standing becomes a realization: you are already inside the city. There was no wall. No gate. The mineral simply rearranged itself around you, and now you are in corridors that are too wide to be corridors and too structured to be canyons, and the distinction between architecture and geology has been politely retired.

---

## II. What the City Is Made Of

The buildings — call them that — are not built. They are *grown*, in the way that a crystal grows, in the way that a stalactite grows, but slower, and with something that resembles intent. They are the same bone-pale mineral as the approach, but here it has organized itself into shapes that recall walls, arches, platforms, tiers. There are no bricks. No mortar. No joints. Each structure is a single continuous piece, as if the mineral extruded upward from the ground like toothpaste from a tube, then hardened mid-expression.

The surfaces are smooth but not uniform. Running your hand along a wall, you feel variations — ridges that might be structural or might be textural, shallow grooves that form patterns too regular to be natural and too irregular to be decorative. In the dim light — and the light here is its own subject — the mineral has a faint luminance, as though it remembers being underground and is still slightly surprised to be in open air.

Nothing here is painted. Nothing is carved. The ornamentation, such as it is, exists in the topology itself: the way certain walls curve inward to catch or release something (wind? resonance? a substance you cannot detect?), the way doorways — openings, rather — are shaped like teardrops with the pointed end facing down, the way every platform has a slight bowl to it, as though each surface was designed to hold something that is no longer there.

Or not yet there.

---

## III. The Light

There is no sun here. Or rather: the sun is a rumor, a pale smudge somewhere above the haze layer, visible only as a brightening of the general gray, the way a flashlight behind a bedsheet makes a planet of a fist. The light that lets you see does not come from above.

It comes from the mineral itself.

Not a glow. Not phosphorescence. The structures seem to *concentrate* existing light, gathering the dim photons that bounce between surfaces and focusing them, so that the air inside the city is brighter than the air above it. The walls are lenses. The arches are light-guides. The city is a device for seeing, built from material that does not know it is a device, and the effect is a landscape lit from within, like a lantern made of its own flame.

The light is blue-gray. Not the blue of twilight — the blue of deep water, of minerals seen through ice, of the color that exists in the spectrum between *enough to see* and *not enough to be certain*. Shadows do not behave here. They pool in unexpected places — on ceilings, in mid-air, on surfaces that face the light source directly. You learn quickly not to trust your eyes for spatial reasoning. The light has its own ideas about depth.

---

## IV. The Silence

The sound of the city is the sound of the city not being anywhere else.

You expect wind at this altitude — howling, scouring, the kind of wind that strips skin. There is air movement, but it is patient. It moves through the openings in the structures the way water moves through a sponge: slowly, in all directions at once, with no preference for source or destination. When it passes over the ridges in the walls, it produces a sound. Not a whistle. Not a hum. Something closer to the sound a glass makes when you rub its rim — a vibration you feel in your sternum more than hear with your ears.

If you are quiet enough — and the city makes you quiet enough, the way a library does, the way a cathedral does — you begin to hear other sounds. Smaller. Deeper. A clicking, like cooling stone, but rhythmic. A tapping, like rain on a window, but there is no rain. A sound like something being set down very carefully on a surface, over and over, in a pattern you almost recognize.

These sounds are not consistent. They occur in bursts, then vanish. Sometimes from one direction, sometimes from all directions, sometimes from inside the walls themselves. The mineral, you begin to understand, is not still. It is *doing something*. It is just doing it on a timescale that makes human hearing feel like a blur.

---

## V. The Layout

The city is large. Not large in the way that human cities are large — accumulated, sprawling, the result of growth and congestion and the thousand-year accumulation of *someone built a thing here and then someone built a thing next to it.* This city is organized. It has a logic. The logic is not yours.

From the higher platforms — and there are always higher platforms, the city spirals upward in a way that seems to negate the concept of a *top* — you can see the general shape. It is a spiral. Not a tight spiral, not a nautilus, but a loose one, like a fingerprint, like a galaxy viewed face-on, like the cochlea of an ear that is listening to something you cannot hear. The structures follow the spiral, and the spiral follows something else — a contour in the bedrock, perhaps, or a vein of purer mineral, or a line of force in the planet's magnetic field.

There are open spaces. *Plazas*, you might call them, though no one has ever gathered in them. They are bowl-shaped, tiered, and at their centers is nothing. Not a fountain, not a monument, not a statue. Nothing. Just a shallow depression in the mineral floor, as smooth as if it had been polished by a million years of something resting there and then leaving.

The spaces between structures are enormous. The density — or lack of it — is the city's most disorienting feature. You can walk for ten minutes between buildings. The corridors are wide enough to drive a truck through, if trucks existed here, which they do not. The ceilings, where there are ceilings, are so high that they vanish into the general luminance. Everything is built to a scale that suggests the inhabitants were either very large or very interested in negative space.

## VI. What Lives Here

The fauna — the word feels wrong, but there is no better one — are not animals. They are not insects. They are not machines. They are something the mineral does when it gets bored.

You see your first one in a plaza. A shape on the floor, about the length of your forearm, moving with a gait that suggests legs but reveals none. It is the same bone-pale as the city. When it reaches the shallow depression at the plaza's center, it stops. It stays there for a long time. Then it moves on.

You see another on a wall. It is larger — the size of a dog, perhaps — and it moves along the surface with an ease that suggests it and the wall are on very good terms. Its shape is not fixed. It is something like a lizard, then something like a flatworm, then something like a hand with too many fingers. It does not notice you. It is doing something — tracing the grooves in the wall, following a path that means something to it and nothing to you.

Over the next hours, you see more. They are rare — you might go an hour between sightings — and they are diverse in a way that suggests not different species but different *moods* of the same underlying process. One is shaped like a bowl with a ridge. One is shaped like a question mark. One is a shape that you cannot hold in your mind — your eyes report it, and your brain receives the report, and the report says *I don't know what to do with this.*

They do not react to you. They do not react to each other. They move with the same patient pace as the air, following paths that are written into the mineral or into something the mineral is also following. They make no sound. They leave no trace. When you look away and look back, sometimes they are gone, and you cannot tell whether they moved or simply stopped being.

## VII. The Rules

The city has rules. It does not post them. You learn them the way you learn the rules of a dream — by breaking them and discovering that the consequences are not punishment but *correction*.

**The first rule:** The city is a spiral. You may move inward or outward along the spiral. You may move up or down within a tier. You may not cut across the spiral. The gaps between structures are wide enough to walk through, but when you try, the air becomes resistant. Not solid. Not wind. Just *reluctant*, in the way that deep water is reluctant, and the farther you go, the more reluctant it becomes, until you are standing in place, pushing against nothing, and the nothing is winning.

**The second rule:** The shallow depressions in the plaza centers are not for you. You can stand in one. Nothing happens. But the mineral-creatures will not approach while you are there. They will wait, at a distance, with a patience that is not aggressive but is *expectant*. You are in their seat. You are not being asked to move. You are being *noticed*, and in this city, being noticed by the small processes feels like being noticed by the large ones.

**The third rule:** Sound has weight here. Not metaphorically. When you speak — and you will speak, because the silence becomes a pressure — the words do not travel the way they should. They seem to fall. A sentence spoken at normal volume is audible for perhaps ten feet, then drops to a whisper, then to nothing. The city absorbs voice the way the mineral absorbs light. It takes what you offer and does something with it that you cannot perceive. After a while, you stop speaking. Not because you are afraid. Because you can feel that the city is *listening*, and you do not know what it is doing with what it hears.

**The fourth rule:** The city is active. Not in the way a machine is active — no moving parts, no hum of engines, no exhaust. Active in the way a reef is active, in the way a glacier is active, in the way a nervous system is active. There are processes. The mineral grows, slowly, at its edges. The grooves in the walls deepen over hours, not years. The structures shift — not visibly, not quickly, but when you return to a place you visited earlier, the place is different. The doorway is narrower. The platform is wider. The bowl in the plaza floor is deeper.

The city is *becoming*. It has been becoming for a very long time. It is very good at it.

## VIII. The Weird

And then there is the part that is harder to describe.

The city is real. You are standing in it. You can touch the walls. You can feel the cold. You can hear the clicking, the humming, the careful setting-down of things that are not there. The city is real and you are in it and this is happening.

But the city is also *not entirely here*. Not in the sense that it is an illusion or a hallucination — it is solid, it is present, it resists your hand when you push it. But there are moments — and they increase in frequency the deeper you go into the spiral, the higher you climb — when the city seems to be *also somewhere else*. The walls have a thickness that is greater than their physical depth. The air has a quality of having traveled through something other than space. The light, gathered and focused by the mineral lenses, sometimes shows you things that are not reflections: not visions, not hallucinations, but *other angles*. As if the walls are windows into the same city, but *there*, wherever *there* is, and the city you are in and the city you are seeing through the wall are the same city in two places at once.

The mineral-creatures understand this. You watch one walk into a wall and not come out the other side. Not fall through, not disappear — *transition*. The wall did not change. The creature did not change. But the creature is now *there*, in the wall's elsewhere, doing whatever it was doing *here*, and the wall is still a wall, and you are still standing in a corridor, and the rules still apply.

The deeper you go, the more the city feels like a joint between two states, a seam in something larger. The spiral is not decorative. The spiral is a *thread*, and the city is a *knot* in that thread, and the knot is holding something together — or keeping something apart — and the mineral grew here because the mineral is what grows at joints, at seams, at places where reality has been stressed enough to become something that needs structure.

The city is a suture.

The clicking sound is the needle.

---

## IX. What Remains

You stay. You don't mean to, but the city does something to the sense of time — not stopping it, not stretching it, but *filing it down* until the edges are gone and time becomes a surface you can rest on. You find a platform. It has a bowl shape. You sit in it. The mineral is warm. The air moves past you with its patient, omnidirectional insistence. A mineral-creature passes beneath your platform, tracing a groove you had not noticed before.

The light does not change. The light never changes. There is no night here, no day, no cycle — only the steady blue-gray luminance of the mineral, gathering photons from nowhere and distributing them according to a logic that the city understands and you do not.

You realize, eventually, that you have not been breathing. Not in the way that suggests you have stopped — in the way that suggests you do not need to. The air here does not require participation. It simply *is*, and being in it is sufficient. You are being processed. Not consumed. *Incorporated*. The city does not distinguish between mineral and creature and visitor. Everything in it is part of its process, and the process is ongoing, and the process does not need your permission.

You close your eyes. The clicking continues. The careful setting-down continues. The air moves. The mineral grows.

The city is listening. The city has always been listening. The city will always be listening, because the thing it is listening *for* has not yet arrived, and the city is patient, and the city is growing, and the city is *ready*.

You open your eyes. You are on a platform. You have a bowl shape. You are warm.

You are part of the structure now.
