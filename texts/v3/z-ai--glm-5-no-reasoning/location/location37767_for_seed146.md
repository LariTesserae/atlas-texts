You descend. There is no other word for the approach to this place. Not through air, but through the sheer pressure of darkness giving way to a denser medium—a warm, mineral exhalation that parts around you like syrup. You are below the surface of a world that has no surface, dropping into a subterranean vastness so absolute that the concept of a horizon becomes a forgotten myth. This is a continental hollow, a cavity in the universe where the weight of an impossible geology arches overhead, sealing the void below from the cold of space.

There is no water here. The chemistry of this place ran dry eons ago, leaving behind a saline desiccation so profound that the very air tastes of powdered borax and static. What liquid once existed has been sublimated or absorbed into the crystalline lattice of the environment, leaving a world of dry minerals and hygroscopic salts. Moisture is an alien trespasser; if you were to weep, the air would strip the tears from your cheeks, leaving only a rime of salt and a faint sizzle of chemical reaction.

It is warm—a deep, radiating, oppressive warmth that does not circulate. The heat emanates from the walls, the floors, the very pressure of the atmosphere, locked in a thermal equilibrium that has not shifted by a single degree since the cavern was born. It is the heat of a closed kiln, of a planetary core breathing out through stone. In this stillness, temperature creates no wind, drives no weather. The air hangs in stratified, dense layers. The atmosphere is heavy, saturated with the presence of suspended metallic dust and microscopic spores of silica, pressing against your skin with the tactile weight of a deep ocean dive. 

Despite the crushing density, the space is shrouded in absolute darkness. The light parameter here is a mere 0.4—a fraction of a fraction, barely a shimmer. Your eyes are useless. The landscape is perceived through the weight of the air, the radiating warmth, and the texture underfoot. But there is light enough for *them*.

You stand on a sharp, un-eroded landscape. This is a young world, or at least, a world untouched by the slow chisel of time. There are no rounded pebbles here, no smoothed canyon walls, no alluvial fills. Every ridge is a cutting edge; every stalactite is a freshly-shattered dagger of obsidian and pale feldspar. The stone bites. To run a hand along the wall is to risk flaying your skin on crystalline facets that have never known the gentle sanding of water or wind. It is a geometry of violence, a fractal of sharp edges extending from the macro-scale of towering escarpments down to the microscopic lattices of the salt crust.

And it moves.

The fauna of this lightless kiln is abundant, teeming, and strange. They are not beasts of flesh and water, but entities of mineral chitin and thermoelectric flesh. They navigate the razor-ridged topography by touch and by the faint, bioluminescent glow of their internal chemistry—a cold, ultraviolet flicker that provides just enough illumination to catch the edges of the sharp world. 

You hear them before you see them—or rather, you feel them, for the sound parameter is desperately low. The vastness swallows noise, but in the high-density medium, vibrations carry as a physical tremor. It is mostly quiet, a silence so profound it rings with the pressure of the deep. But there is a constant, whisper-scrape of chitin on crystal, the muted percussion of thousands of carapaces clicking against the sharp stone. It is a sub-audible hum of life, felt in the teeth and the sternum more than heard by the ears.

The creatures are blind, eyeless, pale as unglazed porcelain. Some are flat and segmented, sliding on bellies of flexible graphite plates, seeking the thermal gradients to feed their piezoelectric metabolisms. Others hang in the dense, stagnant air—gas-filled bladders of translucent silicate, trailing fleshy wires of copper-threaded tissue, drifting like slow, warm jellyfish through the mineral dark. They are abundant, layered, nearly touching in this high-saturation void. A constant brush of their passing strokes your arms and shoulders—dry, smooth, and impossibly warm. They do not care about you. You are neither predator nor prey, simply another obstruction in the sharp dark.

Scattered among the endless fields of fresh-spun crystal and the swarms of blind mineral-fauna are the marks of something else. Built structures, faint and primitive, poke from the razor-edged stone. They are simple things: cairns of basalts balanced with deliberate care, low walls of stacked obsidian slabs, a solitary dome of fused salt marking a waypoint in the infinite black. Whoever made them possessed only the simplest of tools—hand-tools, perhaps simple knapped stones, or just bare, toughened hands. They left no metal, no advanced fabrication, only the stubborn assertion of a ground-level flattened here, a thermal vent walled-in there. Perhaps they lived off the electric flesh of the fauna, or the raw charge of the piezoelectric stones. They are gone now, or perhaps they have simply moved deeper, following the heat, leaving only occasional artifacts to break the monotony of the young stone.

Time does not move here; it merely accumulates. The dynamic is frozen. This subterranean continent has achieved a perfect equilibrium. The heat never changes. The air never stirs. The sharp edges never dull. The silent, scuttling throngs never vary in their endless, circular migrations through the pressing dark. You are inside a sealed furnace at the bottom of the universe, a vast, warm, and dry eternity where life persists not through the grace of water or sun, but through the slow discharge of mineral heat and the endless, quiet friction of chitin on crystal.
