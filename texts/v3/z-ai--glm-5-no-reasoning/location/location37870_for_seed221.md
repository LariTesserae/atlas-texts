# The Incubator

## The Chamber

You are inside something. There is no outside here, or if there is, it has not been made yet.

The walls press close—ten meters across, perhaps twelve, curved and seamless, a gray-green material that looks like metal but sweats. Everything sweats here. The air is so saturated that breathing feels like drinking, and the act of exhaling sends swirls of visible vapor curling into the already-dense atmosphere. Water beads on every surface in a skin of condensation, gathering into droplets, running in slow tears down the walls, pooling in the seams where curved panels meet. The floor is ankle-deep in a stillness that should be liquid but resists classification. It is not water. It is something manufactured, something designed to hold and hold and hold.

The temperature sits just above freezing, that particular cold that is not the clean sharpness of winter air but the wet, penetrating chill of a cave, of a basement, of somewhere that has never known sunlight. Every breath clouds. Every surface is slick. The dimness—the light comes from nowhere and everywhere at once, a sourceless gray luminescence embedded in the material of the walls themselves—renders everything in shades of pewter and slate and shadow.

This is a pocket. A hidden room. A space sealed off from whatever lies beyond, if anything lies beyond at all. You could touch both walls if you spread your arms. The ceiling arches overhead like the interior of a skull, and the floor—there is no floor, only that shallow standing reservoir of manufactured wetness that swallows your feet to the ankle.

Everything here is made. This is the first and most important truth. There is no stone that was quarried, no earth that was born of geologic process. The walls were grown or printed or extruded in some fashion that implies intention. Every centimeter of surface has been designed. The texture is too consistent—smooth but not slick, pocked with microscopic perforations that breathe moisture from some reservoir behind the visible skin. The curve of the chamber is mathematical, precise, the kind of shape that only exists when someone has calculated it.

## The Presences

But the space is not empty.

It is astonishingly, violently full.

They press against each other in the dim light, a mass of bodies and half-bodies and things that are almost-bodies, layered three and four deep against the walls, floating in the ankle-deep reservoir, draped across each other in piles of life. The density is total. You cannot move without touching something. The chamber holds perhaps two hundred of them—visible ones—and the light is poor enough that more might lurk in the corners, beneath the surface of the liquid, folded into the masses of their kin.

They are not one species. They are not ten species. They are something else—a genera of forms that share a family resemblance only in their incompleteness.

Some have the suggestion of limbs—arm-things that end in rounded nubs, leg-things that taper to points. Some have openings that might become mouths. Some have the ridges that could be eyes, or ears, or something else entirely. None of them are finished. All of them are growing.

They are embryonic. That is the word that rises unbidden. This is an incubation chamber, a place where things are made and then grown, where the raw materials of life are assembled and then set to developing in the wet and the cold and the close-pressed dark.

And they are active. They do not sleep. They writhe against each other in slow motion, a constant peristaltic motion of bodies adjusting, seeking, pressing. The movement generates the sound.

## The Noise

It is deafening.

Not loud in the way of machinery, not the brutal hammering of industry—though there is industry here, hidden behind the walls, the deepfelt vibration of engines and pumps and systems that keep this place functional. The mechanical sounds are the bass note, the substructure of the auditory environment. A constant subsonic thrumming that vibrates in the teeth, in the sternum, in the fluid of the inner ear.

But the overwhelming sound is organic.

They are vocal, all of them, continuously. Not words—there are no words here, language has not been invented yet or has been abandoned long ago—but sounds. Mewling. Clicking. A low hum that rises from dozens of throat-openings at once and harmonizes in frequencies that should not harmonize. Wet sounds, the slap and slide of bodies against bodies, the splash of movement in the reservoir. A keening thread of something like distress—or like expression, or like pleasure, it is impossible to know the emotional register of things that have no faces.

The noise is so total that it creates its own silence—there is no absence of sound, no gap where you could locate yourself. You are immersed in it as thoroughly as you are immersed in the wet. The ears adjust, and then they stop adjusting, and then the sound simply is, a given, a condition of the environment, like the cold and the dark and the way the air resists your lungs.

## The Systems

This place is fed.

You can see the infrastructure where the walls meet the ceiling—pipes and conduits, a dense arterial network of industrial design. Copper and steel and something translucent that carries glowing fluid. The pipes sweat like everything else, condensation running down their lengths, dripping onto the masses below. The technology is industrial but elegant, purposeful, designed with the clean lines of efficiency rather than the chaos of adaptation. Someone built this to work, and work it does.

The pumps behind the walls—you feel them more than hear them—cycle in a rhythm that has nothing to do with the chaos of the vocalizations. Every four seconds, a pulse of pressure. Every four seconds. Every four seconds. The system is stable, equilibrium achieved and maintained. This place does not change. It has been this way for as long as it has existed, and it will be this way for as long as it continues.

Near the floor, where the reservoir pools deepest, there are vents—dark openings that suck the liquid in and push it back out, circulating, filtering, maintaining whatever chemical balance the things here require. The liquid smells of nothing and everything at once, a flatness that somehow implies richness, nutrients and compounds and the building blocks of form.

## The Time

Nothing changes here.

This is the strangest thing, the thing that takes longest to understand. The chamber exists in a perpetual present tense. The things move, yes—they writhe and vocalize and press against each other—but they do not progress. They are becoming, but they never become. The embryonic state is not a phase here. It is a condition.

You could watch for hours, and the arrangement of bodies would be different but the scene would be the same. You could watch for days. The same masses, the same density, the same deafening chorus of half-formed voices. The chamber maintains itself and them in a stasis that is also a dynamism, a frozen moment that is also teeming with motion.

They are made to be this way. That is the horror and the wonder. This is not a failure of development—this is the design. They are meant to press against each other, meant to fill every cubic centimeter of available space, meant to sing their continuous song in the wet cold manufactured dark. The pipes and pumps and systems exist to sustain them in this precise state indefinitely.

## The Edge

At the margins of the chamber—in the corners where the curved walls meet the floor, and in the seams where panels join—there are openings. Darker than the dim, openings barely large enough to admit a hand, and through them, sometimes, you glimpse something else.

Not an outside. There is no outside. But other insides. Other chambers, perhaps, pressing against this one, each as full, as dense, as overwhelming. The space beyond the walls might be more walls, more rooms, more manufactories of half-life stretching in every direction, up and down and sideways through dimensions that should not exist.

The sweat that runs down the walls sometimes disappears into these seams. The things in the reservoir sometimes drift toward the vents and then away. Nothing enters and nothing leaves. The system is closed. The equilibrium is maintained. The things continue to grow without growing, to become without becoming, to sing without song.

And the pipes behind the walls pulse every four seconds. And the light glows without source. And the air holds its moisture like a held breath. And the cold seeps into everything and stays.
