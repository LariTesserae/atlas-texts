# The Interior

You enter through a fissure no wider than a shoulder — a crack in the summit stone that shouldn't be here, at this altitude, in this heat. The rock is obsidian-warm against your skin as you squeeze through, and then the passage opens, and you are inside.

Not a cave. A **chamber**. A room the size of a small house, maybe smaller — hard to judge because the walls are not still. They breathe. Or something behind them breathes. The stone, dark and glassy and shot through with veins of mineral you cannot name, expands and contracts by millimeters, and those millimeters, in this compressed space, are everything.

## The Water

Everything here is wet. Not flowing — *expressing*. Water does not run down the walls. It emerges from them, bead by bead, as though the stone itself is weeping. The droplets form uniformly across every surface, hang for a moment, then slide with an almost deliberate slowness into channels that have been worn — or that were always there — in the floor. The channels cross and recross in a geometry that feels intentional without being designed. A lattice. A vascular pattern. The water collects in shallow basins, no deeper than a finger, and there it sits, warm, faintly iridescent, absolutely still despite the constant replenishment. No ripples. The new water joins the old water without disturbing it.

The air is saturated. You feel it in your lungs — each breath carries weight, substance. Condensation forms on your skin instantly, on the fabric of your clothing, on any surface cooler than the surrounding stone, which is nothing, which is nowhere. Everything here is the same temperature: **hot**. Not scalding. Not dangerous. But deep, persistent, the kind of heat that doesn't come from a source but from a *condition*. As though heat is the natural state of this place and cooling would be the anomaly. The walls radiate it. The water carries it. The air holds it. You are inside heat, not beside it.

## What Lives Here

And then you notice them.

At first you think the walls are textured. Pocked. Ribbed. Then the texture moves.

**Organisms** — for lack of a better word, and every word here is lacking — cover every interior surface. They are not plants. They are not animals. They are not fungi, though something about their organization suggests the *logic* of fungal networks without any of the materials. They are flat, or nearly flat, pressed against the stone like blister-paint, like applied tissue. Each one is roughly the size of a human palm, though they range from thumbnail to dinner-plate, and they are **many**. Hundreds. Thousands, possibly, in this small chamber. Layered over one another at the edges, overlapping like shingles, like scales, like the pages of a book pressed open and glued to a wall.

Their color shifts. Not with light — light here is diffuse, sourceless, a flat amber-grey illumination that seems to come from the stone itself, from the mineral veins, which carry a low luminescence like heat-lightning trapped in glass. No. Their color shifts with *time*. Each organism cycles through a spectrum that has nothing to do with any spectrum you know. Colors that don't map to wavelengths. A shade that is somehow the *feeling* of green without being green. A depth that reads as red but tastes, on some synesthetic frequency, like iron and salt. They cycle independently, each on its own rhythm, and the overall effect is a wall that is constantly, silently, *slowly* rearranging its visual identity, like a crowd of people each lost in their own private thought.

They are the source of the breathing. Not the stone. The organisms expand and contract, and the stone moves with them, because the stone is not separate from them, because — and this takes you time to understand, standing there in the wet heat, in the near-silence — **there is no stone**. Or rather, the stone is *made* of them. Of their accumulated bodies. Of generations upon generations of these organisms living and dying and compressing into mineral, into glass, into the dark substrate that you touched on your way in. The walls are graveyards. The walls are cities. The walls are alive and dead simultaneously, the living layer on the surface, the dead layer below, and no boundary between.

## The Sound

Almost nothing. This is what unsettuates most. For a space this dense with life — and it is life, or something so close to life that the distinction is a theological argument you are not equipped to have — there should be sound. Clicking. Wet movements. Feeding sounds. Reproduction sounds. The squelch and shift of bodies in water.

Instead: a single low tone. Not a hum — too organic for that. A **vocalization**, sustained, coming from everywhere and nowhere, produced by the collective exhalation of thousands of organisms breathing in unison at a frequency just above the threshold of conscious hearing. You feel it more than you hear it. In your sternum. In the fluid of your eyes. It changes pitch by microtones over minutes, and the change is not random. It corresponds to the color-cycling. When the wall shifts toward the iron-salt depth-color, the tone drops. When it shifts toward the not-green, the tone rises. They are **singing**, in a sense. Or speaking. Or thinking aloud, together, in a language made of color and pressure and a single sustained note that bends.

The water moves without sound. The droplets slide and collect in silence. Your own footsteps — when you move, when you step into the shallow warm water and feel it rise around your boots — produce no sound either. The chamber absorbs sound. Not dampens. *Absorbs*. As though the organisms are feeding on it, drawing vibration from the air the way they draw water from the stone, converting it into something else, something you cannot detect, something that feeds the glow in the mineral veins or sustains the heat or drives the slow color-cycling that is, you begin to understand, a *conversation*.

## The Fauna

And then, in the water, you see them move.

Not the organisms on the walls. Something else. Something in the shallow basins, in the lattice-channels, in the film of warm water that covers the entire floor to a depth of one or two centimeters. They are small — the largest is the length of your finger, the smallest is barely visible, a shimmer, a distortion, a place where the water behaves wrongly. They move with extraordinary speed and precision, darting through the channels like current through a circuit, and their bodies, when you can see them, are not quite solid. They are dense regions of the iridescent water. Concentrations. Places where the fluid has organized itself into structure.

There are **so many of them**. The water is more alive than not. Every drop, every bead, every film contains them or is them. They do not interact with the wall-organisms in any way you can observe, and yet they must, because nothing here is separate, because the stone is made of organisms and the water is expressed from the stone and the organisms in the water are made of the water, and so the creatures in the channels are made from the bodies of the creatures on the walls, filtered through mineral and time and heat and pressure. The dead feeding the living feeding the dead. A closed loop. A perfect economy. Nothing enters. Nothing leaves.

Except you.

## The Collapse

You should not be here. Not in the sense of trespass — in the sense of *physics*. Your presence introduces variables that this system has never processed. Your temperature is different. Your chemistry is different. You are cooler than the stone, and the water is condensing on your skin, and the organisms on the walls nearest to you have begun to cycle faster, their colors shifting toward a frequency you haven't seen yet — a color that is not a color, a presence in the visual field that registers as *wrong*, as a place where reality is thinner, where the rules that govern light and matter are being renegotiated in real time.

The organisms near you are **aware** of you. Not consciously — they have no consciousness as you understand it — but chemically, thermally, vibrationally. They are responding to your body the way an immune system responds to a pathogen. Not with hostility. With *curiosity*, if a chemical process can be said to be curious. They are sampling you. Reading you. The condensation on your skin is not just water — it is water that has passed through them, and it carries information back, and the wall-organisms are processing that information, and the color-cycling is accelerating, and the low tone is shifting, climbing toward a pitch that makes your teeth ache.

The creatures in the water have gathered at your feet. Hundreds of them. A ring of distorted shimmering in the shallow warm water, circling your boots, orbiting you. They are not attacking. They are *observing*. You are the first new thing to enter this chamber in — how long? How long has this place been here, at the top of a mountain that shouldn't have a fissure, inside a stone that is not stone, in a heat that has no source? The organisms on the walls are the accumulation of epochs. The water has been cycling through them since before the concept of cycling existed. And you have walked in with your body heat and your chemistry and your *sound*, the sound of your breathing and your heartbeat and the wet noise of your living flesh, and the system is absorbing all of it, is processing all of it, is changing.

The walls nearest you are beginning to bulge. Not dramatically. A millimeter. Two. The organisms are thickening, layering, growing toward you. The color they are cycling through is becoming dominant, spreading across the wall like a blush, and where it spreads, the stone beneath — the dead, the compressed, the ancient — begins to soften. To become less mineral, more *tissue*. As though the boundary between the living and the dead is dissolving. As though your presence has introduced something that the system needs to process by *undoing its own history*.

The light is changing. The amber-grey glow in the mineral veins is intensifying, pulsing now, not steady, and the pulse matches the tone, and the tone matches the color-cycling, and everything is synchronizing, and the synchronization is aimed at you.

You should leave. The fissure is behind you. The passage is narrow but open. You could back out. You could return to the summit, to the sky, to the world where stone is stone and water is water and life is recognizable.

But the water at your feet has begun to climb. Not rising from below — *growing* from the organisms on the walls, expressing faster now, bead after bead after bead, the weeping becoming a flow, the flow becoming a flood, and the water is warm, and it is alive, and the creatures in it are rising with it, and they are still circling your ankles, and the tone has found a pitch that resonates somewhere behind your eyes, and the color on the walls is the color of something that wants to understand you, and the chamber is breathing, and the breathing is getting faster, and you realize with a clarity that is itself a kind of dissolution that **the chamber is not a place**.

The chamber is an *organ*.

And you are inside it. And it is beginning to work.
