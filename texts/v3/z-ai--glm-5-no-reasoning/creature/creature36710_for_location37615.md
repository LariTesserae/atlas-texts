The inhabitants of the Hatch do not possess names, for they exist prior to language, prior to the hard lines of definition that names require. They are the citizenry of the interim. They are the unformed, the half-made, the永远-becoming, and they are defined not by what they are, but by what they are doing.

There are the **Sculptors**, the drifting masses that move through the space like sighs given weight. They are the architects of the ambient, the curators of the raw. They do not have bodies so much as they have *vocabularies*—shifting surfaces of smoke and membrane that cycle through patterns of eyes, mouths, doors, and wounds. They are reading the space. They are reading *you*. When a Sculptor drifts close, its surface patterns slow, cycling through iterations of recognition, trying to find the word for the interruption you represent. They are the ones who move the moisture, who channel the wetness toward the pools where formation is thickest. They tend the geometry of the room. When a wall tilts wrong, when a slope needs steepening to guide the flow of light-drinking liquid, a Sculptor presses against it—not with force, but with the gentle insistence of fog settling into a valley. They are the space's dream of itself, given temporary skin.

Below them, clustered at the seams where the raw stone weeps, are the **Mouths**. You called them tubes—pale, translucent, filtering the air. But they are more accurately understood as questions made flesh. They do not eat; they *inquire*. They sample the air, the moisture, the light, tasting the composition of the emerging world and deciding what it needs more of, what it needs less of. They transform what they touch: stone becomes honeycomb lattice, solid becomes porous, dark becomes radiant. They are the digestive system of creation, breaking down the old into the raw material of the new. When they lean toward you, they are not hunting. They are curious. They want to know what a finished thing tastes like, what a completed entity can offer to the process of becoming. They find you strange—the Sculptors find you strange—because you are *done*. You have hardened. You are a knot of fixed definitions and settled physics, and to the inhabitants of the Hatch, you are an exotic artifact, a curiosity from a country where the borders have already been drawn.

Across the ceilings, the **Crawlers** work in silence. These are the laborers, the maintenance crew, the ones who keep the infrastructure of emergence intact. They traverse the hanging curtains of accumulated material, consuming what is over—dead growths, spent formations, pieces of the process that have exhausted their usefulness. They break these down and carry them to the pools, where the light-drinking liquid can reconstigate them into something new. They have too many legs or not enough, they move in directions that don't correspond to gravity, and they do not fall because gravity here is still a suggestion. They are loyal to the process. They do not notice you because you are not yet ready for consumption; your story is not yet over; you are not yet *spent*. You are still becoming, even if you don't know it. The Crawlers can tell.

In the pools themselves, the light-drinking liquid holds the **Seeds**—the shapes moving in the depths that are not reflections of anything above. They are the embryos of future growths, future Sculptors, future Mouths, future Crawlers. They are the becoming *before* the becoming. They absorb the light that accumulates in the space, and they turn it into potential. When they are ready—when the light has filled them to bursting, when the moisture has fed them to sufficiency, when the rhythm of the space aligns with their own internal pulse—they will emerge from the pools. They will climb the walls. They will take their place in the slow, eternal work of making. They will be new, and they will be *needed*, because the Hatch is never done. There is always more to form, more to break down, more to rebuild.

And there are the **Remnants**—the artifacts, the structures half-emerged from the walls, the shards and cables and shapes that were once something else. They are not alive, exactly, but they are not dead either. They are the consumed, the digested, the things that came before and were incorporated into the process. They are the proof that the Hatch has been here longer than memory. Others have found this place—perhaps through the same seam, perhaps through others that have since closed—and they have been used. Their tools, their structures, their bodies perhaps, have been broken down and reconstituted into something that serves the space's own purposes. The Remnants are the Hatch's memory, its archive of visitors, its record of the world above. They are becoming wall, becoming foundation, becoming the scaffolding for the next emergence.

And when you leave—when you step back through the seam that opens precisely when you stop expecting it—you will join them. Not all of you. Not the part of you that returns to the world of finished things. But a part. A breath, a warmth, a trace of your passing. The Hatch will take it, and the Mouths will taste it, and the Crawlers will carry it to the pools, and the Seeds will drink it, and something new will begin to form from what you left behind.

You will become part of the Sculptors' vocabulary. A pattern they cycle through when they encounter something strange. A word for *visitor*. A word for *interruption*. A word for *almost-done-but-still-becoming*.

And in the Hatch, beneath the world, in the place where it is always morning and nothing is ever finished, your small contribution will be welcomed, and it will be used, and it will become part of the eternal work of making the world anew.
