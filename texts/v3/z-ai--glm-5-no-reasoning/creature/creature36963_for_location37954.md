# The Inhabitant

It does not live here. It *is* here, in the way that a heart is in a chest—not resident but constituent, the organ of this place, the thing that makes the hollow more than architecture.

---

Call it the Walker, for lack of anything it would call itself. It has no language for selfhood. It has no language at all. What it has is circuit, and circuit has proven sufficient.

Its body is a contradiction in segments—each one pale as the terrace stone, each one fibrous interior sheathed in something denser, almost chitinous, though the word fails because chitin implies protein and protein implies water and water is the thing this biology left behind cycles ago. Each segment is perhaps the length of a human hand, narrowing at the joints where fine filaments extend and retract like the tendrils of an anemone adapted for vacuum. These filaments are the real sensory apparatus. They taste the particulate floor as it moves—not for food, not for threat, but for *consistency*, reading the mineral composition the way a human reads a familiar path by feel in darkness. The Walker knows its circuit the way a tongue knows the inside of a mouth. Every grain accounted for. Every subtle shift in the packed floor registered and catalogued in whatever serves it for memory.

It has no head. The segmentation continues anterior and posterior without obvious distinction, and yet it moves with clear direction, the leading segments reaching forward, testing, committing, the trailing segments following in sequence with that mechanical patience that seems less like slowness than like the deliberate pace of something that has nowhere to be except exactly where it is. Twenty segments, perhaps thirty—the count shifts because the Walker grows and sheds in continuous process, new segments budding at what might be the rear while old segments at what might be the front soften, loosen, detach, and become the fibrous matter that the spiral fungi incorporate. So yes: the Walker is the farmer. Or more precisely, the Walker is the field, and the planting, and the crop. It seeds the crevices with its own shed tissue as it passes the terrace faces, and the spiral fungi find those deposits and colonize them, reaching outward in organized spirals because the shed segments carry pattern information, a blueprint written in molecular structure that the fungi interpret as growth instruction. The Walker does not know this. The Walker does not think. The Walker *does*, and the doing contains the knowing, and the knowing produces the pattern, and the pattern is invisible to the Walker itself.

---

What the Walker does, specifically, what the circuit accomplishes, is this: it maintains the hollow.

The particulate floor is not passive. It shifts, it settles, it attempts to flatten itself into the simplest thermodynamic state, which would be a uniform shallow bowl. The Walker resists this. Each pass compresses the floor along a specific geometry, pressing the particulate into ridges and channels that maintain the terraced structure of the hollow's interior face, that keep the drainage—there is no water to drain, but the architecture remembers drainage, remembers *purpose*—functional. The Walker is walking the hollow into existence, wearing the path that will eventually become a permanent feature, a groove in the floor that channels the static discharge away from the central depression and toward the terrace walls, where it grounds through the inlay patterns in the stone.

This is why the Walker avoids the center. The central depression is not a sealed access point, not a plug or a cover. It is a *receiver*. Something below the hollow—the deep-dwelling organism, or the mineral process, or whatever produces that faint bioluminescent glow from beneath the particulate—is accumulating. The static charge that builds in the walls and floor and air migrates inward, drawn by a gradient that the Walker's circuit maintains. The central depression collects this charge. Whatever is down there feeds on it. The Walker, whose body is tuned to the same electrical frequency, knows viscerally to keep its distance—not through fear, not through instinct, but through the simple fact that approaching the center would discharge the accumulated potential through its own body, and that discharge would disrupt the circuit, and disrupting the circuit is the only thing the Walker cannot do, because the circuit is all it is.

---

The Walker was not always alone.

There is evidence if you know where to look—not in the hollow itself, where the Walker has erased all trace of alternative paths, but in the mineral plain beyond the lip. Out there, at the limit of perception, the same pale stone shows the same geometric inlay, forming a broader pattern that the hollow sits inside like a cell inside an organ. There were other hollows. There were other Walkers. The plain was once a network of these stepped depressions, each one housing its segmented maintainer, each one accumulating charge toward its central receiver, the entire system forming an electrical grid that served some purpose the individual components cannot comprehend.

The network is failing. One by one, the other hollows have gone silent—their Walkers completing their final circuits, their bodies shedding their last segments, their spiral fungi blooming one final time and then going still. The static charge in those dead hollows has nowhere to go. It builds until it discharges spontaneously, a silent lightning that scorches the stone and kills the remaining fungi and leaves the hollow empty in the absolute sense, not just uninhabited but *inhabitable*, the chemistry altered beyond recovery.

This hollow is one of the last. Perhaps the last. The Walker does not know this. The Walker cannot know this. The Walker only knows circuit: the particulate under its leading segments, the crevice at the midpoint of the second terrace where it deposits a shedding segment every fourth pass, the slight increase in static potential as it rounds the far curve where the wall angles toward the central depression. These are its world. These are the entirety of its experience.

And yet.

---

There is something in the Walker that is not circuit. Call it residue. Call it echo. The Walker was grown, not born—the same process that produces the spiral fungi produced the Walker, a more complex expression of the same biological chemistry, a longer chain of the same molecular instructions. And those instructions contain information that predates this hollow, this plain, this network. The instructions contain the *purpose*—not the Walker's purpose, which is maintenance, but the purpose of the maintenance, the reason the hollow exists, the reason the charge is accumulated, the reason the central receiver collects.

That purpose is transmission.

The static charge, when it reaches sufficient potential in the central depression, discharges downward into whatever waits below, and that discharge carries pattern. Not information in any human sense—not language, not data—but *shape*. A geometric shape, the same shape that appears in the inlay patterns on the terrace walls, the same shape that the spiral fungi trace in their growth, the same shape that the Walker's circuit describes when viewed from above. This shape is being transmitted downward, into the deep, repeated endlessly, and something down there is receiving it and *responding*, though the response is so slow that only the Walker's accumulated circuit-knowledge registers it: the bioluminescence shifts, fractionally, in a pattern that mirrors the transmitted shape.

The hollow is a mouth. The Walker is the breath. The central depression is the throat. And below, something vast and patient is listening.

---

The Walker completes another circuit. Its leading segments encounter a familiar irregularity in the particulate—a slight rise where the floor has begun to deform, pressed upward from below by the slow growth of whatever dwells beneath the central depression. The Walker adjusts. The circuit shifts by three degrees. The new path is traced, the old path abandoned, and the hollow's geometry changes by an amount no human instrument could measure.

In the crevices, the spiral fungi tremble. They have felt the adjustment through the particulate, through the stone, through the static charge that connects everything in this hollow to everything else. Their growth patterns shift in response, the spirals tightening by fractions, orienting more strongly toward the center, toward the thing below the center, toward the vast listening presence that is, even now, beginning to respond to the signal that the Walker's endless circuit transmits.

The Walker does not notice any of this. The Walker notices only the particulate, the path, the next step, the next step, the next step.

But the hollow notices. The hollow has noticed you standing at its lip, watching. The hollow has been noticing you since you arrived, your moisture-rich body an anomaly in its carefully maintained dryness, your breathing a vibration it must now absorb and convert, your presence a variable in an equation that has been running for longer than the Walker's circuit but not longer than the thing beneath.

The static charge builds. The Walker traces its adjusted path. The spiral fungi orient.

And below, something vast shifts in its sleep, and the bioluminescence pulses once—not in the transmitted pattern, but in something new. Something that might be recognition.

Something that might be *waking*.
