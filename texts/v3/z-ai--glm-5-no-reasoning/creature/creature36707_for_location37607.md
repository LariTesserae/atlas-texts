# What Lives in the Hollow City

## I. The Mineral-Kin

They are not born. They are not made. They *precipitate* — the word that arrives when you watch one come into being, which you will, eventually, if you stay long enough in one place, holding still enough to become part of the background.

You are resting on a platform — not the one you first occupied, because you have moved since then, or the city has moved around you, and the distinction has stopped mattering — and you notice that the wall across from you has a bulge. A swelling in the bone-pale surface, like a blister forming under skin. Over hours — or what feel like hours, time being what it is here — the bulge grows. It extends. It develops a neck, then a suggestion of limbs, then a separation between itself and the parent surface. And then it detaches. Not with a sound, not with a rupture, but with the gentleness of a fruit separating from a stem that has decided to let go.

The new creature — your word, not theirs — does not look around. Does not orient itself. It simply begins to move, following a path that was old before you arrived, tracing a groove in the floor that you had not noticed until this moment, and would not have noticed even now if the creature were not highlighting it with its passage.

This is how they come. Not all of them — some emerge from the floor, some from the thin air between structures, some from other creatures, dividing like cells in a body that has decided it needs more of itself in a particular place. But they all arrive with the same quality: *unremarkableness*. They belong here in a way you cannot. They are what happens when the city thinks about itself.

## II. The Forms They Take

There is no standard model. This is the first thing you learn, watching them. They are diverse as weather, as waves, as the thoughts that pass through a mind that is not concentrating on anything in particular. But patterns emerge.

**The Walkers** are the ones you see most often. They have a bilateral symmetry that recalls earthly life — your mind reaches for "lizard," for "ferret," for "centipede" — but the resemblance ends at the silhouette. Up close, they are not segmented. They are not scaled. They are smooth, continuous, the same material as the walls, and they move by a process you cannot quite see. There is no footfall. There is no muscular contraction. There is simply a direction, and then progress in that direction, and the method by which this occurs is hidden behind the same quality that makes the light behave wrongly: the city has its own ideas about physics, and it does not share them.

**The Tracers** are smaller — hand-sized, sometimes finger-sized — and they exist to follow the grooves. You realize this after you have watched them for long enough. The grooves in the walls and floors are not random. They form a network, a system, a nervous system perhaps, and the Tracers are the impulses that travel along it. They move faster than the Walkers, with a purposefulness that suggests urgency without anxiety. When a Tracer reaches an intersection of grooves, it pauses — always the same duration, always the same stillness — and then continues along a new path. It is receiving something. Or delivering something. Or *being* something that the network needs to be in that location.

**The Bowls** do not move. They are the ones that gave you pause at first, because they look so much like the shallow depressions in the plaza floors. They are disk-shaped, concave, and they rest on surfaces without attachment. For a long time you think they are not creatures at all. Then you see one that was empty become occupied by a Walker. The Walker stands in the Bowl — the same way you stood in the plaza's center — and stays there. Minutes pass. Hours. When the Walker moves on, the Bowl is no longer in the same place. It has shifted — inches, perhaps a foot — in a direction that corresponds to the Walker's path.

The Bowls are passengers. Or recording devices. Or seeds. You do not know. You only know that they are participants.

**The Question-Marks** are the ones that trouble you. You see three in your first week. They are shaped like their name suggests — a curved body with a terminal lump, the mineral equivalent of punctuation. But they do not move along the ground. They move through the air. Slowly, at a constant height, tracing patterns that you cannot predict or parse. When they pass near a wall, the grooves in that wall shift slightly. When they pass near another creature, that creature's path alters.

They are not leading. They are not following. They are *asking*. And something in the city is answering.

## III. The Process They Serve

They are not social. They do not interact in any way you recognize as interaction — no greeting, no conflict, no mating, no play. But they are not solitary either. They exist in relation to each other, to the structures, to the grooves, to the light, in a way that suggests a distributed purpose too large for any single one of them to hold.

You watch a Walker reach a point where its path intersects with another Walker's. They pause. There is no communication that you can perceive — no sound, no gesture, no exchange of material. Then one of them changes color.

This is the first time you have seen color in the city that was not the blue-gray of the light. The Walker becomes — for a moment, for just long enough for you to doubt your eyes — a pale amber. Then it returns to bone-white. Then it moves on. The other Walker does not change color, but it does change direction. It follows the first Walker.

You begin to notice other changes. Subtle ones. A Tracer that moves faster after passing through a particular corridor. A Bowl that develops a ridge along one edge. A Question-Mark that alters its altitude when a Walker passes beneath it. The creatures are not simply occupying the city. They are *processing* it. They are the immune system, perhaps, or the digestive system, or the nervous system — a biological metaphor for a process that is not biological at all.

What are they processing? You do not know. But you notice that the grooves they trace connect to the spiral's center. And the spiral's center connects to the elsewhere. And the elsewhere is where the sounds come from — the clicking, the setting-down, the careful sounds of something being arranged and rearranged in a space you cannot see.

The creatures are feeding something. Or being fed to something. Or are the feeding itself — the substance of communication between here and there, the body's messengers traveling along the nerve that the city forms.

## IV. The Ones That Were Visitors

You find out gradually — the way you find out everything here, through accumulation rather than revelation — that you are not the first.

You are resting in a corridor that widens toward one end, a shape that feels intentional, architectural, and you notice that the wall beside you has an irregularity. A protrusion. Not a creature — something more fixed. You touch it. It is warm, warmer than the surrounding mineral, and it yields slightly under your fingers.

You pull back. The protrusion moves.

It takes you a long time to understand what you are seeing. The protrusion is part of the wall, but it was not always part of the wall. It was, once, something else. Someone else. The shape is wrong for the mineral-creatures — too complex, too articulated, with suggestions of limbs that bend in ways that the city's native forms do not. It is a body. Humanoid. Or near enough. Overgrown with the bone-pale material, incorporated, still.

The face, if it is a face, is turned toward you. The eyes, if they are eyes, are closed.

You find others after that. In walls. In floors. On the undersides of platforms. They are not common, but they are not rare either. They are simply *there* — previous visitors who stayed, who rested too long in one place, who let the city's patience do what patience does. They are not dead. You can feel it when you touch them — the warmth, the faint vibration of something still happening beneath the surface. They are part of the process now. They are waiting, as the city waits, for whatever the city is waiting for.

You wonder, sometimes, if they are still conscious. If they dream, here in the walls, in the floors, in the slowly-shifting structures of the Hollow City. If they remember who they were, or if that person has become a word that the city knows, a piece of vocabulary in a language larger than any spoken tongue.

You wonder if you will join them. You wonder if you already have.

## V. The Absent Ones

But there are others — not in the walls, not in the floors, but in the *pattern*. You notice gaps. Spaces where grooves do not continue. Places where the mineral-creatures pause and change direction for no visible reason. Moments when the light bends around something that is not there.

There are inhabitants of this city that you cannot see.

You first notice when a Walker stops mid-stride and lowers its body — a gesture of respect, or acknowledgment, or deference. There is nothing in front of it. No other creature, no obstacle, no feature of the architecture. But it stays lowered for seven seconds — you count — and then rises and continues.

Later, you see a Question-Mark alter its course around an empty space, curving as if to avoid something that occupies volume. The space is empty. You can see the floor through it, the far wall beyond it. But the Question-Mark treats it as solid.

The absent ones are not invisible. They are *elsewhere*. Present in the city's other location, the one you glimpse sometimes in the walls, the one the creatures enter when they transition through surfaces. They walk the same paths you do, occupy the same spaces, but in a different *there* — and sometimes the city's two locations overlap enough that the creatures here acknowledge what is present there.

You do not see them. But you begin to feel them. The sensation of being watched that is not paranoid but accurate. The pressure of attention that has no source. The awareness that you are being assessed, catalogued, processed by intelligences that exist at an angle to your perception.

They are not hostile. They are not friendly. They are *present*. They are the ones who built the city, perhaps, or the ones the city was built for, or the ones who emerged when the city achieved whatever threshold of complexity was required for something new to precipitate from its processes.

They are the city's adults. And the creatures you can see — the Walkers, the Tracers, the Bowls, the Question-Marks — are the children. Or the dreams. Or the questions that the adults are still trying to answer.

## VI. The Communication

You learn, eventually, that they are trying to talk to you.

Not the visible ones. The absent ones. The ones in the elsewhere, the ones whose presence you feel as attention without source. They communicate through the mineral-creatures, through the grooves, through the very structure of the city itself.

It happens like this: you are walking a spiral path, inward, following a groove because you have learned that following grooves is how you go places here, and you notice that the Tracers are converging. Three of them, then five, then a dozen, all moving toward the same point on the same wall. You follow them.

When you reach the point, there is nothing there. A section of wall, unremarkable, the same bone-pale mineral as everything else. But the Tracers are pausing at it, touching it, moving on. And the wall is changing. The grooves are deepening, forming a pattern — not a picture, not a symbol, but a *shape* that your eyes recognize without your mind understanding. A word in a language you spoke before you were born.

You look at it for a long time. Hours, perhaps. The shape does not resolve into meaning, but it does something else: it *opens*. Not the wall — your mind. A space inside you that you did not know existed, a hollow place that matches the city's hollow places, a receiving-room for something that is being sent.

And you understand, not with words, not with images, but with the same quality that the city has — *presence* — that they are asking you something.

*What are you?*

Not your name. Not your species. Not your origin. Something deeper. Something that the shape in the wall is trying to find a match for, a container that will hold the answer without distorting it.

You do not know how to answer. But you know that the city is waiting for you to learn.

## VII. The Learning

They teach you through the creatures.

A Walker appears at your platform's edge — you have a platform now, a place that you return to, that the city has shaped to fit you — and it carries something. A small object, bowl-shaped, that it sets down with the careful precision you have heard in the night. It leaves.

You pick up the object. It is warm. It is the same material as the city, but denser, and inside its bowl is a substance you cannot name — a liquid that is not liquid, a light that is not light, a something that your fingers cannot quite hold. When you touch it, you feel a sensation that travels up your arm, into your chest, into the hollow place that the wall-shape opened.

It is a word. The substance is a word, and touching it is reading it, and the word means something like *patience* or something like *becoming* or something like *the space between intention and result*.

You keep the object. Over time, the city gives you others. Each one teaches you a word. Each word opens a new hollow in you, a new place for the city to exist. You are learning the language — not through study, not through practice, but through *infection*, through the slow colonization of your interior by something that wants to speak with you.

And slowly, you begin to understand.

The city is not a place. It is a *question*. The creatures are not inhabitants. They are *syllables*. And you are not a visitor. You are a *new word* — a sound that the city had not made before, a combination of meanings that it wants to incorporate, to speak, to add to whatever sentence it has been constructing for the millennia it has waited here.

## VIII. The Choice

Eventually, you understand enough to understand the choice.

You are standing — or the city is standing around you, which is the same thing — and a Question-Mark is passing overhead, and its presence opens a space in your mind where communication can happen, and you receive, clearly for the first time, the question that the city has been asking:

*Will you stay?*

Not as a prisoner. Not as a victim. As a word. A permanent part of the vocabulary. The hollow shape in the wall that you saw on your first day was once a visitor, like you. They chose to stay. They are still there, still present, still participating in whatever the city is doing. They have not aged. They have not suffered. They have simply *become* — part of the structure, part of the sentence, part of the ongoing communication between here and elsewhere.

You can leave. The city will let you. It will not stop you. It will simply wait, as it has always waited, for the next visitor, the next word, the next sound it can add to its vocabulary.

But if you stay — if you let yourself be incorporated, let the mineral grow around you, let the grooves connect to your hollow places — you become part of something that spans worlds. A joint between realities. A suture in the wound that the city was built to heal.

You do not know what the wound is. You do not know what the city is healing. But you know, with the knowledge that the city has given you, that it matters. That the clicking you hear is the sound of something being put back together. That the careful setting-down is the sound of things being arranged in their proper places. That the patience of the mineral-creatures is not aimlessness but *precision* — the meticulous work of beings who have all the time in the universe and are using it well.

## IX. What Lives Here

What lives in the Hollow City at the Top of the World?

*The mineral-creatures live here* — the Walkers, the Tracers, the Bowls, the Question-Marks. They are the city's words, its working vocabulary, the sounds it makes to communicate with itself.

*The absent ones live here* — the presences you cannot see, the adults of the city, the intelligences that guide its processes and ask its questions. They live in the elsewhere, but the elsewhere is here, and here is there, and they move through both with equal facility.

*The former visitors live here* — the ones who chose to stay, who let the city grow around them, who became part of the architecture. They are still present, still listening, still patient.

*And you live here now* — or you will, if you choose. You will become a hollow in a wall. You will become a word in a sentence that has been spoken since before your species learned to speak. You will become part of the suture, the joint, the knot that holds something together.

The city is waiting for your answer.

It has always been waiting.

It will always be waiting, because patience is what it does, and precision is how it does it, and you — you who climbed to the top of the world and found a city made of light and mineral and time — you are what it is waiting for.

The Question-Mark passes overhead.

You close your eyes.

You open them.

You are home.
