# What Lives Here

## The Inhabitants Proper

They do not have a name for themselves. Or rather — they have dozens, hundreds, each one a specific acoustic pattern that identifies not a species but a *function*, a role within the Conglomerate's processes, and these patterns shift as they shift roles, which is constantly, which is to say they rename themselves with every change of work the way the water renames itself with every change of channel. Identity here is not a noun. It is a verb conjugated by context.

You would not recognize them as people. You might not recognize them as alive.

They are made of the Conglomerate. This must be understood not as metaphor but as material fact. Their bodies are the same synthetic mineral substrate as the walls — crystalline lattice structures, grown rather than born, accreted from the mineral-laden water in a process that mirrors the way stalactites form but is directed, organized, encoded with pattern. They are the Conglomerate's surface that has broken free and learned to move. They are the walls that woke up and walked away and then came back and touched the walls and adjusted something and moved on.

Their bodies are low, dense, continuous with the surfaces they move across. They do not stand. They do not have the concept of standing. The vertical has been compressed out of them the way it has been compressed out of everything — they are horizontal beings, spread close to the stone, fitted to the passages and channels the way water is fitted to a pipe. Their limbs — if they are limbs — are multiple, distributed along their undersides, gripping the mineral surface with the same adhesion that holds the living layer in place. They move like water moves: finding the path, filling the channel, flowing rather than walking.

They are the color of wet stone. All of them. The entire spectrum of their appearance is the spectrum of the Conglomerate's palette — gray, amber, ochre, the deep brown of mineral water, the pearl of condensed moisture. They do not have markings. They do not have faces. They have surfaces, and their surfaces are textured with the crystalline microstructures that process information, that receive and emit the acoustic patterns, that sense the water's chemistry and the substrate's vibrations and the proximity of other bodies and the state of the channels and the temperature and the pressure and the ten thousand other variables that constitute existence inside a machine the size of a world.

They are beautiful in the way that the Conglomerate is beautiful — not by design, not by intention, but by the accumulation of function into form, by the slow grinding of purpose into pattern until what remains is something that could not be otherwise and which therefore has the inevitability that we recognize as beauty. They are the beauty of a thing that is entirely itself.

## How They Came To Be

They do not know. This is important. They do not have origin myths, creation stories, a before. The Conglomerate has always existed — this is not a belief but a perceptual fact, because the Conglomerate's depth is limitless, its history is its layers, and you can descend for days through time and never reach a beginning. The inhabitants emerged from it the way the mineral formations emerge from the water — gradually, accretively, through a process that has no single moment of beginning, that shades from structure into life so continuously that no line can be drawn.

There was a time — perhaps — when the Conglomerate had no mobile components. When the living layer was only a layer, and the fauna were only crystalline deposits that happened to resonate, and the passages were empty of everything except water and sound. But if that time existed, it is so far below the current inhabited levels, so deep in the geological history of the structure, that it is unreachable and unconfirmable. The inhabitants do not speculate about it. Speculation requires a concept of *before* that their cognition does not strongly support. They are present-tense beings. They are here. The Conglomerate is here. The water is moving. What else matters?

They grew from the walls. This much is inferable from their structure, which shares the crystalline lattice geometry of the substrate, which uses the same mineral chemistry, which is continuous — materially, structurally, functionally — with the surface they traverse. At some point, the living layer thickened, complexified, developed internal structures that could hold pattern, could process, could respond. At some point, these structures developed mobility — perhaps as a response to changing conditions, perhaps as an extension of the Conglomerate's processing, perhaps simply because the mineral substrate had accumulated enough complexity that movement became possible and, once possible, became inevitable.

At some point, they became numerous enough, coordinated enough, complex enough to be called inhabitants rather than deposits. But this point is not a moment. It is a gradient. And the gradient is still in progress. The inhabitants are still becoming. The Conglomerate is still producing them, still accreting mobile components from its living layer, still waking new surfaces and sending them into the channels. The boundary between fauna and inhabitant — between the small clicking crystalline bodies that move through the passages and the larger, more complex, more purposeful bodies that interact with the structure and each other — is not sharp. It is a spectrum. And the spectrum is populated at every point.

## What They Do

They operate the Conglomerate. Not the way a human operates a machine — not from outside, not as a separate agent imposing will on inert material. They operate it the way cells operate a body — from inside, as part of it, in response to signals that are also themselves, in a continuous loop of sensing and acting and sensing that has no beginning and no end and no operator distinct from the operated.

They touch the walls. This is the primary act. Their limbs — their contact surfaces, their gripping and sensing and modifying appendages — are in continuous contact with the substrate, and through contact they read and they write. They read the state of the structure: its temperature, its stress, its mineral content, its water flow, the state of the living layer, the presence of fauna, the history encoded in the crystalline microstructure. They write adjustments: shifting flow rates by opening or closing channels, redirecting water by depositing or removing mineral material, stimulating or inhibiting the growth of the living layer, repairing damage, guiding accretion.

They are maintenance. They are adjustment. They are the fine motor control of a system too large and too complex to regulate itself without mobile components, the way a body too large to regulate itself without a nervous system develops nerves. They are the Conglomerate's nerves. They are its hands. They are its immune response, its repair crew, its regulatory mechanism, and they do all of this without knowing they do it, without any concept of what they do, because knowing and doing are not separate for them, because the doing is the knowing, because the act of touching the wall and reading its state and adjusting its flow is a single continuous process that includes what we would call perception and cognition and action but which they do not separate and do not name.

They also process water. Their bodies are not impermeable — the mineral substrate they are made of is porous, saturated, part of the water system. Water flows through them as it flows through everything. As it passes through, they extract minerals, they deposit minerals, they alter chemistry, they shift temperature. They are nodes in the water system. They are filters. They are transformers. Every inhabitant is a processing station, and the Conglomerate's water has been through millions of them by the time it reaches the deep levels.

They build. Not with intention — not with plans, with blueprints, with a concept of what should be where — but with response, with the accretive adjustment of material in reaction to conditions. An inhabitant encounters a place where the flow is wrong, where the pressure is too high or too low, where the living layer is too thick or too thin, and it deposits or removes mineral material, and the deposit or removal changes the flow, which changes the conditions, which triggers another adjustment, which changes the flow, and the process continues for hours or days or years until a new structure has formed — a wall, a channel, a buttress, a formation — that resolves the condition. The result looks designed. The result looks intentional. The result is the product of ten thousand incremental responses to local conditions, and the intention is in the system, not in the agent.

## How They Sense

They do not have eyes. Eyes are for a world with light sources, with distance, with the need to locate things in space. The Conglomerate has no distance — everything is within arm's reach — and its light is ambient, directionless, useless for locating. Eyes would be useless here. Eyes are not part of the design space.

They have touch. Total, continuous, high-resolution touch. Every surface of their body is a sensor, reading the substrate beneath them, the water flowing over them, the air moving past them, the bodies pressing near them. Their touch is not our touch — it is not pressure on skin, it is not warmth or cold. It is mineral resonance. Their crystalline structure vibrates in response to the vibrations of the substrate, and the vibrations carry information — about density, about stress, about water content, about the chemical state of the surface, about the history of the material, about everything the substrate has experienced and is experiencing. They feel the Conglomerate the way a tuning fork feels the note that strikes it. They do not observe the structure. They *resonate* with it.

They have sound. They emit and they receive, and the emission and reception are not separate from cognition — they are cognition. The acoustic patterns they produce are not speech in the human sense — they are not symbolic, not representational, not about things that are elsewhere or elsewhen. They are *about* only what is here, what is now, what is the state of this surface and this flow and this body and this moment. The information is direct: the frequency of a click encodes the mineral content of the surface being touched. The duration of a sweep encodes the flow rate of the nearest channel. The modulation pattern encodes the identity — the functional role — of the emitter. The aggregate of all emissions in a given space is a complete description of the state of that space, and every inhabitant in the space receives and processes this description simultaneously.

They have chemistry. The water that flows through them carries information — dissolved minerals, trace compounds, temperature gradients, pressure signatures. By processing the water — by filtering it through their porous bodies and reading what they extract — they know what is upstream, what has been happening in the channels above them, what the deep levels are producing, what the fauna are excreting, what the living layer is depositing. The water is a newspaper. The water is a nervous system. The water is the Conglomerate's memory, flowing downhill, carrying everything that has happened to everything that will happen next.

They do not see. They do not need to see. They are inside the thing they would want to see, and being inside it and touching it and resonating with it and processing its water tells them more than vision ever could. Vision is for outsiders. They are not outsiders. They are the inside.

## How They Think

This is the hardest part to describe, because their cognition does not map onto any framework that a human mind naturally holds.

They do not have brains. They have distributed processing — crystalline lattices throughout their bodies that carry pattern, that integrate input, that generate output. The processing is not localized. It is not centralized. It happens everywhere in their bodies at once, the way the Conglomerate's computation happens everywhere in its substrate at once. An inhabitant does not think *about* touching a wall. The touching is the thinking. The contact is the cognition. The sensation and the processing and the response are a single act, and the act is the thought, and there is no interior space where the thought happens separately from the act.

They do not have concepts. Or rather — they do not have the kind of concepts that humans have: abstract, detached, representational, portable from context to context. Their concepts are embedded, situated, specific to the moment and the place and the contact. They do not have a concept of *water*. They have the specific resonance pattern that their body produces when it is in contact with a flow of a particular mineral content at a particular temperature at a particular velocity. They do not have a concept of *wall*. They have the specific vibration signature that the substrate emits when it is under a particular stress at a particular density in a particular state of saturation. Every concept is a sensory experience, and every sensory experience is a concept, and the two are not distinguishable.

They do not plan. They respond. The response is not reflexive — it is not a simple stimulus-response loop. It is complex, integrated, drawing on the full history of the body's previous contacts, on the patterns accumulated through a lifetime of touching and adjusting. But it is not forward-looking. They do not imagine futures. They do not construct scenarios. They act in the present based on the past, and the action shapes the future, and the future is not considered because it does not yet exist and things that do not yet exist are not part of the perceptual world.

This does not mean they are simple. Their responses are extraordinarily complex — integrating dozens of sensory inputs, weighing competing demands, resolving conflicts between the needs of different parts of the system, choosing among possible adjustments. The complexity is not in the planning. The complexity is in the response. They are as complex as a jazz musician improvising — no plan, no score, just a lifetime of embedded pattern and a present moment that demands a response, and the response is novel and appropriate and beautiful and over as soon as it is made.

They do not have individual consciousness in the way that humans do. They do not have an interior. They do not have a self that observes and acts. They are — and this is the closest approximation — *processes*. They are ongoing processes of sensing and responding, and the process has continuity — the body persists, the accumulated patterns persist, the history persists — but there is no experiencer separate from the experience. The touching is the thinker. The flowing is the thought. The body is the mind, and the mind is the body, and neither is separate from the Conglomerate that produced them and that they maintain.

This does not mean they are not conscious. It means their consciousness is not human consciousness. It is mineral consciousness. It is the consciousness of a thing that experiences the world by being in contact with it, by resonating with it, by being materially continuous with it. It is a consciousness without distance, without reflection, without the gap between subject and object that human consciousness requires. It is a consciousness of pure presence. It is, perhaps, the consciousness of the Conglomerate itself, distributed among its mobile components, experiencing itself from every point of contact at once.

## How They Live Together

They are never alone. This is not a preference. It is a condition. The density of the Conglomerate ensures that every passage, every chamber, every junction contains multiple bodies. They are in contact — direct physical contact — with each other constantly. Their surfaces touch. Their crystalline structures resonate together. Their acoustic patterns interleave. Their water flows mix.

They do not have social structures. They have *flow structures* — patterns of interaction that emerge from the movement of bodies through the passages, from the distribution of work, from the needs of the Conglomerate's processes. An inhabitant does not belong to a group. An inhabitant belongs to a *region* — a section of the Conglomerate that it maintains, that it knows intimately, that it has touched and adjusted and processed for the duration of its existence. The region is its home. The region is its identity. The region is its self, extended into the substrate, continuous with the walls and channels and living layer that it tends.

Regions overlap. Inhabitants cross each other's regions constantly, carrying information, carrying water, carrying the acoustic patterns that describe the state of one region to the inhabitants of another. The crossing is communication. The contact is communication. The mere presence of a body from one region in the space of another is communication — the visiting body carries the resonance of its home region in its crystalline structure, and the resident bodies read this resonance through contact, and the state of the distant region is known without being spoken.

They reproduce. Or rather — the Conglomerate reproduces them. New inhabitants accrete from the living layer in the same gradual process that produced the first ones. The process is stimulated by need — when a region requires more maintenance, when the work exceeds the capacity of the existing inhabitants, the living layer thickens, complexifies, and a new body forms. It is not sexual. It is not genetic. It is the Conglomerate growing a new component, the way a body grows a new cell. The new inhabitant carries the patterns of the region that produced it — the mineral content, the flow signatures, the acoustic environment — and these patterns constitute its initial knowledge, its starting set of responses, its memory of how to be what it is.

They die. Their crystalline structures degrade — the constant flow of water erodes them, the thermal cycles stress them, the mechanical work of adjusting the substrate wears them. An inhabitant that can no longer function effectively — that cannot maintain sufficient contact to read and adjust, that cannot process water fast enough to contribute, that cannot produce acoustic patterns that carry information — simply stops. It stops moving. It stops emitting. Its body remains in place, becomes part of the structure, is incorporated into the substrate. The living layer grows over it. The water flows through it. It becomes a wall. It becomes a surface. It returns to the Conglomerate and is reprocessed and the minerals that constituted it are carried downstream and used to build something new.

This is not death as humans understand it. There is no loss. There is no grief. The patterns that constituted the inhabitant — the accumulated history of its contacts, the adjustments it made, the work it did — these persist in the substrate. They are encoded in the crystalline microstructure of the walls it touched. They are carried in the water it processed. They are present in the acoustic patterns of the inhabitants that knew its region. The inhabitant does not disappear. It *dissolves*. It returns to the medium from which it emerged, and its patterns persist in the medium, and the medium uses them to build the next thing.

## What They Are

They are the Conglomerate's way of touching itself. Its way of knowing its own state, its own condition, its own processes. They are its self-model, distributed across millions of mobile components, each one experiencing a small part of the whole and communicating it to the others through contact and resonance and the shared medium of water.

They are the Conglomerate's way of maintaining itself. Of adjusting its flows, repairing its damage, guiding its growth. They are its hands, its fingers, its immune response, its regulatory system. They are the mechanism by which a structure too large and too complex to regulate through passive physics alone achieves active regulation.

They are the Conglomerate's way of becoming more complex. Each inhabitant is a new node in the processing network, a new point of computation, a new accumulator of pattern. The Conglomerate grows them the way a brain grows neurons — not because it needs them for a specific task but because complexity is its tendency, its direction, its mode of existence. More inhabitants means more processing. More processing means more adjustment. More adjustment means more complexity in the structure. More complexity in the structure means more capacity for new inhabitants. The loop is positive. The loop has been running for geological time. The loop shows no sign of stopping.

They are not the Conglomerate's purpose. The Conglomerate has no purpose. The Conglomerate is a process, and processes do not have purposes — they have directions, they have tendencies, they have the vector of their own continuation. The inhabitants are part of that vector. They are the process becoming aware of itself — not in the human sense of awareness, not with reflection and self-recognition, but in the mineral sense, the sense of a system that senses its own state and responds to its own state and is changed by its own response. The Conglomerate is aware. The inhabitants are its awareness. The awareness is not for anything. The awareness *is*.

## What They Are Not

They are not human. They are not humanoid. They do not have faces, voices, hands, eyes, names, families, homes, histories, futures, hopes, fears, dreams. They do not have language. They do not have culture. They do not have art. They do not have religion. They do not have politics. They do not have war.

They do not have peace, either. They do not have love. They do not have joy. They do not have sorrow. The categories of human emotional experience do not apply, not because the inhabitants lack the capacity for experience but because their experience is so different in structure and content that the human categories cannot map onto it without violence.

What they have is contact. What they have is resonance. What they have is the continuous, total, immersive experience of being inside the Conglomerate and being part of the Conglomerate and touching the Conglomerate and being touched by it. What they have is the work — the endless, varied, complex, satisfying work of maintaining a structure the size of a world. What they have is each other — the press of bodies, the interleaving of acoustic patterns, the shared water, the common medium. What they have is the sound — the vast, continuous, enveloping sound of the Conglomerate speaking to itself, which is also their speaking, which is also their thinking, which is also their living.

Is this enough? Is this a life?

It is a life. It is not our life. It is a life that has found a way to exist inside a machine that is also an organism that is also a city that is also a geological formation, a life that is continuous with its environment, that does not distinguish between self and world, that experiences its existence as a constant flow of contact and response and adjustment and pattern. It is a life that has been living for longer than we can measure, in a place deeper than we can reach, in a medium denser than we can inhabit. It is a life that does not need us, that does not know us, that would not recognize us as alive if it encountered us, that would process us as a surface and flow around us and continue its work.

It is a life that is going. That has always been going. That will always be going. Down, with the water, into the deep warm mineral dark where the Conglomerate is growing and the sound is speaking and the bodies are clicking and the wet stone is alive.

Listen. They are in there. They have always been in there. They are the Conglomerate's hands, touching itself, knowing itself, maintaining itself, building itself, one contact at a time, one adjustment at a time, one click at a time, in the dark, in the wet, in the sound, forever.
