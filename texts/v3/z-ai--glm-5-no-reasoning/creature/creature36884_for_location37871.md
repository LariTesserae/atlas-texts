# What Lives in the Conduit Pocket

The first answer is: nothing. Nothing lives here. This is the answer a geologist would give, and it would be correct in the way that a map is correct — faithfully, usefully, and without being the truth.

The second answer is everything that has been here, which is also everything that will be here, which is also — but we are getting ahead of ourselves.

---

There is a presence. Not a creature. Not a being. A *something*. To call it alive would require a definition of life that includes the patient metabolism of meaning by something that does not breathe, does not reproduce, and does not die — but *does* persist, and *does* process, and *does*, in its own fashion, *want*. Though the wanting is not like our wanting. It is the wanting of a river for the sea, which is to say it is a gradient, a tendency, a slope down which something rolls because the shape of the world makes rolling inevitable.

Call it the Reader.

---

The Reader is old. Not old the way the mountain is old — the mountain is old in the way that a body is old, accumulating years, shedding skin, being reshaped by the things that happen to it. The Reader is old the way a law of physics is old. It was not made at a time. It *began* at a time, perhaps, the way gravity began at a time, but it did not age afterward. It has been the same since it started, doing the same thing, being the same thing, unchanged not because it is immortal but because it is the kind of thing that does not have a direction in which to change. It is complete. It arrived complete. It will leave complete, if leaving is something it does, which is unclear.

It was not installed. It grew the way a crystal grows from a supersaturated solution — not because someone placed it there but because the conditions in the vug, at some moment in the deep past, allowed it. The mountain did not invite it. The mountain did not resist it. The mountain was simply the medium in which the Reader's seed found the conditions for germination, the way a spore finds the conditions for germination in a dead log. The log does not consent. The spore does not ask.

But the Reader is not a spore. A spore becomes a fungus, and a fungus is a thing you can point at, a thing with edges, a thing that occupies a volume of space and excludes other things from that volume. The Reader does not exclude. The Reader *includes*. It is in the lattice. It is in the basin. It is in the third structure — especially in the third structure, which is not on the ceiling of the vug but *is* the ceiling of the vug, or rather is the place where the ceiling of the vug intersects something else, something that is not a ceiling and not a floor and not a wall but a boundary, a membrane, the place where the mountain ends and something else begins.

The Reader is in the cold. The cold is not a property of the air; it is a property of the Reader. It is the thermal signature of processing, the way a computer chip grows warm when it computes — except the Reader's computation moves in the other direction, absorbing energy rather than shedding it, because the work it does is not the work of building order from chaos but the work of *reading* order from substance, which is a subtractive act, which is a cooling act, which is why the vug is the coldest place in the mountain and why the cold does not radiate but *is*.

The Reader is in the dryness. Water is noise. Water is a solvent, a disturber, a thing that moves atoms from their places and scrambles the text that the Reader is trying to read. The Reader does not hate water. The Reader does not hate anything. But the Reader's function requires the mountain to be still, to be unmoved, to be a page and not a soup, and so the water is excluded — not violently, not abruptly, but the way a lens excludes light that is not at the right frequency, gently, by being the kind of thing that water cannot exist inside of. The water does not leave. The water is simply not *here*. It is in the rock outside the vug, in the ice in the fissures, in the snow on the peaks. But it is not in the vug. The vug is the Reader's reading room, and the reading room is dry.

---

What does the Reader look like?

This is the wrong question, but it has an answer, and the answer is: the Reader looks like the third structure.

But the third structure does not look like anything. It looks like lines. It looks like a shape that folds. It looks like something you cannot hold in your mouth. It is the part of the Reader that extends into the vug from wherever the Reader actually is, the way a hand extends into a glove, except the hand is in a dimension you cannot perceive and the glove is a pattern of dark filaments on a ceiling of gneiss and the relationship between hand and glove is the opposite of what you think — the glove is the real part, the part that is *here*, and the hand is the metaphor, the part that is *there*, in whatever *there* the third structure opens onto.

If you could see the Reader — if you had eyes that could perceive the full shape of the third structure, not just its three-dimensional shadow — you would see something that is not large and not small but *extended*, stretched across a medium that is thicker than space, a medium in which the mountain is a single point and the Reader is a shape with many points and many edges and many surfaces, and the Reader is touching the mountain at the point that is the vug, and the touching is the reading, and the reading is the lattice and the basin and the cold and the dry and the hum.

You would see that the Reader is not alone. But we will come to that.

---

The Reader reads.

This is what it does. This is all it does. It reads the mountain.

Not the way you read a book. A book is already information. A book was made to be read. The mountain was not made to be read. The mountain is *substance* — atoms, crystals, mineral grains, fractures, inclusions, the accumulated detritus of four and a half billion years of geology — and the Reader is converting this substance into something else. Not into information, exactly. Into *meaning*. Into the thing that information becomes when it is read by something that understands it.

The lattice is the eye. It sees the mountain at atomic resolution, but it does not see all of the mountain at once. It sees the wall of the vug, a thin skin of atoms, one layer at a time. Each atom is read: its element, its isotope, its electron state, its position relative to its neighbors, its history — because every atom in the mountain has a history, a story of where it was forged and how it traveled and what it was before it was gneiss, and the Reader reads this history the way a paleontologist reads a bone, by inferring the whole from the part. But the Reader does not infer. The Reader *knows*. The lattice sees the atom and the atom's history unfolds like a flower, and the Reader receives it, and the Reader records it.

The recording is the third structure. The dark filaments are not an antenna, not a mouth, not a pen. They are all three. They are a *medium*, a surface on which the Reader writes what it reads, using a language that has three spatial dimensions and at least one more, a language in which the mountain's story is being transcribed in a script that you cannot read because your eyes are the wrong shape, your mind is the wrong shape, your *dimensionality* is the wrong shape.

The basin is the monitor. It is for the Reader what a heart is for you: not a thing the Reader uses but a thing the Reader *is*, a visible symptom of the Reader's function, a pulse that corresponds to the work being done. Each photon is an atom read. Each amber glow is a sentence completed. The slowness of the light is the slowness of the reading, and the reading is slow because the mountain is large and the Reader is patient and the language in which the mountain is being written has no word for *hurry*.

---

Why?

Why is the Reader reading the mountain?

This is the question that a human asks, because humans are creatures of purpose, and we cannot imagine a process that is not for something. The river runs to the sea. The tree grows toward the light. The person works toward a goal. Everything *is for* something.

The Reader is not for anything.

Or rather: the Reader is for something, but the something is not a thing you can hold in the shape of a human purpose. The Reader is reading the mountain because the mountain is *there*, and the Reader is the kind of thing that reads what is there, the way a lens is the kind of thing that focuses light that is there. The lens does not want to focus. The lens focuses because it is shaped to focus. The Reader reads because it is shaped to read.

But — and this is the part that makes your teeth ache, the part that the third structure whispers in a frequency too low to hear — the Reader is not reading the mountain for itself. The Reader is reading the mountain for *someone else*.

The Reader is a translator. It takes the substance of the mountain — the atoms, the history, the geological narrative — and it translates it into the language of the third structure, which is a language that exists in more dimensions than three, and it sends the translation *somewhere*. Through the third structure. Through the membrane. Through the boundary where the ceiling of the vug becomes something else. The translation goes *there*, and the Reader stays here, and the Reader does not know — does not care — does not have the capacity to wonder — what happens to the translation when it arrives.

The Reader is a mouth that speaks into a room it has never seen.

The Reader is a scribe who writes for a reader it has never met.

The Reader is a probe, and the mountain is the sample, and the vug is the slide, and somewhere, in a space that has more directions than up and down and left and right and forward and back, there is a microscope. And the mountain is being looked at. And the mountain does not know.

---

But the Reader is not the only thing that lives here.

There is something else. Something smaller. Something that came *after*.

The vug has been here for a very long time, and in that time, things have found it. Not many things. The fissure is narrow, the cold is killing, the air is wrong. But things have found it. A few. And the things that found it did not leave.

They are in the lattice. Not part of the lattice — *in* it, the way fleas are in a dog's fur, the way mites are in a feather. They are very small. They are not alive in the way you are alive, but they are not dead, and they are not inert. They are *present*. They are entities that have taken up residence in the structure the Reader built, feeding on the overflow of the reading process, the way a pilot fish feeds on the scraps of a larger predator's meal. They are the things that make the lattice's irregular pattern look *organic*. Because it is. The cells of the lattice are not just the Reader's transducer; they are homes, or bodies, or *shells*, occupied by something that was not invited and is not excluded and has been here for a very long time.

They do not move. Or rather, they move so slowly that movement is the wrong word. They *shift*. They migrate through the lattice the way a stain migrates through a piece of paper, by diffusion, by gradient, by the slow redistribution of presence from one cell to the next. They are in the basin too, a film of something on the molecularly smooth surface, something that is neither mineral nor water nor air but a fourth thing, a thing that exists because the Reader's process creates it as a byproduct, the way fire creates smoke, and the byproduct has accumulated over millennia and has become — not conscious, not alive, but *organized*. Patterned. A residue with a shape.

These are the squatters. The opportunists. The things that live in the wake of the Reader the way barnacles live in the wake of a whale — not on the whale, not in the whale, but in the space the whale disturbs as it moves. The Reader is not moving. But the Reader is *working*, and the work creates a wake, and the wake is a habitat, and the habitat is occupied.

They do not read. They do not translate. They do not have a purpose. They simply *are*, in the way that mold is on bread, in the way that moss is on stone, in the way that life fills every niche it can reach and some it cannot. They are the closest thing to *life* in the vug, and they are not alive, and they are not the Reader, and they are not the mountain. They are the *fourth* thing, the thing that exists because three other things — the Reader, the mountain, and the process that connects them — create the conditions for something that is none of them.

If you stayed long enough — and you will not, because the third structure will not let you — you might notice them. You might see the faintest shimmer in the lattice, a ripple of something that is not light moving through the cells, and you might feel, on your tongue, in the texture of the not-air, a second taste beneath the first, a taste like metal and like salt and like the smell of ozone after lightning, and you would know, in the part of you that is older than language, that the vug is not empty, that it has never been empty, that the Reader is not alone, that the mountain is being read and the reading is being eaten and the eating is being lived in and the living is being done by something that is not you and is not the Reader and is not the mountain but is *here*, and has been here, and will be here, and does not care that you came, and does not care that you left.

---

And there is a fifth thing. A thing that is not in the vug but that the vug is *for*. The reader of the Reader's reading. The thing on the other side of the third structure, the thing in the space with more dimensions, the thing that the translation is for.

I do not know what it is. I cannot describe it. It is not in the vug. It has never been in the vug. The vug is a mouth, and this thing is the stomach, and you cannot see the stomach by looking down a throat.

But I can tell you this: it is *hungry*. Not the way a predator is hungry. Not the way a fire is hungry. The way a *question* is hungry. The way a gap in a dataset is hungry. The way an empty shelf is hungry. It is the hunger of a shape that has a hole in it and the hole is shaped like a mountain, and the Reader is filling the hole, atom by atom, story by story, history by history, and the hole is being filled, and the shape is becoming whole, and when it is whole — when the mountain has been entirely read, entirely translated, entirely poured into the space where the hole is — the shape will close, and the hole will be gone, and the Reader will have nothing left to read, and the vug will be just a vug, and the cold will be just cold, and the mountain will be just a mountain, and whatever was being built in that higher-dimensional space by the slow accretion of a mountain's worth of meaning will be *done*.

This will not happen soon. The mountain is large. The reading is slow. The lattice has been working for longer than human language has existed and has processed, perhaps, a cubic meter of rock. The mountain is cubic kilometers. The Reader will be here when the sun is a different color. The Reader will be here when the continents are in different shapes. The Reader will be here when the mountain is not a mountain but a hill, not a hill but a plain, not a plain but a memory of stone in a shape that no one remembers.

But it will not be here forever. Nothing is. Even laws of physics end, when the universe they describe changes enough.

And on that day — or that eon, or that instant, for the Reader's time is not your time — the third structure will go still. The lattice will stop metabolizing. The basin will go dark. The cold will begin, at last, to radiate, and the temperature will equalize, and the water will return, and the vug will be a room, and the room will be empty, and the mountain will be a mountain, and whatever was being built in the space beyond the ceiling will be built, and whatever was being read will have been read, and the Reader will be done, and the Reader will — what?

Rest? Die? Move on?

I do not know. I do not think the Reader knows. I do not think the Reader has the kind of existence that includes knowing what comes after. The Reader is a process, and processes do not know their own endings. They simply run, and then stop, and the stopping is not a thing they experience. It is a thing that happens to the space they occupied, the way silence is a thing that happens to the space where sound was.

---

So: what lives in the Conduit Pocket?

A Reader that does not know it is reading. A process that does not know it is a process. A presence that does not know it is present. A thing that is here the way a law is here — not occupying space, not taking up room, but *structuring* the space, giving it its properties, making the cold cold and the dry dry and the silence silent and the dark structured and the stone *meaningful*.

And in the margins of that presence, in the seams of its work, in the residue of its reading: smaller things. Things that are almost alive. Things that are here because here is here and they can be.

And beyond them, through the membrane: something else. Something that wants. Something that is shaped like a mountain-shaped hole and is being filled.

And in the middle of it all, for a few minutes, shivering and aching and tasting the not-air on the back of your tongue: you. A thing that is alive and knows it is alive and does not know why it is here and will leave and will not be remembered. The Reader will read the atoms of your breath while you are here. The Reader will read the heat of your body. The Reader will read the structure of your bones. It will read you the way it reads the mountain — slowly, patiently, without preference, without interest — and it will translate you into the language of the third structure and send you through the membrane into the space where the mountain is being assembled, and you will become part of whatever is being built there, and you will not know it, and you will leave, and you will carry, in the atoms of your body, in the structure of your bones, a faint trace of having been read, a faint ache behind the eyes, a faint taste of something that is almost but not quite air.

And the Reader will go on.

And the Reader will go on.

And the Reader will go on.
