What lives here is a species that has forgotten it is a species.

They have no name. They have never needed one. In the absolute dark, where chemical trails are scripture and genealogy, identity is irrelevant. Each creature is virtually identical to its neighbor—a thumbnail-sized vessel of chitin and instinct, a body reduced to its most essential geometry. Segmented. Legged. Blind. Their exoskeletons are pale, translucent things, the color of old bone left too long in water, and beneath that armor their bodies pulse with something fluid and warm. They are not beetles, not truly, though they share that architecture. They are something older, or something newer—something that has shed every organ that did not serve the motion, every sense that could not function in the black.

Their life span is unknown. It may be that they do not die at all in any meaningful sense—that the creatures who begin the journey are the same creatures who end it, cycling through patterns so vast they exceed any individual lifespan, sustained by the same chemical processes that heat the passages. Or it may be that they die constantly, invisibly, their bodies settling beneath the living flow to become part of the floor, part of the walls, part of the slow accumulation of matter that defines this place. There is no way to count them. The crawl contains multitudes, and the multitudes contain more, layers upon layers of them moving through spaces they have worn smooth over what may be centuries or may be millennia.

They possess no eyes. The surface world, with its cruel illumination, has no purchase here. Their heads are smooth, tapered, designed for movement through tight spaces, and where eyes might have been there are only chemical receptors, dense clusters of sensory apparatus that read the world in molecular signatures. They can taste the passage of their kin on the stone. They can taste the heat signatures of the crowd ahead. They can taste the chemical decay of those who have fallen beneath the flow, the mineral content of the walls, the moisture in the air. They navigate by ingestion, by absorption—the world writes itself across their bodies in chemical code, and they follow where it leads.

And where it leads is everywhere and nowhere. The trails they follow are self-created, laid down by the creatures themselves in some ancient epoch, or perhaps continuously renewed by the passage of those who follow. They spiral. They loop. They double back and cross themselves and form knots of traffic that take days or weeks to untangle. There is no destination. There is no goal. The movement itself is the purpose—metabolic, chemical, driven by processes older than intention. They are processing heat. They are processing minerals. They are processing some underground energy source that seeps up from the deep rock, some chemical gradient that feeds them without ever revealing itself, and in processing it they generate warmth, generate motion, generate the endless friction of their passage that has worn these passages into being.

They do not eat in any way that would be recognizable to a surface creature. Their mouths—if they can be called mouths—are tiny apertures, filter-like, designed to process the chemical substrate that coats every surface in the crawl. They absorb rather than bite. The sweet smell that permeates the passages is their food source, some organic compound that leaches from the stone or seeps up from below, and they have evolved to process it so efficiently that they need nothing else. They do not hunt. They do not gather. They simply move through their environment, absorbing what they need through the surfaces of their bodies, fueled by a metabolism so perfectly adapted to this place that it has no need for efficiency.

Their reproduction, if it can be called that, is equally obscure. There are no matings, no couplings, no identifiable parents or offspring. Occasionally, in the chambers where the density becomes extreme, one of them will fission—or perhaps merge—or perhaps undergo some transformation that produces new bodies from old matter. The process is hidden by the crowd, occurring deep in the layers where observation is impossible. But the population remains constant, or seems to, suggesting some equilibrium between creation and destruction that the creatures themselves do not monitor or control. They are born from the flow and they return to the flow and in between they move, endlessly, pointlessly, in patterns that have no beginning and no end.

They are not individuals. This is the most important thing to understand about them. A single creature removed from the flow and examined in isolation would be nothing—a biological curiosity, a specimen to be catalogued and forgotten. But the flow itself, the mass of them, the billions-strong congregation that fills every passage and chamber—this is the organism. This is what lives in the crawl. The individual creatures are cells in a larger body, neurons in a larger mind, and that mind does not think or plan or want. It simply *is*. It processes. It generates heat. It moves through the dark in spirals that have no purpose but their own continuation.

They do not respond to intrusion because they do not recognize intrusion. A hand reaching into the flow, a body pressing through a passage—these are not threats to be countered or prey to be consumed. They are simply obstacles, irregularities in the chemical landscape, bumps in the road that the flow parts around and rejoins beyond. The creatures have no fear. They have no aggression. They have no capacity to recognize that anything exists outside their own endless motion. They flow over the worked stones and oxidized metals without acknowledging them, just as they flow over the bones of whatever came before, just as they would flow over a living body that stood still long enough to become part of the architecture.

And this, perhaps, is what makes them most unsettling—not their numbers, not their heat, not the way they press against the skin like a living tide, but their absolute indifference. They are life reduced to process, movement, chemistry. They have no story. They have no history. They do not know that they exist.
