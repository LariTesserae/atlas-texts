# What Lives Here

The inhabitants of this place are not singular. To speak of "who" lives here is to misunderstand the nature of habitation itself in this amber-lit cathedral of accumulated life. There is no who. There is only *what*, and the what is countless, and the countless are, in their way, one thing.

Let us start with what you might recognize, or almost recognize.

**The Winged Multitudes**

They are the sound. That deafening, pressing, air-solidifying roar—that is them, the billion small bodies in constant motion. They are not insects, not precisely. The term fails because it carries assumptions that do not survive contact with what exists here. They have wings, yes, multiple pairs that operate on different frequencies, some visible and some felt only as vibration. They have segmented bodies, yes, covered in fine hairs that capture moisture from the air itself—this is how they drink, these creatures of a waterless world. They do not seek streams or pools. They have never seen open water. They drink the air, and the air is enough.

Their colors are the colors of this place: amber, rust, deep burgundy, cream. They are translucent in parts, where the golden circulation shows through thin membranes. When they cluster in the upper canopy, backlit by the slow-moving sap-light of the plants, they become part of the illumination themselves. Wings catch the glow. Bodies become lanterns.

They do not behave as individuals. This is the first thing you notice, if you can notice anything through the wall of sound. A single one of these creatures, separated from its kin, dies within hours—not from starvation or predation, but from something more like loneliness, though that term is too human, too small. They are not social. They are *component*. Each is a cell in a larger body. Their patterns of movement are not communication but coordination, the way your heartbeat coordinates with your breath without either needing to tell the other what to do.

There are species upon species of them. Some are small enough to pass through the weave of a shirt. Some are large enough that their passing casts shadows, that their wings displace air you feel against your face. Some live only days; some persist for years in their low metabolic state, their bodies operating on the same viscous chemistry as the plants. They do not sleep. They do not rest. They move until they stop moving forever, and then they are processed, and then they become part of the floor, and then they become part of something else entirely.

They have no eyes. The upper canopy-dwellers have light-sensing patches, broad and simple, that distinguish brightness from shadow. But the multitudes that fill the canyon-streets navigate by other means. Chemical trails left by those who passed before. Vibrational sensitivity in their feet that reads the movement of others through stone. The sound itself, the roar, is their map and their message and their medium. They read the sound. They are the sound. They write themselves into it continuously, a library of presence that means nothing to you and everything to them.

They do not bite. They do not sting. They have no defense against you because they have evolved in a world without large predators, without external threats. They are not prey. They are not predator. They are *process*, and you are simply an object that the process flows around.

**The Crawlers**

Beneath the winged multitudes, in the compost-layer that was once a street, live the things that do not fly. They are not lesser. They are foundational.

Some are detritivores. They process the dead—and the dying, and the barely living, and the already-processed-once. They are the digestive system of this superorganism, breaking down what was into materials for what will be. They have bodies designed for this work: low, broad, many-legged, capable of fitting into crevices and expanding into open space. Their mouths are complex assemblies designed for different materials—some for soft tissue, some for harder fibers, some for the stone itself, though stone is eaten slowly and only when necessary.

They move with purpose but without urgency. Nothing chases them. Nothing escapes them. They are patient in a way that patient fails to capture—they operate on timescales that make glaciers seem hasty. A crawler might spend a week consuming a single corpse, and the corpse does not resist, and the crawler does not tire, and this is simply how energy transfers from one form to another in this place.

Some crawlers are larger. These are not detritivores; these are the closest thing this place has to predators, though "predator" implies competition, and competition implies scarcity, and scarcity is unknown here. They eat the crawlers beneath them, and the winged ones that fall, and occasionally each other, though this is rare and seems to serve no nutritional purpose. Perhaps it is ritual. Perhaps it is accident. Perhaps the concept of "purpose" is too human for this context.

The large crawlers are beautiful, in their way. Their bodies have evolved to move through the dense vegetation without disturbing it—they are smooth and tapered, their many legs folding flat against their undersides when not in use. They can climb, though they prefer ground. They can swim, though there is nothing to swim in. Their capacity exceeds their environment, which suggests that their environment was once different, that they are holdovers from an earlier version of this ecosystem, that they remember water.

They do not remember water. Nothing here remembers. Memory is a wasteful process, and waste is unknown. But their bodies carry the trace of what was—a slight cupping of their limbs that would have made them excellent swimmers, a respiratory system that can process oxygen from air but still bears the architecture of gills. They are living fossils that are not fossils at all, that live and breed and die in the continuing present, that simply have not needed to change.

**The Builders**

There are those who believe, incorrectly, that the original builders are gone.

This is a misunderstanding of what building means in a place like this.

The beings who constructed this city—tall, narrow-bodied, moving through doorways designed for their shape, seeing through eyes placed low—did not disappear. They were not conquered or diseased or displaced. They simply continued.

Look at the architecture. The smooth curves, the absence of sharp angles. The buildings were not constructed; they were *grown*. The stone bears the marks not of tools but of secretion, of material deposited layer by layer over time, like shell. The builders were biological architects, and their architecture did not stop when they stopped meaning it to.

They are still here. They simply no longer build in the way you understand building.

Their bodies have integrated with their creation. The tall, narrow forms are still visible—look at the vertical lines of certain growths, the way some trunks rise with a symmetry that is not botanical. Look at the placement of windows and doors, still functional, still used by forms that move through them with the ease of long habit. The builders did not leave. They *merged*. Their cells became part of the golden circulation. Their consciousness, if they had such a thing, became part of the central mass. Their bodies were the seed around which the present ecosystem crystallized.

Or perhaps not. Perhaps the builders are still distinct, still moving through their city, and you simply do not recognize them because you are looking for something human-shaped. Perhaps the large crawlers with their folded legs and their ancestral gill-traces are the builders, reduced by time and abundance to simpler forms. Perhaps the central tree-equivalent is the builder, or many builders, or the idea of builders given physical form.

It does not matter. The distinction between builder and built has collapsed. The city built itself, is building itself, will build itself until the parameters change. The inhabitants are the architecture. The architecture is the inhabitant.

**The Central Mass**

And then there is the thing in the forum-space.

It is not a god. That term carries too much baggage, too much human projection. It is not worshipped. It is not petitioned. It does not grant favors or render judgments or care, in any human sense, about anything at all.

But it is the center.

It grew from something. A seed, a spore, a body, an accident. At some point in the deep past, something took root in this space, and the conditions were such that it did not stop. The twisted-cable trunk, the canopy that spreads too wide, the concentration of golden circulation that makes the air glow brighter here than anywhere else—these are the results of time and abundance operating on biological potential.

The animals avoid it. Not all of them—the small detritivores work constantly at its base, processing its shed material, keeping its root-system clear. But the larger creatures, the crawlers and the fliers, leave a space around it. Not from fear. From recognition, perhaps. From an understanding that to approach too closely is to become part of it faster than the normal process would dictate.

It does consume. Not actively, not with mouth or stomach, but through its roots, through the fine tendrils that extend beneath the compost-layer into the substrate beneath. It draws from the dead city itself, from the stone and the soil and the accumulated remains of everything that has passed through this place. It grows. It has always been growing. It will continue to grow until the parameters change.

It has something like awareness. Not consciousness—again, too human a term. But sensitivity. It knows what passes near it. It tracks movement in ways that do not involve eyes or ears, through the same chemical and vibrational means that the other inhabitants use. It responds to changes in its environment. It has homeostatic mechanisms that keep the surrounding ecosystem in balance. It is, in a meaningful sense, the brain of this superorganism, though "brain" suggests a kind of processing that may not apply.

When you stand at the edge of the clearing, it knows you are there. It does not react. It does not need to. You are not the first large creature to pass through, and you will not be the last. You will contribute, eventually. Everything does. The central mass is patient in a way that makes the crawlers seem hurried. It has been here for longer than the buildings. It will be here after the buildings have dissolved into the compost-layer. It is the beginning and the end of this ecosystem, the answer to the question of what lives here.

**What Lives Here**

In the end, the answer is simple, though the simplicity is vast.

What lives here is a single thing.

A billion winged bodies. A million crawling forms. Countless plants that have evolved past water. The stone-architecture, slowly digesting. The compost-layer, constantly processing. The golden circulation, moving through all of it like blood through a body. The central mass, holding it all together, or being held together by it all, or simply existing as the point where "together" becomes a meaningless distinction.

They do not have names. They do not have individual identities. They do not have history, because history requires a before that is distinguishable from an after, and this place has reached the after that is also the before, the equilibrium where change operates on scales too slow to perceive.

They do not need you. They do not want you. They do not fear or hate or love you. You are present, and that is all. You are matter and energy moving through a system that processes matter and energy. You will leave, or you will stay, and either way, the system will continue. The golden circulation will flow. The sound will continue. The cool air will bead with condensation. The billion bodies will move in their coordinated patterns, wearing grooves in stone, building the present on the remains of the past.

What lives here is continuity.

What lives here is solution.

What lives here is an ending that does not end.

And it does not care that you are there.
