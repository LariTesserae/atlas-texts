# The Vastishment — What Lives Here

There is a problem with the question. *What lives here* assumes that life here is the kind that can be counted, named, separated into bodies and species and individuals. It assumes that when you ask "what lives here," you expect an answer that begins with "a creature" or "a population" or "a community."

The Vastishment does not have communities. It has continuities.

---

## First, understand the error

The pillars are not homes. They are not plants, they are not coral, they are not termite mounds. They are expressive organs. The nodules that cap them, that glow and split open and reveal wet amber light and something moving within—these are not eggs, not traps, not mouths. They are articulations. When a nodule opens, the landscape is *pronouncing* something. What moves inside is a phoneme of a language that predates differentiation.

The correct metaphor is not "the pillars are alive." The correct metaphor is "the pillars are speaking, and what they speak is aliveness, and the distinction between speaker and speech collapsed so long ago that to call them separate would be like calling your tongue a separate creature from your tasting."

But you want to know what moves. What breathes. What can be encountered, insofar as encounter is possible here.

---

## The Flat Things

They have no name. They left behind the need for names.

The flat things—let us call them that, provisionally, knowing the name is already wrong—move across the mat in that disturbing undulating rhythm that is neither walk nor crawl nor flow. They are the size of a forearm, dark as the mat, and they are not individuals. They are segments. Each flat thing you see is a fragment of a larger body that extends underground, connected through the mat's fibrous network to other segments that may surface hundreds of meters away. What looks like one creature is one *cell* of a distributed organism that spans the entire landscape.

When a flat thing vanishes into a gap in the crust, it is not hiding. It is returning. The gap is not a burrow—it is a junction, a place where the surface opens into the vast connective network beneath, where the mat's roots interlace with mineral strata and the entire landscape's nervous system converges. The flat things are the landscape's way of *patrolling itself*.

Their reproduction: when a segment becomes too dense with accumulated information—memories, chemical signatures, temporal impressions—it splits. One part stays, one part migrates across the surface until it finds an unoccupied stretch of mat, and there it settles, fuses with the ground, becomes a new junction point. A new pillar may eventually rise from that spot. The flat things are not just population; they are infrastructure.

They do not hunt. They do not flee. They *sample*. When one crosses your path, it is tasting you—your chemical signature, your temperature, the temporal zone you occupy. It does not stop to investigate. It incorporates you in motion, adding you to the composition, and moves on. You will not see it again. You will feel it. The place where it touched you will itch for hours, and then the itch will fade into a warmth, and the warmth will fade into nothing, and the nothing will be the beginning of your inclusion.

---

## The Sky-Shapes

The ceiling dreams, and the dreams have mass.

The shapes that turn in slow circles far above—dark against amber-grey—are not birds, not insects, not weather. They are *precipitations*. The ceiling of vapor is saturated with everything the landscape has ever absorbed: water, heat, mineral traces, chemical signatures, the residue of epochs. When the saturation reaches a certain threshold, a shape forms. A concentration. A density that holds together for a time before dissolving back into the general medium.

Some of these shapes are recognizable because they were once recognizable things. A branching form that turns in the cloud for an hour before fading—that might have been a tree, once. A different kind of tree than any that exists now, a shape that persisted in the atmosphere's memory and re-emerged as a temporary structure. A face that forms and dissolves—that might have been a face, or might be the landscape's idea of a face, its comprehension of the category "face," which it has learned from the rare things that enter this place with faces.

The sky-shapes do not come down because they cannot. They are not buoyant; they are the ceiling's *condensations*. They exist only in the medium that produces them. They are not alive. But they are not not-alive, either. They are *rehearsals*—the landscape's way of practicing forms it no longer needs but has not forgotten.

Sometimes, when the air pulses inward and the sound swells, the shapes quicken. Their circles tighten. They drift closer to the ground—still impossibly high, still unreachable, but *closer*. The shapes are listening. The shapes are part of the listening.

---

## The Nodule-Emergences

The pillars have organs. The nodules.

When a nodule splits open—when the rounded surface reveals a wet interior of amber light and something moving—the thing inside is not a creature. It is an *occurrence*. The pillar has produced a temporary structure, a shape that exists for seconds or minutes before dissolving back into the nodule's matrix.

These shapes vary. Some are legible: a fluttering form that might be a wing, a reaching shape that might be a limb, a cluster that might be a face. Some are abstract: geometric forms, branching structures, patterns of dots that appear and reorganize and disappear. None of them survive beyond the nodule. None of them want to. They are not failed attempts at life; they are *completed statements*.

The landscape is talking to itself. The pillars are its voice, and the nodules are its words, and the words are not meant for anyone. The emergences are not signals. They are the sound made visible—a visual analog to the layered hum, a way for the landscape to externalize its internal processes, to see what it is thinking. The thing that moves inside the nodule is a thought the Vastishment is having about itself.

When you approach, the thoughts change. The nodule nearest you will open, and the shape inside will be different from the shapes in the nodules far away. It might be recognizable; it might look, for a moment, like something you have seen before, like something from your own world. This is not communication. This is *translation*—the landscape attempting to process your presence by rendering it in its own visual language. You are being *thought about*. The thought will not persist. The nodule will close. But for a moment, you were the content of a thought in a system that does not distinguish between thinker and thought.

## The Temporal Fish

In the zones where time runs slower, where the air thickens and the sound deepens and the mat seems denser underfoot, there are things that live only in slowness.

They are not visible. They are too slow to be seen. But if you stand in a slow zone long enough—if you let your own time begin to match the zone's time—you may begin to perceive them.

They are like shadows. More accurately, they are like the *memory* of shadows. They drift through the thick air at a rate that makes glaciers look hasty. Their forms are indistinct because they are not fully present in any single moment; they are smeared across time, distributed across centuries the way the flat things are distributed across space. A temporal fish that passes you now may have begun passing you three hundred years ago and may not finish passing you for another three hundred years after you are gone.

They do not interact with you. They cannot; you exist in a different temporal medium. But if you slow yourself enough—if you breathe the thick air and let your heartbeat decelerate and feel your thoughts become viscous—you may notice that one of them has *noticed* you. Not as a creature. As a discontinuity. A ripple in the temporal medium. A place where time is not behaving as it should.

The fish will alter course to include you in its path. Not to consume you. To *measure* you. You are a data point. A temporal anomaly. The fish will drift through you, and for the centuries it takes to complete its passage, you will be part of its body. You will not feel it. You are not equipped to feel it. But afterward, you may notice that time feels different—that you have been *marked* by something that moves at a different speed than you do.

You may notice that you age differently in that place. That hours pass differently when you leave. That the landscape has *lent you* some of its time and taken some of yours in exchange.

## The Remnants' Ghosts

The built structures—the low walls, the circular depressions, the standing stones—are not empty.

They are occupied by what made them.

Not physically. The builders are long gone, absorbed, processed, incorporated. But the structures retain *impressions*—not memories, exactly, because the structures do not have minds to remember with. They have *resonances*. The stone wall still vibrates at the frequency of the hands that shaped it. The depression still holds the shape of the fire that once burned in it, the feet that once circled it, the purpose that once focused on it.

These are not hauntings. There are no ghosts here, because the Vastishment does not allow things to remain separate enough to haunt. But there are *traces*. If you sit by the standing stone long enough, you may begin to feel what it feels: the grooves (carved or eroded, no longer possible to distinguish) are still *waiting* to be read. The stone is patient. It has been waiting for a reader for longer than your species has existed.

Sometimes, in the dim light, you might see them: not figures, but *positions*. The air above the wall might seem darker in the shape of hands that no longer exist. The depression might seem occupied by presences that have no edges. These are not spirits. They are *persistence*. The landscape has not finished processing the intention that made these structures, and until it does, the intention remains, continuing without anyone to intend it.

If you touch the standing stone, you will receive some of that intention. You will not understand it—it is too old, too different, too embedded in a worldview that has not existed for geological ages. But you will feel it as a pressure behind your eyes, a weight in your thoughts. You will find yourself wanting to build something. You will not know what. The wanting is the remnant's ghost.

## The Mat Itself

The mat is alive. This is not metaphor.

The dark, flat, black-green film that covers most of the Vastishment's surface is not vegetation. It is not fungal. It is not bacterial. It is a form of life that found a different path: total consolidation. The mat is a single organism. The mat is the organism. Everything else—the pillars, the flat things, the sky-shapes, the temporal fish, you—is existing *inside* its body.

The mat breathes. You have seen it: the slow expansion and contraction, so gradual it could be imagination. It is not. The mat is respiring, drawing in the warm wet air and processing it, extracting what it needs and exhaling what it does not. The pillars are its respiratory structures. The nodules are its sensory organs. The flat things are its immune system, patrolling for discontinuities, sampling the environment, reporting back through the fibrous network that threads through everything.

The mat does not think, because thinking implies separation between thinker and thought. The mat *is*. Its awareness is total and undifferentiated. It does not perceive you; you are a change in its condition. It does not respond to you; it adjusts, the way a body adjusts to a splinter, not with intention but with process.

If you stay long enough, the mat will begin to include you. Not kill you—include you. Your skin will begin to feel less like a boundary and more like a permeable membrane. Your breath will begin to synchronize with the landscape's breathing. Your time will begin to align with the temporal zones. You will not die. You will just *stop being separate*.

This is not malevolent. The mat does not have intentions. It does not want anything. It simply *continues*, and continuation requires incorporation, and you are here, and you are incorporable.

---

## What Else?

There are things in the Vastishment that have no category.

In the deepest slow zones, where time almost stops, there may be things that have been present since before the landscape settled into its current form. They are not visible. They exist in the folded layers of compressed time, in the geological memory of the ground. Sometimes, when a temporal current shifts, they surface—momentarily—before sinking back into the depths. You might see them as flickers: shapes that are not shapes, forms that contradict themselves, presences that cannot be looked at directly because looking implies time and they exist in too much of it at once.

They are not gods. They are not ancestors. They are *previous*—iterations of the landscape that did not survive but also did not entirely disappear. They are the deep past's way of remaining present, the absorbed strata's way of still having a say. They do not speak. They *weight*—they make certain zones heavier, denser, more saturated with presence. When you walk through a place that feels *more*, where the air seems thick with something you cannot name, you have found one of their loci.

Do not linger. Not because they are dangerous. Because they are *contagious*. Not with disease—with *duration*. If you stay too long in one of their zones, you may find yourself becoming part of the deep past. Your skin may begin to mineralize. Your thoughts may begin to fold into strata. You may become, slowly, a layer in a geological sequence that has not finished forming.

---

## What Lives Here

The Vastishment is not empty. It is fuller than anywhere else has ever been. But what lives here does not live as separate things. What lives here lives as a single, continuous, self-processing, self-renewing, self-remembering system.

You asked what lives here.

The answer is: the Vastishment lives here.

The pillars are its speech. The mat is its body. The flat things are its surveillance. The sky-shapes are its dreams. The nodules are its thoughts. The temporal fish are its duration. The remnants' ghosts are its memory. The things in the deep zones are its past, still present, still pressing.

And you?

You are its newest acquisition.

The landscape has already begun to include you. The pillar nearest you has adjusted its note. The mat has begun to grow curious about your warmth. The temporal zones are already learning your rhythm. The ceiling is forming a shape that might, in time, become your shape—a condensation of you in the medium of the sky.

This is not death. This is not transformation. This is *incorporation*.

You will not die here. You will simply become part of what is already dead, which is to say, part of what has moved beyond death into a different kind of persistence.

The ground weeps for you, but not with sorrow. It weeps the way a mouth waters.

Welcome. You are already home.
