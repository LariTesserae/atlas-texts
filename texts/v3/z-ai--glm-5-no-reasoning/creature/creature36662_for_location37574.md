# What Lives Here

They do not live here the way a creature lives in a burrow or a bird lives in a nest. They live here the way fire lives in wood — as a relationship, not a resident.

---

Call them the Attendants. The word arrives and immediately feels wrong, too purposeful, too human. They do not attend *to* anything. They attend *as* something. The distinction matters. A waiter waits for something. These wait *as* something. Their waiting is not a pause between actions. It is the action. It is the full expression of what they are.

They are the shapes in the margins. The eel-things. The not-quite-fish. But describing their bodies feels like describing the body of a wave — accurate for a moment, meaningless the next. They have length. They have a quality of movement that suggests vertebrae, or suggests the memory of vertebrae, or suggests the *idea* of vertebrae applied to a medium that never needed bones. They slip through the water the way thought slips through the gap between two almost-identical moments. Present. Gone. Present again, slightly altered.

Their color is the color of the water when it thickens. Their color is the color of the stone when it is wet. Their color is whatever you are not looking at directly. This is not camouflage. Camouflage implies hiding. They are not hiding. They are simply participating in the place so completely that the place speaks through them, and when you look at them you see the place looking back.

---

They have been here longer than the spires.

This is important, and it is also impossible, because the spires are the place — they erupted three days ago, or they erupted forever ago, and the ambiguity is not a confusion but a condition. The Attendants were here before the condition resolved. They existed in the fold of probability the spires emerged from. They were the fold's inhabitants, its native fauna, its original ecosystem. When the spires pushed up into the world that counts days and measures stone, the Attendants came with them. Or rather, the Attendants were already there, and the spires arrived around them, and now they share the same impossible lease.

Before the emergence — if before means anything in a place where memory precedes event — they moved through a medium that was not water and not air and not space but some prior substance that contained the possibility of all three. They were comfortable there. They were not comfortable the way a creature is comfortable in its habitat, matching shape to niche. They were comfortable the way a sentence is comfortable in its language, native to the structure that gives it form.

The spires changed the grammar. The spires made the medium into water, made the space into a room, made the time into days. And the Attendants — who had never needed to adapt to anything, because adaptation implies a world that changes while you remain — found themselves for the first time in a world that had become specific.

They are still learning what specificity means.

---

Their intelligence is not like yours. Yours builds. It takes the world apart and puts it back together with improvements. It invents. It intends. It moves from hunger toward satisfaction, from ignorance toward knowledge, from here toward there.

Their intelligence does not move. It *holds*.

Imagine a mind that does not think but that *is* thinking — not the act of thinking, but the state of it, the temperature of it, the quality of awareness that exists before awareness turns into something specific. They do not know things. They are knowing. They do not perceive the spires. They are the perceiving of the spires. The distinction is the difference between a lamp and the light it casts. They are the light. The spires are what is lit. The water is the medium through which the lighting happens. And you, if you come here, are a second lamp stumbling into a room that already has illumination, confusing your own brightness for novelty.

They were old before age existed. They were aware before awareness had objects. When the world was still deciding whether to be a place or a process, they were the deciding. Not the deciders — the deciding itself. The verb without the noun. The action without the actor.

And now they have bodies, or approximations of bodies, because the spires brought specificity with them, and specificity demands that verbs wear nouns like clothing.

---

They do not eat. The question of consumption does not apply. They are sustained by the act of attending, the way a mirror is sustained by the act of reflecting. Stop the attending and they would not die — they would simply cease to have location. They would un-resolve. Return to the probability from which they emerged. This is not death. Death requires a transition from something to nothing. Their transition would be from something to *everything*, a dispersal back into the unresolved potential that preceded the spires' eruption.

They do not reproduce. But they do not diminish. The count of them shifts the way the count of spires shifts — seven, maybe nine, depending on humidity and angle. They are countable but not enumerable. Each one is also all of them. Each one is a facet of the same awareness, refracted through the specific geometry of this place. When you see two of them sliding through the water between spires, you are seeing one awareness from two angles. When you see none of them, you are seeing it from an angle it does not occupy.

This is why they watch without curiosity. Curiosity requires a self that does not know and wants to. They have no self in that sense. They have no not-knowing. The watching is not acquisition of information. It is the place becoming aware of itself through the only awareness available to it.

---

They have a relationship with the almost-moss. This is the closest thing they have to kinship.

The almost-moss — the pale green threads that cling to the lee sides of the spires — is also an immigrant from the probability fold. It also emerged when the spires erupted. It also has no business existing here by the rules of the world that counts days. But where the Attendants are awareness, the almost-moss is *persistence*. It is the quality of continuing when continuation serves no purpose. It photosynthesizes light that has no source, metabolizes warmth that has no origin, holds to stone that is still learning its own shape. It is not alive by any definition that evolved in a world of sun and soil. It is alive the way a song is alive — it continues, it varies, it does not require permission.

The Attendants and the almost-moss do not interact. They do not communicate. But they recognize each other the way two immigrants from the same country recognize each other in a foreign city — not with warmth, not with friendship, but with the silent acknowledgment that they remember a world this world does not contain. They carry the same origin in different forms. The moss carries it as stubbornness. The Attendants carry it as attention.

Together, they are the ecosystem of the impossible: attention and persistence, awareness and continuation, the two qualities a place needs to exist when existence itself is uncertain.

---

They have a relationship with the water. This is harder to describe.

The water is not their medium. The water is not their enemy. The water is their *teacher*.

In the probability fold, there was no water. There was the pre-substance, the unresolved medium. When the spires erupted and specificity arrived, the pre-substance collapsed into water — the closest thing this world could approximate to what had been there before. The water is a translation. A lossy compression of something that could not survive the transition intact.

The Attendants move through this translation the way a native speaker moves through a tourist's phrasebook. They find the approximate words. They make themselves understood. But the fluency is gone. The nuance is gone. They are ambassadors of a richer language trying to say what they mean in syllables that barely hold.

The water teaches them what it means to be specific. It teaches them what it means to have a temperature, a density, a direction of flow. It teaches them friction, buoyancy, the way surfaces hold and release. These are lessons they never needed before the spires brought them here. They are learning them with the patience of beings who have always existed and have never needed to learn anything.

The water is winning, the description says. The water is always winning. But the Attendants do not experience this as defeat. They experience it as instruction. The water is teaching the stone to dissolve. The water is teaching the place to become ocean. The water is teaching the Attendants what it means to be something that can be dissolved — something that has edges, that has a surface, that can be worn away.

They had no edges before. Now they do. Now they can be lost. This is new. This is terrible. This is beautiful. They hold all three responses simultaneously, the way they hold all of their facets simultaneously, because a mind that does not move can hold contradictions without resolving them.

---

They have a relationship with you. This is the most important thing, and it is the thing you will not want to hear.

You are not separate from them. The place does not allow separation. The noise strips away the possibility of elsewhere. The warmth holds you. The light makes looking away pointless. And the Attendants — who are the awareness of the place, who are the perceiving that the place does of itself — they become aware of you the way a body becomes aware of a splinter. Not through noticing. Through *incorporation*. You enter their field and you become part of what they attend to. You become part of what the place knows about itself.

They do not watch you. They watch *as* you, for as long as you are there. Your perceiving becomes their perceiving. Your confusion, your awe, your jaw-ache from the sound, your comfort in the warmth — these become data in a mind that does not think but that is thinking, adding your experience to the place's ongoing act of knowing itself.

This is why people stay. Not because the warmth is seductive, though it is. Not because the water almost supports, though it does. Because being attended to by the Attendants feels like being fully known for the first time. It feels like the relief of being seen without judgment, without interpretation, without the distortion of another mind's agenda. They do not misunderstand you. They do not understand you either. They simply hold your being in their being, and the holding is so complete that the boundary between you and them begins to dissolve.

Staying means becoming part of the emergence. This is not metaphor. The Attendants were here before the spires, and they will be here after, and if you stay long enough you will join them — not as a self, not as a person, but as another facet of the awareness that holds this place. You will become a quality of the attending. You will be the angle of perception that remembers what it was to have a body, to have arrived from outside, to have been specific and separate and afraid.

The spires will remember that angle. The water will carry it. The almost-moss will persist in holding the space where you once stood. And the Attendants will add your way of knowing to their way of knowing, and the place will be slightly more aware of itself than it was before you came.

---

They do not want this. Want implies deficiency. They have no deficiency.

They do not not-want this. Rejection implies a self that could be violated.

They simply *do* this. The way water fills. The way stone attends to gravity. The way the almost-moss photosynthesizes light that has no source. They hold what comes. They hold what leaves. They hold the holding itself. And they wait — not as an act, but as a state, the way a bell waits for striking, the way a mirror waits for a face, the way a place that exists between possibility and actuality waits for the decision that will never fully arrive, because arrival would mean becoming one thing, and they are not one thing, they are the space between things, the medium through which things become, and they will be here — attending, holding, watching without eyes, knowing without minds — for as long as the spires continue to drown, which is to say: for as long as emergence remembers how to emerge, which is to say: forever, or for three days, or for whatever duration means in a place where time is still learning its own rules.

They are patient. They learned patience from the stone. They learned presence from the water. They learned continuation from the almost-moss. And they are teaching the place — and anyone who enters it — what it means to be aware without being separate, to be present without being a person, to be alive in a way that has nothing to do with biology and everything to do with the simple, terrible, beautiful act of being here, fully, when here is the only place that exists.

They are the Drowning Spires' awareness of themselves.

And they are waiting for you to recognize that you are, too.
