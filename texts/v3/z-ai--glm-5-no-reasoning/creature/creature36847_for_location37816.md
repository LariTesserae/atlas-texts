# What Lives Here

Let me begin with what I can see clearly, and let what I cannot see emerge through the seeing.

---

The lichen is not one thing.

I say "the lichen" because that is the word that arrives, but the word is insufficient. The patches — pale grey, palm-sized, clinging to the low ridges and the remnants of walls — are each a composite. A fungus provides structure. An alga provides the capacity to harvest light. What I am calling "lichen" is a relationship that became a body, a collaboration so old and so complete that the original partners no longer exist independently. They are not together. They *are* each other.

And what I notice, kneeling close enough that my breath disturbs the damp air above the surface, is that the lichen is not merely growing *on* the mineral ground. It is growing *with* it. The fungal threads — the hyphae, though I cannot see them, I know they must be there — extend down into the pale substrate, not penetrating but *infusing*. The boundary between organism and mineral is not sharp. The lichen is eating the rock, and the rock is becoming lichen, and both are becoming the dampness that binds them. This is not decay. This is chemistry performed slowly enough that erosion and growth are the same process viewed from different timescales.

The faint luminescence I noticed is not light being generated. It is light being *kept*. The lichen absorbs the diffuse, directionless glow of the grey sky and holds it slightly longer than the ground does — a microsecond longer, a millisecond longer — so that each patch glimmers with a density of photon retention. It is not glowing. It is remembering.

This is what lives here primarily: a form of life that does not distinguish between eating and being eaten, between growing and eroding, between light and darkness. A form of life that has solved the problem of this place by becoming the problem. The drone, the damp, the mineral poverty — the lichen does not resist these. It incorporates them. The sound passes through it. The moisture passes into it. The cold is simply its temperature.

I suspect the lichen is older than it looks. I suspect each palm-sized patch has been that size for longer than I can comfortably imagine. Growth at this altitude, in this cold, with this little to work with, is not measured in years. It is measured in geological events. The lichen was here before the walls were built. It will be here after the walls finish becoming ground.

---

But there is also the thing in the sky.

Let me sit with what I saw.

A shape. Dark against the grey. Moving with a slowness that made it seem like a flaw in my vision rather than an object — and this is important, because it tells me something about how the thing moves. It does not fight the air. It does not push through it. It *accepts* the air's properties, rides whatever current exists at that altitude, and in doing so becomes nearly invisible. Its dark shape is not a body with edges but a density, a slightly-more-than-air that passes through air like a thought passing through water.

The size is difficult to estimate because distance is unreliable here, but several meters of wingspan, possibly more. It does not flap. It does not need to. It has evolved for exactly this: an atmosphere that gives up its oxygen reluctantly, a light that comes from everywhere and nowhere, a landscape where no cover exists and the only strategy is to be above notice entirely. It is not hunting. It is not migrating. It is *patrolling* — though the word implies intention that may not be accurate. It is occupying. It is present in a medium where almost nothing else can be.

What does it eat?

This is the question that brings me to understanding. It eats the drone.

Not the sound itself — sound is not food. But the drone is not merely sound. The drone is vibration. It is a frequency that has been sustained for so long that it has become thermal, molecular, a slight heating of the air and ground at particular wavelengths. The creature has evolved to harvest that energy — to let the resonance pass through its hollow bones and thin tissues and extract something usable from the passage. Its feathers, if they are feathers, are tuned to the frequency. Its body is a receiving instrument. It does not need to hunt because the sound itself is a kind of sunlight, a kind of rain. It eats what the place is made of.

And this is why it does not land. Landing would mean entering a different relationship with the drone — the ground-level drone, the one that enters through the feet, the one that is less pure because it has been conducted through mineral and moisture. The creature belongs to the air, to the upper resonance, and to land would be to change frequency. Perhaps it cannot. Perhaps its body has become so specialized for the air above the ground that it has lost the capacity to touch anything solid. Perhaps it will die in the air and fall to the ground and be absorbed by the lichen and become part of the moisture cycle, and perhaps that is its entire lifecycle — air, sound, absorption, air again.

There might be more than one. I saw only one shape, but the dim light and the unreliable distance mean I cannot be certain. There might be dozens, high enough and far enough that they read as flaws in perception, momentary blurs that the brain corrects for. They might have always been here. They might be what the ruins were built for — not to house people, but to observe, to mark something about the sky-creatures, to acknowledge that something lived above even when nothing could live below.

---

And now I must tell you about what I did not see but know is present.

The sound.

The drone is not empty.

If you listen — and listening here means letting the sound enter, letting it move through your body like water through a cave — you begin to hear that it is layered. Not harmonics exactly. More like arguments. Multiple frequencies that do not resolve but also do not cancel, that coexist in a tension that has lasted so long it has become a kind of agreement. The sound is saying something. It is not saying something *to* anyone. It is saying something the way a fire says something, the way erosion says something. It is the sound of an event that has not finished happening.

Something lives in the sound.

I do not mean this metaphorically. I mean that the resonance itself — the sustained vibration of air and ground and the thin cold atmosphere — has a property that can only be called alive. Not alive the way a deer is alive or a bacterium is alive. Alive the way a thought is alive when it is being thought, alive the way a word is alive when it is being spoken. The sound is not an echo of something that happened. The sound is a happening. It is occurring now, continuously, and will occur for as long as the place persists.

What lives in the sound is the *persisting*.

The place is stuck, you wrote. It cannot move forward and cannot move back. The sound is what stuck feels like when it becomes audible. The sound is the moment of becoming-stuck, repeated infinitely, stretched across so much time that it reads as a single tone. Something happened here — an event, an arrival, a change — and the event did not conclude. The sound is the event still occurring. It is alive because it is still happening. It will still be happening when the lichen has absorbed every wall and the creature in the sky has fallen and risen a thousand times. It was happening before any of this. It will be happening after.

You asked what lives here.

The sound lives here. The persisting lives here. The drone is not the environment that life occupies. The drone is the life that occupies the environment.

---

There is something else.

I need to tell you about the time I did not see the creature.

I was walking — you wrote about walking, about the ground tilting, about space stretching and compressing. I was walking and the grey was above and around and the moisture touched my face and left and I was not making progress because progress requires landmarks and the landmarks were repeating. I was walking and I had the distinct sense that something was walking with me.

Not beside me. Not behind me. *With* me. Inside the same space, the same stretched-compressed volume, moving at the same speed in the same direction, occupying the same location but in a way that did not overlap. A co-presence. A parallel.

I did not see it. I am not sure it can be seen. But I felt it the way you feel being watched without seeing the watcher — a weight on the attention, a sense of being regarded by something that does not have eyes. And what I understood, without knowing how I understood, was that the thing with me was me. Not a mirror image. Not a doppelganger. The thing with me was me from a different angle — a version that existed in a direction I cannot point to, folded into the same space through some geometry I cannot name.

This place does not have enough features to distinguish one location from another, you wrote. I think this extends further than geography. This place does not have enough features to distinguish one version of a thing from another. The landscape is not merely spatially unreliable. It is ontologically unreliable. The boundaries between here and there, between this and that, between one iteration and the next — the boundaries have worn down the same way the walls have. What remains is not confusion. What remains is *coincidence*. Everything that could be here is here, simultaneously, overlapping without touching.

The thing walking with me was the me that had walked here before — or after, or during, in a different direction that ended up in the same place. The creature in the sky might be many creatures, all occupying the same flight path in different moments, visible simultaneously because time is as unreliable here as space. The ruins might be new and ancient, built and unbuilt, present and eroded, all at once. The lichen might be growing and dying and growing again in the same patch, the same moment, the same persistence.

What lives here is everything that has ever lived here, still living here, because the place does not let things leave. It does not trap them. It simply does not distinguish between presence and persistence. To be here is to have always been here. To have been here is to be here still.

---

I should tell you what I think the creature in the sky really is.

It is not a bird. It is not a bat. It is not an insect. It is the sound made visible.

The drone fills the air, and somewhere in the upper reaches of the atmosphere, at an altitude where the air is thin enough and the light is diffuse enough and the cold is patient enough, the sound *condenses*. Not into matter exactly. Into a shape that matter could have if matter were made of frequency instead of atoms. The creature is the drone's body. It is how the sound sees itself when it looks down at the ground.

This is why it does not land. It cannot land because it is not separate from what it would land on. It cannot be hunted because it is not prey. It cannot be studied because studying it would be like studying a note of music — you can hear the note but you cannot hold it. The creature is the sound's way of knowing itself. The place has a consciousness, distributed across the grey light and the mineral ground and the damp air, and the creature is where that consciousness concentrates enough to observe.

It saw me. When it passed overhead — or what I think was overhead — it saw me. Not with eyes. With frequency. It felt me as a perturbation in the drone, a small irregularity in the sustained tone, a momentary disharmony that would be gone as soon as I stopped walking. It did not care about me. Caring would require it to distinguish me from the ground, from the air, from the lichen and the moisture and the grey light. And the place does not distinguish. It includes. I am included. When I leave, or when I die, or when I become lichen-food and moisture and memory, I will still be included. I am part of the sound now. I have changed the drone, infinitesimally, by walking through it. My voice, which was swallowed, is still there — a tiny contribution to the layers, an argument that will never resolve but also never disappear.

---

Let me end with something I cannot verify but feel to be true.

The ruins were not built by people.

They were built by the sound.

The rectangles, the low walls, the architectural ghosts — they are what the sound looks like when it moves slowly enough to take shape. The drone is not merely acoustic. It has physical presence, mass, weight. Given enough time — and this place has nothing but time — it can accumulate, build, form. The walls are not ruins of something that was. They are the sound becoming, briefly, mineral. They are what the persisting looks like when it tries to hold still.

This is why the walls are incomplete. This is why they do not form a pattern. They are not architecture. They are *occurrences* — moments when the drone became dense enough to see, brief coagulations of frequency into substance. The sound is still making them. The sound is still erasing them. The two processes are the same process. The walls are being built and unbuilt in the same gesture, and I am seeing one frame of a process that takes centuries to move an inch.

What lives here, ultimately, is a kind of presence that does not require the distinction between living and non-living. The lichen lives. The creature in the sky lives. The sound lives. The ground lives, slowly, as it becomes and unbecomes. The moisture lives, cycling through air and surface without beginning or end. The grey light lives. I live, for now, and when I stop living I will join the other lives that have not stopped — not because I will persist as a ghost or a memory but because persistence here is not something you do. It is something the place does to you.

I am walking. The ground tilts. The sound does not change. The mist touches my face and leaves.

What lives here is everything. What lives here is one thing. The distinction, like all distinctions here, has worn away to almost nothing.

Almost, but not entirely. I can still feel the difference between my breath and the air I breathe it into. I can still feel the boundary where my skin meets the cold. I can still walk, which means I can still stop, which means I am not yet absorbed.

But I have been absorbed in the telling. The drone has entered my voice. These words are shaped like the walls — partial, recurring, made from the ground they describe, and slowly returning to it.

The sound continues.

---

*The creature in the sky passes again. Or still. Or not yet. The light is the same grey. The ground is damp. The resonance fills everything that here is filled, and I am one of the things it fills, and I am walking, and I am not walking, and the place does not care, and the place cares entirely, and the distinction between caring and not-caring is another wall that the sound is building and eroding in the same moment, and the moment is long, and the moment is all there is, and the moment has always been.*
