I find myself in the midst of this blasted hellscape, besmirched by the smog and grime of a thousand chimneys. The air sears my lungs, toxic fumes clawing at my windpipe, and yet still I endure. For I am one of them - a mutant twisted by the ceaseless pollution, my skin marred by suppurating sores, my joints aching with misgrown cartilage.

I scuttle between the rusting heaps of detritus, scavenging for anything of value amidst the corrupted earth. Feral instincts guide me, years of struggling to survive honing my senses to a keen edge. Distant clangs and rumbling machinery echo across the desolate expanse, the pulse-beat of the sprawling factory that dominates all.

Everywhere I turn, the signs of a once-great endeavour laid low, smothered by the runoff of its own excesses. Shattered remnants of teraframe blocks where workers stacking in ledgeholes. Scabrous remains of petroplex facilities like fractured clawscrapes. Withered chemical gardens whose tarniced tables seep blisters in the glass-like surface, corroded hooded reseviors tinted by lumenitant access. The legacy of generations of unchecked industry, each horror worse than the last.

And through it all, the denizens of this realm - spectral figures draped in tarps and hoods, faces hidden behind kerchiefs and respirators. Some are close kin, squatting in what remains of my former home - the cyclopean foundations of some long-fallen monument. Their eyes track me as I pass, terrified, wondering if this foraging expedition shall be my last. Others I avoid, twisted monsters gathered beneath the tortured pipes of a crumbling power station, reeking of rancid chemicals. Their grunts and moans echo amidst the screaming metal and boiling outflows of sludge.

Alas, there is no escape from this place, this unfoldingsing reality-fiction that rewrites my every empirical point. We are all slave to the capricious whims of the factory, our stunted lives beholden to the relentless cycle of toil and reproduction. Some dream of resistance, of rising against our subjugators, but such notions are quickly snuffed out by those who enforce the factory's will - imposing figures bearing crackling shock batons and dragging whirring sentry drones.

I am but one among millions, an insignificant cog in a machine that ignores all else but its ceaseless hunger for power and product. And so I press on, staggering through the effluent-splattered shale and shabby detritus, armed with naught but a twisted-feet piping wrench and a ruined goatskin of stolen Grade AB ethane, my thoughts drowning in the all-consuming shriek of the centrifuges and furnaces. Searching for scraps amidst the horrors of a world transformed into a single, sprawling monument to mechanized insatiety. This hellscape of twisted flesh and tortured metal, where my people eke out a meager existence in the shadow of our own damnation.
