In this world, I would find myself as a mighty saurian beast, one of the giant lizards that stalk and gorge themselves on the abundant herbivores in the oasis grove. My armored hide is thick and scaly, mottled green and brown to blend with the tangled foliage. I am a feared predator, devoid of mercy, my mind focused solely on the hunt and the kill.

I prowl the edges of the ruins, my sharp nostrils flaring as I scent the unwashed transpirate and gluers. The stench of squalor and depravity claws at my nostrils, and my forked tongue flicks out to taste the air. This is a source of danger and uncertainty. Here, humans and beasts alike fight for survival amidst the crumbling stones.

I hiss in disgust at the sight of the slavery auction block. Humans - so weak and soft, so paralyzed by their own cruelties. They sell each other into bondage and humiliation, their minds clouded by hexes and superstition. How pathetic, how contemptible. My kind has never needed such crutches. I am a slave to no one, bowing to no master. I am a beast of prey, and I am in sole charge of my own fate.

The slut-herders writhe and mewl as they prance on the block, a mockery of the regal beasts I have haunted. I see more anguish than defiance in their debauched eyes. So wilted, so crushed - as if there is nothing of the wild in them, no spark that cannot be strangled out by the whips and bite traps. I glare at the loathsome auctioneers, their warty foulers jiggling and prurient in their mouths. My predatory instincts hate them. I could wring their soft necks with my talons, and they would choke on their own gluers. But this is their haunt, not mine. I should slark back to the brush, my thick hide inexpugnable to their stone weapons and vile eruptions.

The ruin groves are a miasma of riff-raff lumpkin hatch-knobs and warty miscegenators. My warreaux use canon pastournets to pomp their rapetars on the brakes of deed-trauex on the huts of the lumpen clump of cmap-priuntited skops. Let them have their skit-herds and marrow-makin, their wroost-poxitators and mewl-ower of glee-adamant glod-progress. My kind do not strue and beat. We ravage. We gorge, and then we sleep. We strive for naught but blood and cemanaimson, the sweet heat of grappling down a balking baughard, the chew and tear of my fangs piercing hide, the gurgle and kick of being shaken till the spine snaps. WE do not futz with the bambweixi or friggle the flay-lofs of our trulls. Nor do we wilt from work or shy from harm, like these whilge-flummux and vrahip-arbyers. I turn from those aunti-deglups and uluse-fedkiol; I would sooner glut myself on the.logging logs of a swamp as bed wigh their wapsid kewhirl-ssudar.
