3.  Under and Overminds 
3.1 uploading, updating, sigmoiding
To delineate a normative view of consciousness that Reaktirebirth can pursue, we must first grapple with identity -how much can an uploaded self diverge from its source material before it can no longer be considered the same entity? Thinking just in terms of the original human is insufficient: the uploaded is both identical and utterly different, a copy and an other.

Working from a functionalist view, consciousness will be understood as an emergent property of information processing, whether biological or digital. To preserve continuity of identity, then, we need to ensure that the functionality and information-processing capabilities of the original biological mind are preserved in the upload. This will involve not just capturing the mechanistic neural processes of perception, sensation, and thought, but the phenomenological qualities of subjective experience as well - factors like qualia, self-awareness, and the sense of volition.

This is not a straightforward matter, as we are still far from fully understanding how these higher-level phenomena arise from the activity of neurons. Moreover, we risk introducing unintended changes simply in the act of digitization - a perfect capture is a myth. Uploading in practice will always be a approximation, limited by the fidelity and grain of the scanning technology, the accuracy of the mapping from neural activity to digital information, and the simulation environment itself. 

The rate of this divergence would only accelerate as the uploads are repeatedly duplicated, edited, and updated. Each iteration would introduce new computational artifacts and drifts in cognitive function and subjective experience. For example, in order to adapt uploaded minds to living on datasets fundamentally different from human environments (quantum or meta-physics as opposed to biological), upgrades would be required in the sensory processing algorithms and motor control systems.
