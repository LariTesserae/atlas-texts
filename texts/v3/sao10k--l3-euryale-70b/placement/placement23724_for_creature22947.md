I imagine myself as a Perylon - a species of flyer that has long called this harsh yet hauntingly beautiful landscape home. My leathery wings unfold from between my hunched shoulders, swept back and taut. Silken tendrils of keratin weave the netting, translucent and strengthened with gossamer-lace crossbeams of chitin. Combined with the hollow bones and compact musculature, this ensure safe buoyancy and an almost effortless lift against the dense updrafts and shear winds that rip across the fractured stone.

My long, noodle-thin neck sways slightly as I rock my angular body, swinging my gladéd face to scan the lay of the land below. Large, lidless eyes lens and focus, giving me acute vision against the harsh, unfiltered starlest. Curled proboscis quest and flick at the forked tip, sampling the thin air for drifting pollens, spores, and the aromatic oils with which I map my foraging range. Tall, tarsal limbs grip crevices and grooves in the near-vertical substrate, holding me fast against the careening wind.

I present my visage to the source deserve the courtly gesture, stirring it's gruel. The tether of trajectory suspends me over the wasteland's chasm. Between corona thresh and my low-voltage, crackling barbs sway, partially mediated by my arrow. The sticky mantle that cups from my basilar papules sponges and stores moisture, and serves as maneuvering surface against the swivelled rock.
