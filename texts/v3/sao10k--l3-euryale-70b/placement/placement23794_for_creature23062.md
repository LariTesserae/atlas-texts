Perhaps I would be one of the reed creels here in this watery landscape of ancient balance between land, water, and creature. As such, I would be one of the few humans who call this eerie and fecund place home. Like the rest of my kind, my origins are far-flung, and the story of how I came to this place is long.

We reed-creels are the inveiglers of this land - mystics and artificers and sorcerers of various stripe who came to this region centuries back, only to find it already burgeoning with power. Our robing is dyed a particularly vivid green by the alchemical means granted us by the ancestral weft-cerning traditions in which we trained, out in the distant antipodes gone to seed. The reeds and sedge-gass of the tortuous waterways provide our shelter, as well as the material for our books and instruments of rite.

Mine may be the most pungent of smoke you spied afar off making its steady banner against the bleak sky. I live in one of the ample dredged spaces excavated in the banksides, woven in a inconspicuious portion of river_combined with whose spiral causey winds about its grassy levee. Mine is an active one, and my regular comings and evening goings have run the vegetation byre to a craze of receding loams and choked reeds and gauyhtned pools around narrow causeys from the riverside to drying structures up above the feotid mire.

By day, the exeruptions of estuaries and evapitory accretions is a little more proudly apparent than elsewhere, but no less serenely entrained. As my circles and bookes and instruments altere the wether, my office is to tend the ages-long stases of the eternal dance in this place - to keep it betwixt and between in this happy medium of wet and dry, flesh and mire. The is a freedom in it.
