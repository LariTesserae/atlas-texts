In the frigid darkness of the abyssal plain, I find myself as an anglerfish, drifting through the slow waters, my lure casting a faint bluish glow in the darkness. Like my brethren, my face is overleptine and my eyes bulbous, adapted to the total absence of sunlight that has bathed this deep-sea ecosystem since eons past. Copepods cluster on my scales, their bioluminescent bodies casting hazy pearls and cilia in the black water.

I swim through the sedimental fields, the fine particulates of silt and clay drifting over the abyssal trench in dappled blankets. The occasional skitter of a shrimp or lollop of a gelatinous jellyfish breaks the monotony, their undulating bodies pulsing in viscous heaps. All around me, bioluminescent invertebrates cluster together in cylindrical colonies, feeding on upwelling fluids on all sides and probing the current with curling setae.

Now and then, a giant isopod scutters out from beneath a fold in the sediment layers, its seven hooved legs propelling it through the delicately etched silt. Sea stars move by me in slow motion, overturning the sediment into furrows and mounds in their wake. The ceaseless water currents are constantly in action here, meandering through the seafloor in undulating scrolls and refugia.

Eldritch creatures lurk in the darkness alongside me. Dumbo octopuses siphon fluid with flickering umbrella lascivious subvectors. Ghost shrimp chatter and posture to each other, flickering antennae siphoning and digging. Foraminifera undulate from cilia-taking cysts. Radiolaria subjugate pewter-dancing osteophanes.
