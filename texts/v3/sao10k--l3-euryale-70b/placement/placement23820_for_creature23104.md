In this harsh, polluted world of crumbling ruins, I find myself embodied as one of the small, porterous beings that forage for metal among the collapsed shale mine tunnels. My armored chitin plates rattle and scrape as I skitter across steeply-pitched rock faces on six splayed legs. Multifaceted eyes, ruby red and bulbous, are set like jewels across my torso. I feel my way with sensory stalks, tasting the air and dark stone as I clamber through the tunnels.

The acrid fluids from my book-lung in the acrid air sting my eye ball, but I am used to it. My chitin plates are dull black and have strarted to grow in other strange, jagged formations that make me look like a scrawny, hump-backed thing as I lope through tunnel after tunnel. It is hard to remember what my kind were like before the transition to life in the tunnel system. Now we are inextricably bound to this maze of stone and stale fluids, perpetual seeking nodules of unworked metal between the collapsed ceilings and walls.

The rustling of my compound legs echo back and forth from wall to wall as I clamber onward, pushing through a rancid pond of effluent gathered in a depressed hall. I scuttle faster in order to escape the fumes. My residual gills flare to syphon miniscule oxygen from it, before I feel the air get fresher on the other side of the pool as I climb into a side-trance...to find an expansive domed chamber of shale and obsidian. When I look up, I recoil and skitter back, rearing up in alarm at what I see.

There, hanging from the high, stalactite-dripping roof, are bones - not fossils, not remains of the degenerate beasts that plainly roamed up on the surface, but newly dead piles of bones of my own kind. The bones have been pulled free of all nerve, cartilege and softer tissue, picked clean by unseen tainted air and scuttling insects, to gleam in the ruby light of my many eyes like shelves of death...I shiver. Three steps back and I am skittering, legs to motion, towards some more anonomus tunnel, not thinking but propelled in some species-ancestral horror, my kind's instinct to flee the bodies of my own dead.
