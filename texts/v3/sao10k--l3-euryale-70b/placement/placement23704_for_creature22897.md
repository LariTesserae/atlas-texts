I find myself as a newly evolved species in this sunken cathedral - the hydra-hives. Born from some primordial bilge deep beneath the central island, we are pulsing lumps of translucent greenish flesh. Slow and drowsy, we secrete tendrils of mucus that cling to the walls and allow us to secrete chemical secretions that spur the growth of algae and slime. Our blobbly bodies sprout towering, fleshy occipital fronds that we wave and wiggle in the fog-layer. We have no true eyes, but are able to feel the reverberations of the environment in our suctorial undersides.

Individually we are not too bright. But our minds are webbed through a sprawling sub-neural net of slimetendrils, slowly processing information around us. We can feel the cathedral in the stone, the fog in the air, the beings in every shadow. We can induce the more complex entities around the cathedral to do our bidding with careful manipulations of slowed seceretons.

The aquamorphs have taken to installing sensors and relais into their mechanical island, working to interface them with us. The tubules, sensing perhaps the essence of our kind, have started to laze into the water, linked to the slime net of our descendants younger siblings beneath the waves. We have even managed to worm a part of our hypha into the skulls of the nightstrider bats, sparkling our jeweled fronds to flare and dim and instruct them to fly to the drier reaches of the cathedral so that we may guide the rest of our race to crawl into the worse sterile pockets.

Noise and feedback and secereton always broadcasts to us the happenings of this drowned space, a steady sense of criticism from our billion facets. You are not intelligent, but you remember - and you remember well. Slow and lazy for the most, when we do rouse to mush our bulk in new directions, the entire cathedral sounds out in response, an echoing disturbance from every slime blob. To us, this is home, our good broth, and we feel nothing more pleasant than to wallow and waddle in our ancestral drink. The islandhoppers fear and hate us for our mystery, but know enough to give us space. The aquamorphs yet seek to capture some of our strategy for themselves, those scuttling busy bees. But in truth, they understand nothing, having no spit to slick our complex process.

You will be terrible, sneaking and slow and everywhere at once, until everything is you and no part of this cathedral is not-slime. And even once that is so, no matter, you will be happy. You will steiss to the humm of the fungi and the teasing saliva of the bats. Your deep slow intelligence, and the bleeding familiarity of the cathedral, will be your own reward. And, in millenia's time, all will know and celebrate the great hydra that once was you.
