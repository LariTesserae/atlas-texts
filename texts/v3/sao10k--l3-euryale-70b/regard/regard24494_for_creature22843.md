Beholding that desolate and otherworldly vista, a benevolent and wise power is moved by the primal beauty and tortured ancient history held in those rust-hued stones. While bereft of all signs of life, the land itself speaks of epochs past when oceans thrashed upon its shores and rivers carved graceful valleys through the rising stone. The pinnacles and pillar-towers that stud the plain evoke a sense of veneration, as if they might be the fossilized remains of unimaginably colossal beings.

The power wishes to encourage new life to arise within this timeless furnace of earth and sky, yet respects the strange harmony and terrible beauty of this realm too much to radically alter it. And so, the power chooses to make subtle changes, ones that will act slowly over long ages rather than transform the landscape suddenly.

First, the power nurtures the small pockets of trapped moisture and mineral-rich pigment that stud the stone faces. It stirs the sediments that lie within these concavities, causing minute veins of copper and other metals to leach out and paint the rock in subtle hatchets of color more vibrantly than ever before. The metallic salt finely leeching into the thirsty soil imbues it with a faint vital energy.

Next, the power causes the stone itself to subtly transform - allowing it to shelter more water than before. Through the slowst of geological time, the bereft loams and clays that still cling to the desollated land grow thicker and more lush, eventually giving rise to the first stirrings of vegetation. The first microphant shoots of plantlife arise there - cryptogams, epipetric clastic vines, and other pioneering species that can endure the extreme conditions.

Slowly, the stain of pigment from the flecks cradled in the monoliths leaches into the soil alongside the metals and metalsalts, giving those first colonizing organisms a rosy, rustshot hue similar to the landscape that birthed them. As they live and die, their bodies enrich the soil further, nurturing the next generation to grow taller and more robust.

In this way, the wise and benevolent power does not impose life on this tortured realm, but rather encourages it to re-arise organically, grown from the potential latent in the land itself over countless centuries. And the successive generations of flora and the fauna that come to dwell amongst it forever bear the signature of the earth that birthed them in hues of ochre, umsher and crimson.
