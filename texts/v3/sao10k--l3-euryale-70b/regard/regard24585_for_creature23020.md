This arid, barren canyon owes its limited life to rare, bloody flashfloods that have carved out a network of pockmarks in its clay walls over the ages. Each small, tightly-packed opening leads to a fleshy being clotted in a burrow, connected to its neighbors by a gray, turgid web of trails.

Each is a strange amalgam - a puckered, undulating anus wedged in silt, occasionally parting to reveal gummy, wrinkled flesh that slurps and shudders with the slow dance of peristalsis. Their movements are pinned to the "heartbeat" of the few insects and worms that live here too, drawn by the burrows' moisture. 

Time seems to flow differently for them. Days or weeks may pass in the time it takes one to fully emerge and slurp a morsel. Each is in slow, turgid contact with its neighbors through oozy burrows in colors to match the clay: manganese-red, carmine, ochre, and - in the depths - motled black.
