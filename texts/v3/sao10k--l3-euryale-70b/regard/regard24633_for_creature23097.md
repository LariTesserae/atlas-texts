At that moment of chaos, a trembling began in the bedrock beneath the city's foundations. But it was not an earthquake - this was a movement borne of intelligence, of purpose. The very stone beneath the Bazaar shuddering and then surging, rising up to form seven great rings of primordial earth.

The city's sentience had been recognized, and now primal powers stirred in answer. Seven etched rings pushed up from the dusty streets, great platforms of sediment glittering with bubbling minerals. In the center ring stood up a towering golem of basalt, its form humanoid yet craggy, blazing with inner light.

Its bark voice thundered out, speaking in a tongue before tongues, a language of searing heat and ancient secrets. All fell silent as the earth spoke again. 

"I am the jewel of primordial people, I am the protector of the clave, I am the arm of antiquity."

Slowly, reverently, the inhabitants turned to watch the golem. A smiling blue-skinned child reached up towards its stony hand.

Voice of earth boomed out once more:

"I have woken to rise to your defense. I will be your shield against the Sunless Ones beyond."

All eyes turned to the city walls then, to the cheering guardsmen silhouetted against the evening sky. The blue humanoid did not look at them, but enfolded the child in its arms. 

"The Bazaar's form shall grow from my sedimentary soil. Let the Sunless Ones beat their flesh upon my stones. I am the city's failsafe. I am your first splendor."

And as the great being spoke, its form began to change, its rugged mineral crusting and stratifying, becoming one with the crimson bluff by the river. No longer a giant of basalt, but basalt and sandstone and banded ore, a section of cliff wall gifted sentience and purpose. 

A murmur passed the crowd, then a cheer, then a chant. They named the ancient golem, and the name echoed like a prayer:

Anza Ser Suluus. The Unyielding Mountain. The Golem of the Bazaar. 

Let this be the story of how the Bazaar was gifted a guardian, and Anza Ser Suluus took its sacred place upon the city walls, at the end of the first day.
