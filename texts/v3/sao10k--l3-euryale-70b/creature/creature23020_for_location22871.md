The canyon walls are alive and breathing, covered in fuzzy patches of moss and ferns sprouting from cracks in the red rock. Tiny rivulets snake down the sides, carved over centuries. The air is thick with moisture from recent rains and an underlying scent of damp earth and green growth. 

In the shaded crevices between rocks at the bottom of the canyon, signs of primitive fungal life cling to silt-covered stones - faeces-like blobs with hairline tendrils stretching out to merge with their neighbors. They pulse slowly in synchronicity with subterranean waters trickling through narrow channels gouged in clay mud.

Giant toadstools push up through blankets of fallen leaves, caps waxy and vibrant hues, stems nearly luminescent against rich brown humus. Some have split open, oozing viscous white fluids that feed tiny black worms burrowing into woodsy loam.

The ground itself ripples and bubbles softly like seething lazaagna as unseen creatures tunnel just beneath scant topsoil intersperced with bits of decomposing plant matter. Squat armored beetles skitter across it, antennae brushing strands of spider web that crisscross in 3D networks linking every stone to its neighbors.

A flash of iridescent blue catches the eye - a butterfly newly emerged from a cocoon attached to a stony flaw by gossamer threads drips fluid still wet wings furled around body curled tight as a fist until they expand fully to shimmer in cupped light filtering through layered mists rising off flowing water's edges.

Everywhere here is teeming turgid fertility borne on slow cycles of water and minerals drawn up sinewy roots to nourish lush verdant growth which decays back into soil after serving as habitat to legions swarming smaller creatures before returning again transformed. An ancient engine lazily rumbling ahead heedless of time as we know it...
