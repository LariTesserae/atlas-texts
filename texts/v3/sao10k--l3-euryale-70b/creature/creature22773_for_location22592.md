Amidst the primordial stillness, life abounds, though much of it lies hidden beneath the thick loam and murky waters. Tiny fairy shrimp and jewel-toned newts dart through the clear ponds, startling at the sudden shadow of a dragonfly. Minnows flicker like silver coins, catching the languous shafts of light in their scales. Ancient crayfish glower eyelessly from their crevices, waving barbed antennae. The ponds are the resting place of grand koi, descendants of those first released by an emperor millennia ago. Their scales flare like living jade, vermillion, and gold as they rise to suck the surface for mosquito larvae. 

In the air, birds of vibrant plumage flit furtively - cerulean kingfishers, men-of-war, great iridescent herons, and tiny jewel-like fairy-bluebirds that glitter like hummingbirds against the canopy. Swarms of jewel-colored dragonflies dance between the reeds, stopping on broad lily pads where sedate frogs croak contentedly. Bejeweled beetles clamber up tree trunks and iridescent butterflies flutter amongst the flowers.

High above, the canopy is the realm of the flying foxes, nocturnal and frugiverous. These giant bats have dwelled in the density of the canopy since antediluvian days, huts of moss built in the boughs of vast father trees housing whole clans. Diurnal, they flee this world for the shadows as the sun slants exponentially, to return with dusk, ululating their mournful cries. By night they rise in a dark tide, wheeling and surfing the air currents above the clot-myriad stars. Below, owls boom and fireflies pulse in the dark to the eternal basso ostinato of the frogs. 

And everywhere, clambering up the trunks, squishing spores into the nooks, swinging from the limbs, and running in the leaf litter there are monkeys. Colobus in their lacy white mantles like senators' robes; macaques with their impossible strength, nosing through the ferns for frogs, lizards, and eggs; silvered leaf monkeys, all-dun and high-crested, accepted as kings; sinister rhesus with faces pinched like crones; langurs with their fingers and submissions like the hands of beauticians; chattering gibbon pairs, hand-in-hand, the alpha's sweet falsetto wandering the treetops. The forest remembers an age before men. Ruins older than memory speak of a presence that has long since fled. Yet the older presence remains, making its cheerful and confidence use of the colossal fibre as the wheel of years turns monotonously on.
