The misty mountain valley is home to a close-knit community of learned scholars, master craftsmen, and earnest students. They reside in the rustic yet sturdy wooden huts huddled together for warmth and protection against the biting alpine winds. 

The scholars, cloistered in their obsidian black academy, toil ceaselessly to expand the boundaries of human knowledge. Some pour over yellowed, crumbling tomes in the library's great cedar hall, transcribing abtruse rune and cipher. Others hunch over workbenches, intently focused on intricate upperworks of brass and copper, assembling complex horologic automatons. 

The most wise and ancient adept among them, rumored to have communed with the great winged bronze varix itself, makes her salon high in the dome-topped tower. From an ornate filigreed oriel window, she gazes out upon the hamlet and the mist-shrouded peaks, studying the crimson and amber light of dawn. Is she running calculations of orbital conjunction and stellar alignments to determine the auspicious timing of a sacred, ancient ritual of the order? Or simply ruminating in solitude before beginning the day's lessons with the novices and initiates?

The sigh of pipe-organs and tintinnabulation of bells drift down from the tower as the adept leads plainsong hymns in the chapel. The believers, shouldering scamels plaited and limned in silver filigree, chant invocations of the maker of the bronze. Copies of the varix, small automata cleft from brass, perch everywhere like bird-shaped gargoyles, watching the townspeople with emerald and sapphire eye. They trill staccato mechanical chalune phrases of the cantos.

As the granite-pocked peaks blaze to agate and fire-brick red in the first lucent light, the townspeople of the hamlet begin their diurnal routines. Young girls ferry ewers of kettle-steaming sowansilk up from the benditional hall overlookning the jets of the wdrawable well. Old men creak back from the smiriestër forges, arms sawdust-scalded and boot-soles speckled with white from the kiln. Presumptive scholar-students borne on gamy retinue-sled, scholar-caps hanging gray to their ears, slide clattersnow and rag-foot compacting the drifts to the academy parvis.

It is the lambing season; the shepherds are abroad, birthing lambs by lantern by naked stone. Their nimble collie dogs shrill keen to summon the flocks into the lambing fields. Overhead slinks a great bronze stalk-thin automata of a peregrin falcon, green-painted eyes noon-bright and winking, swooping to survey the valley. Fiddleheads of rosy ice still cling on decliveties and shelf-breeze carved out of the peaks. The hamlet's fountains gush gallons of font-oxanting freshwater, ladled into washbasins, scuttles and lidded fix-pipes. Shiver-tasseled apricocks and domnæilian hens, draked in dried feathers, skirl and skirl, piping to the dawning of the world. The wather bell, rung by an unseen machine behind the gard, claps astronomers and shepherds and schoolars and the rest to the long day of building and the endless research ahead.
