The deep subterranean realm is teeming with bizarre, nightmarish life adapted to survive in this hellish environment devoid of light and sound. Sight and smell are irrelevant here - instead, organisms navigate by sensitivity to temperature, chemical gradients, vibration and touch.

Amongst the writhing fungal stalks and fibrous tendrils that form canopies over the caverns dwell clusters of eyeless creatures. They have bulbous bodies covered in twitching cilia that tremble at the faintest chemical shift in the air. Pseudopods extend from their spongy flesh, tasting for nutrients in the mineral soup they float through. Occasionally they bud off new offspring which drift away into the burbling fluid currents.

Skittering across pendant roots and cables are segmented arthropodal beasts - things with too many limbs to count. Their chitin exoskeletons are etched with intricate patterns that shimmer opaquely in what passes for light down here. Clusters of waving setae sample every surface they cross while mandibles grind incessantly nearby. These multi-limbed scavengers scour for carrion or detritus amidst the wrack line where rock meets water.

Flopping about in stagnant pools are gel-sacked creatures that hoot dismal chords from throbbing sacs along their backs as they wallow. Strings of globular organs undulate within translucent skin sacks stretched out around them like bloated bladders. Fungal hyphae have invaded some individuals and sprout from rents in their integuments, growing thick fruiting bodies that spew spores into the air.

Shambling on bowed legs between towers of fungal meat are ox-like beasts shedding flaking keratin scales tinged shades of ochre and sienna with spicy secretions trickling down their sides. Tusks curve up from gaps between plating jaws bearing only molars, used for grinding tough mycelium and algae scoured up on swinging snouts from crevices in mineral encrustations. Heavy tails flick back and forth as it searches for the next patch of food amongst knotted mats of symbiotic floræ.

Roosting upside-down inside dripping caves mouthed by rings of hook-formations are blind salamanders, long-necked necks leading to spotted heads gaping wide to bare rows upon rows of needle teeth stained orange-brown by iron oxide dissolved in the water seeping past stalactites overhead. Wormy tongues slurp stray nutrients drifting through before snapping shut with audible clicks against serrated radula upon finding something suitably proteinacious to eat.

In alcoves alongside petroglyphs etched along walls pockmarked by hemispherical voids left behind when spherical fossils weathered free archaeologists believe these were once ritual sites sacred to some long-age cult - likely prehuman - based on reliefs depicting serpentine deities rising from seas to walk upright on land...or "land".
