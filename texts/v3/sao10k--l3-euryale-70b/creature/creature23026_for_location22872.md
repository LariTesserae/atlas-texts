Upon this bleak and blasted landscape, there dwell an array of organisms that have adapted to the harsh conditions. Small, insectoid creatures scurry between the rocks - arachnids the size of a man's hand, their carapaces hard and multi-jointed. Some have uplifted mandibles that click and chitter as they move. Others have multiple eyes that gleam with predator eagerness.

Amphibious beasts dwell in the cauldrons of the hidden waterways. They are bulbous and warty, clad in glistening skin that can absorb moisture from the air. Beady eyes on stalks rotate independently to survey surroundings. Their mouths are filled with needle-like teeth. Long, coiled tongues snap out to snare unwary insects or stunned fish.

Mammals, great and small, eek out meager lives. Hopping rat-like mammals, furred and whiskered, nibble the sparse vegetation. A smaller variety have burrowed into the cracked earth, scrabbling flea-like beneath the surface. Large ton-kine creatures,.planar and slab-sided, their coats patchy with plate-armor scales, forage in the barrens. Horned and tusked, the bulls use their brutish strength to rip clumps of wiry grass from between rocks.

Some creatures are refugees from the subsurface, fleeing the dark and the machinations of their industrious brethren. Their exposures are ghostly pale, their limbs atrophied. No light-grafted flora have grown on this poverty-stricken skin. Some shuffle and drag their fragile forms over the ground as if surprised by such abrupt exposure.
