Amidst this primordial scene of rock and atmosphere, life finds space to cling. Lichens paint themselves across boulders in streaks and splotches, their fungal hyphae extending into any crack or pore for purchase. Tough stalks of bearberry and grouse whortle anchor themselves in shards of soil, their waxy needles and course furze-leaves conserving moisture beneath them. 

Even the bare mineral faces host colonies - where a patch of dust has collected, desiccated insects scuttle along hairline fractures. Their chitinous exoskeletons are tinted violet and ochre by contact with the rocks' chromic hues. Some few spider webs strung between outcroppings shimmer with dewdrops, each silken thread picked out in prismatic colors by the refracted light.

Closer to the streams, where the soil is moister, moss mats carpet every surface in emerald velvet. Delicate shoots emerge from this greenery - fairy cups, elfin saddles, and dead man's fingers all sprout their cupped caps and phallic heads amidst the liverworts and sphagnum. Fungi have established symbioses across the landscape too - morels pushing up beside roots, chanterelles growing shelflike off trunks, lobster mushrooms encrusting fallen branches in lurid orange.

Life collects itself tight against life in these meager niches. One can imagine still smaller organisms sheltering unseen amongst the visible flora - tardigrades drying out in puddle-bottoms until revivified by rainfall; nematodes burrowing blindly through capillary networks of root hairs; lichen-dwelling rotifers spinning merry-go-rounds at the tips of foliose lobules.
