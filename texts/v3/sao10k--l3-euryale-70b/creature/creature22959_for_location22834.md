The jagged cliffs host a multitude of creatures well-adapted to life amidst stone and vertiginous heights. Hardy goats are perhaps most iconic, their nimble hooves and surefooted gait allowing them to traverse the sheer rock faces with an ease belying their size. Thick, shaggy coats insulate them against biting winds and adenine cold. They nibble lichen and scrub from crevice and ledge with patient persistence.

Other creatures make the cliffs home as well - small rodents scurry between crags, darting furtively under rocks and into cracks. Lizards cling motionless to warm spots when the sun breaks through cloud cover. Hawks, eagles and vultures ride thermals above, scanning for prey far below in piercing gazes.

The towering cliffs themselves seem almost alive, great slabs of stone canted at chaotic angles by unfathomable geologic violence aeons ago. Gnarled roots of hard-bitten trees burst from fractures like veins of wood. Ferns and moss carpet any surface not shielded from wind-scoured stone. It's easy to imagine these ancient rock-walls as ruins of some long-lost city, cycloptic towers hacked by giants of myth rather than sculpted by unrelenting weather.

Shadows shift across naked rock in patterns both stark and subtle - slashes of black shade cleaving silver basalt, dusk gathering in hidden cavities like velvet-lined stony rooms. The cliffs can look unreal, timeless and changeless yet haunted by ghosts of cataclysms past.
