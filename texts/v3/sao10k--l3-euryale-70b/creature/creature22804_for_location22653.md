Deep within the primordial depths of this planet's core lies a ancient, ancient world, a hidden realm of convergent evolution and proto-life. The starlit lake, its luminous waters undisturbed by the stilled currents, is the abode of an antediluvian flora and fauna, unchanged for eons, just as it emerged in the first flickers of life.

On the lake's surface, the fallen star plankton drift; a slow-moving, shapeless herbivore drifts stately between the pale, fleshy leaves of the lank, sedate vegetation. The plants plunge their leaves into the placid waters to forage for some lost nutrient, while ichor-red berries dangle, gleaming in the starlight.

On the artificial islands rising from the crystalline depths, great halls and towers stretch to pierce the obsidian darkness above. Carved bridges arch delicately between these floating lands, their moonlit eternal scenes lit by the protean mist swirling through the bizarre scene. In this fundamental realm, evolution moves at the scale of continents colliding, an eternally slow dance at a vast, almost imperceptible pace.

Time flows strange here, still as the instant before a cataclysm, all action toward further stasis. Metastability holds sway with no ambition for change. The heavy, viscous acids fume and churn in the lost air, drawn from the bedrock at this depth. All breaths and pulses are reduced to trickle.

The single, soughing sound of the realm emerges from the compressed cells of every living thing, their contributions to the world-song compacted and heavy, a dense transmission, shared and locking them all lament into a slow, deliberate, orienting sound, from which the worms and their ilk can measure the fathomless depth to the lake's yielding mucus, and know by the arrangement of stillness and chaf at the heart of their senescence how they may best stretch to reach the faint signal of possible competition, just bordered by stimulations to lie in fermentation at the levels for their generation, before hurling meiotically out from their meiosis their immediate salvation.
