Amidst the shattered rock and cyclopean ruins, life of all kinds clings to existence in the bleak valley. The furred, saucer-eyed herd animals graze warily on fungi, lithe bodies poised for flight at any unknown threat. Their snouts twitch and flinch as unknown sounds echo through the mists. 

In the lee of ruined towers and erratic stone uplifts, other creatures huddle - small things of fur and feathers and chitin. Tiny furred snakes, red-eyed and feisty, hiss and spit from the shelter of moss and nest-stuff. Blind tube worms ooze through the brackish soil, feeding on shallow roots and spores. Burred fanged land leeches attach to warm-blooded prey, gorging on blood. In the running sheet-wash and scouring deluges of the storms, they cling tenaciously to boulders as the water threatens to scour them away.

Cloaked feltylads in billowing robes stalk the fog like wraiths, stalking on stilts through the muck and fossil-scoured boulders. Their high-pitched nasal argots cut through the storm din as they chitter and chatter in unknown tongue. With glowing staff and probing gloved hands, they cast longitudes seeking artifacts of power in the ancient stones. Atneed, they have been known to slay the grazer-beasts with crackling skyfire, triumphantly brandishing the still-warm carcases. Their hovels and hideaways are cunningly woven amongst the tumbled stones, as old as the ruins themselves.

Couring the rusting iron posts and cryptic inscriptions are other folk - rude, grunting troll-kyn, the detritus of forgotten wars and hatebloods and wrier cults. Hefting cyclopean clubs of stone and rusting irons, wild-eyed and rag-mantled, they guard their domain with homicidal paranoia. They sleep in the bellies of toppled arches, glutted from the feltylad's foodstores and sedimentary bushes of spore and mosses. Wheedled and hollow-eyed children peep out from the shadows, malnourished and mute as mushroom growths.

Amidst the ruin and detritus, the very rock itself seems to writhe and twist, to writhe efflorescent in eldritch ochres and grime-greens and earthy ambers. One might spy glows and smolders of unknown stones and earthworks. Luminous creeping mists writhe away in the dark like living smoke, as if they grasp and curdle the failing light from the storming sky. The brackish rain hisses and fizzes as it hits the hot fractured rock, filling hewns and scour drifts with stinking brine. Sulfurous vapors reek and stritch in the mist. The split rock belches sporadically, heaving with swaddling heat. In the unpeeling deeps, one may imperfectly sense hugh wet things lurking, immense and loathsome.

Thus it is and was and may ever be in that fallen land, where rock and earth and water seethe in uncanny rhythm as during the Age before Ages. And all things that crawl or trod or slump through that valley happen to their fates with grim heed, as if scryed by the shattered cyclopean towers and rusting emblems of the forgotten, who wrestled with the land itself to their shameful dooms.
