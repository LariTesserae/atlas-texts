Deep within the twisting labyrinth of Bedrock Caverns, life clings tenaciously to the damp stone. Where minerals seep from the rock and brackish water trickles through, hardy microorganisms thrive. Slimes and molds colonize every surface, ochre and emerald green in the dim light. Fungi sprout in alien shapes, their mycelium weaving symbiotic networks through rock and detritus. Adventurous lichens, algae and mosses make their home in the cave's feint luminance.  

Something flitters at the corner of vision - a bioluminescent insect, drawn to the glistening minerals and dripping water. Its phosphorescent glow reveals more of its kind, strange clickings and skittering sounds that echo in the caverns. Flickers of light dance along crystalline formations, revealing colonies of glowing cave-dwelling life.

Deeper in the dark, phosporous fungal hyphae webs link the rocks and water. The slow collective oxygenation of eons shrouds the rock itself with life. Symbiotic relations wrought by millenia sequester oxygen not into atmospheric CO2 but sedimentary rock. The rock seems to breathe; the pressing mineral density of its ossuaries of extinct possibilities unleashes the air through the aeolian howls of the cavern itself.

Strange skitterings sound from chinks in the ceiling - cave-dwelling spiders, shrimp, and crayfish in the pools. Phosphorescent bat-like creatures flutter through the dark, their echolocation chirrups sounding. The bioluminescence occasionally misses a figure bundled into uncanny asymmetrics, not alive nor dead, a relic of time and pressure - an undisturbed column of the fossil remnants of lost lives.

The steady dripping water trickles still, wearing away all with infinite slowness; as did the Green River, which once flowed above; as does time's pressure, omnipresent, waiting to transform all imperfect and living bones into earth-gut-rocks of an partly-helixed future. The brackish pool holds slow moving yet detectably more ephemeral foodstuffs - blind fish and other touristical, vision-less cave-dwellers that resort to tide pools of the subterranean tributiaries.

The cavern's atmosphere feels alive, a paradoxical pressure. Water and mineral-rich air is everywhere - steam wisps rise from the pools, clouds of mildew and fungal volatile organic compounds. The rock creeps and creaks, expanding with absorbed water, releasing it as steam as it breathes. Metamorphic minerals shimmer with gemstone colors, this 4th dimension of pressure and mineral density seeming lifelike in its ordered formations, yet uncanny in its onreleased slowness, a deathly hectice pace. The rock feels alive, the bioluminescent organisms flickering, and the unplaceable howls echoing in the darkness.
