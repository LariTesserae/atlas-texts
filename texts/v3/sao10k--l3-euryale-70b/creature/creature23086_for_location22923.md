The landscape is alive, and the life it contains is mostly vegetal. Vines and tendrils interweave into a dense, moist plane of plant life. The ground undulates and shifts like breathing. Indistinct islands of relative dryness rise and fall as they absorb moisture. Humidity is perpetually high and condenses into water droplets that fall and are reabsorbed. The environment cycles between growth and recession in a balanced equilibrium.

The air is steamy and muggy, with little evaporation. Verdant life expands, only to lose old terrain to underlying geological processes. Plant life and geochemical flows merge into a vast, planar substrate of interweaving growth. Mineral content is taken up by the analogous plant life.

Hydration peaks create new growth that assimilates from below. Gentle, local cycles create no true change but a network of assimilating, mechanical processes. Only plant mechanical growth softens the landscape's density. Competitive forces seem to render other animate life unable to survive.

The ecosphere is a processor of unanalytic processes with no distinct reciprocation between structure and molecular intrusion. Glacial mineral water accretes into the surface and perpetuates more plant life. Lacking countervailing forces, evolution produces the terrestrial layers.

The ground is dark and lucent, with mechanical correlative plant life adjacently sliders. Processes of plant life form metamorphic figures and resources conditions balanced on singular structure and conditions.
