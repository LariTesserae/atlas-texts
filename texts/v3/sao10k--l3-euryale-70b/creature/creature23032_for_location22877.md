In this primal, teeming valley, a cacophony of wild life thrives in the mists and canyons. The rivers boil over smooth, glassy stones worn slick by eons of swirling water. The air is thick with humidity, almost muggy, yet there is a dryness too, as if the sky siphons moisture from the land each evening only to release it again in the sultry morning.

Every crag and crevice is slammed with dense, climbing vegetation - thorny brambles with succulent leaves, vines twisting up the towering trees, choking off their life. The trees themselves tower like huge, green-roofed monuments, their trunks gnarled and fissured, wrapped in creepers thick as rope. Emerald and violet, every hue of plantlife clambers, sprawls, writhes, overthrowing the stone walls. Fierce shadows choke the forest floor under an onslaught of ivy and fern and thudder.

It is stiflingly hot, breathless, the sun a hammer. Yet there is a coolness to the rock and shadow. The canyons carve the valley in huge, branching fissures - sheer cliffs, crumbling scree, tumbling boulders, all etched and incised. As the sun sinks, it sets fire to the whole scene in shades of molten orange and red, like a glowing lace. Then the first stars burst forth like embers, followed by a billion glinting sparks. The Milky Way looms like a luminous fog.

Under the towering cliff overhangs, the landscape crackles and swarms with life, a physical pressence that quiets to near stillness in tumbling water's roar. Few sounds penetrate the air apart from rushing water and the distant, half-perceptible rustlings of insects and the scattered small tremors hinting at scorpioning life scrambling beneath the leaf-litter. A unseen bird suddenly shrieks, then there is the clatter of a rockslide. The endless patter of churning brooks. From overhead, the susurrus of unseen wings, like a conversation maddeningly just out of earshot. It is as trying to pick out one voice in a hushed bar. All is life, yet conducted in a swamp, a stately creeping, as if even this writhing plantlife is somehow ancient, somehow weighed down and dulled merely by mass and its tenancy of this place.

A humming, unstoppable, oppressive chaos, a riot round the edges, tugging at the soul like the tangled snag of braken on the sleeve, some jitter at the urgent squirm of its delirium, as if a chaos would consume one, as if one's chromosome itself rings with some darkness glimpsed in a forest clearing, some tempest glimpsed at the eye of a hurricane. The land understands - one can see it in its battered and eroded hectares.
