Immense, spherical clutch of gnarled bony material prongs reach from the glassy purple ground, sharp tips piercing high into the slightly thick white sky, yet everything inert, motionless, silent. Nearing the bases of the prongs, curls of ground-up plant material crunch under my feet, lush growth also completely still, rigid - I can't see a single leaf that has dropped. Trees gelatinous amber 30 feet tall, insides glowing weakly, bubbly liquid holding them erect. Their trunks segmented in 4" lengths like sausages and pinched waists between them. No birds. No insects. No wind.

The sky above pure white with a brown ring around the yellowish sun. I take out a magnifying glass, look at an amber trunk. A cluster of inch-wide holes dot the surface. Looking through a glass I see little iridescent insect bodies swaying like puppets in the holes, sticking almost motionless head-inwards into the wood. Catch a quick flash of a small furry marsupialish mammal poking out from a knot in a tree trunk, seems frozen in mid-motion.

An artificial pink-purple lattice of beamed light appears abruptly overhead, passes like radar waves, triangulates the frozen scene, a grid of a million points. Glows through the amber wood like laser light passing through ice. I feel it pass through me, giving a tingle. The light disappears.

No sign of water that I've seen. No puddles, no dew, no running rivers. No wind shifting anything. A view out the keyhole of the viewfinder seems to show the whalloped 30ft amber crowns extending a hundred feet to the horizon in every direction. Everything I see seems to be held in a 10 foot upper layer of organic rich soil over the purplish mineral substrate.

When I scratch my hand, it comes away with a sugary smear. No celophane wraps, no foil, no centari, no big plastic artifacts lying around. This place seems untrammeled in the extreme. I can't see any sign water passed through here recently. From the looks of the amber trunks I'm guessing the material is similar to tree guts turned to hard candy by being cooked in its own sap in some kind of heat-blast. The question is what's keeping everything here from drooping or degrading.
