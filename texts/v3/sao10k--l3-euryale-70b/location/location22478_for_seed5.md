Deep in the misty, unchanging valleys, beyond the eroding cliffsides, lies a peculiar pocket of land known to the locals as Ooth'gavar Terroris: the Fearless Desolation. This cold, lushly-vegetated grotto is nestled between towering trees, their thick roots tangled into one another, forming natural barriers to outside influence. Puddles of water collect in every depression, fed by the frequent mists that shroud the place. The air hangs heavy with moisture, though true rain never falls.

Set against the verdant foliage stands an odd, ancient structure - an improbable city, packed into the valley like a green organic reef. Distinct buildings rise from the landscape in layers of rammed earth, half-timbered wooden frames, and intricately carved stones. The architecture seems to have grown more than it was constructed - walls twist at odd angles, doorways turn in on themselves, and sharp corners sprout into organic forms. Intricate glyphs, rendered in pale limestone, weave across the buildings like lichen on stones. They allude to esoteric runes of nature and magic, but none have deciphered their meaning to this day.

Life teems here, though its emanations are never heard. Frogs and insects texturize the air with muted calls. Unseen critters scuttle through hidden passages in the rambling buildings. Everywhere, water flows in burbling rivulets, ducts, and channels, providing power to all the town's operations. But no animal sound carries any distance - the place is bottled away from the outside world like a terrarium model.

The inhabitants of Ooth'gavar Terroris are themselves a hidden thing. When not working rolling lichen-dappled barrels of gleaming metal in the foundries, or knitting wind-chimes of twisted wood and painted bone, they spend their days carving delicate seals from combinations of grain and seed, communicating in sprechsprache. To the visitor, their language sounds like the sound of seeds pouring. Wrapped in layers of garment made of woven grass, digitalis, and bark, they aim to keep their town a secret.
