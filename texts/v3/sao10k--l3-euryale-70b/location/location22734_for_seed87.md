The frigid waters press in on all sides, condensed and dense. This is an underwater grotto – an intimate pocket buried in the seafloor, shielded by boulders and twisted coral. Shafts of weak light pierce the gloom from above, casting eerie spotlights on an otherwise dark realm.

The space is nearly a room, walled by stone and shells. The walls are pockmarked by untold numbers of small, irregular crevices - the ancient lairs of long-dead or long-vacated invertebrates. Bulbous colonies of bioluminescent algae cling to the rock, pulsing dimly. The light they provide seems to exacerbate rather than mitigate the dark, giving a hellish undertone to the grotto's surroundings.

Wreckage of wood and iron litters the space - splintered planks, barnacled cannons, piles of corroded coins. A sunken ship long since submerged pours its contents amidst the boulders. Rusted nails, hooks, harpoons. Shattered telescopes and other instruments. Gilt frames enclosing mildewed portraits. The earth has taken its own - a few mermetic blooms, swaying above a carpet of tangled ribbon-kelp and sponges. A mille-pore, a sea whip, a fiercesome thorny urchin, a gelatinous moonfish.

Alongside ancient containers filled with crumbling records, you find jars filled with preserved hallucinogens. Elongated, unblinking eyes stare up at you - it becomes clear these are viscera of some species, floating in the gallon containers filled with chemicals, preserved parts of something, yes, urgently alive, urgently strange, urgently watchful. The stink of formaldehyde ghosts the air, a cloying invasive miasma loaded heavy with the warning rot of old strange biologies. What manner of things were these? It does not bear considering.

Fabrics and urns and scrolls of ancient texts. Bones and dark glimmering arrowheads. Tattered flags of some nation nowhere now. How many shipwrecks contribute to this jumbled haul? Centuries' worth of flotsam and jetsam mixed in a vast underwater room. The almost imperceptible rush of currents carrying sediment swirl the detritus forever restless.

So this is the world that remains - a tub of the forgotten and the discarded, cut into the bone of the Earth by the sea. The effect is shocking. Quiet, mindful, but perpetually noteworthy rather than tranquil. How untold an unpleasantness lingers. What a malevolent place! And how engrossing - how demanding - the wild enigma it provides.
