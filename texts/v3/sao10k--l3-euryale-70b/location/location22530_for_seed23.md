The valley sits at the base of a towering mountain range, its steep peaks soaring upwards beneath a clear azure sky. Rugged cliffs and rocky outcroppings form the valley's perimeter, their weathered, flaking stone surfaces bleached nearly white by the unforgiving sun. In the depths of this arid valley basin, a small cluster of crude huts and smithy buildings are huddled together, the ramshackle dwellings of a humble town.

The built environment here is unassuming and rough-hewn, the buildings constructed using local stone and wood, with roofs of thatch, hide, or tarpaulin when repairable. Narrow, packed-dirt streets wind between the structures. Livestock enclosures corrals and ramadas are interspersed with the homes. Rising smoke shows the presence of cooking hearths and forges. A few fresher constructions in progress spread beyond the edges of the older settlement, demonstrating a modest but promising growth trend.

Water is a rarity in this parched land, but the space above the valley holds the secret to their agricultural success. High above, a vast eternal snowfield gleams among the peaks. Each year, when the sun grows strong, a trickle swells to a torrent flows from the snowmelt, plunging cascading down the sheer valley walls before spilling out into an oasis at the base. A rambling complex of terraced irrigated plots have been carved into the rubble at the foot of the cliffs, fed by canals and aqueducts plumbed into the waterfall. Yams, beans, and tubers grow lush and green in this small strip of arable land.

The ring of stony dirt beyond the oasis is dotted with hardy scrub vegetation and infested with a profusion of small life - horned beetles, thorny reptiles, burrowing mammals. Naked roots tufts of bleached grasses wave at the base of the rocks. Heatshimmers dance above the naked stone, creating a surreal mirage effect.

Scalewise, the valley covers an area equivalent to a moderate city park or perhaps 25 acres. The cliffs and slopes beyond are immaculate and evacuated, other than the barest whiffs of mist and wind above the snowfield far up the stony uplands. So the actual useable realty is a confined perhaps 2-5 acres.

Weirdnesswise, the place seems to be cosmic, otherworldly, almost diabolical. The sharp difference between the bright dried desert oasis landscape in the valley and the glowering snowy crags above is surreal. The sense of threat from the sheer exposed stone and wind, the winter isolation, and the science-fictional cliff aqueducts makes it strikingly alien. The cheery lush crops in distorting contestation with the minerar ombience punches up the surreal unease. While a purely human settlement, it seems to transcend into minor cosmos all its own.
