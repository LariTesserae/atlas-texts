In the high cliffs and narrow gorges of this vast and turbulent land, an eerie dimness permeates the air, even in the day. Low fog swirls around the craggy, recently-exposed rock formations that jut out at impossible angles, as if vomited up from the churning earth itself. Lush vegetation in vivid greens and purples clings to every surface, from delicate vines to oversized mushrooms three meters tall, their caps a canopy over the damp paths winding through valleys choked with undergrowth.

The violent geography betrays the land's youth - canyons carved by sudden storms, sharp peaks thrust up by organic subterranean forces, landscapes that trek across the scale of miles in an instant. Billowing clouds / lightning storms hover in the upper reaches of the atmosphere like distant engines bellowing smoke and thunder.

Life abounds in the pockets of fertility amidst the rocky waste. Wild animals are abundant and strange - three-legged herbivores, winged serpents, bioluminescent hunter-predators. One cannot move between the organic formations without disturbing colonies of insects or startling flightless birds from their roosting sites. The vegetation seems to have an alien will of its own, tepid tendrils grasping like hands.

Water oozes from hidden crevices and seeps from the roots of the plants as if the landscape itself sweats it free, but the water never gathers into streams or rivers on the surface before being absorbed again into thirsty flora or evaporating into the smoggy air. The humidity is stifling even from a great height.

Occasionally, a rotor sound like helicopters descends from above, then a massive tentacled regulatable organism emerges into view, some form of airborne harvest-filter creature, and it commences sucking up fog from the valleys and filtering out mist-water and nutrients which it spews to the fungal colonies below as a bounty of silence for the constant ferment of ecological engagement in the dimness at high elevation, in a young earth reeking of green fecundity stretched at the limits of chaotic hyperdense interaction.

There is a constant sense of transmutation and cross-media exchange here, organic and inorganic merging into mechanical-insectal-diffusive forms of physicist interaction, as if the globalscale engine dwarfs human conceptions against the baselines of evolution. Everywhere, without exception, is growth and its consequences at intense scale and speed, through soil and wood-bark, between stony masses, in and out of electrified mists, never pausing for extinctions.
