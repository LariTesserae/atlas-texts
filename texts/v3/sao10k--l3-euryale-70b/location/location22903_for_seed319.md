In the still gray light, the landscape stretches out in an uneasy balance of water and land. Narrow rivers trace sinuous paths through broad floodplains of dense, muddy mires. Everywhere, the water sits close to the surface and sometimes breaks through in pools and marshy puddles, cafground in sage green and slate blue-gray. The air holds a perpetual damp mistiness.

Through the haze, you can make out the terrain undulating in gentle rounded hills, sometimes coming to a flat edge at a curve of the serpentine rivers. Perhaps it is the result of eons of soft erosion by the patient rivers on the gentle grade, or cycles of ancient seas lapping at the land then receding. Either way, the hills seem at peace with their settled forms.

In this balance of elements, only a few hallows of hardy trees — old gnarled oaks and dwarf willows, their trunks swollen with bulbous knobs — punctuate the flatness. They grow wherever the land has risen far enough above the mud to find purchase. Tendrils of creepers and vines wrap their rough bark. Not much else grows on the claggy ground.

But if the other vegetation is meager, the air sings with the sounds of running water and the gurgle of the pools. It is an evensong that burbles underfoot and out of sight where the denser marshes hide. If you stand still you can begin to pick out the bare few other sounds — a distant bird's flutey call, the skitter of some hidden creature into the concealing muck.

You notice unusual weathered stone poking out here and there from the turf like fragments of teeth. Maybe it was once part of structures, before the slow-moving forces of water and time worked to bring them low. Now their remaining blocks and course-felled stones have weathered to rounded shapes that mirror the smoothness of the hills, proof of the patient lengendary nature of the erosion. Water seeps from their bases and flows in rivulets to the channels. Whatever was built here is now lapped at by a tide that is as sedate as the waters themselves.

A smell of wet loam underlies the landscape's perfume, and you can't see far in any direction in the trenchant air. Visible over one hilltop are inert smoke stacks in the distance, hints of pagan industry. Such a view might mean there is civilization somewhere nearby, but you get the sense they too are just one element of a conspiracy of slow gradients and processes. The natural and artificial are mingled in this place, and seem to be in its own equilibrium for the moment. The moments feel calm, and all the rhythms are low.
