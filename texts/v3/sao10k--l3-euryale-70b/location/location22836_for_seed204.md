In the heart of the snow-capped Intrasil mountain range, there exists a strange and wondrous place that defies the typical classification of the surrounding peaks and valleys. It is a cavern, a pocket hidden away in the largest mountain that is known as Obsidian Spire. The Cavern of Ephemeral Waters, as it is sometimes called, has a singular feature that separates it from the rest of the rocky, mineral world of the Intrasil range - it is home to an olympic stream that flows through its center.

The narrow, pocket cavern is perhaps 20 meters across at its widest point. Though it connects to a much larger cave network, the Cavern of Ephemeral Waters is generally considered a self-contained micro-environment, without any meaningful plant life. Instead, it is home to shimmering deposits of obsidian and snowflake marble that pool on the cavern floor.

For visitors who first encounter the cavern, the most striking feature is likely the temperature. In an environment surrounded by snow and ice, the air here is almost tropical. Steam wafts from the flow of water, which splits into narrow, channelized streams on either side of a central isle made from a single large obsidian slab that almost fills the cavern's width. Underfoot, the uneven cavern floor is slick with moisture that has condensed out of the overheated air.

Despite the vibrant mineral colors of the wall and floor, the Cavern of Ephemeral Waters is a place defined by the water, not the rock. The isle's jagged obsidian edges and the rocks oriented perpendicular to the steaming stream all hint at the geological forces that created the cavern. To a keen observer, it seems that the cavern is a dynamic, active place, not a static one. The stream runs along subtle channels between the rocks without any sound of quiet trickling. Its surface tension is almost perfect, resisting any large perturbation, so it barely changes even when wind from the cavern entrance passes overhead. 

This freakeous, post-modern technology of the Cavern does not stop at the stream itself - the cavern's moss-like walls bear more than passing resemblance to circuitboards or other interfaces found in human technology. Breathing in the steam at the cavern entrance gives the visitor an uncanny sense of electrical energy that seems a bit too lively to be simply water droplets in the air. At night, an ethereal luminescence emanates from the the grass-like texture along the cavern floor. In a world so static and unremarkable, so drained of dynamism and mystery, any rock formation that whirs with technology the way the Obsidian Spire does is significant - and an exciting find for speleologists and technology obsessed prospectors alike.
