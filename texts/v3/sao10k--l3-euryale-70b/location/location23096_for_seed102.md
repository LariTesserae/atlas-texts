Fissures of Jade
by Claude Monet

Chiaroscuro. The light, so dim and strained, filters through unseen crevices far above. Dust motes dance in the luminous blue beams that latticely pierce the still, dry air. The space is small, pocket-sized - just a few dozen meters across in any direction. An intimate, subterranean grotto.

The temperature is warm but not oppressive. Skin prickles with the dryness, the hairs standing out like cilia bristles. Lush growths of twisting root-masses emerge from the eerie green stone walls, curling through the shadows. The leaves whisper in unfamiliar susurrations. These matted filaments drip oddly shaped nectars - viscous and bioluminescent. It drips and pools on the uneven ground.

The air is thick, laying heavily on the ears and eyes. Vibrations thrum through the jade and resonant growths. Despite the stillness, it is loud in here. Organic squeals and wet slithering noises emerge from dark crevices. A colony of scuttling blights roams the roots, polypoid and oily, leaving trails of digestive fluids.

Scattered through the growths are remnants of primitive structures. Twisted iron bars. Vine-locomoted stone. Rusted beads. The remnants of long forgotten ritual sites. Where blood was spilled and ecstatic mercury-poisoned shamans raved to the brassy skies (now occluded) above. Scattered artifacts - beaten discs, smoothed amulets, twisted limbs of flaked flint. All is thick with age and incrustation.

Time seems to work differently here, or not at all. The strangeness seeps from every fleck and hollow. Ubiquitous, insectile; the inexorable pulse of the uncanny burbling through the environs. Here is a place where light and sound churn viscously through the substrate. Where the material world is young and pliant, but simultaneously petrified. A habitar of the dread between forms.
