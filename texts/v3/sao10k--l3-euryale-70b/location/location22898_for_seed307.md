You find yourself in a small, hidden pocket realm barely more than 20 feet in diameter. The space is contained within a natural rock formation — a honeycomb of limestone cells, still dripping with the condensate of the overlying stratas. The air is thick and hot, the smell of sulfur heavy in your nostrils. 

The ground is uneven, a messy tangle of slow-growing fungal hyphae that cling to the damp stone. Wisps of steam waft up from cracks in the floor, as though there are fires smoldering deep below. A few stunted, semi-sentient lichenous growths seem to grasp, blindly, at your legs as you weave between the stalactites. 

The fungi pulse in odd, arrhythmic patterns beneath bioluminescent caps an angry shade of orange. Shadows dance on the walls as if cast by an invisible lantern. Your light source is unseen, but you sense it behind the shifting patterns of lichen and fungal growth, blinking to the unheard rhythms of the hidden cavern like the lantern of a deepcover sea jelly.

As your eyes adjust, you begin to make out the shapes of strange creatures huddled in the crevices of the rocks. Small and multi-limbed, their flesh is a patchwork of fuzzy discolorations, mottled and translucent as tadpole eyes, with a fungal patina to their skin. They crawl, lurch, wriggle smiling idiotically to themselves in the nooks and furrows. Lunar-moths of the deep. Glimmers of teeth, needle-tongues probing air fissures. 

The space seems to turn and twist in on itself, surfaces transitioning smoothly one to another. Up is down, bottom is seeming top, and it's hard to writhe free of the sensation that the cavern is just a shell around some greater, incomprehensible space. With each blink the fungal clusters and glassine wands might reform, or not, after their long moments' peace. The air trembles just outside the datasphere, a volatile pudding quivering with potential. The only sounds are dry, skittering, and a susurrus of exhalations from the fungoids and the crested pustules in the rock.

Just out of sight, you sense something moving. The bugs along the cavern floor shiver and scatter. A shape pulls itself into an opening, an umbilical cord of flesh the diameter of your arm, stippled with bulging barnacles and ruffles, muscles rippling greasy as it exerts to emit a tubulose proboscis crowned with barbed spikes. The frill pushes ahead of itself in a spray of exposulascent spores. A great ropeslave crawling like a segmented ocean worm, an endless spigot gouting a barbed-and-edged ovoviviparity, dribbling myriad progeny onto the walls to wriggle up sheer and glass-ceiled and ferment the lens-shaped openings in overlooked corners with wrentence of weeping cracks.
