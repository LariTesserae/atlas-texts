The place is a vast, cavernous underground environment filled to the brim with dense tropical vegetation. Towering trees with thick, twisting trunks and broad, waxy leaves stretch toward the dimly glowing fungal masses coating the cavern roof far above. Their branches interlace into a dense canopy that filters only slivers of eerie light through to the misty air below. 

Condensation drips steadily from the leaves and rocky outcroppings, sliming roots and flooding the uneven earth to create small ponds and rivulets that feed the fungi. The catspaw mounds of the fungi puff clouds of spores into the thick, damp air. Blaze of bioluminescent vines wends vines across the canopy, their yellowish light collecting in swampy pools.

Chitterings, howls and snarls echo eerily off the cave walls as strange creatures skitter through the undergrowth and lumpy fungal stalks. Pinkish, worm-like beasts the size of deer squirm sluggish and unseeing through the thick layer of translucent fungi carpeting the forest floor. Bat-winged insectoids pump and flutter through the umbrage, throngs coloring the canopy with their ruby abdomens. Saucer-sized toadstools whose caps blossom with thousand of flickering lights cluster beneath the waxy leaves.

Deep in the dripping shadows, immense tubes of spongy fungal mycelium cling to the rocky walls, pulsing as if pumping water deep into the dense vegetation carpeting the cavern floor. Clumps of fungal hyphae the size of tree stumps writhe minutely as the tangle of threads within unceasingly absorbs nutrients and minerals from the gossomy soil. Ponding water collects in sinkholes of flattened, spongy bracket fungi. Spouty hyphae break the surface in swaths like tawny fur showing through scaly bark.

As the vegetation is near all-encompassing and regularly eradicates itself in profusions of spores and hyphal growth, habitable areas are sparse. Clumps of fungi mass megalithically, much larger than any tree, blotting out dozens of square meters of the cavern floor with their smooth, hard caps. One could hollow out a castle in a larger clump. Others slump and ooze their flesh back into the earth as hyphae break them down. The fungal growths, and the life they harbor, are the only structures to speak of in this place.
