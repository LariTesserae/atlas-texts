The world is old and strange, a realm where the laws of nature have been twisted into knots. From this vantage, an alien vista unfolds - a mid-sized town nestled in the heart of an impossibly deep valley. Towering above loom cyclopean cliffs, their faces worn smooth by primordial forces. The peaks are shrouded in a perpetual mist, an endless veil of moisture that pours in great rivulets down the sheer rock faces, splashing into the rivers that cut through the lime-green valleys below.

In the lowlands, vegetation riots in a verdant tangle. Vines as wide as wagon wheels writhe through towering groves of trees with trunks of living obsidian and leaves like sapphire panes of stained glass. Ferns and mosses carpet the ground, mounded into hummocks ten feet high here and there. The plant growth is so lush as to render paths and architecture purely theoretical; in an hour, even a beaten track will slope inward with wet leaves and tendrils probing over carven stone. At night, the plants shine with a viridian luminescence, transforming the overcast valley into an eerie preserve of verdant gloom shot through with strange magics.

The town itself is nestled to one side of the gorge, where a remnant shoulder of rock forms an inland plateau. Pleasantly alien, it is a patchwork of bunched concrete structures in all the purples and emerald, built according to patterns no earthly architect would conceive but that somehow work. Pipes and wires vomit from the buildings, not in a mess, but in ponderous gargoyled curves like exposed omasums. Wisps of steam plunder the air.
The constructions sprawl and rise and slope, mezzanined along the contours of the micro-plateau. Ligatured by ramps and slung by bridges, enfiltrees across chasms and by viaducts of mist. Arches spawn arches upon arches, in tendriled fractals, like Calabi-Yau geometry made literal and masoned and columned into piazzas and arcades. The buildings are dimly interilluminated by sputtering magics, and larded with quivering fungal-pores.
Wander the town, and you'd be wont to confuse machine-shop for bazaar, study or parlor, radio for temple, climbing library for multistoried tenement, and so on: use and purpose blur to enigma here. The built environment jumbles the functions of our world into an inseperable braid.
The town merges into the cliff eventually, the organic and inorganic construction wrapping the mountainside, jutting and furling in a ganglion of masonry triming up into balloon-spires. Water pours back and forth between heights and depths, through gearwheels and vessels in superposition. The town is bots a filter and deep synthetat; it is an instrument of interconnected reaction chambers, equal parts alchemist's lab and steam-irrigated arboretum.

Dwelling in an setting nigh unimaginable is the varietal foliage-sharpened and lichen-sharp fauna of the settlement. The population seems serene, but one would mistake in taking this repose as trouble-free. Even at a glance, it is obvious their habits and beliefs are inscrutable to outsiders, woven in surpassing strangeness...
We apprehend, the world is out of joint, perched at the balance point of collapse into utter incoherence, but miraculously maintained by the actions of adepts, the mechaniations of the settlement and the ravishments of its verdure: a delicate balance of the unmaintainable, where all thrives and disintegrates in equipoise...

From the cliffs, the vista plunges down into the valley far below - What do you think the world looks like, down in the dens forest? The rilled crests of the valley are just barely visible through a sublimated scintillance of waterlogged sheen varisized by Heraclitean hydromorphic magic. .
