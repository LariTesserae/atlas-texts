An otherworldly city risen from the mists of a drowned world. Lush vegetation, thick and green, clings to every surface, twisting through the crumbling stone and rusted metal of innumerable strange structures. Trees of unknown varieties, with thick, gnarled trunks and bifurcating branches, rise to dizzying heights, grafting and fusing with the city itself until it is hard to tell plant from architecture. Ivy, creepers, and epiphytes drape every wall, balcony, and archway, forming dense curtains of foliage that wave gently in the mild breeze. 

This city is ancient, far older than the plant life that now dominates it, but it was clearly long abandoned before the deluge which has all but drowned it. Now, only the uppermost spires and minarets of the city's center peek out from the waterlogged street level. Dark canals, brackish and still, divide the huddled enclave of buildings still clear of water. 

The structures themselves are strange. Esoteric symbols and hieroglyphs are etched into every surface, giving clues to the unique properties of this water-saturated location. They appear to channel the omnipresent moisture into metaphysical flows and eddies. Aqua-crystals glimmer and pop with eldritch energy where they have formed on overhangs and downswept cornices. Arcane warded gates puddle at the abrupt ends of streets, leading out into the dark waters between buildings.

The louder sounds are all water and vegetation. Plashing and dripping, a simmering hiss where water meets aqua-crystals and releases trapped gases. Rustling leaves and shimmering feathers as nameless birds below the canopy. Gentle splashing as strange aquatic insects skitter and dive between the water-roots of buildings. In the distance, the occluded sun gleams in crackling jets of steam released from the core atop the greatest tower at the drowned city's center. Animal cries echo out from the clustered top-story glade clades—athe species one might expect of damp forest cape capsules, and others more exotic.

Before any of this miasmic flaura, the overt technological clue to the nature of this strange preservation is the pervasive presence of pillars. Tall, slender, and made of a smooth, pale material resembling petrified bone, they rise up at regular intervals to tangle in the canopy and steeple-tips above. Each place the water below their bases has turned to a clear blue-green, unlike the choked murk around. Most importantly, wherever they stand, the water level has stayed consistent for as long as the city's ruins have lain drowned - these appear the key to this strange aqua-stasis and the environment's number of peculiarities.
