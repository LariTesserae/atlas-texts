The landscape before you stretches endlessly in all directions, a vast expanse of low-lying terrain that seems to merge seamlessly with the horizon. The elevation is uniform and barely slopes at all, only occasionally broken by the occasional outcropping of white, chalky stone poking through the ground like exposed vertebrae. 

Despite the lack of elevation, the sense of scale is enormous. A glance about reveals no exceptional geographic features, no distant mountain ranges to provide a sense of perspective, only an extended flatness that seems to shrink and flatten all in its presence. The land itself is dry and alkaline, harsh and crumbly, like the floor of a vast evaporated lakebed. No humidity hangs in the air, only a baked, desiccated atmosphere that rasps the lungs and drunkards the eyes. 

One might expect such a barren and unwelcoming vista to be utterly devoid of life, but the treeless surroundings are actually lush with thick, wiry tufts of vegetation. These sickly plants are spare and mongrel-like in appearance, sprouting stark white or mottled grey leaves that cling sparsely to their dry stalks. They grow in isolated knots and clumps, each tree deciduous and twisted in strange, gnarled ways.

This disjunct between the creation of plant life and the crushing normalcy of the environment is repeated and multiplied as one observes the landscape on closer inspection. Strange, large worms burrow through the arid depths, many of them tubular and gelid tubes of muscle that leave wet trails in their wake. Swarms of small erbischer insect-life skitter and dance in the low brush, clicking and clacking with the only sound besides the isolated push of dry wind. 

Eventually, after an hour's more of pale, desiccated travel, one reaches a long stone frontage hacked into the sky - a vast wall of seamless moon-white stone, three hundred feet high and encircling an area of many hundreds of acres. It's looking at this wall - featureless save for an arched openings at regular intervals, like the groove circuits of a vanished machine - that the true reality of the landscape is most immediately mortifying. The frontage is not a wall, one soon realizes - it is a city, a series of linked hollow sanctum-cities that march in a featureless gentle arc that completely encircles the landscape like a spine that frames a continent.
