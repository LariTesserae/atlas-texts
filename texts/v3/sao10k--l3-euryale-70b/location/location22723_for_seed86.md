In this vast underground cavern, the atmosphere is cool and damp. Stalactites and stalagmites rise like sentinels from the rocky cave floor, some not yet joined but others forming thick columns that support the vast vaulted roof high above. Droplets of water condense on the end of each stalactite, occasionally falling with a quiet plink to the echoing chamber below. The walls are rough-hewn limestone, ridged and rippled from eons of geological upheaval. Bare roots of trees poke out from the edges of the cavern opening above, their leaves and branches invisible in the dimness.

The only light filters down from the high entrance, barely enough to see by. Lanky ferns and mosses cling to the damp rocks in the perpetual twilight. Tiny white mushrooms sprout from the nooks of boulders and columns. Birds chirp faintly from overhead, their droppings white streaks on the cavern floor. A smallish freshwater spring bubbles up from beneath a raised lip of stone, feeding a narrow stream that trickles across the uneven rocky ground and through a crack at the far edge.

Scattered through the cavern are large stone humps, like ancient ruins. But they have no doors, windows or evidence of construction - they rise organically from the cave floor itself, solid stone monuments holding messages and purposes now lost. Painted lines and symbols, faint with age, run across their flanks in sweeping designs.

The air is filled with the echo of falling water and the rustle of wildlife unseen - bats flutter through the depths of the vaulted ceiling, while the calls of unfamiliar creatures reverberate from the mossy hidden corners. The sense is one of ancient timelessness - as if the cavern has lain unchanged for eons in the dark womb of the Earth. There is a strange perfection to the place, an equilibrium of water, stone and life that seems to have persisted for untold ages with no need of any outside agency.
