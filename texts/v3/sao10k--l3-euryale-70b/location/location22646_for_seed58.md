Subterranean Waterways

Winding through the rocky earth, an intricate network of tunnels and caverns shimmers with perpetual moisture. The air is thick and heavy, never quite dry, as if the remains of a thousand rainy days still linger in the stale air. Luminous minerals embedded in the rock cast an eerie semi-light on the damp surfaces.

Webs of fungal hyphae crisscross the stone, while sketchy tufts of algae put enough photosynthesis to account for the ambient greenish light. In the stiller air, remnants of ancient waterways remain, muddy rivers now reduced to trickling streams. Ranks of stalactites and stalagmites rise from pools left behind by the drought.

Emerging from the vast undercity, you see the steel and glass monoliths of a underground metropolis rise far overhead, blotting out any distant light. The level of construction has risen along with the dryness, leaving an under-layer of waterways abandoned by rising dwellers. Still, some inhabitants linger, adapting to the strange new conditions.

Inside the labyrinthine depths, water-powered clasping gates border many a watery drop, now fenced off from the inner lanes. Shaded kiosks marked water stops line the carve rock. Citizens move deliberately as if through a reversed city, higher elevations now more desirable than the echoing pools. At corners signal lights indicate water level, air composition, dark or light. Here commerce still happens on long catwalks over sunken waterways- inverted bridges open to the depths above, more unsettling than in earlier days.

Closest to the dry spaces you sense a micro-city forming, enveloped by the rising towers. Stairways and pulleys connect decks at different levels. More frequent sontgelights mark the outlines of the colony pressing surrounding shadows inward. Here, attentive to the impossible quality of their surroundings, dwellers adjust their habits to this strange prisoned world.
