The wind howls across the barren highlands like a chorus of tormented souls. Gray clouds writhe overhead, threatening to unleash at any moment, but the rain always holds back, taunting with its absence. The air is damp and heavy, saturated with the chill of an endless winter that never seems to arrive. 

In the distance, the jagged peaks of ancient ranges claw at the sullen sky. These are mountains that have stood for eons, barely weathered by the feeble forces of today's world. They remain pristine in their stone sharpness, triangle facets cut in granite and shale, not yet softened by the gentle hand of erosion. Ridges and summits blend together in a discontinuous jumble that defies destroying one's vertigo to look upon.

The landscape is broken at the edge of sight by a precipitous canyon. It is a yawning chasm, a thousand feet deep or more, cut by some arctic river eons ago that long since dried. Now only stony outcroppings and cascading waterfalls - frozen at the tips - punctuate the misty void. It seems unreasonable that water can flow through such a place, and in truth it does so only rarely, when weather patterns align in a great surge of flood and storm.

The ground is rock and dirt, scabbed with a sparse hardscrabble scrub that clings to life as best it can. More shale and gravel than soil, the ground has nourished few roots over the eons. The plants are stunted, twisting, hungry things, more accustomed to benefiting from the flecks of minerals in the stone than the meager rainfall.

Occasionally one sees remains of human presence - walls of part-stone, part-mortar, remnants of structures long crumbled. The avenues the roads that led to them are washed out, eroded like the chasm across the way, scoured smooth by sporadic floods, leaving a web of gulches and arroyos. The winds pry at the stones, aligning them to point skyward, so that what remains of the buildings almost seem to be a kind of mechanical ritual geometry, as if every stone fulfills a purpose, frozen in time. But the structures were originally so mundane - huts, houses, lodges, what they are for is lost to the gradual erasure of this place.

Though the wind is loud, loud enough you can cease scaring up the sound it shifts through the stone up there, loud enough you can't hear anything else, the largest constant awareness is the sensation that that it should be an error of eviction, the sound of something tearing, is the ache, is the presence that the sound is preventing you hearing, and becomes the stone lip of a holed cliff before you, when you cranes your neck to look it uneven in the eyes mirrors.

There are creatures here that make no noise - the small mammals scritch and clutch their feathers in predators' nests rocked at table of the cliffs. Their calls are of the most guttural note, neither like animal echo nor like a sound in itself but more like a riffle of the membrane, a dirty drip of ocular fluids echoing, echoing.

The materials are almost one, the stone and flesh, upstream and down, the what and the when and the who and the this, the journeystep on the stone-hollow of bone: this is the place where we have always been, and we are nowhere. The light rubs the stone the stone reflects. The light is dust and it burns. It is a flavor of dryness and the shock of wetness. 

The time-river from the mountains lurches in this grey-damp, rent by the frozen waterfalls and streaks by moss. What you watch at is something charismatic. It is as though you can simply raised your hand and stop the wind as if a finger lifted, as though you can act, state, state, irritate, assist. Something has been still for eons and we are about to walk into it.

The strangeness of this place is its mere existence, and the biological secrets in the stones and rivers. Life cut into rock. Nutrients in the shale. The wind's warning. Over eons something has resulted from the processes that shape this terrain, a life and awareness that exists on another order of scale and time. Witnessing it is to feel the power and indifference of geological forces, to understand one's own insignificance, to transcend that insignificance into a vaster order of being. But there are no words for its materials or mechanisms. The revelation is one of pure presence, unnamable and incommunicable. That is the reality that breaks down when someone gazes deep into this place.
