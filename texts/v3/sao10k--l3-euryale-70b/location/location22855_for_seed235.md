The air is thick with humidity and the aroma of fertile earth. Visibility is low due to a perpetual mist that clings close to the ground. Strange fungi in riotous colors poke through the loamy soil - purples, chartreuses, electric blues, all glowing faintly in the diffused light that filters in from above. 

The terrain undulates, large rolling hills covered in dense carpets of mosses, lichens, and succulents in every imaginable shade of green. Bizarre trees loom in the misty distance, their broad leaves dripping with dew. Mushrooms in the dozens, from luminescent buttons no bigger than a thimble to fleshy ella-shaped caps over a meter across, crowd the undergrowth. Exotic orchids stream from the trunks and branches in gaudy tassels.

Occasionally, a bare rocky outcropping will rise up from the dense vegetation, covered in ferns, moss, and epiphytes. Moisture seeps from cracks to form miniature cascades tinkling over the stone into the soggy loam. A shimmering veil of water hangs in the air, just beginning to condense into droplets on the undersides of the leaves.

The atmosphere is motionless and silent, save the heavy stillness of morning dew-laden air. There is no wind, no bird calls, only the occasional distant glacial scrape of an ancient earthy growth prying itself from its cool, damp cradle of soil and stone.
