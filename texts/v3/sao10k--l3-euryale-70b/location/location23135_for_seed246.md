The sun is setting over the vast expanse of the Frost Marsh Delta, the light casting an eerie, otherworldly glow. 

The landscape is an endless sea of frost-crusted mud and ice, etched with snaking rivulets of frigid water that wind between islands of tundra vegetation. Scraggly cotton grass and wisps of sedge cling to the ground, their brown and gold blades glossy with moisture from the perpetual damp of the marsh. Sombre arctic willows hunch over the waterways, their bare branches etched against the leaden sky.

The air is bitterly cold, with a damp chill that penetrates to the bone. Frigid winds rake across the level expanse, setting the surfaces of the fog pools to shuddering and sending eerie cascades of frozen crystals tumbling from the willows. With each gust, more of the marsh's mist swirls into the air, adding to the constant chilling dial of fog that wreathes the landscape like a grim blanket. Over it all broods a heavy overcast, the sun struggling vainly for view through the sullen ice clouds.

The deltas are ancient and age-worn, their low banks no more than a few feet high, crafted in eons by the ceaseless flux of freezing water and silt. Here and there stumps of petrified logs protrude from the mud and ice, all that remains of long-ago forests flooded and then frozen in place for millennia as the delta advanced. A few stunted black saplings have taken root amidst their dendritic roots and uplifted branches to create a nightmarish forest of wood. 

Off in the receding distance, the dark tangs of ruins are just visible through the thinner fog.
