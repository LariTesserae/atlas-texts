The air is thick with humidity and the scent of greenery and juxtapositions of death and decay intermingled through the ponds, wetlands, and sprawling swamp. An ancient river winds through what was once a valley but is now choked with a single organism, a vast network of interconnected cypress and mangrove-like trees. Their thick, gray, warty trunks extrude from the brown soup of the swamp, rising 30 meters to a tangled canapé of twisting branches. Massive roots drop like pillars from high in the canopy to the water below, forming tangles of writhing limbs that create separate the ecosystem into hidden pools, tunnels, swamps and wetlands within the larger swamp.

The forest floor, such as it is, is a mad tangle of roots, driftwood, tannin-stained water and spongy mosses. In sun breaks, fig vines drip with droplets of water and orchids bloom in vibrant colors. Ripe, heavily-scented fungi burst from fallen logs and mats of soft moss and lichen spread where light can reach.

The soundscape is one of constant dripping, echoing bird calls, the distant grinding and whoosh of far-off waterfalls and rapids, and the interminable drone of insects. Large macaws and parrots inhabit the canopy and brightly-plumed tanagers flit between branches. Humungous butterflies as large as a human hand flutter through sunbeams and clouds of gnats swirl in slanting shafts of light.
