The landscape stretches out before you, a vast expanse of eroded hills and canyons carved by eons of wind and water. Red rock formations, ancient and weathered, rise up on either side of you, twisted into impossible shapes by geological forces. The air is thin and cold; you can see your breath in the chill morning air.

Pockets of moisture cling to the landscape in the form of small, hardy trees and scrubby brush. Gnarled branches claw at the red sky. Tiny puddles of water remain in potholes and crevices from a long-ago rain. Rivers of water left patterns of dark, damp soil as they cut their way through the crumbling stone eons ago, now long since vanished. The rocks still bear the ghosts of those waterways.

The city sprawls as far as the eye can see, filling the canyon with its winding streets and honeycomb of dwellings cut into the cliff faces. Buildings and architecture of untold ages - some crumbled and ancient, others barely standing, the newer ones well-worn and dun-colored - create a landscape of their own within the natural one. Internal light flickers from cave-dwelling windows.

Life clings to existence, sparse and hardy like the vegetation, perpetuating its cycles in stony crevices and some few well-watered oases. Mammalian forms emerge from shadows, watch with eyes that hold the weight of too much time. Strange sounds echo in the canyons that sound an awful lot like speech, but none of it sane.

Your flesh crawls with an unnatural stillness in the air.
