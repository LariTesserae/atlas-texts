The landscape before you is a deep, shimmering grotto, a natural amphitheater of stone filled from edge to edge with crystal-clear water. The sheer rock walls tower up on all sides, closing in the grotto save for a small archway presided over by a two-petaled waterfall, sending a curtain of water down into the motionless pool with a perpetual, gentle hush. Globules of droplets cling another obsidian face, glistening black, while below their runnels are drained to the pool—leaving that face polished and dry.

The water is packed with masses of floating plants that inspire in the diffuse green dim—dangling straps like oak leaves in puffed lacy little rosettes, pink and yellow blooms rising in serene profusion to the surface. Sticks poke up like spires capped with half ellipses of bright gree. A lush and dense verdure hovers just beneath the surface, a submerged forest, between whose leaves flit tiny fish and let drift dull little water birds, napping with faces tucked behind wings, swathed without waking as they undulate in their drifting current or spiral upward in the water column.

The air is cool and the only touch of wind is the one that drifts in the arch gases may rely from the falls, with a slight idle lack of oxygen on occasion. There is a lack of light, but the thick glaze of water illuminates the ceiling to the point of psychological brightness.

No features alter under the water, nor any on the stone walls. no shaping nor any movement. The water birds sleep; the fish glissade in their sera; the water level is silent and deep and still. Only the plants swing and twine idly to manage their motion. The scale is small, a cathedral or small grove, but the quality is very much-personal, facing the viewer, embracing them—bringing them inward to the counter motif of the falls.
