The damp air clings like a wet blanket, heavy with the scent of rich soil and ancient stone. Mist swirls between towering trees, creeping tendrils curling around their trunks and dripping from their leaves. The forest floor is spongy with deep layers of loamy mulch. Underfoot, thick mosses carpet tree roots while ferns and wildflowers dot the undergrowth. Shafts of weak sunlight filter through the dense canopy, glinting off beading dew. Damp-soil-scents muffle distant birdsongs and the skittering of creatures unseen. 

Stands of bamboo and reed crowd the edges of still, mirror-smooth ponds overhung with weeping willows. Giant mushrooms sprout like the caps of fairy chimneys. Clumps of mud squelch underfoot by the water's edge. Bronze and purple dragonflies zip through, their iridescent wings shimmering. Frogs croak a basso ostinato to the mosquito's high trill.

The air seems to tremble and the trees sway slowly as if pushed by restive zephyrs. High in the canopy, a flock of flying foxes leap gap to gap, piercing calls echoing lonesomely. On a distant tor, the squared-off ruins of some ancient megalith glint in the weak sun. Pools of water collect in its high spaces, supporting colonies of lotus blossoms. 

Time seems to move slowly in this sodden, moss-draped bower, as if the wheel of years turns reluctantly, one heavy ancient century at a time. The air is heavy and still, the forest floor dense and undisturbed, the ponds glass-calm. Everywhere is the sense of incalculable age, as if the landscape has sat undisturbed for times as vast in their scales as the trees are in their girths and the ruin in its weathering.
