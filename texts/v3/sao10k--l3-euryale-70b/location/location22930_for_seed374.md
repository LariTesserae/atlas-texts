Wet, uneven cobblestones glisten beneath wobbling torchlight. Damp air clings, warm and thick. Scant grasses and dyed mosses barely manage a toehold in narrow cracks, squeezed between intersecting buildings. All is layered and overlapping, walls rising above one another, sometimes in balanced arches, more often in bricked chaos. Ribbed stone, red and gray and brown, and clay, muddy red and gray-brown. An scattered vertebrae of catwalks, single-file-wide, on which lean out citizens, watching, looking, going elsewhere. From trusty signs, of welcome change, never helping climb. All seems ancient, stratified, winding. The water dashes, new, a narrow river's channel'ing, joining out of seeing.
