In the midst of a dense jungle, where thick vines and roots twisted through the earthen floor and curtained the canopy above, there existed an oasis of sorts. A stretch of mudflat perhaps a hundred yards long, bracketed by trickling streams that meandered through the undergrowth. The water was algae-choked, swirling with life - small fish darting about, tadpoles clinging to every muddy outcropping, insects skimming the surface, leaving trails of disturbed water.

The mud was squelching and spongy underfoot, warm and gummy between the toes. It squished out a beach of dark sediment as one waded the edge of the stream. Undulating heatwaves shimmered above the basin, and a fug of mosquito buzz and bird calls hung in the air, loud in the dense press of green. 

Dead trees stuck up here and there like massive broken teeth, covered in thick curtains of moss and lichen. The air was still and muggy, heavy with humidity that condensed on the skin and clothes. Squelching onward across the ankle-deep mud, the shore rose to meet a ledge of weeping rock crusted in minerals - sulfur it seemed, judging by the bright yellow patination.

Water dripped from its underside, splashing into hollows eroded by its flow over long ages. The deeper basins were slick with algae and moss and squirming poliwogs. A few fluttering dragonflies hovered around them. Closing in, you saw the dripping stone was caked in bits of something that looked like old bone. Fragments of yellowed teeth, splintery splinters, lavender-colored were they? 

It was the rock itself, or at least the mineral composition of it, rather than bone... The stone was impregnated with dense lodes of etched fossils. Millions of years old they must be, details of skeleton and jaw and fin rendered in perfect lifelong diorama, but small - the size of a thumbnail or less usually. Somehow, the rock had formed slowly and replaced their old bones, yet preserved their shape in exquisite detail doggedly.

Gently scooping out the water, you could see the scallop of one such ancient jaw, studded in minute-sized teeth. Or part of a primitive-plated tail. Wrist of some ichthyosaur or long-necked plesiosaurus. Then, the glint again of hacked bone - a different color, more random, more recent. The mud, it seemed, was seeded with bones that were less old, taggants and splinters and weird sugical fragments. 

Here and there knobby branches twisted from bedding planes in the stone and fractured water. Raw stubs, like the joints of a skeleton, covered in moss. That white flurry could be driftings of flung cowry shells. Though bit of rotated ivory gleamed here and there.

What was that? A large concealed hatch or well in the mud, like a plugged leak in the world. Water sipped over the heavy ceramic of an edge, pellucid as fronted milk. Each minute it slid a little more open on its hidden hinges. Concealed totally by the natural mulch, until the draw of curiosity, even debased curiosity, fingertip tracing the rich syrup down. Toward a dark rhetor in the shallows, reeds grown up into the air like gut-grass. Vines, and tassels of cataract, rinsing water from the higher sides of the regular shape. The plug reel in the ruined edge of the ancient door where it fluted to the rim. Glowing back up the induct like an eye - or like a eye. Undead day of the Briis - cold mote, some eldom specie, something mutated in its long danger. Tonight the conduit shuddered, spawning towards the grope of access. What could crawl out?
