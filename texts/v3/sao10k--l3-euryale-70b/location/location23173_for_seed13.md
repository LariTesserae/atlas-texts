Wisps of fog drift through the quiet canyon, clinging to the rough stone walls and snaking through the sparse brush. Shattered shale and jagged quartz glint in the pale, gray light that filters down from the high rim. The air is damp and cool, carrying the sharp mineral scent of the damp rock. 

A narrow river rushes through the center of the canyon, spilling over rough boulders and rapids. The water is crystal clear and icy cold, carrying silt and minerals from the eroding walls. Fresh torrents of whitewater burst from narrow side canyons above after every rain.

Despite the aridity of the landscape, pockets of life cling to the miniature environments along the canyon floor. Nestled between flat-topped boulders stand copes of spruce and fir. Hollowed logs and hidden pools support lush fungi, mosses and lichens. Violet rock hyacinth blooms in gem-bright patches. Tiny sage hares, bushy tailed martens, and bush warblers flit through the shade. Tower eagles, gryphons, and condors sometimes shadow the valleys.

Two meandering talus trails wind up the sheer rock faces, vanishing into weakened layers of limestone, coal and shale. Other ancient trails backtrack into the wilderness — overgrown ruins of an old mining boom. Only scattered weathered hovels and prospectors' shackes remain. The rusted skeleton of an earthmover sprawlled in an old riverbed marks their end.

Gargoyles are the most common ruins though. Scattered heedless across standstone ridgetops and valley mouths, pulverized by beast-hides and creepers and creeping floral colonization. Sinuous opal vase shapes fringe their sharded bases where a fluid emerald ooze pours slower than the river itself.
