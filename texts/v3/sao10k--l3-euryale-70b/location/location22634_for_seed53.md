The landscape is cold and still, the air crisp and sharply chilly. An otherworldly quiet pervades this place, only occasionally broken by a faint wind that whistles through the strange forms that have taken shape here.

As far as the eye can see, the ground is bare earth, unforgiving grey stone and crumbly half-baked clay, pocked with depressions and mounded into uneven swellings. No green shows anywhere, not a sprig of grass or shred of lichen - only the naked substrate of the planet's surface strewn about. The few features are abrupt and sharp-edged, glacial flensing of stone scoured by ancient ice sheets, or unpredictably twisted moraines knotted beneath their mass. Scattered erratics, boulders hefted from far-off ranges, stud the relatively flat tress like discarded playthings.

The cyclopean stones loom above, many the size and shape of small domains, some split vertically and lent majestically against their brethren. Some slabs and sheets of the basaltic rock scrape the attenuated sky, driven almost against it, though they are bedded far from level. In between and around them, brook and rill cut heedless of any purpose, as if geology unfolded long after any great powers finished shaping the earth.

The sun stands at high noon, but little grows warm. The wind cuts almost ghostly frigid and flees away wherever birthed. Shadows slant long away from the towering stones and settle cold in their creases, some of whose freezing depths could hide unsuspected brook or dell of their own. It seems to glide bird-rounded to roost, though lustrous gaze is not beared here, invisible attendees set watch and ward on this great but unglorious place. Shadow watch, opposed to the upreaching invasive wraith of light, a frequent enterprise of life in this clime, would even frozen settle high in sudden decline and abstinences. Disappearing at wide array into the newer clime, it pins sound to the slopes far drawn behind the tactless touch of other invaders, eyes tight to undesirable vacancies in unwanton torpor.
