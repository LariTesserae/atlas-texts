Caves of Subterran Stone

Cool, damp air clings to your skin as you descend the worn steps, carved long ago from living bedrock. The passageway opens into a cavernous space - a pocket realm hidden deep beneath the earth. Stalactites and stalagmites rise from the obsidian floor like trees on a landscape of prehistoric stone, frozen in a moment of primordial time. 

The cavern is alive with a strange quiet - the deafening stillness rings in your ears, emphasizing the absence of any other sound. Condensed mist swirls in tenebrous air currents, casting opalescent halos around the flickering light of your torch. Freezing water drips from above, pattering onto the glassy black flowstone in mesmerizing rhythm.

Scattered about are remnants of an ancient presence - the flaked remains of sharpened stones, the fossil of a cryptic script etched into a stalagmite's base. Some vanished culture once made camp here, exploiting the respite from the frigid surface to ply rude trades and tell grim tales.

The cavern is a pocket of protections, a place set apart from the bleaker supra-environment it lies ensconced within. Beyond these carved walls, all is desolate - a blasted torrential badlands of, hyper-evaporated lakes and cracked salt beds. The rare water that reaches this deep is held and heard. Life clings to it, pressed in hidden refugia.

In this sheltered realm, contemplation comes speculative and appreciation slow. Thoughts stratify as the rock, through a long sympathy with the relaxed patience of this subterran pocket. The absence everywhere around speaks louder than any echo. The waiting of eons presses on the stone, and all within it. Stillness is the only movement. The deafening silence compels the mind headlong into itself.
