The landscape stretches before you, an expanse of rugged, craggy rock emerging from the thin, sparse vegetation. The air is cold and brisk, wisps of mist clinging to the peaks and canyons. Water trickles and drips from every crevice and ledge, the rock itself seeming to weep in the chill dampness. 

In the distances, a series of sheer canyon walls rise and fall in undulating waves, each one a short slope of stony rubble leading down to a thin trickle of a river at its base. The rock layers are alive with swaths of purple and orange, psychedelic minerals bleeding through the mottled grey and brown. 

Light is in short supply, the high elevation and moisture-heavy clouds blocking much of the sun's rays. The sky is a uniform pale gray, blotched with glutinous black clouds clotted on the mountaintops. Spindrift breaks from these kidney-shaped thunderheads and unfurls in translucent banners, soon dissipating in the thin air.

Despite the harsh conditions, life clings to every ledge and crevice. Low shrubs and bristly grasses stud the thinner soils, their leaves matted together for warmth. Pools from the seeping water are the color of strong tea from the dissolved minerals, but they still host colonies of fungi, mosses, and stunted trees.

The silence is almost palpable in the still, heavy atmosphere. No birdsong pierces the overcast calm, and even the rustling of the stunted plants is muffled. A higher pitched hush of wind tugs fitfully at the spires and ridgelines.
