The landscape is a vast, cold, and lifeless microcosm. 

At the scale of a few meters in diameter, this is a self-contained environment, an artificial pocket of space plucked from the outside world. Where outside is the frigid vacuum of deep space, inside is a bubble of stable, stale air, maintained by a humming network of unseen climate control systems.

The air is thin and very cold - just a few degrees above absolute zero. What little warmth there is still present emanates from the machinery itself, clanking and droning and zinging away ceaselessly. Hysterical and deafening, ever-present.

In the darkness of the void, the only light by which to see this miniature world is the violent glare off of sparks and welding arcs and molten metal spattered from forges scattered throughout by the ceaseless activity of immense and twisted machines. Skips and crucibles bubble up clouds of noxious smoke and steam by the bucketful. 

Long conveyors carry ore in and raw materials and supplies of every description up to the pulsing core of this manufactured micro-environment. Forges and furnaces, drills and presses and mills. Pounding and churning. Cog-globbed gear trains and chains and pistons clinging together like a ball of snakes make a web of mechanical arms and claws and blades that grasp and manipulate and shape and carve the raw ingots into...into...some unknown product, hauled off to storage racks in an unintelligible, abstract jumble.

Rubble and dropped rivets and shavings and boiler ash from the internal furnace clinkers rain down from the unseen machinery onto the cluttered, haphazard floors. Bare metal and still-glowing ingots and hot atoms ripped from grinding stones domino in volatile stacks, every so often to collapse in a heavy roar and avalanche and shower of fresh, shattered fragments. No plants to speak of on this petroleum-shiny ground.

The clatter of pneumatics and crankshafts and spit and hiss of steam which pounds in the air locks out any other sound like a wall. No wind. No animal cries. No birdsong. Nothing but the heavy, clanking, relentless and supremely artificial din of the machinery. Pumping and pounding. Rattling and clicking. Humming and buzzing. Undeviating monotonous all-consuming machine music that seems to resound through one's bones.

Yet for all this chaos, the purpose of all this activity is utterly obscure. The product up on its shelves and racks is incomprehensible, all bizarre angles and colors, organic not quite, all twisted and fleshy and slick, somewhere between random configuration and racially stylized unpurposeful design. 

Between all of this, jetting, clashing, shuddering, sparking, rolling, falling, grinding and crashing lie narrow walkways of black greasy steel that heave and tilt with each movement of the machines. An environment of sheet metal and toxic chemicals and molten metal, of violent temperature gradients and crushing pressures and immiscible function grinding in a rhythm that is almost, but not quite, musical. A claustrophobic pandemonium of hyperindustrial hypertrophe in a closed pocket universe, too small for telecommunications or even, perhaps, safety. A strange place, disjointed and alien and wrapped in ellipses, where no organic thing can have survived the melting point of copper. Heated, laden with pollutant, lit only by the arc of spikes and electric drills. The air thick with coal smoke and the reek of suphur. Molten flows of copper and sewn copper thickly vulcanized slick and electromagnetic and carbonized and blackened both extra Sect inside. Lava. Piping hot and sparking chained and riveted. Exfoliated. Carbon amelioration. Compression. The spalling decay of the building unsupported.

No consequences, no upshot, but only for the strange compulsion of the human soul for the infinite creation.
