Cathedral of Inundation

The place you have arrived is a strange amalgamation of water, stone, and machine. Imagine a cavernous space, a vast underground chamber that rises and falls in natural arches of igneous rock. The ceiling vaults high overhead, at least a hundred meters up, but it is lost in a thick, heavy fog that perpetually hangs in the air, dimming the flickering fluorescent light that runs in lines along the walls and ceiling. These lights, installed into nooks and crannies, have moss growing on their power cords, dripping tendrils of mineral-rich water that runs in rivulets down the rock.

A still, mirror-like lake fills the center of the chamber, reflecting the rock and fog in subdued hues of mossy green and steely grey. The water level is high, only a narrow rim of rock around the edges. A series of radial platforms radiate out from a central island, each connected by waterlogged wooden piers. On the island, a large mechanical platform of rusted steel looms above the water, covered in a strange collection of strange devices - what look like a mix of industrial machinery and esoteric occultic symbols.

Over the whole of the space hangs the steady pounding drone of dripping water and the heavy, thick atmosphere seems to press down. There is an air of relaxation and stasis, as if time itself has escaped notice in this strange location, locked down beneath the earth. Despite this, there is something unsettling here - a sense that one is being watched from the shadows in the fog, watched by unseen eyes.

The air is humid and dank, shot through with the scent of minerals and the taste of old concrete. Everywhere you look, green things cling and grow - algae on the water, moss on the stone, even vines twining up the metal of the central island. It is like this space exists separate from the world above, rejecting the light and warmth, embracing instead perpetual drowned twilight. And yet, life flourishes here, strange forms reaching out from the shadows to bloom under the faint fluorescent glow.
