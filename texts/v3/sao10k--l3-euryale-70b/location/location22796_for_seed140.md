In the depths of the void, an oasis of life clings tenuously to existence. A small, high valley sits nestled between towering cliffs, its ancient sedimentary walls bearing witness to untold eons of geological time. Water seeps from the ground in rivulets that wind lazily across the valley floor, and an otherworldly dew clings to every surface, keeping the lush greenery perpetually damp. Lianas and vines writhe up the cliff faces, their tendrils probing for purchase among the ancient fossils and jutting ledges.

The light is meager here, just the feeble red glare that filters down from some dim, unseen source above - not a sun so much as a great, ageless heat. It casts long shadows across the valley floor, and highlights the scattered ruins that dot the landscape. Ancient monoliths still stand guard over the valley, their carved faces pitted and worn by time. Tumbled debris is strewn about their bases, eroded nearly to rubble but still betraying hints of the advanced technology that built them. Strange punctures mar their surfaces, curving channels that seem to imply subject to forces beyond the known laws of physics.

Careening off the cliff faces, the water collects in clear pools that dot the valley like bruised eyes. Ancient carapaces and glittering, jewel-like shells litter their edges, products of sluggish evolutionary arms races waged between the radically exotic organisms that dwell in their near-boiling waters. Sentient mist swirls above the water's surface, coalescing briefly into globular, pulsing forms that collapse under their own density before reforming a moment later. The water, in places, swims with opalescent objects that seem to conform to fractal geometries no natural creature could produce - the remmants of long-dead machines, reduced to semi-biological reefs that slowly degrade back to their constituent elements.

Cascading down from the valley's rim, a spine of sharp boulders and hummocks marks an ancient watercourse, its stony flanks channeled by water that long since vanished. Amid the stones, the weathered shell of a domed structure squats, its sealed entranceways pocking its spheroid form like open mouths. Its smooth, glossy surface is all but unmarred by time, looking to be soe recent that the air itself should be shivering with felight - and yet it must have been there for millions of years. And the space inside - best not to dwell overly long, lest you want the touch of scales caressing your back. Such it is, in this place, to drift too far from the calibrated, sculpted borders of the paths and engineered glades.

Some miles down, the valley drops away in a sheer, plunging face. Still-fuming lava spouts from the depths of the rent, giving the gloom a lurid, hellish red. The heat from below sets up a near-constant updraft, carrying half-consumed lumps of earth and flaking stone into the dark above. It is best not to go poking into those fuming mouths, lest a lungful of raw, magical smoke sets your guts to lava and your fingers to shackle. Nor is that the worst of it, for these gapes lead deep and deep - down past stone, past water, past leaden-kit windows and curdling acid, sheer deductions admits a silicone green, sulfuric sun at the DARKENESS' core... and what sinks there is meat for the beasts of the blackest abyss! Such is the shape of things.
