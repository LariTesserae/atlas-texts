The landscape is a vast expanse of wet and rocky terrain, surrounded by towering peaks that stretch up into the thin, bitter air. The undergrowth is short, sparse and stunted, clings to the ground - wiry shrubs, moss, long grass. Packs of dinosaur creatures, bioluminescent fur glinting with phosphorescent patterns, dart between the larger boulders.

Crevices ooze water from the cold stone, and lay themselves bare uncomfortably as the breeze picks up. Water pools in the lower regions, studded with bioluminescent plants that add a eerie rippling glow to the nights. It is cold, so cold that breath puffs in crystallizing clouds. The earth is young, barely cooled enough to bear even this etiquette of flora, the black stone fault-tangled and geologically raw.

Against the near horizon, a vast city is being born, excavated from the earth's mantle by lumbering, studded automata. Monoliths of fused stone, veined with transit tubes and strung with bioluminescent cords, reach up to the thick clouds. Winds these days are turbulent and the city's forming buildings shrug them off with a grim, armored resilience. Harper points from the city, sensing the learning ecosystem - the city is a cultural and biological process, something to be tuned and retuned.

The tension between the cold prebiotic landscape and the seething polycaste city occupies most of the environment. One wants to teem; the other does not. The landmass is plainly too young, too thin, to make much finding in the way of soil and climate that the city might use. They part slowly, a seeping gray ooze against the uncompromising bulk of live technologies. The city thinks, by imperceptible degrees, the planet thinks, in a tinny technological screech unheard.
