The scene opens on a small, dense town huddled among twisting rivers far from any sea. Damp air clings to the ground in thick mists. The rivers, petty but insistent in this low-lying landscape, have over centuries cut a hundred small canals and ponds into the spongy earth. Long ago, the town rose up around and even onto the water in wooden bridges and heavy mat-roofed houses on tall stilted posts.

Now rot clings to the sides of every building. Vines and mushrooms sprout in every seam of wood. Moss covers every surface drenched by the mists. The porches sag on blocks half-rotten. Peeling sidings hold torches on brackets, but hurry by from one to the next shows the light cuts little into the fog that meanders the narrow lanes as thick as anything finer than soup coal slurry.

Industry has left a strange mark. Pipework leans between buildings, thick as the thickness of a man, still rolling with steam and fluids from beneath. Strange engines grind such as never ran anywhere else jabbering such gears and sparking such lights through holes hacked in the buildings to shove clean air to where inland fish are harnessed to drive the mills, but their systemd scrithes impossible loud in the mists sucking up all other noise and the constant mewl that comes from east in the fog. Some rare times one stumbles out of the mists to the river shore beds, fails flailing and fails and perishes yowling and flakes out on the stones.
