The town perches on the edge of a tranquil lake, its cobble streets winding between sturdy clay-brick houses glazed in graduated blues, from misty-sky above to deep sapphire below. Rows of potted reeds and lotus flank the streets, atop canal-side walkways. Wisps of color drift above the rippling shallows - bladderworts, water-wisteria, cross-leaved heath in shades of purple and white. 

The buildings slope into each other and the canals, honeycombed and textured, windows arched, colonnaded. A story or two, mostly, earth-toned and colonaded, with peaked tiled roofs. Cats sprawl in the perpetual glazed light that seems to come from everywhere at once. Mushrooms grow in the thatch, from domestic chicken coops.

From the town, a squared-off road of flat river-rock leads up to the 2000 meter basalt cliffs that hem the lake valley, and the mines and forges and quarries that produce its building materials. 

Birdcalls echo from the sheer walls. A few grasses and woody shrubs, but no trees on the cliffs, mostly exposed rock. Winding paths up to the mines, with basket-carrying oxen and miners in flowing robes. Low stacks of charcoal and cord-wood. Smoke trickles from the furnaces, making a warm mugginess with the daily mist.

From its stone quay, on two hour intervals day during waking, a silver ferry crosses the lake, gonging gently. Jubilees and lilies and rice, then stone and raw ore on the return trip. The ferry glints, well-maintained, gliding without wake.

Fallow luxuriant rice-paddies occupy the rest of the valley floor, scalloped in irrigated plots that each contain a differnt phase of the rice lifecycle. Water is being sluiced between them by youthful women in simple dresses of blue and white cotton, moving rhythmically and working as first sun rises and last light frets. Pylons of stacked terricotta are 10 meters high, under-scoring occasional reed-roofed outhouses sunk into the algae-slicked banks. 

Quiet and steady and dipped in endless muted light, unruffled in its cycles. Showing only the most regal signs of this year (fish, wings, sap, grain) bursts of rebirth which (of the day) is the only time this land holds its breath.

You notice it first as the western sky bulges, an infusion of lava-lights in the powder-blue. Silver gongs, anf the ferry returns for evening, just prior to ground-mist raising. Lights are showing in the town, in the windows under layered black, red, blue, redblue sheds and shooting up to the mines. You are already in the air.
