You gaze across the broad, flat plain. The sky overhead is a strange sort of greenish grey, with swirling clouds that seem to writhe and twist in the turbulent air. The light is intense and feverish, like a sunbeam after passing through thick mists.

The ground beneath your feet is damp and spongy — sopping wet with some nameless fluid. Not quite water, but something similar. It soaks your boots and clings to your legs above the knees as you wade through the thick, inky muck that coats the ground. Strange bulbous plants push up through the mire, their thick fleshy leaves as much as a yard long. They ooze and glisten, slick with moisture.

And there are sounds — so many sounds — all battling for your attention. Screams tear through the air, animal and inhuman, as some unseen creatures cry their wrath or agony, or perhaps something stranger. Hisses and bleats and squeals blend into a cacophony. The plants themselves crack and pop where they grow, viscous fluids spurting from their wounds. Deeply, from beneath your feet, there is a constant thrum, something massive and alien thrusting or pulsing in the dark. Unseen insects hum in your ears, their vibrations tickling your skin. It is deafening, maddening.

In the distance, you spy structures. A handful of them, scattered and strange, dribbling off across the muck and mire. They are not quite structures, nor artifacts, nor ruins. Hard even to say if they are "man-made"... though what idea of "man" and "made" might apply in this land is beyond guess. Some combination of the three, perhaps. Organic-seeming growths of metal and stone and flesh.

One seems to have a jewel-like light blinking along its length, the color of plasma. Another froths and churns at the edges, as if it is melting into the thick mud that grips it. A third has the shape of a mountain, and wings, and a head that hangs down and stares at you with one filmed-over eye. Unicum. Unanimal. Noumenon. Alive? Once alive and now petrified? Never alive but pretending to have been, or to be now? Hard to say, hard to say, hard to say...

There is a tight band of pressure around your head. Not quite pain. Pressure. To your ears this swells into a deep rushing noise, as if your blood is being pulled up to burst out your ears. To your eyes, plumes of color unfurled across the sky. Yellows and greens and purples — but more too, hues without names, colors past the human range perhaps. The plants seem to open and stiffen for a moment, as if holding their breath, then wilt and release. Another pulse runs through the earth beneath your feet, and light seeps up through the muck to flush the sky again with boiling colors. An inhuman rhythm, some clock or breath or heartbeat from beneath and beyond. This is no place for man, no place for anything known, no place for anything yet named or known, but only for the nothings-scrambling-in-the-being-dark.

You must pick a direction. There are things in the distance, tall and mad and alien. Far. Swirling. Wailing. Waiting. You must...
