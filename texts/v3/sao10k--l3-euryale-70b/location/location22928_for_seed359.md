The landscape is one of stark contrast and unsettling familiarity. One's gaze is drawn to the dense, lush vegetation that clings to every surface, from the sharply-angled walls to the narrow catwalks that weave between buildings. The plants bear no fruit, their leaves a sickly pallor, and their stems twist into unnatural shapes. The air is thick with motion, insects and small animals darting through the foliage with frantic energy.

The structures themselves are the most disorienting element. There are no buildings with clean lines and right angles, no clean floors, no flat roofs or sharp corners. Every surface appears organic, curved and rounded like the interior of a living thing. The walls pulse with a bioluminescent light that rises and falls in a slow, breath-like rhythm. Gasses vent from the surfaces, giving the landscape a dreamlike, hazy atmosphere.

But examining the structures more closely reveals eerie signs of manufacture. Certain angles and curves are too precise, too uniform, to be natural. Here a repeating pattern of grooves, there a flush fitting distinctly machined. One can't shake the feeling that this is a "natural" landscape of the same sort a kitschy dollhouse might portray - all fake green under glow-lights, ordered from a catalog.

The scale is difficult to reckon. The élan vital of the plants and creatures makes the structures hard to parse. Turns a corner often reveals the vastness of an indoor cavern, the fleshy walls climbing a hundred feet to a manufactured sky vanishing into a mephitic mist.

There is little sound, save the susurrus of the plants and insect skittering. No sounds of outward engines or water flow as one would expect - the landscape is hermetically sealed, its internal workings obscure. Only the occasional wet sounds of the walls' hidden bodily functions, a belch or a sigh. Here and there a animal's cry, muffled and distant, or the skittering rush of high-pitched debate. Nothing has the texture of mechanical work.

The whole landscape is a simulation, and one constantly catches hints at the seamed-together parts. Creepse representations of eyes stare blankly from the walls, optical sensors here, actuators there. The traveler has a constant sense of observation, of being a possible thing occluding the true arrow of the mechanism. The thick atmosphere shudders, and the fleshy walls tense, and the light wells down to a ratty red. The ecosystem is one that leaves no residue of its violence under the surface, but the traveler's certain it's there, in the winking wires and pulsing chitin, if only one knew how to turn the horizon past it.
