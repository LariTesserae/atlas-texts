You find yourself standing at the edge of the Grand Bazaar, a sprawling ancient city nestled within the depths of a vast canyon system. The landscape here is one of primordial stone - towering cliffs and deep gorges carved over eons by long-vanished rivers. Yet the area is curiously bone-dry now, the only hints of moisture being the wizened scrub and succulents clinging to every surface.

The canyon walls around you are so massive and sheer that they cut most sunlight, situating you in a realm of dimness with the city's lights flickering on long before dusk. Yet the view outwards is unavoidable - layer upon layer of eroded sediment holds the timeline of this place, from red desert sands to gleaming bands of crystalline minerals. Far to the west, the purple range rises up, rains shadow cast over the dry valleys.

This is a dense city of organically-grown adobe architecture, buildings sprawling and branching like the desert plants themselves. Everywhere are structures falling towards crumbling bends, yet still pegged by fractal layers of materials - packed earth, bitumen mortar, importer tiles. Among them wind deep street-canyons, tunnels and stairwells forming hidden spaces within. The front-facing buildings around the huge market square are gaudy in colorful tiling and banners, barkers shouting offers in tongues unknown.

The sounds and scents are overwhelming - bleats and clucks of animals, scenting spices and fresh bread, iron ringing on copper, screams of enjoyment, baby wails, croakers of merchants. Humans and earthly humanoids move the streets in bright robes, though lithe blue-skinned cousins with spaded ears and twitching snouts call out from deep shade. Everywhere is an absorbent warmth that seems to muzzle cold.

Your senses are already straining trying to track the hundreds of lives proceeding normally around you in this subcanyon, city carve out of ancient geology, when the street is bisected by a line of guzzing flames drawing back a horde of pale shambling forms that studder the packed earth and rattle the city walls in their desperation.
