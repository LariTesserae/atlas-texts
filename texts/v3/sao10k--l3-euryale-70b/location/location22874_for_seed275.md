A vast subterranean cavern network, stretching far in all directions, lit by a diffuse bioluminescence emitted from the walls and the profusion of flora that clings to every surface. The air is cool and dry, circulating through a complex system of ventilation shafts that criss-cross the cave. 

The caverns vary in size, but even the smaller ones are huge, with vaulted ceilings and pillars of rough-hewn rock that have been shaped by eons of geological forces. The stone is shot through with mineral veins in colors of silver, iron-red, and unexpected purples and greens. In some places, crystalline formations have developed, refracting the ambient light into patterns of shimmering color.

Every surface is coated in translucent fungal hyphae that serve as substrate for sprouting mats of glial-like fibers - devoid of chlorophyll but still photosynthetic. Shimmering sheets of photosynthetic membranes, perhaps derived from cyanobacteria, lap gently in the circulating air currents, converting the diffuse light into chemical energy. Pools of oily metabolic fluids glisten on the cave floors, ground into emulsions by churning, writhing mats of_below.

Ropes of something that might be called moss or lichen, laden with spores that drift in the currents like pollen, dangles from the cavern ceilings, swaying gently in the breeze. Vines and root-like structures link distant surfaces and pools, pulsing and rippling as they shift and uncoil. Shelves of folded fungal "leaves" balance on the rock ledges, bouncing and quivering, but absorbing the light.

Flickers of luminescence burst and fade across mats of fibers; sprouts of fungal hyphae emerge from soil-like mats, developing into bubbling cups and tubes. Thorny fibers scour the air currents and collect spores from the gaseous stream of air. Quivering, feather-like structures fan stamens and style into shifting currents, consolidating the pollen into turgid sac-anthers.

Gas-sacks the size of houses strain with wind, moored by wirey cables to the cluster of massive, dimly luminescent flowers blooming on every tower and dome. The flowers are formed of fleshy, non-vascular petal-like structures laced with pulsing fibers, their stamens rising and falling with interior fluid reflections. Active venation flushes pods chambered like a thousand eggs and glowing like fat candles drips from the serrated brim of the sepals into the liquid pools where it beads up in egg-sized mounds and granules.

Where the cavern walls emerge from the pulsing mats of fibers, vast insect-like forms with chitin-plated carapaces emerge on bundles of spindly, articulated limbs. Hive-like mounds and domettes rise from the suppurating tubes of roots and tubers, symbiotic whitewashed by vast quantities of insect-like forms, shedding and losing form to each other's digestive fluids in an undulating mass. Lashing and humping, the mounds pulse and heave, regularly emitting thronghs of agitated, ecstatic insects that rejoin the process, clambering in copulatory displays through and over each other to smother the fertile face of the hives in their scented fluids before falling to join to swirl in the source pools.

A calm, muffled stasis seems to reign over the caverns, as if the massive size of the ecosystem allows for no anxiety over time. Metabolic violence underscores fall of spore-sacs, but the insect-like forms that pick them up are indistinct from the flowers in terms of common filament. The great flowers seem to consume and merge with and produce the seeds, their spores decanulating onto the energetic pools of nutrient fluids to sprout at once into fungal whorls. Fungal membranes seem to constitute growth centers linking flower and insect to disrupted spore-sacs.

The result is not chaos but an intense, vigorous ecosystem of a sort. The being feels a tranquil purpose and unity to the space, despite the chaotic details and evident violence of the exchanges between the various members of the ecological whole. The insect-like forms are not helter-skelter or wrestling for space so much as interweaving in their largest motion. The flowers seem as deliquescent as the fluids, their fibers pulsing genital as if they hankered for the violence of the fertilizations with the insect forms. There is no sense of generations or decay, but of a constant exchange, the spores a nutrient flow rather than a posterity surviving an inclement future. Though colony scale collapses occur, there is no sense of real time cycling or urgency.
