The landscape before you stretches to the horizon in every direction, an endless sprawl of uniform black carbon-prepreg composite. Sleek black spires and domes rise at regular intervals from the sea of material, defined by crisp edges but no extraneous decoration. The architecture is sleek and modern in style, with hints of an industrial aesthetic in the visible bolting and plating. 

The terrain is completely flat and level, without any natural features at all. The composite is slightly warm to the touch, radiating a steady, even heat. Droplets of water occasionally bead and roll down the slick surfaces, having seemingly condensed from the still, damp air. Though it's warm, the humidity is high and makes the air feel heavy and oppressive.

Aside from the perfect black buildings, the surface is devoid of any vegetation or geological features. No dirt, sand, or other natural materials are visible - this is a completely artificial environment, constructed to the last atom. Dim red safety lights mark paths through the compound.

The only sounds are the occasional distant hum of unseen machinery, and a low background hissing that could be superheated water pipes or pneumatic conduits. A strange subsonic thrum vibrates through the soles of your boots. There is an almost complete lack of animal life and no wind at all.

Huge automated slider doors rhythmically pulsed open and closed on the larger buildings, revealing cavernous dark interiors that the eyes struggle to penetrate. The vague outlines of half-assembled vehicle chassis and other unfinished industrial products lurk within. 

Your dizzyingly entire field of view is filled with the black, futuristic facility, with no visible horizon. Distant lightning, possibly man-made, flashes across the starless sky, each stroke of light revealing the landscape in awful clarity, a cookie-cutter pattern of factory and hab buildings repeating for miles in every direction. 

The air itself seems to press down on you, an immaterial weight generated by the oppressive architecture, the heavy humidity, and the totally alien nature of the place. It's hard not to feel like the living equivalent of a spore bacterial culture, just one of uncountable many in the vast petri dish of the facility's inner workings.
