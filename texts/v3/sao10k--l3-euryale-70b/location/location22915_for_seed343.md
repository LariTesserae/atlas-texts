The air hangs heavy with moisture, pregnant with the promise of imminent downpour. Yet the skies remain stubbornly clear, a brilliant blue tortured by meandering clouds like arthritic fingers. Pools of shallow water dot the landscape, their glassy surfaces utterly still as if the very wind itself dare not disturb their opacity. The water is warm against the skin, laden with minerals that leave the flesh tinged faintly red.

All around, vegetation engulfs the world, an overwhelming profusion of forms and species beyond count or categorization. Vines the thickness of ship's rope lash and dangle from diminutive, twisted trees more root than branch. Grasses strain tower-high, their blades as wide as a hand is long. Ferns and flowers metaphorically spew pollen and scent into the thick air, plunged into uncontrollable ferment by the claustrophobic abundance of teeming life.

There is no horizon, the sea of plant-life extending beyond sight. The eye strains against unseen distant limits, unable to find purchase on any distinction between growth and sky or soil. Distant peaks of land, if they exist, are utterly concealed, occluded behind the hectic entanglement of vegetable forms.

The air is loud and hot and heavy and alive with movement outside of view. Sounds propagate through the vegetation like a physical medium - the spanking creak of unseen predators loping through the undergrowth a dozen gnarled fists high, the pushy pustule-burst of belches and farts from unseen tubular maws packed ridiculously with mulch and muck. Nights are bedeviled by the sawing skreee of insects a hundred times as large as expected, enmeshed in the fibrous stalks like colossal burrs.

Shaped structures exist in this miasma only as hexagonal prisms mottled gray-green with lichen and spleenwort. They rise from the tangled humus at infrequent intervals, eleven again, equally spaced by some arcane inhuman geometry that tugs at the edges of apprehension.
