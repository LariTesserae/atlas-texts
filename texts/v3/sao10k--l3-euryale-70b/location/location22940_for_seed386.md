The Tropemont region is a dense and extreme set of subtropical highlands. The terrain is a jumble of steep, eroded hillsides covered in thick moss and ferns that seep water. Mist perpetually condenses along the slopes, adding to the dank humidity of the warm air. Shallow streams trickle and gush from nearly every gully, flowing together into roaring rivers that plunge over moss-covered cataracts into valleys far below.

Towering above sit volcanic peaks, their flanks thickly furred with huge club moss and vines. Steaming vents puff sulfurous fumes into the muggy air. Every surface is slick, damp and slick with condensation, creeks burble in hollows, water trickles from crannies. The soil is thick and ropey, never drying.

Swarms of gnats and midges hover over every watercourse. Huge electric blue morpho butterflies alight to sip from streams, fluttering their wide wings to hover.
