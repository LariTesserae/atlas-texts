The ancient river valley stretches before you, flanked on either side by low, eroded hills, their curves like the gentle swell of a slumbering titans. Between the slopes lies a vast, flat expanse of bare rock, pocked and pitted with tiny pools that glitter like scattered mirrors. Savanna grasses and shrubs sprout from the cracks and crevices where soil has accumulated, spaced far apart as if unwilling to crowd one another.

The air hangs heavy and humid, thick with the musky scent of earth and vegetation. Puddles still shimmer on the ground after the latest rain, though no clouds can be seen in the vast blue dome overhead. Water seeps from the underlying bedrock, staining the stone a deep ochre, the iron oxide bleeding down in watery rivulets. Caverns yawn in the hillsides, enticing and dark with mystery.

Shadows writhe across the landscape as a given day wears on, thrown by the lowering sun, but the light remains bright and strong for all that even at dusk. The sun itself is obscured in a sort of haze, not thick enough to hide the sky's color yet lending a softness to everything. Little wind stirs the air, and no leaves rustle in trees, for there are next to no trees to do so.

The only sounds are the distant, ever-present gurgle of the river, the skittering of lizards and insects, and the infrequent cries of birds wheeling overhead on the thermals. A deep, mindless serenity permeates the place. Time seems to crawl, as if the very stone itself is so ancient it can barely remember speed. The ponds steaming gently in the warmth look like they must be undisturbed by anything more than the occasional falling frog. Anjections are etched in the rock, long dried river courses become stone, the abandoned paths of countless dry seasons. The bucolic tranquility hugs you like a stallion, and you feel the centuries element into your bones.
