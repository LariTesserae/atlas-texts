The Flooded Abyss

Deep, deep down in the darkest abyss of the ocean, where no light penetrates from the surface far above, a vast flooded plain stretches out in every direction. The water is frigid and sluggish, a cold, undisturbed chasmal sea floor. Faint benthic currents only occasionally stir the dense silt that blankets the terrain, and then it shimmers like shifting black silk.

The landscape is one of gently sloping trenches and canyons carved by primeval processes, eons before multicellular life emerged to crawl the Earth. Basaltic rock, cemented nodules of minerals, and compressed precipitated sediments form the bed of the abyss. Nowhere are hills or outcroplings; instead, the seafloor is all long valleys and hazy reaches of water-eroded grooves and refugia.

Scattered across these cold chasms, rare clusters of sea life cling diffidently. A few species of anglerfish and viperfish with bioluminescent lures drift through the darkness, their light organs pulsing faint color. Everywhere is starred with skittering shrimp, starfish, and scavenger fish skulking in the silt. The fish are deformed, their emergent evolution adjusted to hearing, smell, and sensitivity to colonial cycles in the long drift of nutrients. Bioluminescent haze shimmers in clumps, viscous and mucoid jellyfish with trailing stinging tentacles, pulsing in a dim unearthly glow.

Rare too, almost invisible, a few meandering lines of mineral hydrothermal vents scratch the surface. Supercritical fluid gouts from the quirks in the Earth's crust, thick with sulfides and metals. They meander through the cold soft sediment of the seafloor plain in tinged viscous channels. Clumps of brine shrimp scurry through the silt and concretions of the vents, grazing on bacterial zymurgy. Carnivorous tubeworms and Titing Pitch Hot tubeworms grip the vent chimneys' mineral tubes, their plumes tasting the chemical riches.

The abyssal plain is no stable ecology but a flux of water and matter, a conversation of fluid and sediment. So it has been since the Earth's mantle first cooled and gave up its sweet terrors of gas and fluid to excavate the sea chasms. Aeons in abeyance, it endures as perhaps the oldest living landscape, never known to those pulse-hopping elbows of insects and lizards that clog the flesh above. It's a cold-ecology, manifestum of water and sediment and chthonic chimney of epigenesis, within which ancestral forms of life first resembled and divided and exolved. This landscape has carried through to the present not changed, undercurrents of unrealized potential for the new novelties of fluidity into the gene-pool of life.
