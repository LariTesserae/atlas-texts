High, sheer cliffs rise and fall in rugged undulations, the shattered remains of some long-vanquished mountain range. Crags and spurs jut out at impossible angles, as if the earth itself had been rent asunder and then frozen in time. The stone is a rich, almost ebon dark gray, eternal and unyielding against the elements. 

Yet green growth clings to every surface, from hardy lichens and mosses that paint the cliffs in vivid but muted colors, to gnarled thorn- and scrub trees that find purchase in the slightest breach. Hardier trees and vines have colonized ledges and crevices, roots boring deep into cracks and fissures. In sheltered grottoes, ferns and other lush greenery adorn the misty air.

Wild goats, their coats thick for the biting chill, navigate the impossible paths with balletic grace, edging along narrow ledges, leaping from outcrop to outcrop. Their perfectly hollow footsteps echo through still air.
