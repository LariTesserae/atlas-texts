Cascading down gentle hills and meandering through misty valleys, an utterly alien world sprawls before us. The landscape is dominated by a curious architecture, not of stone or metal, but of a strange organic matter. Gently undulating quasicrystalline spires reach towards a pale, diffusely glowing sky, their edges fuzzy with a constant condensation that drips down in dripping rivulets. The material has an almost gelatinous quality, pulsating with a very slow rhythm in hues of wash blue and violet. 

Rising along these bio-pillars and dripping structures, a unbelievably lush tapestry of non-foliage clings. Biotowering fronds in purple, turquoise, and electric pink unfurl from curving stems. Better to call them sensors, antennae, or transceivers, for they tremble in the resonant sounds consistent with water movements, inaudible squeals, and even the electromagnetic hum we cannot hear. The entire nonvegetation comes to life, palpably growing even in the minute scales of time, as if driven by vastly faster cycles than our biota.

From this built expanse, life emerges, swarms, migrates, and interacts in density beyond imagining. Beings that might be thought of as spiders, pseudofloria, jelly-like creatures, and viscous insectoid pseudoflora creep, hover, dive, and slough themselves against the buildings. Everywhere, from skies to substrate, the world is so thick with living entities that the concept of background is nearly lost in the profusion of active entities. We are far from alone.
