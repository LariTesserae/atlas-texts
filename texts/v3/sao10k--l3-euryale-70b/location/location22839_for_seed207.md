In the depths of a vast, dormant volcano, a cavernous chamber swells with an ethereal mist. The atmosphere is an unnatural cold, a chill that seems to seep into the bone just from being present. Tiny droplets of moisture condense on every surface, collecting in the crevices and cracks of the basalt walls into glittering beads of dew. 

The cavern itself is lush, overgrown with a verdant carpet of fungi and lichens that coat every inch of the lava rock. Iridescent mushrooms clutch themselves to the walls, pulsing with an organic bioluminescence. Thready mosses and tangled lichens drape themselves across boulders and fallen rock, a thick living blanket. The vegetation seems to thrive within the darkness, nourished entirely by the constant humidity and the faint light that filters down from above.

Structures poke through the organic foliage - not ruins, but more organic, organic shapes somehow grown or merged with the local stone and fungus. Geodes with crystalline cores that pulse and wink like captured stars. Patches of silver veins ramble like fungal hyphae. Glimmering fiberoptic tendrils worm through the earthen walls and roots of the fungi. Inert, long-dormant machines coated with heavy biofilm nap through the velvety loam.

The cavern is deathly silent except for the sound of trickles, drips and a slow susurrus of growth. A slow, sluggish wind occasionally rustles through, bearing the musty odor of mycelium and damp stone. In the corner, a crystal rock deposited by an uplift or deluging streams of nanite soup rests still in the loam, its flat informational display glittering in a low-tech pidgin from before wireless ever was invented. 

There is no animal life here, only a plethora of fungi and bacteria and lichen undulate the surfaces of the dormant volcano. A planetoid fabber, fossil annex, xenotech outcropping or some other senescent transmutative organism of the old orders of self-organization. The cold stilness of a museum. A living vault of Dziga.
