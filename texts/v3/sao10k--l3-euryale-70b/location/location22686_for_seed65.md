The land stretches out before you in undulating waves. The terrain is a patchwork of arid plains, prairie-grasses, and craggy outcroppings of gray limestone. Jutting out from these flatlands at irregular intervals are the towering monoliths - great pillars of stone a hundred feet tall. Their sides are worn smooth by untold ages' weathering and streaked in swirling patterns of ochre, sienna, and ashen gray.

The air is hazy and cool, sharp with the scent of dry loam and limestone. A breeze stirs grit and pebbles, whispering over the stones. Strange shapes flit and dance at the fringes of vision - shards of light reflecting off flinty rock faces, gnarled roots writhing through crevices.

Life clings hardily to every crevice and high ledge. Vines with heart-shaped leaves clamber up cracks and spill down in tangled curtains.
