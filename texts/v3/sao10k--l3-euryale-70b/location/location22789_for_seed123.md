The fog rolls in across the narrow river valley in thick tendrils, swirling around the bases of weirdly twisted trees that claw at it with gnarled branches. Rivulets of runoff cut gullies into the damp grey soil as spatters of rain hit and immediately soak in. Droplets form on every surface - leaves, twigs, scaly mottled bark, and bead off into the rivulets. It's a strange landscape that's squarely in its early days - buckled rock formations crunch up through the earth, angled at sharp jabbing corners like teeth. Shattered slab fragments choke the river as it gurgles past. Nothing here feels old, weathered to softness. It's raw, rock starting its conversion to soil, scraggly foliage barely gaining a foothold.

Squat, rounded factories dot the landscape concealing themselves in the foliage. Steam burbles up from their willowing metal stacks, curling tendrils warming the fog in small pockets. Soon, an entire network of small structures will be built between the buckled rock and the river's edge. But for now they are sparse, giving plenty of room to the strange, prodigious vegetation.

Flowering vines with punch-holes writhing over their surfaces have claimed uncanny, irregularly-shaped metal artifacts and heaped their leaves over them. Blighted bulbs straining toward anemic purple and bioluminescent blue light sprout right out of the grey soil, quivering to some unheard frequency. Huge spots of mold and patches of vivid red fungi freckle the tree trunks. The place teems with life straining to claim every surface. Extraordinary life, but there all the same.

A doling dawn begins to wash in on the other side of the rippling river. Weedy banks give way to more constructed areas - ramshackle agglomerations of metal and dhjalk, galleries and catwalks forming vast warrens amidst stunted plazas and muddy lots. Slice-balconies jutting at odd non-angles give out onto the river. Eventually the factories will turn their lathes and pitches to supplying the growing sprawl but for now the transition from Greater Wild to Frequented Quarter is still pointillated, enhanced in stop-motion by the weird pause of the sprawling river's swirling currents. For now the condensed air remains heavy and chattering with the susurrus of the valley and its industry and its river and its teeming gradual opera of life in unending quiet.
