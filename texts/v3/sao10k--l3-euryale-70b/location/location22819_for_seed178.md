A vast, liminal wetland spreads before you, an unstable boundary between land and sea, constantly shifting with the tides and the terrible energies that saturate this place. The air is thick and wet, heavy with sea-foam and condensing moisture. Visibility is limited to a few dozen meters, the atmosphere churning with fog but also strange, pulsing lights—a degaussed glow that seems to come from nowhere and everywhere at once. There is no visible sun, just a gray-blanketted sky seething above.

Lush, alien vegetation thrives in this cold, lightless place—gnarled pseudotrees like giant fungi, their trunks and twisted limbs dripping with bioluminescent moss. Rice-grass and reed-like stalks grow to heights of 5-8 meters tall, undulating in the roiling miasma. Everywhere, claws and tendrils of roots tunnel through waterlogged soil and churning mud. The ground is a sodden mat of organic material, frothing and squelching with each step. Fresh springs and seepage keeps the ground perpetually sodden.

The ruin of some cyclopean structure looms in the distance—remnants exceeding 60 meters in height, unknown materials mostly fused and glassified, like petrified wood. Mottled in pastels of biege and gray in the weak light. Scintillating with some residual technology within its matrix. The much-degraded arcology burrows its stricken roots many kilometers down into the inevitable caverns that honeycomb this unstable strata. Pale mobs of erratic, semi-visible wraiths haunt the failing site, eerie whispers and hateful lights in the gelid mist. They are concentrated in its vicinity but range further.
