The place is a gloomy, verdant grotto, hemmed in by steep, vegetated walls of stone. The air is humid and cool, with a perpetual mist swirling through the shadows. Tumbling rivulets and quiet, gurgling springs thread through the rocky floor, gathering in emerald pools. Climbing vines and thick moss blanket the overhanging ledges, glistening in the half-light. 

Rising from one corner of the grotto is a massive stone column, studded with close-set crystals that wink with faint, cold light in the dimness. Atop this phallic monolith, an unadorned bronze bell hangs, nearly silent except for the occasional sigh as the damp breezes caress it. 

Scattered about the twilight crevices are hand-sized carved stone figures, some obviously human, others more ambiguous. Tiny tokens and bead necklaces drape some, while finger-smoothed prayer wheels are recessed into the rock of others. Worn, unadorned paths thread between the stone icons and statues, leading to small, formalized collecting pools of luminous mineral-rich water emerging from the bedrock.

There is no sound of wildlife -- or any other sort of life in the grotto -- but the heaviness and richness of the sodden atmosphere suggests a inward-focused vitality. Water and undercutting have carved the stone into fluted fins and hollowed out wing-like sweeping geodes, shaping the available space toward if not inviting meditation then introspection. The water, the stone, the thick air and faint minerals are the only presences, and they feel less inert than impartient, heavy with ancient and contemplative purpose.
