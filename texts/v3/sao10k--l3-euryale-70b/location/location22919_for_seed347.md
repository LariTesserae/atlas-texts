The land is young and raw, with young mountains rising sharply from the plains, their peaks jagged and cloud-wreathed. Valleys and canyons carve their way through the stony metal-rich bedrock, in them fast-flowing rivers thick with silt. Aquifers near the surface bubble up to form seeps and springs that gather into boggy lakes and marshes along the low ground. The water-saturated earth pushes up from below, lush vegetation fast claiming every rocky outcropping.

The marshes are a vibrant riot of waterflowers and rushes, cat-tails and cattails, their leaves an extravagant display of greens from deep emerald to palest chartreuse. Butterflies dance in the humid, heavy air, sensilla twitching at the stilled water. Turtles sun themselves on half-submerged logs, and fish bunch in the shallows. 

Inland, if you can call it truly separate, grow dense forests of towering trees with strong gray boughs and thick furry needles, the air between them packed with epiphytes and aerial roots and vines. Their trunks are strung with ferns and mosses, and mushrooms of all colors rise from the rotting down. The floor itself is densely matted with bracken and thick with layered duff. 

The whole atmosphere is heavy, oppressive, almost carmic. There is no wind. The air is damp and cold and exhausted of oxygen. It is smothering, cocooning, the landscape feels alive, half-asleep. Somewhere a spring bubbles up in a thick ribbon of vapor. Somewhere a rock cracks with water in the cold. An insect wings tap-taps at your ear. Damp softens the edges of your thoughts.
