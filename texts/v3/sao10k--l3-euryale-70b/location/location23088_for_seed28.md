Deep in the heart of the Searing Saguaro wastes lies a hidden pocket-valley, an arid oasis of life in the vast lifeless expanse. The sheer redrock walls rise a hundred feet at a 70° angle around the sandy floor, caging in the lush yet fragile micro-ecosystem within.

The valley is filled by a riot of twisted organ pipe cactus, 30-foot spires of leafy fleshy columns studded with wicked thorns. Wedged among them are squat barrel cacti, waxy blue-green globes piled in precarious columns and spires. Where there is open space, saltbush and cholla throng the sandy soil.

By day, the East Washington sun blazes without mercy. Red heat devils swirl off the sand, igniting scraggly tuft grasses that shell away to ash. The tall saguaro cacti creak and exude beads of waterless sap that blaze like brandy. The air burns; tin cans left unattended in the open will burst their seams.

By night, the world cools within minutes and a million stars pop to flower. The moon, uncovered by cloud, sheet-light the ravine. Owls pass silent as spectres, peppered booms of cannon crickets, the faint grinding of pack-rats in their sleep-warrens.

The valley's ecology thrums, a intricate dance of life at the most minimalist fringe of possibility. Chuckwallas and desert tortoises duel for plumpest barrel cactus fruits. Gila monsters stalk over the sand, budding from redroc timberlined dens to hunt grubs and beetles. Pumas feast on the screaming hares and nibbling sharks of the night.

The little stone remains of an old Hohokum village ride the rim, eriodte slabs sealed by wind dust, ashes, and sheepfat. The dead doorway arches, small as if to frame only the acacia curtain of spines that drag the darkness behind. In the village are petroglyphs carved into a block wall, a venus of Willendorf with acacia-leaf pudenda— the female fertility engram re-imagined to the foliage-dry Saguaro demiurge.
