Your journey begins on a barren, windblown plain of powdery golden sand that stretches to the horizon in all directions. Despite the harsh desert conditions, this alien landscape is alive with vegetation - but not the greenery you would expect. Bioluminescent fungi in shades of violet, blue and purple grow in odd, sinuous shapes, casting an eerie glow that fluctuates to an unknown rhythm. The night sky blazes with constellations of red and orange stars. 

Angular, crystalline outcroppings of mineral rise up at intervals like monuments, their facets catching the starlight. Intricate carvings cover the surfaces of the formations, the runes of an ancient forgotten language that once held great power. Now they are just mysterious symbols, slowly being worn away by the constant winds. 

Small, organic structures appear occasionally, sprouting from the ground in clusters. Curved spires of twisting wood and pulsating membranes in earth tones of ochre and umber, like some cross between a stupa and a sea anemone. They seem to be the living homes of the native sapients, their membranes undulating gently as they respire the wind and light. The sapient's aerated religion is bound to the wind and sky.

You can feel the landscape is unsettled. An event of cosmic imbalance has unleashed unnatural pressures and ripples through the underlying fabric of the world itself. The bioluminescent flora flicker erratically, the starry canopy swells and shifts like the skin of an animal. Distant stormclouds the size of entire ecosystems are always on the horizon, lightning dancing along their margins. 

This is an unstable space on the edge of a cataclysm. The acid blood running in the shrilling undergrowth turns the sands to glass in curling eddies. The hermaphrodite spirits that inhabit these sites emerge from the iron trees en masse, once per century to mate their permission and turn the world inside out. An astral storm has scoured the land, leaving only the weathered ruins of the crumbled precursor cities, petrified in crystallized ash.
