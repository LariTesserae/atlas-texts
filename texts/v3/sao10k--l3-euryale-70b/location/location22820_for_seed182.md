We find ourselves in an unusual, subterranean cavern. The temperature is sweltering hot, the air thick and humid. Steam rises from various pools of water scattered about. The cavern is Baptized in an eerie dimness, lit faintly by glowing mushrooms and lichen growing on the rock walls. 

The cavern is filled to capacity with packed stands of bamboo and ferns, their leaves dripping with condensation. Winding between the dense vegetation are the remnants of an ancient hydrothermal system - channels and pools filled with mineral-rich, gently bubbling water. Scattered tufts of cottony fog drift over the waters.

From the cavern walls sprout crystalline formations shimmering in hues of green and purple. Their surfaces are pierced and embedded with curious mechanical contraptions - burnished copper pipes, hissing steam pistons, click-clacking gearwheels dappled in verdant moss. The odd clank of settling machinery echoes through the swampy air. 

Occasionally, a submerged mechanical creature 'breaches' the surface of a pool - and oddly organic-looking mergence of leaves and gears and burnished copper, propelling itself through the water. Or a colony of gnat-like insects swirl and dive around a bubbling fumarole, trailing glowing filaments.

The place has the feel of a incubation chamber, or a hatchery of unusual hybrids of creature and machine. An alchemical brew where organic and inorganic blend and spawn bizarre new forms within the stewing, steam-saturated depths.
