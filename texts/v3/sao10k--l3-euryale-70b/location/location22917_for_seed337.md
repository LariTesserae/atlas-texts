I imagine a landscape of compact stone buildings rising from an rocky, arid plain, the grey-brown structures blending into the spare scrub and jumbled boulders around them like the warty growths on a lizard's hide. The earth itself seems raw, untouched by the human constructions scattered across its surface, terrain of clefts and crests and dead-end canyons. It is a texture of stone and stone and more stone, varied by only slight gradations of shade — flint, umber, rind, cinder.

The buildings of the city crowd one another at their bases then rise at angles to meet overhead, forming a geometric pattern of triangles and parallelograms, with the smaller dwellings of the outskirts fanning out like clothespins spread across a rocky tablecloth. Eaves cantilever out to support rooflines of small, semi-translucent crystalline tiles that fit together like a precise toothed gear. The agglomerate ductwork of chimneys and airshafts tunnels through the stony matrix of the city like an ant-farm. Polished copper pipes shine like glistening wires where they curl between buildings.

Here and there motionless figures can be glimpsed, perched at the edges of stone ledges and in shadowed doorways, looking out over the rugged tract of land between the city's precipices and the horizon — a carpet of small rocks and round stones sparsely studded with twisted saltbush and barrel cactus, ravens tumbling silently in the head of the wind above, a single track of dust tailing behind one of the birds, dissipating.

Things move in the distance but do not come towards the city — feeding passing birds glide out of sight in the vanilla-tinted haze on the horizon, a small mended pack crests a small bluff, a manicule of antelope-beetle strides across the exile landscape under the halved face of a young volcano, regular puffs of dusty smoke pluming from its crescent caldera and dispersing in the air. Worn switchbacks of trail finger their way up the rock to the rim of its dead crater. The air is keen, then stale — warm, then chill — a permanent three-o'clock sun stares from a thin-walled upper atmosphere.
