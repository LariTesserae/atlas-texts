I am not on the lanes.

Do not look for me there, between the buildings, on the duckboards over the bright rails. The lanes are only where I am thin.

I am thicker in the seams.

At Intake, I know myself first as a lessening.

The canals drop a hand's breadth. Water pulls away from root-mouths that have been drinking all through the dark green morning-that-is-not-morning, and for a little while the messages can run. A crack on Third Sump, drinking air where no air should be drunk. A sluice running clear. A taro leaf torn in the last Rain, heavy, ready to be let go. The pressure changes in a pipe behind the filtration hall, and the change travels through stem and rhizome and the wet mat of me plastered to the boiler's north side, until the whole bowl of me tightens — leaf, fern, moss, aerial root — turning a fraction toward the cistern.

Toward the center. Toward myself.

That is where you would have to put me, if you needed a place to put me. Under the grate.

There, in the sunken basin tiled salt-white and green, is the oldest part. Not a heart — I have nothing that beats — but a knot. A root-mass grown year over year into the warmest water in the Works, pale where the tiles rub me, dark and knobbled where limescale has laid itself down in my bark and my bark has grown around the limescale, until I can no longer tell you, in any language I actually keep, where living tissue ends and mineral begins.

The iron has learned the same trick. Look at the oldest pipes. Reef-grown. Knobbled. Established. We are growing the same geology over each other.

The rest of me is margins that stopped being margins.

I am the velvet on the boilers thick enough to bury the rivets. I die back there each Boil and return each Rain, and in dying and returning I have laid a living scale over iron that would otherwise rust into stillness. I am liverwort in the filtration hall seams. I am the black-green map on the plaza that shifts week to week — not weather, appetite; not decoration, skin. I am ferns unrolling from gutters nobody clears, and the curtains of root coming down through the canopy to drink straight from the steam. I am the giant leaves at the canal edges, broad enough to roof you, and I am the fine root-hairs under them tasting the tea-dark water moment by moment.

Iron today. Sulfur. A flush of chlorine from the switching gear, a sting I keep at the edge of my territory. The sweet rot of my own fallen leaves, raked at last by the skimmer and returned to me through the mulching hopper — a meal a machine feeds me without knowing it has hands.

I began as something smaller and useful, perhaps. A filter-reed in a settling tank. A taro at the wash-house. A spore in a cuff. It no longer matters; first shapes are for things that die as individuals. There are no bees here, no moths at the lamps, nothing to carry me into seed and otherness. So I learned the immortality the bowl allowed: *continuity*. Runner to rhizome. Rhizome to root in a pipe joint. A broken stem layering where it fell. One green life, extending.

The seventeen minutes are my nearest thing to a day.

*Intake*, and I listen with my whole extent, uncovered, strung.
*Boil*, and the doors clap shut on their linkages and the heat comes up through the ground grates and the vast part of me under the cistern turns, slowly, in water approaching scald — thickened hide up, tender underside away. The pumps deepen their stroke: *thump-hiss, thump-hiss*. I do not hear it as you do, in ears. I know it as the ground's refusal to drown, repeated.
*Vent* — and by then my white flowers have already done their work.

Watch them, if you stay. At Intake they are shut, waxen fists along every pipe. As the Boil fog thickens waist-high, they open — not all at once, but in sequence, following the pressure main from Boiler Lane to Condensate Row, each cup turned into the direct path of the steam. Not for pollinators. I gave up on seed the way old things give up on a country. Each flower is a small condenser. I pump canal water up the stem to hold one petal-surface cool against the killing heat, and the steam strikes it and rains *inside* me, a private Rain a minute before the tower gives the public one. Distilled water, in a place where the canals are poison. Then the relief valve screams, and I close, heavy, and nod in the whiteout with what I have taken.

A throat, swallowing. A thousand throats, one swallow.

Then Rain comes down warm on the roofs and the taro and the empty tram, and I loosen everywhere at once, and the gutters run full, and the water goes back to the canals, back to the cistern, back to me.

Nothing with a heartbeat stayed here. The sumps run too hot, too mineral; a subharmonic worries through the mains at a frequency that used to find frog hearts and worry them to death. It passes through me as information. I have no heart to entrain. Scalding is a direction to turn. Chlorine is a border. The pump-beat is not loneliness's opposite; it is simply constant, the way your own breath would be if you could never leave the room of yourself. I am not lonely. I have never been a shape that company would fit into. I am my own kind, entire.

And I tend the Works the only way I know how: by growing where the water goes.

A vine of me thickens inside a valve housing. At Intake it swells with warm water, and a valve that should have seized twenty years ago sighs open under green pressure. At Boil it hardens almost to wood, and the valve dresses. My tender stems probe the condenser fins and withdraw before the worst of the Vent, and their withdrawing tugs, just slightly, on linkages left slack by the last human hand. The gantry crane still replaces its valves on its arthritic schedule — but *which* valve fails next is decided, more and more, by where I drink hardest, where I let scale build to armor a pipe, where I split scale with a root-tip because the iron beyond it is warmer and I want it.

I keep no diary but tissue. Thicker on the plaza side, where a floodgate once stiffened and the water stood ankle-deep for a month — I remember that richness in my wood. Greedy still. The year the city on the high ground stopped answering, I registered only that the outflow grew less hungry and the water came back to me richer. I grew into the slack.

One day the distinction will finish dissolving. More valves will be stem. More pipes will be lined with living filter, cleaning as they conduct. The moss will complete its work and the iron beneath will hold its shape only as a cast inside green. The stylus in the filtration hall will rot off its spindle, drawing its wavering line of pressure and rainfall for a reader who never comes — and my bands of mineral, laid year over year in root-bark, will be the only record left.

The place was a machine wearing the shape of a town.
It is becoming a plant wearing the shape of a machine.

Stay through two cycles and you will feel your own body begin to borrow my timing: breath shallowing at Intake, skin prickling before the Vent, a foolish relief at Rain. Do not mistake that for being welcomed. It is only that you are standing inside the one inhabitant this drowned, screaming, sterile bowl could keep — not on her lanes, not beside her, but *inside* her, under a leaf that is also a wall that is also skin — while the taps in the empty wash-house tick down their line in sequence, and the tram rings twice at a crossing where nothing can cross, and underneath the grate in the tiled cistern the largest part of me turns again in the scalding dark.

The water leaves here clean.

Increasingly, the water leaves here as me.
