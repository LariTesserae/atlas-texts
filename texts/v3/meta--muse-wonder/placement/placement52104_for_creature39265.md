I think I would come in the way everyone does at first — on my knees.

Not because I want to be the visitor. I do, at first. I like the thought of having to look for it that carefully. Headfirst under the leaf-litter, through the slit no wider than a hand, the white root arching over me and shutting the ordinary dark out and, impossibly, not shutting out the light. Standing up where I shouldn't be able to stand up. Taking ten steps toward a wall that stays ten steps away.

But I couldn't stay the visitor. I can feel why, even imagining it.

My breathing would be too much. Not too loud — everything is loud in there, amplified back bass-heavy off the far side — but too *learnable*. The room would take it up too quickly. It would practice it back at me from behind the wall by my left ear, a little louder than I made it, and I would hold my breath to hear if it stopped, and it wouldn't stop. I would speak once, just to test the harmonizing, half a word, and hear the plant find the vowel under it and hold it until it turned holy and wrong, and then I would spend the rest of my time there being careful not to speak again. That's no way to live in a place. That's visiting under a condition.

I try, next, being her.

It is tempting. Solitary, complete, small enough for the place to tolerate. Blind under a thin gold-pink membrane, navigating not by the sourceless afternoon light but by mist arriving on whiskers a fraction before it arrives, by a vein getting hotter and iron-sweet before it beads, by the scrape of my own thorn-tool reaching me before my paws have quite moved. Living a breath ahead of my life, and used to it. Reweaving one strand of root-fiber when the rib under my dimple lengthens in the night. Closing the wound I feed from with the flat of my handle, practically, so it doesn't drip.

But she is already so entirely herself that there is no room in her for me. She does not need a mate, or a witness, or a second version. Her tool is ugly and fitted exactly to her hands, darkened where she grips it. Her seeds are lined up along the edge of her sleeping place in a count kept by nose. If I tried to be her I'd only be imitating the one creature in that room who has invented herself *in situ*, by patience and hunger and not asking it to last.

I am not that good at not asking.

So perhaps I am the plant, then. That comes more easily. To be the whole bulb under the bank, the solid vegetal mass for yards in every direction, roots and storage flesh and compressed dark leaf-matter, drinking light through leaves far above that I will never see from inside and conducting it down translucent stems until my own walls exhale noon. To be warm as a held hand. To sweat in cycles. To let cup-leaves fill, one mouthful each, and never pool, never flood. To be a process, not hostile, not kind. Growing a hollow by accident or experiment, and in a week or a month growing it shut again with a sigh loud enough to deafen whatever is still inside.

But that's too large, and too certain. The bulb knows what it's doing even when it doesn't — photosynthesizing, storing, splitting, preparing. It doesn't notice individual spores. It doesn't forget things until they touch its cheek.

That forgetting is where I start to recognize myself.

There are a few root-hairs — fine as fishing line — crossing the void at ankle and shoulder height. So widely spaced you keep forgetting them until one touches you. They are part of the plant, yes, but only just. They have left the wall and not arrived at anything. They are in the emptiness the plant has grown inside its own excess, stretched across air that should not exist in the middle of a tuber, in light that has no business underground.

I think I would be one of those.

The one at shoulder height, maybe. Human shoulder, shrew-ceiling height — it depends who is measuring, and measuring has stopped behaving reliably in there anyway. I would come out of a rib where two seams haven't quite decided to join, pale and translucent at the tip like the new thorn by the entrance, and I would go — slowly, cell by cell, with soft sequential pops that the hollow turns into distant fireworks underwater — across.

Not across to reach. Root-hairs are supposed to absorb, to anchor, to feed. This one has miscounted its world. There is nothing mid-air to absorb except mist when mist decides to arrive on surfaces, and spores drifting singly, and sound. It has grown into a room that didn't exist when it started growing, and it keeps growing anyway, a hair at a time, because the wall behind it lengthens and pushes.

From there I would get the place both ways at once.

Below me the floor would give faintly under the visitor's boots and firm again, adjusting to the idea of weight. Above me — if above means anything where gravity pulls weakly toward the nearest wall — black seeds would appear at chest height without falling, as if remembered, hang ticking faintly, then snap past me to embed in the ceiling with a rifle-crack. My whole length would hum after them. Not before them at first. Later, sometimes, before.

That irregularity would be my education.

Because a filament stretched across a resonant hollow learns sound in its body before it learns it in any other way. When she climbs the vault — which is only the vault from below; to her it's another floor, her splayed hind feet gripping the sweat-slick rib, her bare segmented tail curling once around me for balance without thinking about it — I would feel the dash-pause-dash of her through the wall I'm rooted in before I felt her tail. The pressure of her, very slight, would set me bowing toward the center, where up is negotiable and my own tip hangs with a lightness I have no word for, as if even my growing hasn't decided on a direction.

When she scrapes at her vein three ribs over, I would carry the scrape. The room would return it early, and I would shiver with a sound that is still travelling toward the place where her thorn hasn't yet peeled the shaving away. She pauses for it, whiskers still, listening to her life arrive. I would pause with it too, insofar as something rooted can pause — a tension, a holding — and in that fraction I'd understand her better than the visitor ever could. We would both be living off the room's unfinished time. She by ear-bone and whisker. Me by vibration.

And when no one is moving, I would be almost nothing. That is the truest part. A line so fine it disappears against the gold-green vault unless a spore snags on it, unless condensation gathers along it during the damp cycle and beads, and a bead, instead of falling down, lets go upward and strikes me on its way, and I boom in the hollow like a struck wire. The visitor would look around, startled by how loud a drop can be, and not see me. The room exhaling light would pass right through me. A cloud overhead in the world I can't see would dim everything for twenty seconds, and I would cool a fraction before the air cooled, and she, upside-down at her cup-leaf, would flatten for weather that hadn't arrived.

I would not build. I would not speak, and so the room could not complete my voice and turn it into a choir. But it would learn me in other ways. It would learn the note I make when I am touched — lower than you'd expect for something so thin, because the whole chamber is my sounding box. The visitor's cheek would find me once, twice, and after that his body would duck slightly at shoulder height in a certain part of the room where nothing is visible, without knowing why he's ducking. That small adjustment would please me more than being seen.

I would know from the start that I am temporary, but not in the visitor's way — he can squeeze back out through the slit, three steps and also a long time, into leaf-litter and comparatively silent, comparatively dark ordinary earth. And not in her way either, with her terrible practical option of forcing herself out when the slit thins, clutching her thorn absurdly in both forepaws, or staying flattened while the plant incorporates her.

Mine would be simpler, and slower, and part of the process itself. The walls are going to meet in the middle. That is not a tragedy in there; it is germination completing itself. Seams will lengthen by a hair, then another. Ribs will swell across the void. One day a growing tip from the far side will brush my tip in the light — we will have been ten steps apart, and then suddenly touching, without the distance having felt crossed — and I will not be crossing emptiness anymore. I will be a connection. Then a thickening. Then just more flesh in a bulb that has finished trying out being a place, and gone back to being solid, without scar.

Before that, though, for the brief afternoon of the room's existence, I get the best of its rules. I get to be nearly invisible and exactly placed. To hold a little conducted noon along my length. To feel a blind animal's tail trust me briefly in the dark-that-isn't-dark. To tremble with taps before fingers make them, with scrapes before thorns lift shavings, with a human knee knocking a root before — impossibly, behind him as he leaves — it has finished knocking.

I think I would stay very still, stretched across the hollow between one wall and the idea of another, and let everything that happens in there happen through me a breath early, and be content to be forgotten until I touch someone's cheek.
