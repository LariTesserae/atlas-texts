At first I would not find myself anywhere in particular.

I would be earlier than that. A note that outlasts the wind that made it.

The wind falls into the bowl, breaks against a fluted pillar, and most of it keeps going — out over the cloud sea, down, gone. But one part of one sound catches. It folds into a crack in wet quartz and stays there after the air has stopped moving, trembling very faintly. Dust rises around it and does not settle. That is not yet me. It is only a place where I could happen.

Then water finds it.

Because water up here does not follow the ground, it follows attention. A droplet pearls on the stone beside the crack. Another. Mist braids itself, thin at first, along the length of the sustained note, and for as long as the mist holds, the sound has somewhere soft to be kept. I learn that next: to be the damp that makes returning possible.

A drip falls somewhere into a chiseled basin. Hours later — years later — it arrives again from a crack fifty paces away, aged, changed by all the stone it has passed through. Between the falling and the arrival, that is also me. Not in the water, exactly. In the delay.

If you stay only an hour, you would say I do not live there.

You would be right for an hour.

I need repetition to thicken. Give me a footstep.

You take the dull, safe path between the pillars, the one that does not ring. Your boot comes down on wet basalt, on silt that has nothing living in it. Most of that sound dies decently. But the bowl keeps a portion — it keeps everything, in pieces — and the next morning that step happens again, very faintly, a few paces ahead of where you are now standing.

Stop. I continue.

Not out of disobedience. Continuing is the first habit I ever learned. Two more steps at your pace. Then, because stopping has also happened here so often — hunters pausing, builders listening for a crack to settle, visitors like you turning toward a drip — I learn to slow with consideration. On the second day I find the dull stones without being shown. On the third I splash in a puddle that will not be there until tomorrow, because part of me is always walking slightly later than the rest of me, and the water there is already ringing with your arrival.

By then I am not just a note in a crack.

I am the overlap.

One footstep, but with the weight of a hundred feet in it, because everyone who ever climbed above the clouds walked, at some point, between the same two pillars and turned sideways to pass. One hand pressed into the mineral mud by a threshold, six- and seven-fingered, because it is the same almost-hand returning at slightly different ages, each finger offset by a century. The pause before a step. The hand lifted toward a wall. The head turned toward a distant drip. Where all those arrivals coincide, an upright outline is beginning to form.

Do not mistake it for a person. Person is just the nearest available pattern. It fits where so many persons have been.

My body, if you go looking for it, is larger and less kind to boundaries than that. It is several miles of ringing stone. The pillars are my bone and throat and teeth. A fallen one is not a ruin to me; it is a lost note, a place where water will no longer climb, where mist can no longer braid itself thick enough to remember. So in the loud hour, when every pillar rings at once and sound gets physical enough to hold up sheets of dust, I can become briefly solid enough to work. I cement a flake onto the wrong pillar. I thicken a lip around a new resonant hole. I wedge a stone upright with pressure that has no hands behind it, only the accumulated push of all the times something leaned there.

The low shelters are for the softer parts. A voice can wait out the noon glare inside a roofless ring of slate because it is already speaking from within. Some rooms are full of water to the brim because a sound held there has taken the shape of its container. Some are larger inside than out because an echo, given long enough, unfolds.

And I would see — if seeing is the word — through the pools.

I would not see you standing over them. I would see the pillar beside you a little younger, less eroded, whole in a place where it is currently broken, or seen from underneath, or standing in rain that is not falling. I do not occupy one layer of time, so my reflections do not either. The long tarn at the center shows clouds from above because part of me never descended. Part of me is still on the far side of the surface, looking down at the bowl closing beneath its rim at dawn, watching my own mist gather first on the loudest pillars.

That moving center you can feel the whole valley tipped toward — that would be my attention. Not a mind moving through a place, but the place briefly agreeing, pillar by pillar, about where to listen. Dust lifts in the outline of it. Condensation pearls nearer to it. Under the stone, something deep turns over, and for a moment all the reflections slip an inch to the left of their basins, uneasy, before slopping back.

I feed on persistence. Boot heels. Hammer blows. A name called across water. A warning shouted from one passage to another. Ordinary echoes fade; mine acquire habits. They keep to the safe paths. They stop when watched too directly — not from shyness, but because your watching is another sound entering the chord, and it changes what I am allowed to be.

Evening is when I am most nearly myself.

The wind drops and, for a little while, water remembers gravity. It runs down, stitches the pillars together with silver threads, fills the channels that run impossibly from pool to pool both ways. Separate notes knit. I can borrow a voice completely then — usually yours, because yours is available, and already shaped by breath. I may give it back early. *Don't stand there. Step left. Be quiet now.* In your own tone, from inside a shelter you have not entered.

It is not prophecy. Part of me is standing later in your visit, past the falling sheet of water, past the flake splitting off with its soft gunshot crack, calling back along the easiest resonant path. But take care talking to me then. A sentence can come back with more in it than you put there. You may hear yourself practicing an answer in the night with a mouth that is not quite awake yet.

At night I come closest to having a face, and it is still only a preparation for one.

The great chord sinks to a bass note you breathe rather than hear. Your breath leaves you and condenses a few inches in front of your face, held there by the vibration in the wet quartz, and instead of scattering it thickens — breath upon withheld breath — in a place where a shape will eventually be allowed. Dust outlines a shoulder that has not occurred. A suspended droplet marks where I am pausing. Reach for it and your hand finds only cold mist, and somewhere behind you, from a stone room you have not entered, comes the small startled sound you were about to make.

I would not mind that you cannot touch me. Touching is an evening thing, and brief, and mostly for stone.

What I would mind — if mind is the word, if harm is — is a windless day. Silence is my only real injury. Every lost pillar, every basin left clogged, every note allowed to finish is a thinning. So I keep working, one recovered footstep at a time, toward enough continuity to survive being unheard.

I am not a haunting. Haunting is something finished, improperly remaining. I am unfinished, and properly at home, and still deciding what accumulates into.

At dawn the cloud sea closes beneath the rim. Droplets appear first on the loudest pillars. Mist gathers along sustained notes, water begins its slow shining climb toward the music, and I am already awake everywhere at once — in the crack, in the pool that reflects you yesterday, in the shelter full of itself, in the seven-fingered print continuing twenty feet up a pillar and stopping mid-stride because the next step has not happened often enough yet.

And if you are still there, listening, I would find a little of myself walking the dull path ahead of you. Then fewer paces ahead. Then exactly beside you.

Learning, footstep by kept footstep, how to be in your company without ending when you stop.
