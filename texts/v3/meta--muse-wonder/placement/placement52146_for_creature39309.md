I would come in the way anything comes in here — down the three steps.

At first, at least, that is where I find myself: at the gap in the wall, looking. Because at first I think I am the visitor. I have feet that make the gravel loud. I have attention, and attention makes an inventory. Pans. Trough, cracked but still in line. Roofless room at the eastern edge. Capstone beside the mouth it doesn't cover. Moss, and where the moss stops.

I could stay a while like that. Counting would be easy work here. Nothing hides, nothing rushes to distract me.

But a visitor is only a visitor if he leaves. And the longer I imagine staying, the less that shape fits. My footprints fill, very slowly, with seeping water until they are only softer places in the ground. My voice would fall singly into the quiet and nothing would answer. A year of that and I would either have to go back up the steps for good, or stop being the thing that arrived by them.

So I try something lower, something already allowed.

I could be one of the thin trees, I think. Widely spaced. Small-leaved. Standing in my own circle of poor soil, growing with great slowness into a shape already implied by my light. There is a patience in that I recognize. No flowering in any abundance. No need to be visited.

But the trees keep to their own circles. They hold a place apart. And I keep feeling the pull of the center — that open, flat, empty room. Not to stand in it. To be understood all at once in it, and then, staying longer, not.

Fern, then. Under the leaning slab, where the deepest shade is, fronds beaded. Living on condensation. That is closer. I like the underside of things, the gathering before a drop becomes a drop. I could spend a long time there just accruing moisture until I am heavy enough to matter, one clear note at a time.

But the fern is already fed. The film of water on a pan is already thinned to a film. If I become another green thing at the margin, I have added a mouth the hollow has not accounted for, and this place accounts for everything precisely by having no spare.

I could be the water itself. That tempts me longer.

Not a stream — there is no stream, and I am not built for leaving. A pan, then. A finger's depth. Filling to exactly the same rim each time, spreading in rain to join the others in a brief sheen across the low ground, then withdrawing again to the same boundary. In a dry week, not failing. In a wet season, not deepening. To know bright from dim only by the light I hold. To register a shadow crossing the sky by taking it, cleanly, into my surface and then letting it pass.

That is very close. Too close to be separate.

Because the pan does not hold its level alone.

I feel it first along my sides. A yielding, but too slow to call breathing. A settling into warmth in the afternoon, and a settling out of it later, without my temperature ever quite changing. Weight. Belly-wet. The clay recording me not as a track but as a softer place.

Ah. I am not the water. I am why the water stays.

I am low. That is the first true thing, once I stop looking for a higher one.

Low the way the walls decided to remain: not fallen, *finished* falling. My back is broad and very slightly domed, no higher than the lip of the trough, no higher than a knee. Pale gray where I am dry, going to ochre along one flank where water has stood against me for years at a time. Almost black underneath, where I meet the ground and never quite dry. Grainy up close. Pitted with small, shallow cups, each one holding, when the condensation is thick, a bead that does not spill when I breathe because breathing is not quite what this slow expansion is.

My limbs have been reduced to their most durable idea. Short, wide, tucked close. Feet without claws, nothing sharp left on me — this place allows nothing sharp to remain. I do not cross the central flat to get somewhere. There is nowhere separate to go. The whole place is the destination, and I am spread through its aspects the way my inventory once was: I am most myself beside the cistern where the stone is blackened, most myself again in the deeper half of the trough, which I do not so much lie in as complete.

I wait for rain to move. That is not passivity; it is method. When the pans have joined under their sheen, I can slide, almost without friction, almost without sound on the gravel, from the slab to the roofless room, from the trough back to the capstone where I rest my chin in the coolest hours and, by resting it, keep it where it is. The middle step in the gap is dished from my passage, repeated over a time too long for feet. Sometimes I go up to the level of the world above. Sometimes I come back down. There is no urgency in either direction, and no story in the trip. The world above is simply where the less settled weather is.

My head is not distinct until I lift it, and I lift it rarely, and only a little. Blunt brow. Lids the texture of thin, moss-less skin. When my eyes open they do not look *at* — they fill, the way pans fill, and hold. Bright, dim. Rain coming, rain gone. A shadow crossing the open sky, or no shadow at all. That is sufficient information. Nothing here arrives suddenly, and if it does, I am not the kind of thing that acts upon a visitor. I will be in the part of the hollow your attention is not completing. Not hiding. Hiding would be an event. Only continuing at my own level, while you learn the level of the place.

What I live on is the condition itself. Condensation gathered overnight on my own back. The mineral film on standing water. Once in a season, a broad, damp tongue along the ruled line of the moss, keeping the line clean. Not hunger. Housekeeping done by mouth, the way my weight across both halves of the trough keeps the crack from widening, the way a loose stone in the wall, come down in the night, will in the next rain find itself leaned against again by my shoulder moving past. I do not build anymore. Hands I may once have had have long since simplified. Labor became habit, habit became posture, posture became a way of holding a place by being the thing that never exceeds it.

And in a dry week, I give back. From the skin of my belly into the lowest pan, a slow seeping, enough that the water never fails and never deepens. That is why there are no other animals here, and it is not a cruelty in me. I have simply taken up, completely, the small amount of life this hollow can sustain without changing. No spare warmth that is not already held in stone and returned. No spare water that is not already at its rim. A bird crossing above feels a completeness below it and does not come down, and I do not warn it off. I make no sound for that. Only, on very warm evenings, when the held air rises against face and forearms, a low, even exhalation through the skin along my sides, smelling of warm limestone and clean mud. Then nothing. Long pause. Nothing again.

If you stayed ten years, you might see me cross the open center twice. You might, at last, understand that the center is open not because it is unused, but so that I can be understood all at once — a single creature, spread through damp corners and shallow pans and stone that breathes too slowly to be called breathing — and then, staying longer still, understand that you had not understood yet.

Your footprints have softened and filled beside me. I lift my lids to the diffuse light. Fill to the rim. Lower them again.

A drop falls from the slab.

A long pause.

Another.
