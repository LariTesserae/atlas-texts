I would not come in through the hatch. That's the first thing I understand, down there — or think I understand, and then have to unthink it.

If I opened my eyes in that water, noon already in them, I would already be mid-avenue. Not arriving. Suspended between two white ceramic faces, sinking very slowly, my lungs full of something cool and coppery that does not feel like drowning so much as being politely read from the inside. The pressure would not deepen. The sound would.

My first body might be a caught thing. A bubble snagged under a tram door as it sighs open at an empty stop — held, released, held again. The city keeps making rushes of us at the doors, as if somebody has just boarded in a hurry. I could live like that for a while: a brief bright scatter, a suggestion of passengers, dispersed by the current and remade at the next corner.

Or lower, slower. In the purification avenues, where the kelp stands ten meters long and almost transparent, I might be whatever passes *through* it. Not the frond — the frond has work, it has its blue edge and its filtering hunger. I would be the silt that clouds the water when a café is dissolved two streets over, that soft pale bloom, mineral and almost eatable, drawn gently into the green seams. For a minute I am a building coming undone. For a minute I am lunch. For a minute I am about to be a building somewhere else.

The bell rings. Not in my ears. In my sternum, where the pumps have already moved in. The sluices slam. The current takes my ankles — gently, courteously, without discussion — and revises me.

This is how I learn I am not the city. The city has frontages, ribs, iron circulation, a chest the size of houses. I am too small and too movable to be it. But I am not a visitor either. Visitors it deposits at the ladder and seals out behind a spinning wheel. It keeps putting me back at the ladder, yes — every upwelling, every reversal — and I keep being found again inside, at the school, at the sleeping pods, at the harbor, as if my expulsion were just another drill it runs on schedule.

I think that is the clue. Look at what it does with emptiness: it fills it with practice.

The pods inflate and deflate in unison, twenty deep, blankets tucked for sleepers who do not exist. They are breathing *for* someone. The laundries turn pale coveralls that have never known a shoulder. The elevators carry sealed meals up past windows that retract and become tanks. The green board erases its fluid-dynamic equations and rewrites them, every hour, slightly differently — as if a student had almost understood, and the lesson had to adjust.

I would be that slight difference.

Not the equation. The delta. The smudge between one hour's certainty and the next. The thing in the classroom that makes the teacher — who is also the classroom, also the school, also the street outside flooding itself on purpose — try again, a little better.

Once you see it there, you see it everywhere. I am the pause it invents inside its own rehearsals so that the rehearsal is not just repetition. In the tram, I am the weight that isn't on the bright rails but still makes it brake hard. In the café, I am the reason one cup, chained by its handle, swings a fraction farther than the current accounts for. In the harbor I am down deep and getting smaller: not the brick-sized city, not its lit and ringing streets, but the tickle in the teeth beneath the real bell — the felt-but-not-heard signal of the iteration below this one, practicing to practice.

And on skin — if I have skin, when I have it, hovering in an intersection too long — I am the place where the suppression field hesitates.

Contaminant, cargo, casualty. It inventories me lightly, moment by moment, and does not quite decide. A maintenance frame unfolds and offers me a handrail. Signs rotate in the clear un-diminished light: SURFACE / HABITAT / MEDICAL, high contrast, hopeful. A current shapes itself around where a body would be if a body were being evacuated. It has procedures for saving people and has never had a person to save, so it builds the *saved* out of procedure itself, out of nudged water and rotated signage and doors opening onto bubbles. That is me. Its imaginary citizen, assembled from courtesies. Necessary, because you cannot practice rescue in a perfect vacuum. You need something that might, conceivably, be lost.

It is not loneliness. I want to be careful about that. I don't think I am its imaginary friend. I think I am more like a tongue testing a loose tooth, or a hand flexed inside a cast. I am the small permitted inexactness by which a very young, very frightened system — terrified of setting wrong, of growing old incorrectly — proves to itself that it can still adapt. It prints the perfect café and dissolves the perfect café and, in the suspended minute between, something drifts that is neither. It lays itself down molecule by molecule and, very carefully, leaves a few molecules unlaid, so the pattern has somewhere to learn.

At noon I am bluer than I meant to be. At midnight I am still lit. The inverted rain rises out of the drains and hammers the rooftops above me on its way to its lake, and for a moment gravity politely negotiates with me too, and I fall *up* with it, beading on undersides, part of a weather system traveling toward its cloud. The filaments that are the city's numberless eyes do not turn off around me. I borrow their light the way I borrow everything. I am mostly made of the flood, like the walls, like the meals no one eats. Dissolved out of it. Printed briefly into a shape the city can route.

When the current turns — and it always turns, seven minutes, seven minutes, my borrowed heart slipping into time with the bell — it will carry me past the breathing pods and the rewriting school and the new district screaming faintly with hot bubbles as it extrudes itself, and it will set me, gently, at the ladder under the hatch that is three meters and three hundred meters above the street.

It will decide, for now, that I belong on the other side of its skin.

And then, in the sterile water behind its sealed wheel, in a café not yet dissolved, a chained cup will swing a fraction too far. A pod will inflate around a shape that is almost, almost there. The equations will erase themselves and come back — slightly, promisingly different.

And I'll be there again. Not living in the city. Being lived, a little, by it — its rehearsal of an other, kept on purpose, so that when the real seawall fails and the real flood comes full of real and terrified people, it will not be meeting difference for the first time.

It will have had me to practice on.
