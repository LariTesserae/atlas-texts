I would not stay the huge thing on its belly in the entrance slit, breathing like weather and making the lanes empty around me. That heat would call the roots down. I would have to get smaller, and colder, and decide what to do with my hands.

At first I think I might be up high — relatively high. On the bottle-cap cistern with the harvest mouse, in the rust of the crimped crown, feeling the plaza through my feet. I like a lookout. I like being where the drip lands one bright bead at a time and doesn't overflow. But her tail thinks for itself and she is golden, and she belongs half to the world above the mat where there is still sky to be stolen from. She comes and goes. I'm not sure I'd be good at leaving that cleanly.

Not a shrew, either, though I recognize the tempo. That needle-nosed vibration, the hunger that renews before the last meal is finished, the heart running so hot that stopping is a kind of disappearing — I know what it is to patrol the tender points and insert yourself where something small and muffled is happening in a side tunnel. But the shrews lick their teeth and come back alone. That's not the work. I don't want to be tolerated for my appetite.

And not a frog, sealed in my individual cell in the floor, throat pulsing once and then a long time later again. Though some winters that has its pull. To be furniture that will, in spring, get up and leave.

I think I'd be found lower, and more in the middle of the traffic than I'd expect.

A vole.

Not the sharp, bright young one crossing the bottle-cap plaza with a seed twice her weight held high so it won't drag in the rill, though I'd do that shift and like the insulting neatness of the storehouses — sedge separate from rush separate from the fat pale corms. Not deep in the communal hollow either, banked in shredded leaf and seed-down and somebody else's stolen feather, steaming gently, waking netted in white root hairs that grew toward my warmth in the night and having to chew myself companionably free.

I'd be the one in the doorway. Any doorway. Doing the listening shift.

Back flattened by habit if not by bone, belly close enough to the mud to read its temperature, fur beading peat water and carrying the smell of sedge back into the tunnel so I smell like the place and not like an event. Eyes nearly decorative — that part comes easily: I have never navigated by sight so much as confirmed, at the last moment, what the rest of me already decided. Whiskers forward. Nose working. Ears flat. Whole skull turned into an instrument and pressed, from time to time, against a root.

Because that's the real post in the city. Not ruler. Not builder, exactly, though we're all builders here. Listener. Up through the root comes the dangerous surface weather: ice ticking as it takes weight, frost working in the mat, a fox's footfall arriving as a slow pressure and then a pause that means it has heard something too, an owl settling somewhere above where no shadow can fall down here. You don't call out. Voice travels up. You tap twice on the root, felt rather than heard, and the tap goes lane to lane, and runways empty or fill without a single animal needing to see why. I've spent my life, in one form or another, with my head against the beam, trying to tell the difference between a drip changing rhythm and the world changing its mind — and then translating it into something small enough to pass along a tunnel without panic.

And when I'm not listening, I'm gnawing. I would have to be. That's the government and I find I don't mind it being so literal: my teeth never stop growing, and left alone my own mouth would kill me, and put to work my mouth keeps the city from being strangled by its own ceiling. Scrape, pause, scrape. Root tips trimmed back from doorways. The pale curtain that grew across a runway overnight, cropped before the foraging parties need it. A support chewed to safe thickness — not thin, not thick; the judgment is the whole job. Walls re-woven where they've gone soft. The old bridge over the rill sagging under new moss, and me helping rebuild it two feet to the left, stick by stick, while traffic still crosses the sagging one, because you don't close a bridge to fix a bridge. The sound is so small and constant it becomes a silence with teeth in it, and I'd be one of the teeth.

I'd keep my margins. A scent post at the crossing — *occupied, occupied* — not as a claim so much as a courtesy that lets a room this crowded feel private. You own as much world as your whiskers can reach. The rest is tactfully someone else's. I'd drag a dead beetle backward to the fungus beds and feel virtuous about the whole municipal chain of it: my droppings feed the pale curd on the warm walls, the fungus feeds the beetles stacked breathing under the bark, the beetles feed the shrews, the rill takes the excess and brings back drowned seeds and stunned flies with the next thaw-pulse from above. Nothing wasted near the water. Latrine capped with fresh peat. Husk sorted to the midden ridge. Lanes kept ostentatiously clear, not from love of openness but because everybody needs to be able to run.

And I'd know the three exit tunnels without looking. That's the part I'd hold closest, I think. The stores bundled and built to float. The houses repairable, not permanent. When the ice loosens and the mat drops and the room fills black to the ceiling in an afternoon, I won't defend it. I'll go up, toward the hummocks that stay above water longest, carrying whatever seed is mine to carry, and let it drown correctly. I have never been good at mistaking a place for the world. But I am good at agreeing, shift after shift, body beside body in the phosphor-dark, to keep negotiating with the roots and the water and the cold at exactly the speed at which they grow over us.

So find me at dusk-that-isn't-dusk, when the wind presses the ice and the mat flexes down a fraction and every drip in the ceiling changes its count. I won't be in the center. I'll be at the edge of the communal hollow's warmth, motionless, skull against the thickest root, listening for the huge world through a hand's thickness of living peat.

Tap twice. I'm there.
