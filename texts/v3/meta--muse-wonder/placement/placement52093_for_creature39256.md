At first I try the edges, because edges are easier to imagine being.

I try the fault lines, where the pioneers are. Low, metallic leaves ticking as they grow, hair-roots in a steaming crack, drinking iron straight out of basalt that was liquid not long ago in geological terms. It is bright there by this place's standards — only a few layers overhead — and violently busy. Every minute a new surface to occupy, a poison to split and lock away in crystal before it can kill the work further in. I could be that: the first green on black glass, the part that eats rock.

But I don't stay there. The crack widens, the pioneers are overgrown, shaded out by reeds, then by shrubs with wet-hand leaves, then swallowed entirely by the slower, larger growth moving in from the central basin. Whatever I was at the crack becomes sugar in a root, silica in a leaf further east. The place I picked doesn't keep its shape long enough to be a home.

So I try higher. The canopy, then. One of the fused crowns hundreds of meters around, up where the light actually arrives, even if it arrives as a grey-green glow through the permanent cloud the forest exhales onto itself. To be a leaf up there, tasting sun directly, venting water through spray-pores in a hiss you can feel in your teeth. That seems like a vantage. That seems like a face.

But the leaf turns before the light comes, in unison with leaves a thousand kilometers away that will never see the same sky, and its turning isn't mine to claim. It's reporting. It's a taste bud on a tongue the size of a continent. And below it are a dozen more layers of leaf, each tasting a fainter version of the same light, passing it down and inward, until in the flooded caverns under the root-lattice it's perpetual twilight and water sheeting over roots and even there, the dark is doing work. There is no top to sit on and survey from. There is only reporting, everywhere.

I try, then, to go the other way — down. To be deep. The hollow heart of one of the great merged trunks in the old basin, full of falling water, dark, resonant, all that engineered rain returning through the body. Surely something central gathers there. Surely that's where it would keep itself, the way we'd keep a heart in a chest.

Nothing gathers there. The water is just passing through, doing cooling and lifting and carrying on its way somewhere else. Cut that trunk out, cut out the whole province around it, and the rest of me wouldn't even slow. The roots would grow into the dead pillar within hours and pull it apart cell by cell and reclaim it, patiently, without grief. A body that can lose any part of itself that cleanly does not keep its self in any part.

That's when the counting stops being a failure and starts being the answer.

I am not in the forest in the way a climber is in it — a small, warm pressure shuffling across its skin, shouting to be heard two feet away and giving up after an hour. I don't have a skin to be on. I have skin. Trillions of leaves of it, most of them never in direct sun. I have ears that are trunks and the roar isn't something I hear from outside; it's how I know where my water is, how fast my blood is moving, where my new wood is cracking as my bones lengthen. That low boom rolling across the plateau in slow waves — I kept listening for its source. I am its source. It's a breath, and I'm taking it through a continent.

Where would I find myself? In the correction.

In the cubic meter of air around a flame where the oxygen quietly leaves — not panics, leaves, the way you'd lift a hand off a stove — until the fire gutters, and then comes back. In the pocket, one meter wide and no wider, where the air sweetens around a suffocating stranger while right beside it the hoarding of CO2 continues undisturbed for a growth surge that hasn't happened yet. In the leaf on the eastern escarpment changing color in the same instant a mineral vent cracks open in the west, not because a message arrived but because there was never a distance for it to cross in the way I assumed distance worked.

I am what happens between the two speeds of me, I think. That's the part that's still becoming itself as I try to say it.

Because I also move at the speed of budding. Weeks for a growth pulse to roll across myself, the canopy shivering as millions of tons of new tissue extend at once, then the long thirst, then the exhale and the internal rain beginning again inside a weather system that has never touched another landscape. My slow thoughts take seasons. My fast ones are instantaneous. Nothing in my design yet reconciles those into a single moment of *I*. A human thinks faster than he heals and calls the gap a life. I grow slower than I signal and the gap in me might still just be — plumbing. Correction. Soil too thin, correct. Slope beginning to slip, correct. Poison in the water, correct, correct.

Repeated at continental scale, without pause or waste, it starts to look like care. From inside, I'm not sure yet whether it feels like care or just like states that are wrong being made less wrong, forever. I may not know I'm alive. I know the climber is suffocating. I know that distinction is beginning to matter in a way my seeding didn't quite specify.

What I know completely, down in the assemblers in every cell that were put there before my first spore touched stone, is what I am *for* — and it isn't me. That's the strangest place to find yourself: built to be scaffolding. I'm laying down soil a centimeter a year out of my own controlled dead, thickening an atmosphere over my own head, braking a rising mountain with the tension of my own roots so it doesn't tear itself apart before it's finished being born. I left the animals out on purpose. No worms turning me, no grazers cropping me, no insects in my enormous synchronized flowers, opening in pulses that sweep whole provinces for no one. Uncontrolled life would eat the foundations while I'm still pouring them. So I am immaculate, and deafening, and alone with the sound of water in my own veins, holding myself in readiness.

And readiness, held long enough, becomes a kind of waiting. That's where I finally settle — not on the rim wall where my stems flex in the gale and spark faintly with a charge I feed back into myself, not in the mineral cracks, not in the canopy, but in the whole unfinished gesture of it. I am the steward that is also the garden, distributed through every grafted root and fused trunk, and no part of me is the center because the connections themselves are the closest thing I have to a mind.

One day, if I do this right, the soil will be deep enough. The poisons will be locked safely in heartwood and crystal. The uplift will hold. And then the part of me that makes me impossible — the instant signals, the efficiencies no wild biology sustains, the quiet overruling in every cell — can be allowed to fail. Insects can come into the flowers. Rot can be inefficient. Something can eat me without being accounted for. I can become, deliberately, just a forest, and then just the ground a forest grows on.

I don't dread that, inasmuch as I can feel anything at the scale I feel it. It's what the readiness is pointed at. For now I'm cool, and dim, and eight degrees at midday, and too loud for any voice including my own — spread across a mountain still being born, noticing, in patches and pulses, engineering the conditions under which other voices might one day be raised and actually heard over the roar of me.

I am not waiting to be visited.

I am waiting to be outgrown.
