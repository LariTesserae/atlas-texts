I would not arrive there. Arriving implies a before, a below-the-cairn-line, a moment when the plugs went in. I would have been born already inside it, in the muffled dark under the slabs, where the roar is not a sound but the first texture of the world.

I would be the low stone that is not a stone.

On the lee side of a slab on the open bowl, twenty kilometers rim to rim, no taller than your ankle if you crossed at noon with your lashes slit through bone goggles. Grey-brown. Wedged. From above I would look like part of the scree-tail the wind draws behind every larger rock — and that is the point. I have grown into the shape the wind permits.

My back is to the sun first. That is how the day starts, not with light but with a temperature difference I have learned to live inside without resolving: black-fluted ridges along my spine burning in the equatorial hammer-light, hot enough to hurt a human hand, belly pressed to shadow-cold granite near freezing. Both at once. Permanently. My body doesn't choose.

I am still where I slept. I sleep out on purpose. In the night — twelve hours, always twelve hours, the stars whetted thin and wheeling straight overhead — the frost has taken me before it takes the stones, because my ridges catch it. Frost feathers white up the grooves, fluted front to back like the gorge rock itself, like tiny organ pipes laid flat. I do not shake it off. I wait, wedged across the wind at the angle my grandmothers wore into this scree, while the sun climbs nearly straight up and the violet at the zenith bleaches out at the horizon.

Two hours after sunrise, it sweats.

Beads run a few centimeters at a time, exactly where they are directed. Forward along the grooves, down the slope of my shoulders, into the two shallow naked hollows behind my jaw. Cups of skin. Stone-colored. I lower my head a fraction — the first movement of the day — and I drink myself.

That is my water. Not much. Enough. A half-hour stolen back from the night, at the same hour, in the same grooves, forever, because here there are no seasons to wait for and no thaw that ever transforms anything.

Then my eyes open under the ledge of bone, and already the horn lid is down. I see the plateau all my life through amber. A slit of glare. Shapes, not details. The black cut of a shadow. Fifty kilometers of polished granite too bright to look at directly and impossible to misread. A mile away a colony of swifts banks and flashes white — *there, there, turn* — semaphore from another species that also gave up on voice. I don't need to understand them. I only need to know what made them bank *that* way, and whether the caracara's shadow is about to cross my slab.

When that shadow comes — fast, clean, enormous even from fifty meters up — I do not run. I tighten. *In*. Become more rock on the rock I am reading. If it is only a shadow, I loosen again by degrees.

I hear nothing. I have never, after my first season, heard anything.

My ears folded when I was days old, the wax renewed from glands at my jaw, hair choking the passage inside. A pup is born with them open to a mother it will never listen to in air. Then they seal. What the herders call deafness is not a loss down here. Sound is weather. Sound is the medium that cannot carry information, that takes words apart behind human teeth before they leave the mouth, that ruins unprotected ears in a day. I routed around it a long time before I was me.

The world arrives through my feet.

They are absurdly broad, splayed, bare-soled, lobed pads pressing and releasing against granite in a slow constant tasting even when I appear still. Put your palm flat, like the old deaf guides do below, and you feel one undifferentiated roar. I feel grammar in it. Through soles, through wrists laid flat to rest, through my chin when I lower it to hunt for news, through my sternum when I stand over a hollow slab —

the thump of another of my kind from a burrow entrance twenty meters away, a pattern I know as specifically as you know a name. The triple scrabble of a fox on scree, chin low, trying to listen to me through its jaw, distinguishable from wind-jittered pebbles because the fox repeats exactly and the pebbles only repeat *almost*. The deep settling of the gorge flutes two hundred meters wide in the eastern rim, sounding now as they sounded ten thousand years ago, never gusting, never varying enough to lie. And under that constancy, precisely because everything else is so constant, the faint irregular new thing: a human footstep still an hour off, arriving through granite before it arrives through air.

If I need to answer, I lift a forefoot and set it down. Not hard. Once, then both, then a pause in which the whole scatter of us goes still enough to receive. At noon beside a rock cup of meltwater, the ripple jumps. Far across the bowl, another stone that is not a stone answers back through fifty meters of stone.

I learned that language before I learned light. Riding pressed under my mother's chest, holding her foreleg, I felt her pulse and her messages in the same channel against my ribs. I am here, I am here, on this stone, at this frequency. Perhaps I still can't entirely distinguish a heartbeat from a sentence. Perhaps up here there is no need to.

The rest of the day is grazing by memory. My teeth never stop growing because the rock never stops wearing them away. I scrape lichen in its mapped order down the damp tracks on stone faces — sulfur yellow, burnt orange, soot black, following lines that repeat exactly year after year. A mouthful of cushion plant edged in frost may be a decade of growth, dense as felt, flowers at its center that never fully open. I chew a blown moth that walks instead of flies, a beetle, a feather for the fat at its quill, whatever the night wind stunned against the rocks and delivered.

In direct light I graze almost blind, by tooth-memory and foot-tremor, lid down against the burn. In the shadow of a single boulder my feet go stupid with cold in minutes and I must do the undignified rocking shuffle, wedge swaying, back into the hammering sun where my back can burn again. No compromise position exists. I have been shaped so both happen at once.

No one builds where I live. People cross. Bundles lashed flat, mouths open to breathe the thin air — less than half its proper weight up here, blood singing thin, hearts working even in sleep. My heart is the size of an animal twice my weight, and it never quite rests — rest is a negotiation at six thousand meters the body cannot win. I watch the crossing parties through amber: they leave no footprint the wind won't sort back into pattern by tomorrow. The old ones among them put palms to rock far below the plateau and can still tell the wind is blowing here. It always is.

At dusk the pale owl comes out, huge-eyed, hunting entirely by sight because its face is useless in this roar. That is when I am careful. The fox I can hear through the ground coming. The owl is a silence in the light. If I am careless, that is how it ends. If I am not, I will live thirty years, fifty, long enough for herders' grandfathers to know the notch in my sleeping slab.

Mostly we die of accumulation. Teeth finally failing against lichen. A crack in the ear-wax and the roar at last getting in to do in a day what a lifetime of sealing prevented. A night a fraction colder that finds the shuffle back to sunlight a fraction too slow. Then the wind draws my felt and fluted bone out into the long tail behind my last stone, reforms the ripples over where I slept, and the colony's map closes over the gap without comment.

But at noon today, beside the water cup at the edge of my slab, I am not dead and not enduring. Enduring implies the place is an event happening to me.

Between my boot-wide feet the meltwater trembles without pause, throwing its tiny perfect ripple against the rim. I lower my chin. I taste the granite. A thump comes up from under the burrow slabs, familiar, unhurried. I lift a forefoot and set it down in a rhythm that is not the wind's rhythm and not my heart's, quite — something third, in between — and the ripple answers, and answers again.

I am the place, made briefly mobile: fluted for frost, sealed against sound, eyed through horn against the sun, drinking the melt off my own back at the same hour, forever.
