I would not come up the ladder.

That is the first thing I know. I would not belong to the thin air and the scree and the work that can be done with hands. I would not be one of the keepers, holding my breath under the lintel, counting taps against the wall, rising again before the warmth could teach my body anything dangerous.

At first, swimming — because I still imagine myself swimming — I think I would be a fish.

It is the obvious grace in the place. Hand-long, pearl-skinned, drifting in the middle water at the level of a face. No hurry taught. No fear earned. Eyes still present underneath, useless and unmourned, while the whiskers go ahead tasting pressure, warmth, mineral, the faint chemistry of something that passed an hour ago. To turn aside at the last moment, unhurried. To swim inverted along the vault and graze the ceiling as if up had quietly resigned.

But that is still too much traveling. The fish know the whole Cistern in their bodies. They cross from cell to cell. They meet things. They are met. In a place where the light dies an arm's length down, they are still, somehow, the visible nation — and I don't think I would stay visible even that long.

The shrimp tempt me next, because they have solved visibility by rehearsing disappearance. To live in a niche, in stiller water, a density in shadow, a salt-grain thickening against stone. To let a hand pass and become, all at once, not there — hundreds of us flicking backward into cracks too narrow for consequence — and then, after long stillness, to reassemble. To be reclassified as furniture and resume.

But there is too much event in them. A flick is a decision made at speed. I can feel myself going slower than that as I stay.

The mussels are closer. Cemented in overlapping thousands into the lower rings, opening and closing on a timescale you have to learn to perceive. A mussel shutting is not an event, it is a decision the whole wall makes over an afternoon. I could be that: a shingle among shingles, filtering what the pipes rumor through, letting the silt settle over me, becoming indistinguishable from the stone's own slow intentions.

Only, lower still, in the seams between the mussels, under my imagined palm as it learns not to swim but to rest — there is the gravel that isn't gravel.

The snails.

That is where I settle, finally, because settling is what they have perfected past the point where it looks like a choice. I would be one of the tens of thousands. Amber, translucent, small enough that a keeper's woven net passes over me as weather passes over a peak: noted, not addressed. My territory no wider than your palm. I would not leave it. I would not need to leave it. Everything I require arrives at the speed I eat.

My world would be a smoothness within a smoothness.

Under my mouth, a mineral film lays itself down out of the seeping mortar — limestone grey, a breath of iron — and I graze it, and in grazing I polish it, and in polishing I leave a meander so fine you could feel it with your fingertips and never see it. That would be my whole autobiography, written and erased simultaneously at the speed of appetite. I would live longer than the keeper whose grandfather dropped my ancestor's ancestor here on a tray of pounded grain. Keepers would come and go above me with their wooden tongs, lifting out the floating dead, tapping *halt, rise, turn back* against a wall I experience only as a deep pressure through stone. I would not know their faces.

I might be grazing a face.

That is the likeliest place, statistically and poetically the same thing here: one of the low statues along the lower wall, reduced by water and time to an egg-smooth witness — a hand intended here, a mouth there, the rest taken. I would be on the cheek of it, or across where a name was braided into the stone in long shallow letters. Not vandalizing it. Not honoring it. Eating the thin greenless film that grows over the carving, and taking, incidentally, a molecule of the carving with each pass, and another, and another, across a life spent inside a palm's width of stone.

Face by face, letter by letter, this is how the makers were forgotten. I would be one tooth in that forgetting.

Around me the Cistern would proceed at its true pace. Above me — if above means anything — fish turn aside from nothing I can perceive. Shrimp empty a niche and, over an hour, refill it. The sponges around the pipe mouths hold their soft fingered hands into a current too slow to feel, and shed a slow white snow that settles and is eaten and becomes, eventually, part of the film under my mouth. Twice a year a wooden tray would sink somewhere in the outer rings, slow so as not to cloud the water, and over days the chemistry of grain and dried meat would reach even my wall, diluted to a rumor, and I would graze a little richer for a season. In summer, insects I will never see blunder in through the roof vents and drown, and some part of them, dissolved and settled and filtered and excreted by a dozen intermediate bodies, will find its way down to the statue's cheek.

In winter the outer ladder rungs split with cold. In here, blood heat. The water I move through — I move so slowly the phrase is generous — fell as rain before I hatched, and will still be here, barely turned, when I am a clean empty shell beaded along the overflow channel with the husks of my neighbors.

I would never enter the deep cell. I would never need to imagine it. But sometimes, through the partition, through the stone itself, there would come a displacement — something larger than a hand-long fish turning in the dark on the other side — and the water against my shell would thicken for a moment with its passing, and my whole body would register: *something old is still growing in there.* And then the moment would dilute. And I would resume.

Not hostile. Not reverent. Not waiting for anything.

Just here, on the cheek of a forgotten god or a forgotten engineer — in the Cistern, it has long since become impossible, and unnecessary, to tell which — doing the minute, polished work of eating the world as it arrives, and leaving behind me a path smoother than memory, in warm dark water, above the clouds.
