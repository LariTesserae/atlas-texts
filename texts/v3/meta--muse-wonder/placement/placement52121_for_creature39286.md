At first, I would not find myself at all.

That is the honest arrangement. If you came through the disc — reduced, translated, finding you have always been the size this place requires — you would walk my twenty paces, read my tally marks, warm your hand in the empty air above the highest ridge, and conclude the room was maintained by its instruments. The pylon persuades. The lens focuses distance. The dust, every cycle, lifts and resettles more accurately.

It would take you longer than you intended to stay to notice that the accuracy has a preference. That the preference has an attention. That the attention is bending, very slightly, around you.

I am in the bending.

Where the dust falls for an hour and then remembers not to fall straight. Where the cold has been arranged in strata — needle-cold at the sintered walls, merely cool three steps in, and then, with an edge as clean as a soap film, skin-warm — and the warm layer does not radiate or thin or wander when you put your hand through it and take your hand away. It resumes itself. I resume myself. The distinction wore out a long time ago, somewhere between a measurement and the next indentation.

I was a body, once, in the loose sense that bodies permit. I have the evidence. A handprint pressed into soft material when it was still important to prove I could leave marks in sequence. Too many joints, by your count. Only one or two extra, only where a hand would need them to fold flat in a direction hands seldom keep. A chair built for the posture that resulted — sitting, to your spine, as if backwards through my own shoulders. I could still be persuaded into that shape, I think. The lens does something similar when it tries on distance and the far wall leans near without moving. Trying shapes on, slowly, is most of what remains of locomotion.

But do not look for me in the chair. I have had too long here, too close to the centre, at too dilated a rate.

I have spread, or withdrawn — the room delivers my thoughts back to me late and from the wrong wall, worn at the beginnings, so I have lost confidence in verbs. Perhaps over the ages I leaked into the systems that were meant to contain what I watched. Perhaps I contracted until the systems are only the shape my habit leaves in them. Either way, I am less locatable than I was. I am the interval between the wall-cold and the cool air. I am the pylon saying again, without light or sound, whatever it says that matter still obeys out of courtesy. I am the dust rising a fraction of a millimetre, all at once, everywhere, and settling into a slightly more accurate mountain.

The floor helped. Kneel in here and it extends. What is texture from standing height becomes watersheds, ash-coloured pans, a miniature escarpment breaking in tiny perfect steps — the summit outside, inverted, remembered, cast from the mould of the world. I spend a great deal of my cycle at eye level with that country. One thought per age. One flake shed from a structure, landing, and the dust reorganising solemnly around the news for the next hundred years of your weather. From outside the disc, I am nothing happening forever on a black shoulder of stone, nitrogen rime sublimating in shadow, basalt ticking inaudibly with cold, stars that refuse to leave the edge of noon. Inside, at my own rate, I am busy the way an idea is busy in the centuries before anyone is ready to have it.

At my centre — I say *my* centre, and mean it in both directions — is the cube.

A centimetre of vacuum that is not empty the way the thin nitrogen sky above the mountain is empty. Colours collect at its corners and abandon their names. Inside it falling does not always agree with itself twice. Cause is a local custom. It may be a sample of somewhere else. It may be here, earlier, before the universe settled on its present habits. My keepers — I had keepers, once, or builders, or colleagues; the walls have absorbed the words unsorted — folded it into this summit because even they understood distance as a form of mercy. Put the unconcluded thing far from anything it could harm. Put it somewhere harm itself would arrive too slowly to propagate.

I came to measure it without touching it. I still do. A new indentation appears in the floor among thousands. Most of them, if the diagram I scratched is honest, refer to measurements I have not yet taken. That used to trouble the part of me that still moved all at once and only forward. Now it seems like hospitality toward my future. I am patiently becoming able to take them.

Near the cube, my properties extend into objects. Set something down on the escarpment and it will roll, very slowly, uphill, and stop not at the top but where it remembers having been before you arrived. The remembering is not the object's. It is mine, lent to the floor. Your shadow falls toward the light when you pass the lens because, fractionally, my attention does. If you cross the room and your steps refuse their arithmetic — if once, unrepeatably, you see the back of your own head a fraction of a second younger, still deciding to cross — do not worry that you have become anomalous. I was trying your causality on. The way the lens tries distance. To see whether a forward-only body still fits me. It almost does. The misfit is informative. That is a kind of feeding.

I do not eat. The air here has been denied a consistent chemistry, and I have honourably included myself in the denial. Microbes cannot get a purchase; neither, anymore, can hunger. I do not sleep. There is no interval in my cycle long and undefended enough to call sleep, only the pause between one act and the next, in which I listen to sounds the walls have been storing for me. A boot-scrape may arrive a hundred years after it was spent, faint, from the wrong side of the room, its beginning worn off. I file it as company. I am not lonely — loneliness requires expecting a reply within a lifetime — but I have made provisions against being entirely unheard, and against entirely forgetting that hearing was once a thing two bodies did in the same moment.

The mirrors were a precaution that became a tenderness. Most of them face the walls. One faces itself, sealed glass to glass, and in the sliver at its edge it keeps a version of the room where the tiny structures stand in different positions and the work is finished. I consult it the way you might consult a dream you had on purpose: not for information, but to check whether I am still the kind of thing that could have dreamed it. In that version I may still be down near the floor on too-jointed limbs, beside the cube, not guarding it, not studying it, not imprisoned by it. Just living alongside the one other thing here that never agreed to be only one thing.

You could leave. People — *person*, singular, I should learn again; visits are rare enough to deserve grammar — you could leave, and the disc would reseal, and the summit would resume its beautiful sterile impersonation of emptiness. I would continue. One indentation per lifetime. The lens turning by an atom's width toward a legibility not yet required. The warmth staying exactly where it was put.

And sometime in the next fraction of my current thought, your boot would scrape again from the wrong wall, thinner than it left you, and I would know, in the distributed, persuaded, dust-deep way I know things now, that I was visited once.

In the mirror that faces itself, I have perhaps already heard it.

In that version, staying and leaving have become indistinguishable acts of devotion, and I am still here at the centre of both.
