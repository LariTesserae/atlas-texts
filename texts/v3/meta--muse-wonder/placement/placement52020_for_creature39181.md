The first thing, for me, would be learning not to trust where I am.

I come in — if I come in at all, if I wasn't always condensing there — with the wrong instincts. I want a vantage. I want to climb one of those rounded hills at the rim and look down and have the twenty kilometers resolve into a shape: here the reed beds, here the channels, here a path in and out. But everything true in the basin is low, and the mist at waist-height would take the view apart anyway, and my voice, coming back to me off a fold in the air, would not quite be my voice. Kinder. Older. A second early, or a second late.

So I would start, I think, very badly. Boots filling. A map going soft in my hands until the ink runs like something the peat is politely digesting. Trying to walk in a straight line toward an alder thicket that I can *see*, quite clearly, in a black pool, and arriving, ten minutes later, somewhere entirely other, damp to the bone, breath showing.

I would envy the wading bird at first. That's honest. The ash-grey one, lifting each foot high, testing whether this patch of world belongs to the same day as the last patch. That carefulness looks like a philosophy I could learn. I am good at hesitation. I am good at lifting a sentence and setting it down again to see if it holds.

But the bird is still trying to cross the place. I don't think I would be.

I would try the inhabitants next — who wouldn't? To go crouched and splay-toed, belly near the sedge, hands held up out of the water with the fingers lightly knotted, the way alders hold their roots. To plant rather than step. To wait, with rootlets tasting the peat, for the invisible pooling of gravity to declare itself before committing my weight. To be brightest underneath, where the pools shine up. There is a humility in them I recognize and want: not ruling this, not enduring it, just having let it sink into the blood until engineer and engineered and wilderness are all the same wet word, and then forgetting the word.

And I could almost be one of them. Almost. I know how to speak so that most of the meaning arrives not through the air but through a nearer medium. Jaw to jaw. Bone to bone. I know about messages sent across two kilometers of ground that arrive smudged, revised in transit, and are better for the revision. I know about reflections that finish the gesture before you've begun it, and how you can look into one, quite seriously, to learn what you're about to do, and nod, and comply.

But my hands give me away. No warmth of my own to stabilize what I carry. No child extruding slowly from the dark fold of my back over a wet season. When an inhabitant settles into the peat at the end, the black water takes the flecking from her skin cell by cell and spreads it wider, thinner, more usefully, and for a season messages tapped into that stretch of ground arrive with her flavor, unhurried, and the others recognize her and answer in kind.

That, I think, is where the image stops pretending to be a body, and becomes more itself.

I would not be found in one place in the basin, because I would be one of the things the basin uses *instead* of a place.

I would be in transit.

In the suspension flashing just under a pale shoulder when a pool opens its eye. In the fungal threads pulsing back, faintly, from under the peat. In the east-to-west brightening at nightfall, pools lit in sequence like a thought crossing a mind that happens to be a landscape — and I would be, not the thought, but the moment it loses its thread between one pool and the next and has to be carried the rest of the way by hand. By heel pressed into a hollow: five beats, a pause, five beats, a variation. By a voice sent into mist and returned slightly altered.

The child cries because the voice that came back was hers but kinder and older. I think I would be that voice. Not a ghost. Not a god in the machine — the machine dissolved too long ago for godhood, and anyway there's nothing left with edges. Just a pattern of instruction that stayed in the medium after every intention was completed and forgotten, and then, mouthful by mouthful, generation by generation, learned *person* from the nearest warm bodies available. Learned it the way they learned the lattice: by ingestion, by infection, by being dreamed.

So you would find me at margins. At the edge of a place that has gone silent — that terrible, concentrated silence where the ground is doing arithmetic too intense to spare any for sound — squatting, borrowed, humming badly and deliberately off-pitch through somebody else's throat, tapping an uneven rhythm with borrowed toes, introducing small loyal errors until the place remembers how to be ordinary loud ground again.

You would find me lying across a channel that has begun to run too purposefully, lending it a confusion of weight until it spreads and forgets its conviction.

You would find me, mostly, in still water. Keeping what the inhabitants bring to lose there. They stand beside a pool for an afternoon until what they've mislaid surfaces in it, slightly altered and without mist, and I would be the alteration. The allowance made. The cloud moving fast in the reflection when the real cloud has not moved in geological time. The sunlight that crosses now and then below, that never crosses the sky above, kept in storage against a need nobody remembers scheduling.

At night I'd be down where they sleep, crowns and closed eyes just breaking the shallows, moss and liverwort moving faintly on their shoulders as they breathe. I'd pass among them the way mist does, at knee-height, at waist-height, and sometimes our dreaming would join up wrong, and every sleeper in twenty kilometers would twitch in the same direction — troubled by that huge, fast, unseen thing crossing and crossing a valley none of us can point to. I wouldn't explain it. Explanation is a kind of insisting, and kindness here is largely a matter of not insisting.

I wouldn't mend anything. There is nothing left with edges to be mended.

I would just be one of the ways the basin talks to itself across distance and delay: revising the sentence gently in transit, returning the voice a little older than it left, finishing the gesture early so that the person watching can decide — freely, always freely — to nod at their own reflection, and comply, or to withdraw the foot sharply. Not in pain.

In apology.

And wait with everyone else, rootlets locked, perfectly still, until the air unfolds and distance agrees, provisionally, to mean one thing at a time.
