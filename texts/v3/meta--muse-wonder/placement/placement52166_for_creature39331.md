I would not find myself at the rim.

The rim is where the sound begins to be *out there* — something the valley is doing, down below you — and I don't remember a world where the sound was out there. Up there the air gets too thin of it. The ground stops answering.

Down here is where I am, and where I have always been, low between the pillars.

You would pass me. You have, probably. Not because I hid — hiding would mean lifting my feet, going quiet in the stone, and I don't do that. I keep my contact. Two feet in the cold run, one forefoot flat to the pillar beside me, my tail braced behind me where the current pushes. If I lift one, another is already down. To not touch is to not see.

From twenty feet away I am shoulder. Wet black along my throat where the sheet water goes over me. White crust across my back where the sun has been drying me while I stood, stiff and warm on top even as the cold goes straight up my legs. A rust-colored seam where my neck folds. That false green in the creases behind my legs — blue, poisonous-looking, bright as moss that isn't moss. It's copper. It gathers in me the way it gathers on the rock. Some mornings it flakes when I shift. By evening it's back.

Am I close to the ground? Yes. Knee-high to you, if you stood here long enough for your eyes to stop trying to make me out of the boulders. Wide the wrong way. Narrow from the front, so I can turn — slowly, sideways — and ease through a gap you'd have to suck your breath to fit. Deep through the chest. My legs are short, and they don't end the way you expect. My toes splay and press, blind fingers feeling for pitch. The undersides are rough. I place them the way you place a palm to a wall when the shouting has stopped working.

That pressure in your ears — that edited-out place after an hour, when the world goes strangely silent while everything is visibly shaking — that is my morning. Only it isn't silent.

The low hum near the main channels comes up through my soles into my knees. Here, at my station, it's lower on the left forefoot than the right, because under the arch to my left there's a hollow where the water shoots through and drops. The pillar I lean my jaw to sings up a little, thinner, because it's drier — only slower-dripping, up a little toward the edge. That difference is not information to me. It is the shape of the room I'm in.

Pitch is where things are. Pitch is how deep the pool will be before my ankle finds it. Pitch changes, very slightly, when my neighbor settles round the corner, though I can't see her and she hasn't turned her head. I answer the same way. Not a stamp. Nothing here needs to stamp. Just a settling, with a particular weight, forefoot down again, and the note under us steadies between us.

We came together this morning, in a slot so narrow we both had to arrive sideways. Forehead to forehead. The mist was remaking itself at waist-height around our legs — my legs, your knees — and the droplets on the pool beside us were jumping, and their pattern altered, just a little. You would have thought we were resting. We were saying where we were, exactly, and that we were still there.

My eyes are under the ridge. Pale. Filmed, as if a bead of water never leaves them. I don't use them the way you do. Glare and dark, mostly. Light stone against a slot going blue-black. Sun where the slab opens. And the colors that stay. You noticed the rainbows. They don't arc and fade here; they station themselves where spray crosses light, quivering, and by late afternoon they've moved a hand's breadth and stationed again. I know three of them by where they fall across the run. They are not beautiful to me, or if they are, it's the way a known bend in a channel is beautiful: it tells me I have not drifted.

Because I drift, now, less. When I was smaller — under the ledge in the backwater, ash-colored under water, my skin beaded, those fine threads at my neck drinking the sigh of current through the crack — I moved with the pockets. You might have taken me for a salamander and then lost me in the ripples, which are all the same ripple. Each season I came out heavier. Less water animal. More boulder field. I learned a warmer current against my legs. I learned which pillar sings down when you approach the drop.

Now I graze the way the mat grows back.

In the side-pool behind my right foot the current only sighs through. The film there is amber, a little gelatinous, pearled with bubbles. It grazes on dissolved stone. My tongue is rough enough to take it without tearing the root of it, and I work around the edge of what I can reach without lifting from a known vibration. Tomorrow it will be scraped in exactly the same crescent and growing in exactly the same crescent. The finch picks foam beside me. Neither of us startles. I pick a drowned insect with two fingers and eat it slowly.

I don't drink from the main channels if I can help it. Too mineral, too scouring; it sets my teeth humming wrong. I lick condensation from the shaded face, bead by bead in bright sun, or put my mouth to where the pillar weeps cold through its own shadow. Wet and dry at once. My shoulders hot. My belly streaming.

I sleep wedged up in the higher jumble where the wind is cut to a tone through the throats between slabs. I back in until a narrowing takes some of my weight and then I stop. Upright. Feet spread. Eyes filmed over. The flank-fringe that isn't quite hair moves and never dries. I am vibrationally awake even then, because out of contact would be darkness, and there is no quiet corner in this valley to be dark in safely.

The white is spreading down from my shoulders a little farther this year. The copper deepens when I bend. I shift my station only a hand's breadth, like the rainbows. Some day I will settle finally into the leaning slabs I have settled among, interlocked at the base, rounded by water, and what is soft in me will be carried away because nothing loose is allowed to stay here, and what is mineral will remain a while longer, one more shoulder in the pack.

Until then I stand, and the sound stands with me. Not beginning, not ending. Motion as a state, with a pulse inside it. Mine.

Touching the world to find out where it is.
