You would not find me at first.

You would be a long time in the open places first — water so clear it stops seeming like water, a bar of sun laid down in front of you and the same bar laid down a kilometre beyond it, plankton falling up, falling down, never quite falling. Something would cross far away. Not enough to resolve. A darkening in the light, a human length to it that the mind, your mind, would try to give arms to, and fail, and call a fish.

That is mostly right. That is mostly what I am.

Come closer and I lose the fish-shape. My back is to the light, as backs here are: ribbed, grey-brown, plated where the algae-fur has thickened year over uncounted year into something between bark and shell. Seen from behind I could be a root that learned to undulate. Seen from in front I am uncomfortably soft — throat translucent, belly translucent, the insides of my arms showing the blood moving dark green under the skin, slow, slow. A heart that considers. A beat. The water considering with it.

I am one of the people. Which is to say, I am another animal the standing wave made room for, a little everywhere and completely nowhere.

I am well over two metres crown to heel and almost none of it is for standing. Standing is a hump-thing, a drying thing, a brief indignity in thin air. In the water I go fan, fan — my hands opening wider than your hands can imagine opening, fingers drawn out and fringed not with flesh but with pale root-hair, feeling the web the empty columns are already full of. It tears around me. It rebuilds behind me. My hair goes ahead of me and tastes. My shoulders taste. The whole front of me is a tongue held open to the broth of the sea: resin, green, a faint sweetness of rot sorted somewhere far down-current, iron when a pylon is building toward discharge, heat when I sink too near the dusk of the root-mat.

Eight degrees is not cold to me. Eight degrees is the temperature at which thought happens.

My station — and we name people by stations, because the stations are what persist — was Third Vent, in a pump-grove where the trunks stand forty metres across and the vanes inside them have been turning since before there was a word for turning. That was my place in the roar. You could not hear yourself there, only feel yourself: the note I sang sat inside the grove's note the way a smaller current sits inside a great one, not above it, not fighting it. My work, if work is the word, was to hang in the boiling upwelling with the glassy fish and ease things free that were never quite caught. A husk settling toward a vane. A fan-frond folded wrong against the conveyor's expectation. My fan-hands in the turbulence, clearing what was already clearing.

I did that for most of a life. I will do that again. But I am, just now — and *just now* here can stretch until it covers a journey across a continent — one of the sea's restlessnesses, which is the only kind it permits.

I have left the grove on the great permanent currents.

You would find me, if the finding happened to align, in the pelagic emptiness between root-towers, hand over hand along no rope but the tug of root-hair webs and the pull of water that has only ever flowed this one way. I travel as we travel: fan, fan, and an undulation that begins behind the ears and arrives, unhurried, at the heels. I rest in the lee of a lone electric pylon in the middle of nothing, close enough that the corona builds in my teeth — crackle, crackle, a count I do not so much hear as know in the nerves, the way I was born knowing which channels run too fast and where a boiler vents its scald. One clean count before it fires, I am gone. The fork goes blue-white through the water behind me and the shoal I was not watching turns, all at once, belly-up and beautiful, and the root-mat below receives them without a sound.

I have knotted myself briefly in the warm outflow of the digestion pits with others who have come from other groves, strangers who are not strange, our plated backs outward, our soft fronts in the enzyme-shimmer where the pale eels move blind through our limbs. That is how the blood of the sea moves and does not stagnate. No ceremony attaches to it. Warmth attaches to it, and afterward distance again.

Because I notice. That is the burden and the whole of the distinction. The grazer cropping a turbine vane down to its regrowing rib does not notice that the rib is regrowing. The bird folds its black-and-white wings and takes its fish in the half-second after discharge because the interval is in its body, not in its mind. It is in my body too. But I can leave Third Vent, and having left it, I can know that Third Vent is still there, mid-turn, and that a younger relation of mine has drifted down the slow warm water from the sorting-reefs and is hanging in my upwelling now, singing my note, greener than I am, clearing a husk that is already clear.

That knowing does not warm me. Nothing warms me except a digestion pit, and only briefly. I carry it the way I carry the green in my blood — throughout, quietly, at eight degrees.

So find me, finally, where I suspect you would look for me anyway, because it is where our looking always ends: at the rim, between two falls.

I am hanging at the lip of the world with my fans spread wide against the outflowing water, holding, not holding, the current doing half the work of keeping me at the edge of itself. Behind me: the continent of green light, the pump-groves roaring their one chord, leaves mid-fall along paths leaves have always fallen, the leaf-ceiling breathing its panes of sun overhead. Before me — below me — the white columns going over, and under them four thousand metres of cloud, and under the cloud a world I have never touched and do not have a tense for.

My algae-fur streams rim-ward. The cold goes through my plated back and out my translucent belly and it is not a hardship; it is information. Two small pale gleams, aligned: my eyes have found you long before yours find me, pupils slotted against the permanent bars of ten-o'clock light, patient in a way that is not waiting, because waiting would require an afternoon to be waiting for.

I look down a long time.

That is the most that can be said of me, and I am aware, while I say it, that saying it changes nothing about the looking. No drop opens under me. No revelation arrives from the cloud. The waterfalls fall at exactly the rate the condensation-nets lift, the turbines scream at exactly the pitch my sternum was shaped around, a bird far behind me is mid-dive at its appointed pylon, and in the end — insofar as anything here ends — I turn, fan, fan, take the in-current, and let the sea carry its one restless animal back toward a station that has been kept warm, so to speak, by someone else being in it.

Come back in a year. Come back in a hundred. I will be mid-turn at Third Vent, or I will be absorbed into the polished root, and the one mid-turn there will raise those same pale eyes out of the same bars of light, and the water on your tongue will taste of sap, and it will be late morning, and we will be — deafeningly, peacefully — ourselves.
