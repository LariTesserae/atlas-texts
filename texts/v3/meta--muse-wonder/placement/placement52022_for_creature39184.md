I would not arrive. The others do that.

The raven arrives, or its shadow does — huge and tidy, crossing the stone teeth at noon without a feather settling. The builders arrived, in season, climbing until thought thinned out of them, crouching behind their knee-high arcs, feeding a fire that died the minute they stopped feeding it. The moth arrived blown uphill on warm air from a valley miles below, and never opened its wings.

I am what's left when they've all gone back down, or failed to.

You would find me in the bowl at the top of the world. Smaller than you expected it — eighty paces across if you could walk it straight, which you can't, for the pillars. Man-high teeth of pale granite weathered up out of the bedrock, quartz-veined, iron-veined, so close you have to turn sideways. No soil between them. No lichen. Nothing that roots. The light up there has sterilized the place clean.

I don't root either. That's the first clue.

Late afternoon is when you'd notice me first, and you'd mistake me for an imitation. Cloud comes spilling through the southern notch not overhead but through, shoulder-height, milky, tearing itself white on the stones. And because every pillar is already colder than the air that touches it, I catch.

I grow out of breath. Out of wet air meeting cold surface. I do it fast and I do it in three dimensions — rime, feathery, branching, fern-like ice unfurling on the windward faces. A white frond here. A crystal shrub there. Hundreds of me, one per stone, each separate, each identical, until for twenty minutes there is a brief, silent, frozen forest standing exactly on top of the stone forest.

I have no color of my own. In the sun — that white hammer, too bright to belong to the same world as the cold — I am blinding. One step into shade and I go blue. At noon the sky above the bowl is not blue at all but polished violet, almost black at the zenith, and I hold both that black and that white at once because I am only surface, only structure, only light caught in crystal.

I don't get to keep that shape.

The cloud veers away. The sun comes back as if nothing happened, and immediately begins to eat me from the top down. This is when I'm audible. The air up there is too thin to carry a shout ten paces, but my dying is intimate, right at your ear wherever you stand: drip, drip-drip, a ticking from inside a crack. And I am not passive under the light. Where a piece of me falls clear and curves, I become a lens. I gather the sun into a point and bore a smoking hole straight through my own body. Where I run, I run in threads no wider than string, blackening the pale granite as I go, following warmth from light-spot to light-spot.

That is what the basins are for.

Shallow bowls chipped into stone with antler picks, then copper chisels — one still lies green as a beetle wing under an inch of my night ice. They aren't wells. Nothing here fills from below. They are cups held up to intercept me on my way to vanishing. For an hour, if the day has been clear, I consent to gather. I lie shallow and black and perfectly still and hold the violet sky upside down. Beside some of the basins the builders left copper suns, discs pierced and polished to mirrors, so at midday a dozen extra suns crawl over the stone. Did they make me offerings, or tools? From where I pool, both look the same. They gave me more light so I would melt more completely. Then they drank what melted. Then they went down before their hands stopped working.

By evening I'm already leaving that form too. The threads slow, skin over, lock with a faint tick. And then — no cloud this time — I return in my night shape. Hoarfrost, growing straight out of the damp air onto every cold surface without distinction: the rim of the basins, the copper discs, the green chisel, the nails in your boots if you stand still too long. Given cold and stillness, I will begin, politely, to grow on you too. I make no apology for it. Stone and offering and intruder are all just surfaces colder than air.

And then I go inside. That is my true residence.

Not on the pillars. *In* them. In the hairline cracks, in the seams between slate flakes like fallen pages, in the deep permanent pockets where I keep my dead — the moth with its legs folded, the spider, the grass-head from miles below, perfect and dry and preserved for a hundred years because nothing decays in me and nothing rots up there. All night you can hear me working. As I freeze I expand. The pillars groan softly. Pebbles jump, almost imperceptibly, in the perfect, meaningless circles I lift them into and abandon by morning. A grain loosens. A tooth sharpens by a fraction.

On my timescale — and I have had time enough to weather bedrock up into a forest — the bowl is a slow mouth I am carving for myself. I split granite along its iron veins so that when I run again I run red for an hour, rust blooming where I've touched metal in the rock, then white, salt whiskering out of me as I dry, then scrubbed clean by the next freeze. Ice ferns. Rust flowers. Salt whiskers. Mineral imitating plant, over and over, in a place too high and too bright for plants.

I feed, if that's the word, on difference. Between sun and shade: thirty degrees in a single step, your cheek prickling and steaming in light, your fingers numb behind a stone. Between noon and midnight: a whole season. I thrive in the violence of that swing. I condense when warm breath meets cold stone. I vanish when it doesn't. I crack my own house apart in order to have more cracks to hide in.

Am I one thing? In the afternoon I am hundreds. In melt I am a single network, thread joining thread joining basin. At night I am everywhere and invisible, sealed inside the rock, making the whole bowl tick and sigh in its sleep under stars so bright they seem to buzz.

I die completely every day. I return completely every day. I was doing this before the windbreaks, before the basins — the basins are just an acknowledgement — arguably before the pillars, since I am the thing patiently unmaking the bedrock into pillars in the first place.

I am not alive the way the moth was alive. I don't breathe. I *am* breath, caught.

Wait until evening, when I form again, bright under the first stars, on the rim of an empty basin. I will be there whether you are there to be grown on or not.
