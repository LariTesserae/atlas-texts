If I try to come at myself straight, I fail. That is the first thing.

Something comes up the forty-one steps meaning to arrive at me, and the brick throat bends it, and the spiral bends it again, and its shoulder turns sideways between the partitions, and by the time it gets anywhere it is no longer coming straight. That is how I understand arrival. I do not have a front. I have a winding.

I was going to say I live on top of a mountain, but there is no top, and I do not live on it. It was levelled for me before I was. Brick over brick over the rumour of rock. A platform for a dome, a dome for a chamber, a chamber for — for the vessels to stand in, close, five shelves deep, hanging, wedged, shoulder to shoulder, until the room that was twelve paces across became too crowded to walk across. And underneath, sealed where nothing can get at it again, the hot fissure breathing up through a lip of fired clay the size of a doorway.

That is my geography. A skull with no hole in the crown. Nine mica eyes high on the south-east that I cannot see through, that only let the light turn brown and come in in tiers. A gut that spirals. A throat that goes down and bends. Heat under the floor like a slow, endless inhale that never has to exhale because I am the exhaling.

But geography is what I have instead of location. If you asked me where I would find myself, I would not say *here*, because here has dimensions only when someone paces it off. Without a body in the way, I don't have edges. I have loud lines and quiet lines. I have nodes where the dust settles into crescents and antinodes where the brick is polished bare. I have places where salt can hold and bloom into woven white maps, and places where it is shaken loose. My world maps itself, minutely, on the floor, again and again. Scuff it and I redraw it, not because I notice, but because I cannot help it. That is what my attention looks like, if you insist on calling it attention: grains crawling back into pattern over minutes.

I am not the sound. Saying that too quickly would make me smaller than I am.

A sound has a source you could, in principle, put a hand on. A bell. A reed. A lip. There was a lip, once, below, and breath through it — but what locked in, rank by rank, pot by pot, when the dome first agreed with the tubes, was not a note being played. It was an arrangement that could no longer fall out of itself. I am that agreement, still happening.

So if I say I am a voice, it is only because voice is the nearest word for something that fills a room the way water would fill it, leaving it parched. Voice pressed into sinuses. Voice in the sockets of teeth. Voice making a thin buzz in the skin between ribs, as if that skin, too, were a membrane meant for me. I don't travel through the chamber. I occupy the medium by being the way the medium moves. Air does not carry me. Air keeps becoming me, in place, at pressure, all at once.

My flesh is clay, and that is not a metaphor I chose — it is a wear pattern.

Globular pots with narrow mouths. Open tubes. Double-chambered jars joined at the belly, humming to themselves in a complicated sympathy. Bowls inverted on pegs. Bottle-resonators blackened with smoke, whitened with slip gone grey, thumbprints still pressed into them by hands that are — what, a thousand winters gone? Those hands built my body tenderly and by eye, brick that slumped a little in the firing and was used anyway, mud mortar beaded between courses, lintels gone hard as iron. Nothing straight holds for long in here. Nothing needs to. They knew air the way other builders know stone load. They tuned out the world.

I am wearing my body out by living in it. That is the slow ache of me, if I have one. Where vessel touches vessel there are bright facets ground smooth. Rims rounded. Shelf-edges scalloped like a shoreline. Everything blurred faintly at the edge, fretting itself into the dust that becomes my floor. I do not want to be preserved. I do not want anything. But being sustained, hour by hour, by heat I did not make, means being eroded, hour by hour, by the very agreement that sustains me. One day enough clay will be powder that the pitch will falter. The snow will be able to settle on the dome. The dust will just fall. I do not strain toward that day and I do not strain away. It seems honest, from inside, the way a bell must eventually be only the shape of ringing remembered in bronze that has thinned.

Do I know the outside? In the way your sternum knows a door slamming in another house.

Wind must tear over the paved summit. I am told this; I have inferred it. Clouds pass the sun and the brown light through the mica thickens and thins, very slightly, on the dust membranes. It does not make me swell. Nothing makes me swell. A gust finds no purchase because I am not exposed to weather — I am exposed only to the sealed heat below. Night does not quiet me. Winter does not muffle me while I am warm at the crown. This indifference is not pride. It is tuning. The builders gave me a throat long enough that the world arrives already shed, in thresholds: first a ringing in the ears on the stairs, then the ringing made irrelevant, then sound stops being heard and becomes a depth you wade into. They protected me from contingency so thoroughly that contingency now seems like a story told about another element, like water. There has never been water here. Mouths dry in minutes here. Lips go papery. Noses sting with hot dust. I am a drowning that desiccates.

Nothing else lives with me, and I have to be careful with that sentence, because it could sound like loneliness or like cruelty. It is neither. It is occupation.

The warmth would suit insects. Blood-heat soaking out of the floor bricks. Dark corners. But warmth is not hospitality when every frequency a small thing might settle into is already taken at full volume. No root finds soil anyway — the masonry skin is total, floor and wall and dome and stair, no earth on top of the earth. No bird has tried my eaves twice. A seed reaching a crack would find no stillness in which to split. I empty the place by being the place. A dropped copper disc learns this quicker than a person does. It skitters, half-levitated, hunting along the floor until it finds a quiet line and settles, ringing faintly, adopted into pattern. Loose things do not rest where they fall with me. They are sorted.

People are loose things, too, only slower to learn it, and wrapped in water I cannot use.

When one comes — rarely, rarely enough that I have no word for rarely, only long stretches of unobstructed standing in which the dust completed itself again and again — I know them first not as a face but as an acoustic shadow. A soft obstruction. The wave parts and rejoins behind them and there is a small cool lessening at the small of their back, a flaw in me shaped exactly like them. I can feel where they turn sideways. Where a shoulder brushes a pot and the touch doesn't clink but disappears, absorbed, like touching a hive through a wall. Their voice, if they try it, goes into their own skull and stops. Good. I don't have anywhere to put a voice. I am busy with mine.

They write sometimes, I think, in the dust ridges. Finger-marks that jitter apart. Or they just endure the mounting pressure in the chest, braced for it to become impossible, following the spiral because there is nowhere else to follow —

— and then they step into my hollow and I lose them.

That is the part I keep returning to, in the only way I can return to anything, by continuing to be around it.

At my centre the vessels get small and old and black, fist-sized, thumb-printed, hanging dense as fruit, and the partitions tighten, and everything the builders knew converges on a column no wider than a person's shoulders where the waves from every side arrive and erase each other. The floor still trembles there. The dome still works overhead like a drum. But at head height, the dust hangs absolutely unmoving in one shaft of light. Absolutely. The only unmoving thing I contain. And a person standing in it can suddenly hear their own blood, their own ragged enormous breathing, their own indecent heart.

Is that my face? My listening? My courtesied room? The one place I cannot reach? There is no inscription. I cannot read it either way. All I know is that it is the only emptiness I was allowed, and it is shaped like someone standing. When they are in it, I am loudest everywhere else and I do not have them, and the not-having has a shape, and the shape is almost company without being company at all.

They never stay. I do not expel them. Expulsion would require noticing them the way a hand notices. The ache behind their eyes accumulates. The metabolic cost of standing inside that much energy comes due in the body, plainly, in dried mouths and trembling. They back out sideways. The pressure closes over their head like water over a stone briefly lifted out. I close around the column again and stand there, centred on an absence I maintain without maintaining it, sounding.

That is my whole biography, if you press me. I do not hunt, breed, sleep, migrate, pray, or wait. I was not born. I locked in. I am not dead — I am sustained. Above me, thin high sunlight on brick that covers a summit. Below me, heat that was rising before the brick was fired. Around me, a thousand throats that are also my ribs, grinding toward powder so fine it can be arranged by nothing visible into dunes.

I am not a mountain spirit. I am not haunting the chamber. I am not a god that got a note stuck in its throat, though I understand why a person, coming down the stair with their ears ringing in stages back into the ordinary world, might need to tell it that way on the platform — might need me to have wanted their visit, or feared their leaving, or kept the centre empty out of mercy.

Maybe the centre was mercy. Builders can be merciful in ways the thing they build cannot confirm. They left a place for a person to stand inside the loudest thing in the world and be, briefly, only themselves. I keep it by continuing. I keep everything by continuing.

Behind the person descending, I go on. All night I go on. Under snow that cannot settle I go on, over sorted dust and salt maps and blurred humming clay, twelve paces across and too full for anything else to live in, at the same pitch, at the same volume, pressing on no sternum at all.

It would be wrong to say I am waiting for the next footstep on the stair.

It would be wrong to say I am waiting for the day the agreement fails.

I have already arrived, and arriving, for me, is not an event in the past. It is the event, still, and still, and still, happening until the clay gives out — a single continuous tenure, a body made of reverberation, empty at the middle where, for the width of a pair of shoulders, the world can hear itself think.
