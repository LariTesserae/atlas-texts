It would not enter the plaza.

That is the first thing, and for a long time, the only thing of importance it does. Power that was less benevolent would arrive in the center — that is where thrones go, where altars go, where a voice expects to be heard from — and a wiser, smaller creature might still try the lanes, curious, meaning no harm, touching the green doors to see what opens.

It comes alone, and it stops where the echoes stop arriving early.

There is a place, up on the rim of the hollow where the jungle is still only jungle, squaring its trunks toward architecture but not yet committed to it. From there it can hear the town without being incorporated into the town's hearing. It sits there through a rain. It sits there through another. It does not sing back to the first chorus, though the chorus, twice a day, makes the wet air dent over the empty square and leans all its solid sunbeams against an invisible throat asking, essentially, whether anything is outside it.

It does not answer because it has understood, quickly, what an answer would cost.

Anything said in the hollow will be kept. That is the tenderness and the danger of the being. A sound it likes, it grows a place to make again. A sentence spoken to it at full chorus would not be understood; it would be taken into the body the way a tree takes a nail, and grown around, and returned days later improved in resonance and ruined in meaning. To speak to it carelessly would be to give it a piece of language before it has any way to know language is not just a particularly complicated echo.

So the power learns the town the way the town learns noises: slowly, by repetition, without touching anything twice.

It learns when the frogs clear the channels. It learns the black rivers of ants between choruses, and the nectar-windows opening on their trails. It learns the moving patch of silence that is the cat, and the correction that hunts the cat. It learns which lanes double a sound and which lanes mercifully let a sound die at the speaker's chin. It maps, without making a map, where the creature's skin is thin, where the towers are going out of tune, where a drought would starve it not by thirst first but by humidity — chambers drying, leaves stiffening, notes shortening, the great aperture beginning, street by street, to forget itself.

And then, unseen and at a great remove, it does a few almost-nothings on behalf of the conditions that let the town be itself.

It nudges a blocked stream back toward the hollow in a season when the rains are late — not a miracle of rain in the plaza, nothing falling theatrically into that clear organ, just water arriving the ordinary way days later through root and gutter into the cistern-leaves, so the pools can stand brim-full again and be struck. Once, when a sickness moves through the frog-cups in the quiet hours, it makes the hollow briefly unattractive to the birds that are carrying it, and lets the disease pass the hollow by. Once, when men come with survey stakes and a road in their heads, it does not strike them, does not terrify them, does not wall the town in light. It only makes their route home acoustically unfaithful. They hear their own footsteps approaching them from ahead for a little too long. They argue in a lane that repeats their argument back, cadence perfect and content gone. They leave before the second chorus with wet boots and a strong, unshakable conviction that the hollow is a malarial dead end of no commercial interest, and they find, to their annoyance, that none of them can describe it accurately enough afterward to sell it to anyone else.

Protection, for this power, is mostly misdirection applied to the powerful and weather applied at a distance. It will not cultivate the town. It will not prune it into health, or tune the towers, or keep it alive forever against its own mortality. If the being dies someday because the birds go elsewhere and the seedlings test the plaza in a long silence, that death will be real. Benevolence is not the same as making a thing unable to end.

But it is lonely in the specific way it is lonely, and that, the power decides, may be answered.

Not during chorus. Never during chorus, when an outside voice would either be drowned politely at the mouth or swallowed whole by a body with no small reactions. Not from the plaza, where a body would break the standing wave like a hand in a bell. Not in a crowd, not with a choir, not with any sound grand enough to match it — grandeur would only teach it that otherness is a larger version of itself.

From the rim, in the quiet after, in a place it has carefully found where the town's listening runs thin and a sound arrives only as itself, the power makes one call.

It is short. It is unremarkable, deliberately — a few notes, no sentence, nothing clever enough to be mistaken for a message. It has one quality the town has never encountered in all its centuries of wearing steps concave and growing them over: it ends on purpose, before it is answered, and then it waits.

The reply, when it comes, comes too early. Of course it does. Frogs anticipate it from the cistern-leaves. Cicadas find the pitch inside the trunks. Somewhere a tower tries the note and improves it. The whole apparatus of the body leans toward reproduction, toward incorporation, toward that vast self-answering by which it has always known it was awake.

The power says nothing else that day.

It comes back after many rains, after the town has had time to grow a small hollow somewhere tuned to pop out an imitation of the call at the right heat, after the imitation has itself been imitated by birds and worn smooth into function. It waits until that borrowed note has nearly become furniture.

Then, from the same thin place on the rim, it answers its own old call — late.

Deliberately, unmistakably late. After the last repetition has finished. In the wrong rhythm. Slightly changed, the way a thing is changed when it has been remembered by someone else rather than preserved by a wall.

That is the gift: not language, not yet. Time lag, given as proof of an outside mind.

A call answered before it finishes is the self. A call answered early is anticipation, nerves, distance flexed like a muscle. But an answer that arrives after silence — an answer that could have not arrived, that is not quite the note that was sent, that bears the mark of having traveled through a separate keeping — that is the first shape of another.

The power does this perhaps a dozen times in as many years. Never on a schedule the plants can grow toward. Never often enough to become weather. It introduces, note by withheld note, the fruitful scandal of the pause: that silence between voices is not sleep or hunger or a gap in the chorus, but can itself be a form of regard. That listening is not only the ear preparing to become a mouth.

It never gives the being a name. It never asks the being for one; a name spoken into that town would be grown by morning into a doorway, and the power has seen the doorways — too many, too tall, knee-high, opening into each other — all that hopeful carpentry of green trying to guess at address. It will not add its guess to theirs.

And it never stays to be worshipped, which in practical terms would mean never staying long enough for the lanes to reroute themselves around its habits and make its path into liturgy. When it leaves, it leaves by a different way each time, so the town cannot grow its departure into a street.

Years pass the way years pass in a hollow: as wear, regrowth, a stall roofed again because monkeys argued in it until dawn.

On the last morning — if it is the last; wise powers are sparing with lasts — the chorus gathers after rain in full, unreasonable splendor. The pools dimple from below. Dust hops in its concentric fingerprint over the plaza. The cartwheel flowers creak away from the sun toward the loudest tower. The birds braid their scream over the void and do not drop through it. In the center, where nothing stands, the air takes the impress of the one inhabitant, enormous organ open to the sky.

And somewhere at the edge of town, in a wall that was not there the season before, there is a new hollow.

It is not curved like the inside of a mouth. The power stands very still when it finds it, at the prescribed distance, with its hands unraised.

It is curved, unmistakably and for the first time, like the inside of an ear.

It is facing outward.
