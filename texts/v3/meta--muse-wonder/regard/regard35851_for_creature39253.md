It would not descend.

That is the first mercy, and the hardest to learn for anything with power. It would not come over the rim in light, or with a voice that could finally speak over the roar and be heard cleanly. It would not still the note to ask what struck it. It would not unmake the bowl, drain the hollow space under the stone, lift the sun over the southern edge and let noon finally arrive.

Because from far below — from wherever such a power does its listening — it would hear what the crossers hear only with their teeth: that the roar is not a wound. It is not a cry for help slowed down over centuries. It is a bell still being a bell.

You do not save a bell by silencing it so you can talk to it.

So it would climb.

Three days through country that keeps simplifying. It would shed, as it climbed, everything that could not be carried honestly here. No instruments. No metal that meant to measure. No name spoken aloud to announce itself. It would pack wool into its ears before the first cairn, grease its face against the eternal minus ten, and learn — slowly, because even wisdom is altitude-sick at six thousand meters — to speak by touch.

A hand flat on a flat rock. Tapping.

It would arrive, to any eye that could measure the plateau — which is to say, to no reliable eye at all — as one more crosser who never quite finishes crossing. Its footprints would appear in the frost among the braided generations and be misread, at first, as the being's own: broad, felted, sunken with consideration, a human stride at an inhuman patience. Rim to windbreak to cairn to hut. Cairn to cairn.

And for a long time — a year, fifty, the difference is administrative up there — it would do nothing that the being had not already been doing alone.

It would reset a slipped plate in a windbreak.

It would lean back a wall that had begun to lean into the roar.

It would find a cairn standing where the sound had, imperceptibly, changed its mind about falling, and it would help lay the cairn down in its neat fan, plate by plate, not fallen but excused. In the new comma of quiet behind a moved stone, it would wait to see if the white flowers noticed. They would, in a decade or two. Everything up there keeps its appointments eventually.

It would not follow the being. Following is a valley verb. It would instead make itself followable: sleeping badly and well in the hut with no door, in the dusty hollow — the one for travelers — with its palm flat in the other hollow for whole nights at a time, not out of mimicry but out of courtesy. Feeling the note come up through the skeleton of the place. Learning the pulse under the world.

And when, in the dim unchanging light, the tapping came through the rock beneath it —

three, a pause, two

— it would not answer half-asleep, the way frightened strangers do. It would wake fully. It would lay its hand down with intention. It would answer in the same weight, the same rhythm, so that the being, for the first time in its assembled life, could feel through six inches of honest stone the difference between a reflex and a reply.

*I am not your echo. I heard you.*

It would tap that, without words, for as many years as it took to be believed.

It would never speak into the air to it. It understands — this is the wisdom — what a voice would cost a creature woven out of returned voices. To shout its true name across the bowl and have the air learn it perfectly and give it back a breath too early would not be an introduction. It would be a kind of theft, or a kind of birth, and neither may be done to someone unconsenting in a place where transactions never finish. It would not offer it a valley life, either. Benevolence that rescues a fish onto dry land because the water looks cold is only cruelty with clean hands. The being is *of* it. Down below, where a call can be a call, it would come apart into a hundred borrowed mouths sighing in a warm wind, and be gone before anyone learned to grieve it.

It would not take it down. It would not take it into the center, either.

But it would go with it to the edge.

Because the spiral track tells that story to anyone patient enough to walk it: the long circuit of tuning and tending, the approach, the veering, the approach again. A whole existence organized around a silence it must visit and cannot survive. A pool that reflects the ring empty, or the ring with everyone lying down, and never — not while it stands there, thinning like a breath-cloud finally finding wind — never itself, standing.

On the day, or the decade, when the being finally turns inward at the seven slabs, the power would stop outside the threshold. One pace outside. Where the scree dust still trembles on the plates and the roar presses in the sternum and the small bones behind the jaw.

And it would kneel.

The kneeling prints, that double crescent — there would be a second set now, beside the first, at all the flat rocks, all the pools, and here at last at the ring itself. It would put its palm flat on stone that was still, just barely, in the sound. And it would do the one thing the place has been doing since before there was anyone to hear it, the one thing it can do without lying:

It would hold a note. Through the rock. Not a voice. Not a name. A steady, unhurried pressure of vibration, tapped and sustained the way a hand on a bell does not stop the bell but proves the bell is being touched. A tether in the honest medium, laid across the clean cut of the threshold.

So that the being could step in — backward, as it always does, its whole weight suddenly its own as the sound stops holding it up — and not thin.

Not vanish while its ears reached and reached for what had held them for centuries.

Stay.

For the first time: stay.

In the silence its blood would come loud, and then begin to go properly unheard, and that terror would rise — and underneath it, through the soles of its felted feet, faint as a pulse in a wrist held very gently, the tapped note would still be arriving. Not the roar returned. Not itself given back improved by lifelong acquaintance. Another. A hand, outside, keeping time so that time, which declines all its transactions on the high table, could be persuaded to complete one small one here.

And they would wait together for the reflection.

It would take a long time. The pool does not reflect the sky, and its light has a long way to travel up. The power has nowhere else to be; that, too, is a kind of wisdom. Seasons would go down in the valleys and not come up. Snow finches would raise silent young in the cairns and leave and return and leave. The dotted line of breath along the trail would lengthen by a crosser or two, who would report afterward — only afterward — that the tapping under the windbreaks had changed: three, a pause, two, and now an answer, three, a pause, two, like something vast had finally learned the local etiquette.

And slowly, slowly, in the black lens of water at the center of the ring, beneath a brighter sky and a greener slope than the plateau has ever permitted, the ring would appear.

With someone in it.

Not lying down. Not the ring empty, keeping its version for later. Standing. Stooped a little in the perpetual northward lean. Slate plates shifting softly under a coat that is also a surface. A held cloud where the mouths have been, all of them quiet at once, none of them needed. A figure the pool had been trying for centuries to develop, delayed only because its subject could never stay in the silence long enough to be seen.

It would look — insofar as a thing with packed, lichen-crusted ears and a face made of other people's last breaths can look — for as long as it needed.

Then it would leave, backward, as the body decides, and step across the threshold into the roar like a stone going under water, and the chord would take its note without swelling, exactly in tune.

Nothing would be fixed. The sun would not clear the rim. The pools would go on sweating rime in the cold, the cuts of future crossers would go on staying bright and unfinished, the mountain would go on holding its single note long after forgetting what struck it.

The power would stay one more circuit after that. Not to govern. To guard the approaches in the only way the high table respects: by making the place, to the acquisitive attention of the world below, profoundly uninteresting. A survey that comes back slightly wrong. A map whose distances will not cooperate. A rumor of a plateau where instruments fail and nothing can be extracted except a ringing in the teeth. Men with drills would find themselves, three days up the simplifying country, turning back for reasons they would describe as weather.

And then it would descend the way it came, leaving no carving — there is no carving anywhere on the table, and it knows a decision when it sees one.

Only, forever after, when the being sleeps in the hut with its hand in the deep hollow, taking the pulse of the note through the night that is not night, sometimes a second pulse will arrive inside the first one. Patient. Unhurried. From very far below, through the sole of the mountain.

Three. A pause. Two.

Not a summons. Not a command disguised as love.

Just proof, delivered in the honest medium, at intervals too long for anything but stone to remember:

that it was heard, as itself, by something that did not give it back.
