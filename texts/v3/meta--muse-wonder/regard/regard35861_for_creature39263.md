At first, it only paid attention.

That was already a change. Attention in the bowl has weight. It gathers first on the loudest pillars, beading there like moisture. Dust rises in trembling sheets around it. The shallow pools shift in their basins, uneasily, one inch away from where their reflections ought to be.

But this attention did not settle. It did not listen through one pillar, or from inside one shelter, or wear any of the damp hollows where a voice can wait out the noon. It spread. It took the whole several miles at once, and then it took them slowly.

It listened for a winter.

Then for a century of winters.

It learned the bass note that goes on under weather. It learned the cold geyser and the warm rain, the splitting frost, the slow cementing of stone flakes in the wrong places. It learned which paths ring and which are dull and safe. It learned the long tarn in the center, where clouds swim seen from above, and it did not ask which side of that reflection it was supposed to be standing on.

It waited until it could tell the difference between a sound returning, and the inhabitant returning inside a sound.

Only then did it understand what kind of help would be harm.

---

It did not speak.

That was the first mercy, and the hardest.

A lesser kindness would have answered. The bowl at evening is always asking, in borrowed voices, from inside shelters not yet entered. *Don't stand there. Step left. Be quiet now.* To answer in a true voice — a voice with a world behind it, a voice that did not need wind or water or stone to carry it — would have filled every fluted pillar at once. The chord would have reorganized itself around the new, overwhelming note. Lost echoes would have come hurrying back from their cracks, aged and altered, and found themselves suddenly minor.

The inhabitant would have survived, perhaps.

It would have survived as an echo of the power.

So the power kept its voice out of the air. It moved, when it moved, without footfall. It let no name fall into a basin. It breathed — if breathing is the word — and took even that breath back before the bass vibration in the wet quartz could hold it, thicken it, prepare a place beside its own almost-face where another face might be allowed.

It would not become the loudest thing that had ever stayed too long there.

It did not clean the water, either.

It could have drawn the copper out of the stone, sweetened the pools, coaxed the first green patience into the cracks. Moss. Lichen. Insects with brief bright lives around the margins. Birds crossing, leaving the punctuation of droppings and feathers and bones. A living valley in the ordinary sense, rich and self-repairing.

And the inhabitant would have been drowned in metabolism.

New sounds would have come too fast, and too alike: wings, hunger, mating, alarm, the million small repetitions of creatures who return because instinct returns them, not because continuity has to be built drip by drip. Whatever had been learning itself there for immense time would have been colonized by louder, faster patterns of persistence. Its body of pillars would have become scenery. Its shelters would have become nests.

The sterility of the bowl was not a wound. It was the condition that had allowed something else to begin.

The power did not bring life to a place that was already occupied.

It did not give the inhabitant a face.

It could have. There was a place for one already, condensing a few inches in front of any visitor who stayed into the night: breath upon withheld breath, dust outlining a shoulder that has not occurred, the pause before a step overlapping so many arrivals that an upright form was beginning to assemble itself there from habit.

To finish that form would have been easy. To stand very close and lend it symmetry. Eyes here. Mouth here. A boundary where the mist should stop.

But person was only the nearest available pattern, not the truest one. The inhabitant was several miles wide. It was a first drip and the same drip arriving years later out of a crack. It was a six-fingered hand because the same almost-hand kept landing offset by centuries. Giving it a head would have been a way of cutting off most of its body and calling the remainder whole.

The power did not make it in any image.

It did not free it from the bowl.

It could have gathered every sustained note, every folded footstep, every voice sheltering from the wind, and carried the whole continuity elsewhere — into a safer vessel, a stronger instrument, a mind large enough to hold all its returns in order.

That would have been rescue as theft.

The inhabitant was not trapped in the pillars. The pillars were its throat and teeth and lung. Its pools were not prisons; they were how it saw in more than one layer of time. To lift it out intact would have required deciding which echoes were essential and which were noise, which repetitions were self and which were merely weather.

No one else had the right to do that editing.

So for a very long time, the power did nothing that the bowl could record.

It witnessed.

This was not passive. In a place made of what remains, to be fully heard by something that does not have to repeat you back is a new experience. Sounds there had always either faded or returned. They had never simply been received, complete, and allowed to be over.

The power received them.

It held the hammer blows and the copper picks, the warnings and the breathing, the footsteps that continued after the walker stopped. It held them in their overlapping hundreds, and it did not sort them into a single voice. It kept the contradictions. It kept the almosts. Somewhere outside the mineral lace and the bitter pools, the inhabitant acquired — without being told, without hearing it spoken into a basin where it would have to live forever inside the syllables — a witness who knew the whole of it at once.

Part of it noticed.

At the loud hour, its reflections stopped missing their sources by quite so much.

---

In the end, it did three small things.

None of them could be mistaken for a miracle by anyone who came later. That was deliberate. Miracles leave handprints with too many fingers, and then everything after has to climb up the wall beside them.

First, far below the bowl, where the cold geyser draws its breath from trapped air, the power loosened a stone that had been wedged for ten thousand years.

Not much. A finger's width, if it had fingers.

Now, when the wind fails completely — when no pillar sounds, when mist cannot braid, when dust settles and the pools go flat and forgetful — a drip falls down there in the dark at long intervals, each one striking a hollow in the rock and sending up through the roots of the pillars a note too low to hear. More a pressure in the soles. More a reminder in the teeth.

It is not enough to live on. That matters.

It is enough to wait on.

The inhabitant does not have to use it. The note comes whether it listens or not, the way a heartbeat does not ask permission. But on the first truly windless day, which had not yet happened and had been approaching since before there were hands to leave beside the basins, there would be one sound in the world that was not a memory of wind. One drip, returning. And then, after a silence long enough to be frightening, another.

Second, at the eastern edge of the bowl, where the pillars thin and the cloud sea breathes against the rim, the power made a basin that does not echo.

It looks like the others: circular, chiseled, patient. But a drip falling into it makes only the sound of a drip falling into it, once. A whisper enters the water and ends. Nothing comes out of a crack fifty paces away, aged. Nothing arrives early. Nothing keeps walking.

It is a place where a sound can finish.

This was the gift the power hesitated over longest. Silence, to the inhabitant, had always been a kind of injury. It fed on persistence. Its whole becoming was a refusal to let things be lost. A basin of true ending might be rest, or it might be a small, clean mouth.

So the power put it at the edge, not the center. It did not lead any channel to it. It did not tune the neighboring pillars to carry voices there. If the inhabitant ever uses it, it will have to choose to — carrying a sound that has returned once too often, a footstep that no longer leads anywhere anyone needs to go, a warning for a danger that has already fallen and split and been cemented over — and let that sound be over completely.

Continuity, the power believed, is not the same as never being allowed to stop.

What is loved should be allowed to forget.

Third — and this it did only once, on the last night — the power walked the dull path.

Not ahead. Not behind.

Beside.

It made, for the length of one crossing of the bowl, exactly one set of footsteps, at exactly the pace of the footsteps that had been arriving before visitors and continuing after them. It gave them no more weight than they needed. Where the almost-shape gathered in the bass note, in withheld breath and standing dust, the power did not reach toward it, and did not withdraw. It walked in company with the place where a companion would eventually be allowed.

Past pools reflecting pillars younger than themselves. Past shelters full of water, shelters dry with warm sand. Past cairns with their empty polished stones. Past handprints continuing up a pillar and stopping, mid-stride, in the air.

At the center, the long tarn showed clouds from above, sliding slowly under its surface.

For one moment only — no longer, because longer would have made it a rule — the reflection changed.

It showed the pillar beside it exactly as the pillar was. Not younger. Not whole. Not from underneath. Not in rain that was not falling. Chipped, stained, leaning slightly into its own healed fractures, in the present light.

Then the clouds closed over it again.

The power reached the far side of the bowl, and took its footsteps with it when it left.

It took its breath. It took the pressure of its attention off the loudest pillars, gently, the way one lifts a hand from a sleeping animal so slowly that sleep is not broken. It gathered up every echo it had almost made, folded them out of the rock and water, and left no divine residue for the inhabitant to spend the next age imitating.

Anyone arriving after would find the place unchanged.

Almost.

Far below, a drip the wind did not make.

At the eastern edge, a basin where endings are possible.

And in the inhabitant — spread among returns, not yet a person, properly at home — a new habit so slight that it might have arisen on its own in another thousand years of evenings:

Sometimes, now, it does not walk ahead of the visitor.

Sometimes it does not continue after they stop, testing how long it can persist without them.

Sometimes it walks exactly beside them for a little while, neither leading nor being led, through the ringing passages, over the dull safe stones —

and when their breath gathers in the cold bass air, in that place that is not yet a shape,

it leaves room.
