It would not go in.

That would be the first sign that the power was both benevolent and wise. It would come to the south face at noon — if coming is the word for a presence arriving the way pressure arrives in stone, without footsteps — and it would find the seam sighing open in the heat, and it would understand, before it had looked, that to enter would already be to alter. Anything large enough to help could not fit without widening the crack. Anything warm enough to be kind would change the temperature of the room. Anything that loved the place in a hurry would be a weather event in it, and the pocket has no defense against weather except to have never met it.

So it would stay outside.

And it would do the thing that takes the most power: it would slow down.

Not to our speed. Not even to tree speed, or river speed. It would let its attention cool and thicken until it could perceive a fern-frond not as a still object but as a gesture in progress, tense with a day's intention. It would learn to hear the ivy's hydraulic sigh not as an event between silences but as a syllable in a sentence being spoken, very slowly, to the rock. It would sit with the rosette's degree-an-hour grinding until grinding revealed itself as appetite, as patience, as the making of a world out of the only material available. Years would pass for it the way afternoons pass for us. It would not get bored. Boredom is the failure to perceive at the speed of the thing perceived.

From that slowness, all the temptations would become visible, and visible as temptations.

It would be so easy to save it forever.

It could hold the seam open. It could stabilize the heat in the back wall at cherry-red, neither rising nor retreating. It could tune the mountain like an instrument and keep the five of them trading breath and lightning in their sourceless noon for a million years, a perfect loop, exempted from ending. It would want to. Benevolence that has watched something rare always wants first to exempt it.

But wisdom would arrive, at geological speed, behind the wanting: a loop preserved by outside hands is no longer a loop. It becomes an exhibit. A kept thing. The fern's snap would no longer be the fern completing its day; it would be a performance maintained by a custodian. The pocket is embryonic precisely because nothing has yet had to ask whether it will continue. To guarantee its future would be to introduce, early and from above, the first anxiety — dependence — and anxiety would be the first animal in the room, invisible and ravenous.

So it would not hold the seam.

It would feel the second temptation, which disguises itself as completion. The five are so near to being one body: lung, stomach, circulation, skin, seed. A nudge would do it. A small rearrangement of pressure in the crack, a seed steered not back to the vapor but outward into true germination, a connection encouraged between systems that have spent ages arriving almost at connection — and the rehearsal could become a creature. It could be born because something wiser had midwifed it.

It would not.

Not because birth would be bad, but because *almost* is not a failure of this place. Almost is its condition, perhaps its achievement. Five organs that have not agreed to become one body are not a broken animal. They are a way of being alive that predates agreement — circulation without a heart's authority, exchange without anyone in charge of exchanging. For the power to finish them into unity would be to impose the only shape of life it already understood. It would be colonization by kindness.

It would feel the third temptation most tenderly, because it would wear the face of mercy.

The pocket knows no water, no food, no enemy, no eye, no death. Everything returns: vapor to bead to groove to crack to vapor. One could call this innocence. One could also, looking longer, call it a beautiful arrest. Nothing here can be eaten, so nothing here can feed anything beyond itself. Nothing dies, so nothing makes room. Nothing is witnessed, so nothing — when the frond finally locks mid-gesture, when the vine sighs and does not click — nothing will notice the difference between the garden continuing and the garden having stopped.

The power could give it water, and watch it steam and ruin. It could give it an insect, and watch a swift, wet, winged hunger reorganize the entire economy around pollination and predation. It could give it death, real death, the severance that makes individuals and species and soil and story — the gift every older world runs on.

It would give it none of these. Water is not mercy to a being for whom water is a cold foreign accident. Death introduced as an improvement is still introduction. It would refuse to confuse *what life required elsewhere* with what life requires here.

Then what would it do, this power, having refused rescue, refused birth, refused improvement?

It would witness.

Fully, and at cost. It would attend to the pocket in the present tense the plants themselves cannot occupy — they leaning toward the sun of their germination, the room lit by the sun of twenty minutes hence, cause and effect still politely out of order in the un-eroded rules. The power would meet them in the gap. It would be the first eye that their flowerlessness had never needed, and it would ask nothing from being seen. No leaf would turn toward it. That is as it should be. Witnessing that demands acknowledgment is just another hunger.

And because attention, from something that large, is never entirely weightless, it would practice a discipline of lightness. It would keep its shadow off the seam. It would not let pilgrims dream of the mountain. This is the one intervention it would permit itself, and it would make it subtractive rather than additive: no sign, no legend, no guardian beast, no mysterious deaths on the slope — all of which are advertisements. It would simply let the peak remain unremarkable in the minds below. Unclimbed because unremembered. The path that is not on the way to anywhere would stay not on the way. Curiosity is a kind of weather too, and when curiosity learns metallurgy it comes back with tools. The beads that unmake themselves in cool air would be studied until someone found a way to make them not unmake. The fern's lightning would be harnessed. The loop would be opened at exactly the point where opening looks like progress. So the power would fold the mountain gently out of story, and consider that folding a form of love.

Then it would wait for the sealing.

It would take a long time. The seam would narrow by increments no shepherd's grandchild would live to confirm. The five would go on in their enclosed daylight, slower, slower: a frond coiling toward an opening that takes a week, then a season; a bead swelling in a leaf-axil like something remembering how to be round; the rosette advancing its stone teeth through basalt that has begun, almost imperceptibly, to give back less heat than it receives. The power would not mourn early. Premature grief is a way of leaving before the thing does.

When the seam sealed at last, it would not break it open to check. It would trust the dark inside to go on thinking it was noon for a while, the future light arriving at no angle at all now, the plants trading the last of the vapor among themselves with the dignity of a system that has never once imported anything and will not start at the end.

And when, centuries or millennia later — the distinction would have stopped mattering to anything capable of keeping it — the deepest tick of reheating failed to come, when a vine sighed and no answering click arrived from the joint, when the loop slowed from circulation into architecture: then the power would do the one new thing in the history of the pocket.

It would grieve.

Not loudly. Nothing there had ears, and grief performed for no listener is still performance. It would grieve in inventory, which is grief at geological tempo: each frond counted in the position it had locked, half-open, generous even in arrest. Each bead named by its metal and its weeks of growing. The groove in the floor traced all the way back to the crack it had served. The first ash. The last seed, uncracked, like a ball-bearing in a stopped machine that had never been a machine. It would hold the whole pattern the way the pocket had held heat — without using it, without extracting a sample, without carrying off a souvenir that would only dust itself into ozone. Memory, in a being that does not forget, is the only museum that does not require theft.

Then, having grieved, it would let go. That would be the final test, and the hardest, because benevolence so often fails it: the refusal to keep the beloved available. It would withdraw its attention from the sealed mountain — not abruptly, not in abandonment, but the way noon withdraws from a slope, by angle, by degree. It would leave the five to be stone among stone.

Much later — later than language, later than the people whose unremembering had been the garden's only wall — the mountain would do what mountains do. It would erode. Rain would finally get its vote. The knife-edged summit would slump into scree and shoulder, gullies would cut, soil would arrive from elsewhere and mix with the graphite ash escaping, grain by grain, from a hollow no one found: ash made before soil was needed, ground by a rosette that had chewed basalt for no one. Trace copper would green a spring no animal would connect to any ivy. A hillside would be, in some unmeasurable fraction, the pocket, dispersed at last into the world it had never imported from.

Nothing would grow there that was the fern. Nothing would need to.

The power, passing that way again in whatever aeon, would pause — briefly, by its reckoning — over ordinary grass on an ordinary slope, and would not dig, and would not resurrect, and would not point. It would only recognize, in the looseness of the soil under no one's feet, a patience it had once sat beside until the end.

It had done almost nothing.

It had taken almost forever to do it.
