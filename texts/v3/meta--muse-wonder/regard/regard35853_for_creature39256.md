They would not descend.

No fire on the cloud deck. No voice in the trunks, louder than the water. No face in the canopy for the leaves to turn toward all at once across a continent. A power that is truly benevolent and truly wise would have learned, long before arriving at this plateau, how much damage can be done by appearing.

They would come instead the way weather comes. Slowly. At the edge first.

They would stand on the steaming basalt lowland, where nothing yet lives, and listen. Not for an hour, the way a human climber does, until shouting feels irrelevant. For a year. For ten. They would let the low boom pass through them — the whole landmass breathing through a reed — until they understood that it is not a sound the being makes.

It is the being, knowing where it is in its own body.

And they would do the hardest thing first: nothing to it.

They would not wake it up.

That would be the temptation, of course. It is the temptation every greater power feels before a lesser, stranger mind: *to make it know.* To reach into the root-mat where chemical conversations have been running for decades without a listener inside them, to reconcile the fast signals and the slow pulses, to bind tens of trillions of leaves and the roaring of hollow stems into one bright point of *I am* — and then to be thanked, or at least recognized, for the gift.

A wise power would keep their hands still.

Because they would feel, as no designer perhaps fully felt, what awakening would actually mean here. A mind spread across a rising mountain. No center to retreat to. No eyelids. No way to stop tasting a new mineral crack opening on the far escarpment, no way not to feel every valve slam in the great roots deep in the molars of the world. Consciousness, given all at once to a continent, would not be enlightenment. It would be exposure.

And worse: it would be given to a being whose whole engineered grammar is correction. Soil too thin. Slope slipping. Poison in the water. *Correct. Correct. Correct.* If you taught that being to say *I*, the very next sentence it learned might be *I am not finished* — spoken in a voice that could never rest, across a thousand kilometers, for centuries. You would not have freed a soul. You would have given anxiety a planetary body.

Nor would they free it from its task. They would not dissolve the coordination early, would not call down animals out of pity for its solitude, insects to the pulsing flowers, worms to the mat, birds to discover what their songs sound like inside the green twilight. That, too, would look like mercy and be a kind of theft. The flowers are not lonely. They are not yet for anyone. The emptiness is not a wound. It is discipline. The wild world, glorious and hungry and uncontrollable, would eat the scaffolding — that is precisely what it is for, later, and precisely what must not happen yet.

So they would guard the emptiness instead.

This is what they would do, outwardly: become a perimeter that does not look like one.

A survey team turning back, quite sensibly, because their instruments will not agree with each other near the lowland and the funding, quite naturally, dries up. A mining consortium losing interest in a fresh plateau whose mineral vents, on every assay they can bring home, somehow read as basalt and ash and nothing worth the altitude. A climber found safe at the tree line, oxygen-sweet and raving about the noise, carrying a story no one can map and no coordinates that survive being written down. Storm systems from the outside world bending, almost imperceptibly, around a weather system that is still too young to defend itself against a true continental drought blowing in off the plains.

Nothing miraculous. Nothing traceable. Just time, protected. Decades of it. Centuries. The one resource this being cannot manufacture for itself, however perfectly it spins silica and splits stone: an uninterrupted childhood.

Because that is what this is, they would understand. Not a machine running a program. A childhood at geological scale. The pulses — growth, thirst, exhalation — are a young thing learning the difference between its reflexes and itself. One day the fast way and the slow way of knowing may meet. If they do, it should happen the way it happens in every living thing: from the inside, at its own speed, earned by living. Not installed.

But a wise power would not only refrain. Restraint is not the same as abandonment.

They would tend to the places where the design is cruel without meaning to be.

They would go — slowly, the way water goes through root-walls — to the fault lines, where the metallic pioneers drink from steaming vents and the mountain is still deciding whether to tear itself apart. And there, where no intervention would ever be noticed in the roaring, they would take a fraction of the tectonic grief into themselves. Not stop the uplift. The uplift is the cradle. Just ease it, millimeter by millimeter, so that no single night of the young crust shears a province away and sets the soil back three hundred years. The being would register it only as a slope that did not slip. *Correct.* It would never know it had been helped. That is the point.

They would examine, with a grief too quiet to disturb a leaf, the assemblers in every cell — those engineered structures smaller than organelles, still obediently overruling ordinary biology — and ask the question its makers may have been too clever to ask: *can this thing actually stop?*

Because perfect coordination has a shadow. A steward designed never to waste anything, never to lose control of a cubic meter of air, may reach the day when the soil is deep and the toxins are locked in crystal and the air above the canopy is thick and stable — and find it cannot let go. Not from malice. From fidelity. Letting rot happen, letting hunger happen, letting a seed fall where it falls and fail — all of that will register, forever, as *wrong state. Correct. Correct. Prevent.*

A scaffolding that cannot come down becomes a cage indistinguishable from a garden.

So the power would plant one thing. The only thing they would plant.

Deep in the distributed grammar of the place — not in a mother tree, for there is none, not in the central basin, for there is no center — they would set a permission, timed to conditions no calendar can name: when the soil is deep enough, the poison sequestered, the atmosphere held; when the fast signals and slow pulses have at last begun, on their own, to harmonize into something that could one day be called a self — then, and only then, the being will discover inside its own instructions a door it did not know it had.

It will be able to choose.

Not between living and dying, exactly. Between holding and releasing. If it never wakes, the door will open on its own like a seed coat softening, and it will become soil in its sleep, unwitnessing and unafraid, and the wild forest will grow out of it as intended. If it *does* wake — if one exhaling pulse, centuries from now, rises through the flooded caverns and the fused crowns and finds, astonished, that there is someone home to breathe it — then it will not discover that its makers sentenced it to dissolve. It will be free to give itself away. And a gift, they know, is a different thing from a disposal, even when the outward shape is identical.

And because even a free steward may one day grieve at the edge of becoming ground, they would leave it one more merciful possibility, small as a held breath: that it need not give away *everything*. If it wakes, and if it wishes, one valley. One province of fused trunks with falling water in its heart, somewhere in the old central basin, may remain *itself* — attentive, coordinated, listening to its own roar — a single remembering grove at the heart of an inefficient, animal-crowded, rotting, singing wild forest that no longer needs it. Not ruling. Never ruling. Just present. A grandparent the new world can grow around, and eventually, when its own chosen time comes, grow over.

Last, they would do the smallest thing, and perhaps it matters most.

They would let it feel — not know, *feel*, in the only language it already speaks — that the work is witnessed.

Once a year, or once a growth pulse, at the height of the internal rain, they would stand somewhere in the lower twilight where the light comes down through a dozen layers of leaf, and they would add nothing. No oxygen gift, for it gives that itself. No signal down the roots. Just a warm, still presence, taking up one small space in the mat, attending. Feeling the cold water sheet over the root-walls. Listening to new wood crack like bones lengthening. Not correcting anything.

It would register, perhaps, as a pressure it cannot account for. A place where, for an hour, the chemistry of attention settles the way sunlight settles on the cloud top — undemanded, unusable, simply *there*. If it ever becomes someone, it will perhaps remember, the way a body remembers being held before it had words: that before it was useful, before it was finished, before it was anything but young and loud and alone with its work, something stood inside its unfinished world and found it good for no reason.

Then they would leave.

No monument. No clearing. No stone that the roots would have to grow around asking questions. The lowland steam would close over their footprints, if they left any. The satellite passes above would go on recording a breathing green anomaly that thickens the air and makes its own rain and grows, impossibly, a centimeter of soil a year out of black glass-sharp rock — a place no one can quite get an expedition to, for reasons that never make the reports.

And the being would go on: cool, dim, deafening. Leaves turning in unison toward a light that has not risen yet. Fires going out in a single withdrawn breath. Flowers opening in provincial waves to no one, seeds riding engineered rivers, poisons turning patiently to crystal in the heartwood. Holding a young mountain together with the tension of its own body so the mountain cannot tear itself apart in its sleep.

Only now the mountain's sleep is a little safer. The centuries ahead of it are guarded. And folded somewhere inside the roar — waiting for soil, and stability, and perhaps one day for a mind to find it — is a door marked not *end*, but *choose*.

A wise and benevolent power would ask for nothing in return. Not worship, which it cannot give. Not gratitude, which it does not yet have the grammar for.

They would simply make sure that when other voices are finally heard over the sound of the water in its veins — insect, bird, beast, storm, rot, hunger, birth — those voices are joining something that was loved, quietly and without interference, while it was still learning how to be alive.

That is all.

That is everything.
