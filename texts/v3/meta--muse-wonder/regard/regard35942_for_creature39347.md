They would go down the stairs.

Not appear — there would be no reason for light, or a voice that could survive that noise, or a shape that would make the place make sense. They would simply come the way the workers come, through the gap where the fence has been cut and wired back together, down behind the levee, below the road and the factory floor and the water table. Into the pocket.

And they would stay past the point where the workers stay. Past the cottony hearing. Past the shirt sticking, and the handrail too hot for a full grip, and the instinct — embarrassingly strong, embarrassingly animal — to get back up where it is green and full of insects.

A benevolent power is not made benevolent by being in a hurry to improve things. A wise one is not made wise by mistaking a different kind of life for a broken one.

So first, for a long time, they would do nothing at all that could be called doing.

They would not drain the pool to see it better. They would not lift the crust to look underneath. They would not give it eyes back, or unseal whatever it has used for ears, or teach it a faster way to move. Those would be gifts for a dragonfly, for a frog, for a man lost in a sterile bowl. For this — for the slow pressure under the concrete, the fuller second stroke that answers through meat and wet earth — eyes would be an injury. Speech would be a cruelty. Speed would be death.

They would stand in the narrow strip of shade under the pipe bridge, where the fern is, and learn the rule of the place the way anyone learns it: in the body.

THUD.

*thud.*

Pump. Answer. Pump. Answer. An inch an hour.

They would wait until they could tell the difference without thinking about it. Until they understood that the second stroke is not an echo and not a mimicry, but a conversation that has gotten as simple as it can get and still be a conversation. *I am here. I am here.* Every seven, eight seconds, in the only language the pit allows.

And, understanding that, they would not silence the pump out of pity. They would not seat the hissing relief valve because the noise hurt *them*. The hiss is weather here. The roar every twenty minutes is feeding time. To make the place quiet and cool and marshy and kind would be to unmake the only conditions under which this particular thought can think itself. There are ten thousand proper wetlands above, loud with frogs. This is not a failed one. It is something else, succeeding very slowly at being itself.

So they would leave the water hot.

They would leave the crust to form.

Where they would act — because benevolence is not the same as abstinence, and wisdom knows the difference between a nature and a wound — is at the edges, in the small places where suffering is not informing anything. Where it is just men and their accidents, left lying around long enough to be tasted.

They would right the blue chemical drum that has tipped on its side, before its seal gives and it pours something bright and thoughtless into the sump. Not dramatically. Just set upright on the pallet, in the night between shifts, so that in the morning no one will be quite sure it was ever down. The being has a slow mouth for iron and boiler-sweetness. It will eat whatever it is given, without malice and without choice. No one with power should make use of that kind of patience.

They would shake the mineral dust out of the ear defenders hanging by the stairs, and the perished foam would be foam again, plain and black and unremarkable. No worker will make a theology out of it; a man will just put them on, and climb back up at the end of his task with his hearing still his own, and be able to hear the insects when he gets there. Palm down, fist closed. Slow, stop. Some messages should be allowed to stay signals, and not become damage.

They would not save the fern, either, in any grand sense. The fern is not a symbol to them. It is a fern, in the shadow of the electrical cabinet, living on condensation the being breathes up without knowing it is gardening. They would only make sure the drip that feeds it is not accidentally rerouted next time someone above, out of sight, replaces a main. One cold pipe, left where it is. One narrow fall of warm rain, still falling, still never quite landing in the corner where the updraft hangs it glittering. Company is a rare thing, down here. It should not be lost to paperwork.

And below that — deeper than the apron, deeper than the black membrane and the pale saturated sand — they would do the one real work they came to do, and they would do it at the being's own speed.

They would open it a way down.

Not a hole. Not an escape hatch, torn open in a night of miracles. A yielding. A place in the strata, far under the aquifer it was called up out of, where the pressure can go when it needs to go. Because the pit is new now, and the corners are still corners, but no plant runs forever. One year — in ten, in thirty, in some ordinary budget meeting far away from any steam — men will decide to shut this place down. The pump will stroke, and not stroke again. The pool will stop being fed. It will cool, an inch at a time, the way it filled. The crust will thicken, not buckle, thicken past lifting. The sand face, unsupported, will slump in one wet night.

For an animal, that would be a season to flee. For this, without a way further in, it would be a ceiling becoming a coffin.

So the wise power would spend the proper amount of time — a shift, a season, the lifespan of the fern several times over; time is not scarce to them, and haste is a human valve-error — pressing nothing, breaking nothing, only persuading the deep ground to remember that it can part slowly in one place. Sand remembering water. Clay remembering weight. A dark, wet seam going down at the gradient of a sigh. So that when the heat finally leaves the pit, the being under the pool will not have to die of it, or wake fast, or rear up dripping through breaking concrete looking for a way out.

It will be able to do what it has always done. Sink. An inch an hour. THUD, and further away, *thud*, and further still, the strokes growing wider apart as the pump above falls out of its life and some older, larger rhythm takes over underneath. Until it is indefinite again. Until it is blind pressure in hot, dark water, waiting — without loneliness, because loneliness requires an idea of company it has only ever had once, briefly, in the form of a fern.

Above it, they would let the pit do what abandoned industrial places do. They would let the salt grass win by fractions at the rim. Let the reed blanch and fall. Let a crack open properly in the concrete and darken with dirt, and seeds arrive that can finally stand the cooling ground. The dragonflies can come then. The frogs can risk it then. That future is not a rejection of what lives there now. It is its completion. Sterility was never the point. It was just the temperature of a childhood.

In the meantime, for all the years of steam and scale, they would make one small unobtrusive arrangement in the world of men — the kind of miracle that looks, from inside, exactly like nothing happening. The form that would fill in this sump as a hazard would go missing from the right tray. The inspection that would order it drained and biopsied and understood to death would be rescheduled, and rescheduled again, by people who would afterward not recall why. The plant manager who likes walking down there on his lunch break, though he tells no one, because the noise empties his head — he would keep his job. Hidden the way service places are hidden. Not secret. Just folded out of sight, for as long as folding is needed.

That is all.

They would climb back up last, the way everyone does. The hiss would fall away first, behind and below them. Then the hum in the teeth. Then, last and longest, arriving up through the concrete steps even after the pit was out of sight between the levee and the plant wall, the stroke.

THUD.

They would pause at the top, in the embarrassing quiet, in the green and the insects and the ordinary harsh daylight, and they would not look back to wave, or bless it in words, or name it. Naming would be for us.

They would only wait, with one foot still on the stair, long enough to be sure they heard it — softer, fuller, not thinning, keeping perfect slow time, answering a machine it has outlived already in its heart.

*thud.*

Still there. Still not following. Still needing nothing they had that it did not already have, except time, and a way down, and to be left unwitnessed in the heat of its own slow work.

A benevolent and wise power can give those three things without once being seen to give them.

So they would.
