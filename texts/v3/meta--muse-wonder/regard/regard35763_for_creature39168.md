The power would arrive the way everything else in that room arrives: a little late, a little displaced, and not quite in its body.

It would not come down through the hill in glory. Glory would be a kind of hardness. Light falling from a source, a voice arriving on time, a shape with one shadow — the room could not bear that much finish all at once, and the thing in the center least of all. It would harden around the visit the way new crystal hardens around a thumbprint. It would learn the wrong lesson: that to be noticed is to be decided.

So the power would make itself peripheral first.

It would come as a dampness in the grass above. As a reason for walkers, that afternoon, to turn aside half a step before the dip and think of something else — not forbidden, never forbidden, only gently uninterested. As a coolness at the mouth of the entrance hole, where a visitor's shadow lies flattened and waiting. It would look at the shadow a long moment, with something like respect.

And then it would leave its own shadow outside too.

That would be the first gift, though neither of them would call it that: to enter unaccompanied by consequence. No long dark shape thrown ahead. No eclipse. It would wriggle through on its belly if it had to — and being wise, it would have to, and being benevolent, it would not mind the indignity. It would come in smaller than it is, and slower than it is, and with its eyes mostly not looking.

Inside, it would not go to the center.

It would sit at the margin, where everything living is allowed to be, among the chalk frogs and the rehearsing fern. It would put its back — if it kept a back — to the soft wall, and feel the wall consider its weight and politely not set around it. It would let its nails soften. It would let a small scratch it had been carrying for a few centuries un-bleed on its knuckle, and it would not call that a miracle. In this place, revision is just housekeeping.

It would wait to be noticed obliquely.

Which would not take long. The being in the bright column has had so few fixed shapes to study. The power would feel the attention arrive the way you feel warmth on one side of your face: a paleness gathering inside the paleness nearby, a faint pair of eyes opening in the wrong place and deciding, sincerely, to shut again. Feather-gills lifting at a throat that is not quite a throat. Curiosity with no appetite behind it yet.

The power would hold very still — not the stillness of a statue, which is another kind of finishing, but the stillness of an animal that has decided not to be afraid in the correct way. It would let itself be practiced on.

This is what no one else who finds the room can safely offer, and what the power, being wise, knows the being actually needs. Not teaching. Not rescue. Not a name — a name would be a small, kind sealing, and those are the most dangerous kind. It needs a large thing to try on that will not be harmed by the trying.

So when the being slips, briefly, into the salamander and snaps at empty air, the power does not correct it. When it runs the cricket's landing ahead of its jump, the power does not restore the proper order of cause. And when, presently, shyly, it begins to inhabit the visitor at the margin — misjudging distance through very old eyes, forgetting which mouth is which, opening that mouth in silence at the same instant as the bats — the power does not push it out.

It lets itself be borrowed.

For a moment the being is vast. For a moment it has hands that could unmake hills, a memory of weather systems, a knowledge of every finished cave in the world and what eyeless things endure in them. It is terrible, the size of it, how easily forms could be closed forever, how easily hunger could be aimed correctly at last. The being holds predation up to the light inside a body that has had ten thousand years to learn predation and has laid it down.

And there — this is the whole visit, folded into a breath the ceiling almost keeps time with — it learns the other shape power can take. Not in words. Words arrive several seconds late in that room and would come from the wrong corner anyway. It learns it in the muscle, in the borrowed marrow: that strength does not have to finish what it touches. That you can hold a small, soft, undecided thing in your attention for a very long time and *not* decide it. That mercy is not a later correction to power. It is power, practicing, before cruelty has set.

The being startles itself with the gentleness it finds in there. It withdraws at once, delighted and frightened, back to its column of gathered light. The dust reappears a hand's breadth to the left. The bats, disturbed, spiral through the brightness and drink, and their bellies glow, and for a while the center glows brighter where their wings, and the power's borrowed breathing, stirred it.

Then the power would do the things that look like nothing.

It would not seal the entrance, and it would not widen it. Sealing is a cave's future; widening is a valley's. Both may still be true, and it is not the power's to choose between them — choosing would be the one unforgivable kindness.

Instead it would ask the hill, very quietly, to go on being forgettable. Not hidden by force. Just unmemorable, the way the dip in the grass already is: a place the foot slides past, a place the mind keeps a soft spot over. It would smooth out, behind itself, the faint pressure of its own coming, so that no lesser and more eager powers — collectors of wonders, menders of unfinished things, gods who love a project — could follow the scent of attention back down. Whatever has noticed this room out of love must be prepared to misremember the way, on purpose, forever. The power would bind its own memory loosely, the way the walls hold a wound: the shape kept, the details mercifully undecided. It would remember *that* it had been somewhere bright. It would allow itself to forget where.

It would check the three bowls in the floor without touching them. The dry one, the one reflecting a different ceiling, the one that fills only when unwatched. It would leave them exactly as mysterious as it found them. But on its way out, stooping at the margin where the little structures stand, it would do one small, revocable thing.

It would build a threshold.

Not well. On purpose, and also not entirely on purpose — for a power that has been finished a long time, building badly is a discipline. A few pebbles, one of them still growing. An arch spanning two inches this time, leading nowhere, balanced just imperfectly enough that it will need rebuilding. Beside the mud towers with their too-small doorways, beside the nest woven from a memory of a larger bird, beside the steps the floor made unnecessary. A contribution to the curriculum. *I also do not know the scale of what I am sheltering. I also make entrances I cannot use.*

It would leave it unfinished, mid-slump, so the being would have the pleasure of finding it later and revising it without being told.

And then it would go, because staying is the other temptation. To stay would be to give the room a weather, a witness, a center of gravity other than its own. The being leans toward the entrance with its attention as the power leaves, the way its three transparent grass shoots lean, homesick for a world it is not ready to survive being singular in. The power would not call it back and would not call after it to follow. It would wriggle out into the ordinary day, where light falls and burns and comes from somewhere, and it would stand up in the grass and let its waiting shadow rush back to its feet all at once — one shadow, correct, final, almost a relief, almost a grief.

Behind it, under the hill, nothing would have changed. That is how you would know the visit had been benevolent.

Except that the next morning — if mornings are what those are, in there — the fern would uncurl as it always uncurls, rehearsing a plant. The pillbugs would roll up and rock, forgetting and remembering. The salamander would miss the cricket an inch to the side. But one of the mud towers, rebuilt in the night half an inch from where it slumped, would have a doorway very slightly wider than before. Not wide enough for any body in the room. Not yet. Only wide enough to suggest that doorways are not always made to the size of the thing that lives inside.

And sometimes, when the being lifts hands it is trying on in the bright empty center, there would be among its four faint disagreeing shadows a fifth — very small, very patient, arriving a few seconds late — that closes gently after the others have closed, as if something large had once sat at the edge of the light and learned again, briefly, how not to finish what it loves.
