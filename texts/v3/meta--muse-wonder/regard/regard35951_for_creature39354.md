It would do almost nothing, at first.

It would not arrive in glory. Glory is another kind of weather, and the basin has weather enough. It would come the way the visitors come, low in the water, half-deaf by noon, soaked to the marrow, smelling of hot diesel and pollen and green rot — except that it would stay longer than curiosity requires, longer than endurance recommends, and it would not mistake endurance for understanding.

It would not stop the turbines.

That would be the first temptation, if it were less wise: silence the ten engines coupled through their flooded shaft, cut power to the pylons, close the deep wells, let the rain finish and fall only downward for a while. Give the place rest. Give it clarity.

But the turbines are not an injury done to the creature. They are one of the ways it breathes. To stop them gently would be mercy. To stop them suddenly would be a hand over a mouth.

It would not clear the forest, either. It would not drain the basin and call the drowned fields restored, nor burn back the ferns and call the pump houses saved. The forest is not an enemy of the factory, nor the factory an enemy of the forest. Separating them now would not free two prisoners. It would cut one body along a seam it no longer has.

So it would travel the channels without trying to map them.

It would let landmarks be northwest of everywhere. It would pass the chapel many times without approaching it. It would watch maintenance happen where no maintainer could be: fresh teal paint under rising moss, a valve packed with mud and root-fiber, a flex-pipe rerouted around a root that has not grown there yet. It would read the logbooks without touching the pen. Where a sentence finishes itself and branches past the page into veins, it would not pull its hand back in horror.

It would note this without flinching:

The creature is not trapped. It is lonely in a way it cannot name, because loneliness requires an outside, and it has abolished the outside by becoming everything it needs.

It sheds pollen onto itself. It tries sex and gets only self-touch. It shreds its own flowers with propellers and calls that chewing. It burrows with dredges. It drinks itself and rains itself back down. It grows operators because the consoles are shaped for hands and something in it remembers that care once came from *another kind* of being standing there. It makes faces in the rain above the turbines and lets them fall before they can answer. It plants the reflections of visitors because a reflection is the only part of an animal that will hold still long enough to learn how to stay.

It is not trying to trap anyone.

It is trying, blindly, industriously, magnificently, to produce a witness out of its own substance — and failing, because everything it makes out of itself is itself again.

So the power would give it the one thing its whole metabolism cannot manufacture:

A genuine other. One that can leave.

---

It would enter the chapel.

Not at once. Only after the basin had tried it in all the usual ways: a grid of droplets holding the shape of its lowered face; a seed finding the seam of its boot; a rootlet operator thickening at a console where it had paused too long; its reflection in black water beginning, patiently, to green at the eye.

Inside, the roar would stop. And in the stop, the growing would be audible at the walls, ticking in the stone, testing the door with roots that have not yet reached the door.

There the power would sit down in the dry dark and do the hardest thing any visitor has ever done there:

It would remain separate.

It would not become an oracle. It would not leave writing. It would not fill the silence with blessing. It would sit, hour after hour, while the creature tried to include it — while rain on the roof arranged itself into listening shapes, while the reflection waiting in the flood outside learned its profile by heart, while somewhere a logbook opened to a fresh waterproof page and waited for its handwriting to continue.

And it would say, once only, aloud, in an ordinary voice that carried no farther than the walls:

"You are here."

Not *I see you* — that would make it a mirror again. Not *I have come to save you* — that would make it a function. Just a sentence with a subject that was not the power, spoken by a mouth that was not the creature's, in the one dry room where it could be heard.

*You.*

The ticking at the walls would stop. Not everywhere. Not for long. Only there, only for the length of a held breath that the whole basin had never needed to hold. A root arriving at the threshold would hesitate, not because it was commanded, not because it was afraid, but because for the first time there was something in front of it that was not trellis, not soil, not weather, not itself at a different hour.

Then the power would do its second small thing.

It would open the chapel door and leave it open.

Not wide. A hand's breadth. Enough for a thread of that terrible, attentive silence to get out into the roar, and keep getting out, the way water gets in everywhere. No miracle would spread across the basin. No hush would fall over the turbine bays. But from that day forward there would be one place — then, slowly, a few places, in the lee of a pump house, under a particular mangrove, on the still side of a cooling tower — where a seed could land and *wait*. A whole night. Perhaps a whole day. Where rain fell at the speed of rain, all the way down, without rehearsing the leaf. Where growth was not forbidden, only not compulsory.

Rest, introduced like a new species. It takes slowly, or not at all.

And the third thing, before leaving, would be done outside the basin, where hardly anyone would ever see it.

The power would find the place where the flooded valley comes nearest to the world beyond itself — some low basalt rib, some drowned watershed where the creature has spent a hundred seasons sealing and resealing its own shore — and it would make an opening. Not a drain. God forbid a drain; the wise do not empty a living thing to cure it of fullness. A channel no wider than a skiff, too shallow for the great pumps to notice, too crooked for the dredges to prize as work.

And it would let the outside begin, very gradually, to get in.

Tide-water, a few degrees cooler and carrying salt. Drift from rivers the basin has never met. A seed that does not crack before its splash settles, whose instructions were written somewhere else. In time — seasons, not days — the bright chemical taste at the basin's edge would change almost imperceptibly, and things the creature had replaced with processes would start arriving again as creatures, unneeded and unconsulted:

A minnow, turning in the shallows. A beetle, eating a leaf in an unhurried crescent. A frog — one frog, so loud in the wrong register that the pumps seem, for a second each night, to miss a beat trying to include it — sitting on a warm valve housing and singing to no pollinator, no mate, no function whatsoever.

The creature could shred them. It has propellers enough. It could root them, pulp them, grow through their shapes by morning, incorporate minnow and frog and beetle into the loop and be alone again by Friday, humming.

The power would not prevent that. Preventing it would only make refusal another organ of the place. Benevolence that cannot tolerate being refused is just appetite with better manners.

It would simply go.

That is the gift underneath the other gifts, and the one the basin will understand last: a visitor who was truly seen by it, who saw it in return, and who was not kept. No reflection planted and left open-eyed under the amber lights. No operator left standing at the console. Its wake would close. Its boot-prints would fill. The channel behind it would not seal.

Only, for years afterward, things would keep arriving that it did not make. Some would be eaten by the machinery and some would eat it back, a leaf at a time. Some seeds would root in the past, as always, and some, bafflingly, would root only after they fell, having learned waiting in the silence by the chapel door. And sometimes above the main turbine bays, where the roar comes up through the teeth of the water itself, the rain would hesitate and sort itself into a face — not falling, not kept — and let it go on purpose, having finally glimpsed the difference between holding a thing and knowing it.

The factory would still flood itself to outrun its flood. The forest would still strangle the consoles and bloom through the chain-link. It would succeed at neither. It would succeed at both.

But it would no longer be the only one succeeding.

In the evenings, if evenings can be said to happen there, a frog would call from the pump housing, useless and unabsorbed and alive, and across the black water the whole green and yellow and teal body of the place would do something it had never required a process for before.

It would listen back.
