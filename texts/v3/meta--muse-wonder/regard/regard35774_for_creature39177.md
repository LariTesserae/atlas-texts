They would not still the mountain.

That would be the first temptation for power: to lay a hand over the whole uplifted plateau and say *enough* — to let the cracks close, let the dome settle into a summit, let dust fall and rain follow it down, let soil begin. To make it habitable. To make it kind.

A wise power would feel that temptation, and refuse it.

For this place is not a ruined country waiting to be repaired. It is a country before ruin or repair have any meaning. It has not failed to become a mountain. It is becoming one, in the hot, black, violent privacy above the dust. To finish it early would not be mercy. It would be theft — of the thousand slow years in which stone learns to be landscape, in which rivers have not yet cut their arguments into it, in which life has not signed its name because the page itself is still rising out of the deep.

Nor would they rescue the walkers from it, wholesale.

That would be the second temptation: water in abundance, air thickened, ground made trustworthy underfoot, houses with floors that keep their agreement with the walls. But the walkers are not lost here. That is the hard thing to understand from below. They are not a village that has forgotten how to build a village. They are people who have read this place accurately and answered it with the only honest habitation it allows: habitation by walking.

To give them settlement would not save them. It would unmake the particular form of attention that is now, in their soles and palms and widened eyes, the truest knowledge anyone has of the plateau.

So the power does almost nothing.

Almost.

What they do cannot be mapped, owned, dammed, mined, or inherited. Anything else would bring the lowlands up through the dust-sea with charters and thirsty armies and a plan.

They put a little water where a cairn throws its brief shadow at first dark.

Not a spring. A spring could be claimed. Not a well; a well implies a floor and a future. Only a beading, now and then, on the leeward stones of a ring someone built correctly and left without scattering — a palmful, sometimes two, cold for a mineral instant despite the warm rock. It is gone when the shadow goes. It cannot be carried except in the body. A walker who learns of it learns also not to speak of it as a place. It is more like weather that has taken a personal interest.

They make the warning come earlier for the old ones.

Not by healing the inner ear — age is not an enemy they are in the habit of defeating — but by giving the body a second route for the news. In the last years, when balance begins to come late, the ringing of stressed shards arrives also as a faint ache in the teeth, in the healed places of old fractures, in the fillings and the fingertips. A season's grace, sometimes two. Enough time to know what is happening. Enough time to restack one last cairn, to recite one's reports face to face to whoever comes behind, and to make the descent through the dust while descent is still a choice.

And for the ones who choose not to descend, or who are given to the plateau too far from any other hands, they see to it that the burial does not become anonymous quite so quickly. Not an inscription — stone here unmakes inscriptions as readily as cairns. Only this: where a walker has been laid in fresh rubble and marked once, the salt blooms there a night longer than salt blooms anywhere else. White needles at dusk. Perfect at midnight. Gone, as everything is gone, by the next heave.

But seen, meanwhile, from a long way off, if you know how to look through your feet and your eyes at once.

Below, in the brown dusk, the power does even less, and it matters more.

They trouble the dreams of the comfortable, very slightly.

A magistrate who would send up forty porters for copper before spring finds himself waking thirsty three nights running with his mouth split, though he has drunk his fill. A merchant who would double the price of sulfur flowers and halve the ration-weight of the climbers dreams instead, all night, of carrying something sealed and sloshing and unbearably precious up a slope of glass that will not hold still — and wakes with his accounts unchanged, but his orders quietly moderated. No visions. No commandments. Only a persistent, embodied suspicion in the lowlands that the people who walk up there are not a resource, and that reports brought down at the cost of breath should be paid for as though breath were part of the price. Because it is.

They keep the route illegible to anyone unwilling to walk it slowly.

No force stops an expedition. But dust sighs at the wrong hour for men in columns. Cairns that would guide a company with carts seem, days later, to have been restacked into meanings only two walkers meeting face to face can finish for each other. The pass that existed last spring exists still — for feet, for patience, for loads no heavier than a body can love for a week. For anything greedier, the plateau simply continues to tell the truth about itself too fast to be exploited.

And they learn the gesture.

That is perhaps the strangest part, and the most certain: that a benevolent and wise power, arriving at the center where no summit has yet decided itself, where the stars are hard and close and the lowlands are falling slowly away in the dark, would not announce itself. There would be no voice in the cracks, which already breathe hot air enough. No sign in the sky, which is busy being a sky for the first time at this height.

They would only, before anything else — before eating, if they ate; before sleeping, if they slept; before crossing a crack with that dull red suggestion at its bottom — do what the walkers do.

Put a palm flat down.

How warm here. How restless. How near to breathing out.

And having taken, through the sole patience of touch, the mountain's recent and possibly already false report, they would rise and move uphill, calmly, not running, until the ringing stopped — content at last to leave perfection to the sulfur flowers, to leave maps to gossip, to leave the future mountain to its own long arrival, and to guard, for the brief human interval that needs it, nothing more and nothing less than the right of everything up here to keep becoming without having to settle in order to be loved.
