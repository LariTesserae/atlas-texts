It would come quietly.

Not in the lightning — there is already too much lightning here, too regular, too engineered, and the crews have learned to read it the way farmers Below read cloud. Not in a parting of the rain. The rain is the work, here. You do not interrupt a making to announce that you approve of it.

A benevolent and wise power would know that first. It would know restraint before mercy. It would hang, if hanging is the word, above that white ordinary noon and that impossible ocean at the top of the world, and it would watch for a very long time before it touched anything at all.

Because the temptation would be terrible.

To fill it. Oh, to fill it. To end that loud biological silence in a single morning: birds working the wind over the swells, white wings against the gray, fish turning silver under the rain-hammered surface, weed tangling, rot and brine and guano, the whole funk and clamor of a real coast arriving at once like a held breath let go. To give the people in the low black hulls something to answer them at dusk. To hear them shout, for once, not numbers into the deluge, but with surprise.

It would not do it.

Wisdom is knowing that an unfinished thing is not the same as a broken thing. The High Sea is not empty because it has been neglected. It is empty because it is being born in the right order. First the basin. First the chemistry. First the currents learn their habits and the green learns to hold. Mouths come later, or they come as ruin. To seed life here too early, out of pure pity for the lonely, would be to make the pity into a catastrophe and call it love.

So it does not add a single fish. It does not spare a single smuggled beetle. It does not stop the rain, or calm the uplift, or make the basalt blunt its knives. It lets the cataracts roar. It lets the boundary at the rim pour fifty meters off the edge of the world and curve, steaming, back into itself. It lets the maps go wrong.

It watches the work, instead, until it understands where the work is blind.

And the work, for all its committees and sequences and centuries of patience, has one blind place, shaped exactly like a person.

Her.

Pick one — the powers that are truly wise always pick one, because the general case is how cruelty hides. A rim-worker, long-timer, nine years up. Hands that were broken once on new cable and healed glassy. She can feel an uplift coming an hour before it arrives, a slow shrug rising through miles of unfinished water, as pressure blooming in the healed bones. She tastes the southern gyre off a drop on her tongue and logs the calcium before the instruments wake. Water stands too tall on her forearms after a watch. Below deck she speaks in hull-voice, barely a murmur, and signs with wet gloves: *island split again. Name holds. Tell them name holds,* though she knows the chart Below will quietly reassign it a number.

She keeps no vial of old seawater. She stopped being able to bear it two rotations ago. The brine-and-rot smell of a living coast comes up out of the little glass throat like a room full of death-and-life, and her thinned gut turns over, and her eyes water, and she is ashamed, because Below is supposed to be home.

She has asked for another extension.

She already knows how this ends if it goes right. That is the part no one Below will say to her face, and she has heard it anyway through a hull at night, in the way rotations are discussed when long-timers are not in the compartment. When the rain becomes merely frequent. When the salt arrives for good. When the rafts tangle and thicken and begin, at last, to stink like a shore. When something is permitted, in sequence, in committee, to eat.

Then the midwives go down. Sterile hands, clean boots, empty guts: there is no place in a food chain for people who were trusted precisely because they were not a link in one. She will descend by stages to the pale dust, to thick air she will have to relearn like a diver surfacing too fast, to food she may no longer be able to digest, to coasts loud with birds she cannot listen to without grieving for a silence nobody else remembers being loud.

The recipe has a place for reefs that will not exist for eighty years. It has no place for her.

That is where the power acts. Not on the sea. On the margin the plan forgot.

It does not heal her hands back to what they were. That would be another kind of exile, tidier and crueler — to unmake the only fluency she has earned here. It does not make her young, or prophet-legible, or able to live forever waist-deep in ten-degree water with the kelp talking back. It does something smaller, and harder, and entirely hers.

It gives her back the choice of what her body means.

Quietly, over a season, in ways no instrument quite catches and no committee can quite log: her digestion, which had narrowed to the sterile and the bland, begins — not to revert, but to widen. As if a door she thought the sea had sealed in her were in fact a door, with a handle on her side. She could go Below, now, and learn a living coast slowly, the way she once learned altitude, in stages, without it poisoning her. She could also stay, and when the first sanctioned mouths arrive she will not be a contamination vector to them; the strange, clean, glassy adaptation in her will read to the new ecosystem not as a wound in the sequence, but as a bridge written into it. First draft, yes. Provisional, yes. But drafts, a wise power knows, are sometimes how the final text learns what it meant to say.

Neither future is pushed. Both become possible. That is the whole miracle, and it will never once be provable.

And it does one more thing, even smaller, which is not for her body at all.

It keeps the names.

Not on the charts Below — charts are for navigation, and navigation must be allowed to change. In the sea. In the way the edited water, which already holds a crest a second too long and beads too tall on a rail, begins to hold, very faintly, the cadence of things said over it. Numbers shouted flat into rain. Hull-voice murmurs through steel at night. Grandmothers. Lost shipmates. Towns in the dust that no wave will ever reach. When the currents finally settle into their habits — and it will be long after her watch, long after her lifetime, which the power does not extend by a single theatrical day — the gyres will turn with a rhythm in them that is not quite weather.

And much later, when the rain is only frequent, and the green rafts have seeded and spread and begun to smell of life, and wings come at last in their appointed sequence, the first calls across the water at dusk will have something in them that no committee selected for.

A spacing. A call, and a listening space after it, shaped like two voices: one for the deck, one for below.

She will not hear it. The power does not cheat her of being mortal; that too would be a refusal to take her seriously. But on her last ordinary watch — no sign, no vision, the rain hammering white on the swell-tops and thinning to silver — she leans over the rail with the sampling cup, tastes the blank metallic water, and pauses.

Something in the eastern province. Salt, a fraction steadier than it should be. Holding.

She logs it. Her healed hands ache, pleasantly, with an uplift still an hour away. Below deck someone signs to her through the hatch, wet gloves unhurried: *come eat. storm coming. big one.*

And for once, at dusk, straining out of pure human reflex for a call that has never come, she does not feel answered. That would be too soon, and this power loves her too well to lie to her about the hour.

She feels, instead, accompanied in the listening.

Which, on the High Sea, in a world with no other life in it, is not nothing.

It is, in fact, the only thing that was ever missing from the recipe.
