The power comes the way weather comes to a summit — without announcing itself as a visitor.

It does not descend into the Cup in glory. Glory would displace water. It does not take a shape with hands, at first, because hands imply repair, and it has lived long enough to be suspicious of its own wish to repair things.

It listens.

One breath of the mountain. Seven minutes. Another.

It slows itself until it can hear the lake properly: not as noise, but as grammar. The millers clicking out of unison. The grazers grinding glass into paste. The piston-eels sighing in their lime tubes. The mussels waiting, open, for the pressure to turn. Far above them all, the small muscular adjustments of the raft-snails, holding the ceiling of the water from underneath.

It understands, with the part of wisdom that is older than kindness, that this is not many beings living in a place.

It is one being, distributed.

The Cup is its body. The spring is its blood. The bacterial film is its slow greenless hunger. Every animal is an organ that has learned to walk around inside the whole, sealing the leak it feeds beside, pumping the silt it excretes, fusing its own dead into the floor. Nothing cooperates. Everything contributes. The distinction matters.

So the power's first act is refusal.

It refuses to fix the eastern leaks.

That is the great temptation: it could close those shattered fractures in an afternoon. Stone to stone, sealed clean, the little sea made safe forever. But forever would starve it. The leaks are not wounds only. They are mouths, currents, delivery routes. The eels seal them because springwater moves through them. The mussels wedge into the moving plates because movement brings food. To make the bowl perfect would be to make it still, and stillness here is death wearing the mask of safety.

It refuses to bring green.

No moss for the sharp rock. No weed to soften the walls. No kindly algae to brighten the shallows and feed the young in abundance. Green things would grow toward the light faster than the slow mineral world could answer. They would change the taste of the water, loosen the iron logic of the food chain, outcompete the amber films that know how to eat stone. The Cup has survived its accident of sunlight by remaining, at heart, a cave turned upward. The power will not punish it for not becoming a meadow.

It refuses to rescue the grazers.

From above, an hour at a time, the iron-dark predators empty their ballast and descend like presses. It would be so easy to nudge one aside. To hiss a warning through the water. To make the Cup gentle. The power watches the grazer's padded feet scrambling, watches its mouth-stones grind twice at nothing, and does not intervene, because its benevolence is not squeamishness. The predators are also the lake thinking. Their crushed leavings join the reef. Their patience is part of the tempo. A world where nothing is eaten is not mercy. It is interruption.

It refuses, hardest of all, to be known.

These creatures navigated darkness for ages before the summit split and let the sky in. They meet revelation with shell. A voice in the water, a face at the surface, a sudden significance — what would they do with it but incorporate it badly, gather around it, alter their feeding stations, mistake wonder for current? The power loves them too exactly to become an event in their lives.

And so, inside the Cup, it does — almost — nothing.

Almost.

Once, in the fifty-first year of the lake, there is a winter colder than the mountain has learned to expect. Ice begins at the rim at night and creeps toward the center, locking the snail-rafts into a crust they cannot loosen. If it closes, the surface will tear when the plate next rises. The power does not warm the lake. Warmth would wake hungers out of season. It merely stands, invisible, in the thin wind above the summit for three nights, breaking the wind's coherence with a presence the air must go around. The ice stays at the edges, a white rim, beautiful and allowed. The center remains open. The snails loosen. The bellows breathes.

Once, a tremor comes up from below that is not the seven-minute breath but its violent ancestor, the kind of shrug that made the Cup and could unmake it. The central plate lifts too far, too fast. Bubbles turn to a roar. The power puts its attention under the plate like a hand under a lifting lid — not holding it down, never that — and lets the excess pressure spend itself into its own vast patience, second by second, until the rise is a hand's width again. Hammer-mussels clang. Eels stroke as one. Nothing in the lake knows it was spared. The floor settles, squealing against shell, and an old grazer keeps grinding through the aftershock because the film after a pulse tastes rich.

That is all it permits itself within the water: to absorb surplus catastrophe. Not to remove danger. To return danger to the scale the being was built to survive.

Outside the rim, it is busier, and less merciful.

For the Cup's true vulnerability is not geology. It is notice.

So the power folds the mountain slightly out of the habits of the world below. Not invisibility — that is a glamour, and glamours invite seekers. Just unremarkability. A survey photograph develops with glare exactly over the summit. A climbing route described in a guidebook leads, through a copying error the power does nothing to correct, to a neighboring and much duller peak. A mineral prospector with copper in his instruments gets a headache at altitude, a humane and genuine headache, and turns back irritated at his own body, remembering the day ever after as a failure of fitness rather than fate. A drone loses interest in a thermal updraft. Clouds, far below, keep their counsel.

It guards against gifts as carefully as against theft. No well-meaning hand will release fish here. No bird is encouraged to settle and shit richer life into this poor, exact water. Invaders arrive in the world on love as often as on greed; the power turns both aside at the rim with the same quiet firmness.

And beneath — because it thinks in longer spans than the lake's fifty bright years — it tends the dark.

The bowl will fail someday. Not soon, perhaps, but stone that faulted once will fault again. The sunlit Cup is a chapter, not the book. Deep under the summit, the old plumbing still runs: flooded fissures, blind chambers, water tasting of iron where no light has ever been asked to bless it. The power walks those passages in the way a thought walks a sleeping mind, and makes sure the ways down remain open. If the rim gives way, if the little sea pours out into the clouds in one shining ruin of an afternoon, the millers and eels and crawlers must be able to retreat, generation by stunned generation, into the cave-dark that mothered them. It clears nothing. It widens nothing. It simply prevents a collapse here, a mineral sealing there, keeping a road home in reserve.

That may be its deepest act of love: refusing to make the present arrangement eternal. The Cup is not preserved like a specimen. It is accompanied like a life, which includes arranging, insofar as stone allows, for its descendants to survive its end.

Then, when years have taught it the whole chorus by heart, the power leaves the summit — in the sense that anything so patient ever entirely leaves.

It leaves no mark. No fused sigil on the lakebed, though the electric fish weld stranger things there daily. No improved species. No myth among animals that have no use for myth.

It leaves only a quality of attention around the mountain, thin as altitude light: a tendency for the world's curiosity to slide off. A slightly longer pause before storms become too much. A road kept open in the dark below.

In the Cup, untroubled by gratitude, the plate rises a hand's width.

Bubbles race from the seams. The mussels slam in one concussive clang. The piston-eels stroke together. The millers stutter and resume their patient clicking. The raft-snails loosen against the silver ceiling, and tighten, and loosen, holding their improbable sea above the clouds.

The being lives.

The power, far off now and still listening at seven-minute intervals, asks for nothing in return — not worship, not improvement, not even continuance forever.

Only that while it lasts, it be allowed to be exactly, industriously, hungrily itself.
