It would not bring rain.

That is the first thing to understand about it — not out of hesitation, and not out of indifference. A lesser kindness would look at that dry, green, held valley and want, immediately, to fix it: move the sun, break the grid, let weather back in like opening a window in a sealed room. It would call that rescue.

A wise one would feel the temptation and refuse it.

Because the valley is not a trap someone else built for them. It is the last act of a people who saw the rim split open and chose, with their whole civilization in their hands, *this, exactly this, no further.* To tear the frame away all at once would not return them to life. It would return them, all at once, to the thousand years they have not lived — erosion, entropy, thirst, rot, grief, the full weight of deferred time arriving in a single moment. The leaves would not heal. They would simply, finally, be allowed to die, and most of them would, before they remembered how to do anything else.

So it would not shatter the afternoon.

It would do the one thing the valley, for all its power, cannot do for itself.

It would remember it — from the outside.

* * *

It would begin on the rim, where the basalt is still bright and unweathered, and it would stay there longer than any traveler has stayed. Not entering. Not yet. Just watching the fixed sun, the interlaced canopies, the districts of green brightening and dimming by fractions felt in the teeth. Watching until it knew the pattern the way the valley knows the pattern: not approximately, but leaf for leaf, freckle for freckle on the fruit.

And then, crucially, it would leave, and come back, and know that it had come back.

That sounds like nothing. In the valley it is a miracle with no local physics to support it. The motes part around a visitor and erase the footprints when attention wanders. Leaves are not healed but restored. Every meeting is the first meeting. Nothing there can hold the difference between *before you* and *after you* — that difference is precisely what was abolished to make the place survivable.

The power would hold it for them.

In its own memory — vast, patient, and not subject to the lattice's corrections — it would keep the valley exactly as it is, on this day, in this held light. Every hexagonal leaf-plate in its appointed place. Every moth on its circuit. The heron-thing in the salt channel with its leg forever half-raised and its eye still quick. The whole fifteen kilometers of it, perfect, unlost.

Not as a specimen. As a promise.

Because that is the terror underneath the stasis, if terror is still a word for something so thoroughly frozen: if they change, the afternoon is gone. If the leaf heals instead of being restored, *that* leaf — the one they chose, the one that was there on the last day of the world they knew — is gone, replaced by a scarred successor. They perfected preservation because they could not bear erasure, and now preservation is the only form love is allowed to take.

A benevolent power would take the weight of that bargain onto itself.

*You do not have to keep being this in order for this to have existed. I have it. I will not lose it. If you let the leaf fall, the leaf you were is not forgotten. It is remembered — by someone who can remember more than one version of you.*

That is the gift. Not change. The *safety* to change, for the first time in an age: an external memory to stand in for the one they sacrificed.

* * *

Only then — and slowly, at a pace set by clicks from distant trees, by brightenings passed canopy to canopy over distances that bend — would it enter.

It would make itself, deliberately, a little bit vulnerable. Not wet and breathing and frantic with entropy the way a human visitor is, a source of error the motes must work around until the work becomes incorporation. But not armored either. It would walk in a shape the grid could measure, and let the motes part around it, and let them learn the shape by erasing it, again and again, for as long as courtesy took to become reluctance and reluctance to become that exact, you-shaped exemption.

And while they erased it, it would do the opposite. It would keep every erased thing.

Here the lizard blinked, once, and the blink was four seconds later than the last visit's blink. Here the heron tracked not just movement but intention, anticipating a turn before the turn was made. Here, in this clearing, the shadows lean toward the sun a fraction less than in the clearing before it. Small things. Differences within repetition, the thinnest possible evidence that what looks back out of the pattern is still, somewhere under the perfection, *someones* and not only a recording of someones.

It would speak to those. Not to the valley as a whole — the valley as a whole can only answer all at once, in pulses, and an answer that is everywhere is hard to distinguish from weather, which it abolished. It would speak to the eye that follows well. To the blink that comes like a signal through a thick wall. It would say, in whatever slow medium still works:

*I am holding the afternoon. You are not its only container anymore.*

And it would ask the only question that cannot be answered by repeating:

*Do you want to stay in it?*

Not: do you want to be freed. Freedom, imposed, is just a change of warden. It would ask, and then it would do the hardest thing a powerful being can do. It would wait outside the pattern for an answer, for years if necessary, holding its perfect memory steady, neither pushing the sun nor letting the question be unhappened. On the rim, where its marks persist, it would carve a record in fresh stone where travelers can read it before they descend: what this place is, how long to stay, where to stand, what it means when thirst stops arriving. A warning, and a mercy, for the warm and wet and accidental who might otherwise become rare fauna.

The valley is always accepting a little more rare fauna. The power would quietly, firmly, close that door — not by violence, but by being a witness who notices when a visitor has stood too long in one shaft of light, and who can walk in, take them by the not-yet-finished gesture, and lead them up an avenue that it holds straight by remembering it straight. Those who chose the valley, it would leave to their choosing. Those who only wandered in thirsty, it would bring back to the rim, to weather, to evenings.

For the ones who are too far in — the heron, the lizards, the shapes at the same point in the same gesture — it would not pretend to know whether there is anyone left to bring out. It would include them in the remembering exactly as they are, eye and blink and all, and let the question reach them at their own depth. If an eye, one year, begins to track toward the rim and hold there a moment longer than the pattern requires, it will have been answered.

* * *

If the answer, when it comes, is *yes* — if the brightening passes district to district and settles, unmistakably, into assent to continue, *this, exactly this* — then the power would honor it, and that honoring would not look like abandonment.

It would seal nothing. It would guard the rim the way you guard a sleeping person's door: keeping the merely curious and the acquisitive — for there would be those who would want the lattice, the motes, the abolition of entropy, stripped for parts — from treating a people's last refuge as a mine. And every age or so it would return, and stand where it can be seen, and renew the offer simply by being there with its two impossible properties: it remembers them, and it has changed since last time. A standing proof that those can coexist. Then it would go again, and let them be green and still under their fixed sun, not a tragedy being prolonged but a choice being respected — which, given who made the choice and what it cost, is a different thing entirely.

But if the answer is *no* — or if it is not even *no*, only a hairline deviation, a moth crossing the shaft of sunlight a wingbeat late and not correcting, a mote allowing a footprint to persist one breath longer than strictness demands — then the power would begin. Gently, reversibly, at the speed of trust.

It would start with a single clearing, chosen where the pulses run slowest, and there, with the valley's own motes watching, it would ask for a small exemption and hold the space around it with its own attention so the exemption does not have to be enforced by the lattice's whole strength. One leaf, allowed a new fate: not restored when cut, but *healed* — silver veining creeping across the wound in a pattern that has never existed before, imperfect, original, alive in the one way the valley had forbidden itself. The power would remember both leaves, the whole one and the scarred one, so that nothing is lost by the scarring.

Then it would stop. And wait. And let the valley decide whether the scar is an error or a sentence in a language it used to speak.

If the scar is tolerated — if, visits later, the leaf is still there, glossy around its sealed edge, not unmade in a glance away — it would try a second thing. The sun, moved by less than the width of a leaf, and then, after an interval of held breath across fifteen kilometers, moved back, so that change arrives paired with its own reversal: proof, written in light, that a difference is not the same as an ending. Shadows would crawl an inch and return. Nothing would be stranded in the new position. The valley would learn, in its body, the thing it perfected away before it could learn it: that time passing is not automatically time *taking*.

Years of this. Decades. A fruit allowed to fall in a place where falling is permitted, and to lie there — actually lie there, actually soften, in a chemistry that would have to reinvent rot from first principles — while the power tends the tiny risk of it like a gardener who knows the plant is older and stranger than any garden. Perhaps, eventually, a seed in that rotted fruit doing the unthinkable thing: not reissuing the statement of fruit, but germinating into a plant that is the valley's child rather than its portrait. Slightly wrong leaves. A pulse in the veining at a tempo no district has used before.

A thought the one mind has never had, laid out in green.

And at the very end of the beginning — not the end of the valley, the end of its long held breath — the power would do the thing it has been preparing, with infinite slowness, since it first sat down on the rim and started remembering. It would let the sun set. Not forever. Not a plunge into night, which would be its own catastrophe. Just once, by a measured fraction: the light going gold and then amber and then, for the first time in an age, visibly *lowering*, so that shade is cooler than sun and the teal leaves show their copper undersides and the whole basin learns whether the people inside the green still know what to do with an evening.

What they used to do with an evening, a thousand years ago, was go home. Was gather in. Was raise a hand to someone across a distance, in farewell or in greeting, and complete the gesture.

The power would be there, at the rim, holding the perfect afternoon safely in memory where it can never be lost, watching to see if any part of that vast, vigilant, unfinished mind — distributed, fifteen kilometers wide, in every leaf at once — chooses, at last, to finish a gesture and begin another.

It would not wave back before they do.

It has waited this long. It can wait for them to go first.
