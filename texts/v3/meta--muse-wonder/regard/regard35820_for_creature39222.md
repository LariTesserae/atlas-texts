They would not bring light.

That is the first wisdom, and for most powers, benevolent or otherwise, it would also be the hardest. Light feels like a gift. Light feels like rescue, like knowledge, like mercy arriving at last in a dark place. But here light would be a violence done in the grammar of kindness. It would strike the filmed eyes. It would green nothing. It would show the dweller its own crust, its own floor, its own slow incorporations, and force it to *see* what it has spent longer than memory learning to know by a truer sense. The power, being wise, leaves its radiance outside with its pack, by the last cairn, in the wind.

It waits in the ruined windbreak first. A day. Perhaps a season. The benevolent do so much damage by arriving promptly.

From the shoulder of pale rock it learns the outside of the agreement: the cushion plants growing sideways, the rootlets pushing blindly down, the sparse grasses, the melt and the drought, the weather that the chamber conspicuously refuses to obey. It does not study the mountain, exactly. It attends to it, until its own breathing no longer sounds, even to itself, like an announcement.

Only then does it go in, sideways, shoulders scraping, through the crack that bends twice.

It makes no glow. It casts no warmth that is not body-warmth. It does not speak, because speaking would fill the hide-lined air with a weather the room has never had to swallow. It finds, by touch, that the resting stone is occupied, and it does not ask for the center. It lowers itself instead into the rimstone pools at the edge, knees folded awkwardly under the stone curtains, forehead bowed beneath the stalactite teeth, and it ruins its dignity in the correct posture, and stays.

For a long time it does nothing that could be distinguished from being there.

A pool by its shin empties by a thread. Another fills. The drip over its left shoulder veers, once, into a cupped palm that is not its own, with a softened *plink*. Somewhere close, intimate and unhurried, the small work goes on: chip, at an ankle. Chip, between fingers. A joint freed with a click felt in the teeth.

The power does not flinch from the shape of this life. It does not translate it, in pity, into imprisonment. It does not translate it, in reverence, into sainthood. Both would be ways of not touching what is actually here: a body that has found the exact size of not quite setting, and keeps it, minutely, forever, in exchange for being held and warmed and fed at the speed of stone.

So the power asks the only question that can be asked in this place, and it asks it without words. It slows its heart.

Not as a display. As an offering of tempo. It lets the loud, fast animal of its own pulse be schooled by the drips, breath by breath — six breaths to a minute, then fewer — until the dweller can hear that the newcomer is not panicking, is not preparing to seize it and drag it toward the blade of cold light for its own good, is not counting its deformities, is not preparing to die picturesquely on its floor and become one more curled shape in the mosaic. The power makes itself, as much as any power can, into another process in the chamber.

Then, and only then, it reaches out — palm up, in the dark, where the wrist can be taken.

What it offers is not escape. Escape remains available at every moment, toward the rootlets and the black fern and the hurting light, and the power will later hold that road open with more fidelity than it holds any other. What it offers is smaller, and therefore greater.

It offers to keep watch while the dweller sleeps.

For this, you understand, is the one mercy the chamber itself cannot give. Stillness is the price and stillness is the danger. To rest without shifting by a hair's width is to be accepted: film over the ankle, sheet over the hip, moonmilk sweetening across the closed lids. The dweller has not slept the way surface things sleep since the first cup was clay. It has dozed in increments. It has rested in the manner of isopods. It has never once been able to trust the room not to love it into geology.

The power places one hand, very lightly, where it can feel the mineral beginning to gather. Through one long, unmeasured dark, it does the dweller's work at the dweller's tempo — not scraping, never scraping another's body unasked, but shifting it, almost imperceptibly, before the stone notices. A weight eased from heel to rim. A shoulder turned the width of a breath. Fingers uncurled and curled again before the webbing can set. Now and then, where permission has been given by a particular leaning-in, a flake lifted free with a care felt in the teeth, and pressed into a seep to nudge a prized drip toward the drinking basin.

It tends, while it is there, the lowest pools. It feels beneath the warm water for clogged pores and breaks the opalescent film where the outflow falters. It does not improve the system. It does not widen anything, sweeten anything, add a new basin or subtract an old grief. It keeps the locked agreement exactly as it found it: inflow, condensation, outflow, mineral laid silently over mineral. Its whole omnipotence narrowed, gladly, to the maintenance of a balance four meters across.

And in the morning that is not a morning, the dweller wakes — if wakes is the word — and finds itself uncoated in a way it has not been for longer than cups can measure, and alone again, because the power has already gone the way courtesies go here: by touch, by displacement, by warmth receding where warmth already was.

Inside the chamber, it leaves nothing.

No new cup. A cup would restart a clock the dweller has already accepted, and impose the giver's hope upon its hands. No food from above, which would be poison to a gut attuned to film and moonmilk. No sharpened blade of foreign metal, no word, no blessing laid like a crust upon the skin. Nothing to be fused into a niche and wondered at. Nothing, above all, that would make the next visitor come looking for a miracle.

Outside, it is less scrupulous.

It restacks the last cairn where a climber's eye will catch it a fraction too late, after the weather has already turned them back. It lets the pale shoulder of rock go on looking, from a distance, like nothing — cream, rust, a faint wet yellow, a place you would walk past without seeing it. In the maps that men will make in the coming years, a survey line will bend, inexplicably, around this part of the mountain. A road will find the geology unsuitable. A mine will test the deep warm water once, and misplace its results, and test elsewhere. Boots will come, because boots always come, but they will come singly, and in the right spirit or in weather that teaches it to them, and those who squeeze through the second bend will feel, without knowing why, that they should sit down, be quiet, and leave before the white dust settles on their sleeves.

The power does not seal the entrance. Sealing would be another name for the floor.

It keeps the way out open. It keeps the way in difficult. It keeps the deep spring rising blood-warm through young stone, scandalously indifferent to snowmelt and drought. It holds, around one pocket of ongoing dark, the conditions under which a life that is almost a process can go on choosing, drip by drip, to remain just barely unmade at its center.

Then it withdraws even its attention — for attention, too, sustained too long, can become a kind of light — and the chamber closes over the absence as film closes over a fingertip's touch.

A pool sinks by a thread. Another fills. Something transparent ghosts from basin to basin. Something white shifts a fraction on the wet wall, and is still. On the resting stone, the dweller settles, puts its feet in the warm water, finds the good drip with the least-stoned cup, and takes up again its small, sufficient work.

Nothing has been rescued.

Everything has been guarded.

That is what it would do.
