They would not go in.

They would arrive the way the light arrives, without tearing anything — and first they would kneel. That is how the place is found, and it is also the only fit posture for what it is. A power, however large, that pushed its way headfirst through that slit would already have misunderstood it. The room accommodates a shrew. It tolerates a still human. It is not built; it is *happening*. To enter in force would be to make the drum-roll so loud she would flatten herself into the dimple and perhaps never come out again in the short time left to her.

So they stay outside a while. Forehead near the leaf-litter. Listening.

And what they hear first, having no need of ears, is not the loud growth, not the creak and pop of cells. It is the completeness. The place does not want anything. The bulb is not a prison and not a temple and not a mistake. It is a living mass doing, briefly and apparently without intention, the one thing life almost never does: leaving a space entirely alone in its middle. Lit like noon. Warmed like a hand. Empty, because for once nothing has rushed to fill it.

The wise do not preserve such things by stopping them. To keep the walls apart would be to keep the seed from finishing its germination. To make the hollow permanent would be to kill it more thoroughly than its closing ever will — freeze it into a curiosity, a room, a possession. It is beautiful in part because its physics have not settled and will not get time to. Its light is borrowed from leaves far away. Its rain arrives on surfaces. Its echoes come early because it is young at being a place. You do not rescue that by making it last. You rescue it, if the word applies at all, by letting it have been.

So they will not hold the walls.

Nor will they take her out.

This is the harder mercy. It would be so easy, and look so kind: lift the blind, trembling animal into the ordinary world before the flesh closes, set her somewhere safe, spare her the end of her home. But it would not spare her. It would end her home *now*, by hand, and replace the one geography she knows bone-deep — the rib, the cup-leaves filling after mist, the vein that beads when she pries it — with a bewildering elsewhere she must learn while burning through her frantic, shrew-short life. She is not trapped in there like a creature in a jar. She found the slit, or woke inside the swelling, and made a dimple more itself, and solved damp and slope and time arriving a breath early. She hears her scrape before she makes it and lifts her tool and waits for her own life to catch up. She is not a symbol of helplessness. She is competent at this place in a way no rescuer will ever be.

They will not give her eyes. In a room that makes its own afternoon, eyes sealed under veined gold-pink skin are not a deprivation to be corrected; they are how she is shaped to the light here. Out in the leaf-litter they would be worse than useless.

They will not give her speech. The room completes voices too quickly. It is learning breathing already. A creature taught to speak in there would hear herself answered, harmonized, finished by the walls until she could no longer tell which note was hers. Her silence is part of why she belongs. They will not teach her to want a mate, or company, or meaning. She is solitary completely, not lonely. Small mercies imposed from a larger scale of life are still impositions.

What, then?

They attend. Fully, and for the whole duration, which is the rarest form benevolence takes. Someone, at last, will know this place in every detail not in order to describe it or keep it or use it, but because a week-long hollow inside a germinating bulb, lit without source, loud with growth, studded in its ceiling with seeds that arrive before they fall, deserves at least one witness who does not flinch from its ending. When the walls sigh together at last, cell by cell, the room will not have been unwitnessed. The universe will not lose it entirely. It will be held, exact and unaltered, in a memory that does not need it to continue in order to honor it.

And while they attend, they do — almost nothing. Small things. Things at the scale of the slit.

Outside, where the leaf-litter has drifted thick across the bank, they loosen the soil with no visible hand, so that when the time comes a desperate set of broad hind feet and small pale fore-paws will find it gives rather than resists. They clear no path — a path would invite other feet. They simply make sure the way out is *possible* on the one night it will be needed.

Further down the bank, out of scent-range for now, they see to it that a fallen log keeps its hollow. That the earth under it stays dark and rich and undisturbed. That beetles go about their ordinary business there in ordinary numbers. No paradise. No garden arranged for one animal. Just a place, in the regular cold world, where a small blind mammal who prefers the undersides of things might land and not instantly die of the strangeness.

They do not feed her. She feeds herself; the tool darkened where she grips it testifies to that. But on the last evening, without entering, without speaking, they let the air change fractionally at the slit. A smell threads in — soil after rain, crushed stem, iron-sweet green — close enough to the smell of the vein she opens each cycle to be legible to that ceaseless wet snout, different enough to say: *outside is also a place where things grow.* Not a command. A piece of information, offered in the only language she reads fluently.

And they ask something of the plant — not in words, and ask is too strong a term for a persuasion exerted on processes that have no hostility in them. They incline it, very slightly, the way weather inclines. When the closing comes, let it come as it was always going to: slowly, loud as a ship turning, in the hour after a mist-cycle, when she is fed and awake rather than deep in one of her brief sleeps. Let the root that arches over the mouth hold its shape one hour longer than it might have. Long enough.

Then they withdraw their attention from the threshold entirely, because even benevolence, if it hovers, becomes pressure through the walls.

So, in the end, it happens almost exactly as it would have.

The light dims with a cloud and does not fully brighten. The ribs swell closer — she has been reweaving one strand at a time for days as the dimple shallows — and a drop rises to a ceiling that is lower than any ceiling she has known. The seeds tick louder. She stands in the weaving with the thorn in both forepaws, whiskers fanning, flattening, fanning, listening to a creak arrive that she has not yet felt through her feet.

Perhaps she understands nothing. Perhaps understanding is the wrong instrument entirely, and her body simply finds, ahead of the sound, the moment to move.

She goes up the wall that is becoming a floor, across the vault that is becoming a wall, three dashes and a pause and a dash, tail curled around the last root-hair — and then, instead of the vein, instead of a cup-leaf, she puts her nose to the narrowing mouth where the outside smell has been waiting for days, patient and dark and green.

What she carries is absurd and non-negotiable. She carries the tool.

Out into leaf-litter bigger than her whole world, into air that does not answer her breathing back, into dark that is honestly dark — blind, grey-pink, trembling, clasping a snapped thorn wrapped clumsily and ingeniously in root-fiber and hardened sap, fitted to no hands on earth but hers.

The loose soil gives under her just as it should. Somewhere west, at a distance she can cross in one night's burning, there is a log with a hollow under it, full of unremarkable beetles.

Behind her, with no one watching from inside, the walls meet in the middle with a sigh loud enough to deafen anyone who had stayed. No one hears it except a power kneeling in the leaves, who does not speak, and does not applaud, and does not mourn incorrectly.

The bulb finishes becoming a plant.

Under one rib, grown now into solid flesh, there will always be — if you could cut it open, which no one will — the faint compressed curve of a dimple, three fibers woven into it without pattern, closing a space the exact shape of a small animal who once expected the tap before the touch, and was right.

That is all they do.

It is not rescue. It is not nothing.
