They would not go down into it. Not at first, and, having understood it, not ever in the way a visitor goes down — with feet, with weight, with a path.

From the low ridge, in that brief interval of white sky before the descent, a lesser power would already be making plans. There is so much here that looks like need. So much darkness that looks like deprivation. So much heat that looks like suffering, so much rot that looks like waste, so much still, patient hunger in the twilight waiting, hours and days and weeks, for another life to wander within reach.

A kind power, but not a wise one, would want to let in the light.

A wise one learns, standing on the ridge, that the light would be a weapon. It would fall through the three canopies like an axe. It would dry the bromeliad cups and the white fungal threads in the litter. It would blind eyes that have perfected themselves for dusk. It would wake a thousand dormant seeds into a riot they could not all survive, and close again — for light here always closes again — leaving the slow things slower and poorer for the interruption. Darkness is not the condition this being endures. Darkness is the condition it *made*, leaf over leaf over leaf, and maintains, because darkness is where its tenancy is safest and its work can proceed undisturbed.

Nor would they cool it. The heat is not a fever to be broken. It is the engine. It keeps the slow fires of digestion burning in the snakes, keeps the fungus gardens of the ants and termites fruiting in their underground and towered chambers, keeps the fallen fruit fermenting for the drunken butterflies, keeps every egg laid in foam and mud and leaf-water moving, cell by cell, toward hatching. Cool this valley and you do not relieve it. You stop it.

Nor clean it, nor feed it, nor thin it, nor spare the things in it from one another. The leech waving under its stone and the warm body it waits for belong to the same sentence. The waste-chamber workers of the leafcutters, who will never surface again, are not a tragedy the colony has failed to notice; they are the colony, in one of its necessary forms. The jaguar moving like dapple down the dry creek bed is not a terror imposed on an otherwise peaceful forest. It is the forest reading its own scent-map at the top of its scale, keeping the map honest. To rescue everything here from being eaten would be to starve everything here that eats, and then to bury the whole valley, uneaten and undisassembled, under its own unprocessed dead.

So the first thing the power does — and it is a doing that takes more restraint than any miracle — is refuse the miracle.

They do not descend. They hover where the ridge gives them sky, and they learn the being properly.

For it is one being, though that is not obvious from the ridge and is even less obvious from within. The moths patterned as bark are not separate from the bark they mimic; the algae in the sloth is not separate from the moths that fertilize it; the fungus in the ant chamber is not a crop the ants own but an organ they carry outside their bodies; the termite tower is a lung built by a million small bodies for a crop that is also a stomach. Lives are stacked here the way canopies are stacked — emergent, continuous, understory — the short-lived swarming beneath and feeding the long-lived, the long-lived making the shade in which the short-lived need not hurry. No single creature carries the life of the place. The life of the place is the processing itself: leaf to fungus to ant to soil to root to leaf again, without a wasted stage, without an unoccupied inch.

You do not help a being like that by adding to it. It is already full. Every surface is tenanted, every fallen thing is met, every niche patrolled by a specialist exquisitely fitted to one exact quality of wet twilight. Add a new food and you unbalance a hundred queues. Add a new creature and a hundred old ones go quietly extinct in their inches before anyone notices. Heal a sickness and you have robbed the things that were queued, patiently, to eat the sick.

What this being wants — if the word *wants* can be stretched to cover a twenty-kilometre dark that has never once spoken — is not improvement. It is continuity. Wetter weeks when the pools join and the trapped fish can move and lay. Drier weeks when the pools contract and the eggs wait in the mud. A giant falling now and then, in its own time, so the shaft of sun can do its brief, scheduled riot. No winter. No fire. No sudden, permanent change in the terms on which slowness is possible.

So the power acts, as wise benevolence so often finally does, entirely at the edges, and entirely against interruption.

They strengthen nothing inside the basin, but they attend to the rim of hills that keeps the basin low and held. They coax the roots a little deeper on the outer slopes where scrub frays into light, so the soil will hold another thousand years against any wash that might breach the valley and drain it. They bless — no, that is too active a word; they *adjust their attention over* — the weather that comes to this country, not to make it wetter or kinder, but to keep it familiar: the brief warm rains through the leaves, the long saturated pauses, the air that sits down in the hollow and stays. If, in the ages to come, the larger world should tilt toward a dryness this valley never evolved to survive — dryness arriving not from its own slow geology but from fires and clearings and poisoned winds far beyond the ridge — then, and only then, they would stand between it and that novelty. They would turn the worst of it aside at a distance so great that nothing inside would ever know a hand had moved. A cloud seeded a hundred kilometres off. A surveyor's route failing, unaccountably, to find the pass. A generation of the violent and the acquisitive remembering this direction as fever country, as useless swamp, as nothing on the map — and passing it by.

That is their gift: not light, not rescue, but anonymity. Invisibility, maintained like a fourth canopy above the other three. No road, because a road is a permanent light-gap with noise on it. No clearing. No name that travels. The place will be left off the accurate maps and misdrawn on the careless ones, and the fraying edges where the canopy breaks will grow, if anything, a little more discouraging, a little more tangled with thorn and stinging things, so that even curiosity tires before it reaches the ridge.

And inside? One thing, perhaps. And even that is mostly a refusal wearing the shape of an act.

They witness it. Fully, and for a very long time. Not the hurried glance of a naturalist, but the one tribute a patient being can actually receive from an impatient order of minds: sustained, unextractive attention. They learn the scent-geography laid over the visible one — the ant highways written in chemistry, the jaguar's map, the ripeness-signals of fruit no eye will ever see ripen. They watch a sloth complete its weekly descent and understand it as liturgy. They put no hand to a termite vent, but they learn, in whatever passes for their body, exactly how warm that manufactured air is, and they marvel at it the way one marvels at a furnace in a house where one is a guest.

Because to be *seen*, without being used or altered or improved — this being has never had that. Everything else that has ever perceived it has perceived it as food, shelter, mate, danger, or shade. A benevolent wisdom can offer it the one experience outside its economy: to be regarded as complete.

At the end — and for such a power the end of a visit might be a century after its beginning, one full turn of emergent generations — they withdraw by the ridge, and they tidy even that. The place where they stood fills back in. No footprint, because there was never quite a foot. The scrub closes. The white hammer of sky above the flat crowns gives no sign.

Nothing below notices, and that is the proof it was done well. The moths do not stir from the bark. The turtle does not lift its nostrils higher in its pool. The leafcutters keep carrying their green sails along the swept highways in the dark, down to the gardens, down to the waste chambers, in the warm sour breath of the colony. A frog gives a single liquid call from a bromeliad, somewhere thirty feet up, and does not repeat it. It does not need to. The message was received by everything it was meant for.

The valley goes on doing almost nothing, very slowly, all at once — but now with an assurance laid quietly around its borders like a held breath that will hold for ages: that the nothing it is doing will not be stopped. That the dark will keep its level. That the processing will continue, indefinite and unobserved, turning the forest into itself, leaf into fungus into soil into leaf, until geology itself changes the shape of the ground.

Benevolence, having looked long enough, becomes indistinguishable from leaving alone. Wisdom just stays at the edge to make sure the leaving-alone lasts.
