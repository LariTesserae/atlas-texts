It came the way the storms come: from above, where the hard sky is.

And at first, being wise, it did nothing at all.

This took longer than pity usually allows. For it had power enough to split the three canopies like curtains. It could have let the noon down into the valley in one clean shaft and kept it there. It could have drained the perpetual damp, stilled the sizzling of the insects so that every small voice could be heard distinctly, lifted the sharp new rock and ground its edges smooth, built — in stone that would not rot, in places that would not quake — shelters, storehouses, shrines. It could have taught the hammer-monkeys to keep their stones in piles, taught the crow-bird to keep its thorn, taught all of them to write their knowledge somewhere safer than in watching eyes and shoulder-to-shoulder stealing. It could have found the large silent one where it waited, breathing through its mouth by the game trail, and taken its hunger out of the world.

It understood, slowly, that every one of those mercies would be a murder.

Light here is not missing. It has simply been outvoted by life. The leaves are black-green and bottle-green because they learned to eat twilight. The flowers are red and bruised purple and heavy yellow because saturation is their answer to dimness. Bring down the sun and you do not enlighten them; you bleach an entire grammar out of existence.

Silence, likewise, is not peace here. Silence is the shape of the predator. Sound is the map, sound is warning, sound is water ahead and fruit coming and the bat-river stirring the fungus into its brief white snow. To quiet the valley so it could rest would be to blind it.

And permanence — permanence is the one predator everything here has outrun together. Tools sink into the mold and are not mourned because mourning them would be a way of stopping. Nests are not rebuilt because the branch will not hold. Knowledge lives in hands because hands are the only archive that rots at exactly the speed of the forest. Give them a monument and you give them the first thing in the valley that cannot be eaten, moved, forgotten, or replaced — and everything, having always lived by replacement, would begin to die around it at the wrong speed.

So the power learned the place instead.

It learned not to arrive all at once. It let its attention come down the way the valley's own rain comes down: never quite reaching the ground as rain, but arriving as drip from bark, as bead on web, as fog made where cool air slides off young ridges into warm vegetable breath. It learned to be wet. It learned to be loud in the right register — not above the strata of cicada and frog and howler, but *inside* them, so that nothing froze at its passing and no half-second tear ripped outward across the chorus to announce a new large thing that does not call.

It moved with a feeding front instead of against it. For two days it was apparently nothing more than a slightly increased drunkenness of wasps over split fruit, a few more butterflies lapping at the pulp, one more patient shadow at the edge where the distracted get taken. It drank only from leaves the frogs refused — no, it learned why the frogs refused them, and then it drank elsewhere. It never trusted an empty patch of floor.

If it had a heart, the valley got into it the way water gets into clothes here: without a drop ever formally falling.

What it loved most, I think, surprised it. Not the swifts up in the bright waste above the canopy — those brief fliers it recognized as being a little like itself, visitors unadapted to the dark, pale underneath and frantic. It loved the continuity below them. The monkeys whose cleverness refuses to accumulate. The frogs shouting from inside plants and feeding their houses with their own droppings. The ants redrawing their roads after every silent war. The fungus waiting all day in the gloom for the exact turbulence of ten thousand wings. Not innocence — nothing here is innocent, everything here is eating something that is screaming about it in another register — but *fit*: a place still being made, on rock not yet decided, inhabited entirely by beings who had not made the mistake of asking to be finished.

So in the valley itself, it did nothing.

Or almost nothing. Once — once only — it allowed itself a small, deniable act, of the kind the valley itself performs constantly. In a lean interval between fronts, when the mast trees were bare and the pigs were thin and the monkeys had gone quiet in the un-courteous way that means hunger rather than plenty, it nudged. Not a tree. Not a season. A single already-ripe cluster, three branches over from where a troop was already quarreling, so that the ripening seemed to spread a day earlier than it otherwise might have, and the front moved on before anything had time to starve in place. No one noticed. That was the point. By the following week the pulp was rotted, the seedlings were up, the butterflies had come and gone, and even the memory of the meal lived only where all knowledge lives here: in bodies that had eaten, and would teach by being watched while they ate again.

Its real work it did at the rim.

Because the valley's danger was never inside it. Inside, danger is accounted for; danger has a sound-shaped hole in the chorus and everything has evolved ears for it. The danger was outside, and coming eventually, as it comes everywhere: the surveying eye that sees young mineral rock and thinks *wealth*, that sees three stacked canopies and thinks *timber*, that hears the deafening and thinks *empty*, that finds no structures, no piles of stones, no monuments, and concludes — with the confidence only outsiders can afford — that no one lives here.

Against that, the power had no scruples about interfering.

It did not raise a wall; walls invite ladders. It did not set a guardian; guardians become legends, and legends become destinations. It did something quieter and much more thorough. It took the valley out of the future's memory.

Paths that might have led down to it began, imperceptibly, to lead somewhere else — hand over slick hand through the layered leaf became hand over slick hand and then, somehow, a ridge top again, blinking in the hard sky, with the traveller quite certain they had turned back of their own sensible accord. Surveys smoothed over the crack in the land the way water smooths a handspan of seep before it sinks. Maps acquired other features in that region, plausible ones, boring ones. The few who had glimpsed the twilight from the ridges found, when they tried to describe it, that they were talking about somewhere they'd been in a dream after fever — green, they said, *very* green, no, they couldn't find it again, no, there was nothing there to find.

It gave the valley the only gift a finished world can give an unfinished one: *more time, unobserved*.

Time for the quakes to rearrange the seeps a hundred more times. Time for a particular good hammer-stone to be picked up for years after its owner dies, and then sink, unmourned and unconsequenced, into the mold. Time for the rock to go on ticking as it cools at night, prying itself a fraction wider, deciding at its own geological leisure what shape this place will finally take — while everything living shouts on top of it as if volume alone could hold it together.

Which, the power had come to suspect, it largely does.

In the end it left the way the fog leaves: by being indistinguishable, by mid-afternoon, from the ordinary humidity of the place. Nothing down there marked its departure. No chorus stuttered. That was how it knew it had done the visit correctly — learned, at last, how to pass through the trunk layer the way the bat-river passes: bending around everything, thinning around nothing, stirring the air just enough that somewhere behind it, unseen, a pale fungus fruited in the gloom and threw its spores upward in a brief white snow smelling of yeast and old blood — the valley, entirely unaware of having been blessed, going on with the ancient and sufficient business of replacing itself.

Above the canopy, in the hard warm sky, the swifts went on with their bright frantic lives and did not dip down.

Below them, at dark noon, it stayed dark.

That was the mercy.
