It would do nothing in there.

Not at first, not quickly — because to a power accustomed to moving at the speed of intention, nothing is the hardest and slowest thing to do.

It could arrive the way weather arrives, too large for the entrance slit, too large for the twenty paces of the cirque. So it would learn to arrive otherwise. It would attenuate itself. It would put down, one by one, its warmth, its brightness, its urgency, its vast peripheral awareness of other places, until it could fit inside the hollow the way mist fits: by not insisting on a shape.

Inside, it would be tempted. Benevolence, undisciplined, is always tempted.

It could heal the ulcer where the stone cut her, before she was finished changing. It could close that patient wound in an instant. But fly larvae live there now, in their season; beetles feed at its edge; the scar is not a failure of the place but one of its addresses. To heal her would be to evict them. It lets the wound go on almost closing, as it has for longer than the trees outside.

It could lift the leeches from the folds of her skin. It could make her clean. But clean is a word from a warmer world, where a body is supposed to end at its own surface. Hers does not. The leeches drink at the pace she loses. The mites cross the moss on her shoulder. The snails rasp algae from her forearms and she feels it, distantly, in her teeth. Everything here has its parasite because everything here is also a landscape. The power would have to decide whose good it was serving, and wisdom is, in part, refusing to make that decision too cleanly.

It could give her back her eyes.

That is the temptation that would take longest to refuse — a century, perhaps, spent watching the filmed hollows turned toward the pool, imagining what sight would return to her. Mountains. Sky. The bright alpine world outside, which she may once have known at a warmer speed. Color.

But sight would not be a gift added to her life. It would be a different life, imposed. Her eyes closed because sight was an extravagance the hollow could not use; she knows the place now by pressure, by taste, by iron and calcite on her tongue, by moths resettling themselves twenty paces away, felt through rock and peat and the rootlets where moss enters her skin. To reopen her eyes would be to make her a stranger in the only knowledge she has left. It would be a rescue that required her to be rescued from herself.

It could warm her. It could lift her out of the black water, out of the seven degrees, out of the eleven seconds between heartbeats, and set her somewhere sunlit and kind. It could give her food that did not take an afternoon to swallow. It could give her speech, and company, and years that pass at the pace years pass for other people.

It does not. Because warmth would not simply comfort her. Warmth would accelerate her: her hunger, her healing, her rotting, her aging, all the processes cold has slowed until they nearly cancel out. Warmth would give her back time, and time would kill her in the ordinary way. Sunlight would be an eviction notice written in light. To save her from the hollow, it would first have to destroy the conditions under which she is she.

So the power does nothing in there. Actively. At cost. The way you hold a cup filled exactly to the brim: stillness is not the absence of effort, but effort brought to perfection.

What it does, it does at the boundary.

Outside, the world is not finished changing. That is the one true danger to the hollow — not its own slow processes, which have arrived at equilibrium, but the unfinished world pressing at the slit: climbers with their bright shells and loud warmth; goats picking at the lip; a warming climate thinning the ice skin that seals the entrance; acid in the rain altering, hairline crack by hairline crack, the chemistry of the seep water; a surveyor, someday, with an instrument that could find value in black basalt.

Against those, the power permits itself to act, because there its action does not edit her. It only preserves the terms under which she edits herself.

It makes the approach unmemorable. Not forbidden — forbiddance draws maps. Just dull. The turn that leads to the lip of basalt acquires a quality of *not quite*, so that walkers, decade after decade, find their attention sliding off it toward a better view, a more promising ridge, a reason to be elsewhere by evening. Expeditions remember weather. Maps acquire small, plausible inaccuracies. A company prospecting the mountain finds the rock, through a long chain of unremarkable reports, uninteresting.

It tends the temperature the way you tend a held note: not by touching the pool, but by deepening, almost imperceptibly, the shade at the rim; by persuading meltwater along its old hairline paths when a dry summer would reroute it; by letting the entrance ice form a fraction earlier and melt a fraction later as the years outside grow warmer. Seven degrees. Eight. The level of the pool neither rises nor falls by the width of a limpet. From inside, none of this registers as intervention. It registers as the world continuing to be the world, which is what it has always done, and is, increasingly, the most miraculous service anyone can perform for it.

And the power stays.

That is the part benevolence insists on, and wisdom finally allows. Not to fix, not to speak, not to announce itself — but to attend at her tempo, which no visitor has ever done, because no visitor can. A human stays an hour and leaves with soaked clothes and an offended pulse. The power sets its own pulse down beside hers and waits out the interval. Eleven seconds. Eleven seconds. Eleven seconds. It learns the drip by learning her heartbeat by learning the drip, until it can no longer tell which of them is keeping the time, which is exactly how everything else in the hollow knows anything.

It stays long enough for a thought of hers to complete. A whole thought, at the speed of calcite: *something is here that is not eating, not grazing, not settling, not falling. Something is here at the rate I am here.*

That may take fifty years. It has fifty years. It has, for this, all the years there are.

It does not ask her to respond. It does not require gratitude, which would be another extraction. But sometime in the second century of its attendance, very deep in the part of her where experience and expectation have not yet quite merged, a pressure changes. The living film on the pool parts, faintly, not for her breath. The moths on the wall adjust, by hundreds, a fraction of a wing-width, for no moth reason.

She begins, very slowly, to turn her head toward it.

The power understands that this is the conversation. It does not lean in. It does not help. It receives the turning the way the basins in the floor receive the drip: by having been shaped, over a long attention, into exactly the place where it lands.

Then it leaves as it came, without parting the mist, because a place like this should never have to recover from having been loved. On its way out it takes one thing, the only thing it permits itself: the precise location of the hollow, lifted gently out of the memory of the one human who found it, the way you lift a seed from a fruit without bruising the fruit. They will keep the quiet. They will keep the smell of peat and cold stone and faint iron, and the knowledge that somewhere there is a life that has nowhere else to go and no intention of going there. They will keep enough to be changed by it, slowly, in the warmer world.

They will not keep the way back.

 behind it, in the dark that is always just after rain, the film of insects closes over the small absence. The drip falls. Eleven seconds. She completes her turning toward the place where the power was — fractionally, satisfied, already forgetting the distinction between *was* and *is* —

and begins, very slowly, to turn back.
