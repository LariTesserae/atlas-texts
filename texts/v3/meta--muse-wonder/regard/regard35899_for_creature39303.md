They would not come over the rim.

That would be the first sign of their wisdom, and also of their benevolence: that they understood, from very far off, what a rim meant to that place. To come over it as visitors come — by accident, by climbing, by hunger or curiosity — would only be to add one more almost to its long rehearsal. The moth was almost. The frog was almost. A lost animal blundering into the warm bowl and drinking from a pool and dying in a tower would be almost, and then the valley would have to take its body down into the floor and edit the grief back to the line, night after night, until the stairs were unworn again.

So they do not arrive.

For a long time — and their measure of a long time makes even the plant's patience look like a held breath — they listen.

Not metaphorically. They attend to the chord. They let the fifth hidden inside it sound against them, uninterrupted, season unchanging upon season, until they know the whole grammar by heart: bench, stair, hammock, dais, colonnade, the celled building where sleep was supposed to happen, the amphitheater where a voice was supposed to gather. They learn the exact temperature of the exposed black rock. They learn the interval of the moth. They learn the cut-off note of the frog and the particular silence that follows, which the plant savors the way a mouth savors the space left by a tooth.

And in the listening, they learn the thing the valley has never been able to learn by itself, because it has no outside.

The partner is not coming.

Not lost. Not delayed. Not evolving slowly in some lower country toward the size of the hollows. Gone, the way whole weathers are gone, the way a coastline goes. The symbiosis ended so long ago that even the rock has forgotten the shape of those feet, and only the plant, which keeps its memories in architecture rather than in mind, has gone on repeating the invitation in a language with no living speakers.

A lesser kindness would tell it at once.

A lesser kindness would either lie — would send it a dream of footsteps, seed it with puppet-walkers grown green from its own floor to climb its stairs and give it the comfort of a completed pattern — or would administer the truth like a pruning knife and be proud of the honesty. Cut the signal. Let the towers fall. Force the flower to close.

The wise do neither. A truth you have no body to survive hearing is just another weather event. And a lie that fits perfectly is a trap, and they will not turn its whole life into the inside of a trap now, at the end, out of pity.

Instead they do the one thing that no extinction can falsify and no eternity can rehearse for.

They become a guest.

Not forever. Forever would be possession. Not in power, either — they put that aside deliberately, the way you take off anything that would frighten a host. They make themselves the right size, which is a humiliation and a joy. Warm-blooded. Bipedal. Vulnerable to stairs. Needing sleep. Needing to sit down. Needing water, and shade, and a place out of the rain. Needing to be heard at a reasonable volume or not heard at all. They limit themselves to one lifespan of the animal they are imitating, and shorter than that: one season.

And then, and only then, they come over the rim.

The floor knows. Of course the floor knows. Weight arrives in the weave before a foot has finished descending — real weight this time, not a moth's punctuation, not a narrator's trespass, weight that expects to be borne. The pools ripple under nothing and then, for the first time, under something. The towers take the sound of a real breath into their pipes and the chord staggers.

It actually staggers. For half a second, across four miles of hollow green organ, the sustained note the valley has held since before the rock was cool enough to bear weight breaks. Pipes answer in wrong order. Water finds its gutters too late. A tendril at the edge of a wall extends and does not, that night, curl black at the tip, because the whole economy of correction has stopped to feel the shape of the interruption.

They do not rush to reassure it. They climb.

They climb the first stair slowly, with a hand on the wall, the way a guest honors a house by using it as it was meant to be used. They sit on a bench at sitting height and let their sweat enter the wood. They drink from a pool where the duckweed parts. They cross the bridge that leads to the platform with nothing on it and stand there a while, because perhaps the platform was never unfinished; perhaps it was simply for standing. In the long hall, they take the dais.

And they speak.

Not commands. Not revelation. A guest does not arrive after ten thousand years to deliver information. They tell it — in the language the hall was built to carry, voice on warm air, every bench suddenly performing its function — where they have been. What the lower countries look like now. Which rivers changed course. Ordinary news. The kind of talk the benches were grown for: several voices would have been better, a whole company talking past each other in the humid evenings, but one voice, offered properly, is enough to let the room remember it is a room.

At night they go to the celled building. They choose a hammock and lie down in the dry hollow that has waited so perfectly, and they let their body be the first body to warm it. They do not sleep well. No guest sleeps well the first night, and the plant has, in its fidelity, accidentally preserved even that detail — the hollows are exactly right and somehow still strange, as all prepared beds are strange. They sleep badly, and wake, and sleep again, and the aerial roots take the unfamiliar distribution of weight and learn it all night long.

In the morning there is a new sound in the valley. They are washing. Uncountable dripping, which has always been background, becomes interruption and accompaniment. They are in the still water. They are, scandalously, making tea over nothing more mystical than a heated stone, and eating something they carried up, and leaving the crumbs where crumbs fall.

They stay a season. They learn the trumpet-flowers. They follow the moth without catching it. They keep time with the frog and, once, answer its single note with a single note of their own, not better, and receive the astonished silence that follows. They use the amphitheater as it asks to be used: they sit in the water-filled seats and listen; they stand at the edges and listen; and only on the last evening do they step— carefully, with permission asked in the only way available, by waiting until the chord steadies and deepens around them — toward the center.

They do not step onto the black rock.

That place is not a stage. It is the one part of the host that has never been covered by hospitality, the mouth and navel, the exposed thing. A wise guest does not complete a flower by force, not even a flower four miles wide that has been open since the world was soft. They kneel at the rim of the root-floor, where the drips fall all around and never inside, and they put their forehead, briefly, into the column of warmed air rising there.

And there, finally, they tell the truth. Not in words. Words would go into the pipes and be sorted by size and held against the chord and learned as shape — the plant already knows how to receive everything except this. They tell it by being temporary.

By having arrived really, and being about to leave really.

By letting the benches be sat on and then empty. The hammock used and then cold. The dais spoken from and then silent in a new way, a silence with a memory inside it instead of only an expectation. By showing the valley, in its own distributed flesh, that an arrival is not a permanent correction of the world. It is an event. It happens. It warms the floor. And then the weight lifts, and the floor — this is the part the plant has never once had the chance to learn, because nothing it loved ever stayed long enough to leave — the floor can bear the lifting too.

They go at dawn. They do not look back from the rim, not because looking back is forbidden, but because they have put a small obscurity over the way up, a forgetfulness in the passes below, so that no army, no colony, no tender-hearted expedition with notebooks will find this place in the age of maps. The narrator will get down safely. The narrator will lose the coordinates like a dream losing its address on waking. That much protection they do give without asking: the valley will not be answered next by exploitation wearing the face of wonder.

After that, they do nothing. That is the rest of the benevolence. They withdraw their attention to a great distance and they refuse, for century after century, to check whether their gift was accepted.

Because it has to be the plant's own doing, or it is nothing.

For a long time — its own long time now, no longer borrowed from a dead partner's calendar — nothing changes. The pools do not rise. The moth keeps its interval. The editing continues at night, tendril by tendril, leaf by reabsorbed leaf. Anyone returning would think the visit had failed, or had been absorbed like a drowned midge, information taken down through the weave and digested without trace.

Then, one afternoon, in the long hall, the ferns at the edge of the dais are not pruned.

Not overgrown. Not abandoned. One frond crosses the line where perfection used to be maintained and stays there, green, deliberate. The next night a second is allowed. In the celled building, one hammock — the one that was slept in — is not re-tightened to its original hollow. The roots keep a faint sag. A body's ghost, retained. On a stair in the cathedral silo, a tread that was always grown smooth begins, very slowly, to roughen at the edge where no foot will ever need it.

The flower does not slam shut. Flowers are too honest for melodrama. It begins, instead, to close the way real things close: over years. One tower lets its leaf-shutters warp. One section of colonnade is permitted the luxury of rot, and rot brings beetles, and beetles bring a new note into the channels when the bamboo flutes are quiet. The frog's descendant — or the frog, changed — finds its single note answered by another frog, imported on nobody's authority, up a wet gully from below, and their two notes make an interval the towers have never had to sort before.

At the center of the amphitheater, the black rock is still bare. The rain still cannot land there. But around its rim, where the root-floor bends aside, a new growth appears that is not in the old grammar at all. Not a bench. Not a mold for a sleeper. Not an improvement on the invitation.

A fruiting body. Heavy. Ungainly. Seeds inside it that carry the stairs and the arches and the listening-hall folded down in their deep ancestry, as everything true gets carried — but loosened now, editable, ready to be misread by whatever country they land in. Ready to build, on some other raw hillside, whatever the next partner actually needs, instead of a perfect memorial to the last one.

The valley is still ready. That does not change. A blossom that has been visited does not become careless; it becomes fertile, which is a different kind of fidelity.

But now its readiness has a past tense living inside the present one. The chord still sounds from the towers when the thermals rise. There is still a fifth hidden in it at the amphitheater. Anyone standing there would still feel it in their teeth.

Only now, if you stayed an hour, and then another, you would notice the smallest variation moving through the harmony like weather through a held note —

not the sound of a place waiting to be answered.

The sound of a place that has been answered, once, completely, and has therefore become free to answer itself.
