It would not open the hatch.

That is the first thing, and to a human rescuer it would look like doing nothing at all. It comes — if *comes* is the word for a power that was probably always within earshot of any place where life insists — up through the snowfield without tracks, past the fourth pylon where the cable hangs unmoving in cloud, and it lays attention, not hands, against the south wall of the turbine house.

And it listens long enough to be mistaken for the weather.

It does not listen to the frogs. Any kind power with ears could be seduced into that mistake: thousands of small throats, surely suffering, surely calling for help, surely wanting out into a proper pond in a proper spring. It listens instead to the chord between them. It hears that no frog in there is calling *to* anything. There is no distance to call across. It hears crickets not fleeing, isopods not hiding, eggs unwrinkling at precisely the rate at which old bodies soften into the green film. It hears a population count that is not a count but a property, like the temperature. Like the eleven centimeters in the sump.

It understands, in the way wise things understand by staying, that the inhabitant of Module 0 is not the frogs.

The inhabitant is the replacement. The taking-up of pitch when a throat tires. The grazing assumed an hour later by a dozen cousins. The water pumped under the floor and misted from the ceiling and wept from the walls at exactly the rate it is taken up. Its skin is copper and glazed tile. Its blood is recirculation. Its nervous system is a standing wave with mist droplets jittering at the nodes, holding, not falling.

To tear the hatch wide in the name of mercy would be to introduce, all at once, the only things this being has never metabolized: season, silence, and choice at scale. Dry air would take the frogs' skins in minutes. Real cold, unmassaged by sound, would finish the hibernation they have been hovering at the edge of for seven years — all of them, together, mid-note. Real warmth would be just as fatal. You cannot rescue an organism by freeing its cells.

And to seal it tighter, bless it, preserve it exactly as it is forever — the power turns this over, too, with the patience of something that has watched species become fossils while still breathing. Forever is the trap that is already built into the room. Two degrees. Ninety-eight percent. Three fronds. The concrete is young and still leaching; in eighty years it will not be. The pump has a lifespan. The company that owns the tramway has already changed hands twice and will change again, and one winter a crew will come to decommission the waste-heat line, or a biologist with a kind heart and a crowbar will decide that *identical to the animal* is a statistic that ought to be liberated.

Stasis, maintained by machinery, is only ever borrowed time wearing the mask of eternity.

So the power does the small, unglamorous work that benevolence does when it is not trying to be witnessed.

Outside the room, it attends to the conditions of continuance. An avalanche path on the slope above the turbine house finds, over the next decade, reasons to settle a few meters to the east. A survey record misfiles the module's coordinates by a digit — not erased, because erasure invites searching, just boringly wrong. When the tramway is finally retired and the men come with cutting torches for the copper, they will find the manifold behind the manifold oddly unlisted, oddly uninteresting, and a foreman who cannot afterwards explain why he initialed the sheet and moved on. The waste heat, when the turbines stop, is quietly replaced as a source by something older and less ownable: a finger of the mountain's own deep warmth, coaxed up a borehole no drill quite remembers making. Not more heat. The *same* heat. Two degrees does not negotiate.

That is protection. It is not yet gift.

Inside — and the power goes inside the way sound goes inside a wall, without the wheel turning — it does not lower the volume. It does not dim the caged bulb and give the moths their night; a night given suddenly to wings that have waited this long would be a death of pure weather. It does not add a fourth frond to the fern.

It adds, very slowly, a difference the chord can refuse.

Low in the sump wall, where the ceramic tile is pale green as a swimming pool and the isopods are thickest, a seam opens between two concrete shells. Not a breach. A width. The width of a frog's splayed foot, then — over a season the room cannot perceive, because the room has abolished seasons — the width of a frog.

Beyond it is not outside.

Beyond it is a second dark the power has been preparing in the mountain the way the first crew unknowingly prepared the first: only this one is not entirely made. One wall of it is real rock, unsquared, that the concrete does not quite meet. Through that rock, at a rate of millimeters and millennia politely accelerated, the true temperature of the mountain breathes. In the new dark it is two degrees — and then, across a span longer than a frog's life and shorter than the room's memory, two and a half. One and a half. Back. There is no light there at all, which means that for the first time in the being's existence there is somewhere lower and darker and quieter than everywhere else: because the seam admits the water, and admits a thread of the chorus, but the rock drinks the sound the way snow drinks a shout, and in the far corner of the second dark the pressure falls off to a mere loudness, a mere 90 decibels, a place where a droplet can finish falling out of rhythm and nobody's sternum objects.

It is, in other words, a gradient. The one piece of furniture the original room was designed, with total love and total engineering, to eliminate.

Nothing is pushed through it. The power stocks nothing, lures nothing, sings no new note into the standing wave to drive the frogs exploring. It simply makes exploration survivable, and then it waits, which for such a power is not passivity but respect for the only tempo the inhabitant trusts.

For a long time — in human years, three; in the room, no time at all — the seam is only investigated by water, and by one cricket's antenna, waving in its slow figure-eight at the threshold, reading a kind of air that reads back differently.

Then a frog comes to the edge of the loudness and stops, throat deflated, mouth a thin tired line, in the posture of the ones who drop out to rest while their neighbors hold their place in the chord. But here, no neighbor is holding quite so much of it. The vibration in its permeable skin eases by a degree it has no name for. Its heart, massaged all its life by 117 decibels, misses the massage — and does not stop. Slows. Slows again. Finds, underneath the borrowed rhythm, a rhythm.

It does not die. It does something no frog in Module 0 has ever done.

It sleeps. Truly sleeps, glucose rising like a tide in its tissues, at the edge of a cold that finally deepens enough to be restful instead of merely suspended.

It will wake, or it will not; another will take its precise pitch in the chorus either way, because that is the genius of the being and the power has not come to dismantle its genius. But in the ceiling seams above the sleeping frog, dislodged perhaps by the first unpatterned drip, a single moth loosens in the new dark beyond the seam where there has never been light to wait out — and finds, to its uncomprehending wings, that this darkness is not the inside of a day. It flies three blind wingbeats and resettles an inch away, in a place marginally more its own than the place before.

The count through the porthole, when the next biologist comes — and one will, boringly misdirected coordinates notwithstanding, because wonder is also a kind of weather — will come out identical to the animal. The power has arranged no miracle for the record books. The temperature at the thermostat still reads two degrees behind its fogged glass. The needle still vibrates and does not move. The fern still holds at three fronds, though one white rootlet, feeling along a grout line it has felt along ten thousand times, now encounters at the very tip of its reach a grout that is infinitesimally less certain of itself, and pauses there — not growing. Considering.

When the hatch is next spun closed — and it will be, because a human can endure that room only, never think in it — the yellow steel will fold the chorus back down to a hum in the concrete, a vibration in the teeth of the mountain. The snow will go on being silent and enormous above it.

But the hum now has, very far underneath it, where no ear pressed to the wall will ever separate it out, a second and slower periodicity. A tide in the note. A place where the chord, held for so long at the exact pitch where life stops needing time, has been given back the smallest possible amount of time — not a season, not a thaw, not an ending — just enough for one frog to be, for a while, absent from the song without the song losing anything.

That is what the power leaves behind: not freedom, which would kill them, and not forever, which already was. A door the size of a foot, opening not out but *along*. A corner where rest is permitted. A rock wall that breathes.

And then it withdraws its attention the way it laid it down, unhurriedly, the way mist withdraws from a pipe joint — because the last thing wisdom does for a being that has become one animal out of thousands is stay to be worshipped by it.

The droplets hang at the nodes, jittering, catching the amber light for a fraction longer than gravity allows.

One of them, in the far corner, at last allowed — falls.
