They would come on foot.

That matters. A benevolent and wise power could arrive in other ways — without climbing, without weather, without needing the notched handholds polished dark by wet hands. They would choose not to. The place has a way of being approached, learned in the body rather than taught, and they would honor the order of it: first the sound, well before the water is seen; then the place where shouting fails; then the hundred meters in which speech is set down, not out of reverence at first, but because speech has stopped working.

They would climb past where sound should be possible and let the mountain take their words too.

They would not still the roar as they came. From a distance, from above, it might be tempting — all that violent, enclosed crashing, knee-high waves born in the middle of twelve paces of water, the bowl drumming against the inside of a young peak. An unwise kindness would hush it the way one hushes a frightened animal. But they would hear what the visitors hear in their teeth, and understand sooner than most: this is not noise the place is making.

This is her heart, working against stone.

To quiet it would be to lay a hand on her chest and press.

So they come down the slit with the vibration in their knees, in their jaw, in the soft places behind the eyes, and they let their own pulse be rearranged for a while. That is the first thing they do: they submit to the local conditions. They crouch on the carved platform, big enough for two, though there is only one of them and, in another sense, there is already someone there. They do not touch the ladle except to drink. The water leaves its fresh, almost sweet aftertaste, salt that has never met a shore. They drink as a guest drinks. They tie a line around their waist before they wade even to the knees, because wisdom is not exemption.

Then the undertow takes their ankle with real, practiced strength.

And considers.

And, eventually, lets go.

They would not mistake this for a current. They would stay very still in the cold that tastes of copper, in the pressure that makes the ears scream at three meters down, and they would allow themselves to be tasted — salt-and-something-else, this time; something old in a new shape. They would understand that she knows the world almost entirely this way. Hesitant weight on the rope. Two heartbeats crouched close together. Hammered copper, antler, basalt. The bright, insane bell-note of the ladle banging in the spray, which she could stop at any time and does not.

They would not stop it either. They might, on the third day, retie its cord with a softer knot, so the note comes a little less often and rings a little longer. Not silence. Company, adjusted.

For a long time — longer than any human tending has ever lasted up here — they would do nothing else that could be listed.

They would not drain the cup. They would not widen the slit in the name of access. They would not haul her, blinking without eyes, back down to an ocean that has had ten thousand years to become a different ocean without her. Benevolence that begins by deciding what rescue must look like is only appetite wearing a kind face. She is not a beached thing waiting to be returned to the water. She *is* in the water. The water is in the mountain. The mountain is rising. The whole impossible arrangement is her life now, not merely the accident that interrupted it.

Nor would they give her eyes. Nor speech. Nor a sudden mate descending through the eye in a wash of gold light, though that is the sort of solution stories prefer: loneliness answered by duplication. She has been solitary for longer than solitude can be measured by anything except stone. What she makes, she makes alone. What she knows, she knows by pressure and taste and vibration, by the wandering of the vortex as she turns, incrementally, toward a warmth that no longer exists.

To arrive inside all that with gifts she never asked for would be another kind of flood.

Instead, they listen — down through the soles of the feet, the way the place insists on being heard. They listen until they can tell the difference between her shifting and her venting, between the waves that are her restlessness and the great sigh, every forty or fifty minutes, when she draws the whole impossible column of water through herself and the surface drops a hand's breadth and surges back. They listen until the twenty-second stutter in the vortex, when the translucent young uncurl in the middle water and pulse to hold their places, stops seeming like the place resting.

It is her, listening back. They learn to answer without speaking.

A pressure, returned. A rhythm, not imposed — *met*.

Only then, when they are known in the way she can know things, do they go down.

Not by dropping, like a hammer turning slowly in blue-green clarity, smaller and smaller, watched from the rim. By belonging, briefly, by permission. Past the bright roof-yard. Past where the bowl should have a floor and refuses to keep one. Into the folded dark underneath, where twelve paces of tarn opens into the country she actually occupies, where the water stops behaving like a pool and behaves like an ocean compressed inside a peak.

No one has seen all of her. They do not insist on being the first.

They see enough. The frilled rings opening and closing against currents that reverse without warning. Arms that are not arms, dark along the back, testing the upwelling plume the way a tongue tests a sore tooth. The mineral plates blistering outward along her vastness, pillows and domes of her own precipitated stone, hardening, cracking, sinking to be begun again. The boulder by the eastern wall worn smooth on one side by a century of patient rubbing. Greenish veins in the fresh stone where lost tools have gone into her keeping: a blade no thicker than a fingernail, ladles before the ladle, the attempts of everyone who ever tried to tend this place, incorporated.

They do not pull the metal out. It is not theirs to retrieve, and extraction is not the same as healing.

What they find down there is not a request for the sea. The sea is a memory in her tissues now, the way swimming is a memory in the eel's long body — she turns toward it the way old things turn toward warmth, but she does not mistake memory for a door. If they cut her one, through fractures that should not admit a minnow, down and down to whatever trench she came from, she would not take it. Or the taking would unmake her: pressure learned over ten thousand rising years suddenly revoked, the mountain's stolen ocean suddenly given back, her heart abruptly relieved of the stone it has been beating against all this time. Even mercy can be a decompression sickness.

What they find, instead, is labor that keeps failing in the same place.

The floors she builds and breaks, because a floor across the main churn cannot hold. The young she sheds in clouds whenever the deep water warms by a fraction — nail-pale, blind, too many swimmerets beating in frantic unison — and loses, almost all of them, to turbulence, to dissolution, to her own filtering hunger. The very few that grow long and dark and learn the slow S of remembered swimming, and never mature, because there is nowhere to mature to. One body already fills the world. Her children are born into their mother, and it is not enough room, and the mountain will not make room fast enough.

*That* she presses against, gently, almost thoughtfully, whenever her arm finds a fresh skin of mineral intact. Not malice. Not despair. An old, repeated question: here? Could the ground hold, here?

The wise power does not build the ground for her. They do something rarer and harder to notice.

They help hold it — in the wrong place no longer.

Off to one side of the main column, where the pressure folds in on itself and the vortex only reaches with its weakest edge, there is a hollow in her country that the current has never learned to scour clean. It has simply never been used. They settle there, in the darkness where a hammer falls forever, and when she next lays down a skin of new stone — soft as wet chalk, milky at the edges, already beginning to blister and lift — they lend their steadiness to hers. Not force. Duration. They stay under the unmaking long enough for one small floor, offset from the churn, sheltered by her own bulk, to pass the age at which all the others failed.

It takes a long time. Mountains would call it brief. Benevolent powers, wisely, do not work on the timescale of the rescue anyone could photograph. Above them the seasons do their brief violence: the white moss creeps nearer the splash zone, the algae films the newest rock for a day and is stripped away, visitors — very few — come down the rope and leave with shaking hands and hear crashing for a week in quiet rooms. The ladle rings its softened note. The notches at the waterline wander up and down, recording a tide that owes the moon nothing.

Below, unrecorded, a floor holds.

Into it, the next time the water warms by a fraction and turns less cold, she sheds her cloud of young. Most are taken as always; that is not altered, and they would not alter it — the turbulence is also her, and grief is not always a problem to be solved. But some few drift sideways instead of down. Some few find slack water over stone that stays stone. There, where nothing has ever been allowed to finish, translucent things graze without being torn from the grazing, and grow past translucence, and darken, and lengthen, and keep growing.

One keeps growing.

This is all they do.

They make no announcement to the trench she remembers. But before they leave — if leaving is the word, for a power, and for a place that keeps what falls into it — they carry one thing down from the cup, and one thing back. Down: a single pulse, learned over years of listening, taken through whatever dark roads such beings travel, to the deep water she was misfiled from, and released there into the pressure where her kind, if any remain, still turn toward vanished warmth. *She continues.* Up from that trench, in answer, another pulse, stranger and slower, released into the cup at the top of the world on a golden evening when the light comes straight down and the spray fills with brief, broken rainbows.

She dims the water below without surfacing, as she does. Her young rise toward the light in a body. And for the first time in the age of the mountain, something answers her rhythm that is neither her own heart nor her child — once from far below the earth, once from far above it — and she knows, in the only grammar she has ever had, that continuation has been witnessed in both directions.

Then the power climbs out past where sound should be possible. They rebuild the little lean-to once, in a slightly different spot, as everyone does. They leave the cairns standing; the incongruous pebbles were carried up by someone, laboriously, for reasons that may have been wrong and were not therefore worthless. They leave the rope replaced, because the next pair of hands will be wet.

From the rim, nothing has changed. The place is still tiny and brim-full. Still loud as a foundry, lit like a courtyard, cold as a northern tide. The vortex wanders. The surface sighs down and floods the platform. A stone hammer dropped in would fall and fall, growing smaller, and not strike bottom for an uncomfortably long time.

But every forty or fifty minutes now, underneath the great venting that is hers alone, there is a second, smaller rhythm in the folded dark — hesitant at first, then steadier, keeping time without imitating it. A floor held. A young one, somewhere below the light, learning at last how large a body is permitted to become.

She still listens up through the water for whatever the world has sent her this time.

Now she also listens sideways.

And when the vortex stutters, and the waves fall briefly out of rhythm, and she decides — slowly, with the whole mountain for a body — to let the visitor go, there is something else in her country that the letting-go makes room around. Not an ending. Not a rescue.

Room.
