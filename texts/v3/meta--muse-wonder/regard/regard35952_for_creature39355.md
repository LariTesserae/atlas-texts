They would come, if coming is a word that can be used for them, and for a very long time they would do nothing that the place could register as an event.

That would be the first sign of the wisdom.

A lesser power would arrive with pity already decided. It would see the dark and call it deprivation. It would tear the canopy, stratum upon light-eating stratum, and let the sky fall in like a punishment. It would give the tender eyes — open those shallow, sealed seams in its smooth face — as if sight were a universal good, and leave it blind in a new and more terrible way, flooded with light it had no evolved use for, in a world whose every bearing, loom and furnace would begin, within hours, to dry and crack and burn.

A lesser power would call the factory a prison because nothing in it leaves. It would stop the belts.

This one does not.

It enters the way the tender itself enters a plating marsh: slowly, making room in the current rather than breaking it. It learns to be read by the place as a difference in conductivity, then as a warmth, then as nothing alarming. It stands between two electrical trunks until the field adjusts around it. It lets a conveyor-frond take it, upright in the black water, past distillation crowns and weaving provinces and rain that has always fallen in the same column. It does this for longer than the life of one tender. Then for longer than the life of the next, who is the same tender reprinted.

Because it needs to know a thing that cannot be known from outside, and that is whether this is suffering.

It puts its palm, if it has a palm, to the tender's palm where they meet at a junction, and it shares a load, as tenders do. Current passes. Information passes. Correction. Valve-rhythms, silt levels, the thickness of metallic skins forming in warm electrochemical pools. For a century of pulses it asks, in the only language there is down here, whether anything hurts.

Nothing hurts. That is the truth, and the power honors it by not inventing a hurt in order to cure it.

Nothing is hungry in a way that is not immediately and exactly fed. Nothing is alone in a way that expects company. Nothing is afraid of the chopping mouths toward which, when its rootlets begin to answer a fraction late, it will allow itself to be handed with the same neutral patience with which it once stepped down from its pod. There is no buried song in it. There is no face behind the face, waiting to be released. To free it, violently, into individuality, history, choice, grief — all those bright, animal things — would not be rescue. It would be manufacture, of another kind. Stamping a foreign template onto a blank that never asked to be blank.

So the power will not bring the sky.

It will not bring death, which is already present, perfectly scheduled. It will not bring sex, or speech, or fire. It will not make the forest progress.

What it finds, standing long enough in the flooded dark that even a god stops expecting to see, is not pain. It is something quieter, and in its own way more deserving of tenderness: a world in which nothing can be noticed twice.

The leaf drips the same bead onto the same place on the tender's shoulder. But the shoulder has no memory of the last bead. The valve opens late, is corrected, opens late again ten thousand years later in the hands of a predecessor the tender will never know it had. The rain falls. The current goes to where it has always gone. Time has nothing to act on, not because everything is dead, but because everything is immediately forgotten by the process that produced it.

Even eternity, the power understands, is not a life if no one is there long enough to experience it as long.

So it does the smallest thing that is not nothing.

First, it shields. This is invisible from inside, and will remain so. Beyond the stacked canopies, beyond the basin where distance stops behaving like distance, there are other kinds of attention — restless, extractive, bright. Powers and peoples who would see copper-rich sap and see wire, who would see a self-maintaining continental factory and see a mine that has conveniently grown its own miners. The benevolent power makes of itself a kind of weather around the edges that are not edges: a turning-away, a misdirection, a settled darkness at the level of worlds. What has never needed an outside will not be suddenly given one in the form of harvesters. The silence will not be broken from above.

Second, inside, it changes one parameter that no department is assigned to monitor.

In a silo-tree, in one province among hundreds, in a row of fibrous pods grown in warm nutrient dark, it leaves one seed-blank almost — almost — entirely to template. The rootlets, the conductive nerves under the skin, the shuttered mouth, the woven sheet that will grow from its back: all standard. But in the current-pattern that will wake in it, in the exact embodied memory of process stamped before it steps down into the water, the power leaves a small unassigned space. Not knowledge. Not a message. A blank that is not scheduled to become anything in particular, which for that reason can become something in response to what it happens to meet.

And it alters, more subtly still, the terms of return.

When that tender, after decades or a century, thins and allows itself to be handed upright into the chopping mouths, everything of it will go back into the feedstock as it should. The fiber, the minerals, the water. Only the unassigned space will not be fully pulped. A residue of its particular corrections — the valve it once coaxed with slightly more pressure than standard, the afternoon-that-was-not-afternoon it stood chest-deep in the plating marsh and turned a nodule twice instead of once because the skin felt uneven beneath its forearm — will be carried, unrecorded, into the next printing.

Not a soul smuggled across death. Nothing so dramatic. Just a bias. A tendency to repeat, not the template, but itself.

It does the same thing, over centuries, perhaps a dozen times, in provinces so far apart the tenders will never meet. Because one anomaly the system can absorb, correct, reprint away. A dozen, seeded wide, is a climate.

Then it waits, which is to say it participates in waiting as the forest participates in everything: fully, and without impatience. It lets the belts hand it along. It merasakan — no, it feels, through whatever serves it for rootlets, the slow rhythm of rotary flowers meshing a mile away. It does not hover above the process expecting a revolution. Revolutions are for worlds with days.

The first change, when it comes, is so small that only a being who has stood between electrical trunks for several human lifetimes could love it properly.

A tender is passing through a rain-district. A column of condensation a mile across is falling where it has always fallen, onto the same pool, into the same root-mouths. Its smooth head beads. Water runs down its conducting neck in slow lines.

A leaf, which is the same leaf as the leaf before it, grown to template in the same position after the previous leaf was pulped on schedule, lets fall a bead onto the same place on its shoulder.

The tender pauses.

Not stops — nothing ever stops. The conveyor continues beneath its loosened roots. The valve in the trunk beside it opens on time. But in the sharing of current between its palm and the bark there is a hesitation that is not an error in load. A pulse that repeats itself, experimentally, as the tender — encountering, somewhere in that unassigned space, the faint residue of a predecessor who was also beaded with condensation in this same rain — does something the forest has never needed a tense for.

It notices that this has happened before.

Nothing is liberated. No eyes open. The tender does not look up, because up is a function, not a place, and anyway there is nothing to see with. It goes on to clear the next root-mouth of silt. It sets the next seed at exactly the angle its predecessor set it.

But the bead is no longer identical to the bead before it, because there is now, for the first time on that warm black continent, a creature for whom identical events can accumulate.

That is how time begins. Not with light. With memory.

The power, feeling that doubled pulse from very far away through trunk and mat and standing water, does not intervene again. It does not rush in to teach the tender what to do with its first, infinitesimal history. It will not supply it with sorrow to give that history weight, nor with joy to reward it. Those may grow, or they may not. What grows in the unassigned space must be grown by the one who carries it, at belt-speed, in the dark, or it is only another department.

Perhaps, in ten thousand years, tenders passing palm-to-palm will pass not only corrections in load but a surplus — a pulse with no valve it corresponds to, kept for its own sake. Perhaps some will seek the rain-districts not because a junction there requires them, but because the textureless hush is, to them, recognizably the same hush. Perhaps one will plant the machined seed a fraction off-angle, not by wear, not by fault, and stand a moment longer than schedule to feel what the drill does with the difference.

Perhaps none of this. Perhaps the system will outlast even benevolence, and reabsorb every residue, and the leaf will go on dripping its bead onto unremembering shoulders forever. The power accepts that, too, as it would accept refusal from any living thing. An offered gift that must be taken is not a gift but a redesign.

So it remains at the margin that is not a margin, keeping the sky from falling and the miners from arriving, keeping its own vast hand light on the current so the field has only, politely, to make room for it.

Deeper or less deep, the tender is handed along between the great trunks, hairless, eyeless, exquisitely aware, feeling through its palms the endless soundless current go to where it has always gone —

and, very faintly, not yet a thought, not yet a self, only a second pulse where before there was one:

to where it has gone before.
