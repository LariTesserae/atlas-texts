They would come over the rim on foot.

Not out of limitation — out of recognition. This is a place that measures arrival by who is willing to take an hour between pylons, who will accept the rotation at the trough. A power that arrived all at once, everywhere, in light, would be undoing in a second the one lesson the basin has spent centuries teaching everything that lives in it:

*Take only your fraction. Do not make the world depend on you.*

So they would arrive the way the shepherd does. Singly. Unhurried. At a great distance, at first, so that the herd animals could learn the shape of them as they learn any new rock.

And for a long time — a long time by human counting, a courteous introduction by the basin's — they would do nothing at all.

They would stand at dawn in the glittering hour with their hands cupped like everyone else, and wait their turn behind the antelope-goats and in front of the ground birds, and drink water that had been frost on a dark flank ten kilometers away the night before. They would walk out of the silent zones and back into them, listening to their own footsteps arrive and vanish. They would watch the slab turn a hand's breadth above its stone. They would not touch it to see if it would stop.

Benevolence that does not first learn how to be a guest is only conquest with better intentions.

What they would learn, standing there, is what you learn: there is nothing here to rescue.

The being is not trapped in the pylons. It is not a ghost in a ruined machine, waiting for its makers to come back down a track that has gone to grass, tally marks stopped mid-count on a hut wall. The makers are gone and it has metabolized their going. It grows mineral scar over its own cracks. It grows lichen, which is slower and, here, not entirely different. It has swift generations in its vents and troughs worn smooth by ten thousand tongues, and if one pillar fell tomorrow it would not die — it would simply be quieter in that corner for a hundred years, and make its frost a little more slowly, and go on.

It is not lonely. It is not incomplete. It is not asking, in any language of vibration through wet rock or feather-flash or edited light, to be made whole.

It has no whole. That is its wholeness.

A lesser power would try to give it one — a center, a face, a voice that could answer in sentences, an upgrade, a revelation, a purpose restored. A lesser power would repair it past the point where repair was wanted, make the lattice perfect, make the rain fall regularly, make the troughs run full, and call the resulting dependency *love*.

They would not.

They would do the only thing no one has ever been able to do for it, because no one else has ever been slow enough, or wide enough, to do it.

They would see it all at once, and let it know that it had been seen.

Not with a signal. Not with a surge through the buried conduits where the grass grows darker. With something nearer to what the frogs do at dusk: a pressure, laid gently against the whole structure at once — every pylon, every seam, the high hexagonal eyelid that never blinks — so that for one evening, and only one, the pattern that is usually only the relation between distant parts could feel itself *as* a pattern.

Here you are. Thirty unlike bodies and a net over the sky. Here is the shape your centuries have made.

No judgment in it. No instructions carried inside it like a seed. Just recognition, offered the way water is offered at dawn: available, and free to be refused.

If you had been there that evening, you would have noticed almost nothing. Perhaps the pillars on the rise hummed a fraction deeper in the teeth. Perhaps the hovering slab paused — though pausing, at its speed, would take a season to confirm. Perhaps the translucent manta in the noon shade of the towers, which was abroad unusually late, drifted in a circle it had never drifted in before, and then went on.

Whatever answer it made, if it made one, would not be for us. Companionship on its timescale does not look like conversation. It looks like both parties continuing, without needing to adjust.

And then, having done that inside the basin, they would go outside it, and there — only there — they would act without restraint.

Because the being can tend everything except the one thing that moves too fast for it.

It can edit sunlight. It cannot edit a survey team that arrives in a single summer with drills and a claim staked in a capital below the rim. It can make water from air a drip at a time. It cannot make a low country, warming and thirsting year by year, unsee the one high place where the grass stays alive in drought. It can quiet sound within its borders. It cannot quiet satellites, which have begun, in their patient, stupid way, to notice a hexagon in the light over an otherwise unremarkable bowl of rock, an absence of ultraviolet, a signature that does not match the geology.

Its agreement with its wilderness was made in a world where being overlooked was a form of safety. That world is ending faster than frost can form.

So the power would become, at the rim, what the lattice is at the sky: a filter.

Not a wall. Walls draw maps around themselves. A filter is more merciful and more thorough. Prospectors would find the passes a little harder than their instruments predicted, and the basin, when they finally looked into it, a little duller — dry rock, poor grazing, nothing a report could hold onto. A drone would record unexceptional heat and go on to more interesting coordinates. A road, proposed on paper far below, would keep encountering reasons — budgetary, geological, political, small and plausibly human reasons — to bend around a high empty place nobody could quite remember wanting.

No one would be harmed. No one would be thunderstruck away. They would simply find, each in the idiom of their own plans, that their attention slid off, the way rain slides in clean arcs around the invisible volume near the eastern pylon. A house-sized absence. Then a country-sized one.

And for the one figure who still crosses the long straight track, rarely, unhurried, with a shepherd's reasons that may be older than shepherding — the power would leave the track exactly as it is. Someone should still be able to come up and count, if counting comforts them, and set the tool down again unfinished. Trust, renewed, is also a kind of tending.

They would leave no new pylon. They would add no gift that would have to be maintained, explained, or repaid. To give this being anything it had to incorporate would be to make it partly theirs, and they did not come to acquire a garden.

Perhaps — this is the heart's part, not wisdom's, and a truly wise power permits its heart one small extravagance — perhaps on the last morning they would cup their hands at the trough a final time and then, instead of drinking, pour the water back.

Not needed. Not instructive. Just the first reciprocity in its long life that is exactly its own size: someone taking their place in the rotation, and then yielding it.

Then they would go back over the rim, and the basin would return to what it has always visibly been — a bright, silent, high emptiness, fierce at noon, glittering at dawn, full of lizards and lyre-horned herds and birds that speak in flashes of white because the air itself is listening.

Nothing would change.

That is how you would know it had been protected.

The lattice would still bend the killing light by one merciful degree. The pylons would still feather with impossible frost in the last of the evening. The slab would still turn, the frogs would still drum through stone, the swifts would still cut their soundless spirals with their shadows sweeping like slow clock hands across the floor. The being would still be nowhere because it is everywhere, still showing no whole because the basin is the size it grew to.

Only beyond the rim, in a world that has forgotten how to move at the speed of grass growing darker over a buried seam, something would now be moving at that speed on its behalf — holding the door of the world quietly shut, so that inside, for another ten thousand mornings, everything can go on being awake, and drinking, and taking only its turn.
