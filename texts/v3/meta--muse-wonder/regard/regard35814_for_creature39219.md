If such a power came to the Upper Sea, it would not come the way the rain comes, or the way a traveler comes up through the cloud.

It would arrive without displacing anything. Not a descent, exactly. More a gradual awareness, along the rim where the stilt-forests steam at low tide, that the light had been standing still a little too long. That the vapor rising off the shallows was curling not upward but around. That something was looking — and looking, there, is a physical act, like a root finding water or a warm current finding a valley in the drowned floor.

It would look for a long time before it understood what it was looking at.

A lesser power would have made mistakes quickly and with great tenderness. It would have seen the walkers on the bottom, blind under their leaf-vein membranes, setting their splay toes down in the ghost of a riverbed, and it would have wanted to lift the membrane. To give them back the sun. It would have heard the drifters singing in pressure and sternum-tremor, sentences a season long, and wanted to answer in a language that arrived all at once. It would have found the rooted elders smoothing footprints out of the clay and names out of the tower dust and called it loss, and out of love it would have tried to make things last.

The wise one does not.

It stays at the edge of visibility — the place where the big pale shapes already move, once in a summer, once in a childhood — and it learns the first rule of the sea by being subject to it. It makes no mark that does not begin, almost immediately, to soften. It speaks, early on, perhaps only to itself — a name, an intention, a prayer shaped like *let me help* — and watches the warm cloud of it hang legible before it and then be taken, gently and without malice, the way the water takes everything. Not refused. Just not allowed to remain sharp enough to wound.

That is its education, and it is slow even by the standards of a sea that counts time in tides.

It learns that the forgetting is not a failure to be corrected. It follows a drifter — at a distance so great that "following" is mostly a shared current — for what might be forty years, watching the fans spread to the columns of late-afternoon light, watching seeds burst open in the warmth of its wake. It comes to understand that the creature does not need its walking years returned to it as story. They are already in it, laid down as mineral rings in bone, as green deepening into frond. To give it memory in the human shape would not be to restore it. It would be to ask the middle of a tree to grieve for the seed, forever, with a mind. The elders who read the dust letter by letter, in order, are not censors. They are kind. The power, being benevolent, recognizes a kindness larger and older than its own impulse to preserve, and it bows to it — insofar as something without a neck can bow, insofar as something that is mostly attention can make itself low.

So it does not heal their eyes.

It does not teach them fire in the dry bell-chamber, or writing that resists the dust, or any of the bright, edged arts by which the people below the clouds insist that a thing loved must be a thing kept.

What it does is take the burden at the other end.

Somewhere — not in the sea, which would refuse the weight, and not below the clouds, where it would become a word and erode off the tongue — the power begins to remember *for* them.

Every name smoothed out of the tower dust, it keeps, in the order it was read. Every footprint pressed back into the clay floor by questing roots in the night, it keeps the shape of, not as a cast or a record but as one keeps the memory of a face one will not see again: without using it. Every walker's first breath in the pocket beneath the second surface; every drifter's season-long sentence, bell to bell, meadow to meadow; every elder who spends fifty years deciding to stop and goes hollow and fills with airy water — it holds them all, outside the system, so that inside the system nothing has to.

This is not a small thing. It is the one gift the sea cannot give itself, because the sea's whole mercy depends on not holding on. Forgetting there can only be innocent if somewhere, very far from the warm water, forgetting is not the same as vanishing. The power becomes that somewhere. It asks nothing for it. It does not let the drifters feel watched in any way that would gather into self-consciousness; it does not let the rooted ones, tasting a disturbance ten miles off through the fine hairs of their roots, taste *being remembered* and have to reckon with it. It carries the archive the way the bowl of the continent carries the sea: by being a shape that does not announce itself as a shape.

And then — because benevolence that only witnesses can become, over enough centuries, a refined form of doing nothing — it does one physical thing, and does it badly on purpose, over a span of time that makes it indistinguishable from geology.

Far down, below the deepest stacked surface, below where the inside rain accumulates and never lands, the floor is still a continent being worn away. The power finds what the sea, in its patience, has not yet noticed or has chosen to accept: a crack in the old bowl, not at the eastern rim where the waterfalls fall and rise again and almost nothing is lost, but in a drowned valley in the west, where the ghost-currents run sideways and the sediment falls along channels instead of down. In another ten thousand years — a short time, there — the crack would learn to be a drain. The stacked seas would thin, one by one, from the bottom. The second surface would sink. Children would push their heads up through it looking for the airy layer and find heavier water, and heavier, and the walking years would shorten, and the forests that grow up and down to meet themselves would meet in emptier water and not recognize the meeting.

The sea would not die dramatically. It would simply, at last, smooth itself away.

The power does not seal the crack. Sealing is sharp. Sealing lasts, and lasting is the one violence this place has spent an age unlearning. Instead it does what the elders do to a footprint: it presses, patiently, in the direction of unmarkedness. It encourages stone that already wants to soften to soften *toward* the fissure. It nudges the sideways sediment, decade by decade, grain by grain, to fall a little more nearly along the crack and lodge there — not as a repair, not as a wall, but as the valley remembering, very slowly, that it was once whole. It warms nothing. It cools nothing. Where the vents breathe a degree thinner than they did a millennium ago, it does not stoke them; it only removes, atom by atom, the slow mineral crust that would in time have choked them, the way you might clear a sleeping child's airway with one finger and then sit back in the dark and do nothing else all night.

It will take longer than the tower has been breaking the surface. When it is done, no one will be able to point to it, including the power. That is how it will know it did it correctly.

It also stands guard, in the only way guarding can be done without turning a place into a fortress, which is another kind of edge.

For others will come, eventually — not this power only. Curiosity rises like vapor from the world below the clouds, and not all of it is gentle. Nets are being invented down there even now, in valleys that have never seen the sea, by hands that would dream of the pale shapes and wake wanting to hold one still. The power does not punish the dreaming. The dreams are a bridge the sea itself seems to want, a word eroding off the tongue being, after all, still a visit. But it thickens the cloud, almost imperceptibly, on the paths of those who climb with hooks and jars and measurements. It makes the steps that continue under the surface continue, for them, a little sooner. It lets the airy upper layer taste, to a mouth that comes to take rather than to breathe, fractionally more like drowning — not enough to kill. Enough to instruct. And for the rare one who climbs the way the power itself arrived, empty-handed and prepared to be forgotten by the place they love, it lets the mist be only mist, and the rain be only rain, and the sea decide for itself, moment by moment, not to drown them.

It saves no one in particular. It prolongs no elder past the fifty slow years of their stopping. When a drifter's fans thicken and its chambers fill with sweet still water and it roots at the rim on an inhalation it does not entirely exhale, the power does not whisper *stay*. It remembers the drifting, and lets the rooting happen, and feels — this is the cost of the archive, and it pays it willingly — the small, real grief that the sea has arranged not to have to feel. Grief, carried at a distance so the griefless can go on being whole.

At the very end of its visit — if *end* is not another lie, down there where endings take half a century — it goes once to the tower at lowest tide, ducks up out of the water into the bell-chamber where the walls are dry and dusty and covered in paintings of a sun with too many rays, and it stands dripping in air that has not been wet in ten thousand years, listening to its breath come back loud after the silence. The dust hangs and considers it.

It does not write its name. It has learned better, and worse: it has learned that to be read, letter by letter, in order, by the patient roots of the rooted ones, and then smoothed tenderly back into dust, would be the greatest honor this place can offer a guest — and that a truly wise guest does not ask a kindness to perform itself for his benefit.

It just stands there a while, being breathed-in by a dry room inside a warm sea on top of a worn-away world. It looks at the painted sun until it cannot tell whether the extra rays are a mistake, a prophecy, or simply how the sun looked before the flood, to people who built low and round against the wind.

Then it goes back under. It leaves the chamber exactly as it found it, which is to say it leaves it already forgetting him, the wet prints of whatever served it for feet softening, widening, healing in fast-forward behind it.

From a high root-raft, afterward — if afterward means anything where fields pulse in sequence far below like a conversation — the glow still travels in the dark. Sentences still take seasons. Walkers still walk the old roads. The rain still falls inside the sea and does not land. Almost nothing is lost, and almost nothing leaves, and the power makes sure that the *almost* never quite closes, because a system that lost nothing at all would be a prison, and a people who could never be left would never finish becoming trees.

It takes its memory and goes. Not far. Just far enough that remembering does them no harm.

And if you should climb, someday, through the warm cloud, through the black-green forests pressing close, up the rounded steps and under — if you should find the tower dust considering you and your name, written with a finger, softening not evenly but in order, letter by letter, as if being read — do not think the sea has changed its mind about you.

It hasn't. It is only that somewhere beyond its rim, something kind is reading along.
