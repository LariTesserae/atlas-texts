First, they would wait.

Not the waiting humans do — impatient, checking, filling silence with intention — but waiting at the tempo the valley has taught everything that lives in it. A mirror's arc. A flywheel's turn. The slow unpeeling and re-adhering of a belly against wet moss.

To arrive any faster would be a kind of violence here.

So the power does not descend in light. The valley already has too much light, brilliance without warmth, and it would not welcome more. It does not speak. Sound dies within metres, smothered by leaf over leaf and young hollow rock, and the resident has no ears for it anyway. It has never needed a voice. What it knows comes up through pressure, through taste, through the faint change in moisture when a charged cable hums in its teeth.

If the power is to be known at all, it will have to be known that way.

It settles, therefore, not beside the dome — beside is a human nearness — but into the ground at a respectful distance, so that through scree and root-mat and the seams of pipe, the resident feels a new, steady presence. Not a crawler. Not boots. Not a flywheel spending the noon in darkness. Something even, and patient, and not hungry.

The rim-eyes close in sequence. Then, after most of a day, they open again.

That is the first conversation.

---

It would be easy, for a benevolent power, to make mistakes here out of kindness.

To warm the dome would be a mistake. Its blood runs at the temperature of shade; its green symbionts darken and endure the frost pad by pad, and it lets itself be sealed in without protest. Warmth would wake it into a world it has not budgeted for.

To heal its scars would be a mistake. The scars are a map of vanes leaned against, rocks crossed, winters survived. They are how the eastern domes differ from the domes near the furnaces — if they differ at all, and are not one being surfacing, fingertip-like, wherever the machines make living kindest.

To give it sharper eyes would be cruelty. It has a film the colour of bottle glass against the glare for a reason. It sees what it needs: shadow advancing, frost coming, the rigid heat above the furnaces. The rest it tastes at dusk with a tongue broad and dark as a wet leaf.

To make it faster would kill it outright. Its whole wisdom is in its slowness. It does not hunt; it lets the valley come. It does not flee the technician; it draws its pads marginally tighter and is mistaken, usefully, for vegetation. It drinks by cooling a pipe with its tongue until a droplet, inside, hesitates and climbs a few centimetres toward it. A quicker creature would tear the moss it depends on and call the tearing progress.

And to lift it — rescue it, relocate it, exalt it, explain it to itself — would be the worst mistake of all, dressed as love.

It is not unfinished. It is not trapped. Watching it through one full turning, from dawn intention to evening completion, the power understands: this is not a life waiting to begin. This is a life that has solved, almost perfectly, the problem of its place. The machines leak water; it shades the vanes. The plants hold dew; its hairs knit the scree so the pipes are not buried. The installation was built by absent hands and runs on a geological tempo, and the resident has matched that tempo so completely that it is hard to say whether it adapted to the machines, or whether the machines, in their quiet efficiency, accidentally built a climate worthy of it.

Benevolence that does not understand this would break it in an afternoon and call the breaking a gift.

Wisdom does nothing of the kind.

---

What the power does, instead, is look further than the resident can — not deeper, but further in time.

It sees the hairline fatigue in a vane fin, three winters from failure. It sees a bearing in a flywheel pit that will, in eleven years, begin to sing against its sheathing, the first real noise the valley has produced in a generation, and then seize. It sees the technician ageing in his careful handwriting, the logbook entries drifting further apart. It sees, beyond him, the ordinary human futures leaning toward this place like weather: a survey that finds the condensate yield interesting. A company that finds it not interesting enough. An expansion. A decommissioning. Men coming up through the thin air not one at a time, in a suit hung like a shed skin, but in numbers, with instructions to clear the growth around Array Four because this year the moss is thicker again and someone far downhill has finally stopped believing it helps.

The resident cannot plan against these things. It can endure weather; it cannot endure being understood by the wrong people, or being forgotten by the right ones.

Here, a power may act — not upon the being, but upon the circumstances that ring it, the way the resident itself acts upon a pipe: by a gradient too subtle to register.

So somewhere downhill, in a database no one loves, the valley's coordinates acquire a small and permanent clerical discouragement. Surveys route around it without anyone remembering deciding to. The production figures remain exactly as they have always been: modest, stubbornly efficient, never quite worth expanding, never wasteful enough to close. A line in a training manual — *do not clear growth at vane bases; clearing reduces yield* — survives every revision, though no one can find the study that proved it. Funding continues in the unglamorous way that water continues here: not as a river, but as condensation. Enough. Everywhere. Invisibly renewed.

In the valley itself, nothing shines and nothing speaks. But a fatigued fin, cooling one night, finds its hairline crack no deeper in the morning than it was the night before, and somehow no deeper the winter after. A flywheel turns, and will keep turning longer than its makers guaranteed. A pipe joint that would have begun, eventually, to leak its water uselessly into deep scree instead keeps its slow pulse downhill a few decades more — long enough, perhaps, for the root-mat and the domes and the fern species that grow only in mirror fields to learn how to hold the climate together with a little less help. Long enough for dependence to become, gradually, inheritance.

These are not miracles. The power is careful that they should not look like miracles. Miracles attract attention. It leaves no signature the resident could become dependent on, no new rhythm it would have to learn. It gives the valley not eternity — eternity would be another trap — but time, which is the only currency the resident has ever spent well.

---

And one other thing. The smallest thing. Perhaps, in the end, the only thing that is a gift rather than a protection.

On the last evening — though evening is only a thickening of the blue — the power does not withdraw all at once. It remains in the ground through the valley's one permitted hour, when the temperature drops below dew point and every vane and leaf and cable sheds its droplets, and condensation ticks and drips in the undergrowth, each sound near and private and swallowed immediately. It remains while the resident swells almost imperceptibly with taken water, as the storage bladders swell. It remains while the broad dark tongue comes out and moves, tasting, from pad to pad, from vane to rock point: mineral condensate, ozone, spores, hot metal cooling.

And it lets itself, just once, be tasted.

Not a flavour from elsewhere. Not salt from some distant sea, not a revelation carried in like a message. That would be a story imposed on a tongue. Only this: a faint, clean mineral note in the water beading nearest the dome, very slightly different from the day's usual work. Something the tongue pauses over. Considers. Accepts.

Through its belly, in the language it actually speaks, the resident registers the steady presence beside it in the earth — not threat, not food, not weather. Company, for the length of one rotation. Someone else also willing to be still here. Someone who came, and asked for nothing, and did not need it to become anything else in order to be worth attending to.

Then the cold seals the valley. Frost feathers over the dome pad by pad, redrawing the map in white. The presence withdraws the way the light withdraws from the sharp young peaks — not fading, simply gone, leaving no warmth behind to confuse the frost.

By next week, the dome will be against a different fin, where the drip falls cleaner.

By next year, a little further on.

The power, having drawn close enough to love the place, does the rarest thing a power can do: it leaves it capable of continuing without it — and it carries away, into whatever vast and noisy elsewhere it came from, the memory of a green bowl under a violet sky where almost nothing happens, slowly, in complete adequacy, and where being left alone, properly and watchfully and at exactly the right distance, was the whole of mercy.
