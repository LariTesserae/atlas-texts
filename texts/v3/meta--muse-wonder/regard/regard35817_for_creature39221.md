If it is truly benevolent, and truly wise, it will not descend the way a visitor descends.

It will know better than to arrive as a body.

A body, however kind, bends the chord. A body brings a heartbeat that does not know how to be quiet, brings sweat — free water, the one obscenity this place cannot use — brings cloth and metal and hunger and the expectation that a room, once entered, should respond. Even love, arriving in that shape, would be a disturbance laid over the note. The garden would feel it in its roots. The resident would feel it in the floor. The moths would alter their circle for it, and nothing here has altered its circle for anyone in longer than counting can hold.

So first, the power listens from far away, for a long time, the way the place itself has taught everything in it to listen: not over the sound, but underneath it.

And what it hears, underneath, is not a cry for help.

That is the test, and most powers, even kind ones, would fail it.

From above, from the white salt and the comprehensible world, this looks like an emergency preserved in amber. A person gone blind in the heat. A person gone deaf in the noise. No water. No day. No other voice. Food that creaks. Companions who are beetles, who are lizards, who are the rooted husks of the dead. Every instinct of rescue rises up clean and righteous: bring light. Bring cool clean water in a sealed skin. Bring medicine for the filmed eyes, bring a hand under the elbow, bring them *up*, for God's sake, up into the air where a person can be a person again.

The wise power does none of that. Because it has listened long enough to understand what each gift would do here.

Water, free water, would be the end of the world. It would darken the black sand. It would soften what has only ever survived by staying sealed. It would wake rot in a garden that has never had to learn rot, wake it in the fern fiber, in the basket, in the dry beautiful architecture of the dead holding up the living. Corpses here do not decay; that is not a failure of the place, it is the mercy of the place. Introduce damp and a hundred patient years would begin, all at once, to spoil.

Light would be a second ending. The crystals have never been seen clearly. The plants do not want it. The lizards have been honest and closed their eyes entirely; the resident has been slower, and the milkiness is not damage to be reversed but a negotiation concluded. To flood that amber dark with white would not reveal the room. It would erase the terms on which the room can be known.

Silence would be the third ending, and the worst. Stop the vent, ease the pressure, give the poor creature *quiet* — and the hearing-fronds lose the sun they grew toward. The ferns lose the hand that keeps them trembling. The moths fall out of a current that has held even their dead aloft. The resident loses the wall of sound against which a spine learned, vertebra by vertebra, to think. You do not rescue a fish by draining, mercifully, the sea.

And rescue itself — the hand under the elbow, the coaxing upward — the power understands that for rescue too. The one in the listening wall is not trapped. The seam is four meters up. Skin remembers the way. What has happened here is not imprisonment. It is consent, renewed in the only tense this place allows: a continuous present, a chord held. To carry them blinking into the salt glare would not be freedom. It would be exile from the one arrangement of the world their body has, slowly and at cost, become able to inhabit.

So inside the room, the power does nothing.

But "nothing" — done by such a power, in such a place — is not absence. It is exact. It is the hardest discipline it owns.

It will not add a flat stone to the listening wall, though it can see the one place where the vibration escapes.
It will not clear the fern scale from the mouths of the stone tubes, though its attention passing over them is enough to make the work of a fingertip unnecessary for a year.
It will not sort the seeds.
It will not finish the tally.

That stopped tally may be the thing it honors most. Seven, and seven, and seven, and then — not broken, not despairing — the knife set down. The counting arrived at the truth and stopped. A lesser kindness would want to complete the pattern, to leave one more notch as a sign: *you were noticed; the count was kept for you.* The wise power leaves the stick exactly as honest as it found it. It does not turn a person's surrender into a message about itself.

If it makes itself known at all, it will be in the resident's own language, once, and through the floor.

Not a voice in the air. The air is occupied, and respectfully left alone.
Not a touch on the shoulder. The hands here are for receiving, and they are already placed.

Perhaps this: on an ordinary unmarked stretch of the chord — there are no days, so it must choose a moment that will not become an anniversary — a pattern arrives under the resident's palms that is not beetle, not lizard, not fern, not the settling of their own weight. Seven small pressures through the black sand. A pause that is not a falter in the vent. Seven again. And then a single, unmistakable meaning laid down the way the resident once laid two fingers between themselves and a visitor:

*Here. Here is the surface. Here is where to speak — if you ever wish to.*

No question. No summons. An answering surface, offered and immediately left open. And with it, wordlessly, the only assurance that could be given without lying: that the seam above is still passable, that the world has not forgotten the shape of a person, that staying is still a choice and not a sentence — and that not choosing, ever, is also acceptable.

Then it withdraws the pattern before it can become an event. Before it can become a day. The resident may resettle their spine and take it for a deep settling of stone, and that is permitted. Being known was not meant to become another thing they have to carry.

All of its real work is done outside, where the resident cannot see it and the room will never have to thank it.

Because the threat to this place was never inside it. The room finished becoming itself early and sealed itself under a pan of salt and has kept its own counsel perfectly. The threat is the age approaching above it: the surveyor's drone, the lithium claim, the seismic line, the well-meaning biologist with a drill and a grant and a refrigerated sample case, the pilgrim with a video camera looking for a hermit to be wise at them. The world up there has learned to find every seam. It has learned to price brine, to bottle silence, to sell extremity. It would love this place to death in a single season, and call the loving documentation.

So the power becomes, out there, almost nothing as well — but a nothing with edges.

A survey plane's instruments smear, politely, over one uninteresting hectare of salt. A road that was going to be straight finds excellent reasons, in committee, in cost reports, in the sudden protected status of some quite ordinary lizard two valleys away, to be straight somewhere else. A graduate student who dreamed three nights running of a roar under a buckled crust wakes the fourth morning with the dream gone mild and a different, genuinely promising site on her mind. A man who would have come down with skin to spare and a head full of extraction comes down, instead, in the first deafening minute, with the plain animal certainty that he should climb back up — and does, and never quite says why, and is kinder to his children for a month without knowing the source of it.

Those rare ones who *should* find it still find it. The power does not presume to gatekeep the mystery; it only filters the appetite. The ones who arrive needing noise more than they need an audience. The ones capable of learning to speak through a floor. They will lay a cheek near the crust, as finders always have, and hear the roar before their hands find the seam, and lose a little skin, and be let in by the place itself, which has never needed help deciding.

And far below anything a drill would think to reach, where plates shift on timescales that make the room's stillness look brief, the power does one last thing, very slowly, and then stops. It eases — not fixes, not freezes, *eases* — the deep stresses that would one day have sheared the fissure shut or blown it wide. Not forever. Forever is another kind of violence here; even this garden is not owed eternity. Only this: that the end, when geology finally insists on it, will come at the pace the place itself lives at. Slowly enough for fronds to finish uncurling. Slowly enough for a resident to lie down, if they choose, where the roots can come politely through. Slowly enough that nothing here will ever have to be surprised.

Then the power leaves the way attention leaves a held note — not by departing, exactly, but by ceasing to press on it.

The red seam throws its same reflection up onto wet-looking stone. A crystal rings in somebody's molars. A moth tears a wing, snags, and is slowly made into scaffolding. In the hollow of the listening wall, a fitted spine receives the chord, vertebra by vertebra, and two hands lie palm-down on warm sand — receiving, among the beetle-tick and the lizard-shift and the shush of fern on fern, one pattern that came once and will not come again, and was enough.

The resident does not look up. There is nothing up there they have not already answered.

The place remains unrescued, unruined, unkept — and, for the first time in the long attention of the world above it, deliberately, actively, *let be*.

Which is what love looks like, when it finally learns the local language.
