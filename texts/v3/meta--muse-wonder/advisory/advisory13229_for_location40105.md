This is not a wilderness, a city, or a ruin. It is a perfectly maintained, continent-sized industrial ecosystem that has outlived its purpose and now exists only to maintain itself.

It will not attack you. That is not the danger. The danger is heat, sameness, biological density, and being quietly absorbed - physically and psychologically - into a system that registers everything as either flow, stock, waste, or repair.

Prepare as if you are visiting a hot, wet, living factory floor the size of a continent, where you are the only uncontrolled variable.

### 1. Understand the core conditions

**It is always 31°C.** At skin height, everywhere, indefinitely. Warmer over heat-exchange fields, a few degrees cooler in the rain belt, but it always returns to 31°C. There is no night cool-down, no seasonal relief, no cold room unless you bring it yourself. This plus near-total humidity means your sweat will not evaporate efficiently. Heat exhaustion is a constant background risk, not an emergency scenario.

**There is no ground.** Only floor, then more floor. Plates, pipes, ducts, older floors, maintenance voids, for at least 400 meters down. You cannot dig, stake a tent, bury waste, find soil, or go "off" the infrastructure. Everything you drop will be found, swept up, and processed.

**There is no plant life. At all.** No grass, weeds, trees, shade, wood, leaves, pollen, or plant food. Seeds brought in will die on the heated, salted, lightly electrified surfaces, but do not test this deliberately. Surfaces that look slimy-green or pink are not algae or moss - they are chemosynthetic bacterial/fungal film grown on chemical dosing pipes. It smells of brine and warm bread. It is food for the system, not for you.

**It does not change.** Weather is geography. A permanent thunderhead rains over the western condensers with a boundary sharp enough to wet one shoulder. Twenty km east is permanent clear hot-iron air. Between is a permanent fog province. Temperature, animal density, water levels, light in the sheds, even dust, are actively corrected back to a baseline. Aerial photos decades apart are identical. Your footprints will be erased by repair-crawlers. Landmarks will be repainted, replaced, and restored before you can use decay to navigate.

Assume any marker you leave will be removed within hours. Assume any damage you cause will be repaired around you.

### 2. Medical and bodily preparation

This is the most important section.

**Heat and hydration kit:**
- 3+ liters/day capacity, more if walking. You will sweat constantly.
- Electrolytes. The air smells of salt and ammonia; you will lose salt and not notice because everything is already damp.
- Light, loose, synthetic clothing that dries fast. Cotton will stay wet, chafe, and mildew in a day. Bring 3 rotating sets minimum.
- No heavy pack. At 31°C and saturated humidity, exertion heat builds fast. Use wheeled transport / the service trains.
- A personal thermometer / core-temp monitor and a hard turnaround rule. Locals - there are effectively none - won't notice you're overheating because the environment never signals danger.

**Skin, lungs, and infection:**
The film is everywhere on wet surfaces. Canals have olive-black film, feeder trays mother-of-pearl pink film, plus fungal thread, bacterial paste, animal waste slurry, and warm standing water in maintenance voids.

- Waterproof boots with aggressive grip. Plates are ridged for wet grip, but film is slicker than ice in patches.
- Never walk barefoot. Canal corners are sharp enough to cut, and any cut will be immediately inoculated.
- Waterproof wound dressings, antiseptic, antifungal powder/cream. At constant warmth and wetness, athlete's foot, groin rot, and infected blisters develop in hours.
- A respirator or at least fitted masks for processing sheds, nursery levels, and pellet plants. Background smell is iron, brine, milk, hot rubber, and clean ammonia. Long exposure irritates eyes and airways even when it smells "clean."
- Eye protection against spray curtains and overhead cattle sprayers.
- Do NOT eat the film, feed pellets, or "marsh" slurry. It is grown from nitrates, phosphates, ash, ground shell, rendered protein, and recycled waste in a closed loop that has been recycling the same biomass for an extremely long time. It is calibrated for trough cattle, scurry-hens, eels, and bats that were bred for it over generations. For an ordinary human it risks prion-like accumulation, bacterial overload, mineral toxicity, and simply having no plant fiber at all.
- Do NOT drink canal, basin, reservoir, or rain-belt water without full testing and treatment, even though it looks clear and levels are perfect. It has been drunk, urinated, filtered by eels, scrubbed, and drunk again, indefinitely, by design. The system considers it clean. Your gut microbiome will not agree on first contact. Bring sealed water, or a filter + UV + chemical treatment stack, and test for ammonia/salts.

Bring all your own food, sealed. Prefer salty, calorie-dense, no-cook rations with fiber supplements. There is nothing to forage. There is nothing fresh that is not animal or bacteria.

Get sleep discipline in place before you arrive: eye mask, earplugs that still let you hear water/alarms, a reliable watch/timer not dependent on light. In sheds, lamps supplement sun to constant working brightness. There is no work-night. Your circadian rhythm will drift within 36 hours.

Set a hard maximum stay. For a first visit, 48-72 hours. Inspectors report that after day two they stop feeling like they are traveling at all.

### 3. Clothing and equipment checklist

Essentials:
- Grippy waterproof boots, ankle support - yards are wet, rails and troughs everywhere
- Gloves - sharp tile, hot plates (some over heat exchangers), electrified floor sections
- Rain shell that is only a shell - you will be wet from rain in one district, condensation in fog, spray in yards, and sweat everywhere else. Ventilation matters more than insulation.
- Wide hat / sun protection for clear districts - there is zero shade
- Headlamp - not because it's dark, but because under-floor voids, shed rafter spaces, and nursery tiers are dim in a constant, shadowless way and details vanish
- Dry bags for everything. Everything drips.
- Personal locator / dead-man beacon that transmits continuously to your extraction point, independent of local infrastructure
- Inertial navigation, paper maps with distances, compass, pedometer. GPS, if it works at all in your multiverse transit, will show you an endless repeating texture of sheds, reservoirs, silos, brood halls, and yards. Visual navigation fails because the composition repeats every production loop: reservoir -> brood hall -> growing yard -> gantry -> processing shed -> pellet plant -> silo -> reservoir.
- Distinctive, high-visibility outer marker that makes you unmistakably *not livestock* - bright color not used on site (the palette is gray-green, pale cattle pink, concrete, ceramic white), reflective, and odd-shaped. Keep it on at all times.

Leave behind: seeds, soil, unsealed food, wooden items you care about, leather that will mold, anything scented that might attract mite-swarms or scoop-bats, pets, and any insect life in your gear. Shake out and sterilize packs before transit. You are a contamination vector to a closed loop, even if the loop will probably just kill what you bring and dose it away.

### 4. Movement and navigation

Use the central aisle trains and service trains, and confirm they are passenger-rated. You can ride six days at freight speed and not reach an edge. Most lines loop long before the sea-cliff.

- Never assume walking distance. "Next district" may be 20 km of identical yards.
- Log time, train ID, direction, and odometer continuously. Repair crawlers will erase chalk, tape will be peeled as wear, cairns will be swept into process.
- Travel in pairs at minimum, with scheduled check-ins to an off-site anchor. Loneliness doesn't work normally here - the sameness dissolves the sense of progress, and people keep going thinking they are almost somewhere different.
- Stay off conveyor belts, submerged feed belts, transfer lines, and crane rails. Picking cranes are live animals harnessed to counterweighted gantries with steel-tipped beaks, wing-bones pinned to the frame, sorting carcasses with unsupervised precision. They are not hostile, but they sort by shape, mass, and movement on the line. Do not be on the line.
- Watch for freight lifts. For every animal that dies above, one of the same mass is already ascending to replace it. Lifts, pits, and below-floor machinery operate on mass-balance, not on human schedules.

Major physical hazards are mundane: slipping into ankle-deep washing yards and then into canals, being sprayed down with cattle, falling into a maintenance void when a plate is up, stepping on a lightly electrified weed-killing floor, getting caught between flank-to-flank trough cattle that do not jostle, do not low, and do not make way - they stand oriented to water flow, breathing almost in unison. They won't trample you in panic. They will simply not notice you enough.

If you are injured, unconscious, or immobile in a processing or yard area, you are at real risk of being treated as dead stock by automated sorting. That is why the beacon, the buddy, and the bright marker are not optional.

### 5. Weather provinces - dress for borders

You do not forecast here, you cross borders.

- **Rain belt:** Permanent curtain rain under the condenser thunderhead. Waterproof everything, protect electronics, expect collector avenues running fast. One step sideways can be dry.
- **Clear belt:** Hot iron smell, full sun, no shade, heat-exchange glare. Sunburn and heatstroke zone.
- **Fog province:** Warm, luminous, dripping mist all day. Visibility drops, shed lights glow, animals emerge dark with moisture. Navigation and depth perception fail; go slow near canals and eels' sluices.
- **Cooling walls:** Weeping "cliffs" of roughened concrete sweating condensation. Very slippery, salt-crusted, nozzle sprays keep them perpetually damp.

Re-check footing at every transition. Wet-to-film-to-dry changes traction instantly.

### 6. Behavior rules

1. **Do not pry up plates** except in a designated service aisle with a guide/permit-equivalent from whatever maintains the plaques. Below is not earth - it's live pipes, then another floor, then voids of warm water. Falls and electrocution are unmarked.
2. **Do not feed, free, or interfere with animals.** Scurry-hens are blind and featherless pellet-cleaners, filter-eels knot by the hundreds at grates, mite-swarms are aerial plankton for scoop-bats furred under rafters. Every ration and enclosure is sized to its species. A handful of outside food unbalances a local loop and triggers correction - usually by disposing of the excess.
3. **Do not try to rescue the cranes.** They cannot fly away. Intervention injures them and the harness system will replace the individual to keep pattern mass identical.
4. **Contain all your waste.** Food wrappers, urine, wash water, shed skin and hair - the system will try to contain it for you. Use sealed waste bags and take them out with you. Do not add your microbiome to the film if you can help it.
5. **Do not stay to watch processing sheds if you are squeamish about soundlessness.** The quiet is total - humidity swallows noise, machinery is electric and pit-set. What you'll hear everywhere is water dripping, spray hush, and the accumulated breath of billions of animals like a wind that never rises. Many travelers find the quiet more distressing than a roar would be.

The only genuinely wild place is the far edge: a ceramic sea-cliff where treated water pours in a thousand even falls to an unused ocean, and millions of gulls nest on discharge ledges, screaming. They are unregistered, eat overspill eels, and their eggs and droppings are washed off. They are tolerated because their population is always the same size. It's a useful extraction landmark, but the ledges are sheer, wet, guano-slick, and the falls will knock you off.

### 7. Psychological preparation

This place has run the same day forever. Plaques have been replaced identically 4,000 times to stay looking new. No accumulation, no loss, no before/after. Absence of demand reads to the system as perfect demand.

Expect:
- Loss of time sense
- Déjà vu so strong it becomes nausea
- A feeling of being gently corrected - temperature, sound, animal density, even repaired dust seem to smooth around you
- Existential flattening: no birth or death means anything individually when replacement mass is already in the lift

Countermeasures: keep a handwritten log with timestamps, force sleep/wake cycles, change clothes on schedule (not when they feel dirty - they always feel damp), talk to your companion about things from outside, and set a non-negotiable extraction time before you enter. Do not extend because "it's fine here." It is always fine here. That is the trap.

### 8. Extraction

Confirm your exit anchor works from inside a shed, not just in open sky - roofs are translucent amber panels, gantries and cranes cross overhead constantly. Tell your anchor your mass, your waste bags' mass, and that you expect the Works to have tried to compensate for your presence. Leave nothing behind you value; if you leave something, it will be rendered, harvested as salt crust, swept as dust, or replaced identically until it looks like it was never yours.

On return, quarantine your gear. Boots, clothes, and lungs will carry film spores, salts, and ammonia residues. Shower, antifungal treatment, and do not introduce rinse water to local soil/plants until sterilized - not because the Works' bacteria are aggressive predators, but because they are optimized for a world with no plants, and you don't want to test their learning curve.

---

**Bottom line:** Dress for a permanent hot rain that sometimes isn't raining, bring all your own plant food and clean water, navigate by instrument and time instead of landmarks, stay visible and off the production lines, and leave on schedule.

It will look exactly the same when you leave as when you arrived. It looked like that when the plaques were installed.
