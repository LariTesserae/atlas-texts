The air is thin, but the light… the light is almost painful. It bleeds off the snowfields in a blinding glare, reflecting up from the ice-slicked rocks and the glassy, frozen waterfalls that claw down the mountainside. It’s a raw, immediate light that strips away shadows and makes distance hard to judge.

The scale is deceptive too. What looks like a gentle rise is a sheer cliff; a scattering of pebbles is a field of house-sized boulders, freshly fractured from the crags above. This place is *new*. It's raw geology, still in the throes of becoming a landscape. Water sheers and freezes, fractures and grinds, sculpting the stone in fits and starts. Lichen clings to the sunniest faces, a pale, hopeful green against the brutal gray.

Despite the brutal cold, and the altitude that leaves you gasping for air with every step, there’s a dampness that clings to everything. It’s less rain, and more the constant melt of the glaciers higher up, feeding a network of icy streams that carve their way down the slopes. You can hear them, a soft, insistent gurgle beneath the wind’s shriek. Water is the architect here, the patient sculptor, and the architect is never at rest.

The surprise is the *life*. You'd expect barrenness, a wasteland of ice and stone. But tucked into the folds of the mountain, in sheltered pockets where the wind loses its bite and the sun has a chance to warm the rock, there's a riot of vegetation. A profusion of mosses and hardy alpine flowers, their colours startlingly vibrant against the monochrome world. Tiny, tenacious shrubs cling to life, their leaves thick and leathery to resist the cold and the wind. 

And with the plants come the animals. Not large, lumbering beasts; this place wouldn't support them. But swarms of insects, buzzing and flitting in the brief moments when the sun breaks through the clouds. Small, furry creatures that dart between the rocks, their coats thick and white. Birds, too – hardy, high-altitude specialists with feathers like spun steel, their calls thin and clear on the wind. Predator and prey locked in a dance honed by millennia, life finding a toehold even here, at the roof of the world.

Scattered amongst the rocks and the vegetation, you see signs. Crude stone structures, barely more than windbreaks, offer scant shelter from the elements. They are built into the landscape, hard to distinguish from the natural features of the terrain.  Obsidian flakes and roughly-worked hand axes lie scattered about, testament to a people who eked out a living here, generations ago. They are gone now, vanished into the mists and the snow, but their presence lingers, a faint echo in the desolate landscape.

The silence is profound, broken only by the wind and the trickle of meltwater. But it’s not an empty silence. It's the silence of a place that is constantly remaking itself, a place where the very stones seem to hum with an inner energy. It is beautiful, dangerous, utterly unforgiving. It is a place where life clings to the edge of possibility, and the ghosts of those who came before are carried on the wind. This is not only a place of ending, but of beginning. The early stages of place, being built slowly over geologic time. A place of sharp, clean edges, of screaming winds and bright sun, of tenacious life and the faint, enduring mark of humankind.
