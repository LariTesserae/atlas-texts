The canyon cradles the heat, holds it close in the dim, still air. Not a canyon carved by water – no river ever kissed these rust-colored walls. Wind, perhaps, over eons beyond counting, has worried the stone down into this narrow defile, but it feels more like the land simply *settled* this way, the crust of something ancient wrinkling into a fold. 

Mostly, it’s quiet, a deceptive quiet. The silence itself vibrates with the presence of life, a heavy, almost tangible hum that you feel in your teeth more than hear. The air is thick, close, cloying with a sweet, earthy scent that clings to your clothes and your skin.

The walls of the canyon rise high and steep, but nowhere near as high as they must have been. The stone is smooth, rounded, worn down by time. Veins of darker rock streak through the reddish sandstone, like the fossilized roots of trees that never were. Here and there, caves yawn in the cliff face, their mouths dark and promising.

What looks at first like dust covering the floor of the canyon is revealed, upon closer inspection, to be a fine, powdery soil, a deep accumulation of the very stuff of decay. It supports a jungle of strange, robust plant life. Huge fungal stalks, taller than any human, crowd the space, their bulbous caps glowing with a faint phosphorescence. These stalks are mottled with dark blotches, and they smell of yeast and spice... and something else, something vaguely unpleasant that tickles the back of your throat. The air vibrates with the sound of scratching, digging, rustling, and the low pulse of countless insects.

Thick, fleshy vines, not quite the same color as the fungal stalks, writhe and snake across the canyon floor, clinging to the rocks and wrapping around the bases of the larger plants. These vines are covered in a profusion of leaves – broad, glossy things that overlap and intertwine, creating a dense, impenetrable canopy in places. Occasionally, a break in the canopy reveals a glimpse of the sky: a dull, bruised purple hung low and heavy.

Life is everywhere. Small, skittering creatures, like armored rodents, dart in and out of the shadows. Larger, hulking shapes move more slowly, their bodies covered in thick plates of chitin. Insects, in unimaginable numbers, swarm through the air, drawn to the light and the scents of the canyon. Flies too plump to fly, droning beetles, and moths with wings the size of your face.

Higher up the canyon walls, platforms have been built – rickety scaffolds of woven vines and crude stone blocks, clinging precariously to the rock face, with only the occasional crude iron spike to hold one in place. These platforms are connected by equally precarious rope bridges, swaying gently in the still air. They're cluttered with tools: stone axes, baskets woven from plant fibers, pottery shards, and strange, bone-handled implements whose purpose is hard to discern. The handiwork isn't fine, but it is clearly *used*; there's a utilitarian feel that implies generations of habit.

The inhabitants of the canyon are small, almost frail-looking, with large, dark eyes that seem to absorb all the available light. They move with a quiet grace, their bodies adapted to navigating the treacherous terrain of their home. They are mostly naked, or clad in rough plant-fiber cloth. Their language is a series of clicks and whistles, barely audible above the cacophony of the jungle. 

It's a difficult place to live. The heat is oppressive. The air is stale. Food comes at the cost of constant vigilance, not simply from predators but from rot, from poison, from every hazard that festers when life teems too abundantly. Yet, there is an undeniable beauty here, a strange, morbid allure. This is the kind of place where death and life are so intertwined that it’s hard to tell where one begins and the other ends, a reminder of the relentless, unyielding power of nature. Despite the decay and the danger, one cannot help but conclude that it is a place of deep power and ancient secrets.
