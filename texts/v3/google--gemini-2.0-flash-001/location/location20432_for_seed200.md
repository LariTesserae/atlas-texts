The air hangs thick and heavy, near its saturation point across this sprawling, low-lying cradle. Condensation beads on every surface, clinging to the broad, oily leaves of colossal plants and gathering in glistening droplets at the tips of drooping tendrils. A constant, soft patter echoes through the dim space as the air surrenders more of itself to the world.

The heat is a presence, not a mere measurement. It presses down, baking the shallow, marshy basins that stretch for what seems like miles. Steam rises in lazy tendrils from the still, dark water, adding to the already oppressive humidity. There is little movement of air, a stillness that amplifies the sense of being trapped in a greenhouse of monstrous proportions.

Vegetation dominates. It's not just lush; it's aggressively abundant. Giant ferns, easily the size of small houses, unfurl new fronds in slow motion. Thick, tangled vines, some as thick as a person's torso, crisscross the landscape, creating a dense, almost impenetrable web. The ground is hidden beneath carpets of moss, luminous fungi, and a never-ending succession of leaves, both living and decomposing. All of it green, in every imaginable shade, but the greens are deep, bordering on black in the dim light that barely penetrates the canopy.

This place feels new, but not untouched. The landscape is pre-formed, it's emerging: the shallow basins are only beginning to solidify where mineral-rich water evaporates constantly at the margins. The geology is less "raw" and more "unbaked" — the mineral-laden clay is still soft and yielding underfoot. Here and there an upthrust of raw earth is exposed, still slick with the moisture of its formation. There's no sharpness, no angularity, but neither has erosion settled on it yet.

The sound is deafening and constant, an overwhelming chorus of life. Insects buzz and drone in relentless swarms. Amphibians croak and bellow from the murky depths. Rustling, chirping, shrieks, whistles, clicks, snaps, growls, splashes — the entire spectrum of possible sounds seems to be represented. It's a cacophony, but not a random one. There’s a strange, unsettling harmony to it, a sense that all the sounds are part of a single, vast, unconscious conversation.

The source of most of the sound is hidden. But sometimes… glimpsed in the perpetual twilight… are the fauna. Not individual creatures, but teeming masses, swarms, packs, schools, armies. Insects so numerous their forms coalesce into shimmering clouds. Reptiles bask in the hot mud with a heavy torpor, their numbers crowding one another. Many appear vaguely familiar, twisted versions of known species. Others are utterly alien, their forms defying easy description.

The place isn't entirely normal. Nothing here seems quite right, it's *ex-*nature -- the components are familiar, it's the assembly that's skewed. A vine with teeth. A toad with too many eyes. A flower that smells of decay. Everything is slightly, unnervingly, *out of place*. Perhaps it's the unyielding heat, or the oppressive humidity, or the sheer density of life. Perhaps it's the subtle feeling that you're being watched, always watched, by something ancient and immense.

Despite the overwhelming activity, there's a profound stillness. The dynamic is unchanging, a frozen equilibrium. This ecosystem has found its center, a peak, and will not move: birth matches death so exactly the population is forever the same. Growth and decay are so perfectly balanced, every fluctuation is instantly absorbed and smoothed out. There are no seasons, no storms, no ebb and flow. Just an eternal, unchanging, deafening, humid, pulsing *now*.
