The city of Aquaderra rests not *in* the water, but *of* it. It clings, somehow, to the high peaks of the World Spine, a mountain range so young, so violently thrust upward, that bare rock still bleeds mineral salts into the constant, warm rain. Rainfall isn’t quite right, though. It’s more sustained, more textured, like walking through a waterfall with the force turned low.

Aquaderra isn’t built in the human sense. It's Grown. The Aquaderrians, possessing only the crudest of stone tools, cultivate colossal, symbiotic jellies that thrive in the mineral-rich downpour. These jellies, structured like massive, inverted bells, serve as the city's architecture. They are anchored by root-like structures that bore into the nascent rock, and their translucent bodies contain intricate networks of bioluminescent algae and responsive pigments that can be manipulated by skilled Aquaderrians to form windows, walls, and pathways.

The city proper is a layered thing, a series of these gelatinous structures stacked upon each other, with water cascading between them. The interiors are surprisingly dry, the jelly acting as a natural waterproof membrane. The algae within provide a faint, green glow, a soft light that illuminates the pale, almost amphibian skin of the Aquaderrians as they move about their daily lives.

The scale is deceptive. From a distance, Aquaderra seems a cluster of glistening fungi on the mountainside. But up close, each jelly-structure is large enough to house a small family. The density of the city emerges less from population alone, and more from the intricate web of watercourses, rope bridges woven from processed kelp, and hanging gardens of epiphytic plants that garland the jelly structures. In the dim light and perpetual rain, it's easy to become disoriented, to lose sense of direction amid the dripping, fungal architecture.

The silence is broken only by the soft gurgling of water, the drone of insects adapted to the perpetually wet environment, and the occasional, high-pitched clicks used by the Aquaderrians to communicate across distances. Their language is intimately tied to the flow of water, with variations in pitch and rhythm mirroring the currents and drips around them. Their lives are slow, patiently attuned to the long cycles of the mountain, the deliberate growth of the jellies, the shifting patterns of the unending rain.

True fauna is rare at these altitudes. Only the hardiest insects and a few species of brightly colored, tree-dwelling mollusks thrive here, clinging to the perpetually wet surfaces. The mountains are emerging so quickly that the surrounding biome cannot keep up. There is life, and life in abundance, but it feels… misplaced, unformed, experimental.

The strangeness of Aquaderra isn't immediately apparent. It's a slow burn, a creeping unease that settles in as you observe the details: the way the jellies pulse with a faint internal rhythm, the unsettlingly human-like eyespots that develop on certain varieties, the complex, geometrical patterns woven into the kelp bridges that seem to defy the Aquaderrians' simple tools. It emerges in the ritualistic gathering of mineral-rich rain in vast basins carved into the very rock of the mountains, a substance they consume and use to accelerate the Jelly growth. It emerges in the quiet contemplation of the ever-shifting rain, a meditation on a source that is both home and ever-present threat.

The rules of this world are simple: Adapt or perish. The Aquaderrians, with their jelly-cities and silent devotion to the rain, have chosen to adapt, weaving themselves into the very fabric of this wet, nascent world.
