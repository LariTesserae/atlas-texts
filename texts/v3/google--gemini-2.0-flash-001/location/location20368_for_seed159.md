The air hangs heavy, thick with a wet warmth that clings to everything it touches. Deeper than a cave, but not so deep to say we're inside a planet. We are in a colossal hollow, a wide shallow bowl with sides that seem to disappear into a hazy distance. Above, a bright diffused light filters through openings in the unseen ceiling, mimicking daylight, but with an undercurrent of something else, something unnaturally strong.

The ground underfoot is uneven. Slick, dark stone, glistening with moisture, gives way to patches of something softer, something…fleshy. No plants, but instead a kind of vibrant, pulsating mat covers much of the ground, in shades of deep reds and bruised purples. This living carpet breathes, rising and falling in a slow, almost imperceptible rhythm. Rivulets of water snake across its surface, collecting in shallow pools before disappearing into unseen openings.

The whole space is dense with life, but not life as we know it. Insects, perhaps, or something like them, form the bulk of the mass. They swarm in writhing masses, their chitinous bodies clicking and scraping against each other with a sound like dry leaves skittering across stone. These aren't the discrete forms individuals, but part of a greater whole. They assemble, disassemble, flow from place to place like something more like a liquid than a collection of bodies. And then there are the larger creatures –– immense, segmented things that shuffle and crawl through the landscape, carving paths through the seething carpet of invertebrates. Their bodies are armored in plates of what looks like carved bone, adorned with spines and glowing, bioluminescent orbs that pulse with a soft, inner light.

Scattered through this tumult, are structures. Jagged, brutal forms of stone or darkened metal sit like broken teeth on the landscape. Some rise only a few feet above the living ground, others are more ambitious: hunched towers that lean precariously as if pushed by some unseen struggle. These structures are old, impossibly old, worn smooth by time and the relentless activity of the creatures around them. There are tools too, but crude ones: hammers of bone and stone, scraping picks of volcanic glass, rough-hewn containers overflowing with a glistening, viscous fluid.

The air vibrates with noise. A relentless cacophony of clicks, hisses, guttural groans, and the rhythmic thumping of the ground pulsing below. Over it all, a high-pitched, almost unbearable keening rises and falls like a wave, setting the teeth on edge. This sound seems to emanate from the largest of the creatures, a constant lament interwoven with the background roar.

This is a place of constant becoming. Nothing stays still for long. The insects endlessly reshape the landscape, tearing down the ruins of the past and building temporary structures that vanish again on the next cycle. The larger creatures are in constant motion, driven forward by inscrutable hungers and desires. The ground itself shifts and morphs, as the pulsing veins of the living ground send new, unknown resources to the surface. It is a world where creation and destruction are intertwined and continuous.

The scattered structures and primitive tools speak of a past, perhaps a past of people, or of precursors to people. It's hard to tell given the state of things. A hint of intention. A ghost of design among the chaos. This place is unusual. It's not immediately alien, but it is deeply other. There is a sense of purpose here, a strange, terrible purpose driving the unending cycle of creation and decay.
