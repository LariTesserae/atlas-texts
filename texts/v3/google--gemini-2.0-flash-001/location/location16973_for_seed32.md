The air smells of chalk dust and ozone. Not the thin, clean ozone of after a thunderstorm, but something heavier, acrid, almost metallic. Sunlight, fractured and multiplied by a million refractive surfaces, burns away any shadow. It is always midday here.

This is a geode, but instead of quartz or amethyst, its interior is lined with a dense, branching, petrified forest. Each tree is no more than a few inches tall, but packed so tightly together that no earth is visible between them. They are made of something like bone, a porous, off-white material that seems to glow from within. The light catches on their calcified branches, creating a blinding, glittering landscape. The trees don't appear to be arranged in any particular order. Some are thicker-trunked, others thin and spindly, some twisted into impossible shapes, all interspersed without visible pattern.

The *sound* is the most striking thing. It's a constant, deafening roar, a high-pitched whine that vibrates in your teeth and bones. It's the sound of the trees themselves. Each petrified trunk hums with silent energy. When multiplied across the entire forest, the collective vibration becomes a palpable force, a solid wall of sound that presses against you.

There is no wind, no movement of any kind. The air is still and dry. Nothing decays; nothing changes. The petrified trees stand in perpetual stasis. The "ground" beneath, if it can be called that, is simply the tightly interwoven roots of the forest, more of the same bone-white material. Here and there, patches of what looks like powdered ivory dust cling to the branches, giving the trees a ghostly appearance.

Despite the absence of animals, there are signs of… something. Tiny, perfectly spherical holes dot some of the trunks, as if bored by an impossibly small drill. The holes are clean and smooth, without any cracks or splinters. Whatever made them must have been unbelievably precise. There are also subtle discolorations on some of the branches, almost like stains. They are a pale lavender color, fading imperceptibly into the bone-white.

The trees are not rooted in soil, that is the unusual part. They connect to a surface of smooth, hard, grey material, like granite but more polished. It rolls slightly, the curvature imperceptible when standing, but noticeable at the edges where the geodes open to a world of darkness. The density of this place creates a false sense of shelter, but it is a tiny ecosystem sealed inside a mineral prison. The bright light ensures that anything inside is visible, highlighted, preserved.

The scale of the geode is deceptive. From the outside, it would appear to be no larger than a melon, but inside, it feels like a vast, albeit cramped, cavern. The feeling is exacerbated by the lack of a horizon. The petrified forest stretches on and on, the trees blurring together in a dizzying, overwhelming mass. You could wander here for days and never find the edge. Because there is no edge. There is only the hum, the light, the bone-white forest, and the constant sense of being watched by something unseen.
