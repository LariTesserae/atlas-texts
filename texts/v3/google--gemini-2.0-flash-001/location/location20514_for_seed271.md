The air hangs thick and heavy, a cloying warmth that presses against the skin. Not quite humid, but perpetually *damp*, like the inside of a well-used greenhouse that hasn't been properly ventilated in years. This place breathes water, even if pools and streams are rare. Condensation collects on every surface, beading on the broad, flat leaves of the sparse vegetation and slicking the faces of the low, gently sloping hills.

These aren't dramatic hills, mind you. More like swells in the earth, barely lifting themselves from the plain. They're composed of a soft, reddish clay, heavily eroded over what must be an unimaginable span of time. Gullies and shallow ravines crisscross their surfaces, testament to ages of slow, patient weathering. The clay itself is oddly smooth to the touch, without the gritty texture one might expect. It crumbles easily underfoot, releasing a faint, earthy odor that mixes with the sweet, almost sickly scent of the dampness.

The vegetation is...unusual. It’s sparse, but not withered. It's more that there are *few* species present, and what is there grows with a quiet, stubborn tenacity. Dominating the landscape are broad, almost fungal shelves that sprout directly from the clay slopes. These shelves are a deep, liverish brown on top, fading to a pale ochre underneath where they meet the soil. Water droplets cling to their undersides, occasionally gathering into trembling beads that eventually fall with a soft *plink* onto the wet earth.

Between these shelves grow clumps of a tall, wiry grass, its blades a dull grey-green. The individual blades are surprisingly thick, almost fleshy, and exude a clear, slightly viscous sap when broken. They stand stiffly, unmoving in the still air, as if carved from some unyielding material. Once in a great while, you will find a small, shrub-like thing that pushes a few branches out of the saturated earth. These shrubs have strange, almost metallic looking leaves that seem iridescent in small flashes as the weak and obstructed sunlight plays against them.

The light itself is a major factor in the feel of this place. It is always dim, filtered through a perpetual haze that hangs low to the ground. It feels as if the sun is always setting, or perhaps never quite rising. Shadows are soft and indistinct, blurring the edges of everything and lending the landscape a sense of muted mystery. It is a strange, obscured light.

The only sound is the almost imperceptible susurrus of the damp air and the occasional *plink* of condensation dripping from the fungal shelves. A profound quiet reigns, broken only by these subtle reminders of the ever-present moisture. This silence is not peaceful, exactly, but rather weighty, pregnant with a sense of timelessness.

There are the scattered remains of...*something*...here. Low, almost swallowed by the earth, are the remains of what might have been walls or foundations. They are constructed of the same reddish clay as the hills themselves, fired to a harder consistency. The structures are small, isolated from one another, suggesting a scale of life no bigger than individual family units. There are no markings, no inscriptions, nothing to indicate who built them or why they were abandoned. It feels like a civilization that merely receded into the same clay it was born from.

There is no fauna here, no birds, insects, or mammals. No signs of life other than this strange, persistent vegetation. Just clay, water, and the slow, inevitable process of erosion.

The overwhelming sense is of a place utterly, irrevocably *settled*. There are no discernible changes, nothing appears to grow or evolve. There is an equilibrium here, a stillness that approaches petrifaction. No processes of entropy appear, so there is no sense of a future to come to this place.
