The heat shimmers off the slag, bending the dim light into wavering mirages. The air hangs thick and oily, tasting of sulfur and something acrid that stings the nostrils. No sun pierces this far down; the only illumination comes from the slow, guttural breathing of the furnaces and the occasional gout of flame that claws its way up the chimneys.

No water exists here, not in any recognizable form. It's been boiled away, consumed in the endless work, driven deep into the rock as superheated steam only to emerge again, hissing and spitting, as a tool for some new, brutal purpose. The landscape is primarily rock—cracked, fissured, and stained black with soot. In some places, however, strange, crystalline growths have formed, delicate lattices of minerals that thrive in the sulfurous atmosphere.

Despite the inhospitable nature, life clings on, but not animal life. Pockets of strange, thermophilic flora erupt from cracks in the stone. These are no conventional plants; rather, they resemble tangled mats of fungal filaments, punctuated by fleshy, crimson blooms that pulse with a faint, internal light. They seem to draw sustenance directly from the geothermal heat, their roots delving deep into the earth. These blooms are strangely lush, almost obscene in their vitality, a stark contrast to the desolate surroundings. They are cultivated within the city.

Scale here is deceptive. One might think of a vast industrial complex, but it's all collapsed inward, become a confined pocket nestled within something larger. The city itself is built into the side of an exhausted volcanic caldera. The original crater has long since been filled and built over by generations of the Heat-Keepers. A network of tunnels and chambers runs both horizontally and vertically, creating a claustrophobic warren.

The city itself is a marvel of primitive engineering. The structures are constructed from a combination of roughhewn stone and carefully mortared slag-bricks. Chimneys belch smoke from countless hearths and forges. The built environment is dense, layered with walkways and platforms clinging to the cliff face. Scaffolding made of intricately woven metal crawls across structures, holding them into some semblance of upright. There's a raw, almost brutalist aesthetic to the place. Everything is functional, designed for one purpose like a tool – to extract and refine the earth’s molten heart.

The silence here is broken by the clang of hammer against metal, the rasp of saws cutting stone, the low growl of the furnaces, and the shouted orders of the workers. This isn't a quiet industry; it is deafening, assaulting sound.

Erosion is slow, but noticeable. The constant vibrations from the machinery, the corrosive atmosphere, and thermal stress slowly wear everything away. The older sections of the city are partially collapsed, their secrets buried under piles of rubble and ash. But this collapse doesn't mean decay; it’s simply another stage. New construction rises atop the ruins of the old, a constant cycle of destruction and creation. Everything here is in flux, even the seemingly immutable stone.

The city isn't deserted, but it is very empty. There are few here. The population is small, a close-knit society of artisans and laborers, the Heat-Keepers. Their culture is one of pragmatism and resilience. They are adept at crafting tools and weapons from the materials at hand, and they possess an intuitive understanding of the geothermal processes that power their city. Their technology is focused on harnessing the heat and extracting minerals from the volcanic rock. Ingenious systems of pipes and bellows circulate hot air and steam, powering primitive machinery. Their clothing is simple, functional, made from fire-resistant fabrics and reinforced with metal plates.

The most striking thing about this place, besides the stifling heat and the pervading smell, is the sense of active force. It is a geoscape in motion. The rhythmic pulse of the furnaces, the constant labor of the workers, the slow but relentless erosion of the stone – all contribute to a feeling of unstoppable momentum. The city is alive, a living engine consuming and transforming the very earth it inhabits. While not overtly "weird", there is an strangeness to this. The rules are different here. The Heat-Keepers believe the heat is an animal, and must be fed with sacrifices and labor. No animal can survive, but something not-animal can thrive in this hell, where the heat itself drives on.
