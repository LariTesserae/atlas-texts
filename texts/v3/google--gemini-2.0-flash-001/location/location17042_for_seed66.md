The city of Khthon hums.

It is built of something like basalt cobbles, slick with perpetual condensation. The air itself seems to weep. Moisture beads on every surface, gathering into droplets that track slow paths downward. The light, though normal, is diffused by this omnipresent wet, softened and spread as if by a translucent fog. There is no sky, only a close ceiling of worked stone, vaults and arches that echo with the city's constant murmuring voices.

Khthon is vast, layered upon itself in cyclopean terraces that climb impossibly high. It sprawls across a high plateau of its own making. The raw rock beneath is entirely obscured by the city's endless infrastructure; only deep within the foundations, in forgotten, dripping tunnels, can the original stone be found.

There is no plant life, not in the conventional sense. Instead, the city's surfaces are carpeted with an oily, black film, a kind of chemoautotrophic slick that feeds on the exhaust and waste products of Khthon's industry. It glows faintly, a bioluminescent counterpoint to the normal light.

The warmth is like a damp breath, radiating from the city's hidden heart. Steam vents hiss from grates in the cobbled streets, releasing plumes of mineral-laden vapor that condense almost immediately into the ever-present drizzle. The air smells of iron, sulfur, and something vaguely organic, like overripe fruit.

Khthon is old, weathered, its edges softened by the slow drip of time. Yet, it is also in a state of perpetual construction. Golems of rust-colored metal trundle through the streets, hauling slabs of stone and pipes of steaming alloy. They are tireless, unthinking servants of the city's inscrutable purposes, their movements slow and deliberate.

The scale is overwhelming. It can take days to traverse a single level of Khthon, to wind through its labyrinthine streets and up its titanic stairways. The noises of the city — the clatter of machines, the hiss of steam, the guttural chants of its inhabitants — blend into a deafening roar, a constant drone that vibrates in the bones.

This is a dense place, layered and packed with activity. Every spare inch is occupied by workshops, foundries, dwelling-crannies, or the strange, symbiotic fungi that thrive in the damp. Khthon is saturated with life, but it is a life unlike any other.

The architecture is urban industrial. Blocky, functional structures, built without ornamentation, but of immense size and complexity. Bridges span chasms of churning machinery. Aqueducts carry streams of viscous, chemical-laden fluids. Towers of interlocking gears rise into the mist, their purpose unknown. The tech is present, but in its own manner. It is scaled, powerful, and industrial, but also somehow... ancient; not shiny chrome and polished plastics, but rather groaning iron and sparking brass. The pace is glacial. Khthon changes on geological timescales.

Khthon's fauna is unlike anything seen elsewhere. Not animals, but creatures born of metal, silicon, and living plasm. They swarm the streets, filling every available niche. Some are simple automatons, scavengers and maintenance drones. Others are more complex, grotesque parodies of organic life, cobbled together from various components. They are all expressions of Khthon's will, extensions of its vast, mechanical mind.

The unusual is present, but not foregrounded. It is woven into the fabric of its everyday. Things here seem wrong, but at the same time settled, and in-place. The population rarely acts in ways that organic beings would consider sane, while taking an un-ironic, sincere position and acting on their individual purposes.

And yet, amidst the noise and the chaos, there is a sense of equilibrium. Khthon is a living machine, grinding and churning, but also self-sustaining. It is a place of bizarre beauty, a testament to the boundless ingenuity of its unknown creators. It goes forward, slowly, steadily, unyielding as metal.
