A narrow gorge cuts through the mountains, a raw wound of dark, wet rock. Rain falls almost constantly here, a near-mist that clings to everything. Pockets of water collect in every depression and crack, reflecting the faint, diffuse light that barely penetrates the perpetual cloud cover. The air is heavy with moisture, almost difficult to breathe.

Vegetation explodes from every surface. Mosses and lichens, thick and vibrant, paint the stone in shades of green, grey, and orange. Ferns, some enormous and others delicate, unfurl in every nook and cranny. Small, tightly-packed trees cling to the cliffsides, their roots burrowing into the cracks in the rock. The effect is overwhelming, a riot of green against the dark stone, a testament to life clinging on in this harsh environment. The feeling is dense. 

The gorge is not silent. The constant drip of water, the rustle of wind through the densely packed foliage, and the distant rumble of falling rocks create a constant background hum. But over this, a louder, harsher sound dominates: the clang and clatter of machinery. Deep within the gorge, a factory has been built. It is more of a sprawling complex of pipes, gears, and pistons than a single building, welded onto the living rock; and its edges are lost within the foliage. The factory chugs along, fueled by the force of the water that cascades down the mountainside. Valves hiss, and black smoke leaks from ill-fitting joins in the network of pipes.

Inside, the factory is a maze of walkways and platforms suspended precariously over the churning machinery. The air is thick with the smell of oil and metal. The darkness in the gorge is darker here, pierced only by the flickering of gas lamps casting pools of orange light on the grimy surfaces. The machinery is old, poorly maintained, its purpose difficult to discern. It seems to be engaged in a process of ceaseless, chaotic change. Sections of the plant go offline, only to be replaced by new contrivances, lashed together out of whatever is at hand.

The water itself is the key. Diversion ponds sit above the main complex, and are replenished almost as fast as they are drained. The factory is always threatening to run out of water, to be smashed by runaway water, to be drowned.

The mountains are young. Jagged peaks cut into the sky, and the signs of erosion are everywhere: loose scree, fresh landslides, and the constant threat of rockfall. This is a place of instability, where the land itself seems to be in a state of perpetual upheaval. The scale is intimate; an individual could walk across nearly any part of the main complex, or follow the gorge from one end to the other, in a day.

There are no animals here. Perhaps the factory's pollution or turbulent energy has driven them away, or perhaps they simply cannot survive in this harsh, unstable environment.

The whole place teeters between industry and raw wilderness. The factory exists as a parasite on the mountain's side, drawing its lifeblood from the water, its metal sinews wrapped around the bones of the earth. The chaotic energy of the place, the constant change, and the sheer density of both vegetation and machinery create a sense of overwhelming vitality. This is a landscape on the edge, a place where anything could happen, at any moment.
