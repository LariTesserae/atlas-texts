The wind howls, a near-constant scream that claws at the edges of hearing. It's a high, thin sound, the product of unimaginable distances and frozen air rushing across a landscape of near-total emptiness. Visibility is abysmal, a perpetual twilight clinging to the edges of jagged, black peaks that claw skyward.

This is the Whispering Scarp, a colossal range thrust skyward in some geological birthing-pang so recent the stone hasn’t even begun to settle. The mountains are knife-edged, uncluttered by scree or rounded by weather. Immense slabs of obsidian and basalt lean precariously, threatening to calve off into the starless void that seems ever-present, even in the dimmest light.

And yet, life clings. Not in the way one might expect, not with hardy lichens or scrubby alpine trees. Here, life is the mountain itself.

This is a fungal lattice, kilometers deep. The Scarp isn't rock so much as the fruiting body of a single, gargantuan organism. The black, glassy surfaces are a specialized fungal chitin, evolved to endure the extreme cold and radiation. This chitin drips, even at these temperatures. Condensation gathers out of the thin, supercooled atmosphere and beads on the surface, trickling down like slow tears. These clinging droplets are the only real source of water, the ‘damp’ that sustains this strange ecology. This moisture forms ephemeral rivulets of viscous, nutrient-rich fluid that trace paths down the sheer cliffs, dissolving minuscule amounts of minerals from the chitin itself.

Wherever these rivulets flow, specialized tendrils of the greater fungus push out, forming vibrant, albeit morbidly-colored, mats. These aren’t plants, but they function as such, absorbing what little energy spills from the diffuse, cosmic background radiation. They are a churning, squelching mass, always moving, always growing. Bioluminescent threads pulse beneath the surface, a faint, cold fire illuminating the intricate network of veins and tubes that spread across the Scarp's surface.

The ‘total vegetation’ is a complex ecosystem, structured like a terraced garden, but on a scale that defies comprehension. Lower down, in the narrow valleys choked with swirling mists, the fungal mats thicken and become almost solid. Here, the dripping chitin has built up into small, stagnant pools of brackish, oily water. These pools are the breeding grounds for bizarre aquatic creatures – blind, worm-like beings that pulse with an inner light and feed on the dissolved organic matter.

These valleys are also home to the "built" environment, such as it is. Scattered along the valley floors are simple, domed structures – not built of stone, but cultivated from the living fungus itself. It is difficult to tell what came first: the fungal domes or the strange, insectoid creatures that inhabit them. The dwellings are perfectly woven into the surrounding fungal mats, appearing almost as natural formations. Inside, the creatures live in huddled groups, their small, multifaceted eyes gleaming in the bioluminescent glow. They are builders and herders, tending to the fungal 'crops' and crafting simple tools from chitin and frozen sap. Scavenging and mining and scraping out an existence in a world that cares nothing for them.

The 'loud sound' is the constant dripping and the howl of the wind. This wind is not merely atmospheric, but also a consequence of the organism itself: metabolic byproducts vented from massive internal chambers. These chambers, located deep within the Scarp, serve as nurseries, breeding grounds, and processing centers. The wind carries spores, and the subsonic hum from within vibrates through the very bones of the world. Some of the insectoids treat the spores as a drug.

Everything is ‘active’. The fungus is growing, dissolving, dying, and reforming. The insectoids struggle to survive, constantly battling the wind and the cold, and each other. Predators lurk – ethereal, semi-corporeal beings that drift through the fungal mats, feeding on the bioluminescent energy of the larvae. They hunt via the low hums emitted by their prey.

It’s a place of constant, desperate struggle, a symphony of decay and rebirth played out on a scale that dwarfs the mind. It's strangely beautiful, yet utterly horrifying -- the Whispering Scarp, a monument to life's tenacity in the face of unimaginable odds, and its capacity for strangeness.
