The air hangs heavy, thick with a perpetual, clinging damp. It's not rain, not usually. More like a constant sweat on the metal and stone surfaces that comprise this entire world. Condensation drips in sluggish tears from jutting pipes and the undersides of vast, curving plates of riveted steel.

There is no ground, not as one understands it. Only layer upon layer of construction, a dense stratification visible in the occasional catastrophic collapse where one level has buckled under the weight of another. These collapses are not uncommon; the whole place groans and shifts with the stressed sighs of tortured metal. It's a built world, through and through. No soil, no growing things, just the cold, unyielding reality of fabrication.

The temperature is consistently cool, bordering on cold. The dampness exacerbates it, leeching any warmth away. Touching anything metallic is a shock, a sudden bite of chill.

This is deep space architecture, but unlike anything imagined in the gilded renderings of planetary prospectors. It's *old*, predating any known star charts. Something was built here, on a scale that dwarfs comprehension, and then abandoned. Now, it's a slow-motion disaster, perpetually coming apart. The erosion score feels wrong, I suspect it's the wrong term. I suspect it's accretion, the world is still building slowly, the rust and the condensation.

We stand now in what might once have been a corridor, though the walls lean inward at alarming angles, their surfaces covered in a rough, scabrous texture of rust and some other, unidentifiable accretion. Wires, thick as a man's arm, snake across the ceiling, occasionally spasming with faint pulses of energy. This is a primitive tech, nothing sleek or efficient. This is the product of brute force, of hammering and welding and relentless, unrefined application. The age of crude invention and colossal engineering hubris.

The sound is overwhelming. A cacophony of metallic shrieks, grinding, and the constant rushing hiss of escaping steam or compressed air. The echoes are complex and multi-layered, because everything is dense and close. Vibrations run up through your feet. It doesn't feel like there are many actual entities making sound. Rather, it's the environment, a deeply active system perpetually at war with itself.

The light is poor. What little illumination there is comes from flickering, sputtering lamps encased in cages of corroded iron. They cast long, distorted shadows that dance with the dripping condensation. There are deeper shadows beyond their reach, obscuring the true scale of this place. The oppressive darkness makes it difficult to gauge distances or maintain a sense of direction.

Rare fauna exist here. Strange, pale creatures adapted to the cold, the damp, and the echoing darkness. We saw one earlier — a segmented worm, longer than a man, that slithered silently through a pool of oily water escaping from a ruptured pipe. It had no eyes, only a gaping maw lined with rows of glittering, chitinous teeth. What it eats here, in this metal labyrinth, is a mystery.

There is a sense of *wrongness* about this place, a pervasive feeling that the laws of physics themselves are subtly warped. Gravity fluctuates, sometimes pulling you downward with unnatural force, other times leaving you feeling lightheaded and unsteady. The shadows seem to shift and writhe with a life of their own. The sense of wrongness does not feel unnatural to the place. This is where wrongness belongs.

This landscape has the scale of a valley, but a valley made of machinery. You could lose yourself in these corridors for days, weeks, even years. The implications of the architecture stretch out of sight entirely into the abyssal darkness ahead.

The dampness of the environment has a flavour which is unique, a blend of ozone, rust, and an odd sweetness like rotting fruit. It clings to the back of your throat, a constant, nauseating reminder of the alien nature of this place.

And yet, despite the decay and the darkness, there is a sense of activity. The systems are failing, yes, but they are *still* failing. This place is not dead. It's dying, actively and fiercely, a slow, agonizing process of collapse and transformation. And what will be born from the ruins is something truly strange and unknowable.
