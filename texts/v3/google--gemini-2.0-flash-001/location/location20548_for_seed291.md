The rain comes and goes. It's not a drenching rain, more like a persistent, sighing mist that beads on the slick, black metal of the city. The fog rolls in off the bay, blanketing the lower levels in a cold, clinging shroud. Above, the skeletal latticework of the upper towers punches through the grey, disappearing into the low-hanging clouds.

This isn't built so much as assembled. Generations of mismatched construction, expansions, and abandoned projects layered on top of each other like sedimentary rock. Corrugated iron shacks cling to the sides of massive, rust-streaked factories; brick tenements huddle in the shadows of gleaming, chrome-plated refineries. You can see the history of its making in every street, every building. Wooden supports prop up concrete walls, which in turn are fused to the steel girders of something newer and far less stable.

Every surface is coated in a film of green: moss, lichen, algae. The city breathes with plant life. It spills from broken windows, creeps up drainpipes, and bursts through cracks in the pavement. There are even entire structures claimed by vegetation — a factory chimney completely enveloped in ivy, its silhouette barely discernible. The plants are hardy, tenacious things, adapted to the pollutants that seep from the soil and the acidic rain.

The air is thick with the metallic tang of industry. The smell of oil and rust and burning coal mixes with the sweet, cloying scent of the rampant vegetation. It's a strange olfactory cocktail, both repellent and strangely compelling. The *clunk* and *hiss* of automated machinery are the city's constant undercurrent, a low and rhythmic pulse that underlies the general quiet, like a sleeping giant’s breathing.

It's oddly still, even with the density of inhabitants. Most life is close to the ground or hidden in the buildings. Rat-sized rodents with bioluminescent whiskers dart through the alleys. Flocks of soot-black birds with unnaturally long, pointed beaks nest in the higher structures, their calls like the scraping of metal on metal.

The people... the people are strange. Their bodies are often augmented with crude but functional technology—prosthetics cobbled together from scrap metal and salvaged electronics. Some have gills grafted onto their necks, allowing them to delve into the flooded lower levels. Others have lenses inlaid into their eyes, enhancing their vision in the perpetual gloom. They are a resourceful, resilient people, hardened by the harsh conditions and driven by a constant, restless energy.

There is a sense of barely-contained mania to the place, a frenetic excitement that bubbles beneath the surface. Innovation and desperation breed side by side. New technologies are invented then abandoned overnight. Theories and beliefs take hold and dissipate at breakneck speed. The market district is a symphony of shouts and barters, inventors hawking their latest creations, scavengers offering salvaged parts, and preachers proselytizing strange new faiths.

Rules here are flexible. There is a basic understanding of property and trade, but beyond that, it's every person for themselves. Corruption is rampant. Gangs control territories. The city guard is understaffed and overwhelmed. And yet, there's a strange form of order to it. A balance maintained by the constant struggle for survival and the recognition that cooperation, however fleeting, can be the only path through another day.

But it's getting worse. The ground is sinking. Sections of the city are swallowed by the swamps almost daily. The industries that built this place are tearing it down, bit by bit. New structures built on decaying foundations cause chain reactions of collapse. The sea is rising, and they are running out of places to go.

And the dreams of the inhabitants have become strange and strange again. They are haunted by whispers of forgotten gods, visions of submerged cities, and the terrifying certainty that the world they know is on the verge of disappearing completely. They are the inheritors of a broken world, trying desperately to build something new from the ruins.
