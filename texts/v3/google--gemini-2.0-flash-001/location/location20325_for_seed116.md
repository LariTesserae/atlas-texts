The roar is constant. It vibrates through the metal grilles underfoot, up your legs, and into your bones. You can feel it more than hear it, a deep, resonant thrum that speaks of immense power barely contained. Above, riveted steel plates, greened with algae and draped with thick, fleshy vines, arch away to meet a perpetual twilight – a normal light filtered through meters of water and manufactured sky. 

This is Level 7, the Hydroponics Sector. Or rather, what *was* the Hydroponics Sector. Officially, it's now designated the "Flooded Zone," and quarantined. But the roar hasn't stopped, the pumps haven't failed, and the ecosystem – twisted and warped as it is – continues to thrive.

Look down. The grille beneath your boots is slick with a bioluminescent slime, thick blankets of which pulse with a soft, ethereal glow. Between the metal bars, you glimpse the 'soil' of Level 7 – not earth, but tanks of nutrient-rich solution, now overflowing and mingling freely. From this artificial soup rises a riot of mutated plant life. Genetically engineered kelp, designed for rapid growth and maximum oxygen production, has exploded beyond its parameters. It climbs the walls, chokes the ventilation shafts, and its swollen bulbs bob obscenely in the water.

The water itself is the primary actor here. It pours down the cliffs of stacked industrial machinery in frothing cascades; it gushes from breached pipes, thick as a man; it fills the spaces between the hydroponic vats, creating a labyrinth of flooded pathways and stagnant pools. The system was built to circulate, to control, to contain. Now, the system itself is the flood, a runaway feedback loop of pumps, pipes, and reservoirs amplifying a single, critical failure centuries ago.

The creatures. They are… unexpected. Tiny crustaceans, iridescent-shelled things with too many legs, scuttle across the submerged surfaces.  Blind, pale fish with rows of glowing barbels navigate the tangled kelp forests. The air – thick with humidity and the earthy sweetness of decay – is alive with the buzz of insects. Giant, clumsy moths with wings like stained glass flit between the artificial flora. Occasionally, something larger breaks the surface with a splash, leaving ripples that distort the reflected lights of the Level.

The architecture here is totalistic precisely because it now appears, in these pocket gardens and miniature waterfalls, as a ruin. The girders and walkways are still present, but they are no longer the primary elements; vines now determine the contours of the space, the way water has eroded the landscape. Here and there, in the least accessible corners, functional machinery still whirs and clicks – automated nutrient injectors, pressure regulators fighting a losing battle, the occasional flickering service light.  The industrial heart still beats faintly, keeping this whole mad ecosystem alive.

The loudest sound is, of course, the relentless pumping. But listen closer. There's also the gurgle and splash of water, the rasp of chitinous legs on metal, the drone of insects, and the sighs of the kelp forest as the currents shift.  There are other, stranger noises too, whispers and clicks in patterns you can’t quite decipher... perhaps some form of communication between the strange inhabitants that have made Level 7 their own. 

The weirdness is not overt, not monstrous, but pervasive, deeply embedded in the very essence of this place.  It is the wrongness of the light, the perversion of growth, the uneasy blurring between technology and nature.  It's the feeling that you're trespassing not just on quarantined ground but on a forbidden experiment, a place where the rules of reality have been subtly, irrevocably bent. Step carefully. The metal itself seems to groan when you press your weight into it.
