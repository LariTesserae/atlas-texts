The Cloud-Mines of Airdale.

The cold bites quickly here. Even in mid-summer on the lower levels, breath steams and wind whips in off the vast, empty plains, cutting through even the sturdiest hidespun cloaks. The wind carries moisture, a constant, clinging dampness that seeps into everything. Rain is rare, but never far away; the air itself is a broth, always threatening to condense. It beads on the metal struts, drips from the hydroponic gardens, sheens the perpetually slick cobblestones of the lower wards.

Airdale is built staggering up the sides of mountains that are themselves barely more than heaps of shale and scree held together by the clinging, engineered moss that covers them. Without the moss – and the tireless efforts of the Moss-Keepers – the mountains would have crumbled long ago, leaving Airdale a collection of isolated platforms perched on eroding pillars. As it is, the constant threat of collapse is a fact of life, another drone in the background hum.

The scale here is planetary, and deceptive. From the highest levels of Airdale, one can see nothing but the endless, rolling plains punctuated by the occasional, isolated mountain. The sky is always overcast, a low, bruised grey that presses down on the city. The sun is a forgotten rumor, a thing spoken of in old songs but never actually seen. The distances are immense and misleading. What looks like a few hours walk is, in reality, days - or weeks - of relentless travel. Travel best avoided. The plains are utterly empty. Nothing lives out there, save for a few hardy lichens and the perpetual, swirling dust devils.

The "Cloud Mining" is what drew people here originally, generations ago. The process is simple and brutally effective. Immense, skyscraper-sized machines that look like gothic cathedrals crossed with oil derricks suck the moisture from the air, condense it, and then subject it to a complex series of alchemical and industrial processes. The end result is… well, depending on who you ask, it's called "Sky-Oil," "Aether-Gel," or simply "The Good Stuff." A viscous, iridescent fluid that burns hotter and cleaner than anything else, and powers the vast majority of the industrial world. The process leaves behind a residue of mineral concentrates, which are painstakingly refined into building materials, machine components, and the all-important moss-feed.

The dripping runoff from the great condensers feeds a network of astonishing, tiered hydroponic gardens. These are the heart of Airdale, the source of its life. Here, under the pale, flickering glow of electric lamps, grow dense thickets of genetically-engineered vegetables, fruits, and fungi. The air in the gardens is thick and warm, a welcome contrast to the biting cold outside. The colors are a riot: phosphorescent blues, deep purples, sickly greens. It's a jungle tamed and weaponized, a controlled explosion of life in a dead world.

But the loudest part by far, is the sound. Airdale thrums with a constant, deafening mechanical roar. The Cloud-Mines themselves are the primary source, their immense engines groaning and hissing, the rhythmic thump of pistons echoing across the plains. But the metal-on-metal screech of the moss-keepers' automated repair drones, the high-pitched whine of the condensation coils, the subsonic moan of the pressure regulators – it all combines into an overwhelming cacophony that never ceases. People who are born in Airdale say they don't even hear it anymore, but visitors find it almost unbearable. It grinds on the teeth, vibrates in the bones. Some say it drives people mad.

And yet, for all its strangeness, Airdale is unchanging. The machines churn, the moss grows, the Good Stuff flows. The temperature remains stubbornly, lethally cold. The population swells and shrinks with the fluctuating price of resources. Accidents happen; collapses occur. But the pattern never varies. Airdale exists in a state of near-perfect equilibrium, a clockwork world ticking away the eons in its own strange, industrial rhythm.

Where is the source of this eternal equilibrium? Some whisper of the Theorems, the ancient mathematical formulas and physical laws that govern this plane. Some seek the Keynote, the signal which must be maintained in perfect alignment for the Theorems to continue functioning. Some say the Theorems predate the city, that the Sky-Oil is merely a byproduct of a greater, unknowable purpose.

But these are whispered theories, mad ramblings, secrets for the desperate hours.

The mines must operate.

The moss must grow.

The sky must drip.
