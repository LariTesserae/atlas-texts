The air is warm, thick with the scent of ozone and minerals, and heavy on the lungs. It feels less like air and more like a viscous fluid, resisting every breath. Above, the darkness is profound, starless, seemingly absolute. But it is a darkness shot through with flashes – brief, intense discharges of violet and crimson that illuminate the landscape for a heartbeat before plunging it back into gloom.

Below, a raw, wounded world stretches out. Mountains, still in the agonizing throes of formation, erupt from a bone-white plain. This isn't rock as we know it. It’s a substance more akin to solidified foam, riddled with microscopic pores, that shimmers with an internal luminescence. The formations are impossibly sharp, jagged, too new, too *clean* to be stone weathered by eons. They appear fresh from the forge, as if a sleeping giant had just flexed its muscles and fractured the world’s skin.

The elevation is extreme. One moment you are standing on a relatively flat expanse, the next the ground plunges away into unseen chasms. The air thins precipitously as you ascend, the warmth giving way to an unsettling chill that bites at exposed skin. And above, the violet and crimson flashes come more frequently, bathing the scene in an unsettling strobe-light effect.

The 'erosion' here is not of weathering by wind and water. It is something more… fundamental. The very *structure* of the world seems to be in a state of flux. You see fissures widening with alarming speed, mountainsides crumbling in on themselves in slow-motion collapses. The air shimmers with heat haze and the almost imperceptible vibration of the earth rearranging itself.

Despite the dramatic topography, the landscape is sparse. Everything is spread thin. The luminescence of the foam-stone is faint, barely piercing the darkness. The flashes are isolated events, illuminating vast swathes of empty space instead of a teeming world.

Here and there, scattered across the plain and clinging precariously to the mountainsides, are structures. These aren't buildings in any recognizable sense. They are more like… shells. Formed of the same porous, luminescent material as the surrounding rock, they are hollow, without doors or windows. Most are small, just a few meters across, but occasionally one finds a larger structure, a cavernous shell big enough to swallow a small building. Their emptiness is palpable, echoing in the silence that descends the moment the flashes cease. They feel like cocoons that have been abandoned, left behind by something that has long since moved on.

There is no technology here, no sign of tools or devices. Yet the 'cocoons' speak of some process, some purpose, that is now absent. The quiet is punctuated by rumbles deep in the earth, tectonic groans that are felt more than heard. Then there are the crackling discharges as the strange lightning ignites – the world clearing its throat, about to speak, but forgetting what it meant to say.

There is no wind. No rustling of leaves, no chirping of insects, no cries of birds. Only the heavy silence and the sporadic eruptions of geological noise. It’s a silence that presses in, that seems to suck the sound from your own breath.

This world is turbulent, dynamic in the extreme. It is a landscape in the throes of becoming, a chaotic crucible where the very laws of physics seem to be bent and broken. It is a place both terrifying and awe-inspiring, a symphony of destruction and creation played out on a grand, cosmic scale. It is a place where one feels profoundly, existentially alone, standing witness to a Genesis that has no clear end in sight.
