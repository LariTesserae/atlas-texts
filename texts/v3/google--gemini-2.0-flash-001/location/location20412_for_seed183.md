The light here is *wrong*. It’s too sharp, too insistent, bathing the jagged peaks in a crystalline glare that seems to cut straight through the eye and lodge itself, buzzing, in the brain. It's daytime and the peaks shine.

The ‘peaks’ are perhaps two kilometers high, or five. There is no horizon here to judge by, but their faces are close, hemming in the valley so that it feels less like being at the top of the world and more like being wedged unforgivingly far inside it.

The rock is black shale, fractured and crumbling under its own impossible height. Runnels of water, fed by unseen springs or perpetual condensation, streak down the rock faces, turning the black to a slick, reflective obsidian. These streamlets join together into larger flows that roar down the mountainside. The black water, thick with dissolved minerals, gathers in pools at the valley floor. The sound here rises up, bounces back, combines and becomes amplified, loud.

There are no plants, no lichens, no mosses, nothing organic that the eye can detect. There are crystalline formations that are more like geometry than anything, and the colors of them are wrong; the light refracts from them. The water rushes, the light blazes.

The density is misleading; everything seems very close, but each element is separated by a strange tension. There is something else here as well – not animals, not plants, but presences. Not alive, but neither are they inert. They are like echoes or afterimages, shimmering in the periphery of vision. They are most visible near the water. They move with purpose.

The air itself vibrates. The prevailing winds, funneled through the narrow valleys between the peaks, carry a fine, gritty dust that stings the skin and coats everything with a layer of gray.

The 'erosion' here is a constant, aggressive process. The steep slopes are unstable, and rockfalls are frequent. The shale crumbles under the constant siege of water and wind, and the mountain seems determined to unbuild itself, grain by grain. The shards of black rock accumulate on the valley floor.

Ruins, small structures, are everywhere but they are difficult to discern from the natural landscape. They are made of the same shale as the mountains, and they blend seamlessly with the surrounding rock. They are, perhaps, not buildings so much as arrangements of the rock. Walls, chambers, passages. The passages weave the shards together, perhaps channeling the winds, perhaps funneling the water. The structures look designed to move the water and the wind and the rock from place to place, as if to accelerate or sculpt the disintegration of the land.

Primitive tools are everywhere - small hammers of stone, chisels of obsidian, prybars of hardened shale. They seem impossibly small and light to have shaped such a landscape, and there is no sign as to who might have used them. There are no bones, no corpses, no bodies, no remnants of lives.

The 'water' here is not water, not exactly. It’s a hyper-conductive fluid, rich in strange elements and compounds. The tools are placed so that they will be swept and tumbled by the water, charging with energy. There is a subtle vibration in the ground, a hum that resonates in the bones. It is the effect of the energy, being pushed by the builders.

There is a kind of logic to it, a perverse and alien calculus. The mountains are not being eroded as much as *tuned*. The black shale is being refined, broken down into its constituent elements and reshaped into something else. The light, too, is being harvested, amplified, redirected through the crystalline structures.

The air buzzes with anticipation. This is a place of transformation, a crucible where the very substance of the world is being reshaped. Something is being built here.
