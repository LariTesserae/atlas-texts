The world roars. It is a susurrus of a scale that unseats thought. Not a roar that *punctuates* silence; here, a susurrus *is* the silence, the silent underlay of everything. 

We are high, though there is no ground underfoot. Around us, the cathedral-scaled architecture is not stone or wood but solidified spray, arrested mist, the brief and brutal architecture of wave-forms cast in an unstable opalescent material that shimmers between sea-foam green and a bruised, internal violet. Sometimes the architecture cleaves and falls, a waterfall turning back into itself, shattering into a deeper, more resonant form of susurrus when it rejoins the undifferentiated ocean below.

Ocean is perhaps the wrong word. It is all water, yes, but nothing rests. The surface is a churning skin that is both the top and the beginning of everywhere else. Down is merely another direction to be folded back into up, another avenue for the blinding, stinging spray to occupy. The density is low here, meaning that the average distance between any two fixed points is very large, and there are very few points to fix on, regardless. Most of what appears solid is just a temporary coalescence in the all-permeating rush, like a thought at the edge of waking.

The light is very dim, yet the world glows. Bioluminescence is not the cause, nor is some kind of chemical incandescence. Rather, the turbulent water itself seems to generate light through friction, through the impossible pressures and vacuums that open and close every instant. The color is wrong, too—not the familiar blues and greens of the ocean, but something closer to the spectrum of the aurora borealis, smeared and thinned out until it becomes an omnipresent radiance.

Here and there, like broken teeth jutting from the gums of the world, are the remnants of something built. Not buildings in any conventional sense; rather, suggestive geometries that might once have served as anchors or resonators for the chaos. These are not made of stone, but of a substance that seems to be compressed and petrified kelp, its cellular structure amplified until it resembles a vast, porous skeleton. They are more absences than presences, hints of order swallowed by the all-consuming surge.

Sparse vegetation clings to the remnants of these ancient structures. Hard, chitinous fronds that filter the light and the water, perhaps extracting some kind of energy from the perpetual motion. The dominant color is a deep, blood-red, not of rust but of something profoundly alive and alien. It provides the only contrast to the world's shifting pallor -- that stark, primary assertion of *being* against the world's constant becoming. There is very little animal life here. Were I forced to guess, I'd wager that what fauna there is consists of filter-feeders of some kind, or perhaps parasites clinging to the red vegetation. Life here is something to be endured, not celebrated. A grim calculus of energy input versus attrition.

"Weirdness" is too small a word for this place. Reality is optional here. Rules are guidelines. The fundamental laws of physics seem to be in constant negotiation with something... else. The air, thick with moisture and an electric tang, feels *alive*. Not in a benevolent way. More like the feverish sentience of a dying brain.

There is no history here. No past, no future. Only the eternal, deafening now. The architecture, the vegetation, the few signs of earlier life—none of it suggests a linear progression. There is no sense of decay or evolution, only a perpetual state of flux, like a wound that never heals. The water rises, falls, breaks, coalesces again. The deafening susurrus, an ocean chewing at the roof of the world.
