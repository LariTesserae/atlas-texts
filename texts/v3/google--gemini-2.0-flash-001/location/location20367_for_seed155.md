The lichen-slick cobblestones are perpetually damp, a clammy film that rises from the stones even when the thin mountain air bites with early autumn chill. Condensation clings to everything: the rusted ironwork of the abandoned factories, the hairy stalks of colossal fungi that have erupted through the cracked pavement, the fungal blooms staining the once-pristine facades of gothic buildings. It’s the dampness that allows this impossible jungle to thrive at this altitude.

The city of Veridia was built on the Bones. That's what the locals, the few that remain, call them. The impossibly ancient, eroded peaks surrounding the valley are said to be the petrified remains of gods or, perhaps, the discarded skeletons of celestial beasts. The city, with its towering spires and web of elevated walkways, was once a monument to industry, built to extract some rare metal from the Bones. But the mines ran dry generations ago.

Now, Veridia is strangled by vegetation. Not vines or trees, though those are present, but something stranger, more pervasive. Fungal masses pulse with bioluminescent light in the perpetual twilight beneath the factory canopies. Giant, fleshy polypores erupt from rooftops, their gills dripping with viscous, nutrient-rich fluids. Strands of luminous mycelium run like arteries through the city's veins, feeding the bizarre ecosystem.

The air is thick with spores and the grinding, rhythmic rumble of ancient machinery. Automated looms, long since abandoned by their human operators, still churn out bolts of fungal fabric, which unravel and drape over the city like spectral shrouds. The factories are not silent tombs, but fungal farms. Vast chambers are filled with cultivated molds and lichen, their alien processes humming with a low, resonant thrum. The sound is inescapable, a constant reminder that life, albeit alien and fungal, still thrives here.

Fauna is rare, but not entirely absent. Rust-colored, chitinous insects, their bodies encrusted with lichen colonies, scuttle along the fungal walkways. Pale, eyeless salamanders inhabit the dampest corners of the factories, feeding on the strange detritus. And sometimes, on the highest peaks, where the eroding Bones meet the sky, winged creatures made of woven fungi soar on the thermal drafts.

The weirdness seeps into everything. The laws of physics seem…mutable. Buildings can shift subtly, tilting at impossible angles. Shadows lengthen and distort, taking on lives of their own. Time itself feels fluid, stretching and contracting in unpredictable ways. It is said that sometimes, in the deepest, oldest parts of the city, the past and future bleed into the present, creating fleeting glimpses of what was and what might be.

Some whispers claim the fungi are sentient, that they are not merely colonizing Veridia, but reshaping it, converting it into a single, colossal organism. Others say that the rare metal mined from the Bones had unforeseen properties, warping reality and allowing the fungal kingdom to flourish in ways not possible elsewhere.

Whatever the explanation, Veridia is a place of constant transformation. The ancient erosion of the Bones mirrors the decay of the city, while the rampant growth of the fungi represents a strange kind of rebirth. The combination of industry and nature, of technology and biology, creates an environment that is both beautiful and unsettling, a testament to the resilience of life, even in the face of oblivion. The few humans who remain are more like hermits than settlers, living in small enclaves, either cultivating the fungi, or attempting to hide from it.

The sound is loud, the air is thick, but beyond that is a strange sense of peace, of reality come to rest, even if that rest is a writhing, fungal kind.
