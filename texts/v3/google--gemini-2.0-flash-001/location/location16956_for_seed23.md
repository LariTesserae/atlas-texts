The city, if it can be called that anymore, clings to the wind-scoured face of the caldera. It’s less a collection of buildings and more a condensation, a geological afterthought of the mountain itself. The stone here weeps constantly, not dramatically, but in a perpetual film so thin it’s almost invisible unless the low, bright sun catches it just so, turning the whole place into a blinding mirror for a few moments each day. This constant dampness has soaked into everything – the rough-hewn stones of the buildings, the woven reed mats that serve as doors, even the very bones of the place.

The buildings themselves are packed tight, almost touching in places, stacked haphazardly upon each other. They are low, rarely more than two stories, and organically shaped, as if grown rather than built. The mortar is a black, glistening mud that seems to subtly shift and pulse, especially in the infrequent aftershocks that still tremble through the mountain. Most surfaces are covered by a thick layer of moss, green and grey, interspersed with tiny, hardy alpine flowers that bloom in a riot of colour against the cold stone. The combination of the wet stone, dense construction, and near-constant bright sunlight result in a reflective glare that necessitates near-constant squinting.

Vegetation is sparse, beyond the moss and the resilient flowers. Higher up the caldera walls, where the wind has stripped the stone bare, you might find a scattering of gnarled, stunted bushes clinging to cracks, their branches warped into grotesque shapes, and a few hardy ferns. These are not forests, or even groves, though. They are desperate survivors, just barely holding on in a place that seems determined to reject life.

The silence is startling, especially given the sheer volume of life that teems within the city. There are no wind sounds, muffled by the tight construction. The echoing drip of water is absorbed by the moss. The inhabitants move with deliberate slowness, as if the mere act of making a sound would shatter something precious from their lives or awaken some terrible spirit within the mountain. The most common sound is the small rhythmic clicking of insects.

And what insects! Fauna here is abundant and intensely weird. Primarily, it's arthropodic. The place is defined by insects. Towering, iridescent beetles with mandibles that gleam like obsidian stalk the shadowed alleys. Swarms of silver-winged flies buzz around the weeping stones, their bodies shimmering with an otherworldly light. And the spiders… gods, the spiders. They are everywhere, spinning webs of incredible intricacy between buildings, shimmering filaments that catch the sunlight like spun gold. Many are brightly coloured, patterned with intricate designs that seem to shift and change as you look at them. All are venomous.

These are not your average spiders. They have a peculiar intelligence, and some seem to communicate with each other through complex vibrations in their webs. The people of the city have learned to live alongside them, even to cultivate a kind of uneasy truce. In fact, some scholars speculate that the city's architecture and the unusual behaviour of its inhabitants are both deeply influenced by the spiders.

The tools they use are simple: polished stone knives, bark buckets, bone needles. Their clothes are woven from coarse plant fibres stained with dyes derived from the insects, rendering unique camouflage or marking to each person. Their technology is primarily focused on manipulating the bizarre ecosystem of the caldera. They cultivate specific types of moss for food, trap the beetles for their iridescent shells (which are used for adornment and in ritual), and even harvest the venom of certain spiders for medicinal and hunting purposes.

The social structure is rigidly hierarchical, with a council of elders ruling over the city. Their power comes not from force or wealth, but from their knowledge of the mountain's secrets - the patterns of the insects, the flow of water, the tremors that presage earthquakes. There is constant scheming, bartering, and negotiating. Everyone seems to be either a buyer or a seller. This never settles into any sort of peace though: there is an undercurrent of tension, fuelled by the city's precarious existence and the unpredictable nature of the mountain and its strange inhabitants. Betrayal is common, alliances are fragile, and life is cheap. This is a place of constant, churning drama, masked by a deceptive silence.

The source of all this turbulence is the mountain itself. The caldera is not dormant; it breathes. Sometimes, a plume of superheated steam will blast from the vent, and the mountain will quiver. These events are preceded by changes in the behaviour of the arthropodal colonies. As the tension builds, so does the speed and volume of their activity. Before each blast-off you can hear their wings humming louder and faster. It’s an insectile song that heralds change.

In short, it is a precarious and bewildering place, a testament to the tenacity of life in the face of crushing odds. A place where the boundaries between nature and civilization, human and insect, reality and dream, are blurred beyond recognition.
