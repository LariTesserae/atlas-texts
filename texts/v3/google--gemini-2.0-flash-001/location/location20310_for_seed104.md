The world is water. Not water as we know it, not a substance that fills a container or waves on a shore, but the fundamental being of the place. This water is viscous, almost solid, yet alive with shimmering currents. Imagine honey that breathes, or glass filled with a thousand sunsets forever swirling.

Here, the high elevation is not a measure above ground, but a depth within the water. The tallest mountains are the deepest trenches in this sub-aquatic world, and the valleys are the shallows where the light barely penetrates.

And there is light. Dim, diffused, and ever-changing, it filters down from an unseen source above - or perhaps the light wells up from within the water itself, an emanation of the living substance. It paints everything in hues of bruised purple, deep indigo, and sickly green. Bioluminescence is rare, but precious when found. It can be a single point of emerald light in the distance or vast glowing swathes of mycelial webs carpeting the lower depths.

The erosion here is so ancient that it has erased even the memory of stone. Everything is soft, rounded, yielding. The 'ground' — if it can be called that — is a bed of silt thicker than any earthly soil. It's composed of the detritus of ages, the dissolved forms of creatures and plants that ascended and descended, and the eroded remains of what once may have been stone. Walking here means sinking up to the knees in something that feels like warm, yielding mud.

Vegetation thrives, yet the word feels wrong to apply to this place. These are not plants as we understand them, but colossal, soft-bodied organisms that pulse with the same light as the water. Fields of kelp-like fronds sway in the currents, each strand thicker than a ship's mast. There are forests of tube-like trees that filter the water for nutrients. Great bulbous fungi sprout from the silt, their caps glowing softly. These are not distinct life forms, but a densely interwoven tapestry of symbiosis, a single, sprawling super-organism.

Animal life is scarce here but the few creatures that exist have carved unique niches for themselves. There are the filter-feeders, with iridescent scales that catch the dim light as they drift lazily through the water. There are the scavengers, blind and pale, feeling their way through the silt with delicate tendrils. And then, there are the predators, elusive and terrifying, whose existence is whispered in the dark corners of the deep.

Scattered across this landscape are the remnants of something else - odd crystalline structures that jut out from the silty bottom. They are not built, not exactly. They seem to have grown, or been secreted, by some life form that is either gone or lying dormant in the ecosystem. They have smooth surfaces that are warm to the touch, humming with a faint vibration.

The silence here is profound, yet broken by the faintest music. The gentle pulse of the vegetation, the quiet hum of the crystalline structures resonate and amplify each other, creating a symphony that is heard only by those who can truly listen.

This is an active world, a place of constant change. The currents shift with every sunrise and sunset. The vegetation grows and decays in cycles that span millennia. The predators stalk their prey in an endless dance of life and death.

And yet, there is a pervasive instability. The physics of the world itself seems to be unravelling. In certain areas, the water becomes denser, heavier, almost impossible to move through. In others, gravity seems to weaken, and objects float aimlessly. There are pockets where sound changes, where it echoes strangely or disappears entirely.

This place is not dying, but transforming, becoming something else. This reality breakdown is not a catastrophe but a natural evolution, a process of birthing something new. The water, the vegetation, the creatures, are all part of this grand experiment. It is a terrifying and beautiful thing to witness.
