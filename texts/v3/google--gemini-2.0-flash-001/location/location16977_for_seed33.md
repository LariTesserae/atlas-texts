The wind sighs — a subsonic, trembling sigh that vibrates through the crystalline spires and needles of Obsidian Reach. It's almost inaudible, a pressure differential more than a sound, but it sets the entire city humming with sympathetic resonance.

Obsidian Reach clings to the precipice of the Unscalable Crag, a basalt formation thrust miles into a thin, cold atmosphere. Below, the Great Dust sea stretches away to a horizon lost in perpetual twilight. Above, only the faintest glimmer of a distant, dying sun filters through the perpetual dust clouds.

The city itself is a testament to the enduring patience of the Makers, a race now long-gone. It's built not of brick and mortar, but of carved and fused obsidian. The original builders knew secrets of molecular alignment long lost to whatever life remained. They could weave a single obsidian molecule into a thread finer than hair or fuse mountains of basalt into single, seamless structures, with an exact knowledge of the material's resonant frequency.

There’s no water here. Not a drop. But the air isn't dry. Instead, it’s saturated with microscopic particles of obsidian dust, perpetually suspended by electrostatic forces and the relentless wind. The dust coats everything, seeps into everything. Over millennia of static discharge, it has bonded to surfaces, creating strange, fractal patterns on the obsidian surfaces.

There is no true vegetation, but a sort of pseudo-life exists. Crystals of metallic elements, leached from the basalt and shaped by subtle electromagnetic fields, grow in grotesque imitation of plant life. In the deepest shadows, they fluoresce with a cold, internal light. They feed on the faint energy of the planet’s magnetic field, slowly extracting minute quantities of metal from the dust. Some say they are sentient, or at least have a rudimentary form of awareness, echoing the lost minds of the Makers.

Fauna is scarce. Scavengers are, however, more common as lifeforms. The dominant life form is the Dust Wyrm – a silicon-based organism that filters the dust sea for organic matter. The Wyrms are eyeless and legless. They propel themselves through the air, using a combination of electrostatic repulsion and manipulation of the wind. They excrete a glassy substance, which hardens into razor-sharp shards that scatter across the obsidian plains.

The Makers were not alone. They were overthrown by something that had an incredible control over the forces of the material world. The Obsidian sea is carved out of the bodies of the Makers, a horrifying, impossible act. The city is built over a layer of that obsidian. The city absorbs and reflects all light, so that the city is always slightly darker than the world around it.

The strangeness here permeates everything. There are temporal distortions around certain structures, echoes of the Makers' experimentation with the fundamental fabric of reality. One might see a figure flicker into existence for a split second and then vanish. At certain times of day, the shadows lengthen and dance independently of their sources, forming terrifying shapes. Those who have spent too long here begin to perceive patterns beneath the dust, geometries that shouldn't exist, a silent language written in the very substance of the world.

Despite the silence, the lack of water, the absence of vibrant life, there is a constant activity at work, a relentless reshaping. The wind scours the obsidian, the crystalline growths spread, the Dust Wyrms hunt. The city shifts, groans, and settles with each passing cycle of the dying sun. Obsidian Reach endures, a monument to the ambition and madness of a long-dead civilization, haunted by the whispers of forgotten science and the ghosts of shattered reality. The weirdness is the most active part of the world now.
