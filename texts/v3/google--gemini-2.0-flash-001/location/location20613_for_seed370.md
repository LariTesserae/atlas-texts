The Foundry-Cathedral of the Unbinding Flame. It is all there is: a single, enclosed volume. The scale is hamlet-sized, perhaps, but every cubic inch is packed with something. The Forge occupies the whole space, has always occupied it, and always will—for there is no gradient here, nothing changes. The air is thick and hot, the rocks are warm, and the light is dim, yet everywhere the din is deafening.

The light is almost entirely from the lava—but here, lava means something other than molten rock. The Lava of Unbinding is not a chemical event, but a metaphysical one; it is the raw material of existence returning to its unbound state. It flows uphill; it pools in the air; it is not hot in the way that fire is hot, but in the way that possibility is hot.

The rocks are all worked; there is no "natural substrate" here. Everything is constructed, though the builders were pre-metal. They worked in ceramics, in stone, and in something else—a glassy, black substance that seems to drink the light. The technology is primitive, but thorough. The hand-tools and simple fabrication techniques have been applied to every surface, every corner, every plane. It is all made.

The Foundry-Cathedral is high—not in elevation, precisely, but in *position*. It exists on a cusp, a nexus point in the architecture of reality. The lava flows from somewhere above, some unseen vent or source, and pools somewhere beneath. Its warmth and light are all that sustains this ecosystem of stone and fire, for there is no water and no life here. Not animal, not vegetable, not anything else that we might immediately recognize as such.

Except for the Echoes.

The deafening din is not from machinery, though it sounds like it. The din is choral, a constant, harmonic chanting of the Echoes. They are not alive, though they resonate. They are not constructed, though they occupy every surface. They are patterns in the heat, imprints in the stone—memories of creation, perhaps, or the lingering resonance of the Unbinding.

They exist in the cracks and seams of the stonework, in the pools of lava, and in the very air itself. They are not visible, not directly. But they are *felt*. The heat is one aspect of this feeling; the endless, deafening chant is another.

The feeling intensifies near certain constructions—great ceramic pylons that seem to focus and amplify the Unbinding Flame, or obsidian mirrors that reflect… something. Not light, not sound, but the essence of the Foundry itself.

The air shimmers with the density of it all. There is no room between presences, not even for dust. Every cubic millimeter is occupied, saturated with energy and sensation, with the endless chanting of the Echoes, with the heat of the Lava of Unbinding.

It is not hell, not exactly. Nor is it heaven. It is something else entirely—a place of constant, unwavering transformation, where the raw material of existence is endlessly refined, unbound, and reshaped. Where the very stones sing, and the fire remembers. And where nothing ever changes.
