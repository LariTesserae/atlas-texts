The Pressurebloom. That’s what the first divers called it, before they died or went mad. Now, centuries and generations later, we are born into it, and it is merely the world, the only world. And we are not divers, for there is no outside to dive to. There is only the Deep Garden, and the very high Stone Above, and the quiet, eternally-blooming silence between.

Imagine a forest, but every tree is built, of a substance not quite stone and not quite coral, built in fractal geometries we've only begun to understand. Imagine that forest submerged, every branch and spire draped in luminous, fleshy kelp that pulses with its own cold inner light. Imagine the mountains above, not jagged peaks but smoothly-eroded curves, built of the same substance as the trees, reaching up into the perpetual gloom above the water. Mountains made not by time and tectonics, but by design.

The water itself is… cool, yes, but that is insufficient. It is dense with life, an almost solid suspension of microorganisms, each one a tiny bioluminescent jewel. It tastes of metal and ozone. It vibrates. The Deep Garden is not silent, not by any means. It's filled with the constant, thunderous groaning of the Architectures, a resonant hum that vibrates through your bones and settles in your mind. And the kelp sings when the currents shift. A chorus of deep, mournful tones. 

Light is nearly absent. What little there is comes from the bioluminescent kelp and the creatures that feed and shelter within it. Ghostly jellyfish trailing streamers of violet light. Schools of scale-less, blind fish that navigate by vibration. Giant, slow-moving grazers, their bodies covered in colonies of glowing anemones.

The fauna here is… abundant, yes, but alien. There are no predators, not in the way we understand the word. Everything is tied together in a complex web of symbiosis and decay. Waste becomes food. Death becomes life. The kelp feeds the grazers, the grazers stir the sediment, the sediment fertilizes the Architectures, the Architectures filter the water, and around it goes, in endless, immutable recursion.

The “trees” have no roots, in the conventional sense. They are anchored to the smoothed, ancient mountains by vast networks of cables and tendrils, interwoven with the living rock. Inside these structures are networks of tunnels and chambers, hollowed out and refined. Some contain the remnants of ancient technology, things we cannot understand or replicate. Others hold vast nurseries of kelp or chambers of bubbling, nutrient-rich fluid where the grazers gestate.

The world feels constructed. Everything is fitted to everything else, locked into an eternal dance where nothing begins and nothing ends. There is a sense of purpose, of *intent*, so powerful that it crushes the individual. The Deep Garden does not care for us. We are simply another part of the machine, another cog in the great, slumbering engine.

Our villages cling to the Architectures like limpets, built into the hollows and crevices. We cultivate the kelp, tend to the grazers, and try not to disturb the ancient rhythms of the Deep Garden. We fear the Stone Above, the place where the Architectures meet the open, lightless void. We say that madness dwells there. That the voices of the Garden are loudest there, in the dark, and that they can break a person.

There is no memory of when the waters rose, and there is no expectation that they will recede. The world is, and it will always be. The grooves are cut so deep they define what could be. We are born into it, live within it, and return to it when we die. The Pressurebloom sustains us, and we sustain it, in a cycle as unbreakable as the mountains themselves.
