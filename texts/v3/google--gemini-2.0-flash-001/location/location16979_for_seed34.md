The air is thin and bites with a cold that seeps into the marrow. Above, the sky is a pale, washed-out blue, almost white, reflecting the snow that clings stubbornly to the higher reaches of the city. This isn't a city of sharp angles and gleaming towers. It's a city carved, grown, and weathered into the very bones of the high plateau.

Rain comes frequently, not in deluges, but in a persistent mist that rolls in off the distant peaks, clinging to everything. It beads on the lichen-covered stones of the buildings, turning them slick and dark. The water trickles down the intricately carved facades, feeding moss and miniature ferns that sprout from every crevice. "Sparse vegetation" is true enough if you're looking for forests, but here, life clings tenaciously to the rock.

The city sprawls, a dense tapestry of structures interwoven so tightly that it's often impossible to tell where one building ends and another begins. Buildings aren't built *next* to each other, but *into* each other, rooms burrowing into the earth, passages snaking through the heart of the stone. It's a living, breathing organism of architecture, constantly reshaped by the elements and the patient hands of its inhabitants.

There is no technology here as we understand it. The "built" environment is not a triumph *over* nature, but an extension of it. The city is a grand bio-engineering project, utilizing the natural processes of growth and erosion to shape the landscape to its needs. Think of coral reefs, but on a monumental scale, a collaboration between life and the environment.

"Established erosion" is written into every surface. Centuries of wind and rain have softened the sharp edges, rounded the corners, and etched intricate patterns into the stone. Water always finds its way. Channels have been deliberately carved to direct the flow, cascading down stepped terraces, filling cisterns within the very heart of the structures. The sound of running water is a constant, almost subliminal presence, a low hum beneath the silence.

The air, despite the dampness, feels clean, almost sterile. There are few animals to be seen, a bird cries out rarely, so high above.

The scale is deceptive. From a certain vantage point, the city seems to stretch on forever, a miniature mountain range of its own, merging seamlessly with the real mountains in the distance. The low, diffused light amplifies this sense of vastness, blurring the lines between near and far. Weather systems originate here, swirling mists that gather over the city before spilling down the slopes. It has its own climate, a microcosm of the greater world.

The silence is broken only by the drip of water from eaves or the very occasional chime of wind-bells.

The scarcity of fauna, the quietude of the wind, the dampness and cold — they all contribute to a feeling of profound age, of slow, immutable processes unfolding on a scale that dwarfs human conception. Life here feels persistent, but fragile, a tenacious grip on the edge of existence. But it is active, not static. The rain is ever-present, mosses continue to grow, the built environment is always evolving. The plateau knows no stasis.
