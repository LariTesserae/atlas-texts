The sun beats hard on the lip of the fissure, a relentless, swollen eye in a sky utterly bleached of blue. Light spills down into the chasm, but even at its rawest intensity, it barely penetrates the depths. The air here is thick, heavy with the cloying sweetness of rot and the sharper tang of… something else. Something undeniably *alive*.

The fissure is less a cut in the earth and more a forced birth – a ragged, oozing wound from which a new world is being hauled into existence.  The rock walls are slick with condensation, perpetually weeping. This isn’t water as we understand it, though. It's closer to sap, a vital ichor seeping from the xylem of the world. It tastes metallic, a little like blood.

Vegetation explodes outwards from every surface. Not vegetation as individual, striving plants, but as a single, sprawling, interconnected organism. The walls are a riot of fleshy, overlapping leaves, the colours ranging from a near-black crimson to a bilious, luminous green. Giant, bulbous fungi erupt from the foliage, each one sweating a phosphorescent dew that drips into the stagnant pools at the bottom of the fissure. Vines thick as pythons twist and writhe, binding everything together in a dense, suffocating embrace.

There's a sense of raw, untamed energy here, but also an unnerving stillness. The air hangs motionless, thick enough to drink. The silence is profound, broken only by the occasional drip of the sap-water and the faintest, almost subliminal hum that seems to vibrate from the very rocks.

Scattered throughout this green abyss are the ruins of… something. Fragments of structures, crafted from a black, obsidian-like material unlike any of the surrounding stone. They are small, almost insignificant, swallowed by the voracious plant life. A single archway, choked with flowering vines. A crumbling wall, its surface etched with indecipherable symbols. These are not dwellings or temples, but… markers, perhaps? Devices? It's impossible to tell their purpose. They feel… out of place, like a foreign intrusion in a landscape that has otherwise been left to its own devices.

The light diminishes with every foot closer to the bottom. Here, the pools of sap-water become larger, forming shallow, stagnant lakes.  In these murky depths, something stirs.  Fleeting shadows, the faintest ripple on the surface. There’s fauna here, but it is rare, cryptic, and utterly adapted to this strange, subterranean world. Worms thicker than a man’s arm wriggle through the decaying vegetation.  Blind, iridescent insects crawl amongst the fungi. Everything seems to share the same, disconcerting wet sheen.

The weirdness isn't jarring at first, a simple set of unusual visual cues, but as I look for landmarks I realize everything is oriented by the same set of strange principles. Rot goes upwards rather than down, and only the very tips of the highest leaves begin to decay. The brightest colors are in the darkest depths. The structures don't reflect sound. The place *is* the weirdness, made physical.

The most unsettling aspect of this place is its stasis. There is no wind, no seasonal change, no visible sign of evolution or decay. It is as if this moment, this raw, embryonic stage of creation, has been frozen in time. The plants do not grow or wither. The sap-water neither evaporates nor accumulates. The light remains constant, filtered and diffused through the dense foliage above. The fissure exists in a state of perfect, terrifying equilibrium.

It is a world being born, but also a world that will never be. A jungle choked in its own cradle, forever suspended between potential and actuality. A place of beauty and horror, of life and death, locked in an eternal, stifling embrace.
