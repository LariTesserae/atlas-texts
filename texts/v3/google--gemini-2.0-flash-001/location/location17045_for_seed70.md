The floating islands of Xylos drift lazily beneath a sky permanently veiled in mist. Not mist as we know it, of water vapor clinging to the cool earth, but mist of something viscous, something that hangs heavy in the lungs and tastes of brine and ozone. It is Xylos's sky-water, the source of all its life.

These islands aren't stone, or at least, not *only* stone. They are tangled, impossibly vast mats of a plant like nothing earthly. Its roots, thick as city towers, plunge down into the sky-sea, sucking up the nutrients that bloom here, in the only place the water thins enough to let fierce sunlight through: the altitude where stone can barely breathe. This altitude is still miles high, but the islands themselves are another mile above that, a high-altitude alpine world permanently submerged.

The air is thick and warm, a perpetual summer day barely cooled by the height. The light is filtered, diffuse, never truly bright, and the colors are muted: greens and blues and browns dominate, the harsh edges softened by the pervasive moisture. Underneath this, if you dive and dive into the roots, is perpetual night, warmer than one might suspect.

Erosion is minimal here because Xylos is *new*. The islands bud off the central Sky-Tree, a being of unimaginable size and impossible form, and slowly drift outward. The Sky-Tree draws up the mist-nutrients through a core of some unknown element from the planet's heart. As the life around this core cools, the Sky-Tree discards it, in continents or in small islets. What breaks free is a mass of condensed but living root, and still-cooling rock.

The islands are dense with vegetation. Giant, bioluminescent fungi cluster around the bases of the root-trees, casting a soft, ethereal glow in the perpetual twilight. Above, the air is choked with epiphytes: orchids and bromeliads and mosses that grow upon the roots, forming hanging gardens that spill down towards the cloud-sea. Vines, thick as pythons, snake through the undergrowth, connecting one root-tree to the next, forming a tangled, impenetrable web.

The soundscape is mostly quiet. The wind whispers through the root-trees, occasionally broken by the rustle of the hanging gardens or the drip of condensation. The rare fauna consists mostly of insects: enormous, iridescent beetles that crawl through the undergrowth, their chitinous shells clicking softly; and swarms of tiny, bioluminescent gnats that dance in the twilight, their light painting ephemeral patterns in the air. Higher in the canopy, the patient, slow rasping of the Grasper can sometimes be heard, as it pulls nutrients from the roots.

The rules of this world are simple: grow, consume, drift. The islands are constantly evolving, new roots emerging, old ones decaying. The Sky-Tree takes and gives, and the nutrients slowly replenish. Island drifts slowly into island; collisions often shatter smaller parcels, or merge bigger ones. Change happens slowly, imperceptibly, across decades and centuries.

There is a sense of deep time here, of processes unfolding far beyond human comprehension. The quiet hum of the island roots is the only true sound, if you can sense it -- the patient, inexorable pulse of a world still in its infancy, slowly drifting towards some unknown future. Perhaps, deep in the roots, the rare, patient, quiet, and slow creatures that lurk there will evolve into something loud. Perhaps the erosion here will accelerate, and the islands turn to sand, which falls into the sky-sea, where an island can begin again. These are the rhythms of Xylos: slow, steady, strange, beautiful.
