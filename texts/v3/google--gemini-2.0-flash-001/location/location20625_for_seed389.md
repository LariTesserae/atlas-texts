The Skiving Towers climb from the iron plain. Not *made* of iron, exactly, but infused with it, steeped in it, as bones are steeped in marrow. The iron is everywhere in the valley, a slow rust-slick on every surface. It is less a color than a film, a taste in the hot, still air. 

The towers are packed close, and tall beyond reason. Their forms are chaotic – ziggurats stacked on cylinders jammed through cubes skewered by bridges that crumble into dust halfway across. Most are windowless. Some of those have doors. Few of those doors lead anywhere but a sheer drop.

This is a city, if a city can be said to exist without people. There are structures intended for habitation – or something like it. Racks. Shelves. Troughs. But the only presences are mechanical: pumps that hiss and cough; ducts that vibrate with the passage of something viscous and foul-smelling; the grinding of unseen gears deep within the towers’ bowels.

The heat is oppressive, baking the rust-flecked stone. It rises in shimmering waves off the iron plain, obscuring the horizon. There is an intermittent *drip... drip... drip...* echoing through the canyons between the towers. It is the sound of condensation forming on the cool metal of the upper reaches, reluctantly surrendering to gravity. The water is thick with minerals, almost oily. Where it gathers in cracks and crevices, it forms grotesque stalactites of rust-colored crystal.

The only movement comes from the machines themselves. They are not maintained, not organized, not aimed at any purpose a sane mind could recognize. They simply *are* — a vast, clattering, leaking ecosystem of gears and pipes and pressure valves. Each generation breaks down and is cannibalized by the next.

There are creatures. Rare ones. Twisted, metallic things that scuttle along the shadows. They seem to be made of the same material as the towers, animated by some internal furnace. They are not hostile, simply indifferent. They pass and repass like iron filings in a magnetic field.

The silence is profound, broken only by the mechanical wheezing of the city. It is not the absence of sound, but the weight of it – the dense, suffocating silence of an engine room about to explode. It is a silence that breeds its own kind of tension, a quiet anticipation of something catastrophic.

This place is not old, but young; it feels raw, unformed. The towers are not built so much as *grown*, extruded from the iron earth like malignant tumors. They are still forming, expanding, slowly consuming the landscape around them.

It is difficult to imagine anyone *living* here. And yet, there is an undeniable sense of… *purpose*. Not a conscious purpose, not a grand design, but something more fundamental. A drive toward complexity, toward density, toward the relentless accumulation of matter and energy. It is as if the city is not a place at all, but an experiment. An experiment in the limits of form, the possibilities of decay, the endurance of iron in the face of entropy. An experiment that has been running for millennia, and will continue for millennia to come.
