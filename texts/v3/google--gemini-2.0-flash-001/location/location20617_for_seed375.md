The air is thick with the taste of iron and something else, something acrid and unfamiliar that stings the nostrils. It's cold, a bone-deep chill that seeps in despite the thick, woven fibers I'm wearing. Above, the sky is a dull, bruised purple, barely lighter than the ground. Light is scarce, a perpetual twilight that casts long, distorted shadows.

This is the Rust District, a forgotten quarter of the Citadel. It is not a city in the way I understood cities, as a place of exchange, commerce, and life. It is a calcified organ. The Citadel, or what remains of it, is carved into the shell of a long-dead beast, perhaps the size of a small moon. Here, in the District, the shell's inner surface is exposed, a landscape of metallic ores and strange, crystallizing compounds.

There is almost no water. Minute droplets condense from the air and gather in crevices, forming shimmering, oily pools that reflect the dim light. These pools are prized, harvested by the few who still dwell here in the Rust.

Vegetation is a rare sight. Twisted, lichen-like growths cling to the metal walls, their colors ranging from a dull orange to a sickly green. They seem to thrive on the mineral-rich air, drawing sustenance from the very bones of the Citadel. I saw one, a puffball thing like coral, burst with a dry popping sound, releasing a cloud of spores into the still air.

I walk along a path that may once have been a grand avenue. Now, it's a narrow track carved through drifts of metallic dust and crumbling structures. The buildings loom on either side, skeletal remains of towering edifices. The silence is profound, broken only by the crunch of my boots on the ground and the occasional groan of metal under stress.

This place is young in some ways, and ancient in others. The metal shell has been exposed relatively recently, perhaps through some catastrophe that peeled away the outer layers of the Citadel. But the geological processes at play – the slow oxidation, the crystallization of exotic minerals – have been grinding away for millennia. The erosion is subtle, but relentless.

The scale is both intimate and vast. The District is small enough to traverse in a few days, yet the towering structures and the sheer emptiness make me feel insignificant. I am a speck in the presence of something incomprehensibly old and immense.

Density is low; I am almost entirely alone. When I see other beings, they are scavengers, clad in patched-together fabrics, faces hidden behind respirators. They do not speak. They move with a desperate and focused purpose, searching for scraps of metal or rare crystalline formations. We pass each other like ghosts.

The Citadel was built, without question. The lines are still visible, though fractured, showing vast complexes of buildings cut into one another over time. This was a planned place, a structured place, an intentional place. But now, the intention has evaporated, leaving only the cold, hard geometry of decay.

There is no technology here, or none that I recognize. This place is beyond tech. The inhabitants are utterly dependent on their own ingenuity and the sparse resources that the Rust provides. If something fails, there is no replacement.

Fauna is nearly nonexistent, and so very weird. I saw something flitting in the shadows earlier, a creature with iridescent wings and too many legs. It vanished before I could get a good look. I also saw larger things, shapes shifting in the corners of my vision.

And now a change. The silence breaks. A pulse, like a gong strike, vibrates deep within my chest. At first I think it's purely internal, a trick of the nerves. But it comes again, louder. The air around me shimmers. Ahead, heat rises off the buildings.

Geothermal vents, or perhaps the core of whatever beast this Citadel was built on, is exposed in the Rust District. Heat is building, releasing pressure. The slow oxidation accelerates, now turning into a full-blown alchemical reaction. The place is coming alive, not in the biological sense, but in a purely elemental one. Gouts of flame erupt from fissures in the ground, illuminating the twisted metal architecture in a hellish glow.

The Rust District is waking up. What it will become, I cannot imagine. But one thing is certain: it will not remain silent for long.
