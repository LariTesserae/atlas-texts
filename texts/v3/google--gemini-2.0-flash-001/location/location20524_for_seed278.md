The Iron Mire stretches for impossible distances, a cold, high plateau sculpted by ages of patient erosion. It is a silent place, or nearly so - the faint, rhythmic *hiss* of condensation forming on metallic surfaces is the closest thing to a constant sound. The air bites with a damp, chilling wind that carries the scent of rust and something ancient and oily.

Sparse, skeletal vegetation clings to the iron-rich soil in the valleys and sheltered crevices. Twisted, black-leaved shrubs and wiry, grey grasses struggle against the cold and aridity, their forms permanently bowed by the wind. Patches of crystalline, orange lichen crust the metal bedrock, their color a mockery of warmth in this bleak landscape.

Everywhere, there is metal. Not the clean, polished metal of manufacture, but the corroded, fractured remains of vast machinery. Gigantic struts, half-buried in the soil, jut from the ground like the bones of colossal beasts. Enormous gears, their teeth worn smooth, lie scattered across the plains. Twisted pipes, their purpose long forgotten, snake through the valleys, carrying only the whisper of the wind. This is not a place of industry, but a graveyard of it.

Despite the pervasive decay, there is a sense of balance. The erosion has finished its work, grinding the once-towering structures into manageable forms. The climate, harsh as it is, has found its equilibrium. The plants and lichens have adapted, evolving to thrive in this metallic wasteland.

The silence is broken only by the movement of the Fauna. A low, continuous *thrum* pervades the plateau. Millions upon millions of chitinous Insects occupy every level of the biome. Towering insectile megafauna, covered in scavenged scrap metal for armour, rumble through the landscape. Smaller scavengers, gleaming with metallic chitin, pick through the rusting wreckage. The Insects are the masters of this realm, having long adapted to the industrial ruins and incorporating its materials into their very forms. They are drawn to the metal in the world like flies to honey.

The Insects exist to process the iron. They consume any and all metal matter, breaking it down to microscopic particles within their bodies, and then excrete those particles across the land as a form of digested 'soil'. This soil, rich in metal and biological waste, is then cultivated by the smaller insectile workers, who nurture the strange plant life of the Mire.

The light is dim, filtered through a perpetual haze of rust-colored dust. The shadows cling to the deep valleys, concealing the true extent of the decay. The vast scale of the Mire swallows any sense of perspective, making it impossible to gauge distances or the size of the ruined machinery.

Change comes slowly to the Iron Mire. The wind continues to scour the plateau, the cold slowly cracks the metal. The Insects continue their endless cycle of consumption and waste. The slow, agonizing decomposition is a process on the geological timescale, a testament to the immense effort that went into creating and then abandoning this colossal industrial landscape. A place where life exists, but also patiently eats its dead. A place where the end has already happened, and yet, the process stumbles slowly onwards.
