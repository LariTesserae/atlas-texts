Okay, here we go.

The air is thin and bites with cold. It's not the kind of cold that settles deep into your bones and stays there for days, but a sharp, immediate cold that pricks at exposed skin and etches itself into your breath. The light, though normal in spectrum, feels diminished, filtered by the perpetual mist that clings to everything at this altitude.

This isn't a mountain peak, exactly, though it shares the same merciless height. Instead, it's more like a plateau, or perhaps a series of interconnected ledges carved into the face of something much larger. And it is built. Unmistakably. Not shaped or 'in harmony with,' but undeniably constructed. 

The 'terrain' is a latticework of metal and something like crystallized glass - probably a type of industrial mineral matrix. It's remarkably free of any soil, despite the dampness. Water is everywhere: condensed moisture clinging to the metallic surfaces, tiny rivulets tracing paths down the crystalline structures, and occasionally, brief, stinging showers that sweep across the open areas. Underneath the lattice lies a huge space, carved into the mountain, and filled with pipes and structures of immense scale that occasionally vent steam from massive, hidden geothermal sources.

The architecture is bewildering. If you could even call it that. It's urban, certainly, in the sense that there's a density of structures, but there's no sense of habitation, at least not as we understand it. Towers of corroded metal reach towards the sky, their surfaces etched with intricate, almost fractal patterns. Bridges of the glass-like material arch across chasms, disappearing into the mist. The whole thing reminds me of a ruin that’s trying – and failing – to be a city.

There are few signs of life beyond the algae that cling to the wettest surfaces. Those areas catch the light and glow emerald green. I spot, one time, what looks like a small group of six legged dog-sized animals with iridescent carapaces move slowly between two of the gigantic metal towers, but they disappear from view quickly, not reappearing later. And there might be something flying very high above that is constantly moving and catching the light, but I can’t quite be sure that it wasn’t an artifact of my eyes or of the light bouncing off the clouds.

The technology is... alien. Not in the Roswell sense, but in the sense that it operates on principles I only vaguely grasp. I see conduits humming with energy, strange symbols glowing faintly on various surfaces. The mist itself seems to be generated by some kind of atmospheric processor, drawing moisture out of the thin air and condensing it, perhaps for some industrial purpose. It feels both immensely powerful and deeply corrupted, like the ghost of a machine.

The 'sound' is telling. There's almost constant hissing of steam escaping somewhere underground. And the sigh of the wind through the latticework. And a deep, almost inaudible hum that vibrates through the ground. But beyond that, silence. A profound, unsettling silence.

It's a very active place, though. Not in a chaotic, bustling way, but in a state of continuous flux. The weather is constantly shifting, the condensation patterns are always changing, and the underlying geothermal activity pulses with a slow, rhythmic beat. It feels like a body, slowly decaying, but still stubbornly alive. The constant movement of water itself is slowly breaking down the metal structures. There’s evidence that structures in the city occasionally collapse. Large sections are fractured or have shifted over very long periods of time. 

And that gives me a sense of time. This wasn't recently abandoned. This isn't the scene of some sudden cataclysm. This has been slowly, inexorably decaying for a very, very long time. And it makes me wonder…What was it *for*?
