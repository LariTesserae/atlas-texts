The salt plains shimmer under a sun that feels too present, too insistent. Not hot, exactly - a steady, pressing warmth like a hand held too long against your back. Heat radiates not so much up from the ground as *out* from it, an emanation of some internal furnace buried deep. The salt itself isn't the grainy powder one might expect. Instead, it forms colossal, interlocking plates, polygons the size of houses, each fitted perfectly against its neighbor. The edges are razor sharp, unnaturally so. They gleam with a faint, internal luminescence, the same color as the sun: a deep, almost bruised-peach hue. 

Despite the absolute lack of surface water, life finds a purchase here. Between the plates, squeezing through the impossibly tight seams, grow the 'lithocord' – thick, rope-like vines that glow with their own inner light, a paler, almost sickly green. They writhe and pulse with a slow, internal rhythm, like the veins of some immense, buried organism. The lithocord doesn't seem to need water; it pulls nourishment from the salt itself, transmuting minerals into… something else. Something alien.

The land isn't flat, exactly. Gentle, wave-like undulations ripple across the salt flats, almost imperceptible rises and dips that stretch for kilometers. The lithocord follows these contours, creating luminous valleys and crests, a mesmerizing, slowly breathing topography. The utter lack of variation, though, reinforces the stillness. It’s a landscape trapped in amber, a single frame held forever.

The silence is almost absolute. A low, subsonic hum vibrates constantly through the ground, resonating in your bones, a constant thrum from the unseen depths. Every few hours, a single, crystalline chime pierces the quiet, echoing across the flats. It emanates from some unknown source, carries a tone of perfect, unnerving clarity. 

This is a place of impossible geometry and physics-defying biology, clearly the product of technology beyond current comprehension. Perhaps this isn't a natural landscape at all but some kind of enormous machine, its function inscrutable. The glow of the salt plates seems less like mineral luminescence and more like some form of intricate energy network, a vast circuit board exposed to the pitiless sun.

Sometimes, skimming a few feet above the salt, you might see the "skimmers" — rare, fragile beings like translucent jellyfish that drift in the air, propelled by subtle electromagnetic fields. Their bodies are filled with shimmering, liquid light, the same bruised-peach color as the salt. They are the only visible fauna here, and they seem utterly unconcerned with anything beyond their own slow, looping trajectories. They are like living sensors, sampling the energy of the flats.

The weirdness here is constant and pervasive. There are moments when the horizon seems to warp, when the geometry of the salt plates shifts and fractures into impossible shapes. Briefly, for a blink of an eye, the world breaks down into raw code, complex equations scrawled across the landscape, then snaps back into its frozen equilibrium. As if something is trying to assert itself, or correct some fundamental error.

Most unsettling is the sense of being watched. Not by anything living, but by the place itself. As though the salt plains, the lithocord, the internal humming, all are part of a vast, incomprehensible consciousness. The silence isn't empty, but pregnant with an awareness that far outstrips human understanding. It's a place that waits, that observes, that never changes... until, perhaps, it does.
