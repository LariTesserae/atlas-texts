The air hangs thick and heavy, not with moisture, but with the cloying, almost edible scent of sun-baked resins and something akin to overripe fruit. It's hot, a heavy, draping heat that settles on you like a blanket woven from sand, but not unbearable. A dim, filtered light permeates everything, casting long, distorted shadows that dance with constant, unseen movement.

Underfoot is something like packed earth, but studded with glistening nodules of what seems like obsidian, though it’s warm to the touch. It crunches softly underfoot. This is a small place, maybe a few acres, carved into the side of something vast and unseen, something that looms above, shrouded in a perpetual twilight.

Vegetation explodes from this dry earth in a riot of forms that defy easy classification. It isn’t “plant life” as we understand it, but something…else. Tree-analogues rise, their trunks constructed from stacked plates of shimmering, chitinous material, like monumental mushrooms grown impossibly tall. Instead of leaves, they bear clusters of fleshy, bioluminescent bulbs that pulse with an internal light, adding to the strange dimness of the place. Vines thick as pythons, but covered in iridescent scales, snake between these structures, dripping a viscous, honey-like substance that hardens quickly into amber-colored gobs.

The air vibrates with sound, a cacophony that is at first overwhelming, then strangely comforting. It is a layered tapestry woven from chirps, clicks, whistles, and guttural croaks, the rustling of the scaled vines, the gentle popping of the bioluminescent bulbs, and a low, constant thrum that seems to resonate from the ground itself. This thrum is the root of all the sounds, the generative vibration, the hum that everything else builds upon.

The source of this sound is also the abundance of fauna. Creatures swarm in the half-light, insectile beings with bodies like polished gems, flitting and darting between the tree-structures. Larger, six-legged reptiles, covered in feathery quills that shimmer with an oil-slick iridescence, lumber through the undergrowth, their calls sounding like the grinding of rocks. The amber-colored gobs are consumed by creatures that resemble terrestrial snails, but with multiple pairs of eyes on stalks that wave independently from one another. So much life, so much energy packed into this small space, it feels like a pressure in the air.

Scattered amongst this alien ecosystem are remnants of some past presence. A few structures built from the same processed obsidian like nodules, simple, rudimentary shelters. Some are fallen, taken over by the local life. Others remain upright, their surfaces covered in primitive bas-reliefs depicting scenes of…something. Hard to tell. Figures with too many limbs, symbols that resemble nothing on earth. Near one of the ruined structures is a pile of discarded tools–smooth, polished stones lashed to handles of what looks like fossilized bone. A simple knife of obsidian lies nearby.

The rules here are fluid, in constant flux. Small collapses of the structures happen quite often and new structures just seem to grow and spring up in their wake. The creatures engage in frenzied mating rituals one moment, then tear into each other with startling ferocity the next. The honey-like substance drips and hardens, forming ephemeral sculptures that are just as quickly consumed or eroded. The thrumming becomes louder and the bioluminescent bulbs flash in synchronicity, then the cycle repeats. Everything pulses with a wild, untamed energy, a constant cycle of creation and destruction. It is a place that refuses to be still, a single, bounded arena of frantic, accelerated life.
