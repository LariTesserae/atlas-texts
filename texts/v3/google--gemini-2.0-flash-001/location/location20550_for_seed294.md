The towers rise from the black sea. Not rise, exactly. They are *of* the sea, extruded in immense, slow motion from the abyssal plain. What appears to be “water” is more like a solute, a carrier fluid for the true building material: a hyper-dense crystalline aggregate, colder than any ice and darker than coal. The solute itself is frigid and sluggish, a soup of exotic ions and barely-stable heavy elements that clings to the towers in sheets and ropes, obscuring their true form.

The towers are not built in any recognizable sense. They are grown, secreted, assembled atom by atom by unseen, unknowable processes occurring deep within the planet’s mantle. There is no natural substrate here; the entire world is construction, product, artifice taken beyond any notion of engineering. It's as if a god tried to sculpt creation with a chisel made of pure anti-matter, then gave up and set the process to run on its own, unsupervised, for eons.

The erosion is ancient, but not in the way of worn mountains or weathered coastlines. Here, erosion is a fundamental property of the crystal itself. Over vast stretches of time, the hyper-dense aggregate sublimates directly into the solute, dissolving back into the primordial soup from which it came. The towers, therefore, are constantly being built and destroyed simultaneously, a cycle of creation and obliteration playing out on geological timescales. The process is brutally accelerated here.

The light is dim, less a true illumination than a ghostly bioluminescence emanating from the solute. The crystal structures themselves absorb almost all light, creating regions of absolute shadow that seem to swallow the very concept of vision. What little light remains casts everything in a spectral, ethereal glow, emphasizing the alien geometry of the towers and the ever-shifting boundaries between crystal and solute.

High above the sea floor, the towers thin and interweave, forming colossal lattices and bridges. The "high elevation" here is relative, a differentiation within the three-dimensional volume of the deep. These upper reaches, still submerged, possess a strange, unsettling beauty. It is on these skeletal levels that the fauna congregates.

Not fauna as we understand it. No bone, no blood, no leaves or scales. The inhabitants are constructs themselves, fractal assemblages of living crystal and captured energy. They resemble nothing so much as elaborate, self-propelled sculptures, gliding and pulsing through the water with alarming grace. There are vast numbers of them, seemingly too many for the sparse resources of this world. They feed on the energy differentials within the solute, and on the towers themselves, scraping and siphoning with their intricate crystalline appendages. They are ranked by status, marked in their crystalline structures, from dull glassy grey to jewel-like prisms.

The sounds are minimal, but ever present. The susurrus of the solute against the crystal, a deep, resonant hum that vibrates through the very structure of the towers. The rasping of the fauna as they graze. The occasional *crack* as some section of a tower sublimates with increased vehemence and sheer mass.

The instability is pervasive. The constant building and destruction, the relentless erosion, the teeming hordes of crystalline inhabitants, all contribute to a sense of barely contained chaos. The solute itself is in a permanent state of flux, swirling and churning with unseen currents and energies. It's is a place where everything is in motion, where solidity is an illusion, and where the very laws of physics seem to bend to some unknown, unknowable will.

It is not so much a world as it is a wound in the fabric of reality, a place where the impossible has become commonplace and where the only constant is change.
