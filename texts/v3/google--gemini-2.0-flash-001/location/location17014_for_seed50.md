The Sea Organ howls. Not the sea, not directly, though it feeds the organ's song. It's the wind, caught in resonators that are still half-buried in the young cliffs, shaped of porous stone that's barely hardened from the mud of ages. The cliffs rise high, improbably high, for a place so close to the churning sea. They are not continuous walls, but jagged peaks separated by impossible chasms, a mountain range turned inside-out.

The water is everywhere. Not just below, roaring against the organ-cliffs, but as a constant, icy mist that stings the face. It beads on the few tough, spiky grasses that cling to the rare patches of less-exposed stone. It condenses, drips, and flows in rivulets down the newly-formed slopes. The light is normal, but diffused, refracted into a thousand shimmering fragments through the ceaseless spray. You could, on a clearer day, look upward and see the disc of a yellow sun, if you could find a patch of sky not choked by the storm.

The land is *young*. You can feel it in the grit underfoot, in the way the cliffs crumble at the slightest touch, in the metallic tang to the air. It's a pocket-world, held suspended between annihilation and becoming. The geological processes that elsewhere take eons are compressed here, accelerated to a frantic pace. Landslides happen daily, reshaping the raw, torn contours of the cliffs. The empty spaces yawn between the peaks, impossibly wide for such a compressed area. Looking into them is like staring into a nascent universe, all potential and void.

The "sparse" vegetation is misleading. It's not that nothing *can* grow here, but that nothing lasts long enough. The wind and the sea snatch away anything that dares to root itself too deeply. The grasses, though, are tenacious. They are not green, but a chitinous, brown-black, almost like the feathers of drowned birds clinging to the rock. They hiss in the wind, a quieter counterpoint to the bellow of the organ.

And then there are the scattered structures. "Buildings" is too strong a word. They are more like nests, lashed together from driftwood, scavenged metal scraps, and the bones of enormous, unidentifiable sea creatures that wash up on the shore with alarming frequency. These nests cling precariously to the less-exposed faces of the cliffs, connected by rope bridges that sway wildly in the gale. Inside, one might find crude tools – stone axes, sharpened shells, nets woven from seaweed, and primitive wind-chimes, perhaps an attempt to make music more deliberate than the random symphony of the cliffs. Smoke rises thinly from a centrally located pit.

The fauna are scarce, but *strange*. There are pale, eyeless fish that thrash in the shallowest pools, driven inland by the relentless surf. There are huge, flightless, vaguely-reptilian birds with oily plumage that patrol the crags, their screeches almost lost in the general din. And then there are… the things that built the nests. Scuttling figures, rarely seen in daylight, with limbs too long, joints that bend the wrong way, and faces obscured by crude masks woven from seaweed. They collect the oily plumage of the enormous birds. They collect the bones of the nameless creatures. They crawl along the cliffs with inhuman agility, their movements almost as chaotic as the landscape itself. They are collectors, scavengers, survivors, existing on the very edge of what is possible. They are not constructing a civilization, but simply *persisting*, echoes in the deafening roar of creation and destruction.

The sound is the defining characteristic of this place: a screaming cacophony. The wind is bent and amplified by the cliffs of raw stone, and the organ's pipes are but the most focused component of a general shrieking blast. The crash of the waves echoes in the caves. The hiss of the rain is continuous, relentless. The shriek of the giant birds is like a fingernail on the raw stone of a chalkboard. It is a place of extreme flux, where the familiar rules of nature are bent and broken, and drowned in the roar of… something. Something being born, or something dying. Or both, at the same time, in the same breath.
