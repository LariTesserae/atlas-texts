The wind screams. It is a sound that does not speak of weather, but of the world's raw, unfinished agony. Here, on the knife edge of creation, sound isn’t carried by air – there is almost nothing *to* carry it. Instead, the very rock vibrates, a bone-deep resonance that obliterates thought and leaves only the primal terror of being.

The peaks are impossibly high, jagged teeth gnawing at a sky that is less a color and more a pressure. It is a cool, oppressive weight, a dim twilight perpetually struggling to be born. The rock itself is new, almost foetal. Imagine obsidian mixed with chalk, a dull, unstable material that crumbles at the lightest touch. Great, raw cliffs plunge into chasms so deep they seem to swallow the light. There is no soil, no weathered comfort, only the newly fractured face of the world.

Here and there, defying all sense, clumps of vegetation explode from the stone. Not soft, forgiving mosses or resilient lichens, but something… harder. Imagine braided cables of hammered copper, each strand tipped with a bloom of crystalline amethyst. These "plants" don't draw sustenance from the ground, for there is nothing to draw. Instead, they seem to… condense the ambient energy, to siphon the raw potential crackling in the air. They pulse faintly with a light of their own, an alien photosynthesis fueled by… by what? The screams of the wind? The pressure of the sky?

The scale is vast and terrible. You could walk for days and see no change, only the slow, agonizing birth of a world. The absence of water is absolute. But the dryness isn't barren; it's a thirst that fuels the whole place. It feels like an element in itself, a vacuum that draws all energy into the rock, into the screaming wind, into the impossible, crystalline flora.

Scattered across this desolate landscape are… cairns. They are crudely built, made of the same crumbling rock as the surrounding cliffs, and yet they stand. They are built in the old way, the stone singing out a truth that the builders have long forgotten. Some are small, little more than piles of rubble, others are immense, towering monuments that echo the impossible heights of the peaks. They’re not built *on* the land, but built *into* it – more like outcroppings of ordered stone – a slow attempt to suture the fractured landscape.

Around these cairns, primitive tools lie scattered— hammers of worked basalt, chisels of some dull, unidentifiable metal, and strange, intricately carved stones. These tools, like the cairns, are on the edge of coming into being. As though someone willed them forth from the ambient energy and then died before they could be properly completed. There is a strange, almost sentient quality to the stone. As though it remembers the hands that shaped it, longs for the touch that will never come.

The weirdness is pervasive. You might see a vista that seems utterly natural, and then, the pressure of the sky will change, and the rocks begin to shimmer, revealing fractal patterns etched into their surfaces. The ground will begin to shake very, very slowly, and the amethysts begin to vibrate, emitting almost imperceptible sounds. Or see a cairn shift in place, a monumental grinding that takes days.

There is no animal life. Only the wind, the rock, the crystalline plants, the cairns, and the lingering ghost of a purpose that was never quite fulfilled. It is a place of deafening silence, a symphony of absence, where the world is still trying to decide what it wants to be. And even the cairns are slowly crumbling back into the raw, potential chaos from which they came, the silence around them getting just a little louder.
