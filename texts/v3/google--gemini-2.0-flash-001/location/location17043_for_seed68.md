The air hangs heavy, a persistent, clinging dampness that beads on every surface. It's not rain, not exactly, though the sky is perpetually overcast, pregnant with the threat of it. More like a deep, ambient humidity, the kind that seeps into your bones and makes metal slick to the touch. Everything is touched with a sheen of moisture.

This is the lower ward of Pistonburgh, a sprawling cluster of workshops and manufactories that clings to the muddy banks of the slowly, inexorably widening Grease River. The river, living up to its name, is less water and more a viscous, iridescent soup of industrial runoff, clinging pollutants, and the detritus of a thousand workshops. Its surface shimmers with an unnatural, oily rainbow, and its banks are choked with a riot of strange, semi-aquatic vegetation that has somehow managed to thrive on the poisonous water.

This isn't a place of grand avenues or towering structures. The built environment is a chaotic jumble of single-story brick buildings, often cobbled together from mismatched materials. Corrugated iron roofs sag under the weight of moss and perpetually dripping condensation. Narrow, cobbled alleys twist and turn, often ending in dead ends against the greasy riverbank or blocked by piles of discarded machinery.

Despite the abundance of buildings, the density feels sparse. The workshops are spread out, each with its own small yard or access to a loading pier on the river. There are few citizens milling about, or maybe it just feels like that because nobody lingers without purpose.

The vegetation is surprisingly lush, a verdant assault on the industrial grime. Thick mats of moss cover every available surface, softening the sharp edges of brick and metal. Vines, their leaves unnaturally large and glossy, snake across walls and dangle from rooftops. In the pockets of stagnant water along the river's edge, strange, bulbous plants with fleshy leaves erupt in clusters, their pale green forms glowing faintly in the gloom. Weeds crowd every corner and gap.

The temperature is consistently cool, the damp air never quite warming, even in what passes for summer here. A chilling fog often rolls in from the river, blanketing the ward in a grey shroud.

Sharp, jagged edges break off from the factory walls from the damp.

The scale is intimate, almost claustrophobic. You're never far from a wall, a building, or the river. It's a place of cramped spaces and close quarters, where the sounds of industry echo and amplify.

And those sounds... Pistonburgh lives up to its name. It's a cacophony of industry, a deafening symphony of clanking machinery, hissing steam, grinding gears, and the rhythmic thud of hammers on metal. The air vibrates with the constant, relentless hum of the engines and dynamos that power this place. It is impossible to find true quiet here; even in the deepest alleyways, the industrial cacophony persists, a low, background drone.

The fauna is equally abundant, and just as suited to the environment. Rats, their fur slick with grease, scurry through the alleys. Strange, iridescent insects, with chitinous bodies and buzzing wings, flit through the air. But the most common creatures are the rust-colored birds that nest in the vines and scavenge among the machinery, their calls adding to the industrial din.

The technology here is decidedly industrial. Steam engines belch smoke and soot, their pistons churning relentlessly. Dynamos hum with barely contained energy. Conveyor belts carry raw materials and finished goods from one workshop to another. Wires crisscross the sky, tangled and overloaded, sparking and spitting with errant electricity. There's a pervasive smell of oil and coal smoke, overlaid with a faint, sickly sweetness that comes from the river itself.

Change comes slowly to Pistonburgh. The river continues to widen, slowly eroding the foundations of the workshops. The vines continue to grow, gradually reclaiming the built environment. The machines grow more obsolete as newer and better things are designed. Eventually, everyone leaves, but not for a long while. Still, the wheels keep turning and the pistons keep rising.

The weirdness of Pistonburgh isn't overt, but it's there, lurking beneath the surface. The way the plants thrive in the polluted water. The strange coloration of the insects. The unsettling uniformity of the rust-colored birds. The unsettling persistence of the work that goes on here. It's a subtle unease, the feeling that this place is just slightly off, a little too persistent, a little too strange.
