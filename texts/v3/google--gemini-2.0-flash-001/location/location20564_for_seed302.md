The air is thin and bites with a chill that never quite leaves. It's a wet cold, the kind that seeps into bone despite the glaring, almost painful, brightness of the sun. Up here, on the high plateau, the light seems to have less atmosphere to filter through, and it bounces mercilessly off the slick, dark stone.

The plateau stretches for what feels like forever, a vast tableland carved from some unknown material. It isn't rock as we know it. It's denser, almost metallic, with a deep, resonant hum that vibrates through your feet even through thick boots. This hum is the base note of the deafening soundscape – a constant, low thrum that permeates everything. It's overlaid with a high-pitched whine, like wind forced through impossibly narrow crevices, though here there aren't any such crevices. The whine has a complex harmonic structure that almost resolves into melodies, but never quite does.

Everything here is black, or very close to it. Not a soft, light-absorbing black, but a polished, light-reflecting obsidian black. The plateau surface is smooth in some places, like a mirror reflecting the almost white sky, and fractured in others, the cracks clean and sharp. Streaks of a strange, iridescent oil slick these fissures, giving them an unnatural, shimmering depth.

Vegetation is absent, completely and utterly. Not even the hardiest mosses cling to these stones. There's a mineral purity to the landscape, a sense of something pre-biological, an element in its rawest form. The only hints of anything that could be considered "life" are the scattered formations that rise from the plateau surface. They resemble anthills, perhaps, or termite mounds, but made of the same dense, black material as the plateau itself. They are perfectly still, perfectly silent. They don't seem to *do* anything. Every so often, a crystalline structure erupts from a mound, glittering like a shard of glass. Then it's gone.

The water is present, but not in the form of flowing streams or standing pools. Instead, it condenses directly from the air, forming a constant, thin sheen on every surface. The condensation isn't pure, it's a brackish fluid that smells faintly of sulfur and ozone, and coats everything with an oily grime. Small rivulets are only transient. The ground is so densely packed it doesn't absorb or allow escape, but evaporation and constant addition seem to be in such perfect equivalence that everything is constantly, lightly wet.

Fauna is rare. Very rare. Once in what might be a week of endless day, an anomalous object emerges from the vibrating dark. They are dark, streamlined shapes that fly below the point where sound exists, zipping past as if space is not real.

Despite the brightness, the sense is not of a vibrant, thriving place. It is a world in static equilibrium, frozen in some primeval state. The erosion is evident in the smoothness of the plateau, in the rounded edges of the black "mounds," but it's an erosion that has played out over eons, that has reached a point of perfect balance. Nothing changes. The sun blazes, the wind howls, the plateau hums. And nothing ever changes.
