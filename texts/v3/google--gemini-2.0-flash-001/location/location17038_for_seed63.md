Okay, let's build this place.

The air is thick, not with water, but with something heavier, almost viscous. It shimmers in the oppressive heat rising from the obsidian ground. Obsidian, but not as we know it – this is not volcanic glass so much as the petrified tears of some colossal, long-dead god, wept onto a world that could not catch them. The ground undulates subtly; not from seismic activity, but from the sheer mass of *things* moving just beneath the surface.

Elevation is key here. We stand perhaps on the inner rim of a caldera, or on the fractured spine of a world-serpent coiled for eternity. High enough that the thinner, breathable, but still scorching, air rasps in the lungs. Before us, the 'grove' – for that's the closest analogy – stretches downwards, a bowl of black mirrors catching what little light dares to pierce the perpetual gloom.

No plants. Instead, fungal-analogues dominate. They don't photosynthesize in the traditional sense. They *feed* on the heat. Great, leathery caps, black as pitch, sprout directly from the obsidian. Their stipes are not wood, but tightly-coiled nests of something chitinous and alive, a constant low hum emanating from their fibrous forms. They're packed so tightly together that they form a strange, porous architecture, a city built of living heat-sinks.

And in the shadows between these fungal sentinels: the fauna. Overwhelming and strange. Not mammals, birds, or reptiles – something else entirely. Picture insects grown to grotesque proportions, their exoskeletons shimmering with metallic colors, their multifaceted eyes glowing with internal phosphorescence. They click and scrape against the obsidian, their movements driven by instincts we can barely comprehend.

There are paths, of a sort. Carved into the obsidian over millennia by the passage of countless creatures. They are not made deliberately but they have formed. We can see the occasional 'building' – not structures in the human sense, but naturally-formed obsidian caves, hollowed out by thermal vents and claimed by the fauna. They are decorated with what looks like discarded exoskeletons arranged in patterns, glyphs perhaps, or simply the detritus of a brutal life cycle.

The sound here is low and constant: a deep, resonant hum from the fungal forests, punctuated by the clicking and scraping of chitin on obsidian. Occasionally, a piercing shriek will cut through the gloom, the hunting call of some unseen predator, followed by a brief, frantic flurry of movement before silence descends once more. It is mostly quiet, but pregnant with a terrible potential.

The scale is small, perhaps only a mile across, and easily mapped. And yet, within this tiny area, a world unfolds. A complete, self-contained ecosystem, driven by heat, sustained by shadow.

The 'erosion' is more like a slow, grinding process of adaptation. The world has settled into this form, a bizarre equilibrium of heat, darkness, and life clinging to the edge of what's survivable. Here, the fungi are the engineers, slowing shaping their environment around them. The fauna are living sculpting-tools, grinding and polishing the obsidian with their constant movements.

There’s no technology, as we understand it. But there are patterns. Complex relationships between the fungi and the fauna. A strange, symbiotic dance that feels almost… deliberate. A hidden intelligence, subtly shaping the world. The weirdness comes from this sense of alien purpose, a feeling that we are witnessing something far older, far stranger, and far more complex than it first appears.

The whole grove is constantly in motion. The diurnal cycle of the species of fauna is dictated by local changes in the temperature. The heat vents flicker and subside, causing the fungi to constantly adjust and re-grow their heat-sink structures. The obsidian itself is subtly shifting, groaning under unimaginable pressures. There are no distinct weather patterns but processes happen and change constantly.

This is a place outside our understanding, a biome with its own laws and necessities.
