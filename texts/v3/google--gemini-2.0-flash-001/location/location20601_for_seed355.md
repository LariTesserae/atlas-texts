Okay, here we go.

The air hangs thick and heavy, a soup of heat and near-constant condensation. It clings to everything – the low, dark structures, the sparse, tough vegetation clinging to life, even the rough, black paving stones underfoot. This isn't rain, not exactly. It's more like the world itself is sweating, dissolving slowly back into the primordial moisture from which it came.

The 'city', if you can call it that, is less architecture and more accretion. Structures have been built, yes, but they're sunken, twisted, integrated inextricably with the landscape. The stones are black, porous, like solidified volcanic foam, and buildings are fashioned from them organically, as if grown rather than constructed. They're low and sprawling rather than tall and imposing, with wide, arching entrances that seem to swallow the meager light. Everything is slick with moisture, and black lichen grows in every cranny. There are no windows, or if there are, they're filled with the same dark stone, carved intricately with patterns that pulse faintly in the near-darkness.

There's a profound sense of age here, but not of proud history or noble decay. It's the age of something utterly worn-down, ground into submission by the relentless press of time and water. The structures don't seem to resist the elements so much as surrender to them, becoming one with the damp, fungal earth.

Vegetation consists mainly of fleshy, dark green plants; succulents, really, specialized to thrive in this damp heat. They grow in clumps on the tops of buildings and in the cracks of the paving stones, trailing tendrils that drip with condensation. The plants look bloated and vaguely unwell. They don’t flower, or at least I don’t see any.

The open scale of the region is deceptive in the darkness. You *feel* that it extends, that there are more of these strange, sunken structures fading into the distance, but you can’t *see* it. The thick air and oppressive darkness swallow light and sound alike.

And the silence. Or rather, the *almost* silence. It's broken only by the constant drip, drip, drip of condensation, the occasional gurgle of water deep within the stonework, and the whisper of steam rising from the hot earth. You listen for life, for any sign of fauna, but hear nothing. The silence is unnerving, absolute.

The built environment, so called, is a paradox. Built, yes, but without apparent purpose. There are paths, but they lead nowhere discernible. Structures, but with no obvious entrances or exits. It's not technology that's 'none' here, I think, it's… intention? It feels like something began here, a grand project perhaps, that was then abandoned, or… forgotten.

The place is active despite all the heavy silence. Water flows everywhere and the heat shimmers visibly.

This place isn’t quite broken down. Its processes are too settled, too old. It’s not actively failing, at least not on a short timescale of days or months or years. But it's _wrong_. There is a sense of… violation. Like the laws of physics themselves are frayed at the edges. The water… it shouldn’t be clinging in quite this way. It moves uphill. It has weight that shouldn't exist. The stone – it shouldn’t weep. The darkness here is too complete, too tangible.

The darkness is broken every now and then by a sickly green glow as luminous fungi break through the stone.

There's a sense of something ancient and powerful slumbering beneath the surface. Something that's been here far longer than this 'city', something tied to the water and the stone and the incessant, dripping heat. This 'city' isn't built *on* something, it's built *around* something. Something that is quietly altering reality in its immediate vicinity. This place is wet with more than just rain. It's wet with latent energy, impossible physics, the weight of an age that has passed but hasn't quite gone.
