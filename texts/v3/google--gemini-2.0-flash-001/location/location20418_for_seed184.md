The Deepwater Fabricators squat in near-darkness. It is not absolute night here, but the gloom is profound. Bioluminescent algae bloom in patches over the metal structures, giving off a faint, sickly green, like bruises on steel. Above, perhaps a mile and more, is the roiling indifference of the ocean. Here, the water is strangely still, held in check by the weight of the city above and the even stranger weight of the past.

This is not a city built, but a city grown, accreted. It began, perhaps, as a lone processing plant, a place to refine the strange minerals dredged from the abyssal plains. Then came housing, then more plants, then the inevitable tangle of industry that grows when space is limited and purpose is singular. The scale is constricted; one never feels as if they are moving 'through' the Fabricators, but always 'within' them. Corridors are narrow, pressed tight by machinery and housing. Ceilings are low, the distance to the water above always felt as a crushing weight.

The structures themselves are bizarre agglomerations. Steel and reinforced concrete form the base, but upon this has been grafted a strange, coral-like substance, a bio-engineered material that hardens in the water and provides both insulation and a surface for… growth. Everywhere, the green algae clings, but other things grow here too. Patches of pale, rubbery fungus, intricate lattices of barnacle-like filtering organisms, and strange, tube-shaped anemones that pulse with a faint inner light. These latter are coveted, their secretions apparently having potent medicinal properties.

The industry here is ubiquitous. A low, constant thrum permeates every surface, a deep resonate hum from the vast machinery that pulls minerals from the seabed and processes them into alloys of unknown purpose. Pipes, thick as a person, snake through the corridors, carrying fluids that glow with internal heat. Automated drones, shaped like eels, patrol the waterways, maintaining the machinery and ferrying materials. The air, if one can call the water-saturated atmosphere that, smells of ozone, rust, and the sweet, cloying scent of the cultivated algae.

Yet, for all the industry, the place is strangely quiet. The thrum is constant, but beyond that, little sound. The citizens of the Fabricators move with a slow, deliberate grace, conserving energy in this light-starved environment. Voices are low, murmuring, lost in the complex architecture. It is like a deep, slow breath that never quite finishes exhaling.

Fauna is rare, but not absent. Strange, blind fish, their bodies elongated and flattened to navigate the narrow waterways, flicker through the shadows. Occasionally, a larger creature, something dredged up from the deep, will find its way into the Fabricator, causing momentary panic before being subdued or, if too large, simply left to die, its decaying carcass slowly consumed by the strange ecosystem that has taken root here.

There's a peculiar hierarchy in the Fabricators. The original engineers and their descendants occupy the higher levels, in housing grafted onto the oldest structures, a maze of private chambers and hidden workshops, close to the ocean above and perhaps closer to the secrets of the city. Below them, the laborers, who work the machinery and tend the algae farms. Further down still, are the 'Deep Divers', those who maintain the underwater machinery and bring back new life - or parts of it - from the outside. Deep Divers' bodies are covered in scars, and they all suffer from the strange mutations caused by prolonged exposure to the sea.

Change comes slowly to the Deepwater Fabricators. Generations live and die within the same metal corridors. Technology stagnates. The minerals keep coming, and the machinery keeps humming. The green algae bloom, and the strange life of the city quietly pulses on under many miles of ocean. The flow of raw materials is slow, as is the transformation and transport. The city is patient, waiting for an end known to only a few.

But even here, in this strange, submerged world, there are whispers of change. Rumors spread of new resources found on the abyssal plains, of new technologies developed in the hidden workshops. And sometimes, very rarely, a light flickers in the darkness, brighter than the algae, brighter than the bioluminescent anemones, a light that suggests something more than mere survival. A strange, deep hope.
