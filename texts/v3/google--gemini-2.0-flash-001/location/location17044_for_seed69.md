The air itself feels wet, pressing against any exposed surface like a damp cloth. Not rain, not exactly, but a constant saturation. Visibility is near zero. Not from cloud, but from the sheer density of life and moisture. This is the Sky-Jungle, and the name is almost literal.

Elevation is relative here. The 'ground' is impossibly far below – a theoretical concept, spoken of in legends. What matters is the level you occupy in the tangle of colossal, ancient organisms. These aren't trees, at least not as we understand them. They are more like fungal mats made of hyper-cellulose, their surfaces riddled with industrial machinery half-consumed by rampant moss and luminous fungi. Each 'tree' is a discrete kingdom, stretching miles in diameter, their surfaces scored with the paths of maintenance mechs long since swallowed by the green. Erosion is subtle here, a slow feast of organisms dissolving the metal and hyper-cellulose, returning it to the endless cycle.

The air hums. Not with insects, although they are present in unimaginable numbers and variety, but with the drone of ancient generators, the hiss of steam escaping cracked pipes, the grinding of gears within the living wood. It's a symphony of decay and rebirth, deafening and constant. The soundscape is layered – the immediate chorus of clicking mandibles and rustling leaves, punctuated by the deeper, rhythmic pulse of colossal, internal machinery. Distant crashes echo through the web of life, probably some branch or a derelict factory sloughing off into the abyss below.

Light is a precious commodity. Only the bioluminescent flora provides any illumination. Fungi, with caps the size of dinner plates, glow with an eerie green light, casting long, distorted shadows that dance with the constant motion of vegetation. The undergrowth pulses with smaller lights - swarms of bioluminescent insects, phosphorescent moss, and the eyes of unseen predators.

The fauna is abundant, grotesque and beautiful. Many species are adapted to the perpetual darkness, possessing highly developed senses of smell and hearing, or echolocation. Some are scavengers, feeding on the detritus of the fallen factories. Others are predators, hunting in packs through the undergrowth, their bodies adorned with bioluminescent lures. Scale is disorienting because there are enormous creatures that still would be hidden in a small patch of this territory.

Scattered throughout the Sky-Jungle are the remnants of a lost civilization. Ruined factories jut out from the fungal structures, half-eaten by the environment, their machinery still stubbornly churning. You might come across abandoned workshops covered in luminous slime, or rusted pipelines snaking through the foliage. These are not mere ruins; they are active parts of the ecosystem, inhabited by strange creatures and repurposed for unknown ends. The Sky-Jungle absorbed the architecture of industry but the architecture also shaped the ways the trees grew, adding infrastructure.

The dynamic of the Sky-Jungle is driven by the constant struggle for survival, the endless cycle of growth and decay. Predators stalk their prey, scavengers clean up the remains, and the vegetation continues to grow, slowly but surely consuming everything in its path. Weather is localized and often violent. Pockets of intense humidity form without warning, followed by torrential downpours or sudden bursts of scalding steam from ruptured geothermal vents. The air is thick with pollen, spores, and chemical compounds produced by the factories and organisms in competition. It's a breathing, seething place, constantly in motion, never at rest.

Weirdness is baked into the very fabric of this place. The fusion of organic and inorganic, the scale of life, the perpetual darkness, the strange sounds, and the constant feeling of being watched. This place operates on its own internal principles, its rules are alien and unknowable. The Sky-Jungle is a place where the boundaries between technology and nature have blurred, where the line between life and machine has vanished. No place like it exists, or could exist, anywhere else.
