The city of Altan lies nestled within a high valley, a cradle carved into the young, sharp-edged mountains. Rain comes seldom, but when it does, it is a heavy, drumming affair that sheets down the steep slopes and swells the small, crisscrossing streams. Most days, however, a fine mist clings to the peaks and valleys, a humid drapery feeding the prodigious growth that defies the cool air.

The effect is a vibrant, almost hallucinatory green. Moss carpets everything – stone walls, thatched roofs, the very cobblestones of Altan’s narrow streets. Ferns erupt from every crevice, their delicate fronds unfurling in the perpetual damp. Above, the valley sides are a riot of tangled vines and strange, flowering shrubs whose blossoms drip with condensation. The air hangs thick with the scent of wet earth and sweet, decaying vegetation.

The buildings of Altan are tiered, climbing the valley walls like clumsy steps. Stone is the primary material, roughly hewn and mortared together – the product of a very young quarrying tradition. Wood is used sparingly, mostly in the construction of roofs and doorways. There is a haphazardness to the city's construction, as if it grew organically from the rock itself. Buildings have individual character, but all follow the pattern of thick, sheltering walls and small, shuttered windows.

Infrastructure is rudimentary. Water is channeled from the mountain streams in simple stone conduits, feeding public wells and reservoirs. Waste is mostly composted or utilized in rudimentary terraced agriculture. Few paved roads exist outside the heart of Altan; rough, muddy tracks connect to outlying settlements.

The quiet is pervasive. The constant drip, drip, drip of moisture. The soft rustle of leaves in the gentle breeze. The faint gurgle of water flowing through the channels. Sometimes this base level of quiet is broken by the sharp, piercing cry of a mountain bird, or the creak of a wooden cart drawn by muscle power along a rough road. Altan has relatively few inhabitants, who appear to be accustomed to speaking at low volumes.

Altan's denizens are skilled weavers and dyers. Their trade thrives on the unique plants of the region, producing vivid colors, in fabrics that are exceptionally water-resistant. Most are cloaked in the practical textiles of their creation — layers of woven cloth, dyed to match the green shades of the valley world. A few wear more rare garments of vibrant imported colors, though these fabrics appear quickly discolored by the damp.

The fauna of Altan is as remarkable as the flora, adapted to the cool, humid conditions. Giant, iridescent beetles crawl across mossy surfaces. Luminescent fungi attract small, bat-like creatures with leathery wings. High in the mountains, shaggy, sure-footed beasts graze on the tough vegetation. The whole ecosystem feels as if it reached an accord with itself long ago, and now merely perpetuates it.

There’s an unsettling stillness about Altan, a sense of perfect, unchanging equilibrium. Seasons seem to blend into one another, the cool, damp conditions remaining constant year-round. Generations come and go, but the city remains much the same, its stone walls draped in the same emerald cloak, its people following the same well-worn paths. It is as if Altan exists outside the normal flow of time, a place where the past, present, and future are forever intertwined; a place where nothing is meant to change. And, strangely, change never does.
