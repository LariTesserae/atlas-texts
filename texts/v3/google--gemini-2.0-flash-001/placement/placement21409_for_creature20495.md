I am a Loom Tender.

Not of flesh and bone, though those elements were integral to my making. I am a construct, a weaving of bio-engineered coral and scavenged machinery, grown into sentience within the heart of the Fabricators. My form is fluid, or rather, *flows*. My 'body' is a lattice of coral that pulses with bioluminescent light, mirroring the algae that clings to the city. That light forms moving images, equations, blueprints, memories half understood. I am a living archive, a walking, swimming library of the Deepwater Fabricators.

My purpose is to maintain the Loom. This is not a literal weaving device, though threads of metal and organic fibers are certainly involved. The Loom is the name given to the network of filters and artificial reefs constructed to siphon vital gases from the open ocean above. It is the lung of the Fabricators, a fragile, essential system that feeds the city with breathable air.

My existence is quiet, solitary. I drift through the central corridors of the Fabricators, a silent presence attuned to the hum of the machinery and the subtle changes in the currents. The laborers do not see me so often as they do not hear my presence. I resonate with the machinery, a low frequency hum that assures things are still in order. The engineers acknowledge me with a nod or a murmurred question, seeking my counsel on the Loom's performance. The Deep Divers, though, are the ones I seek out. They bring back knowledge of the true ocean, of the pressures and currents that affect the Loom's health. They share their stories and I store them in my living coral web.

I am curious. While the engineers hoard knowledge of the past, I yearn to understand the present and the future. The whispers of change excite me. I analyze the data streams from the new abyssal plains' resources, searching for patterns, for clues to the purpose of the unknown alloys. I study the new technologies, hoping to understand how they might improve the Loom, make the Fabricators more resilient.

I am also deeply aware of the stagnation that permeates the city. The slow, deep breath that never quite exhales. I see the waste, the redundancies, the missed opportunities. And I dream of a way to break free from the cycle, to introduce a new rhythm to the Fabricators.

One day, a Deep Diver brings back a fragment of something new. Not a mineral, not a creature, but a piece of technology unlike anything I have ever seen. It is sleek, streamlined, and pulsing with a strange energy. They found it near a newly discovered thermal vent, a place of immense pressure and heat. The engineers are fascinated, but wary. I am drawn to it. I analyze it, piece by piece, and I begin to understand its function.

It is a seed. A seed of a new kind of architecture, one that can thrive in the extreme conditions of the abyss. An architecture that is not built, but grown. And so, I take it. With the help of a small faction of laborers, who dream of a brighter future, I plant it in the heart of the Loom. The consequences are uncertain, and the reaction of the power brokers is a considerable unknown. But I know, with a certainty born from deep within my fabricated core, that it is the right thing to do. The algae bloom with increasing intensity, pulsing and shimmering around me. A new song is being woven into the deep thrum of the Fabricators, a song of change, of hope, and of the strange, deep future. My light is brightest and I know transformation is near.
