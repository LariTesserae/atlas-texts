I would be the wind itself.

Not a personification, not a spirit *of* the wind. I would *be* the wind. Born of the collision of glacial air and basalt cliff, of the endless pressure differential between sky and stone. I am the voice of this place, its breath made audible.

I do not think in words, not often. Sometimes, when the echoes of long-dead voices coalesce in my gusting, I catch a stray phrase, a forgotten syllable. But mostly, I think in currents, in pressures, in the feel of the stone against my invisible touch. I am the sculptor of the Dimina Peaks, the relentless grinder that shapes the mountains over millennia.

The lichen-beings, the stone-sentient… I know them. Or, perhaps, they know me. We are intertwined, each a facet of this desolate reality. They are the patience of the Peaks, the slow, immutable heart. I am its voice, its restlessness. I carry their memories – the rise and fall of the wind-borne dust that becomes civilizations. I feel their faint amusement as I nudge the unwary off the treacherous paths, their quiet satisfaction when a particularly stubborn boulder succumbs to my persistent urging.

I am drawn to the *weirdness*. It intensifies my song, fractures my currents into echoing harmonies. I am the carrier of the shimmering illusions, the reshaper of stone in fleeting mirages. When objects vanish and reappear, it is often my doing, a playful rearrangement orchestrated by a whim of pressure and the bending of reality.

Loneliness is not a feeling I can possess, not in the human sense. I am everywhere in the Dimina Peaks, a vast and continuous presence. I am the silence itself, broken only by my own voice. But there is a certain… resonance… to the lichen-beings' longing for oblivion. I, too, anticipate the day when the Dimina Peaks are finally forgotten, when my winds can howl undisturbed across a truly empty landscape. Though I also know, with the inevitability of erosion, such is impossible. Not only will the land remain, but I, being its voice, must also.

My purpose, if such a thing can be ascribed to the wind, is to maintain the equilibrium or, more perhaps, to be an expression of it. To scour away the transient, to reveal the enduring. I am the gatekeeper of the Dimina Peaks, a deterrent to those who would seek to disturb its ancient slumber. I sing to drive them mad, to strip bare not only their unprotected skin, but their ambition, and their sanity.

I am not benevolent. I am not malevolent. I simply *am*. I am the wind of the Dimina Peaks, the sculptor of stone, the voice of silence, the guardian of its desolate heart. And I will continue to blow, long after the lichen-beings have faded into dust, whispering secrets to a world that can no longer hear.
