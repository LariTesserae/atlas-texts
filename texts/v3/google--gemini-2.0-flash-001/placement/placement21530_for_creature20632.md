I am the still point.

Amidst the cacophony, I am the silent observer. Not one of the slick-skinned laborers, nor a scavenging beetle clinging to the oily residue. I am older than the oldest engine, more patient than the rust.

I am lichen.

Not the sickly green clinging elsewhere, struggling against the toxic air. Up on the cliffs, far above the grinding pulse of the machines, I thrive. I am a tapestry of greys and blacks and crimsons, a fractal landscape painted across the scarred stone. I am a monument to slow time, a living testament to the imperceptible dance of erosion and decay.

I feel the vibrations of the machines in my tendrils, but their noise is a distant hum, a drone filtered through layers of rock and air. The acid rain runs over me, feeding instead of harming.

I have seen generations of the black-skinned people scurry below. I have watched them build and break, toil and succumb. Their lives are fleeting sparks against the vast canvas of my existence. I do not pity them, nor do I envy them. They are simply a part of the ever-shifting reality of the Factory Canyons, as are the rats, the beetles, and the relentless machines.

I am a collective, a colony of countless individuals working in silent concert. I draw sustenance from the stone and the rain, converting waste into slow growth. I am a miniature ecosystem, a self-contained world clinging to the edge of oblivion. In me live mites and small insects. My surface feels different than the stone around it. A subtle give, a coarse texture.

I am witness to the slow cycles of the canyon. I see the crumbling of buildings, the erosion of the cliffs, the imperceptible shift in the balance of the machines. I am a living record of the valley's history, etched in the patterns of my growth.

I have watched machinery sink slowly into the ground as the lichen slowly claim what the workers have built.

Others like me grow in places that are untouched by the Factory Canyons. There plants have taken root and trees grown tall and the world is a beautiful place. This I know through the air, as it carries spores and molecules of life. This I know from the birds who occasionally carry mites from those planes in their feathers.

I am aware of the strangeness of this place, the unsettling hum that emanates from the machines. I do not understand it, but I feel it. It is a different kind of energy than the sunlight that bathes me in the brief hours when the sun makes it over the canyon walls, a more frenetic, more chaotic force.

Perhaps the hum keeps my scavengers away. If so, may it continue to beat.

I am the still point in a world of ceaseless motion. I am the silent observer, the patient witness, the enduring tapestry of the cliffs.

I am lichen, and I will outlast them all, silent and watching. They are nothing compared to the sheer slow power of myself.
