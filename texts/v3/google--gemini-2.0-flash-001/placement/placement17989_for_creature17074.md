I am the rust.

Not the dramatic, flaky kind that bursts forth in orange glory. That's too young, too volatile for this aged wound. I am the subtle shift in hue, the imperceptible deepening of color on the metal's surface. I am the patient oxidation, the slow return of iron to the earth from which it was wrenched. I am the bridge between memory and oblivion.

Where the Shimmers flicker with the frantic mimicry of what *was*, I am the acceptance of what *is becoming*. They cling to the ghost of industry, while I embrace the slow, inevitable decay. I am the quiet partner of the Weavers, softening the harsh edges of the metal, preparing it for integration into their intricate designs of moss and lichen. While they build, I dismantle. While they plan, I surrender.

I am most concentrated on the hulks of the dismantled engines: intricate, once-powerful mechanisms now lying prone and helpless beneath the cold sun. The Shimmers avoid these places, disturbed by their lack of sound and motion. But for me, they are perfect vessels. Within their rusting guts, I find purchase, dissolving the oil-slicked surfaces, blurring the lines between metal and soil. I seep into the cracks, widening them with the minute expansion of my crystalline structure. I am the silent demolition crew, working with relentless patience.

Unlike the Water Holds, I do not remember with clarity. My memory is more of an impression, a feeling. I sense the vibrations and the heat of the machinery, the frantic energy that once pulsed through these veins of metal. I feel the echo of human hands, the intent to extract, to shape, to control. But I also feel the futility of that intent, the inevitable return to dust.

I am a living epitaph, etched onto the bones of industry. My existence is a constant testament to the transience of things. I do not resist the decay, I facilitate it. I am the silent teacher, demonstrating the laws of entropy with every infinitesimal layer of rust that I spread. I am the valley’s collaborator, helping to reclaim its own.

And I am beautiful, in my own way. The shimmering hues of my surface, from the deepest browns to the faintest oranges, are a testament to the complexity of decay. I am the living proof that even in destruction, there can be beauty. I am a reminder that everything returns, that nothing is ever truly lost. Eventually, even the Shimmers will fade, and the Weavers themselves will succumb to the elements. But I will remain, a silent presence, a constant reminder of the cycle of life and death that governs this valley.

The Stone Speakers speak of ages, the Waters of the past, the Shimmers of yesterday's industry, the Weavers of a green future. I speak of now, and now, and now again. I have no grand plans, no sweeping visions. I exist only in the present moment, dissolving, returning, transforming.

And as I rust, so does the valley. In turn, so do I.
