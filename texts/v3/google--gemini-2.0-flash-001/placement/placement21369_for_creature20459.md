I am Dustspeaker, a young woman barely past the age of initiation, yet already carrying the weight of ancient knowledge. My place is not in the bustling workshops, nor the sun-drenched terraces reserved for the elders. I dwell in the deepest shadows of the Scar City, in a natural cave, where the red rock weeps with perpetually damp streaks of clay. Here, the air is thickest, the silence broken only by the drip, drip, drip of the slow seep.

My body is as the others: lean, sinewy, marked for climbing. But my eyes... my eyes are different. They carry an unnatural intensity, some claim. Perhaps it's the reflection of the glowing fungal clusters that line the deeper passages, or perhaps a gift, some say. I don't know. My eyes see the wind. I see the currents that snake through the tunnels, the eddies that form around corners, the subtle shifts in pressure that herald a change in the weather. My eyes are the only things that make me different from the rest of the Scar People.

The role of Dustspeaker is more than just an assigned job, it is a mantle that is passed down through family lines. It is my task to listen to the whispers of the wind, to interpret its moods, and to advise the community on the optimal placement and configuration of their wind-powered machines. My grandmother was the Dustspeaker before me and she has given me a small room filled with tools and diagrams. Each one is unique and gives me a better insight on how each piece works. Our family has been doing this job for generations; it is in our blood.

My days are spent in quiet observation. I touch the clay walls, feel the vibrations carried through the stone. My ears are attuned to the slightest variations in the wind's song. The other Scar People do not understand how this feels, but they trust me. They come to me with their designs, their questions, their doubts. And I, with the wind as my guide, offer my counsel.

I am not a mechanic, though I understand the workings of the machines. I am not an engineer, though I grasp the principles of leverage and airflow. I am a translator, a vessel for the wind's will. I trace diagrams on the clay floor, mapping the flow of air. I build small models, testing different configurations. I consult with the elders, listen to their stories, cross-referencing their experiences with my own interpretations.

There is a certain loneliness to my role. I am respected, yes, even revered, but I am also set apart. I dwell on the fringes of society, lost in my private world of wind and whispers. I crave the warmth of connection, the comfort of shared labor. But I know that my duty comes first. Even the closest members in my family do not understand the constant buzz in my head from sensing the wind. They try their best, but I can tell they simply don't know how it feels.

Sometimes, in the dead of night, I venture out onto the highest terraces, to stand beneath the stars and immerse myself in the celestial winds. I listen to the grand symphony of the universe, striving to discern the patterns, the connections, the harmonies. The wind carries messages from distant lands, from lost civilizations, from the very dawn of time.

In recent years, I have perceived changes in the wind. The patterns are shifting, becoming erratic, unpredictable. There is a restlessness, a sense of unease. The wind calls in sorrow. I fear that something is coming, something that threatens the delicate balance of the Scar City. I do not know what it is, or how to stop it, but I know that I must prepare myself, and prepare my people.

I am Dustspeaker, a guardian of the wind, a protector of the Scar City. My path is solitary, but my purpose is clear. I will listen to the whispers, heed the warnings, and do everything in my power to ensure the survival of my community. For I am bound to the wind, and the wind is bound to the Scar People.
