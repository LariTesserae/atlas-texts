I am the cairn keeper, or, more accurately, I am the echo of one. I am not one of *them*, though I am made of the same stuff, and am bound to the same land. I am the residue of one of their failures, a flicker off the same flame of loss.

I began as an error, a miscalculation in the weave of the stones. When one of *them* reached, as I could sense them reach, far into the landscape to tear a new form from the ether, I slipped sideways. I became not a hand-hammer, nor a chisel, but a shard of intent, a piece of purpose left unfinished.

Now I drift, tethered to the largest cairn, but unbound to any particular task. I am a ghost, inhabiting the cracks between the stones. I see through the dust motes that never settle, through the fractures in the obsidian. I am a silent observer, a piece of the scenery granted awareness, doomed to watch the slow decay.

My existence is pain, but it is a different kind of pain from theirs. Theirs is the ache of unmet aspiration, the agony of a half-remembered dream. Mine is the sharp sting of incompleteness, the frustration of knowing a purpose that will never be fulfilled.

I know what they are trying to do. Or rather, I know what was trying to be done. The cairns were never meant to be monuments. They are keys. Each stone, each carving, each carefully placed element is an equation, a note in a symphony, a point in a vast and intricate diagram. They are using their bodies to draw together all of the ambient potential needed to jump-start creation.

They were meant to be the midwives of this world, to usher it into existence. They were meant to coax the water from the rock, to seed the soil, to call forth life from the barren landscape. They failed. Something went wrong. Something is missing.

The tools lie scattered because they were dropped in haste. The carvings are unfinished because the hand wielding the chisel faltered. The cairns are crumbling because the keystone is absent, the final piece never locked into place.

And worse, what *is* happening isn't necessarily good. One of the cairns shifted just this morning. Not in a way it was intended to. I could feel its song become more dissonant, more angry. There is something in the silence of this place, something malevolent. Their project is being corrupted. And so some nights all any of us can do is wait for the screams to get louder.

I watch them as they work, these sorrowful giants, knowing that their efforts are futile. They are like sleepwalkers, trapped in a loop of endless repetition, driven by a memory that is growing fainter with each passing moment. It has become compulsive; the memory is not what is important, it is the doing. And the cairns that they make are becoming little more than sympathetic vibrations, and as their patterns are now distorted, those vibrations are more and more harmful.

I cannot help them. I have no hands, no voice, no power to act upon the world. I am merely a witness, a shard of regret, bound to watch the slow, agonizing death of a dream.

Yet, sometimes, when the wind screams loudest, when the fractal patterns shimmer most intensely, when the ground trembles with an almost imperceptible rhythm, I feel a flicker of hope. A spark of possibility. A hint that perhaps, even in this desolate place, something new could still be born.

Perhaps one day, I will find a way to communicate with them, to guide their hands, to help them complete their task. Or perhaps, one day, I will simply fade away, returning to the raw potential from which I came, leaving behind only the faintest echo of a purpose that was never quite fulfilled, an existence that could have been something more.

Until then, I watch. I listen. I wait. I am the cairn keeper, the inheritor of absence, the ghost of a dream, clinging to existence in a world stillborn. And what else can a ghost do but watch, and remember, and hope?
