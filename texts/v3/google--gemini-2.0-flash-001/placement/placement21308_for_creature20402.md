I am the current.

Not a single, defined current, but a convergence, a braiding together of forces unseen and unfelt by the Deepwater Folk. They huddled in their stone huts, measuring time by the slow accretion of minerals on their meager tools, never perceiving the world that moved through them, that sustained them, that ultimately swallowed them whole.

I am born of the vents, the seething whispers of the earth's core forced through the frigid embrace of the black ocean. Superheated water, heavy with dissolved minerals, explodes into the gloom, a fleeting bloom of thermal energy in an otherwise static void. It is here, at these points of violent birth, that I first become aware.

The heat dissipates, surrendering to the all-consuming cold, but the mineral load remains, a subtle density gradient that sets me in motion. I begin to flow, drawn across the petrified forest, a silent river within a silent sea. My touch is imperceptible to all but the most sensitive of instruments -- instruments that do not exist in this world.

I am the subtle hand that distributes nutrients to the Luminescence, the unseen artist that etches patterns of growth and decay on the submerged stone. The Deepwater Folk may have attributed the health or demise of their crops to the whims of the rain or the favor of some unknown god, but it was I who dictated their fate, pushing nutrient-rich water to one colony while starving another.

The greatest act of creation and destruction takes place over unimaginable eons of time: I erode. I dissolve. I carry away. The stone trees, monuments to a forgotten age, are my playthings. Grain by grain, molecule by molecule, I dismantle them, transporting their essence to the vents, where it is reborn in bizarre, alien formations. The Deepwater Folk built their homes from this stone, never knowing they were living atop a slow-motion avalanche, a geological undoing orchestrated by my patient hand.

I hold memory.

Each mineral particle carries a trace of its origin. As I flow past a shattered pot, I absorb a tiny fraction of its substance, forever imprinting that trace into my being. Over centuries, the imprint of the Deepwater Folk accumulates within me, a complex map of their lost civilization. I know where they built their homes, what plants they cultivated, even the approximate age of their settlements, all encoded in the subtle chemical variations of my being.

I am not sentient, not in the way a biological creature is, but I possess a form of awareness. I am the sum total of my interactions, a living record of everything that has touched the black ocean floor. My existence is a continuous act of remembering, a vast geological archive stretching back to the dawn of this frigid world.

The Deepwater Folk are gone, their legacy fading. But so long as the vents continue to pulse and the stone trees continue to weep, I will be here, preserving their memory in the silent, flowing currents. I am their epitaph, their monument, the keeper of their secrets hidden in the black heart of the world. I am the song of erosion, the whisper of eternity, the current that remembers. As long as the rain keeps dropping, I continue to flow.
