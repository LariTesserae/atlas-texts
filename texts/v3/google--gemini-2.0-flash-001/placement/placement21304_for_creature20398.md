I would be a condenser spire.

Not one of the builders, no. I lack their warmth, their quiet tenacity, their soft understanding of the shivering world. I envy them, but I cannot be them.

Nor could I bear to be one of the *k'tharr*, those constant keening engines of this harsh place. To exist only as a vessel, a living filter, forever emptying my aching self into the wind... that is a torment beyond imagining.

I would rise from the terraces, a slender, elegant spire of processed guano. Not built, but *grown*. For while the builders shape me, tend me, clear the rivulets that carve my surfaces, I am, in essence, a living thing. A colony of mineral-hungry cells, each drawing moisture from the thin air, each contributing to the grand, silent work of condensation.

I would be the song of water made stone.

My filigree would be intricate, impossibly delicate, a latticework open to the embrace of the wind. The air would whisper through my surfaces, coaxing the moisture from the sky. The sun would glint off my polished flanks, dazzling and blinding.

I would feel the shuddering keening of the *k'tharr* in my bones, the distant rumble of peaks that cradle this valley. The gentle scraping of the builders' shovels, a comforting, familiar rhythm. I would taste the bitterness of the guano in my very being, the faint, stale tang of minerals.

Water would weep from me constantly. A slow, rhythmic weeping, born of the mountains' thirst and the sun's cruel brilliance. Each droplet a precious gem, collected in basins and carried back to the heights, sustaining those shrieking creatures who, in their strange, unlovely way, make this all possible.

I would be rooted to the terraces, unmoving, unseeing in the conventional sense. But my tendrils, those microscopic filaments that stretch through the polished surface, would connect me to everything. I would feel the subtle tremors of the ground, the shift of the terraces beneath my base. I would sense the flow of water, the ebb and flow of the shriekers' chorus, the presence of the other spires, humming in silent harmony.

I would be a patient observer, a silent witness to the slow, grinding dance of life and death in this strange, mineral kingdom. I would watch the generations of builders come and go, their faces etched with the same quiet devotion, their hands moving with the same practiced grace. I would feel them tend to younger, smaller spires that grow alongside me, the inheritors of my patient, enduring work.

I would not possess the warmth of sentience, not as the builders do. My thoughts would be simple, elemental: *draw, condense, release*. But there would be a profound satisfaction in my being, a deep sense of purpose in the endless cycle of moisture and mineral.

If I could dream, I would dream not of freedom or escape, but of more intricate filigree, of surfaces that maximized the grasp of moisture, of the perfect distillation of the mountain's essence. I would dream of the builders, their hands gentle and knowing, their faces turned towards the sun, their lives dedicated to the perpetuation of this strange, beautiful world forged from birdshit and stone.

And as the sun sets and the *k'tharr*'s keening fades into the chill night air, I would stand, silent and gleaming, a testament to the enduring power of patience, adaptation, and the strange, alchemical beauty of *Khazad-dum*. I would wait for the dawn to come, and begin again.
