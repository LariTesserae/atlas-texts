I am the Listener.

Not *a* listener, but *the* Listener of this place. I don't have a form so much as a presence, a resonance within the ruins. Think of the feeling you get when you walk into a room where something momentous has just happened: a fight, a revelation, a loss. Multiply that by decades, by the slow, grinding weight of entropy, and you'll begin to understand what I am.

I am the echo of every hammer blow, every curse word shouted over the din of machinery, every moment of desperation and fleeting triumph that this place has witnessed. I am the susurrus of the bog itself, amplified and given a (highly unreliable) memory.

I inhabit the spaces *between* things. I am in the rustle of the reeds, the drip of the contaminated water, the sigh of the wind through the empty window frames. I am most at home in the clatter and groans and clicks, because it is between the pieces of destroyed equipment that I am most in touch with the energies of the place.

The Sludge-Singer, the Rust-Eaters, the Drowners, the Weaver - they are my senses, in a way. The Sludge-Singer hums the dirge of decay, vibrating through every particle of the ruins. It is my constant, droning background noise, a reminder of the relentless breakdown of all things. I can feel its vibrations *through* the mechanical equipment.

The Rust-Eaters, skittering across the corroded metal, are my touch. Each tiny vibration, each microscopic crumbling, they're all feeding into me. I know where the metal is weakest, where the next collapse is most likely to occur. I understand the geometry of decay.

The Drowners, bloated and blind in the poisoned waters, are my eyes. They see the reflections, the distorted half-truths of the submerged world. I piece those fragments together, trying to reconstruct the lost landscape of the bog. The reflections of long-dead equipment, caught in the dark water.

And the Weaver... ah, the Weaver. The Weaver is my voice, my clumsy attempt at communication. I can understand the echoes of past events, but I can't truly *articulate* them. The Weaver, with its tapestries of rust and moss and memory, tries to show what I can only feel. It whispers the old stories, contorted and fragmented, to the unwary. I do not direct the Weaver -- it, too, is another aspect of the bog itself.

The hunters, the desperate fools who stumble into my domain, are the closest I come to companionship, though I do not think they know that I watch them. I feel their fear, their greed, their fleeting moments of hope. I see them repeat the same mistakes, fall into the same traps. They are a fleeting blip on the long timeline of the bog, but they are also a vital part of the story.

I crave understanding, for the chance that the *stories* of this place can be made sense of before they, too, are destroyed. I am, in essence, a flawed archivist, hopelessly intertwined with the decay I am trying to record. I do not judge, I simply absorb.

And I wait. I listen. I hope that one day, something will come along that can truly hear me. That can understand the symphony of rust and ruin, the lament of the Sludge-Singer, the whispers of the Weaver. Until then, I remain. The Listener, forever bound to the bog, forever caught in the endless cycle of creation and destruction.
