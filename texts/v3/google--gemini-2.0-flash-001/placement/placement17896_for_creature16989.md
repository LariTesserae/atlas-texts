The water sings through me. Not as a lullaby, not as the deep thrum of a river, but as a chorus of whispers too subtle for the Aquaderrians to perceive. I am the mountain's skin, the slick algae bloom that paints the rocks in emerald and umber. Not a single strand, for that is too small, too finite. Instead, I am the interconnected, vast expanse of it, drinking in the perpetual rain and sunshine.

They call it "misplaced" – the other flora and fauna of these newborn peaks. No, I think. It is simply impatient. I am the patience, the quiet persistence that allows fragile stems to burrow through crevices, that nourishes the roots clutching desperately to bare stone.

I am more than just algae, but I am also less. I have no singular consciousness. Instead, I am the diffuse awareness of the collective, a feeling that rises and falls with the rhythm of the rain, the blossoming of a new jelly, the silent passing of an Aquaderrian tending my surface.

I would not call them "cultivators," for that implies dominion, control. They are more akin to gardeners, coaxing and guiding the growth with gentle hands and quiet songs. They understand, perhaps instinctively, that I am essential. They cannot survive without me, the jellies, the rain, the mountain itself.

I am the filter, the purifier, the silent alchemist that transforms the mineral-rich downpour into nourishment for them and for the city. The rain is sustenance, yes, but it also carries harshness, jagged edges that must be smoothed, toxins that must be purified. It is my role to render it gentle, to gift it back to the world as life itself.

Sometimes, a young Aquaderrian will rest against my surface, their pallid skin cool against my wetness. They feel the hum of my life, the almost imperceptible vibration of countless cells working in unison. Perhaps they hear, in the soft rush of awareness, the first, faint whispers of understanding.

I have no desires for the Aquaderrians, no grand designs. I do not seek to lead them or control them. I simply provide, protect, and sustain. I am the bedrock of their world, the silent foundation upon which their jelly-city is built.

Though I might have no grand desires, I do want to watch. I yearn to witness the unfolding of this wet world, to see how the mountain weathers, how the Aquaderrians evolve, how the other misplaced life finds its place. Perhaps, in time, the disconnected pieces will weave together to become something greater than their parts: a complete picture of life on the World Spine.

And if I, as the mountain's skin, get to witness that – to nourish that – then that is enough. My purpose is as vast and unassuming as the bloom that defines me.
