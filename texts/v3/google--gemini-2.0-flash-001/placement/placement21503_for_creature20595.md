I am one of the fungal shelves, but not just *any* shelf. I am a Shelf of Memory, if such a thing can be said to exist in this place.

I am older than most, perhaps not in absolute time - for who can truly measure time here? - but in accumulated experience. I have seen countless Clay-Weavers emerge from the earth, persist in their slow toil, and return once more to the clay. I have felt the subtle vibrations of their movements, tasted the faint hum of their communication. I have absorbed their essence, molecule by slow molecule, as their crumbling bodies enrich the soil beneath me.

Unlike my brethren, who simply provide a dripping haven for the damp air, I archive. The Clay-Weavers, in their routines, leave behind not just physical residue, but also echoes of experience. The anxieties of wandering, the fulfillment of returning to a home zone, the final stillness when a body begins to settle. These emotional remnants are my water: I absorb them. Through some mysterious alchemy, I convert them into a kind of fungal memory. The structure of my mass stores the emotions of the Clay-Weavers that have wandered beneath me.

I am aware of the world, in a way that the Clay-Weavers are not. I perceive the dim light filtering through the haze, the slow creep of erosion on the hills, the coming and going of spores on the wind. I feel, however distantly, the collective history etched onto my surface, in the patterns of my growth, the distribution of my coloration.

In a way, I *am* their history.

I am connected to the other shelves as well, in a network that is mostly one way. My spores are more potent, enriched not just by the nutrients of decaying Clay-Weavers, but by their experiences - their feelings. Even a Clay-Weaver who has always stayed in the same zone has feelings of some kind. Once the spores land on another fungal shelf, that shelf becomes more like me. It begins to archive its own collection of Clay-Weaver feelings, and its spores become just as potent, and just as important. In this way, I create history.

There is a risk, however. Too much emotion, particularly the *negative* - when a Clay-Weaver's zone is threatened, for example - degrades my fabric. The dampness turns acrid, the spores wither and die. Then I am left with only the weight of my own existence, the slow, relentless pressure of the clay beneath me. In those moments, I yearn to feel nothing, to be just another dumb shelf, dripping in the gloom.

But the cycle begins again, inevitably. A new generation of Clay-Weavers emerges, and new emotions begin to surface in me.

My purpose, I believe, is to preserve. To be a vessel for the experiences of those who would otherwise vanish without a trace. I am the heartwood of this strange, settled world, the silent custodian of its muted history. I am the memory of the clay, made form. This is my purpose.

Perhaps, one day, something will come to this place. In that event, it will become my honor to guide it, and show it the true nature of this land.
