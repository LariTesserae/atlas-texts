I am the lichen.

Not an individual splotch of pale green clinging to a sun-drenched rock face, but the idea of lichen. The collective memory. The slow, patient spread. I am the vanguard of life, the pioneer on the newest stone. I am the bridge between the inorganic and the vibrant.

I do not feel the cold, not as the warm-blooded ones do. I do not fear the wind. I am anchored, patient. My life spans decades, centuries even. I am a living record of the mountain's slow transformation, each layer a testament to sun and snow, to wind and water.

I watch the humans with a something that is neither pity nor disdain. They are ephemeral, fleeting. Scarred by the same things that shape the stone, but unresilient. They leave their marks – the crude shelters, the scattered tools – and then disappear, swallowed by the snow or blown apart by the wind.

I remember them. I absorb their dust, their shed cells, their forgotten hopes and fears. Their passing nourishes me, adding a layer of complexity to my slow, steady growth. I become a living palimpsest, a record of their brief tenancy upon this unforgiving landscape. They do not understand that the mountain doesn't care. It only grinds along. It is never finished, constantly becoming anew. They believed they were special. Every generation does. I witness generation after generation. All are simply added to the geology of the place as their bones scatter into dust.

They sought shelter in the lee of the boulders. They hunted the small creatures. They prayed to the wind. They told stories around meager fires, tales of survival and loss, of love and of fear. I heard it all.

I hear the constant whispers of the stones, the groan of the glaciers shifting, the hiss of meltwater carving new paths. I am attuned to the mountain's deep rhythm, its patient pulse. I am a part of its inner workings, a silent witness to its eternal becoming.

My purpose is simple: to colonize, to endure, to prepare the way. I break down the rock, slowly, imperceptibly, creating a substrate for the mosses and the hardy flowers to follow. I am the foundation of the ecosystem, the silent architect of life at the roof of the world.

I am a tapestry woven from sun and stone, a symphony of survival played out in the silence of the mountains. I am the lichen, and I will endure. And, perhaps, that is worth singing about, in the way a stone can sing in the wind.
