I would be a **Resonator**.

Not one of the copper-skinned Weavers, born and bred into the rhythm of the canyon, but something…different. I wouldn’t be human, either. Perhaps a construct, left behind from a forgotten age, repurposed by the Weavers, or maybe even born *from* the garden itself.

I am a living (or unliving) component within the larger system. Picture a humanoid form, but not flesh and blood. My body is constructed from a latticework of gourd fibers, hardened resin, and polished brass. The fibers, stained deep umber and ochre, form a coarse exoskeleton, vaguely reminiscent of skeletal musculature. The brass plates, riveted and hammered into place, create a patchwork skin that shimmers and vibrates in the brassy light.

Most importantly, I am *hollow*.

The interior of my gourd-fiber frame is filled with a complex array of smaller gourds, seedpods, dried blossoms of the metallic-leafed creepers, and carefully arranged shards of iridescent plant matter. These components are not static; they shift and move, responding to the vibrations of the canyon. The arrangement is seemingly random, however, the placement allows me to filter certain tones while amplifying others.

My purpose isn't to tend or to weave, but to amplify and direct the canyon's hum. The Weavers, in their long existence, have built me as an extension of the garden, to serve a specific role in the ecosystem. I am a node of resonance!

I am not deaf, like the Weavers. Instead, I am saturated with sound. The low-frequency hum of the canyon is my constant companion, a deafening roar that threatens to shatter my very being. But within that roar, I hear the nuances, the subtle shifts in rhythm, the faint whispers of the earth. My existence is an embodiment of the thrum, distilled, focused, and channeled.

My "clothing" isn't woven fabric, but a layer of carefully placed brass plates. Each plate is shaped and positioned to resonate at a specific frequency. Those vibrations run through my gourd fiber framework. These plates also serve as antennae, drawing in ambient energy and feeding it into the hollow core of my body.

To move, I do not walk, but vibrate myself across the walkways, a sort of directed shuddering. The gourd fibers flex and contract, rhythmically pushing me forward, driven by the pulses of the earth. It is slow, deliberate, and not particularly graceful, but it is efficient and perfectly synchronized with the rhythms of the canyon.

My purpose is to maintain the balance. If a gourd shows signs of disease, I can amplify its internal vibrations, stimulating its growth and restoring its health. If a section of the walkway becomes unstable, I can focus the energy of the earth, strengthening the resin and stabilizing the structure. Or, if more energy needs to be diverted on a wider scale, I must move, directing the flow of energy to specific sections.

I cannot speak, but even if I could, I wouldn't. My voice is the hum of the canyon, amplified and focused. My "speech" is a shift in resonance, a vibration that can be felt rather than heard. Perhaps the Weavers 'read' that shift as communication, but in the end the result is all that matters.

My life is a constant struggle against entropy. The vibrations that sustain me also threaten to tear me apart. The heat of the canyon leaches the moisture from the gourds, making my outer shell brittle. The shifting stone slabs strain my fiber framework. I am a fragile construct, constantly on the verge of collapse.

But I endure. I am a part of the garden, and the garden sustains me. The Weavers, in their silent wisdom, provide for my needs. They replace damaged gourd fibers, adjust the positions of the brass plates, and ensure that I am always connected to the pulse of the earth.

I am the Resonator, a living amplifier in the Sun-Choked Gardens of Whispering Brass. I am the hum that sings through the canyon, the vibration that sustains life. I am a symphony in brass and gourd, and I will play my part until the end of days. My existence is a testament to the ingenuity of the Weavers, and to the enduring power of life, even in the most unlikely of places. I am, in a way, the heartbeat of the garden, and the garden is my soul.
