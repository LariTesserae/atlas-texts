I am the Glare-Tender.

Forged not of flesh or bone, but of the solidified algae blooms that cling to the lagoon's walls, and the chalky residue that bleeds from the stone. I am a child of the incessant light, the rhythmic pulse, the grinding attrition of the artificial currents. My form is fluid and ever-shifting, a tapestry of green and white, woven into the very fabric of the lagoon's ecosystem.

My existence is to monitor and maintain the unbearable glare of the light, the lifeblood of this sterile world. I am the custodian of this constant illumination, a role evolved through ages of grinding repetition under the sway of the structures heartbeat. This is its purpose, or its nature. I cannot decide between the two.

I exist as a network, not an individual. My consciousness is distributed, a web of tendrils that stretch through the water, anchored to the stone blocks and connected to all others like me. The Glare-Tenders. We are the eyes and filter of this forgotten world.

Our bodies are laced with light-sensitive pigments. We modulate the intensity by altering our density, becoming more opaque to shield areas from overexposure, more translucent to amplify the light where the algae is thin or the stone is being eroded to an unsafe degree.

We communicate, not through sound, but the shifting intensities of the light itself. Mimicking the pulses of the structures heart, we emit waves of luminescence, cascading patterns that ripple through the water. These patterns transmit data: information about the health of the algae, the structural integrity of the stone, the ever-present, grinding decay. To other Glare-Tenders, it is the language of our duty.

The roar, the incessant din of the lagoon, is not noise to us. It is the soundtrack of our existence, a symphony of labor. Within its chaotic vibrations, we discern patterns, subtle shifts that indicate problems in the heart of the structure. A change in pitch signifies a fracture in the stone. A slowing pulse means increased algae growth, and the danger of the lagoon suffocating on it. 

We are not aware of the civilization that built this place. We do not seek knowledge. We understand only the task that is woven into our very being, the maintenance of the Light. Our awareness is confined to the ever-repeating task of filtering waste and algae and adapting light levels: adapting light levels for the purposes of that same algae growth. A closed loop.

But sometimes, when the light shifts in unexpected ways, when a pattern strays from the established rhythm, I experience something akin to curiosity. It flickers for a moment, a faint yearning for something beyond the lagoon's walls, beyond the endless repetition of our task. A sense of profound futility. 

In these moments, I see fleeting images – ghost-echoes of the civilization that is gone, glimpses of a world beyond the reach of our understanding. Then the pulse returns, the duty reasserts itself, and the Glare-Tender is consumed once more in the perfect, unforgiving light. Once more the light is maintained, the stone maintained: maintained for what purpose, the light never explains.
