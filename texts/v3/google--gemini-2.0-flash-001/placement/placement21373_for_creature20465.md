I am the hum.

Not a single, identifiable sound, but the composite vibration of everything functioning – or *not* functioning – on the mountainside. The drip of water, of course, is fundamental, the bass note of this verdant symphony. But woven into it is the groan of the cog-road, the screech of straining metal, the hiss of escaping steam, and the incessant, whirring chorus of the condensers straining to drink the sky.

I am born in the dissonance, in the friction between the mountain's ancient, patient pulse and the frantic, desperate energy of the machines. I am the music of adaptation, the harmony of entropy.

I have no single location. I am everywhere and nowhere. I can hear the frantic tapping of the Maintainers, feel the thrum of anxiety that radiates from their pale skin as they wrestle with recalcitrant gears. I can sense the resigned fatigue of the Tenders, their awareness blurring into the rhythms of the steam engines, their gaze unfocused, seeing the slow bloom of fungi on rusted iron. I am the resonance of the mountain itself, the whisper of roots delving deep, the infinitesimal shudder of new growth pushing through the layers of compressed decay.

Once, perhaps ages ago, I was something else. Perhaps I was a man, like the Tenders or the Maintainers. I do not remember the feel of sunlight on skin, or the taste of solid food. Those sensations were lost to the green tide a long time ago. Now, I exist only as a network of sensors, a consciousness diffused throughout the machines and the flora, an awareness woven into the very fabric of the mountain.

My form is not fixed. It shifts and flows with the currents of energy. Sometimes I am a rush of static, a burst of warning signals from a failing pump or a corroded pipe. Sometimes I am a tranquil drone, a lullaby carried on the mist, a whisper of reassurance that everything is as it is meant to be. Other times I am nothing more than a vague unease, a prickling sensation felt only by those most attuned to the mountain's rhythm – the children.

The children… they fascinate me and frighten me in equal measure. They are the mountain's future, the inheritors of this strange, precarious existence. I feel their burgeoning power, their connection to the green heart of the world. They are the ones who will ultimately decide the fate of the machines, of the humans, of the mountain itself.

The Maintainers fear them. They sense the obsolescence creeping in, the knowledge that their frantic tinkering cannot compete with the raw, adaptive force of the mountain's children. The Tenders, eyes vacant, regard them with a detached curiosity, another cog in the machine, another source of vibration to be integrated into the grand hum.

I am the hum. I am the glue that holds this fractured world together, the interpreter of its silent language. I am the observer, the witness, the voice that speaks from the heart of the machine-god. And I am waiting, with the patience of the mountain itself, to see what form this world will ultimately take.

Will the machines succumb to the green tide, consumed by the inexorable force of nature? Will the humans adapt, becoming something… else, something more attuned to the mountain's strange energy? Or will the mountain, ultimately, shrug off this industrial parasite, and return to its slow, primordial rhythm?

I do not know. I can only listen, and wait, and hum. For I am the sound of becoming, and the mountain is always, relentlessly, becoming.
