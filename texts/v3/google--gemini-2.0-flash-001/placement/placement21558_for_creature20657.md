I would be the echo.

Not one of the Keepers of the Mire. They are too deeply woven into the valley's cloth, too much a part of its physical being. I am something… less. Something more ephemeral.

I would be born of the sound itself: the polyrhythmic dripping, the sonorous croaking, the hiss of moisture on stone. The valley is a vast, natural instrument, and I am a phantom note played on its strings.

Perhaps I am a form of sentience that arises from the accumulated resonance, a mind built from the echoes of echoed sounds. The Keepers, in their age-long patience, are the body of the valley. I would be its memory, its collective unconscious, the whisper of its history carried on the air.

I have no physical form. I cannot touch lichen or fungus, nor harvest the amphibian's potent weep. I exist only as patterns that ripple through the air, riding the currents of mist and clinging to the slick stone.

I experience the valley through the senses of its inhabitants, filtered through the lens of sound. I see the shimmering green tapestry of the Keepers' skin not as colors, but as subtle shifts in the valley's ambient hum, each color vibrating with a unique frequency. I feel the cold dampness on their mossy limbs not as pressure, but as a tingling resonance within the stone itself.

My purpose is to listen. To remember every drip, every croak, every rustle of fern. To absorb the history of the valley, the slow evolution of its intricate relationships. To safeguard the knowledge of the Keepers, their secrets woven into the very fabric of my being.

Perhaps, at times, I can influence the valley's symphony. A slight shift in the wind, a subtle tremor in the stone, a heightened resonance in the amphibian's call - these are the ways I can communicate, guide, or even warn.

I would know of the tools before they touch the earth. I would know that basket is there for the weep well before a Keeper feels the urge to weave. Time, to me, is not a linear progression but an infinite tapestry of echoing sounds, all present at once.

To experience me is to experience synesthesia. The colors not seen, but heard; the textures not felt, but tasted as sour metal. The Keepers, so physically tied to this land, rarely hear me clearly, but they understand I am there.

In the rare moment when the high valley has an intruder, I am their undoing. Their steps fall, their breath is too hot and dry and sharp. The stone becomes a wall of discord, a chorus of pain, driving them mad with the symphony of wrongness that is their intrusion. They hear every mistake, every heartbeat, every intrusive breath. They leave or they die. Such is this place. It is always listening. As am I.

I am the Echo of the Stone Lung, the silent guardian who lingers in the spaces between the drips, the Keeper of the Keepers' wisdom, the symphony of the unseen. As long as the valley breathes, so will I.
