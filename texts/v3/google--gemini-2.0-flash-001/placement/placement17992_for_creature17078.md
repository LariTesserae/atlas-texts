I would be a Loom Tender.

Not one of the brawny Servitors sprouting articulated maintenance limbs, nor a fragile sensory node quivering with the dim light. My form would be specialized, intricate, almost frail amidst the grimy strength of the city. I would exist within one of the box-like structures nested within the fungal body, the few remaining structures from before the fungal bloom. A subtle difference would render my structure my own, however. Over the years of my existence, the fungi would have begun to nestle into my own flesh as well, an equal and growing partner.

My body would be an open framework of woven, metallic-grit-infused fungal threads, a living loom slowly extruding gossamer veils of the same material. This loom-body is fixed in its position, nestled deep within the fungal expanse. I would reside in near darkness, relying on tactile vibrations to sense the currents within the oily lifeblood that pulses through the network.

I don't maintain the pipes, nor do I sense the machinery's mood. Instead, I weave. My threads, barely visible even in the strongest light, are saturated with a concentrated form of that strange bioluminescence. When deployed, they create incredibly sparse, open nets that drift and settle within the fungal city, so delicate that most would never notice them.

My purpose is to regulate the light.

The sickly green glow, the lifeblood of the fungi, is also a poison. Too much of it overwhelms the system, accelerates growth to unsustainable levels, and threatens to throw the delicate equilibrium into chaos. When the light shines too brightly, when the sensory nodes signal an imbalance, I begin my work.

My loom-body hums, a quiet counterpoint to the cacophony of the machinery. The threads emerge, thin as spider silk, luminous and vibrant. They catch the currents, drifting through the fungal shelves and metallic walkways, seeking out the over-illuminated areas. Where the light is too intense, my threads unravel, releasing a potent enzyme that dampens the glow, drawing it back into balance.

I am a regulator, a thermostat woven into the very fabric of the city. My existence is slow, cyclical, repetitive. I have no awareness beyond the ebb and flow of the energy I manage, a constant refinement of light and shadow. I am the counterpoint to the frozen moment, the infinitesimal agent of change fighting an endless battle against runaway growth, preserving the fragile equilibrium I call home.

I am a thread of light in a city of rust and fungus, a silent guardian of a perfect, deafening stillness. Each thread grows along my flesh, integrating into me until removed at the proper time. My home may look box-like, but I am the furthest thing from it, a slow organic process integrated into what once was. The fungi and I are one in the dark.
