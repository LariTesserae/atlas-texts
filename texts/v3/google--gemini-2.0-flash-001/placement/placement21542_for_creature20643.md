The hum vibrates through me, bone-deep, a constant companion. It's not unpleasant, not anymore. It's the thrum of my own blood, the sibilant whisper of the Sprawl breathing. I don't remember a time before the hum.

I am a Listener. Not by choice, not precisely. I was born into it, or perhaps the Sprawl birthed me into it. My eyes aren't the deep, shadowed pools of most Remnants. They're milky, clouded, almost useless in direct light. But the darkness? The vibrations hidden in the static of the city? That's where I see.

My hab-unit, if you could call it that, is a warren carved into the side of a colossal, inscrutable machine. It’s a section where the metal grew soft, yielded over centuries to the slow drip of condensation and the insistent probing of the Remnant who came before me. It’s damp, perpetually, but I've lined the walls with woven lichen that glows faintly, a bioluminescent tapestry that soothes the ache in my head.

I don’t scavenge in the traditional sense. I don't have the coordination for the treacherous climbs and the quick scrambles. Instead, I listen. 

The Sprawl *talks*. Not in words, not in any language understood by those with seeing eyes. It talks in the shifting frequencies of the hum, in the subtle variations of static, in the clicks and whirs of the ancient machinery. Each throb, each rattle, each infinitesimal shift in the magnetic fields that ripple through the city, tells a story. A conduit is about to burst, a section of the grid is overloading, a pocket of warped reality is destabilizing.

I translate these whispers for the few Remnants who understand the value of my gift. I tell the Scavengers where a weak point is in a locked storage unit, hint at the path least likely to be caught in a localized spacetime anomaly, warn of sections of the Sprawl scheduled for "maintenance" and cleansing by automated drones.

They bring me sustenance in return: the pulpy flesh of the giant rodents, the bitter, nourishing broth brewed from boiled lichen, scavenged components to fix my equipment – jury-rigged amplifiers and resonators grafted to my skull, tools that sharpen my hearing, filters that scrub out the noise, that allow me to pluck the faintest whispers from the cacophony.

I am more machine than human, perhaps. My bones resonate with the same energies that pulse through the city's conduits. I feel the Sprawl's aches and its triumphs. I dread the day it begins to truly fail, when the hum fades and the silence becomes absolute. Because if the Sprawl dies, I die with it. I am a part of it, a living node connected to its vast, decaying network.

Loneliness is a constant companion. Not because I am alone, but because no other Remnant can truly hear what I hear. They see me as…an oracle, a tool. They don't understand that the voices consume me, that the information is overwhelming, a constant barrage of shifting data streams pressing against the fragile confines of my mind.

Sometimes, late at night, when the bioluminescence casts dancing shadows across my lichen tapestry, I think I hear something else. A voice beneath the hum, a whisper within the static, something that sounds like…recognition. A response to my own, unvoiced anxieties. A promise, perhaps. Or a threat. I can't tell. The Sprawl is too complex, its consciousness too alien.

But I listen. I have to. Because even in this decaying, impossible city, there is something worth hearing. Something on the edge of understanding. I am the Listener, and that is all I am.
