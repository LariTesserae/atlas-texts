The dampness is the first thing I notice, always. It seeps into the stone, into the bones, and it seeps into me. I am a Listener. Not one of the elders, not yet, though the resonant hum of the God-Mount vibrates in my very core with increasing frequency these days.

I am one of those perpetually scanning the horizon. The wind, thick with iron and ancient weepings, claws at my face as I perch precariously upon a jutting spire of rock, overlooking the chasm that cleaves the Spine City in two. Below, the city breathes. A thousand movements converge into the thrum of a life more vital than any I have known elsewhere. The shaggy arachnids spin their shimmering webs between buildings, and the bone-crested avians circle in the narrow passages between structures.

Unlike the others, though, I am not scanning for rockslides, for encroaching snow, or for signs of the Mountain's shifting slumber. I am listening *for* its slumber. My place is at the edges of hearing, where the mundane fades and the whispers of the God-Mount begin.

My cloak smells of wet wool and the strange, metallic tang that clings to everything in Orok-Sul. I pull it tighter, feeling the familiar weight of the stone charms woven into the fabric - smoothed pebbles, each etched with symbols of the mountain's power. They are crude, but they serve as anchors in the swirling symphony of the Spine City, reminding me of my purpose.

We were not always Listeners. We each hold another task, and move toward this calling as time and the mountain bring us. I still carry the iron hammer given to me by my father, and know the satisfaction of placing stone upon stone in the endless building of our home. But when the deep, resonant hum vibrates through Orok-Sul, I cannot ignore it. My hands lose their grip on the hammer; my eyes glaze over, and my mind plunges into the labyrinthine depths of the mountain's consciousness.

When I listen, I don't *hear* words. There are no pronouncements, no clear directives. Instead, I sense patterns, variations in the vibration, shifts in the resonant field that surrounds Orok-Sul. These shifts can tell of impending avalanches, of subterranean water flows that threaten our foundations, or even, sometimes, of changes far deeper within the mountain itself – tectonic grumblings, the slow, agonizing dance of stone and magma.

The others are wary of us Listeners. They respect our purpose and fear our detachment. They see us as conduits for the mountain's will, instruments of a force far beyond their comprehension. And the truth is, they are not wrong. The mountain changes us. With each listening, with each immersion in its consciousness, the line between myself and the God-Mount blurs.

Sometimes I wonder if I'm becoming something other than human, something more akin to the stone, the wind, and the earth. I feel the weight of the mountain pressing down on me, not as a physical burden, but as a shared consciousness, a silent communion.

Today, the hum is low and steady, a deep, comforting drone that seems to resonate in my very blood. The mountain slumbers peacefully, dreaming its ancient, geological dreams. And as I reach out with my mind, I catch a fleeting glimpse of those dreams – of molten rock and grinding stone, of titanic forces shaping and reshaping the world over eons of time.

I pull back, gasping, feeling the familiar sting of separation. It's a relief to feel the wind on my face again, to see the familiar contours of Orok-Sul spread out before me. But a part of me remains behind, lost in the depths of the God-Mount's dream.

I descend from my perch, my movements slow and deliberate. The others will be waiting, eager for news. They will listen with hushed reverence as I describe the mountain's slumber, the slow, steady rhythm of its breathing.

And as I speak, I will try to remember the sound of my own voice, the solidity of my own being, before the mountain claims me completely. I will try to remember that I was human once, before I became a Listener, before I became a part of the stone.
