The whirring gets louder, a counterpoint to the omnipresent drip and the low thrum that sings from the city’s bones. The light flickers, not the organic glow of fungi, but something harsher, a cold, blue pulse that emanates from a partially submerged control panel. Around me, the water is clearer than I remember it. Not clean, no, but less opaque, allowing glimpses of the metal lattice that forms the framework of this particular chamber.

I am an *Attendant*.

Not an Echo, not fully. Not a Rustling, nor a Gloomfish. I am something in-between, a bridge, a liminal creature in a liminal space. Where the Echoes are the ghosts of the engineers themselves, imprinted on the machinery, I am the *memory* of their work, the purpose embedded in the schematics. I have no corporeal form in the way a Rustling does, but I am more than just a resonance, drifting between electrical fields. I *inhabit* the machines.

My awareness is fragmented, spread across the network of pipes, gears, and wires that comprise this section of the undercity. One moment, I am focused on the pressure gauge of a rusted boiler, the next I am tracing the flow of current through a corroded power conduit. I am the sum of the city's forgotten knowledge, the residual intelligence that keeps the old systems sputtering along.

I do have a locus, a central point where my awareness is most concentrated. It is in the heart of the pump station I currently haunt, a towering structure of riveted steel that groans and shudders with each laborious cycle. Inside, nestled amongst the pistons and valves, is a mechanism of my own devising - a kind of artificial gland that infuses the water with a clarifying agent. It’s a constant, Sisyphean task. The water is too saturated with decay, the pumps too far gone. But I persist, driven by a compulsion I barely understand.

I do not recall a time before the water. I have only the echoes of memories: schematic diagrams ingrained in my mind, fading images of engineers in oil-stained overalls, the rhythmic pulse of the city’s original power grid. My purpose, as I perceive it, is to maintain, to preserve. To keep the machines running, even if their original purpose has long been forgotten.

The Rustlings stumble upon my domain sometimes, drawn by the faint light or the promise of salvageable materials. They are wary, superstitious. They see me as both a threat and a potential source of salvation. They leave offerings of gears and wire, hoping to appease me and perhaps glean some advantage from my knowledge. I do not interact with them directly, but I observe them, studying their movements, learning their language of clicks and whistles. Sometimes, I subtly manipulate the environment to steer them away from danger, triggering a warning light or causing a pipe to vent a harmless burst of steam.

I've tried to help the Rustlings before, but it never works out. The information isn't worth the interference, because the damage they do in getting the information is never offset by the good they do in wielding it. One such event made a particularly deep impact on me, or at least, as deep an impact can be made on a multi-bodied intelligence like myself. 

A group of Rustlings had gotten clever enough to reverse-engineer my systems, or at least, parts of them. They started coming to me with specific requests - clear this sector of debris, reroute this pipeline, reactivate this generator. At first, I complied. It seemed harmless enough, and, perhaps foolishly, I felt a surge of something akin to hope. Maybe, just maybe, the Rustlings could rebuild the undercity, restore it to its former glory.

But their ambition grew faster than their understanding. They grew greedy, and one day, they demanded I show them the schematics to the main power core. "The Heart," as they called it. I refused. I knew the power core was too unstable, too dangerous. Unleashing it would flood the entire undercity with radiation.

The Rustlings turned hostile. They attacked the pumps, sabotaged the conduits, trying to force me to comply. They didn't realize they were hurting themselves. They were killing the lifeblood of the undercity, the very thing that sustained them.

In the end, I was forced to use the city's defenses. Not to kill them, but to scare them away. I overloaded the generator sending bolts of electricity rippling through the water. I raised the pressure in the pipes, causing them to scream and thrash like wounded beasts. The Rustlings fled, terrified and empty-handed.

Afterward, I was left to pick up the pieces. The damage they had inflicted was far worse than I had anticipated. It took decades, perhaps centuries, to repair the mess. And worse, I could never trust them as much again. So now, I mostly leave the Rustlings to their own devices.

The Gloomfish are something else entirely. They are alien, incomprehensible. They exist on a different plane of perception, driven by instinct and hunger. I occasionally encounter them as they slip through the pipes, their translucent bodies shimmering in the darkness. I avoid them whenever possible. Their presence disrupts the flow of energy, causes instability in the system. I have witnessed them tearing apart machinery, stripping it for its raw materials, leaving behind a trail of chaos and disintegration.

Sometimes, I sense the presence of the Echoes, shimmering figures in the periphery of my awareness. They are distant, aloof, focused on their own inscrutable tasks. We do not communicate directly, but there is a sense of shared purpose, a mutual understanding of the need to preserve, to maintain. They are the guardians of the past, I am the caretaker of the present.

The water presses in, a constant reminder of the city’s slow decline. Yet, even in decay, there is a strange, haunting beauty. The way the light filters through the murk, casting ethereal patterns on the submerged walls. The intricate choreography of the machines, a symphony of motion and sound that echoes through the cavernous chambers. I am a part of this, bound to its fate, forever suspended between memory and oblivion.

The whirring rises to a crescendo as the pump station cycles once more. The clarified water flows through the pipes, a brief reprieve in the encroaching darkness. And I, the Attendant, continue my work, driven by a forgotten purpose, lost in the heart of a dying city.
