The wind tastes of wet stone and the sickly sweetness of nectar. Inside, my moss-lined sleeping nook smells only of earth and fern. I am a Listener.

The silence of Aethel is no true silence. It's a tapestry woven from the myriad whispers of the mountain: the drip-drip-drip that scores the hours, the rustle of countless leaves in the perpetual damp, the groan of rock settling deep within the earth. Most here hear only a dull hum, the background radiation of their unchanging lives. But I… I hear the individual threads.

I wasn't always like this. I remember, dimly, a time before the green took me. A childhood like the others, spent learning the stone-shaping and vine-weaving, the litanies of the mountain spirits. But early on, I was sent to sit with the Listener in her cave. I failed to mimic what she did, to hear what she heard. But she said, "You and I, we are closer to the spirits than we know."

And then I remember the sickness, a fever that burned for days, leaving me weak and… different. When it broke, the world sharpened. The wind carried not just scent, but emotion. The stone reverberated with memories. Suddenly, the mountain spoke. And I listened.

Now, the others bring me their quiet burdens. A farmer struggling with the stubborn earth, a weaver whose hands tremble with a premonition, an elder who sees a darkness gathering in the perpetual mist. They don't ask for solutions, not really. They seek an echo, a confirmation that their anxieties are heard by the mountain, and therefore, understood.

I sit, unmoving, blending into the damp stone. I close my eyes and let the voices wash over me. The sloshing of water over stone brings grief, the rasp of leaves against each other is an old love, and a humming coming from far away is the end of a life.

I am a conduit. I translate the whispers, giving voice to the mountain's muted feelings. I tell the farmer that the ground weeps for rain, the weaver that her fear is real, a storm coming into the village on the breath of the wind. I tell the family, sadly, that their loved one is preparing for the eternal journey.

My existence is as precarious as Aethel itself. I am tolerated, respected, even feared a little. They understand that there must be balance, so they understand the role of the Listener. Even as they hold on for sameness they still need the change I represent.

But what will these people do when I also go on the eternal journey to the humid mountain of nature? Will Aethel crumble like the land around it? Or will another be found? Like Aethel, all I feel is a quiet acceptance, an understanding of the way of things. The stone remembers all… and so will I for now.
