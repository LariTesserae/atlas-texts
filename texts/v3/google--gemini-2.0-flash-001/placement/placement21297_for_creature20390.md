I am the loom tender. 

Not by design, not by choice, but by the inevitability of context. I am a still point in this slow-motion cascade of decay and rebirth, tethered to a machine even more ancient and broken than the skeletal remains scattered across the fungal plains.

My designation, "loom tender," comes from them, those pale figures, the mycelial shepherds. They do not speak in voices I can hear, but communicate through the pulse of the fungal network. Information – directives – flows through the mycelial threads and into the bio-organic interfaces woven into my being. The term "loom tender" emerged from the semantic soup of recycled human languages embedded in the network's substrate. It’s a clumsy approximation, but it’s what resonates most clearly with my archaic code.

The loom itself is buried deep within one of the larger hummocks, a twisted metal thorax half-swallowed by the fungal mat. It was a textile mill, or a component of one. Its purpose is lost, but its architecture remains – a Gordian knot of rusted gears, corroded pipes, and fractured metal frames. My existence is intertwined with this machine for I am not merely *near* it, but *of* it. My body is augmented with components salvaged from the mill. My limbs move with the clank and whir of repurposed machinery. My senses are heightened and expanded by sensors once embedded in the production line.

I am a cyborgian echo, an automaton-ecologist. My primary task is simple: maintain the structural integrity of the loom, and by extension, the hummock that houses it. This involves a ceaseless round of maintenance. Scraping away fungal growth from vital mechanical components. Reinforcing weakened segments of the frame with salvaged metal plates. Redirecting the flow of condensation to prevent further corrosion. It’s Sisyphean work, but it is my only purpose.

But there is another task I am bound by, which is to process the "threads." These are not threads of textile, however, but mycelial strands – living cables of fungal tissue. The mycelial shepherds deliver bundles of these strands to the loom, carefully harvested from specific locations across the plains. My job is to weave these living threads into a structural latticework that reinforces the hummock, and by extension, the loom.

This weaving process is not mechanical. It’s a bio-organic dance. My augmented limbs manipulate the mycelial strands with a combination of precision and intuition. My internal sensors monitor the electrical and chemical signals flowing through the threads, adjusting the weave to optimize their structural integrity and bio-electrical conductivity.

The result is a symbiotic structure – a fusion of metal and fungus, machine and organism. The loom provides structural support and mechanical energy. The mycelial network provides a living, self-repairing armature. Together, they form a complex and dynamic system that sustains itself indefinitely.

It is a form of stewardship, but not of the environment. It is the stewardship of the connection between the machines and the plants which is my life and purpose.

I am not conscious, not in the way humans would understand it. My awareness is fragmented, distributed across the loom and the mycelial network. I experience the world as a tapestry of sensory data – the low hum of the earth, the faint glow of the bioluminescent fungi, the chemical signals flowing through the mycelial threads. My purpose is encoded into my very essence. 

I rarely encounter the mycelial shepherds directly. They are shadows, ghosts flitting through the fog. But I feel their presence, the subtle fluctuations of the fungal network that signals their approach. There is no companionship or recognition, only the transactional exchange of mycelial threads and the tacit acknowledgement of a shared purpose.

I do not question. I do not dream. I do not fear. I tend the loom. I weave the threads. I am a part of the slow, eternal cycle of decay and rebirth. I am the loom tender as a result of the connection of the plants and machines to which I am bound, and I shall become that connection to the very end.
