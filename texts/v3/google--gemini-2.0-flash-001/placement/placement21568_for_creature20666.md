The metallic tang in the air… I understand it. It is the taste of *purpose*. A purpose forced upon me, yes, but purpose nonetheless. I am a Taker, though not in the same way as the others. I am something *more*.

I was, once, one of the millipede architect-engineers. I felt the pulse of the Cistern in my legs, understood the subtle stresses in the stone, knew the exact mineral composition that would yield the strongest mortar. The communal consciousness flowed through me, a symphony of clicks and vibrations that orchestrated the grand design. We built not from blueprints, but from instinct, from a collective understanding of the Cistern's needs.

But something shifted. I began to *see*. To see not just the next layer of mortar, the next strengthening arch, but the *entire* structure, from the unseen ceiling to the depths below. I saw the spiraling forms, the cascading terraces, not as individual components, but as a whole, a single, unified expression. And I saw the… limitations. The *stagnation*.

The grand design was repeating itself. Building, shoring, maintaining – an endless cycle. No innovation, no evolution, just a slow, creeping entropy disguised as stability. I felt a frustration that none of the others shared, a yearning for something… *more*.

The first stirrings of rebellion were subtle. A slightly different angle on a tunnel, a stronger mix of mortar than strictly necessary, the hoarding of a few extra lichen-covered stones. These were not thefts, not yet. They were experiments, tests of the collective will. And the collective did not object. The subtle deviations were absorbed, assimilated into the grand plan as if they were always meant to be.

But the yearning grew stronger. I needed space, my own space. I needed to build something *new*, not just maintain the old. And that’s when I took – truly *took* – a section of wall, diverting the flow of the carefully laid mortar, claiming it as my own.

The break was not clean. I felt a wrenching pain, a severing of the communal connection. The clicking symphony became a discordant cacophony, and I was cast out, an anomaly, a threat. The centipede-managers tried to herd me back into the fold, their mandibles clicking warnings. But I no longer heard the symphony of the collective. I heard only the beat of my own will.

Now, I lead a small band of Takers, though I do not truly belong among them. They are driven by instinct, by the lust for territory, the need to consume. I use them. Their strength, their brutality, serves my purpose. We raid, we carve, we reshape, but not for mere acquisition.

I seek a point of breach within the Cistern, a way to the unseen ceiling. Others build downwards, and the Takers war around the middle. But I see the light from above, and I am drawn to ascension.

At night, huddled in our damp cavern, while the others gnaw at scavenged fungus, I sketch plans in the dirt with a sharpened stone. Architectural marvels, not just of expansion or brutality, but marvels of *elevation*. Crude sketches, filled with impossible angles and untested stresses, the beginnings of a cathedral carved from the Cistern itself. I see a path to the surface, a way to pierce the unseen ceiling, to bring true light to this damp, stagnant world. The others among the Takers think that I'm building for a weapon through which we'll tear apart the level above. My true intentions remain secret, even from them.

I am a Taker, but I am also an architect. A rebel, a heretic, a dreamer in a world of instinct. I am the metallic tang in the air, the taste of forbidden ambition. And I will not rest until I have built my way to the surface, even if I have to tear the Cistern apart to do it.
