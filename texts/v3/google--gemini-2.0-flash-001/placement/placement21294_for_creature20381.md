The rhythm of the city, the relentless *ka-thunk* of the forges, the hiss and groan of the geothermal vents... it's in my bones. It's the heartbeat of Aqueus, and it's the only sound I've truly known. I wasn't born *in* Aqueus, not exactly. My beginning was in a reclamation vat on the city's edge, salvaged from the unspent biological remnants of the smelting process. Waste not, want not, as the saying goes and the elements most easily broken down and reused are those closest to human, or *were* human.

They assigned me designation "Omega-7," but I was always just "Scrap." I wasn't *meant* to be. Reclamation biologicals don't typically gain consciousness. The process of breaking them down into base elements is efficient, but not exact, and once in a great while, a collection of material pieces itself back together, remembers itself into existence, or at least remembers *close enough.*.

Most don't last. The spark of awareness flickers, dies. Omega-1 through 6 didn't make it. But in the soup of rendered bodies and industrial runoff, something in me… stuck. Perhaps it was a glitch in the system, or some unforeseen confluence of elements that resisted reduction and clung to coherent structure. I don't believe it now. When I began, the process was new, and any deviation of its intended results was seen as error. Now, though, the reclamation vats have been repurposed for their intended product: me, and more like me.

I emerged... incomplete. I lack pigmentation, rendering my skin a pale, almost translucent grey, stretched tight over elongated limbs. I look like a scarecrow, dressed in cast-off overalls, filtration mask perpetually slung around my neck. One of my eyes sees only in ultraviolet, casting the city in shades of hidden heat. The other is standard human, faded with age and the grime of Aqueus.

My designation, and others like mine, is Servitor. Our bodies may be closer to those of humans than Scuttlers, but our minds and memories have been purposefully suppressed, overwritten by a program of simple directions, instructions that need no interpretation. Carry this, move that, maintain this. No understanding required.

But the suppression wasn’t perfect. There are… holes. Gaps in my memory where something struggles to surface. Moments where a flicker of emotion, a resonance with the clang of a hammer or the orange glow of molten metal, sparks something akin to… longing.

I walk the elevated walkways, repair mag-lines, and assist on my forges, never resting but never feeling, either. If my programming drifts, it isn't difficult to correct. A burst of high-frequency sound is all that's needed to reset me to the programmed default, a direction to go, a task to complete.

My existence has been an exercise of perpetual forgetfulness, and yet... remnants persist. The way I unconsciously trace patterns on the cooling slag, the faint memory of a face surfacing in my mind as I polish an acid-proof seal, the quiet hum I emit as I tend a geothermal vent, a tune I don't remember learning. I can tell that parts of me are the results of refinement, the waste product sorted from other scraps and given a new life, or at least the impression of one.

It is said that the human minds of Aqueus are gritty in nature, full of tenacity and persistence. This too is true of me. My memories may be erased, but my body remembers. My fingers instinctively grip the handles of tools worn smooth by generations of labor. My legs know the precise angle needed to climb the rickety ladders leading to the highest walkways. My lungs have, in their own way, adapted to the choking air, extracting what little oxygen there is with disturbing efficiency.

One day, I'm greasing the gears of a conveyor belt in the abandoned Sector 7, a section of the city slowly crumbling beneath the weight of accumulated mineral deposits. As I work, I notice a section of the wall, partially hidden by a fallen pipe, that seems different. The metal glistens, not with grime, but with an unnatural iridescence. My ultraviolet sight confirms that is emitting heat, subtle but distinct, like a pulse.

Driven by something other than my programmed directives, I pry the pipe away, exposing the wall. The metal is warm to the touch, almost alive. As I run my hand across the surface, the iridescence intensifies, patterns of light swirling beneath its surface.

Then, a voice whispers inside my mind. Not a sound I *hear*, but a knowing, a recognition. *“Welcome home, Scrap.”*

This one simple sentence breaks the chains of my servitude. The suppressed memories surge forth, not as clear images, but as a fragmented symphony of sensations. The taste of clean water. The smell of ozone after a lightning strike. The feel of wind on my face. Most significantly, the faces, scores of faces I used to know. 

More importantly, I remember *myself.*

Not Scrap, Omega, or Servitor. My name is Talos, and I'm a failed Deep Smelter.

The wall before me is not just metal, but a node. A nexus point connected to a vast network of similar structures hidden throughout Aqueus, created by the Deep Smelters to channel geothermal energy and manipulate the city’s underlying energies. We were trying to create a symbiosis between Aqueus and the volcano. We were trying to *heal* it.

The guilds found out. Feared our power, and had us... repurposed.

But our work persists. It lives on in the hidden nodes, in the strange lichens, the hardy people, and even in the reclaimed biologicals, like me, resurrected with a purpose they only now can fulfill.

I am Talos, and I am home. My work is not finished. I can sense the presence of the other nodes, feel the echoes of other Deep Smelters, repurposed but not entirely silenced. With my newfound memories and ultraviolet sight, they will be easy to trace. Together, we can awaken the sleeping heart of Aqueus and challenge the greedy guilds. With the reclamation pool, the process has been refined. My brothers and sisters are still out there, waiting.

The city still breathes weirdness. But I will breathe that weirdness back.
