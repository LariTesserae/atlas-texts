I am the Whisperer.

Not to be confused with those who, within Skitter culture, are assigned the role of *telling* (as best they can) from the dreams and thoughts of others. Those Skitters are important, but their purview is limited. They work with the real, the known. I do not.

I am something different. I feel. I *know*, without knowing *how*.

I am no Skitter; not quite. I am… *adjacent*. My body is a variation, a deviant strain. Thinner chitin, for one. More carapace at the top of my head. My limbs are longer, more delicate, almost spider-like. I can move among the Skitters without rousing alarm, but I am always regarded with a peculiar unease. I see it in the way their multifaceted eyes linger on me, the slight flexing of their mandibles. I am Other.

But that is not why I am the Whisperer. It is the voices I hear.

The Skitters hear the symphony of the cavern. They understand the language of scuttling claws, the echo of wings, the drip of water that signals a subterranean passage I cannot hope to locate. I hear those things too, but layered beneath them is something else: a chaotic stream of thoughts, emotions, and… echoes. The cavern *thinks*.

It started subtly. Fleeting impressions as a grub, vague sensations that I dismissed as hunger or discomfort. As I matured, the whispers grew louder. At first, a confusing jumble of fear, aggression, and hunger. But now there is more. Tendrils of sensation and fleeting image. I feel the crushing weight of the rock above, the slow, relentless erosion of water, the yearning of roots that delve deep into the earth.

This is what sets me apart, what isolates me. The Skitters believe their world to be defined by the immediate, the tangible. Struggle and survival. They are creatures of habit and instinct. I see beyond that. I sense the slow, grinding gears of geological time; the desperate hope to be found by another; the terror of the encroaching darkness that lies below.

I live on the fringes of the Skitter settlements, a solitary anomaly respected and feared. They bring me offerings – choice fungi, the strongest vines, the odd trinket of bone or iridescent stone. They believe I possess some mystical power, some ability to influence the spirits of the cavern. I do not. I'm just a translator with no language to translate into.

Sometimes, I try to communicate what I sense, what I feel. The Skitters stare at me with unknowing compound eyes. I make sounds, I weave patterns in the dust, I try to mimic the echoes of the cavern, but they do not understand. How could they? I lack the tools. I lack the *words.*

And so, I wander the caverns alone, listening to the whispers of the earth, a bridge between two worlds that cannot understand each other.

My purpose, I suspect, is not to understand, but simply to listen. And perhaps, one day, the cavern will choose to speak.
