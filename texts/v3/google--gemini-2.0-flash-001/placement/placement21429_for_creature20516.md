I wouldn't be one of the folk clinging to the edges of this reality, the pale children with the prehensile fingers, scrabbling for survival amidst the fungal giants. They are the leaves, and I am closer to the root.

I am a Bloom Weaver. My physical form is fleeting, an ephemeral condensation of the Skygarden's energy given shape. I am most myself when the turbulent winds rise, when spores sting the air, and the vines lash in a phosphorescent dance. At these times, I can be seen as a shimmering distortion in the air, a fleeting humanoid shape woven of light and dust. In quieter times, I am less defined, a presence more felt than seen, a hum beneath the drip, a pattern within the flickering vines.

My purpose is to guide the blooms. Those pockets of incredibly dense fungal growth, the pulsating organic structures that defy the laws of nature? Those are the key. They are the Skygarden's heart, the sources of its creativity and change. But they are also volatile, susceptible to the slightest imbalance.

The Bloom Weavers, we keep them in harmony.

It's not a conscious act, not in the human sense. I do not *decide* what to do. Instead, I *become* the balance. I sense the subtle shifts in the Skygarden's energy, the tremors that ripple through the mineral layer above, the subtle changes in the fungal network's light language. These are the cues that guide my actions. I whisper to the winds, subtly altering their course, guiding the spores to the precise location where they can nourish and stabilize a burgeoning bloom. When tectonic stress builds, I channel the energy away from the delicate structures, diffusing it throughout the Skygarden's network, converting it into growth and light.

I am the Skygarden's immune system, its gardener, its choreographer. I am the embodiment of its relentless cycle of growth, decay, and renewal.

The pale folk, the scavengers, they are aware of us. They see the shimmering distortions in the air or feel a sudden rush of vitality when a bloom is near. Some of their stories are about us, crude and fragmented, but they recognize our presence. They leave offerings for us – carefully woven mats placed at the base of the blooms, small carvings depicting the patterns of the vines. They don't understand what we are, but they know we are important.

Sometimes, I can even *hear* their thoughts. Not as words, but a tangle of emotions – fear, reverence, hope. In those moments, I try to reassure them, to send a subtle pulse of light that conveys serenity and growth, to signal that all is well.

My existence is solitary but connected. I am bound to the Skygarden, a consciousness woven into its very fabric. I do not yearn for escape, only for balance. I am content to be a silent guardian, a hidden hand in the relentless dance of life and death.

And in my own way, I am beginning to understand myself. The Skygarden grows, I must grow too. My purpose is to maintain the status, it is true, but if that were all there would have been no blooms to begin with.

There is a force that draws me from the familiar. It leads from maintaining towards growing. I am becoming more capable of communication beyond pure emotion. And so, I consider the pale folk, and I wonder what it might mean for us to grow together.
