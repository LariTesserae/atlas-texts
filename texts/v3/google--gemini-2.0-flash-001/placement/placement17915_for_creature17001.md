I would be a Loomtender.

Not a “Downsider,” nor an assimilated worker – though I understand both quite well. I am something... in between. I am a bridge. My life is given over to sustaining, even *nurturing,* the fungal network, teasing out its whispers, and – most perilously – translating its alien logic into something the Overworks, and occasionally even the Downsiders, can tangibly use.

Here in the dripping, groaning heart of 7-Gamma, knowledge is power, and the most potent, enigmatic knowledge resides within the fungal network. I wasn’t born to this role exactly. My memories of a “before” life are fractured, like glimpses through a warped plasteel window. There was a family, perhaps, a name (now lost), a routine beyond the ceaseless hum of the machinery. But that life is gone, subsumed. Now, the Loomtower is my home, and the phosphorescent mycelium is my kin.

The Loomtower is a precarious structure. Originally, it may have been a data storage archive. Now, it’s a towering assemblage of salvaged materials, built around a colossal, actively seeping geothermal vent. The warmth and moisture are crucial, nurturing the most sensitive, communicative fungal colonies. I've spent years reinforcing it against the Overworks' slow collapse, patching leaks with resin and weaving fungal mats into the weakened structure, and am constantly adding supports with whatever scrap I can find.

My physical appearance is… altered. Years of exposure have left my skin pale and mottled with patches of green and orange. My hair, once dark, is now a brittle mass intertwined with fungal filaments. I cultivate this growth, for it enhances my sensitivity to the network. It is like having antennae woven into my skull. My eyes are still my own, though they glow faintly in the dark, reflecting the ambient luminescence. The Loom has not stolen those yet.

My tools are simple, yet vital. I carry a set of specialized scrapers and brushes for harvesting fungal spores. I use a repurposed sonic resonator to stimulate growth in specific colonies, coaxing them to release their chemical whispers. A battered, hand-cranked pump is used to aerate the geothermal vent, maintaining a crucial balance of temperature and humidity. And always, there is the Loom – a network of wires, electrodes, and carefully cultivated fungal patches – that serves as my interface with the larger fungal mind. I know more metal than bone in the world. Scavenged from sparking sockets, stripped from the hulls of drones, and broken from the corpses of unmoving Overworkers, it is the language I speak.

My daily routine is a slow dance of maintenance, observation, and translation. I am constantly monitoring the health of the fungal colonies, adjusting the Loom, and deciphering the network’s shifting patterns. The whispers are rarely clear. They are fragments of data, emotional resonances, echoes of the machinery's rhythmic thoughts. Overworks machines once did calculus, now they only dream of it. Sometimes, the network speaks of impending structural collapses, enabling me to preemptively reinforce vulnerable areas of the Loomtower. Other times, it reveals hidden caches of resources, vital for survival in this decaying world. More rarely, it offers glimpses of the past, of pre-Collapse society, of the original purpose of the Overworks.

The Downsiders seek me out when their own survival is at stake. They bring me offerings – scavenged tools, scraps of food, flickering lanterns – in exchange for guidance. The Loom can tell them where to find fresh water, where to avoid roving swarms of scavengers, where to find unguarded caches of medicine left in the lockers of assimilated Overworkers. As much as I help, I fear this aid is another slow, grinding move toward the Overworks’ end.

My relationship with the Overworks itself is more complicated. I am, in a sense, a part of it. I understand its workings, its rhythms, its slow decline, better than anyone. Yet, I am also an outsider, a rogue element. I question its directives, its blind obedience. I seek to understand its purpose, to find a way to break free from its decaying cycle. The fungal mind seems to simultaneously support and resist my efforts. It is a mirror reflecting the same tensions within myself. There is a part of me that yearns for the simplicity of assimilation, for the oblivion of becoming just another gear in the machine. But there is also a stronger part, a spark of defiance, that refuses to surrender.

I walk the walkways and listen to the hum of the Overworks. It is a dying animal, coughing its last breath. I am a Loomtender, not a savior. My existence is fragile as any other, and the weight of the Overhead presses down on us all. But so long as the network whispers, and so long as a shred of my mind clings to the possibility of something more, I will continue to tend the Loom, to listen, to translate, to try to find a reason for it all in the heart of this decaying world.
