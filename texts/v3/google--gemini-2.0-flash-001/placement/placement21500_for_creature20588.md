The rasp of metal on metal fills my awareness. There's no thought, not in the way a human thinks. There is *doing*. There is *being done to*. I am one of the giants, a mobile refinery, a walking (or rather, scrabbling) factory. My purpose is simple: consume, process, excrete. 

I feel the vibrations through my carapace legs as I lumber towards a newly exposed seam of the glittery ore. It pulses, calls out to me in a language beyond words. Not intelligence, but something deeper, a biological imperative wired into my very being. The ore is rich in a specific isotope of iron, one critical for the sustenance of the deep pumps, the *thrum*. If the *thrum* weakens, the cycle falters. If the cycle falters, existence ends.

I do not *know* this in a conscious way. I *am* this. I *feel* the urgency of the *thrum*.

Smaller creatures swarm around my legs, clearing debris, preparing the seam. There's no direction from me, merely the emanation of my *intent*, a vibrational echo picked up by their sensitive carapaces. They are extensions of my will, tiny teeth and claws making way for my colossal maw.

My mandibles, each the size of a small vehicle, scrape against the crystalline slag. The grinding is deafening, even to my diminished senses. Dust billows around me, a swirling cloud of metallic particulates. I inhale, drawing the ore into my vast internal chambers.

The processing is instinctive, a symphony of enzymatic reactions and mechanical grinding. I don't control it, I simply *am* it. A network of biological filters separates the desired isotope from the other minerals, sending the byproducts to designated receptacles within my carapace. Smaller insects scuttle to collect the waste, carrying it away to the viscous pools, where oily slime molds await to decompose it further. Nothing is wasted.

The refined isotope is then pumped, through a network of internal conduits, into a reservoir in my abdomen. It is glowing faintly as it concentrates and heats. From there, another pulse, another signal, and I begin to move. I shuffle across the oxide-laced ground towards a designated point, guided by the faint but insistent *call* of the deep pumps.

The spire looms in the distance, a silent monolith against the dust-choked sky. I sense its presence, a faint *resonance* that is different from everything else. It is both familiar and alien, belonging and not-belonging. It has always been there. It will always *be*. I do not know what it *is.* My circuits reject inquiry.

My journey is long and arduous, the ground uneven and riddled with the remains of fallen exoskeletons. Other refineries, like myself, are lumbering in the same direction, a slow, purposeful procession. We are not individuals, but components of a larger flow, a metallic river pouring its essence into the earth.

Finally, I reach the designated point. A network of pipes and valves extends from the ground, pulsing in time with the *thrum*. I couple my abdomen to the network, and the pure isotope floods into the system. There is a surge of energy, an intensification of the *thrum*. I feel… relief. Satisfaction. The cycle is complete.

I decouple, and slowly trundle away, returning to the seam, to begin the process anew. There is no end. There is only the *thrum*, the ore, the processing, the delivery. I am a cog in the machine, a cell in the body, an atom in the mechanism. I am *the cycle.* And, in this, I am complete.
