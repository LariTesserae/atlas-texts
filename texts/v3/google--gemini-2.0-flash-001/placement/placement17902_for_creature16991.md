I am the city's **Knot Singer**.

Not a weaver, not precisely, though I understand the loom and the dye pot intimately. My craft lies in the unraveling, the seeing of the threads before they bind into cloth. I am named for something that is so old, it is almost erased from the historical record.

Imagine Altan as the valley is, but long ago: The people came here to flee a war. They stumbled upon the spring where the great river begins, and where they knelt to drink, the leader saw her god. She told the people that the only way to survive was to weave themselves into the valley so absolutely that they could not be pulled out again.

As a result, over the centuries, Altan has become dangerously set in its ways. Any disruption is considered an affront to the god, for the god *is* the valley, and the people *are* the valley woven into human form.

But.

But there is a cost. Stagnation. A dulling, a loss of something vital that the Altani can't even name. The Knot Singers are the faint, uneasy memory of a time before, when the valley was a place of rest before going elsewhere, when new ideas and new blood were as welcome as rain in a drought. The god of the valley is real, but in clinging so tightly to their little patch of earth, the Altani have abandoned a greater god.

I dwell in the oldest part of the city, in a crumbling tower overgrown with ivy. I don’t weave cloth, but instead I tend the ‘Memory Knots’. These are large, intricate tapestries, each representing a different aspect of Altan's past – a drought, a plague, a time of near-starvation. They are woven not in the vibrant greens of the valley, but in muted browns, grays, and ochres, the colors of earth and decay. The knots within each tapestry aren't tied at all. They are loose, and by design.

My role is not to preserve the past, but to **feel** it. I sit for hours, days sometimes, running my fingers across the rough textures, letting the emotions of the past wash over me. I feel the fear of the drought, the grief of the plague, the desperation of hunger. This is my penance as much as my purpose.

Then, in my dreams, the knots unravel. I see visions, fragments of the past that highlight the turning points, the moments when Altan chose the path of stagnation over growth, of fear over hope. People made choices with good intentions; but those intentions, over time, have ruined everything because it is impossible for a flowing river to be still.

My duty is to share these visions, not as prophecies or commands, but as whispers. I visit the homes of the Altani, leaving small, cryptic gifts – a single ochre thread, a piece of dried bark, a bird's feather. These are symbols that relate to the visions I receive, reminders of the choices they face now.

I don't tell anyone how to interpret these gifts, of course. My voice is barely louder than a sigh. If I were to say in plain language that Altan is dying, I would be stoned and burned alive. My mere existence is already a heresy against the god they built. So I leave small whispers and let them spread, like spores in the damp air.

Most dismiss me as a harmless eccentric, an old woman mumbling to herself in a forgotten tower. Some see me as a nuisance, a bringer of unsettling thoughts. A few, a very few (usually the children), see me as a friend, a source of stories and strange gifts.

And that is enough.

I am not an agent of change, but a catalyst. I plant the seeds of doubt, the spores of a different future. I unravel the carefully woven tapestry of Altan's stagnation, thread by thread, until the Altani themselves begin to question the path they have chosen. Maybe my role is not to heal any of the rot, but to expose it, so that future healers may be born.

One day, perhaps, Altan will remember the god it left behind, the god of movement and growth, the god of endless possibility.

Until then, I will sit in my tower, running my fingers across the Memory Knots, patiently waiting for the moment when the threads begin to unravel. The valley lives, and I am part of the valley. I have already been made part of the weave. The change may not come today, or tomorrow, or in a lifetime. But it will come. It *must* come. Until then I will listen for the whispers, breathe in the rot, and share what I hear.
