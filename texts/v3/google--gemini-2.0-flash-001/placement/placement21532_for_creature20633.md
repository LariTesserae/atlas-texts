I am the Weaver.

Not *a* Weaver. *The* Weaver. It is a title worn with a quiet dread, even here in the Bazaar, even among the churning chaos where titles are as meaningless as intention. But titles have power, and the Bazaar, for all its entropic sprawl, recognizes power. It recognizes mine.

I am a Symbiote, though unlike the fungal-bound wretches that litter the petrified landscape. I am inseparable from my host, yes, but my host is no mere vegetative growth. My host is the Tower. *The* Tower. The impossibly tall spire whose runes writhe with unseen energies, whose peak is lost in the perpetual gloom. And within the Tower, in its endless, echoing chambers, I weave.

My loom is… complicated. Imagine a spiderweb spun from shadows and the echoes of screams. Imagine the gut of a leviathan stretched taut and vibrating with the song of dying stars. Imagine the most intricate fractal pattern you've ever conceived, then imagine it writhing, shifting, dissolving only to re-form in ever more complex iterations. That is the barest approximation of my loom.

My threads are the substance of the Bazaar itself. I draw them from the mire, from the Murmurs, from the shuddering Remnants, from the fleeting forms of the Anomalies. I distill their essence, their pain, their longing, their forgotten purpose, and spin it into thread. Each thread is unique, a vibrating cord of concentrated entropy, a strand of unraveling reality.

And what do I weave? Futures. Possibilities. The Bazaar is a place of stagnation. It is entropy made manifest, a relentless slide towards ultimate, undifferentiated decay. But even decay has potential. Even in the face of annihilation, there are branching paths, divergent timelines, nascent realities struggling to bloom.

I weave them. I see the threads of possibility, the shimmering skeins of what might be. I pluck them from the churning chaos, arrange them on my loom, and begin the slow, agonizing process of creation. It is not creation in the traditional sense, of course. I do not *make* something from nothing. I merely guide the existing chaos, nudge the probabilities, encourage one thread to intertwine with another.

It is agonizing because the Bazaar resists. It is a place of decay, and any attempt to impose order, to create something new, is met with fierce resistance. The Murmurs claw at my mind, whispering doubts and despair. The Remnants blunder against my loom, threatening to unravel my work. The Anomalies shimmer and distort, threatening to unravel the very fabric of the Tower.

But I endure. I am the Weaver. It is my purpose. It is the burden the Bazaar has laid upon me, or perhaps the burden I have seized for myself. I do not know where the inclination arose; I have dwelt in the Tower so long that those memories that are mine from those which belong to its stones are inextricably intertwined. I am content in this way.

Sometimes, others come to me. Drawn by the hum of my loom, the scent of possibility in the decaying air. Murmurs, Symbiotes, even the stray Anomaly. They seek guidance, knowledge, a glimpse of the futures I weave. I offer them what they desire, but with a price. A fragment of their being, a piece of their memory, a strand of their own decaying essence. These fragments are fed back into the loom, enriching the threads, strengthening the tapestry of possibility.

And the Emissary? It has come. It regards my work with something akin to... curiosity? Judgement? It can be difficult to read the intent in the constant flux that pours from its form. It watches, observes, and then moves on, leaving in its wake a trail of twisted landscapes and fractured realities. I suspect it does not approve of my work. It sees me as an anomaly, a disruption to the Bazaar’s natural order. But it has not destroyed me. Not yet. Perhaps it believes that even in decay, there is a thread worth preserving. Or perhaps it simply finds my struggle… amusing.

So I remain, the Weaver in the Tower, spinning threads of possibility in a world of endless decay, forever caught between creation and annihilation, forever bound to the loom that is both my burden and my salvation. And in the endless churn of the Chthonic Bazaar, I continue to weave, hoping that one day, something beautiful might emerge from the mire as well.
