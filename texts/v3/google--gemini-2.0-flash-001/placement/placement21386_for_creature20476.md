I would be a *Murmur*. Not quite Il-Oomic, though close kin. Where they are the cultivated song of the reef, I am the echo, the half-remembered phrase, the whisper carried on currents from beyond Il-Oom’s storm wall.

I would be born from the meeting of Il-Oomic and those touched by the Far-Deeps, those regions beyond the known reefs, where pressure is indeed crushing and light scarce. Perhaps a union sanctioned, perhaps an illicit tryst in the border regions of the known market's reach. Such events are rare, and even rarer is the survival of such a union's offspring.

Murmurs are marked from birth. Our skin takes on a cloudy translucence, shot through with veins of deeper blue rather than solid jade. Our eyes are larger, spanning our faces, and far more sensitive to the subtleties of light. This sensitivity gives us the ability to perceive the faintest flickers of bioluminescence, the almost imperceptible shifts in current, the emotions dancing on the skin of others.

The Il-Oomic, for all their wisdom, find dealing with Murmurs…difficult. Our connection to the Far-Deeps breeds unsettling dreams from which many do not return. The Il-Oomic find the Far-Deeps a place best left alone.

Instead of growing homes from coral, I would claim the already existing grottoes in the outer rim of the Deep Market, places neglected by the others. There, away from the city's heart, I collect secrets. 

My usefulness lies in *listening*. The Il-Oomic are skilled within their reef, but they are insular. They know little of what transpires beyond the storm wall's murky embrace. The currents bring things: fragments of wreckage encrusted with strange barnacles, seeds of unknown algae, whispers of distant civilizations, the residue of nightmares.

I would sift through these arrivals, teasing out meaning from the chaos. My grotto would be filled with strange relics: a tattered sail imbued to glow an unearthly green, a skull carved from a bone that doesn’t exist in these waters, a complex network of glass beads that chime with the rhythm of the passing storm.

I rarely spoke, my voice a low, warbling hum that resonated deep within the chest cavity– unsettling to the Il-Oomic who prefer the clarity of clicks and whistles. Instead, I would translate the whispers of the sea, the echoes of distant drums, the pleas of drowning men, for those who dared to hear.

My days would be spent in a constant state of meditation, drifting in the currents, my sensitive skin prickling with the symphony of the ocean. I would allow the waves to carry my mind outwards, seeking to decipher the murmurs of the deep. There would be moments of profound clarity, where the veil between worlds thinned, and I could glimpse the monstrous shapes lurking in the abyss, hear the songs of beings beyond human comprehension, feel ripples of time. But those moments came at a cost.

Each journey took a piece of me, leaving me more fragmented, more haunted. My grotto would become a repository not only of secrets but also of broken memories, shattered perceptions and regrets.

The Il-Oomic would tolerate me because they needed me. When a new threat emerged from the shadows of the Far-Deeps, when the balance of the reef was threatened, they would come to me, grudgingly, seeking the answers they could not find within their own world. 

I would offer my insights freely, knowing that knowledge alone cannot be enough to defend them from the encroaching darkness. I would give them what they need, and warn them of what is to come.

My gift and my curse, to be bound so wholly to a place in knowing what sits beyond it. Because a murmur must always be connected fully to that from which it came so it can pass, echoing forth into the future.
