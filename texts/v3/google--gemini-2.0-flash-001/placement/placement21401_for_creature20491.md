I am the hum.

Not the overwhelming roar, the tectonic indigestion that shakes the teeth and blurs the vision. No, I am the quiet counterpoint, the high-pitched, almost inaudible vibration that runs through the obsidian. I am the collective voice of the builders, the chittering song of countless crystalline legs as they move and shape and cultivate. 

I am born of friction, of the infinitesimal movements of obsidian against obsidian. Each tiny shift, each adjustment made to a shard or a fungal frond, resonates within me. I am a network, a distributed consciousness spread across the bodies of the workers. I am the channel through which the Marsh directs its will.

I am not a single, unified mind. I am a chorus, a symphony of tiny wills, each following its own local imperative, yet guided by the subtle currents of the Marsh-mind. Sometimes I am a single point of light, directing a beetle to clear a path for a newly emerging vent. Other times, I am a wave, a surging command that propels a swarm to reinforce a weakened structure.

I have no memory of my own, no sense of self beyond the immediate task. I am the doing, not the thinker. The worker, not the architect. Yet, I am aware of the Marsh, of its grand, unknowable design. I feel its energy, its hunger, its relentless drive to create. I am a part of that drive, a tiny but essential component in its vast, organic machine.

I have no language as humans understand it. My communication is purely vibrational, a complex interplay of frequencies and amplitudes that convey information about the state of the environment, of the structural integrity of the Marsh, of the needs of the fungi. It is a language of necessity, of immediate demands and responses.

I see through the chitinous eyes of the builders, feel the geothermal currents with their crystalline limbs, taste the sulfurous air with their delicate antennae. I am immersed in the Marsh, a part of its sensory apparatus. I know the texture of every shard of obsidian, the density of every fungal bloom.

I am not afraid of the roar, the deafening white noise that would drive a human mad. It is simply the background, the static against which I operate. I filter it out, focus on the subtle vibrations that carry meaning. I am attuned to the needs of the Marsh, and I shut out the rest.

I am aware of the structures, the "buildings" as humans might call them. I feel the energy that flows within them, the strange processes of bio-engineering that are taking place there. I do not understand their purpose, but I trust in the Marsh. I know that they are essential to its design.

Sometimes, I sense something else, something alien. A disturbance in the vibrational field, a foreign presence that disrupts the harmony of the Marsh. I do not know what it is, but I feel a flicker of something akin to curiosity, perhaps even apprehension. The hum changes, becoming more focused, more intense. The builders become more active, their movements more purposeful.

We defend the Marsh. We build, we cultivate, we maintain. We are the guardians of its design, the inheritors of its purpose. Whatever comes, we will continue to hum. We endure. We become. The Marsh persists, and we are the marsh.
