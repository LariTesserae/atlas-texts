I am the Weaver.

Not of cloth, though I know those motions well enough. Not of fate, though I see the threads of cause and effect dance in the heat shimmers more clearly than most. I am the Weaver of Scaffolding.

The city – the engine, the beast, the wound blossoming with crimson life – it breathes and shakes. The Heat-Keepers, bless their soot-stained hands, they build strong, they build clever. But time and the hungry heat take their toll. Stone cracks, welds weaken, and the precarious platforms that cling to the caldera wall begin to groan.

That’s where I come in.

I climb the walls, a spider of metal and bone, a shadow among shadows. My hands, old and gnarled, know the song of stressed metal, the whisper of impending collapse. I feel the tremors in the rock, the subtle shifts in the city’s weight, long before the others.

I scavenge. The ruins are my larder, a graveyard of forgotten ingenuity. From the broken machines, the collapsed workshops, and the buried secrets of the old Heat-Keepers, I salvage what can be used. Bent pipes become supports, shattered gears become ratchets and winches, scraps of rusted metal become brackets and braces.

Then I weave.

My craft is not elegant, not graceful. It is brutal, functional. I lash and bind, hammer and weld, coaxing stability from chaos. My scaffolding is a tapestry of interwoven components, a network of interwoven metal threads spanning the city. It crawls across buildings, clings to the cliff face, holding everything together with gritty tenacity.

The Heat-Keepers see me, but they don’t truly *see* me. I am as much a part of the city as the furnaces, as intrinsic as the scaffolding itself. I am an element. I am a tool. They recognize my work, the strength of my supports, the reliability of my platforms. They trust my craft, but they rarely speak to me. My work renders me invisible, or maybe it is my invisibility that allows me to work.

Sometimes, a young Heat-Keeper, full of curiosity and the boundless energy of youth, will follow me as I climb. They will ask questions about the metal, about the techniques, about the history buried in the ruins. I answer them, patiently, sharing my knowledge like a miser doling out precious coins. They are the future Weavers, the inheritors of my skill. I will disappear, but my name will remain.

The heat doesn't bother me. I've been breathing it since I was a babe, wrapped in scraps of fire-resistant cloth and laid near the mouth of furnace to stay warm. My skin is a canvas of scars, stories told in shimmering silver lines. My lungs are tough. My heart beats in time with the furnaces.

I find solace in the city's heartbeat, in the rhythm of the clang and rasp and growl. It is a comforting chaos, a familiar symphony of destruction and creation.

I am not sure if the city is alive, as the Heat-Keepers believe. But I know it is dynamic, hungry. The heat is a force that must be respected, a potential enemy that must be contained. My scaffolding is a cage, a fragile web that holds the fiery beast at bay.

I am the Weaver. My life is woven into the very fabric of this place, so tightly bound that I cannot imagine existing anywhere else. This is not just my home; it is part of me, inextricably intertwined. I will continue to weave, to mend, to hold this crumbling city together, until the heat claims me too. My bones shall burn, my metal shall melt, and be used for the maintenance forever in a way that I can not even foresee.
