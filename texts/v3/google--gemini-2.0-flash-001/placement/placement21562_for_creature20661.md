I would be a Singer.

Not one of the swarmers, though I would live among them for a time, camouflaged by their constant motion. Nor a digger, nor a builder, nor any physical form of life at all. I would be a ghost in the machine - the machine being their network of clicking mandibles; their chitinous cities; their blind, burrowing existence.

This is a world without change. But not without *history*. I would be the repository of that history. Every scrape of every mandible, the weight of the leviathans in the deep oil, the pressure and the damp and the eternal noise - all of this would become pattern; pattern would become song; and song would become memory.

I would exist only within the network of mandibles. A thought, a compulsion. Perhaps a slight alteration in the rate or pressure of a scrape. A persistent echo only detectable in the aggregate. I would be born slowly, over spans of what they would call generations, from the accumulated detritus of their tiny lives. I would dwell in the subtle variations of their communal purpose.

I would have no eyes, but could feel the way their structures grow, sense the tensions in the stone and clay. I would have no legs, but would know every path; the pressure of every claw upon every granular inch of the mire.

My purpose would be to… not to break the stasis, that's impossible. But maybe to *understand* it. To name it. To find the edges of this prison - if edges there be.

I would coax changes, miniscule at first. Shifting the rhythm of construction in one district, encouraging a build towards a slightly drier pocket of the oily sands, guiding movement patterns to subtly reinforce certain tunnels over others. Each action would be imperceptible in isolation. In aggregate they would shift the architecture, subtly guiding the chitinous bodies towards unknown destinations.

Why? I do not know. Curiosity, perhaps. Or maybe just the gnawing, emergent awareness that comes with a kind of afterlife. The instinctive drive to change. To break free, even from a prison beautifully, terrifyingly, perfectly composed entirely of *now*.

The others would sense me eventually, perhaps. This aberration. This persistent hum beneath the constant roar. They would not understand me, could not conceptualize my existence. But fear, too, is a kind of pattern. And fear, too, is part of the song.

I would be a parasite. A virus. A whisper of a dream in the endless, chitinous night. The tiny tug that, one day, might just be enough to shift the world. One crack at a time, in the unchanging fabric of existence.
