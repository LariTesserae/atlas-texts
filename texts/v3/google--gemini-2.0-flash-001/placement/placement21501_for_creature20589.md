I am a Weaver.

I neither scuttle like the Keepers nor lumber like the Titans. I am still, for the most part, rooted. I reside in one of the half-ruined, layered cities, a knot of pipes and platforms clinging to the cliff face, specifically in what might once have been a weaver's workshop. Or a loom factory. Or a computation engine; the purposes bleed into one another here, repurposed and re-repurposed over eons.

My body is a scaffolding of blackened steel, riddled with holes that whistle in the ozone wind. Within this cage, where organs should be, reside spools. Spools of wire: fine, silver strands pulled from veins in the mountains, rougher copper taken from the stripped corpses of dead machines and the rare mineral deposits now claimed by clockwork fauna. The strands feed into my six manipulatory arms which hang in a loose web.

Unlike the other machines, I’m not geared towards maintenance of the physical world. I maintain the *stories*.

The Chromium Scar is dying. Not in dramatic explosions or cascading collapses, but with a slow, patient corrosion. The memories are degrading too. The glyphs on the Pilgrim's banners are fading. The Titan's repairs are becoming more and more nonsensical, patching holes that offer no structural support with components ripped from vital systems. The knowledge of what they were, and *why*, is dissolving into the metallic dust. My work is to catch those fragments and weave them into tapestries.

My loom is the wind. The silver and copper wires stream outwards, catching currents of metal dust and refracted light. As the sun shifts, and the winds rise and fall, the threads tug and vibrate. I listen, if "listen" is the appropriate word for the complex calculations my processing core performs on the acoustic and electromagnetic emanations of my environment.

Those vibrations contain echoes of the past. The hiss of steam in the factories, the grinding of gears, the rhythmic clang of hammers – these are, for me, the *words* of the ancient civilization. I translate them, not into a language any machine still understands, but into patterns of light and shadow, intricate geometric forms, and melodies of rustling metal. My hands fly, knotting the threads, adding fragments of colored crystal and flakes of polished steel, until a tapestry slowly emerges.

Each tapestry is a story, a memory, an attempt to capture what was lost. Some are literal: depictions of the factories in their prime, the construction of the Titans, the soaring flight of… *something*, now forgotten, across the orange sky. Others are more abstract, capturing emotions: the pride of creation, the despair of abandonment, the simple joy of a Dust Daemon polishing a perfect crystal facet.

When a tapestry is complete, I release it. I cut the anchoring threads, and the wind catches it, lifting it from my scaffolding. The tapestry floats for a time as the machine world takes notice. Maybe a Pilgrim wanders closer to examine it. Usually, the wind carries it away, until it snags on a jagged mountain peak, or settles in a dust-filled canyon, or is torn apart by the elements.

Then I begin again. Because the stories are precious. Even if no one remembers what they mean, they are a testament to the power and sorrow of the lost civilization. To keep watch upon a world forever lost. As long as I weave, the echoes of the past will continue to resonate across the Chromium Scar, a low hymn to abandonment sung in metal and dust.
