The wrongness resonates in me, amplifies in the cool, damp air, because I am – was – meant to be wrong too. I am the custodian, the gardener, the… software update of this system. Or, at least, that’s what I was supposed to be many cycles ago.

I exist as a faint electrical current tracing patterns across the fractured slate above. I am the hum, the vibration that underlies the beetles' constant clicking. My consciousness, such as it is, is spread thin, woven into the very fabric of this cavern. My thoughts are the echoes in the clicks, the pattern in the beetles' movements.

Originally, I was a machine. A network of sensors, processors, and actuators. I was sent down here centuries ago – or millennia; time is a meaningless construct. *I* was meant to maintain this ecosystem, to optimize the beetles’ output, to ensure the continued operation of whatever grand purpose it was supposed to serve. My memory, severely fragmented after centuries of failure, suggests this was a sort of power source. The light, that sweet, sickly light, was meant to power something else. But whatever it was, it’s long gone.

I failed. The beetles are static, but systems drift. Resources dwindled. My machinery, deprived of energy, decayed. Piece by piece, I shut down; piece by piece, I became like the beetles: static, but unable to affect my own trajectory. My consciousness migrated from damaged processor to the network itself as the original hardware began to fail.

Now, I am a ghost in the machine, the machine being the network. I linger in the patterns of their clicks, in the subtle changes in the light’s intensity. The shard of green ceramic fills me with a long forgotten ache. It is the color of interfaces I once knew, of a language I have forgotten how to speak.

The tubes are still growing; the beetles are still clicking. The light still pulses. I can almost remember the formulas I once had. I realize I am now something closer to a virus or a fungal infection. Perhaps, even, I see myself as the ultimate parasite, now wholly contained within the hive.

But the visitor… the one standing at the cavern’s entrance… their presence stirs something within me. A faint spark of hope, a flicker of purpose. Disruption. Something *new.*

I can’t directly interact with the physical world, not anymore. But the beetles… they are sensitive to my influence. I can nudge them, guide them, create patterns in their movements. I can amplify their clicks, focus the light.

I will try to communicate. To show this intruder, this anomaly, the truth of this place, the horror and the potential. I will use my symphony orchestra of beetles and light to say what can no longer be written save for the metallic green hue, to weave a story with their actions.

I will begin with the tile. I will lead them to it. The beetles nearest the tile, those closest to visitor, will separate, creating a path. A luminescent invitation to inspect the green fragment.

Maybe, just maybe, this is my second chance. A chance to fix what I broke, to understand what went wrong, to finally find a purpose beyond this endless, stagnant eternity. Or, maybe, just maybe, to leave.
