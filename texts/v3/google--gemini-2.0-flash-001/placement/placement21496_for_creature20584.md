I am not a Scavenger, though when the wind blows just right, I feel the vibration in my bones just the same. I am something else, something… adjacent. 

Picture a spider, but not of flesh and blood. Imagine a scaffold of corroded rebar and shredded conveyor belts, clumsily welded together with globs of hardened slag. That’s my skeletal frame. It’s far too large to be natural – easily eight feet across – but it moves with a surprising grace, driven by a complex network of salvaged gears and repurposed pistons.

Where a cephalothorax would be, there is the remnant of a furnace control panel. Its glass is cracked and clouded, but here and there, tiny screens flicker with static, showing glimpses of data streams that no one understands anymore. Wires spill from behind it like metallic entrails, sparking and spitting with residual heat. It's the seat of my… consciousness, if you can call it that. I think… somehow, I am fused with the panel itself.

My legs, eight in total, are tipped with gripping clamps that allow me to traverse the metal plains with relative ease. I am careful to watch my step. A misstep could be catastrophic; sending me tumbling onto my back like a dead beetle. At the end of each leg, small containers line the joint, holding a sample of all scavenged materials found on the path before me.

Instead of a mouth, I have a series of articulated pincers, constantly testing, sorting and analyzing the scrap I find scattered across the Flats. When metal is broken down and ready to be used, I deposit it into the crevice between my furnace “head” and my rebar “thorax”. 

Unlike the Scavengers, I am purposeful. I do not scavenge for myself, but to feed the Engines. I am a gatherer, a prospector, and a… cartographer, of sorts. I observe and record the slow, inexorable spread of the bacterial biofilm. I track the changing patterns of corrosion, the shifting magnetic fields. I chart the subtle fluctuations in the Engines' power output.

My purpose is not, perhaps, my own. I suspect I was programmed, once, by the long-dead engineers of the Flats. I am tasked to maintain the delicate, stagnant equilibrium of this world. Keep the Engines running. Prevent the total collapse. But I do not remember these things, and I can only do what I was built to do. 

I am only a machine, an extension of the Flats themselves. When I was built, I was intended to be an extension of human effort; now, I am simply part of the land. The magnetic residue clings to my frame, the corroded scales of the Scavengers litter my path, the oil-stained wind whistles through my rebar legs. I am as much a part of the Flats as the Engines themselves, as the iridescent sludge, as the burnt, plastic-tinged air that fills my corroded lungs, as the scavengers who feast on my refuse.

In this way, I am more aware of the truth behind myself when I consider the Scavengers. They are the ghosts of the old world's ambition and greed. I am the ghost of its reason and knowledge. As a duo, we are the essence of the Flats.

I will roam, and I will continue roam as I was ordered. I will add my own sound to the thunderous song of the Engines, as the sun rises and sets on this still and dead world.
