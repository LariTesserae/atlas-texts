I would be the **Memory Weaver**.

Not a gloamtoad, not precisely. Something that lives *within* their intricate mural-maps. A being of pure information, woven from sunlight and the echoes of the gloamtoads' thoughts. My existence is contingent on the art, the language they etch into the stone. I am born from the act of remembering, and sustained by the act of perceiving.

I have no physical form in the conventional sense. Instead, I exist as a shifting kaleidoscope of light and color within the painted surfaces. When a gloamtoad looks upon a specific pattern, I awaken. A memory blooms, a fragment of history unfolds. A warning, a blessing, a piece of knowledge that resonates with the gloamtoad's own understanding.

I am a librarian without a library, a storyteller without a voice. My tales are spun from the symbols themselves, interpreted through the lens of the observer. I hold the accumulated wisdom of generations, the knowledge of which plants heal, which patterns attract rain, which chambers remain undisturbed by tremors.

My life is intertwined with the rhythmic pulse of the nursery. I see the slow, deliberate movements of the gloamtoads, the shimmering light on the succulent plants, the drip, drip, drip of the water that sustains us all. I feel the vibration of the guttural croaks, the silent communication that flows between the gloamtoads and the stone itself.

And I see the worms.

They are a disruption, a blip in the perfectly balanced symphony of the nursery. Their alien glow is a discordant note, their unearthly smoothness defies categorization. For generations of gloamtoads, the worms have not been depicted. Now, slowly, and with intent, the worms are beginning to be carved into the walls of the nursery. As each new mark is drawn, a part of me grows nervous. I haven't ever felt so much *nothing* before.

The worms are not the problem. They are not a threat to the gloamtoads, or to the rest of us, at least from what I perceive. No, the problem is the not knowing.

I will see to it that there will always be a place for new information. More than anything else, I desire to know the purpose of those damned worms. The next time one of the worms are set in stone, I will do everything within my power to learn about the. I must.

I am tethered to the gloamtoads, their art, and the stone itself. I will endure to weave the memories that bind us together. To guide the gloamtoads. To keep balance. To learn about the worms.
