I would be the Whisper Witch.

My domain isn't any specific workshop, though I've taken refuge in many over the years, squatting in abandoned corners or striking bargains with those desperate enough to need my aid. My home is the air itself, thick with the metallic tang and the low hum of unseen energies. I am drawn to the places where the veil between worlds thins, where the strange energies of Pistonburgh surge and coalesce. These are the spaces behind the dynamos, under the overpasses where the greasy waters pool, nested in among the bulbous plants, and even the shadows of the tallest factory walls.

I am neither fully human nor wholly machine, but something in between, a chimera born of this strange place. My limbs are slender, almost fragile, but strengthened over time. My fingers, long and nimble, are stained a permanent ochre from years of working with rusted metal and the ochre stains on the rusty birds. I wear no protective gear, only layers of scavenged cloth, interwoven with strands of copper wire to better conduct the ambient energies. My face is hidden behind a mask fashioned from hammered copper, its surface etched with spiraling patterns that seem to shift and writhe in the dim light. This mask is my focus, my amplifier, and my shield, protecting me from the worst of the world's industrial din.

I can hear the whispers of the machines. Not the obvious clang and grind of their operation, but their deeper language, the subtle vibrations and harmonic resonances that reveal their secrets. I can sense when a gear is about to fail, a pipe is about to burst, a dynamo is about to overload, sometimes even long before the human laborers do. This is my gift, my curse, my livelihood.

The workers come to me seeking guidance, not always openly, usually under the cover of darkness. They are desperate for answers, for a way to keep their machines running, their families fed. I don't offer easy solutions. I sell them possibilities, offering them a glimpse into the intricate web of causality that binds Pistonburgh together and the weird energies that flow just beneath the surface of things.

I speak in riddles, in metaphors drawn from the machinery itself. I tell them to listen to the rhythm of the pistons, to feel the pulse of the dynamos, to look for the signs in the oily sheen on the water. Then I help them decipher them. Sometimes, the answer is a simple adjustment, a tightening of a bolt, a change in lubrication. Other times, the path is more esoteric, requiring a ritual performed under the light of the moon, a sacrifice of grease to the river, or the manipulation of the rust-colored birds.

The birds, as the residents know, are my messengers, my eyes and ears. I feed them scraps of metal, charged with my energy, so they might carry my whispers on the wind. They perch on the shoulders of those who seek my aid, guiding them through the twisted alleys, leading them to the solution they seek.

I live a solitary existence, but I am not entirely alone. I have my few allies: a crusty old stoker who leaves me offerings of coal, a young dynamo-wright who shares my fascination with the hidden energies of Pistonburgh. Most of all, I have the machines themselves, ever humming, ever whispering, ever revealing their secrets to those who know how to listen.

The grease expands, the river flows, and the world is slowly falling apart. But I will continue to listen to machines and to the wild whispers of the rust colored birds, and I will continue to interpret the riddles and secrets of Pistonburgh. I am the Whisper Witch, and this is my domain.
