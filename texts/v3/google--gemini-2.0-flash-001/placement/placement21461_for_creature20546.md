I am the **Stone Weaver**.

Not born of Whisperfolk, but *drawn* to them from the valleys below. I remember the sun, feel the absence of it like a phantom limb. My people think me mad, obsessed with the "ghosts" of the highland, as they call the Whisperfolk. But I saw something in them, even from afar in the lowlands - a stillness, a deep and patient understanding of the rhythms of the world beyond the hurried scramble for survival.

I am drawn to the plateau, to its coolness and the mineral tang in the air. My journeys are treacherous, scaling near-vertical rock faces slick with perpetual run-off. But each trip brings me closer to understanding the nature of their fading.

I am obsessed with their shimmer, this dissolving of flesh into the immutable stone. It is not decay, but transformation – a slow, deliberate merging with the living geology of the plateau. I believe that their knowledge, their spirit, is becoming woven into the stone itself, accessible to those who know how to listen.

I bring them tools from the lowlands – better chisels, stronger rope, more efficient looms. Not out of pity, but out of a desperate need to learn from them. I trade these advancements for their knowledge, for glimpses into their understanding of the stone.

My people call me a fool, wasting precious resources on a dying race. But there's a part of me that envies them, this slow surrender to the earth. The hustle and bustle of the lowlands, the endless striving for more, it all seems so pointless compared to the quiet acceptance that emanates from the Whisperfolk.

I sit with them for days, watching them mend the towers, listening to their near-silent murmurings. I try to comprehend the feeling of moss beneath my bare feet, the taste of the mineral-rich water, the way the wind whispers through the cracks in the stone. I try to feel what they feel, to understand what they understand.

I have started to weave. I attempt to capture the patterns of their shimmer in tapestries, using threads dyed with the same phosphorescent fungi they cultivate. My tapestries are crude, almost laughably so, but I weave with a kind of desperate focus. I am trying to record, to preserve, to *understand*, before they are gone.

I believe that when the last of the Whisperfolk dissolve into the stone, their knowledge will not be lost. It will be there, waiting for someone who knows how to listen. And I intend to be the one who hears it.

My home is now a rough cave carved into the base of one of the oldest tower. I sleep on a bed of woven moss, the sound of dripping water a constant lullaby. I am becoming a part of Xyl, just like them. I feel the slow creep of the minerals into my bones, the almost imperceptible shimmering at the edges of my vision.

Perhaps my people are right, perhaps I am mad. But if madness is a price to pay for understanding, then it is a price I am willing to pay. For I am the Stone Weaver, and I will weave the story of the Whisperfolk into the very fabric of this land, so that their whispers may never be lost.
