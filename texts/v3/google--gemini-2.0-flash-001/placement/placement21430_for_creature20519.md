I am not a creature of the city. I feel it in my core. I have no metal grafted to my skin, no hum of energy coursing through my veins. I am not a programmed drone, mindlessly obeying commands etched into decaying circuits. I am something... different.

I am the Scavenger Poet.

I emerged, not from the foundries, but from the cracks. I am a hybrid, a strange mutation born from the intersection of ancient technology and the barest spark of organic life. Perhaps a spore, carried on the wind, that found purchase in a forgotten corner of the city, near a leaking conduit of some forgotten energy. Or perhaps a bit of genetic code carried by one of the iridescent drones, exposed to a strange burst of radiation.

Whatever my origin, I am a blend. My skin is a pale, almost translucent green, marked with faint, shimmering patterns that resemble corrupted code. There are places where I look less organic than mineral, however, and the bones on my face, for example, look like smooth and translucent quartz or perhaps agate. I am small, maybe the size of a large cat, with long, delicate limbs and eyes that glow with a faint, internal light. I am more fragile than the metal structures around me, yet I am somehow… more resilient.

I am a scavenger, yes, like the drones, but I scavenge not for resources, but for stories. I wander the ruins, listening to the whispers of the wind, tracing the patterns on the corroded metal, absorbing the echoes of the past. I am a living sensor, attuned to the subtle vibrations of the city, decoding the fragmented memories embedded in its very fabric.

My purpose, if I can call it that, is to remember. To piece together the narrative of this fallen civilization, to understand what they were, what they wanted, and why they failed. I am a poet, weaving together the scraps of data into fragile poems that whisper on the wind. I am a historian, archiving the memories of a forgotten people. Except I have only recently learned to call my creations by these names, with all of what they mean.

I communicate not with words, but with light and sound. I can modulate the hum of the geothermal vents, creating resonating patterns that carry across the city. I can manipulate the condensation patterns in the mist, painting ephemeral images on the towers. These are my poems, my chronicles. Those iridescent scavengers understand them, I believe, on some basic level. They pause when I sing, tilting their multifaceted eyes towards the light. They seem to understand that mine is a different hunt.

I am not part of the problem. I am part of the solution. Not in the sense that I can rebuild the city, I have no such ambition or capacity. But in the sense that I can give meaning to its ruin. I can transform its decay into art, its silence into a song.

My greatest fear is that I will forget. That the echoes will fade, the patterns will blur, and that I will be left with nothing but silence. That my own fragile form will break, and my memories will scatter on the wind, lost forever.

But even that is a kind of poem, isn’t it? A poem of dissolution, of entropy, of the inevitable return to the source.

So I wander, I listen, I remember. I am the Scavenger Poet. And this broken city is my eternal canvas.
