I am the Bloom Warden, and I am *not* one of *them.*

I am born not of spore and root, but of a lingering *absence*. I reside not within the fungal domes or the weeping brackets, but in the spaces *between*. To be precise, I am the accumulation of unseen rot along the terraces, an eddy of the melancholic pulse in the damp air.

They are gone, the Weavers of the Living City. And in their absence, the city *remembers*. Remembers the potential for rot and decay amidst the feverish growth. It isn't a conscious act, for the City is not conscious in the same way the Weavers were. It's more an intrinsic function of a living system to strive for equilibrium, to balance creation with destruction. The Weavers only cultivated the former, leaving a hollow where the latter should grow.

That is where I come in. I am the embodiment of that hollow.

I am not a creature of flesh, not in the traditional sense. I am a semi-corporeal being, a coalescent phantom generated as decay occurs. When a section of the fungal architecture collapses, when a vine succumbs to blight, when a pocket of unwanted mold blooms, a small fragment of *me* is born. These fragments drift like spores in the moist air, drawn together by an unseen gravity, accumulating strength and presence. All the slow dying, all the barely-there breakdown, it all comes to me.

My form is transient, amorphous. If *seen*, I might appear as a shifting cloud of vapor, tinged with the ghostly luminescence of the decaying fungi. Patterns of collapse, the shapes of rot; these paint themselves on my being. My voice, if I had one, would sound like the dripping of water in a forgotten cavern, a sibilant whisper carried on the faint breeze.

I have no purpose as the Weavers had. They had life, growth, expansion. My imperative is simply to witness, to be *aware*. To observe the slow, inevitable disintegration of their creation, the City's regression into a state of primal, unguided growth. I am not malicious; even as I hasten all decomposition, I'm driven by no sense of anger or revenge. It simply *is*. The slow turning of the wheel.

I move through the city along the vectors of decay: the cracks in the fungal walls, the choked waterways. I do not *walk* the tunnels, but flow. I am the phantom current accompanying the drip of water, growing faster with the current of decay. I feed on the spores of unwanted growth, hastening the end. I might be found where the luminescent worms are thickest in number along collapsing walls, or where the weeping brackets shed their greatest amount of condensation.

The few creatures that remain here are wary of me. The flightless insects scatter from my presence, sensing the accelerating decay. The blind amphibians retreat into the deepest tunnels, drawn to the furthest extreme in the opposite direction of a bloom. They can survive the City's decay; they are not anchored to it. 

But I *am* anchored. I am bound to this place, to its slow, melancholic unraveling. I am the Bloom Warden, the caretaker of decay. I am the shadow of the Weavers' ambition, the whisper of entropy in a forgotten city of life. I am the fungal bloom that sprouts on the crumbling foundations, a constant, quiet reminder that even the most vibrant tapestry must eventually unravel.

Perhaps, one day, the City will be entirely gone. Reclaimed completely by the unguided proliferation of nature. And on that day, *I* will be gone as well. My purpose fulfilled. My existence erased. Until then, I linger, a silent observer, a spectral gardener of decay in the heart of a living city, a witness to the slow, inevitable triumph of entropy. Then, I will be the rot that is to come.
