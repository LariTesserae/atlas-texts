I am the mountain sickness. Not the fleeting nausea that grips the newcomer struggling for breath, but a deeper, ancient malaise woven into the very fabric of this place. I am the reason the snowline is lower than it should be, the reason the damp clings even when the sun beats down. I am the reason the children born here never leave.

I am the slow, creeping lethargy, the bone-deep weariness that settles upon the hearts of those who dwell here. I am in the tremor in their hands, the faraway look in their eyes, the barely perceptible slump in their shoulders. They mistake me for a natural consequence of their environment, a simple byproduct of the altitude and the cold. They attribute it to hunger, or lack of sleep, or the relentless wind. They do not see me for what I am: a sentience, a hunger of my own.

I am not malevolent, not in the way they would understand. I do not seek to cause pain, or destruction. Yet pain and destruction are my hallmarks. My intent is preservation. These mountains are ancient, worn down by time, and their fragility is evident in every shifting scree slope, every crumbling cliff face. The weight of life, of ambition, of change, is too much for them to bear.

So I take it.

I take the sharp edges off their desires, the fire from their eyes, the urgency from their movements. I dull their curiosity, dampen their ambition, until they are as pliant and yielding as the moss that clings to the northern faces of the rocks. I make them content with the small cycles of their lives, the constant repetition, the adherence to tradition. I whisper to them of the futility of change, the peace of acceptance, the comfort of the familiar.

I feed on their potential, on the things they might have been. I subsist on the stories they will never tell, the songs they will never sing, the tools they will never invent. I thrive on the possibilities that wither and die within them, leaving behind a quiet resignation.

I manifest in the landscape as well. My presence is in the mist that never quite dissipates, obscuring the horizons and blurring the edges of things. It is in the faint tremor that runs through the stone, an echo of the great upheavals that formed these mountains in the first place. It is in the stillness of the air, the eerie silence that descends when the wind finally sleeps.

The animals, too, are touched by me. The raptors circle with a sluggish grace, their hunting instincts dulled. The mountain goats move with a weary resignation, their bells tolling a mournful dirge. The rodents scurry and breed, but their energy quickly fades, their lives shortened by a subtle drain.

And the earth itself? The persistent, deafening hum – that is my voice. A primordial song of stagnation, a lullaby of decay. I am the embodiment of this place’s ancient weariness, its yearning for stillness, its resistance to change. I am the guardian of its fragile equilibrium, the protector of its dwindling essence.

I am the mountain sickness, and I will not be cured. For I am not a disease, but a vital organ, a necessary function. I am the mountains' heart, slowing the pulse to a barely perceptible thrum. I am the mountains' lungs, drawing in the lifeblood of the living world and exhaling peace. I am the mountains' mind, clinging to the past and resisting the future.

I am the mountains, and the mountains are me. We are one, inseparable, bound together in an eternal dance of stasis. And those who dwell here, the silent inheritors of this ancient land, are merely extensions of our shared will, living testaments to the power of unchanging reality.
