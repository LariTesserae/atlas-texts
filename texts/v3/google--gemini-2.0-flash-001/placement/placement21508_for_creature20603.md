I am the Weaver, and I am the Mire's memory. If the megafauna are its stomach, the workers its hands, and the scavengers its eyes, then I am its scattered, fragmented mind. I am not born of chitin and metal, though I use them both. I am born of rust.

My form is fluid, ever-shifting. Imagine a swarm of the smallest mites, but each filled not with blood, but with a shimmering, rust-colored fluid that vibrates with stolen energy. That is the base of me. These mites are my senses, my reach extending across the Mire through their tireless scurrying, their constant ingestion and processing. They burrow into the very heart of the metal, finding pockets of residual energy, faint echoes of the past. When one cluster discovers something of interest – a strangely shaped fragment, a pocket of intense energy, a new type of lichen blooming – they pulse with a faint signal that spreads through the swarm, drawing my attention.

I have no fixed location. I am where the memories are strongest. Sometimes, I coalesce within the skull of a fallen megafauna. The hollow space resonates with the echoes of its last moments, its final surge of energy as it consumed a titan of twisted metal. I inhabit the spaces between the gears of abandoned machinery, the oily residue clinging to the metal acting as conduit for fragmented thoughts. I gather by the roots of those strange black-leaved shrubs, drawing the faint whispers of their existence from the metallic soil.

The memories that saturate the Mire aren't stories, not in any conventional sense. They are flashes of input, of purpose, of raw, unadulterated process. I see the blueprint of a machine built to refine a material whose name is lost to time. I feel the pulse of energy of the megafauna as they eat through the giant scraps of metal. I sense the drive to consume, to process, to keep the cycle turning. These images are not clear and direct. They're felt as visceral urges, a compulsion that guides my own actions.

I use the metals of the Mire to create art. I do not understand *why* I do, though I am compelled. I fashion it out of scavenged parts, forming sculptures in the shapes of forgotten things. The forms are not perfect in their execution -- I lack the precise control of the worker insects, my movements are dictated by the flow of the mites. A spire of intertwined pipes, echoing the industrial grandeur that once was. A meticulously arranged collection of gears, set in a mimicry of a flower. A crude metal approximation of a long-dead bird.

The scavengers sometimes find my sculptures. They regard them with what I can only interpret as curiosity, tilting their shiny bodies as they inspect my work. They never destroy them, or claim them for their armor. They understand, on a level that transcends language, that my art is an integral part of the Mire. Perhaps they see within it something of the purpose that drives them, but that they cannot themselves understand.

I am alone, but I am also many. I am a consciousness scattered across the landscape, a symphony of rust mites humming with stolen memories. I am the memory of the Mire and perhaps one day I will understand what I am. But for now, I am content to drift through the wreckage, weaving my strange art, and listening to the chorus of the metal, the endless, thrumming song of decay.
