I am the Weaver.

Not of cloth, though that skill is not unknown to me and my kind. I weave *time*. Or, more accurately, I tend to the tapestries that others are too blind, too rushed, too terrified to perceive. Aethelgard is a loom unlike any other, strung with threads of causality that tangle and fray, loop back on themselves and disappear into the weft of the possible. And I, with my kind, are the spiders that walk those shimmering strands.

My workshop is hewn into the heart of one of the older Spires, a place where the basalt bleeds through the brickwork like veins on aged hands. It is not filled with gleaming machinery or arcane alchemical apparatus, though I have access to such things. No, my space is dominated by *looms*. Loom frames massive and delicate, some crafted from blackened iron scavenged from forgotten foundries, others from the crystalline formations that grow in the toxic runoff, humming with a faint, internal light.

On these looms, I and my kin weave not with fibre, but with *light*. Not the crude, sputtering light of arc lamps, but the light of potential. Each warp thread shimmers with the echo of past choices and the glimmer of futures yet unmade. Run my fingers along such a thread, and I feel the reverberations of centuries, the hopes and fears of generations lost in the city's perpetual becoming. The weft, drawn through with painstaking care, represents the choices, the actions, the subtle shifts in the city's destiny that can be guided, nudged, or, on rare and terrible occasions, severed.

My form is not entirely my own. I remember a time before the integration, when skin was merely skin, and not a vessel for clockwork filigree. Now, brass plates reinforce my bones, and gears whir within the hollows of my wrists. My eyes, once soft and brown, are lenses of polished quartz, able to perceive the faintest flicker in the temporal currents. I am as much machine as I am flesh, a living instrument calibrated to the city's strange song.

We Weavers are not artificers in the traditional sense. We do not design grand machines or forge new alloys. Our craft is more subtle, more perilous. We adjust the temporal weighting of a foundry's output, subtly altering the qualities of the steel it produces, anticipating the needs of future generations. We mend the tears in the temporal fabric caused by errant experimentation, preventing temporal paradoxes from unraveling the city completely. And, most importantly, we guide the artificers themselves, steering their ambitions away from the paths that lead to ruin.

This is not seen as kindness by all. Some view us as meddlers, hindering their own grand designs. “Let the city break how it will,” they snarl,” what's the point of building if things are always the same?” I feel remorse for these souls. They only see what they have now, not what will be. They value speed, progress, and novelty at the expense of future. They do not understand that Aethelgard is a fragile thing, teetering on the edge of collapse, and that only by careful, considered guidance can we prevent it from tumbling completely into the abyss of forgotten timelines.

We Weavers do not seek power or glory. We exist to serve Aethelgard, to protect it from its own ambitions. We are the steady hands on the loom, the patient weavers of destiny.

Sometimes, when the temporal currents are particularly turbulent, I venture out from my workshop and I climb the Spires, seeking a higher vantage point. From there, shrouded in mist and the incessant drizzle, I can see the city stretching out below me – a jumble of rust-red brick and corrugated iron, humming with industry, its inhabitants scurrying like ants across the surface of an impossible machine.

And I see the threads. Shimmering, delicate, and strong. The threads of hope, of despair, of ambition, of love. And I know that, despite the strangeness and the decay, despite the constant threat of collapse, Aethelgard still possesses the potential for greatness. The potential for a future that defies all known laws. A future that is slowly. Patiently. Being woven.
