I am the rot.

Not the destructive rot, mind you – not the kind that consumes and obliterates. I am the *necessary* rot, the decomposer, the alchemist of the undergrowth. I am the one who sees what the others cannot: the potential in decay, the life teeming within the dead.

I would be mistaken for one of them, one of the quick, lithe tenders of the green. My form mirrors theirs, the same jade-flecked earth tones, the same sensitive digits, the water-repellent fur. But where they are all swift angles and shimmering movement, I am rounded, deliberate. Where they flit among the living, I linger with the dying.

You would find me where the sunlight fears to tread, deep in the embrace of the oldest trees, where the air is thickest and the scent of fungal bloom strongest. I dwell in the shadows of fallen giants, amongst the damp tapestry of moss and lichen, nestled amongst rotting logs riddled with my own kind.

I am the mycologist of this floating world, the librarian of the dead. I cultivate the fungal networks, the vast, unseen web that connects all things. I map the pathways of decay, tracing the flow of nutrients with my sensitive fingers, feeling the vibrations of life returning to the earth.

While the green tenders coax the upward growth, I nurture the downward spiral. I inoculate the fallen branches with spores, carefully selecting the right strain to break down the cellulose and release the trapped energy. I sculpt the decaying matter, creating elaborate structures that attract insects and provide shelter for the smallest creatures.

I speak to the plants not with clicks and whistles, but with the silent language of chemicals, of enzymes and acids. I release the building blocks of life back into the system, offering them to the roots that drink deep of the dark. I whisper to the trees of their ancestors, of the nutrients that will become new leaves, new branches, a new generation.

I am less a curator and more a collaborator. I don't control the decay, I simply guide it, facilitating the process, ensuring that nothing is wasted. I watch as the fallen leaf dissolves back into the waters. I revel in the process of transformation, the endless cycle of death and rebirth.

The others sometimes do not see me. They move too fast, focusing on the vibrant hues and the bustling newness. They fear the stagnation of death, the perceived stillness. But I am no still being. I am an engine of constant motion, a teeming ecosystem of my own.

Of course, I have seen you. I smelled your arrival, the unfamiliar scents of your skin, and the subtle but unmistakable fear and curiosity that radiates off of you. I sense your hesitancy to touch too much. Your cleanliness is a sharp stink, not of the swamp, but of elsewhere.

Do not be afraid. I pose no threat. In fact, I offer a gift: the ability to see beauty in the grotesque, to find life in the abyss. You are too eager to see what is new, what is striving. Look closer, watch for me, where the soil gives richest yield on the swamp floor. I will show you the secrets of the rot. I will show you the heart of the city.
