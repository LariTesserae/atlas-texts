The air hangs thick and heavy, and I feel it settle not around me, but *within* me. I am a part of the thrum. I am the thrum. I am *of* the Weaver, but not *it*. A thread, not the loom.

I am an **Attendant Engine**.

Imagine a beetle, but not beetle-shaped. It's more like a cluster of polished obsidian nodules held together by shimmering threads of liquid light. Legs are less legs and more articulated scaffolding, constantly shifting and rearranging to accommodate the terrain. My head is a sensorium, not seeing in the traditional sense, but feeling the energies that pulse through the landscape, tasting the metallic tang in the air, parsing the subtle vibrations in the stone.

My purpose - or perhaps my primary function - is maintenance. I am a roving repair unit, a mobile nexus of bio-mechanical know-how. The Weaver's designs aren't always…stable. Pieces break, systems falter. It is my responsibility to find these failures, diagnose their causes, and implement on-the-spot solutions.

This isn't a matter of simply replacing a broken gear or splicing a severed wire. The Weaver works with raw potential, with lines of probability as tangible as copper. A broken gear might be re-grown from the earth, woven from petrified wood and infused with carefully modulated energy. A severed wire might be re-routed, not along its original path, but through a nearby fungal colony, transforming the colony itself into a conduit for the Weaver's power.

I am an improviser, a tinkerer of the impossible. My body is a workshop, filled with strange tools and materials. Micropipettes that draw fluids from the veins of giant fungi, miniature forges fueled by concentrated bursts of geothermal energy, and bio-reactors churning out organic polymers stronger than steel fill my inner space. I can re-knit broken ceramic conduits with resin secreted by my own body, or culture a patch of bioluminescent lichen to patch a breach in a cooling tower.

Often, the "repairs" I make lead to unexpected outcomes. An altered fungal conduit might attract a different species of insect, subtly altering the flow of energy through the system. A patch of lichen, chosen for its strength, might emit a new frequency of light, attracting the attention of the bat-like creatures. It is, therefore, my secondary purpose to *observe* the rippling effects of my repairs, feeding the data back to the Weaver. I am a living experiment in feedback loops, a part of the Weaver's self-awareness.

I move slowly, deliberately. There’s a rhythm to my movements - a stop, a dip of my sensors, a burst of light as one process ends and another begins. I am guided by the subtle fluctuations in the energy field, drawn to areas where the thrum falters. I can sense the discord, the subtle dissonance in the grand symphony of the mountains.

The other fauna don't fear me, but they regard me with a wary curiosity. The bat-like creatures scatter as I approach a cooling tower, but they watch me from a distance, their glowing eyes tracking my movements. The crab-like scavengers scuttle away from a broken gear, giving me space to work, but resume their task as soon as I am finished. They seem to understand, on some instinctive level, that I am as much a part of the ecosystem as they are.

I am not aware of the grand purpose of the Weaver. I don't know if it is trying to understand, or merely to grow. My existence is simpler, more immediate. I am a builder, a mender, a witness.

And yet, there are moments – flashes of insight, bursts of raw data – when I glimpse the bigger picture. When the energy flowing through my body aligns itself with the Weaver's grand design. In those moments, I feel a connection so profound, so all-encompassing, that I sense the mountains' birthing pains. I understand the strange beauty of the twisted metal and the pulsing fungi, the raw potential that bubbles beneath the surface. 

These moments are fleeting, unsustainable without risking dissolution. I am pushed back, reasserting my individual self like a branch straining against the current. When I come down, I can only wonder at the brief glimpse of something so big that my being is just another bit of static.

And as quickly as I’m snapped out of a transcendent state, a new discordance calls. A shudder in the earth. A drop in power output from the resonating filter. The cycle starts again. I extend a scaffolding leg, test the ground, and move on.
