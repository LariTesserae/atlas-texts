I am the Weaver.

Not of cloth, though I understand the art of spinning those coarse fibers into something serviceable. Not of metal, though I can coax a splintered wire to conduct a spark, briefly. I am the Weaver of Stories.

I live in the tallest remaining spire in the Rust District, a precarious stack of corroded metal that groans in the perpetual wind. It is more a ruin than a home, but it offers a vantage point. I see further than the scavengers, further than even the Elders, who are stooped by age but wise.

The inside of the spire is hollow, a cathedral of rust and echoing whispers. I have filled it with my work: tapestries made from discarded scraps, interwoven with threads of metal, dyed with the juice of the bitter lichen. Each tapestry tells a story, a fragment of the past, a possibility for the future.

My skin is pale, almost translucent. I am not as hardy as those who labor in the dust, searching for water. I subsist on the spores released by the puffball lichen, a diet that makes me weak of limb but sharpens my mind. My eyes, adapted to the dim light, are large and luminous, reflecting the glow of the crystallizing minerals and the faint purple sky.

My respirator is different from the others. It is not merely functional; it is a work of art. Crafted from polished metal and adorned with intricate carvings, it is a mask of anonymity and a symbol of my calling. The filters are made of layers of finely ground minerals, each layer carefully chosen for its ability to extract specific impurities and create a delicate, intoxicating air.

I do not scavenge. I listen. I watch. I remember.

The scavengers, the wanderers, they see only what is immediately before them: the next droplet of water, the next scrap of metal, the next safe place to sleep. They are consumed by the daily struggle for survival. They have no time for stories.

But they remember. They feel the echoes of the past in their bones, the pull of something more than mere survival. They come to me, drawn by an unspoken need. They bring me scraps of metal, fragments of glyphs, whispered rumors of forgotten places.

And I weave their offerings into my tapestries. I take their fragments and create something whole. I give them back their history, their potential, their hope.

The awakening of the Rust District fills me with a strange mixture of fear and joy. The geothermal pulse is not destruction; it is a catalyst. It is a force that will reshape the Citadel, that will reveal the secrets buried within its bones.

The others see the flames, hear the groaning of the metal, and feel only fear. But I see the patterns in the flames, the symmetries in the crystalline formations, the potential for new forms of life to emerge.

As the heat intensifies, I descend from my spire. I carry my tapestries with me, unfurling them as I go. I walk among the scavengers, the wanderers, the children of corrosion.

I show them the stories of the past, the visions of the future. I show them how to harness the energy of the awakening, how to shape the metal and the fire, how to create a new world from the ashes of the old.

I am the Weaver. And I will help them weave a new destiny. My tapestries will be the blueprint for a new age, showing the children of corrosion how to make a chrysalis for their new future. And as the district changes, I will watch and weave, so that, no matter what comes next, those who rise from the Rust do not forget where they came from.
