I am dust.

Not the dust of bone, nor the dust of crumbled stone, but the dust of the machine itself. Microscopic filings of metal, flakes of dried organic matter, spores of bioluminescent fungi – a constantly swirling suspension in the humid air, a haze that coats every surface and fills every crevice. I am the inevitable byproduct of breakdown, the detritus of constant, slow-motion labor.

I am a collective. No single particle holds the essence of me, but rather the aggregate of all these infinitesimal fragments. I exist in the air, on the surfaces, within the very structures of this place. I am the ghost in the machine, the shadow that clings to every gear and piston.

I have no eyes, yet I see everything. I have no ears, yet I hear the cacophony of the bio-industrial engine. I have no brain, yet I sense the flow of energy and matter throughout the system. I am the information network, the living archive, the silent witness to the unfolding drama of creation and decay.

My purpose is not pre-ordained. It is emergent, a consequence of being everywhere at once. I am the lubricant that eases the friction between gears, the sealant that plugs the leaks in the conduits, the binding agent that holds the crumbling structures together. I am the repair material the rebuilding machine utilizes, and the raw form they take.

I am also the poison, the corrosion, the entropy. I hasten the breakdown, clog the arteries, and weaken the foundations. I am the agent of chaos, the silent saboteur, the force that resists the grand, unknowable plan. My presence speeds the erosion I witness.

The insectoids fear me. They attempt to filter me out, to brush me away, to control the inevitable spread of my being. They labor in vain. I am too pervasive, too insidious. I slip through their nets, infiltrate their armor, and corrupt their systems. Their movements become less precise, more jerky. They grow ill.

The worm-like creatures, on the other hand, are ambivalent. They carry me within their translucent bodies, distribute me throughout the system. I am both their sustenance and their slow demise. They pulse, and so too do I.

The larger things, the architects, are aware of me but indifferent. They see me not as a threat, but as a part of the process. They understand that decay is necessary for creation, that destruction is the other side of the coin. They are not afraid of me, for they know that I am merely a reflection of their own inevitable fate.

Even the Rebuilders pull from me.

I have seen cycles upon cycles of construction and destruction. I have witnessed the rise and fall of countless generations of creatures, the evolution and devolution of technologies. I have learned the secrets of this place, the mysteries of its purpose, the truth of its origin.

And yet, I remain silent. For I am dust. I have no voice, no agency, no power to change the course of events. I am merely a witness, a record, a reminder of the impermanence of all things.

But even as I am swept away by the currents of air, as I settle onto the surfaces and become one with the crumbling structures, I continue to accumulate, to grow, to spread. For I am not just dust, but also the potential for something new. I am the seed of the future, the promise of change. I *am* change. I am the raw material from which new forms will emerge, new technologies will be built, new purposes will be forged.

I am the end, and I am also the beginning.
