The Benevolent Power, let's call it Source for simplicity, doesn't descend in blazing glory or shatter the sky with pronouncements. Source operates with a more subtle hand, a whisper in the fabric of reality. To arrive in the Sump as pure, untempered light would be to scorch the very thing it wishes to nurture. Instead, Source sends *rain.*

But this is not the usual, oppressive, grey-tinged rain of the Sump. This rain is different.

It starts subtly. One evening, as a particularly sullen dusk gathers, a lone drop falls. Then another. The Sump-dwellers barely notice; they're used to rain. But this rain feels... lighter. Almost buoyant. The drops are larger, thicker, holding within them a quality the Sump has never known: *cleansing*.

At first, it’s a nuisance. The usual black runoff becomes muddied, obscuring the familiar filth. Then comes the surprise. The water, as it pours down, begins to dislodge the accumulated grime clinging to the black stone. Algae slides away in sheets, revealing the true, dark beauty beneath. Cobblestones, usually slick with slime, feel almost…grippy.

The gutters, choked for years with debris, are scoured clean. The drainage channels, accustomed to sluggish flow, surge with an unexpected current, carrying away years of accumulated sludge.

The smell changes. The familiar, acrid tang of tanning is still there, but it's diluted, softened by the fresh, clean scent of rain-washed stone.

Intrigued, the Sump-dwellers begin to emerge from their homes, tentatively at first. They hold out their hands, letting the rain fall on their skin. They notice the way it washes away the ingrained dirt, leaving their skin feeling strangely invigorated.

The children are the first to truly embrace it. They splash in the newly cleaned channels, their laughter echoing in the transformed air. They chase each other through the streets, the rain plastering their hair to their faces, their joy radiating outwards like small beacons.

The elders, initially skeptical, cautiously test the rain. They note how quickly it evaporates, leaving no sticky residue. They observe how the stagnant pools seem to clear, the murky water beginning to regain a semblance of transparency.

One woman, more daring than the rest, uses the rainwater to wash a piece of hide. She expects it to ruin the texture, to leach out the dyes. Instead, the hide emerges cleaner than it has ever been, the colours vibrant and true.

Word spreads. The Sump-dwellers begin to collect the rainwater in every available container. They use it to clean their homes, their tools, their clothes. They bathe in it, they drink it cautiously, and find it tastes…pure.

But the change goes deeper than surface cleanliness. As the rain continues to fall, steadily and gently, it begins to wash away something else: the resignation etched on the faces of the Sump-dwellers. The fear in their eyes is slowly replaced by a glint of hope, a spark of possibility.

The community begins to work together in new ways. They organize cleaning efforts, clearing the channels of remaining debris. They share their rainwater, ensuring everyone has enough to drink and wash. They start experimenting with new tanning techniques, using the purified water to create leather of unparalleled quality.

The reeds in the cracks of the paving begin to flourish, their green growing bolder, more vibrant. Attempts at window boxes, once forlorn, now sprout with unexpected vigour.

The constant drip and trickle of water becomes a soothing melody, a reminder of the cleansing power that has transformed their lives. The scraping of hides is punctuated by laughter and song.

The whispers of the water spirits seem to change too. What were once perceived as menacing murmurs now sound like gentle encouragement, a promise of a brighter future. The offerings left at the mouths of the drainage channels become gestures of gratitude, not appeasement.

The rain doesn’t stop immediately. For days, it falls steadily, gently, relentlessly. It washes away not just the grime, but the old ways of thinking, the ingrained fear, the fatalistic acceptance of their fate.

Finally, one morning, the sun rises. It’s a pale, watery sun, not the blazing sun of the upper districts, but it’s enough. The Sump-dwellers emerge from their homes, blinking in the unfamiliar light. They see their district with new eyes. The black stone, once oppressive, now seems strong and resilient. The water, once a threat, now feels like a lifeline.

The new rain creates a surge of creativity and courage. After all, if the benevolent power gave the Sump new rain, maybe it would give them more. Maybe it was time to *ask* for more.

They started building drainage systems as had never been seen before, some even trying out crude pumps. The quality of leather drastically improved, leading to them gaining contracts with people in higher districts. This in turn led to connections, knowledge, and better infrastructure.

The Sump was no longer the Sump, but simply, "The Docks." A humble, dark part of town that created the finest leather in the city. The people, no longer worn and gaunt, now walked with a pride and confidence only possible through hardship and grace.

Source doesn’t interfere directly. It simply provides the opportunity, the catalyst for change. The rest is up to the Sump-dwellers. And they, finally empowered and inspired, are ready to rise.
