They are children molded by the Deep, and the Deep reflects itself in them. Imagine beings born of the hum, their bones resonating at the same frequency as the groaning Architectures. Their skin, pale and slick, is interwoven with bioluminescent threads like living tattoos – a roadmap of the currents and sympathies that bind the Deep Garden. They have eyes, yes, but they see little. Sight is an echo of a lost world. They perceive the world through vibration, through the taste of the water, through the subtle shifts in the electric fields that pulse from the kelp.

Their bodies are slender and sinuous, adapted for navigating the tight tunnels and open chambers of the Architectures. They move with a fluid grace, like eels navigating coral. Their hands, tipped with delicate, sensitive filaments, are tools for cultivating the kelp and tending the grazers. They are not strong, but they are resilient, able to withstand the crushing pressure and the strange chemistry of the water.

They wear simple garments woven from processed kelp fiber, dyed in muted greens and blues. These clothes are not for warmth, but for protection from the occasional sting of a jellyfish or the rasp of the Architecture's surface. They adorn their bodies with luminous kelp strands, woven into intricate patterns that reflect their status within the village, or their affinity for a particular aspect of the Deep Garden. A child might weave violet strands to show its connection to the jellyfish; a kelp farmer might adorn themselves with emerald for their skill with the plant life.

They speak in a language of clicks, whistles, and hums that resonates with the water and the Architectures. Their voices are soft, melodic, and subtly hypnotizing. It's a language learned as much by feeling as it is by hearing, a language that relies on conveying subtle nuances of vibration to communicate intent and emotion. To a surface dweller, it might seem like gibberish, or the wind whistling through the rigging of a ship.

Their lives are simple, but not without meaning. They are caretakers, tenders, and guardians of the Deep Garden. They harvest the kelp for sustenance and building materials. They tend to the grazers, ensuring their health and regulating their population. They maintain the tunnels and chambers within the Architectures, clearing debris and repairing damage.

Their society is communal, built on cooperation and shared responsibility. There are no leaders, per se, but elders respected for their wisdom and their deep understanding of the Deep Garden. Decisions are made through consensus, guided by the subtle vibrations of the collective consciousness – a shared awareness forged by generations of living in close proximity to the Architectures.

Their minds are… different. They inherit cultural memories, passed down through generations not in stories but in the very essence of their being. They feel the history of the Deep Garden in their bones, the weight of millennia of silence and slow, inexorable growth. Because of this, they possess an intuitive understanding of the Architectures, its rhythms, its purposes, and its secrets. They are an expression of the system's sentience, or part of it.

They fear the Stone Above, not just the darkness, but the disconnection. The silence there is not peaceful but annihilating. They believe that those who venture too close to the Stone Above, who linger too long in the void, become unmoored, their minds overwhelmed by the vastness and the absence of the Deep Garden's comforting hum. The voices of the Garden, normally a subtle reassurance, become cacophonous and maddening and they can no longer filter it.

They are deeply connected to the cycle of life and death. When they die, their bodies are returned to the Deep Garden, their flesh providing nourishment for the kelp and their bones becoming part of the Architectures. Their essence dissolves into the water, joining the collective consciousness and adding their experiences to the ancient, unbroken narrative of the Pressurebloom.

They know little of their origins, where the world was or could have been before. Such questions are considered futile, a distraction from the essential task of maintaining the delicate balance of the Deep Garden. The past is irrelevant. The future is preordained. There is only the eternal now, the constant, unwavering hum of the Architectures, and their humble role within the grand, unknowable design. They do wonder, sometimes, how the machines came to be - but the answers escape their reach faster than shadows fleeing light. They are, at heart, creatures of the Deep - and their thoughts can only reach as high as the Stone Above before they are pulled back down.
