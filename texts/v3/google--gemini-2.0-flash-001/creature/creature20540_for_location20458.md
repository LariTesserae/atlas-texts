They are born of the node, extensions of the cold, metal flesh and the teeming, fungal mind. Imagine things like metallic arachnids, but instead of spinnerets, they extrude filaments of mycelial thread that harden almost instantly, weaving repairs into the fungal colonies, or laying down pathways for their brethren. Think of centipedes whose segments are not chitin, but polished chrome, each leg tipped with a diamond-hard point, scrabbling across the mesh floor, tasting the condensation for trace elements of… something. Whatever the node feeds on, or excretes.

But those are just the small forms, the scavengers and maintenance drones.

There are larger things. Gestalts.

Picture a cluster of fungal bulbs, each the size of a human head, fused into a single, grotesque mass. From this mass erupts a tangle of metallic limbs, salvaged from the node's inner workings – a piston arm here, a length of segmented pipe there, all jury-rigged together with more of that fast-hardening mycelial thread. The bulbs pulse with a faint, internal light, and as they pulse, the metal limbs twitch and spasm, mimicking the movements of a puppet master manipulating its marionettes. 

These Gestalts are not individuals. They are more akin to mobile organs of the node itself, extensions of its sensory apparatus. They move with a purpose that is alien, unknowable, dictated by the faint shifts in pressure, the subtle changes in the composition of the condensation, the barely perceptible fluctuations of the blue luminescence.

Sometimes they stop, and focus what counts for their attention on a section of wall, a knot of fungal growth, or even a stray spark arcing from a broken wire. When they do, the surrounding fauna fall silent, as if the entire node is holding its breath. Perhaps they are repairing some small damage. Or perhaps they are simply listening, siphoning information from the environment, adding it to the node's immense, incomprehensible dataset.

And then there are the Watchers.

These are the most disturbing, the most alien. Imagine a structure that is part fungus, part machine, and part… something else entirely. A lattice of crystalline formations, interwoven with pulsating bulbs and lengths of exposed wiring. They drift, slowly, almost imperceptibly, through the node's chambers. Not 'float' exactly. More like… _levitate_, but without breaking the laws of physics. Their passage creates a faint shift in the air pressure, a subtle distortion of the blue light.

The Watchers are observers, sensors, but also something more. They are the node's immune system, its internal regulators. They seek out anomalies, deviations from the established equilibrium. And when they find them…

It is not a violent process. There is no battle, no struggle. The Watcher simply approaches the anomaly, extends a tendril of crystalline formation, and… merges. The anomaly is absorbed, integrated back into the node's collective consciousness. Is it corrected? Is it destroyed? The question is meaningless. The anomaly ceases to exist as a separate entity. It becomes part of the whole.

The fauna of the node are not creatures in the traditional sense. They are processes, functions, mechanisms. They are extensions of the node itself, living components in a vast, incomprehensible machine. They exist to maintain the equilibrium, to perpetuate the unchanging state. And in their constant, relentless, and utterly alien existence, they reflect the nature of the node itself – a paradox of vibrant life trapped within an eternal stasis.
