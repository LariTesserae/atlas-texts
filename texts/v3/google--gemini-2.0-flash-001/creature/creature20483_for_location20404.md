The Whisperfolk are not born of this place; they are born *into* it. The plateau claims them, slowly dyes them, molds them to its quiet, deliberate rhythm. Their ancestors, perhaps, arrived with quicker steps, with expectations of wresting a life from the land. But the land, being what it is, had other plans.

Generations spent steeped in the perpetual damp have soaked into their very being. Their bones are lighter, almost porous, as if infused with the air itself. Their movements, initially a product of navigating the treacherous, moss-covered terrain, have become an intrinsic part of their nature. Each step is considered, each movement deliberate, a dance in harmony with the whispering reeds and the yielding earth. Haste is a foreign concept, agitation a forgotten feeling.

They are small, yes, but wiry. Their frames are not frail, but resilient, honed by years of clambering over slick boulders and navigating the tangled air-roots. Their hands are strong, capable of coaxing life from the stubborn clay and weaving intricate patterns from the tough plant fibers. Their vision is keen, able to discern the subtlest shifts in light and shadow, the faintest tremors in the pervasive silence. They see patterns where others see only chaos, reading the language of the lichen, the tilt of the rocks, the direction of the dripping water.

The green stain on their skin is not merely a superficial discolouration. It is a marker of integration, a testament to their symbiotic relationship with the lichen. It is said that children born far from the plateau, untouched by the lichen's embrace, are pale and fleeting, unable to fully thrive in this alien world. The green runs deeper than skin, seeping into their blood, altering their metabolism. It grants them a certain level of camouflage, allowing them to blend seamlessly with the verdant landscape, but more importantly, it links them to the plateau's strange, resonant consciousness.

Their clothes, woven from moss and bark, are not mere coverings; they are second skins, extensions of their sensory apparatus. The fibers vibrate with the slightest tremor of the earth, conveying information about the movement of water, the subtle shifts in wind direction, the presence of unseen forces. They are perpetually damp, clinging to the body like a second layer of moss, further blurring the line between flesh and foliage.

The song-stones are more than just instruments; they are conduits to the plateau's heart. Each stone has its own unique resonance, its own story etched in its mineral structure. The Whisperfolk do not simply play the song-stones; they listen to them, coaxing out the melodies that already reside within. The sounds are not meant to be enjoyed, but to be absorbed, to resonate within the listener, to align their consciousness with the slow, deliberate pulse of the plateau. The melodies are not fixed, but fluid, adapting to the changing moods of the environment, appeasing restless spirits and maintaining the fragile ecological balance.

Their faces are etched with the wisdom of ages, but possess a childlike openness. They are free from the anxieties and ambitions that plague those who live outside the plateau. Their concerns are simple: the health of the lichen, the flow of the water, the resonance of the stones. They do not seek to dominate or control their environment, but to understand and harmonize with it.

But above all, the Whisperfolk are listeners. They listen to the sigh of the wind, the drip of the water, the hum of the silence. They listen to the earth beneath their feet, the lichen on their skin, the very air around them. It is in this listening that they find their purpose, their identity, their connection to the strange, silent soul of the Whisperwood Plateau. They are the interpreters of its whispers, the guardians of its delicate balance, the living embodiment of its ancient and forgotten geometry. They are, in essence, the plateau itself, made flesh. And they wait, patiently, deliberately, for the day when the outside world will finally learn to listen too.
