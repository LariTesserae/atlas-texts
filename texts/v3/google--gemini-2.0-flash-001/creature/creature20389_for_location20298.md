The Lurkers of the Lung, as they might clumsily be named if such a place or being were to be given a name by any but themselves, do not live so much as *infest*. "Infest" is a human term, loaded with implied disgust, but it brushes closer to the truth than "inhabit" or "colonize." They are a fever within the mountain's cold bones, a living entropy.

Individuality, as understood by sun-haunted creatures, is a diluted concept among them. Certainly, size and chitinous sculpting vary. One armored millipede might be notably larger, its segments a darker, more polished obsidian, its mandibles dripping with iridescent slime. Another crab-thing, its shell a patchwork of quartz shards and moss, might possess legs a handspan longer than its brethren.

But these are cosmetic differences. Like cells within an immense, sluggish body, they function with a terrifying, single-minded dedication. Their chitin whispers secrets to each other through a symphony of clicks and scrapes that build the constant hum that permeates the mountain hollow. The air trembles not just with sound, but with purpose. A purpose inhuman enough to be incomprehensible.

They are scavengers and sculptors, their rasping mandibles and clawed limbs the instruments of the ongoing erosion. They ingest the detritus of their dead, break down the leathery vegetation, and polish the stone with their ceaseless movement. Everything is returned to the cycle – the condensation feeding the moss, the moss feeding the beetles, the beetles feeding the crabs, the crabs feeding the millipedes, and the millipedes, eventually, feeding all.

Their eyes, scattered clusters of multifaceted lenses across their bodies, are not windows to thought, but sensors of proximity and movement. They do not *see* in the way that mammals see. They perceive the world as a map of vibrations and chemical gradients, a constantly shifting terrain of potential meals and potential dangers. The phosphorescent moss, which pulses with a faint, ethereal glow, is used as a beacon of awareness, drawing them to it. At any given time, you can see them swarming over to it and back again over and over as if it were some kind of hive colony.

Intelligence, if one dares apply the word, resides not in individual brains, but in the collective. They anticipate seismic shifts, navigate the treacherous pathways of the cavern, and repair damage inflicted by rockfalls with an unnerving efficiency. They are each a single thread in a complex tapestry of action and reaction, a system of near-perfect efficiency.

But perhaps the most unsettling aspect of the Lurkers is their patience. They are creatures of immense geologic scales of time. They do not know haste. They do not know urgency. Each action, each scrape, each bite is undertaken with the slow, deliberate assurance that it is part of an endless process. They are gnawing the mountain from the inside out, not for sustenance, but because that is what they *are.*

They are not evil, nor are they good, as such moral concepts are meaningless in the stygian gloom of their existence. They are simply the embodiment of a different kind of life, a life that thrives on decay, a life that finds beauty in the gradual unraveling of the world. They are the Lurkers, the caretakers of the mountain-lung, and they will be here long the sun finally swallows earth whole.
