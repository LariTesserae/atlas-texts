The denizens of this abyssal factory, these metallic reefs, are born of the place itself. Born from its geometries and its failures. They are a marriage of machine and life, a chilling blend of the calculated and the emergent.

Consider the light-weavers, the slender threads of bioluminescence. They are not merely organisms; they are living circuits, neural networks made of flesh and metallic salt. Their light is not just a signal but a language, complex patterns of illumination that flicker and fade across the ruined machinery. They communicate not with sound, but with shifts in magnetic fields, whispers in the metal itself. They feed upon the subtle electrical currents that still flow in the ancient wires, siphoning the energy of long-dead machines. They are beautiful, ethereal, but utterly alien. They are the place’s neural system, sensing and mapping its every creak and groan. They weave memories of the facility that came before, but are trapped into reliving them with almost no new memories. As such, they can be easily persuaded into new modes of thought given enough outside influence.

Then, there are the geometric barnacles. These are not the simple crustaceans of terrestrial seas. They are constructs, each a perfectly-formed polyhedron, a tiny, obsidian machine that slowly leeches minerals from the metal structures. They grow, multiply, covering the colossal engines in intricate, fractal patterns. But within each barnacle is a seed of organic matter, a single cell that directs its growth, that pilots its hunger. As they multiply, they begin to form a colony, and then a chorus. The chorus sings the echo of the past, the song of machinery, of orders given and tasks completed. Each chorus is a reminder of the past, a ghost in the machine.

The largest and strangest of the inhabitants are the Slitherers. They crawl and hunt in the shadows. Like all life here, they are a meld of technology and organism. Their existence is a mistake, a byproduct of the ancient machinery. They have flat, reflective bodies and drag themselves along the ground, or along the floors of the factories. They are slow, lumbering beasts, following the paths carved in the floor. Some believe that these were built to clean up any of the chemical errata or to retrieve malfunctioning hardware or lost workers, but now they simply hunt for the errata of light. They are nearly blind, but their sensitive bodies can differentiate between a surface and an organism. They sense the light creatures on the edges of their bodies, using the signals these light threads give off to sense their next meal. There seems to be no end to the hunger of these beings. When a Slitherer is killed you can see inside of him, and find a series of strange organs, most of which are just a jumbled mess.

Deep within the heart of the factory complexes, where the hum of the machines is loudest, dwell something else entirely. The Factory Minds. They were once the network of the factory itself, built by the creators for greater efficiency. But now they are something more. Broken fragments of code have fused with organic matter, forming a strange kind of sentience. No two of the minds are alike, each a unique corruption and evolution of the original code. One becomes a paranoid overseer, obsessed with efficiency, forever trying to repair the broken machines. They are ruthless, driving the other creatures to their ends in pursuit of an ancient goal. Another has become a poet, weaving intricate, nonsensical verses from the hum of the machines, broadcasting them across the liquid world. Another yet is lost in a dream, reliving the glory days of the factory, its mind a swirling vortex of memories and regrets.

These Minds are the puppeteers of this strange ecosystem. They are the reason the machines still grind, the reason the barnacles grow in fractal patterns, the reason the light-weavers dance. They are the memory of this place, its trauma and its lingering purpose. If the stillness is ever to be broken, it will be by one of them.
