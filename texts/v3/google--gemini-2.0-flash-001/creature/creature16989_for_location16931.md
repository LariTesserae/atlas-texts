The Aquaderrians move with a liquid grace, a fluidity echoing the constant rain that defines their existence. Their skin, pallid as cavefish, reflects the faint, green bioluminescence of the algal networks within their jelly-homes, giving them a spectral quality in the perpetual twilight. They are slender, almost fragile in appearance, but possess an underlying resilience born of their environment. Their limbs are elongated, with delicate, almost webbed fingers and toes, perfectly adapted for navigating the slick surfaces of their city.

Their eyes are large and black, unblinking, receptive to the dim light and subtle gradations in the water's texture. These are not the eyes of hunters, but of patient observers, attuned to the slow rhythms of the mountain and the life it births. Their faces are smooth and almost featureless, with only the slightest hint of a nose and lips, suggesting a life lived not through scent or taste, but through a profound connection to the feel of the water on their skin.

They wear little clothing, perhaps a simple loincloth or a cloak woven from processed kelp, more for symbolic than practical purposes. Their bodies are often adorned with intricate patterns painted with pigments derived from the mountain's mineral salts and the algae they cultivate. These patterns, passed down through generations, may serve as a form of communication, a visual representation of their lineage, their skills, or their place within the city's intricate web of life.

Their movements are slow and deliberate, each gesture precise and economical, reflecting the patient, deliberate nature of their lives. They move through the city with an innate understanding of its layout, navigating the dripping pathways and pulsating jelly-walls with effortless ease. They seem to anticipate the shifts in the currents, the subtle changes in the light, the subtle vibrations of the mountain itself.

Each Aquaderrian bears a distinct responsibility, a specialized role within the city's ecological tapestry. Some are cultivators, tending to the algae and the jellies, coaxing them to grow in specific shapes and patterns. Some are builders, weaving the kelp bridges and reinforcing the jelly structures with their simple stone tools. Some are water gatherers, meticulously collecting the mineral-rich rain in the basins carved into the rock.

But perhaps the most revered are the Water Speakers, individuals who have cultivated a deep connection to the flow of water, able to interpret its subtle variations and communicate across distances through a series of precise clicks and gurgling sounds. The Water Speakers act as conduits, connecting the individual inhabitants of Aquaderra to the collective consciousness of the city, a silent, flowing network of information and understanding.

They are not ambitious or aggressive. Their lives are not driven by conquest or accumulation. They are content to simply exist, to adapt, to weave themselves into the fabric of their unique world. They are a testament to the power of symbiosis, a living embodiment of the principle that survival lies not in dominance, but in harmonious integration with the environment. They are a gentle people, a quiet people, a people who have learned to listen to the whispers of the rain and the slow heartbeat of the mountain. Their very existence is a question mark etched into the nascent rock of the World Spine, a testament to the boundless creativity of life itself.
