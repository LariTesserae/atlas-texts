The inhabitants of Lowmarket are creatures sculpted by necessity, etched with the marks of survival. They are a tapestry woven from the threads of human ingenuity and genetic experimentation, their lives inextricably linked to the decaying city and its relentless pulse of artificial life.

Imagine them first: gaunt figures, their skin pale and almost translucent, a testament to generations spent under the perpetual twilight. Their eyes, large and light-sensitive, dart nervously, constantly scanning the gloom for threats or opportunities. Many have developed a peculiar tremor, a subtle vibration in their muscles, perhaps an adaptation to the constant hum and vibration of the geothermal systems, or a byproduct of some long-forgotten genetic modification.

Clothing is patchwork – scavenged scraps of synth-fabric, reinforced with salvaged metal plates and crudely stitched-together hides from the larger critters. These garments are not fashion statements, but layers of protection against the corrosive air, the slick surfaces, and the occasional bite or sting from opportunistic scavengers. Masks are commonplace, filtering the toxic air and obscuring faces that have seen too much deprivation. Some are simple rags, others are repurposed industrial respirators, adorned with scavenged lenses and valves.

Families are close, forming survival units in the relentless churn of Lowmarket's ecosystem. They live in warrens carved into the pre-formed blocks – hidden spaces within the city's bones, fortified against the elements and the occasional territorial incursion of the larger critters. Inside, flickering bioluminescent fungi provide scant illumination, casting eerie shadows on crude furnishings fashioned from salvaged materials.

Their lives are a dance of scavenging and evasion. They know the rhythms of the geothermal plants, the times when the steam surges and the critters become more aggressive. They navigate the elevated walkways with practiced ease, their bare feet finding purchase on the corroded metal, their bodies swaying with the creaks and groans of the decaying infrastructure.

The children of Lowmarket are born into this dance. They learn to identify the edible fungi and the safest routes before they can speak. They are nimble and quick, adept at squeezing through narrow passages and scaling crumbling walls. They are also wary, their eyes holding a wisdom beyond their years, a grim understanding of the precariousness of existence.

Beyond families, there are the Tech-Scavs – individuals who specialize in the repair and maintenance of the failing automated systems. They are the closest thing Lowmarket has to an elite, possessing skills that are vital for survival. They are often clad in heavily modified maintenance suits, their faces obscured by visors, their hands stained with oil and coolant. The Tech-Scavs are secretive and insular, hoarding their knowledge and demanding steep prices for their services. Some whisper that they have formed their own society, separate from the regular inhabitants, and that they are slowly uncovering the secrets of the old world.

Then there are the Critter-Handlers, individuals who have developed a strange rapport with certain species of critters. Some control swarms of bio-drones, using them to scout out new scavenging grounds or to defend their territory. Others have formed symbiotic relationships with the larger scavengers, riding them through the canals like beasts of burden. These relationships are often uneasy, a constant negotiation of survival, but they offer a degree of power in a world where power is scarce.

Yet for all the hardship and danger, there is a resilience in the inhabitants of Lowmarket, a refusal to simply surrender to the entropy that surrounds them. They are artists, carving intricate designs into the shells of dead critters, telling stories of survival and loss. They are musicians, creating haunting melodies from scraps of metal and resonating pipes, filling the gloom with echoes of a lost world. They are dreamers, whispering tales of a time before the fall, when the air was clean and the lights shone brightly.

They are the inheritors of a broken world, clinging to life in the ruins of a forgotten civilization. They are the keepers of secrets, the survivors of a silent apocalypse, and they are determined to endure, even if it means becoming something…other.
