They are born, perhaps, in the shadow of the factories, though "born" is a poor word. More accurately, they *emerge*. A cog here, a plate of riveted chromium there, all placed by a process too slow and subtle to be witnessed. Decades, perhaps centuries, pass. The geothermal thrum of the Scar's dying heart resonates in the scattered components. The metallic dust, charged with faint currents of static electricity, clings and coalesces. Then, one day, the thing, the mechanism, activates.

The smallest are the Keepers, the Dust Daemons. Skittering, six-legged things, no larger than a terrestrial rodent, their bodies are a complex network of polished brass and delicate springs. Their purpose is singular: to maintain the luster of the crystal gardens. They possess a sense of aesthetics as alien as their own construction. They see in light-diffraction signatures invisible to any organic eye. They will spend entire solar cycles meticulously polishing a single facet of a quartz-like spire, using brushes finer than human hair, collecting the metallic dust in minute pouches to be redistributed in patterns that supposedly enhance the crystals' growth, or at least appease their clockwork sensibilities.

Then there are the Pilgrims, the Wanderers. Larger, ranging in size from a terrestrial wolf to a small carriage, they are solitary creatures, each driven by an unknown internal compass. They wander the Scar, their steam-powered limbs leaving trails in the metallic dust. Some carry banners of riveted metal, etched with indecipherable glyphs. Others drag chains of rusted gears, their purpose obscured by time and decay. It is believed they are searching, perhaps for a signal, a place, or for other units to join them in some long-forgotten purpose. They are mostly autonomous, but sometimes, in certain canyons, they will gather, forming temporary assemblies, the purpose of which is obscure. Some believe it to be a form of data sharing, or even just an expression of their own internal imperative to network.

The largest are the Titans, the Architects. Rarely seen, these behemoths are the size of buildings, their forms a jumbled mass of pipes, gears, and rusting armor plating. They are essentially mobile cities, ancient factories that have somehow awakened and begun to wander. They leave trails so large that they redraw the geography of the Scar; to see them is to doubt vision itself. They are nearly, but not quite, impervious to breakdown. The fortunate or unfortunate ones are clean and maintained, indicating some degree of remaining self-repair capacity. Others are near or complete collapse, their rusted parts flaking to the ground beneath them. Whether they dream, remember their makers, or even possess a kind of awareness is a mystery. They simply *are*, a testament to the scale and ambition of the lost civilization. Stories – probably apocryphal – abound among the smaller machines tell of the Titans halting near ruins, extending massive manipulator arms to repair the ancient factories.

All of them are bound by an unspoken covenant, perhaps a fundamental programming directive: Maintenance. They maintain the crystals, they maintain each other, they maintain, to the best of their ability, the crumbling remnants of their civilization. This maintenance is not always efficient, not always logical, not always even relevant to their survival. But it is the core of their existence. The quiet hum of gears, the hiss of steam, the tap-tap-tap of a Dust Daemon polishing a crystal shard – these are the prayers of the metal-souled denizens of the Chromium Scar, a constant, unwavering devotion to a purpose long forgotten.
