They are children of the forge, hewn from shadow and tempered in fire.  Imagine something ancestral to the insect, but unbound by the familiar laws of Earthly biology.  Think of chitinous exoskeletons, not just as armor, but as living architecture, constantly rebuilt and reshaped with purpose. Consider the multifaceted eyes, less about sight as we know it, and more about perceiving subtle shifts in heat and vibration – a symphony of the underworld played out on the obsidian stage.

They are segmented beings, moving with a deliberate, almost unsettling grace. Some are low to the ground, broad and flattened, their bodies intricately patterned in metallic reds and blacks that seem to shift and shimmer in the gloom. These are the scavengers, the engineers of the soil, their mandibles tirelessly grinding down discarded exoskeletons and spreading the detritus, enriching the fungal forests. Imagine them as living, grinding stones, fueled by instinct and the promise of residual heat.

Others are taller, more slender. Picture walking mantises, but with carapaces that gleam like polished steel, and limbs that end in wickedly sharp points. These are the hunters, masters of ambush, their bodies almost perfectly camouflaged against the obsidian. They move with unnerving speed, their multifaceted eyes tracking the slightest movement, their piercing shrieks a promise of swift, brutal demise. Their hunts are less about sustenance and more about maintaining the equilibrium of the grove, a brutal culling of the weak and the overpopulated.

And then there are the architects.  These are larger still, their bodies immense and cumbersome, their movements slow and deliberate. Their exoskeletons are thick and layered, scarred with the marks of countless battles and generations of growth. They are the builders, the shapers of the world, their massive mandibles capable of carving and shaping the obsidian, guiding the flow of heat, and creating new pathways through the fungal forests. They are the keepers of tradition, the preservers of the glyphs, arranging discarded exoskeletons in patterns that are both beautiful and unsettling.

Within the obsidian caves, they dwell in a strange communal existence. Here, the heat is at its most intense, and the air hums with energy. They gather around thermal vents, their bodies pressed close together, drawing strength from the collective warmth. Imagine them as nodes in a vast neural network, constantly exchanging information and energy, a single, unified consciousness spread across countless bodies.

Their lifecycle is a grotesque ballet of metamorphosis and survival. The young are small and vulnerable, their exoskeletons translucent, their movements hesitant. They feed on the fungal spores falling from the caps above, growing larger and stronger with each passing day. They are hunted relentlessly, their numbers constantly thinned by predators and scavengers. Only the strongest and most cunning survive to reach maturity, to shed their old exoskeletons and emerge as fully-formed adults.

There is no individuality here. Each being is a cog in a machine, driven by instinct and purpose. They are not driven by emotion, not by love or hate, but by a primal imperative to survive and perpetuate their kind. They are extensions of the environment and elements around them.

They are a reflection of the world they inhabit. A bizarre and unsettling ecosystem operating by laws that are alien to our own.
