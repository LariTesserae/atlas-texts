The first life you notice isn't life at all, not in the conventional sense. They are the Ore-Golems, slumbering mostly, but forever active. These immense, slow-moving beings are more akin to animated extensions of the spire itself. Their bodies are a jumbled collage of hematite, scavenged gears, and pipes long since corroded. Some are man-sized, still clinging to the vestige of a humanoid shape, others are simply enormous, irregular masses of metal and stone, fused together by the oily secretion of the Citadel. They lumber through the tunnels, almost blindly, propelled by a primal imperative to consume, to gather the oozing fluids and the nutrient-rich fungi. They are the oldest beings here, predating the Citadel's full development, minds dulled with age. To view them is to see a distorted reflection of the Citadel itself.

Then are the Scrappers. The winged creatures are slight, built for speed rather than brute force. Their bodies are like articulated skeletons of polished steel, their 'skin' being a tightly stretched membrane that shimmers with iridescent oils and dust. They aren't graceful like most birds or bats; their movements are jerky, almost insectile, like clockwork mechanisms wound too tight. Their eyes, if you can call them that, are multifaceted lenses, each one glinting with a different hue allowing them to see in the luminescent dark. They are scavengers, and the spire is their bounty.

Below the golems and the Scrappers are the Niddlers. They are the source of the rhythmic scraping. They are tiny, multi-legged creatures, like metallic woodlice or roaches. They are the micro-organisms of the Citadel, their bronze-and-copper carapaces crisscrossed with intricate engravings. They thrive in the oily runoff, consuming whatever organic muck settles to the bottom, and excreting a faintly magnetic dust that is vital to the functioning of certain forgotten machines.

The Niddlers form colonies, sometimes numbering in the thousands, and their skittering is the pulse of this level. They inhabit pockets in the rock that have long since gone dark, and the tunnels in between are their hunting grounds.

The relationship among these creatures is complex, and brutal. The Ore-Golems, in their blind hunger, crush Niddler colonies beneath their feet. The Scrappers will occasionally swoop down to snatch a careless Niddler, but they primarily subsist on the airborne spores released by the bioluminescent fungi. The oily fluids that the Golems crave are created and secreted by the Niddler colonies. It is a balanced circle of life and constant mutual exploitation.

There are other, stranger things that dwell within the Foundry Citadel. Whispers of creatures that live in the deepest, darkest tunnels, beings that have fused with the machinery, their bodies integrated into the Citadel's architecture. Sometimes, in the distant, metallic screeches and groans of the machinery, there come sounds that are undeniably vocalizations, warped and distorted by the metal that resonates within them.

And then there's the unspoken. The *sense* that you are being watched. The chilling certainty that something, somehow, is aware of your presence, even if you can't see it. Something that is both a part of the Citadel and something else entirely.
