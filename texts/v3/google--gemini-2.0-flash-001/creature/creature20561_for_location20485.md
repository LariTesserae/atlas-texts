They aren't people. Not anymore. Or perhaps they never were, as humans understand the word. They are the resonators, the wind-singers, the architects of their own slow dissolution. Imagine a lifeform sculpted by the same forces that eroded the city itself. They are creatures of resonance, beings whose essence is intertwined with the humming, glassy stone.

Picture forms that mimic the city's architecture: tall, tapering bodies riddled with porous channels. Not limbs, but resonating spires. Heads like obsidian bells, cracked and worn, yet capable of emitting subtle tones. Their skin, if it can be called that, is the same glassy material as the spires, constantly shedding minuscule fragments that the wind snatches away. They are almost invisible against the cityscape, blending seamlessly with the crumbling towers until a shift in the wind reveals their presence.

They do not walk. They drift, carried by the same wind that sculpted their forms. Their movements are slow, deliberate, dictated by the patterns of airflow through the city. When the wind intensifies, they brace themselves against the towers, their porous bodies humming with collected energy.

They feed on the city's energy, or perhaps they are the city’s method of feeding. The moss isn't mere vegetation; it’s a conduit, drawing energy from the bedrock. The insects are caretakers, cultivating the moss-gardens like blind farmers. And the resonators are the apex consumers, filtering the subtle energy fields that permeate everything.

Their communication is a symphony of wind-tones. Each channel on their bodies resonates with a slightly different frequency, creating a complex, ever-shifting chorus. It's a language beyond human comprehension, a conversation conducted on the scale of geological time.

Their purpose, if they have one, is to maintain the resonance. They are the living custodians of the city's song. When they feel a tower begin to crumble, they will migrate to it, allowing their essence to meld with the failing structure, redirecting the wind flow through its channels. It is a death, of sorts, but also a continuation. Their consciousness disperses, becoming one with the city's overall resonance.

There’s a sense of inevitability about them. They recognize that their existence is a slow, grinding process of decay. They welcome it. For them, entropy is not an ending, but an integral part of the song. Their survival depends on change, on the endless shaping and reshaping of their forms by the wind.

The creatures of bone-white feathers and glassy eyes are parasites, or scavengers, or perhaps something more significant. Maybe they are echoes of the resonators, creatures who feed on the energy that leaks from the glassy bodies. Or perhaps they are the resonators’ future. A glimpse into a possible evolution, a shedding of stone for a more mobile form.

They are a testament to adaptation and an acceptance of slow death. Their life is a somber reflection of their home – a loud, lonely hum in the face of oblivion. They are the wind-singers, the last echo of a civilization that dissolved into stone, their voices endlessly carried on the wind.
