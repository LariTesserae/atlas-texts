The inhabitants of the Respiration Gardens are wrought from the same unsettling fusion as the place itself – something born of desperate necessity and unintended consequence. They are *Weavers,* or so they call themselves, though the Forge-folk on the outer levels just call 'em "Rust-lungs" and leave it at that. They are not born, not in the traditional sense; they are cultivated.

Imagine a human form, but stretched, elongated, as if pulled thin like taffy and then allowed to settle in strange proportions. Their limbs are spindly, almost fragile, ending in hands and feet that are more akin to articulated tools than anything meant for walking or grasping. Their skin is thin and translucent, stretched taut over a lattice of visible veins that glow with a soft, internal luminescence – a pale, sickly green echo of the bioluminescent fungi that thrive in the Gardens. This light is not merely aesthetic; it's vital, symbiotic. Within their veins flows not just blood, but a nutrient-rich ichor cultivated from the Gardens' flora – filtered through internal organs that are as much root system as they are fleshy mass.

Their faces are elongated, almost equine, with large, black eyes that are surprisingly gentle. They lack external ears, instead possessing sensitive membranes along the sides of their skulls that pick up the faintest vibrations in the metal scaffold. Their mouths are small, almost vestigial, used more for the slow consumption of nectar and processed fungi than for anything like speech.

Because Weavers don't speak the common tongue, or any language spoken above; instead, they communicate through a complex system of vibrations, tapping patterns onto the girders and walkways, sending ripples of information through the metal veins of their world. Those who know how to listen – the older generations, mostly – can interpret these messages, learning of blossoming fungi patches, shifts in the scaffolding's structural integrity, or the movements of the rare metal-beetles they use in their craft.

Their craft is essential to the Gardens’ survival. The Weavers are the gardeners, the maintainers, the living repair crew. They are intimately connected to the rhythms of this place, understanding the symbiotic relationships between the engineered flora and the failing infrastructure. They cultivate the mosses that seal hairline fractures in plasteel, guiding their growth with gentle taps of their elongated fingers. They graft fungal lungs onto damaged air-purifiers, coaxing them to breathe with their own internal luminescence. They weave tapestries of bioluminescent flora across weakened sections of scaffolding, creating living reinforcement that glows with an eerie beauty.

They are not mere laborers, though. The Weavers are artists, ecologists, and historians, all rolled into one. They meticulously document the changes within the Gardens, creating intricate maps of fungal networks and diagrams of the scaffolding's structural stresses. These maps are not made on paper or plasteel; they are living murals, woven with glowing fungi and cultivated moss, growing and evolving along with the Gardens themselves.

Their society is communal and deeply interconnected. They share a collective consciousness, a network of sensory information flowing through the metal pathways and the fungal veins that connect them. Injuries suffered by one Weaver can be felt by others; the blossoming of a rare orchid brings a shared sense of joy. Individuality exists, but it is tempered by the overwhelming sense of being part of a larger, symbiotic organism.

Their existence is precarious. The Forge is dying, and the Gardens are a fragile oasis in its decaying heart. Every day brings new challenges – collapsing sections of scaffolding, encroaching rust, and the ever-present threat of collapse. The Weavers are constantly fighting a losing battle against entropy, but they fight nonetheless, driven by an instinctive need to preserve their world, their way of life.

Sometimes, scavengers from the upper levels of the forge venture into the Respiration Gardens, seeking rare materials or forgotten technology. The Weavers do not greet these outsiders with hostility, but with a wary curiosity. Some scavengers, touched by the Gardens' strange beauty and the Weavers' quiet dedication, have stayed, learning their ways, joining their ranks, becoming part of the symbiotic tapestry. But most leave, carrying away what they can, oblivious to the vital role these strange beings play in the Forge's fragile ecosystem.

Despite their alien appearance, their unsettling origins, the Weavers of the Respiration Gardens are more than just a biological curiosity. They are a testament to the resilience of life, a reminder that even in the heart of a dying machine, beauty can bloom, and hope can take root in the most unexpected places. They are the lungs of the Forge, and as long as they breathe, there is still a chance for its survival.
