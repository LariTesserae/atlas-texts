They are the inheritors of absence, forged in the crucible of a world stillborn. I imagine them not as creatures of flesh and blood, but as echoes of intent given form, struggling to maintain a spectral existence against the relentless entropy of the place.

Perhaps they were born of the wind's scream, coalescing out of the world's agony. Or maybe they arose from the rock itself, animated by the faint, fractal consciousness embedded within its structure, the stone dreaming of purpose.

They are tall, gaunt figures, their bodies resembling polished obsidian streaked with veins of chalky white. Their limbs are long and fragile, almost insectile in their delicacy. Their faces, if they can be called that, are smooth, featureless planes, reflecting the dim, perpetual twilight like polished mirrors. But the mirrors don't reflect outward, they instead reflect inward, deeper and deeper into an infinity of loss.
They have neither eyes nor ears, yet they perceive the world through a constant, agonizing vibration, a bone-deep resonance that speaks of a world unfinished, a promise broken.

Their movements are slow, deliberate, almost ritualistic. They glide across the fractured landscape, their feet barely disturbing the unstable rock. They are drawn to the crystalline flora, not to consume them, but to commune with their alien photosynthesis, to share in their improbable sustenance.

Their existence appears to be dedicated to the cairns. It is they who raised those stones, though the memory of *why* is fading with each agonizing day. And so their efforts fall short, and begin again, a hopeless devotion to a task they can no longer fully comprehend.

They are not builders so much as tenders, endlessly rearranging the stones, replacing those that have crumbled, reinforcing those that are faltering. They adorn them with the intricately carved stones, tokens as much as tools, and then wander away, always returning. As they work, their forms shimmer and distort, as though existence is the work, as though *their* existence is the cairns.

Sometimes, they gather at the base of the largest cairns and stand motionless for days, absorbing the resonant energy of the place through their bodies. In these moments, the fractal patterns etched within the rock flare brighter, and the ground trembles with a faint, almost imperceptible rhythm. It is as if they are attempting to awaken something dormant within the living rock, to call forth the world's true form, or to commune with their dead.

They possess no language, at least not in the conventional sense. But they communicate through the resonance of their bodies, through subtle shifts in the pressure of the air, through the faintest of vibrations in the rock. These communications aren't expressions of thought, or of emotion, just echoes of a raw intent. *Exist. Do. Remember.*

They mourn the absence of water, the barrenness of the land, the futility of their efforts, but theirs is a silent lament, a hollow ache that permeates the very fabric of their being. It is a dirge played on a bone flute made from desperation.

They are fading, slowly but surely, crumbling back into the raw potential of the place. Their forms become less distinct, their movements less purposeful, and their resonance grows fainter. Soon, they will be nothing more than echoes in the wind, whispers within the stone, ghosts in a world that never was.
