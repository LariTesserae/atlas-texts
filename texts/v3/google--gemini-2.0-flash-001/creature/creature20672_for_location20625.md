The Skiving Towers do not host life so much as cultivate it, in the way that a wound cultivates infection. The creatures that dwell amongst the clattering mechanisms are not inhabitants, but symptoms; expressions of a deeper, more fundamental sickness.

Imagine, first, the *Formers*. They are not born, but assembled. They emerge—or are ejected—from the heart of the towers, birthed by the city's grinding gears. Their basic shape is vaguely humanoid, but corrupted by the same iron-slick that coats everything else. Some have too many limbs, spindly and useless, clicking against the rust-colored stone. Others are missing limbs altogether, replaced by crude prosthetics cobbled together from pipes and valves. Their heads are featureless blocks of iron, devoid of eyes or mouths, yet they navigate the labyrinthine passages with unnerving precision.

The Formers are not builders, nor are they maintainers in any conventional sense. Their purpose is more fundamental: they are the city's immune system. When a section of a tower collapses, they scavenge materials to shore it up, not with skill or artistry, but with brutal, relentless efficiency. They simply *stuff* whatever they can find into the gap—a twisted girder, a shattered pump, the dessicated corpse of another Former. They do not repair, they merely contain.

The Formers are driven by a single, overriding imperative: to replicate. Deep within the towers, in chambers filled with a choking, metallic smog, they cluster together, their mechanical limbs working in concert to assemble new Formers from the raw materials provided by the city. These new Formers are invariably more twisted, more broken than their predecessors. Errors accumulate with each generation, yet the process continues, a relentless, self-destructive cycle of creation and decay.

Then there are the *Scavengers*. Unlike the Formers, they are not born from the city, but drawn to it. They are the parasites of this iron ecosystem, creatures of flesh and bone twisted and warped by prolonged exposure to the towers' toxic environment. They are small and gaunt, with pallid skin stretched tight over their bones. Their eyes are large and luminous, adapted to the perpetual gloom of the canyons between the towers.

The Scavengers live a precarious existence, eking out a meager sustenance from the city's refuse. They pick through piles of rust-covered scrap, searching for scraps of organic matter—a patch of fungus growing on a damp wall, the decaying remains of a bird that flew too close to the towers. They are hunted by the Formers, who see them as a threat to the city's integrity, but they are too numerous, too adaptable to be eradicated.

Lowest, perhaps, are the *Blooms*. They are not mobile. They are more like… growths. Patches of organic matter that have taken root in the city's cracks and crevices, drawing sustenance from the mineral-rich water that drips from the towers' upper reaches. They are a disturbing fusion of the natural and the artificial: bulbous masses of flesh sprouting from rusted pipes, veins throbbing beneath a carapace of iron filings.

The Blooms are not sentient, but they are alive. They pulse and throb with a slow, rhythmic beat, their surfaces glistening with a viscous, oily secretion. They serve as a sort of filter, extracting the last vestiges of organic matter from the city's waste stream. They are also a warning: a testament to the city's ability to corrupt and consume all that comes within its grasp.

These are the primary inhabitants of the Skiving Towers, but they are not the only ones. There are whispers of other, stranger creatures lurking in the city's depths: beings of pure iron, animated by the city's internal energies; colossal, worm-like entities that burrow through the towers' foundations; intelligences that reside within the machinery itself, directing the city's evolution towards some unknowable purpose.

But whether fabricated, drawn, or simply grown, each creature is simply an expression of the city's underlying impulse: not to live, but to *become*. To consume, to replicate, to expand, relentlessly and without end. The Skiving Towers' purpose is found, perhaps, in the creatures it conjures: to manifest the potential of a place beyond life, and before death.
