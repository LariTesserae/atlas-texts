The people of this city are a composite creature, as much machine and moss as flesh and bone. Imagine them emerging from the fog like rust-etched golems, their silhouettes blurring the line between human and the industrial detritus that birthed them. Their faces are a roadmap of hard living: deep lines etched by acidic rain and the worry of a thousand collapsing ceilings. Eyes, even without the glint of embedded lenses, are sharp, constantly scanning, assessing the angles of every shadow for threats or opportunities.

They move with the jerky, efficient grace of scavengers. A limp favored since childhood, now compensated by a hissing pneumatic brace that shines dully beneath tattered trousers. A stoop learned from navigating the low-ceilinged shacks and perpetually flooded alleyways. Hands, calloused and scarred, are as adept at wielding a wrench as they are at picking a pocket.

Their clothing is a patchwork of salvaged fabrics, repurposed industrial gear, and the occasional, surprisingly vibrant patch of something that might have once been fashionable. Think repurposed oilskins paired with fragments of shimmering synth-silk, held together with crude stitching and rusted rivets. Everything worn serves a purpose: pockets bulging with scavenged components, straps for hauling loads of scrap, reinforced patches to protect against the ubiquitous grime. Even adornments are functional – teeth inlaid with luminescent moss that allows them to see in the darkest corners, or scraps of metal hammered into crude necklaces which serve as makeshift Faraday cages.

The city's constant hum vibrates in their very bones, shaping their habits, even their thoughts. They speak in the clipped jargon of mechanics and merchants, punctuated by the squeals and clangs of their augments. Their dreams are filled with the grinding of gears and the gurgling of submerged streets, a symphony of decay and desperate innovation. Sleep is restless, filled with visions of collapsing foundations and encroaching tides, spurred on by the whispers of forgotten, drowning gods.

There is a particular gauntness to many, a perpetually harried look hinting at the constant struggle for resources. Malnutrition is a specter, lurking behind the bioluminescent glow of their modified features. Rickets and other diseases of deprivation twist limbs and stunt growth. Yet, this fragility is belied by an almost preternatural resilience. They are survivors, shaped by the city's brutal crucible.

Beneath the grime and the grit, there are flashes of ingenuity. Quick minds cobbling together new technologies from scraps and forgotten schematics. An innate understanding of the city's architecture, a knowledge of hidden passages and structurally unsound points that borders on the mystical. A fierce independence coupled with a desperate need for community, a willingness to share a meager meal around a makeshift fire if only to stave off the isolation for a few precious hours.

There is also a darkness that clings to them, a palpable sense of desperation that can flicker into violence at a moment's notice. They are capable of great cruelty, hardened by a world where empathy is a luxury they cannot afford. But even in their darkest moments, there is a spark of hope and creativity, a persistent, defiant urge to build and create, even as the city crumbles around them. Despite the sinking ground, the encroaching sea, and the decaying structures, they cling to the belief that something new, something better, can still be salvaged from the ruins. They are the inheritors of a broken world, yes, but they are also the architects of its strange and uncertain future, built on scavenged parts, desperate dreams, and the stubborn refusal to drown.
