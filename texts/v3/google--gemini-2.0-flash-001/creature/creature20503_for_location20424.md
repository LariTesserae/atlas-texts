The Mycelial Weavers emerge pale and slender from the heartwood, newborn and ancient all at once. They are not infants in the human sense, not babes needing nurture, but rather nascent nodes in a sprawling, sentient system. Imagine a newly sprouted mushroom - delicate, yes, but ready, its existence predestined by the mycelial web from which it springs.

Their skin is the color of bone, chalk dust, the underside of a long-unturned stone. It is smooth, almost pearlescent, reflecting the faint bioluminescent glow of their surroundings in a soft, diffused manner. There are no pores visible, no blemishes, only the subtle tracery of veins beneath the surface, glowing with the same viscous, light-infused sap that feeds the forest.

They are tall, reaching perhaps seven or eight feet in height, but their build is slender, almost fragile. Limbs are long and elegant, tapering to delicate hands with elongated fingers, perfectly suited for the intricate work of pruning and weaving. Their movements are slow, deliberate, almost reverential, as if any sudden motion might disrupt the delicate balance of the ecosystem.

Their faces are serene masks. Eyes large and luminous, reflecting every hue of fungal light, hold a deep, internal knowledge, a knowing that transcends individual experience. Pupils are often dilated, taking in the dim light of the forest, or perhaps tuned into the constant thrum of the Arbor Vitae. Noses and mouths are small, almost vestigial. They consume little besides the light-infused sap, absorbing what they need directly into their beings. Any sound that escapes their lips most closely resembles the hum and pulse of the city.

There is no concept of individual clothing. Instead, they adorn themselves with living ornaments. Vines of glowing lichen are woven into intricate headdresses, cascading down their backs like shimmering waterfalls. Polished fungal resins are shaped into delicate breastplates, resonating with the vibrations of the Arbor Vitae. The lines between self and surrounding blur until the two are indivisible.

The elders, distinguishable only by the deeper, more intricate patterns woven into their living garments and the more profound glow in their eyes, guide the newborn through a slow, wordless ritual. They are led to specific points in the network, where they attune themselves to the local vibrations. The elders then place a hand on the crown of one and all glows a little brighter, and suddenly another sapling has taken root in this strange city.

Each Weaver is born with an inherent understanding of its purpose within the network. It understands the specific rhythm of the Arbor Vitae in its assigned zone and the precise techniques required to maintain its health and vitality. There is no need for formal instruction or apprenticeship. Knowledge resonates through their being and the blood-sap it pumps, a legacy passed down through the fungal network itself.

The Mycelial Weavers do not sleep, or at least, not in the human sense. They enter periods of deep stillness, becoming almost statuesque, their bodies absorbing energy from the Arbor Vitae. During this stasis, their minds remain active, processing information gathered from the network, anticipating needs, and adapting to subtle shifts in the environment. It is during this stillness that the Weaver becomes more fully part of the city.

They possess no name or concept of personal identity. To them, such a concept is perhaps irrelevant, even detrimental. The Weaver is simply a node, a conduit, a facilitator in the grand, ongoing process of the Arbor Vitae. Individuality is subsumed by the collective consciousness of the fungal network.

There is a quiet, profound beauty in their existence, a strange harmony in their servitude. Their lives are not their own, but they are inextricably linked to something much larger, much older, and much more mysterious than themselves. They represent the ultimate expression of symbiosis, a testament to the power of life to adapt, to connect, and to create a civilization built not on conquest, but on co-existence. They hear the whispers of the great tree and weave in time with the great heart. They are the city's song.
