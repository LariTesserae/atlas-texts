The Scavengers are the inheritors of the Flats, though inheritors hardly seems the right word for creatures living under such a burdensome legacy. They are not born of this place, but shaped by it, extruded from its poisonous womb like misshapen metal ingots. Calling them creatures almost feels like a misnomer. They are more like animated components, bits and pieces of the old world cobbled together and granted the spark of life.

Picture insects, but scaled closer to the size of badgers or small dogs. Their carapaces aren’t chitinous, but forged from flakes of rust-colored metal, layered like sedimentary rock and constantly shedding tiny, gritty scales. These plates articulate with a disconcerting, jerky precision, revealing glimpses of oily black flesh underneath. Some bear patches of the iridescent bacterial film, clinging to their bodies like shimmering shrouds, pulsing with an unsettling light.

Their legs are spindly, multi-jointed affairs tipped with magnetic pads or sharp, vibrating mandibles that allow them to cling to the slick metal surfaces and carve away at the bacterial colonies. They move with a skittering, clicking gait, their bodies low to the ground, constantly sniffing and probing with antenna-like appendages that bristle with sensors.

Their heads are the most disturbing aspect. They are small and almost vestigial, buried deep within the armored shell of their bodies. Some have simple, multifaceted eyes that glint with a dull, metallic sheen. Others possess only recessed pits, relying on heat sensors and chemical receptors to navigate the dim, polluted light. Their mouths are not mouths at all, but rather complex arrays of rasping plates and filter-like appendages that siphon up the oily sludge and bacterial mats. They never speak; they only hiss.

Most possess some kind of external, non-organic augmentation. Perhaps a repurposed sensor array scavenged from a broken drone, grafted onto their carapace like a parasitic growth. Or a length of corroded cable, trailing behind them like a useless tail, sparking intermittently with residual energy. These augmentations are not intentional modifications, but rather the random accumulation of debris, somehow integrated into their biology.

The Scavengers are solitary creatures, by and large, driven by the constant, gnawing hunger that echoes the Engines' own insatiable appetite. They exist in a perpetual state of scarcity, competing for the limited resources of the Flats. Skirmishes between them are brutal, short-lived affairs, often resulting in shattered carapaces and spilled fluids. There is little in the way of complex social bonds or hierarchies. Only survival.

However, beneath the radar of the Engines, in the deepest, darkest shadows under their metal foundations, lies what appears to be a place for communication and collection. The scavengers meet at the point of origin for certain metallic rivers that run down from the bellies of the Engines. It is in this place that some say the scavengers commune in the primordial language of magnetic hum, mechanical grind, and metallic hiss.

They are adapted to the compromised physics of the Flats. They can withstand extreme temperatures, high levels of radiation, and the constant vibrations of the Engines. Their bodies are saturated with heavy metals, rendering them almost immune to the toxic environment. They are, in essence, living filters, processing the waste of the old world and converting it into the energy needed to sustain themselves.

They are the ghosts of industry, reborn in a twisted, metallic form. They are the living embodiment of entropy, shuffling through the ruins of a forgotten civilization, forever searching for something to consume, something to keep them alive in the stagnant, decaying world of the Flats. They exist simply because the Flats allow them to exist, and they will continue to exist until the Engines themselves finally succumb to the relentless corrosion, leaving them to starve in the silence of a truly dead world.
