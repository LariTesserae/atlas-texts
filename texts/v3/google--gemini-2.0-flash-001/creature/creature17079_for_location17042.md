Those who dwell in Khthon are born of its substance. They are the city's thoughts made flesh, its ambitions given form. To call them "people" feels inadequate, a category too small to contain their alien shapes and purposes. Still, they are alive in their own way, animated by the same strange energies that power the city itself.

Imagine beings of iron and brass, their bodies pieced together from gears, pistons, and plates of riveted metal. Steam hisses from their joints, and their eyes glow with the cold light of plasma. They move with a slow, deliberate grace, their footsteps echoing through the cobbled streets. These are the Servitors, the city's hands and feet. They maintain its infrastructure, repair its machines, and carry out its inscrutable commands.

Then there are the Plasmids, creatures of living metal that flow and coalesce like mercury. They seep through cracks in the stonework, filling the city's arteries and veins. They are the city's blood, its nervous system, carrying information and raw materials from one place to another. They communicate through pulses of light and electrical discharges, their thoughts rippling through the city's fabric.

Consider the Bio-Mechanoids, grotesque parodies of organic life. They are cobbled together from salvaged components, their bodies a jumble of metal, flesh, and circuitry. Some are vaguely humanoid, with limbs too long or faces too asymmetrical. Others are more alien, resembling insects or crustaceans or things that have never existed in nature. They are the experiments, the failed prototypes, the discarded dreams of Khthon's creators. Yet they have their own functions, their own desires. They swarm the streets, scavengers and parasites, feeding on the city's waste and each other.

And there are the Architects, the elder beings who dwell in the city's highest towers. They are the planners, the thinkers, the ones who guide Khthon's endless construction. They are rarely seen, their forms obscured by layers of metal and stone. Some say they are more machine than flesh, their minds integrated directly into the city's vast computer network. Others claim they are immortal, relics of a forgotten age, still driven by the same grand designs that birthed Khthon.

These beings do not experience the world as humans do. Their senses are different, their perceptions skewed. They see and hear and feel things that are beyond human comprehension. They are driven by purposes that are alien and inscrutable. They are not good or evil, not kind or cruel, but simply *other*.

They do not yearn for the sun or the taste of fresh water. They do not dream of love or friendship. Their lives are bound to Khthon, their fates intertwined with its own. They are the city's children, its guardians, its extensions. They exist to serve its purposes, to carry out its will, to ensure its continued survival.

They speak in guttural chants and machine-code whispers. They communicate through the clatter of gears and the hiss of steam. They worship the city itself, its vastness and complexity, its endless churning and grinding. They see it as a god, a living machine that provides for their needs and guides their actions.

They are not individuals in the human sense. They are parts of a larger whole, cogs in a vast machine. But within that system, they have their own roles, their own responsibilities, their own strange satisfactions. They are the lifeblood of Khthon, the souls of its machines. They are the beings who make it more than just a city, but a living, breathing entity, a testament to the boundless ingenuity of its unknown creators.
