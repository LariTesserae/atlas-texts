The inhabitants of this place are children of the green. Not in the metaphorical sense of being protectors or tenders, but born wholly and completely of the jungle's substance. They are beings molded, formed and fueled by the dense riot of life that has become a single, monolithic being.

They are not human, not in the strictest sense. Perhaps once they were, or perhaps the jungle simply sculpted them from the same raw materials, the same basic template it uses for every creature within its borders. They resemble humans but carry within themselves the strange qualities suffusing the air. Their skin is not flesh, but a patchwork of woven leaves and bark, constantly shedding and regrowing in a perpetual molting. Vines twist and coil within their bodies, acting as the arteries which feed them the valley's strange energies. Sunlight filters through the gaps in their patchwork bodies, casting dappled shadows on the undergrowth. They are walking, talking ecosystems, the line between organism and environment blurred beyond recognition.

They walk and crawl with movements unlike those of humans. Limbs seem to bend backwards, muscles stretch too far, and movement appears stilted. But there is an inherent grace to their movement. Like the monkeys and snakes and insects, there is an innate efficiency in their bodies, built not to last, but to be useful, disposable, and replaced.

Their faces are masks of molded clay and woven roots, roughly humanoid but eerily still. If emotion crosses their features, it is communicated through the shifting arrangement of leaves upon their heads, or the slow unfurling of a hidden blossom near their hearts. The clay, too, can be molded, but slowly, as if their minds are not quick enough to keep up.

They are silent, communicating through the subtle shifts in the jungle's chorus. A snapped twig here, a rustle of leaves there, a perfectly timed croak – all form a complex language only they can understand. They also exude pheromones, too weak to be detected, but just strong enough for them to be drawn together or kept apart.

Their understanding of the jungle is not intellectual, but visceral. They feel its rhythms in their bones, know its secrets through the touch of their root-like fingers. They are extensions of the jungle itself, eyes and ears of the ecosystem, responding instinctively to its needs and whims.

They are tool users, but in a way that is both natural and disturbing. The stone axes they wield feel as much a part of them as their own limbs. They weave nets not as a craft, but as an instinctive expression of their understanding of the jungle's geometry, a seamless extension of the vines that permeate their bodies. They could not have made the tools on their own, nor could they have conceived of traps. The valley has given them free expression to utilize its gifts.

They are drawn to the strange, pulsing lights that flicker in the depths of the jungle, not out of curiosity, but out of a deep, primal need. They gather around these lights, absorbing their energy, replenishing themselves, becoming more like the jungle and less like themselves.

They are not individuals, but extensions of a single, vast consciousness. Each is a node in a network, connected by the unseen fungal threads and the hum of the valley. They share thoughts, feelings, and sensory experiences in a constant, unbroken stream.

They are sustained by the cycle of life and death that permeates the jungle. They consume decaying matter, drawing sustenance from the very compost that sustains the ecosystem. They are also consumed in turn, their bodies breaking down, feeding the soil, becoming one with the jungle once more.

They are both terrifying and beautiful, a testament to the power of nature to reshape and redefine life. They are a reflection of the jungle itself – chaotic, relentless, and ultimately unknowable. They are the children of the green, and they are the future of this strange, vibrant world.
