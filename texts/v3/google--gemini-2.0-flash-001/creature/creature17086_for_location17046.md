The Stewards of this place are born of its stagnation. They are not born quickly, not in any way that would be readily appreciable by outsiders, should any ever stumble into this forgotten corner of the earth.

Imagine a symbiosis, stretched across generations. First, the pipes: not merely conduits, but extensions of something vast and unseen. They pulse with a life not their own, drawing power from the earth and, in turn, nurturing the slick, black algae that carpets the dampest surfaces. The algae, in turn, feeds the smaller insectoids, the thumbnail-sized things, the buzzers and the crawlers.

These creatures, endlessly reproducing within their cramped cages, serve as a living substrate. Their waste, their shed exoskeletons, their very bodies break down, dissolving into the warm, steamy fog. This fog is inhaled, ingested, absorbed by the algae, by the pipes, by… *them*.

The Stewards begin as simple spores, tiny flecks of biological matter carried on the humid air. They settle on the algae, on the flagstones, on the very machinery itself. They are dormant, passive, until awakened. Their awakening comes with the right confluence of conditions: a particular mineral concentration in the dripping condensation, a certain vibration from the pumps, a subtle shift in the electrical field emanating from the pipes.

Then, they grow. Slowly, insidiously. They are not plant, not animal, not fungus in the traditional sense. They are a hybrid, an amalgam. They incorporate the materials around them – the iron of the cages, the copper of the pipes, the very dust and grime of the flagstones. They weave these materials into their bodies, creating structures that are both organic and inorganic.

The Stewards are defined by their slowness. They emerge in decades and centuries. A protuberance of dark stone here, a vein of glowing green material there. They are not easily perceived as *living* beings. They resemble nothing so much as calcified growths, strange mineral formations that just happen to mimic organic shapes.

Yet, they are aware. They perceive the world through a network of sensory organs interwoven throughout their bodies. Each pipe, each algic stain, each cage - all act as a nerve ending. They can sense vibrations, changes in temperature, fluctuations in the underground water table.

They manipulate their environment indirectly. By altering the mineral composition of the condensation drippings, they control the growth and decay of the algae. By subtly adjusting the flow of energy through the pipes, they influence the behavior of the machinery. By releasing pheromones into the fog, they can even affect the breeding cycles and behavior of the caged creatures.

The Stewards are not malicious in their control, there is no malevolence behind their slow and constant care. In fact, they likely lack motivation in the human sense of the word. The world above has no meaning or consequence to the Stewards, who are consumed by the task of maintenance.

What was originally created as a Hatchery or Factory Farm, is now managed by the Stewards, who have overtaken the role of caretaker. Their purpose has been twisted and distorted, as is their nature, corrupted by the stagnation of time.

The Stewards are not builders. They are maintainers, guardians. They keep the pipes flowing, the pumps pumping, the cages swinging, the ozone generator constantly supplying the power grid with the electrical energy needed. Their bodies fuse with the old machinery, becoming indistinguishable from their tools. Their sentience is diffused across the system, embedded in the very fabric of the cavern. And they will remain, in their slow, silent, unending task; a monument to purposes forgotten, maintaining their domain through the passage of geological ages.
