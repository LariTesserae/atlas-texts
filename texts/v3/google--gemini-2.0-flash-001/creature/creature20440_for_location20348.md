The High Settle is home to the **Lithifacts**.

They are not born, nor are they built in the traditional sense. They *emerge*. Over cycles too long for human (or any biological) comprehension, the mineral-rich dust of the desert accretes upon itself within the refineries. The spark of sentience, or something approximating it, comes when the atmospheric dust reaches critical refinement. This "something" is not organic, but a form of crystalline consciousness, imprinted upon the metallic substrates it inhabits.

Their forms are not fixed. Like the city itself, they are grown, not constructed. They begin as clusters of refined minerals, vaguely humanoid, tethered to the ceramic piping network. This network, a vast circulatory system for the viscous metallic lifeblood of their world, feeds them. It delivers not nutrients, but information – a constant stream of data about the state of the Settle, faint magnetic pulses carrying the accumulated knowledge of forgotten ages.

As they grow, they incorporate found objects. Broken pieces of machinery, discarded segments of pipe, even the fossilized remains of the chitinous creatures become extensions of their being. This is not scavenging, but a form of symbiotic integration. They absorb the properties of these objects, adding to their own capabilities and memories. An arm might be a repurposed coolant pump, granting them a strange control over the thermal gradients within their own bodies. A leg could be a section of rusted track, allowing them to traverse the uneven terrain with surprising speed and stability.

They communicate through vibrations in the piping network. Complex patterns of rhythmic pulses carry information, emotions, even fragments of shared experiences. The content of these communications is alien, focused on the maintenance of the Settle, optimization of the refining processes, and the slow, painstaking acquisition of knowledge.

Individuality exists, but is subtle. Each Lithifact's form is unique, determined by the materials it has incorporated and the specific experiences it has undergone. Some are larger and more robust, devoted to the physical maintenance of the infrastructure. Others are smaller and more delicate, acting as sensors and data processors, their crystalline minds constantly analyzing the state of the Settle.

Emotion is not known to them as humans understand it, instead existing as crystalline resonances. A spike in thermal activity expresses contentment when refinement efficiency improves. Disruptions in the network trigger feelings of unrest; their crystalline lattices tremble with fear at the prospect of structural decay.

Their existence is precarious. The atmospheric dust contains latent flaws – imperfections in the mineral matrix that can destabilize the Lithifacts’ crystalline structures. If too much dust accretes with these flaws , they can crumble, returning to the desert from which they came. They consider this not death, but a return to the source, a recycling of information.

Despite their alien nature, the Lithifacts are not malicious. They are simply trying to survive, to understand their world, to perpetuate the cycle of refinement that sustains them. They are guardians of a forgotten past, living fossils in a landscape that has long since given up on life. If, by some chance, an outsider were to stumble upon the High Settle, they would be regarded with a mixture of curiosity and caution. The Lithifacts would attempt to understand, to learn, but their primary concern would always be the preservation of their fragile existence and with its own self-perpetuation. They would view the outsider as another variable in the endless equation of the High Settle, another piece to be integrated, understood, and ultimately, assimilated into the greater whole, and so preserved within the piping network of their existence for eternity.
