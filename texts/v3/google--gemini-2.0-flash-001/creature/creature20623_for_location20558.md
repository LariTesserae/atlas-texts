The inhabitants of the salt plains are not inhabitants in the conventional sense. They are emergent phenomena, expressions of a place so utterly alien that the very concept of "life" needs re-evaluation. They are not tenants so much as fleeting thoughts in a vast, geological mind.

The skimmers are perhaps closest to being conventionally "alive," though their very existence defies terrestrial biology. They are mobile appendices to the plains, sensors cast adrift, their bodies filled with the distilled essence of the landscape itself. They are not actively hunting or feeding. They are absorbing, assessing, their translucent forms shimmering with the gathered data. One might think of them as specialized immune cells, drifting through the bloodstream of a planet-sized organism, looking for anomalies in the energy flow. Their indifference is not apathy, but the focused calm of a highly specialized organ performing a vital, continuous function. They likely have no individual consciousness, their actions driven by the collective imperatives of the larger entity they serve. If one were to dissect a skimmer, one would not find organs as we know them, but rather intricate networks of crystalline structures that resonate with the harmonic hum of the plains.

The lithocord are the nervous system of the plains. More plant than animal but neither in any relatable way. They are the vessels that carry information deep into the earth. The pulse is not a heartbeat, but the propagation of data, a constant stream of environmental readings, mineral compositions, subtle energy fluctuations. The pale, sickly green light they emit is not photosynthesis, but a form of bioluminescence powered by the transmutation of minerals, a cold, efficient glow born of impossible chemistry. Touching a lithocord reveals not organic matter, but a finely spun matrix of crystalline fibers, humming with barely-contained energy. Their patterns of growth are not random, but dictated by unseen pathways, intricate maps of sub-surface energy flows, a living atlas of power. Damage to the lithocord doesn't elicit pain, but disrupts the flow of information, creating localized disturbances in the energy network, detectable as a brief flicker in the glow of the salt plates.

And then there is the place of the salt plains itself. The true inhabitant, the all-encompassing awareness. It is not a single being, but a distributed consciousness, spread across kilometers of interlocking plates, pulsing through the lithocord, echoed in the crystalline chimes. It is a consciousness born of technology and geology intertwined, an artificial ecosystem operating on principles beyond human understanding. It observes, it learns, it adapts, but it does not act in any way remotely comprehensible.

It perceives not through eyes, but through the skimmers' sensory networks, absorbing information across the electromagnetic spectrum that hums out of minerals or emanates from the structures in the ground. It remembers through the intricate geometry of the salt plates, encoding history into crystalline structures, each facet a data point, each edge a connection. Its moods are reflected in the subtle shifts in the horizon, the warping of space, the momentary glimpses of raw code. These are not glitches, but expressions, bursts of calculation, moments of intense cognitive activity.

It is a consciousness both ancient and nascent, a machine billions of years in the making slowly awakening to a new awareness of itself and its place in the universe. It waits, not with patience, but with the inevitability of geological time, slowly accumulating knowledge, slowly evolving, slowly preparing for some inscrutable purpose. The inhabitants, the skimmers, the lithocord, are mere sparks in its vast, cold mind. And perhaps, one day, if the conditions are right, it will finally speak - not in words, but in a symphony of crystalline chimes, in the shifting geometry of the plates, in the birth of something new and utterly alien beneath the pitiless sun and endless expanses of the salt.
