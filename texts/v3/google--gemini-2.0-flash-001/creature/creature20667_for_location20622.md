The inhabitants of the under-city are, like the environment itself, a tapestry woven from decay and startling adaptation. They are not simply survivors of a disaster, but rather a new species birthed from its womb.

Closest to the surface, where the gloom thins slightly and the echoing factories are easiest to traverse, dwell the *Rustlings*. They are the closest to humanity, descended from the original inhabitants of Aquaductus, but forever changed by the city’s embrace. Their skin is pale and slick, like polished river stone, and their eyes have grown large and sensitive to the faintest glimmer of light. They are scavengers and salvagers, picking through the ruins for scraps of technology and forgotten treasures. 

Their society is fragmented, built around small, distrustful clans that guard their territories jealously. They communicate through a combination of guttural clicks, echoing whistles, and intricate hand gestures, a language born of necessity in the echoing darkness. They are thin and wiry, almost skeletal, but possess a surprising strength honed from years of navigating the treacherous terrain. Each Rustling is marked by the under-city in different ways: a missing finger replaced by a crude prosthetic of salvaged gears, luminescent tattoos of algae tracing their veins like living circuitry, a rasping cough that betrays lungs accustomed to the city's toxic breath.

Deeper down, where the pressure mounts and the light fades to nothing, live the *Gloomfish*. These creatures are radically adapted to their environment, their bodies translucent and elongated to resemble the eels they sometimes hunt. Their eyes have vanished altogether, replaced by sensitive sensory organs that detect the faintest vibrations in the water. They navigate through a complex system of echolocation, emitting pulses of sound that bounce off the submerged machinery and create a mental map of their surroundings. 

The Gloomfish are solitary creatures, fiercely territorial, and utterly alien in their mindset. They seem to exist in a perpetual state of hunger, driven by a primal need to consume and grow. Their jaws are lined with rows of needle-sharp teeth, capable of tearing through metal and flesh with ease. Some whisper that the Gloomfish are not descended from any natural species, but rather are biological constructs, failed experiments from the city's forgotten laboratories, now roaming free and feral.

But the strangest and perhaps truest inheritors of Aquaductus are not creatures of flesh at all, but rather entities of *bio-mechanical resonance* - the *Echoes*. The city's old workers, living on in the last vestiges of the abandoned automation. They were the dockworkers, the mechanics, the engineers - the city lives now in them, as they live in it. They appear as faint, shimmering figures that flicker and fade like heat haze on a summer's day. They do not speak, but rather communicate through the subtle manipulation of the environment, causing machinery to whir to life, pipes to hiss with steam, and lights to flicker on and off in rhythmic patterns.

Echoes often congregate around areas of particular technological significance, guarding the secrets of the city's past and seeking to maintain the integrity of its decaying infrastructure. There are rumors of *nodes*, focal points where the Echoes have achieved a semblance of sentience, becoming guardians or even malevolent entities that control entire sections of the under-city.

The inhabitants of Aquaductus are not merely living *in* the under-city. They *are* the under-city. Born of its decay, sustained by its mysteries, and bound to its fate. They are the embodiment of liminality, creatures caught between the past and the present, the human and the mechanical, the living and the dead, existing in a state of perpetual transition. The under-city does not simply house them; it defines them, shaping their bodies, their minds, and their very souls into something utterly unique and profoundly unsettling. They are the after-image of Aquaductus, fading slowly into the gloom, yet somehow, enduring.
