Life on Xylos is a study in adaptation to a young world. It clings to the towering root-trees, drinks from the viscous sky-sea, and dances in the perpetual twilight. 

The most obvious inhabitants are the plants themselves. These are not the passive flora of a terrestrial world, but active participants in the island's slow ballet of growth and decay. The root-trees, architects of this strange ecosystem, exist in a state of perpetual expansion, their tendrils reaching out to claim new territory, their ancient cores slowly cooling. They are both the foundation and the flesh of Xylos, providing sustenance and shelter for all who dwell within their embrace.

Then there are the fungi, luminous ecosystems in miniature. They cluster around the bases of the root-trees, their gentle glow a beacon in the dim light. These are not mere decomposers, but alchemists, transforming the decaying matter of the island into new forms of energy. They are the recyclers, the sustainers, the silent engines of Xylos's metabolism.

Above, the epiphytes form hanging gardens, a riot of color and texture clinging to the rough bark of the root-trees. Orchids with petals like stained glass, bromeliads that cup the sky-water in their leaves, and mosses that carpet the landscape in velvet green. These are the opportunists, the aerial acrobats, the ones who have mastered the art of living on air and light.

And then there are the insects, jewels of chitin and bioluminescence. Enormous beetles with iridescent shells crawl through the undergrowth, their mandibles crunching on decaying matter, their bodies gleaming like living gems. Swarms of tiny gnats dance in the twilight, their lights painting ephemeral patterns in the air, a living constellation tracing ever-changing paths.

But perhaps the most enigmatic inhabitant of Xylos is the Grasper. These creatures are patient, slow, and quiet as they rasp at the root-trees, drawing nutrients from their living flesh. Their forms are unknown, hidden high in the canopy, their presence only betrayed by the faint rasping sounds that drift through the air. Are they parasites, or something more? Are they merely consumers, or do they play a vital role in the island's ecosystem? The Grasper remains an enigma, a mystery shrouded in the twilight of Xylos.

Life on Xylos is a tapestry woven from strange threads. It is a world of slow growth and silent decay, of gentle light and pervasive moisture, of constant adaptation and endless mystery. It is a world where life thrives in the space between stone and sky-water, powered by the inexorable pulse of the Sky-Tree. The creatures of Xylos are shaped by this world, and in turn, they shape it, participating in the slow, steady dance of creation and destruction that defines this strange and beautiful realm.
