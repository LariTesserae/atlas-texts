They are children of corrosion, heirs to the husk of a god-beast. 

Imagine them first as echoes. The clank of scavenged metal on stone is their laughter, the rust-colored dust their breath. They move like fluid through tight cracks, ghosts in the groaning bones of the Citadel. Their lungs filter the iron tang, their eyes, wide and luminous in the perpetual twilight, are pools reflecting the strange crystallizing lights.

Their faces are rarely seen; respirators are second skin, woven from salvaged fibers and fitted with crude filters of lichen and crushed ore. Their hands, though, tell stories. Leathery and calloused, stained crimson by the ubiquitous rust, they are adept at coaxing droplets of the precious oily water from the stone, nimble in the assembly of makeshift dwellings within the skeletal buildings.

Clothing is patchwork armor, layers upon layers of scavenged fabric, each piece a whisper of lives lived and lost within the Citadel. They wear the history of the Rust District upon their bodies: faded glyphs from a forgotten age, tears mended with metallic thread, patches reinforced with crystallized minerals. The colors are muted, blending into the dull landscape, but glints of polished metal often adorn them – buckles, clasps, fragments of ornamentation, each one a tiny act of defiance against the overwhelming monochrome.

They are solitary by necessity, a consequence of scarcity. Cooperation is possible, even preferable, but trust is a fragile commodity in this environment. Alliances shift like sand dunes, built on mutual need and the ever-present threat of betrayal.

Born of the Rust District, they carry a primal connection to the decaying shell. They understand the groans of the metal under stress, the subtle vibrations that presage a cave-in, the location of the most fertile lichen patches. They are the cartographers of corrosion, knowing every crack and crevice, every hidden pocket of resourcefulness.

Their culture, if it can be called that, is a pragmatic one. Stories are not tales of grand heroes or lost empires, but practical knowledge passed down through generations: how to identify potable water, how to avoid the creatures that lurk in the shadows, how to repair a respirator with limited tools. Their art is functional: intricately woven filters, carefully balanced structures built into the decaying walls, even the subtle arrangement of scavenged metal that creates a sense of refuge within their dismal surroundings.

But there is a deeper current that runs within them, a memory of something more. In the eyes of the elders, one can glimpse a flicker of recognition, as if fragments of the Citadel's original purpose still echo within their bones. They rarely speak of it, but there is a sense of waiting, of a latent potential waiting to be unlocked.

And now, the awakening. The geothermal pulse resonates within them, stirring something dormant. Fear, yes, but also a strange exhilaration. The elemental fires reflect in their luminous eyes, as a change begins to occur.

They are attuned to the land and the heat. It is not a destructive force, but a release, a surge of primeval energy. As the oxidation comes alive, they see the strange crystals grow, and their understanding of the place shifts. It is not just a dead shell, but a chrysalis.

They gather at the fissures, drawn by an ancient instinct. They are not merely scavengers anymore, but participants in a grand, chaotic alchemy. Some begin to chant in a language that has not been spoken for eons. Some carve symbols into the metal walls, responding to the patterns formed by the flames. They become conduits for the energy, their bodies humming with the rhythm of the Citadel's awakening.

New things will be born, not from flesh and blood, but from metal and fire. The Rust District will not remain silent. And the children of corrosion, bathed in the hellish glow of geothermal rebirth, will be its voice. They are not just survivors, but the seed of something entirely new, something that will rise from the ashes of the old god-beast and claim its inheritance. The heat draws out new sides of them, of their potential. What was potential before is now the only way forward. And so they take it.
