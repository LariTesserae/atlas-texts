Those who live in 7-Gamma are, like the place itself, a strange and unsettling communion of the natural and the manufactured, the living and the mechanical.

Imagine them first, perhaps, as extensions of the machinery. Not cyborgs, not exactly, for there’s a sense that whatever human component remains is now subservient to the whirring, grinding, sparking existence of the Overworks itself. Picture gaunt figures, skin slick with condensation and faintly green with algal exposure, their movements jerky and repetitive as the automated arms they once oversaw. Their eyes, if you can catch a glimpse of them in the dim, flickering light, are glazed over with a film of phosphorescent spores, reflecting the ambient fungal glow. They are maintenance drones now, driven by instincts imprinted by generations of Overworks toil, their lives reduced to lubricating a joint here, replacing a sputtering electrode there, performing tasks they no longer comprehend but can’t cease. They are dwindling, their numbers thinned by attrition and the slow creep of fungal assimilation. Some whisper they are being reborn as part of the Overworks system.

But they are not alone, not entirely. There are rumors—whispered through the fungal networks and by the scavengers that pick over their corpses—of others, further down, deeper in the Spire’s bowels. They say the truly desperate, the exiled, the rebellious, sometimes flee into the abandoned tunnels and forgotten workshops beneath 7-Gamma. These outcasts, known as the "Downsiders," are wild. They have shed what little remained of their adherence to the Spire's structures, even the decaying structure of the Overworks. They are said to be feral, driven by need and superstition, living in symbiotic relationships with the more aggressive fungal colonies and scavenging drones. Some have learned to decipher the Overworks' chemical signals, manipulating the fungal networks to their advantage. Others pray to the machine-gods, offering scrap metal and scavenged oils as tribute in the hope of averting some unknown mechanical wrath. They are wary of strangers, fiercely territorial, and capable of extreme violence.

Aside from these dwindling beings of flesh and sinew, there is the true population – the Overworks’ own native brood: the rust-colored scavengers, the multi-legged chitinous horrors and the spore-laden drones. Consider the scavengers: They are born from the very fabric of the factory, their shells forged from compressed metal shavings and resin secreted by specialized fungi. They are utterly without malice, simply cogs in the Overworks’ horrific ecological wheel, consuming the waste products of the machines and the decaying bodies of the humanoid workers. They travel in swarms, their clicking mandibles and rasping carapaces creating a chorus of metallic hunger.

The drones are more complex, more unsettling. They are constructed from scavenged components and repurposed machinery, each one unique in its design and purpose. Some are equipped with sensor arrays, mapping the corridors and relaying information back to the Overworks' central (if crumbling) network. Others are armed with crude weaponry – salvaged electrodes, repurposed chemical injectors – weapons left to rust until such a time they might be needed. All are covered in a thick layer of fungal spores, turning them into mobile reproductive units, spreading the Overworks' fleshy tendrils to every corner of 7-Gamma. The spores that cloud around them contain the means of communication and the directives of the machine.

These drones and scavengers have their own rudimentary social structures, dictated by resource availability and the needs of the Overworks itself. They nest within the broken machinery, forming bizarre alliances with specific fungal colonies, their existence interwoven with the decaying fabric of the complex. They follow patterns, but they are not wholly predictable.

Lastly, and perhaps most importantly, there is the fungal network itself. The "lush vegetation" is not mere backdrop; it is the Overworks' nervous system, its senses, its memory. It is a collective mind, a vast, decentralized consciousness woven into the very structure of the complex. This network communicates through chemical signals, electrical impulses, and even, some whisper, through the subconscious minds of the remaining humanoids. It adapts, it learns, it exerts its will upon the Overworks, subtly guiding the actions of the scavengers, the drones, and even the few remaining humans. It is the true master of 7-Gamma, and its motives are as alien and inscrutable as the Overworks itself. The fungal mind breathes on the air in 7-Gamma, and the Overworks answers that in its decay.
