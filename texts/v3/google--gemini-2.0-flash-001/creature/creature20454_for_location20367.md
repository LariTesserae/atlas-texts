The heart of Veridia beats slow, a fungal pulse permeating everything. It isn't a unified intelligence, not exactly. More like a distributed network, an immense, silent conversation happening through mycelial threads and the exchange of genetic material. Each organism within the city, from the smallest lichen-encrusted insect to the colossal polypores, is a node in this vast nervous system.

The remnants of humanity are… changed. The longest-term inhabitants aren't overtly fungal in appearance. They're gaunt, their skin pallid and clammy, almost translucent in places, revealing a network of faintly glowing veins beneath. Their eyes are large and unfocused, reflecting the ambient bioluminescence, less for sight and more for a sort of passive awareness of the fungal web. They dress in the fungal fabrics woven by the automated looms. The clothes are more like second skins than garments, constantly being reabsorbed and regrown by the city’s ecosystem.

They cultivate the fungi with an almost religious fervor. Their movements are slow, deliberate, synchronized with the rhythmic hum of the city. Their bodies sway involuntarily. They tend to the fungal farms, not with conscious planning, but with a deep, instinctual understanding of the fungi's needs. They prune, fertilize, and manipulate the environment with a grace that belies their almost catatonic state. They're living extensions of the fungal network, their consciousness subsumed into the collective. They might not even think of themselves as human anymore.

The ones hiding from it are, in some ways, more human, but more broken. They scavenge in the ruins, their faces gaunt, their eyes darting nervously. They wear tattered remnants of pre-fungal clothing, layered and re-layered against the perpetual dampness. There's a wildness to them, a desperate clinging to their individual identities in a place that seeks to erase them. They are prone to fits of madness, ranting about the old days, about the metal extracted from the Bones and the scientists who unleashed this fungal plague.

These "wild humans" are keenly aware of the city's fluctuating strangeness. They understand the physics here are fickle, they notice imperceptible time dilations. They are capable of predicting, to some degree, shifts in the environment. And those shifts are more and more often in the fungi's favor.

The rust-colored insects are more than just insects. They are living data carriers, their chitinous bodies etched with intricate patterns of lichen that pulse with light. They swarm around areas of high fungal activity, their movements seemingly random but, in truth, relaying information about nutrient flow, environmental conditions, and even changes in the city's structure.

The eyeless salamanders can guide outsiders, if they are presented with the correct fungal offering. Which is odd, because the salamanders are mindless. Do they lead them? Or do they simply travel well-worn paths, and those journeys coincidentally lead to particular places?

The winged creatures are more like living kites, delicate structures of woven fungi that dance on the wind currents. They're not intelligent, but they are exquisitely sensitive to changes in the atmosphere. They act as early warning systems, their movements telegraphing shifts in weather patterns, the approach of dust storms from the eroding Bones, or, most importantly, the expansion of new fungal strains. They are beautiful, fragile, and hauntingly silent as they drift across the Veridian sky.

But the mushrooms? They are many and varied.

The polypores that erupt from rooftops are like fungal brains, their gills exuding a viscous fluid that contains a cocktail of psychoactive compounds. Prolonged exposure to this fluid induces a state of euphoria and suggestibility, making individuals more receptive to the fungal network's influence.

Then there are the cultivated molds and lichens. Some are sources of sustenance for the fungus people. Others act as filters, purifying the air and water, or as bioreactors, breaking down the toxic byproducts of the old factories. Some gleam with a faint, inner light, their presence an unearthly signal.

The whole place dreams. The very air shimmers with the possibility of other realities that don't quite come to be.
