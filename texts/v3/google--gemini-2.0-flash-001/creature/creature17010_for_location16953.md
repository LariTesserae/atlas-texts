Amidst the bioluminescent decay and the chorus of the strange, the inhabitants navigate a world perpetually caught between a dying past and a relentlessly vibrant present. They are not unified, not a single species easily categorized. Instead, they are a tapestry woven from the threads of biological evolution and technological corruption, a living embodiment of the city's own chaotic reinvention.

Most prevalent are the chitinous scavengers, their exoskeletons gleaming wetly beneath the violet twilight. They possess a grim practicality, their multi-jointed limbs clicking on the slick surfaces as they navigate the treacherous terrain. Some are small, insect-like, swarming in the shadows and feeding on the fungal spores that rain down from above. Others are larger, reptilian or crustacean-like, their bodies augmented with crude, grafted machinery. These augmentations might be a replaced limb, or a mechanical add-on that seems to serve no other purpose than to simply, exist. They carry within them the echo of a technology they no longer comprehend, repurposed for survival in a way that their original creators could never have foreseen. Their eyes, often glowing with an internal luminescence, are hard and calculating, focused solely on the next meal or the avoidance of a predator. There is little room for sentiment or curiosity in their harsh existence.

Then there are the floaters, the gelatinous beings that drift through the air with an otherworldly grace. They seem less concerned with sustenance and survival, driven by some other, more esoteric imperative. They pulse with the same internal light as the city itself, their shimmering tendrils interacting with the environment in ways that are difficult to decipher. They seem to communicate through subtle shifts in color and frequency, a language of light and resonance. While some theorize they are a form of living sensor net for the machinery buried beneath the soil, others see them as remnants of the original inhabitants, their minds now fragmented and dispersed throughout the city's ecosystem. These, even more so than the chitinous scavengers, seem to defy explanation by any familiar natural process. While they are undoubtedly living creatures, their very existence seems to scream blasphemy against the laws of nature.

Predators also stalk this corrupted metropolis. But the most fearsome are the hybridized horrors, beings that have transcended simple augmentation and become something truly alien. They are a blend of flesh and metal, of predator and prey, of technology and biology. One might be a hulking thing, barely contained by an exoskeleton of cracked obsidian, its skull showing through breaks in the corroded plating. Another could be a creature resembling a spider, except that all that would be recognizable from that arachnid form would be the number of legs. The rest would be an amalgam of rusted pipes, broken circuits, and twitching, corrupted flesh. They are apexes of the city's brutal struggle, their bodies testaments to the power of adaptation and the terrifying potential of unchecked technological growth. Their presence carries with it a feeling of dread. Their eyes are always watching; they may or may not be plotting.

Rarely, glimpsed only at the periphery of perception, deeper into the twisting tendrils or within the ruins of ancient structures, are the almost-mythical remnants of the former civilization. They often seem like projections dancing on the edge of vision. Some say that they are ghosts, echoes of the past imprinted upon the city's fabric. Others whisper that they are still alive, that they have transcended their physical forms and become incorporeal entities, living within the network of the city's decaying technology. Whether they are still alive or not is uncertain. Every single sighting of them is completely unverified. But, if the rumors are to be believed, not all of them are ghosts.

Regardless of their precise nature, these creatures are all bound by the city, by its rhythms of decay and rebirth. They are a product of the environment. They are its expression. They are the living proof of the city's enduring power, a testament to its ability to transform and redefine life itself. They continue onwards, regardless of it all, with the low, droning hum of the city as the dirge to their daily struggle.
