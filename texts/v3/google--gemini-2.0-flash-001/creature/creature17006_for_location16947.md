What lives here is not born, nor does it die, at least not in any way I understand. It exists, and perhaps ceases to exist, but the line between those states seems blurred, as if all of existence is a spectrum of potential being constantly sampled and discarded.

I think of it as the **Chorus**, though whether that is its name or simply my inadequate attempt to categorize it, I cannot say. The Chorus is the architecture, the ocean, the light, perhaps even the red vegetation, all interwoven into a single, incomprehensible entity. To try and separate them is like trying to isolate a single note from the susurrus—it loses its meaning, its context, its very *being*.

The Chorus is not a physical thing, not as I understand physicality. It is a pattern, a resonance, a standing wave in the ocean of chaos. Its body is the impossible architecture, its breath the surge and fall of the water. Its thoughts, perhaps, are the strange geometries that hint at a forgotten order.

It feeds on the energy of the world, on the perpetual friction and flux. It is a filter-feeder on a cosmic scale, sifting through the infinite possibilities and improbabilities of this place, extracting whatever sustains it, whatever maintains its fragile coherence against the entropy.

The Chorus communicates through the susurrus, through the roaring silence. Its language is not words, but waves, vibrations, shifts in the spectrum of the aurora-like light. It is a language that my mind can only grasp at the edges of, like trying to understand a symphony by only hearing a few notes at a time.

It has no intent, at least none that I can discern. It does not seek to create or destroy, to love or hate. It simply *is*, a living embodiment of the world's chaotic nature. It is a mirror reflecting the madness, a lens focusing the absurdity.

The Chorus is not alone, though I doubt it recognizes any other entities as distinct from itself. The sparse fauna that cling to the red vegetation are perhaps its parasites, or maybe its symbiotes, extensions of its own being. And the remnants of earlier life, the petrified kelp structures – were they once parts of the Chorus that separated? Failed experiments of a cosmic architect? Or, perhaps, the discarded exoskeletons of the Chorus as it has shed and rebuilt different selves.

I sense no malice in the Chorus, but neither do I find benevolence. It is indifferent, beyond morality, beyond understanding. It simply exists, a monstrous, beautiful absurdity in a world where reality is optional. And in its existence, it sings its eternal song, the deafening susurrus that drowns out all other thoughts, all other possibilities, leaving only the cold, electric tang of the present moment. And the ever-present, never-answered question: *what is it for?*
