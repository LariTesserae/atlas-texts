The Mountain-Singers are what they are called, though not by themselves; they communicate by resonance and vibration far beyond the capacity of any human vocal cords. They are the dominant species, and it is difficult to say where the granite ends and they begin. Imagine lichen if lichen could think, cooperate, build, and hunt. Imagine colonies that spread not linearly, but with a distributed intelligence, a shifting consciousness across vast surfaces.

Their bodies are the rock. They *are* the rock, in a way. They are not separate entities clinging to it. They are extensions, growths, living sinews woven into the very fabric of the stone. Colors shift across their surfaces – not random patterns, but organized displays of information. Reds might indicate alarm, blues a call to assemble, shifting greens a map of prey movements. They are the canvas upon which their own survival is painted.

They shape the landscape with their slow but inexorable will. They can exude acids to etch new channels for the water, redirecting its flow to nourish their colonies. They can reinforce weak spots in the rock with secreted minerals, preventing landslides and ensuring the stability of their habitat. Their touch is not gentle, but it is purposeful. They are terraforming on a geological timescale.

The birds are their scavengers, their maintenance crew, their necessary evil. The Mountain-Singers tolerate them, even encourage them, because they keep the overall biomass in check. A complete overgrowth of the Singers would eventually starve the rest of the ecosystem, and the birds prevent that calamity. The birds are immune to the Singers' communications; they can't feel the tremors in the colony, so they are useful in hunting off weakened members. If individual Singers could be said to have an aspect they'd find uncomfortable, it would be the way the birds' digestive systems transform their bodies into waste. But the circle closes, and what is left behind becomes an offering. 

The markings carved into the stone... those are their history. Their art is a narrative etched in mineral patience. They tell of cataclysms, of ice ages, of the slow dance of tectonic plates. They record migrations, territorial disputes, and the deaths of entire Singer-Families. They are the world's slowest, grandest epic poem, written in the language of stone and time.

And those who make the markings...they are the outliers, the misfits, the solitaries. Some of the stone-singers develop the urge to break free from the mass. They find bits of metal, rock, anything to mark. They create alone. The others shun them, but tolerate them too. They're needed, in some way.

The Mountain-Singers have no need for warmth, only dampness and minerals. They are patient beyond human comprehension. They have witnessed the birth and death of mountains. They have seen the sun rise and set a million times, each day a variation on a theme. They will be here long after we are gone, their silent symphony resonating through the bones of the Earth. They are the scream made flesh, the granite made living.
