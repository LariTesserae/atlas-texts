They are built from patience and desperation. Imagine the tenacity of lichen, the ingenuity of the bowerbird, and then magnify it a thousandfold, infuse it with the grime and grit of scavenged technology.

They are not one species, but a collective entity, woven together by the fungal network as much as by any shared purpose. The avian creatures, their metal beaks clicking against the fungal walls, are the aerial harvesters, gathering spores from the high canopies and patrolling the skylights, guarding against incursions from the world above. Their wings, patched with strips of processed fungus, beat with a frantic energy, a constant reminder of the fight against entropy. Their eyes gleam with a calculating avian intelligence, sharpened by generations of survival in this brutal biome. They are the eyes in the heights and the talons in the upper reaches.

The insects, colossal and unnerving, are the architects and the engineers. Their chitinous armor is often reinforced with metal plates scavenged from the ruins of some forgotten age. They are the earth movers and the builders, the ones who constantly reshape and reinforce the fungal structures. Some secrete the bioluminescent slime, painting the tunnels with their eerie light. Others are the custodians of tools, constantly tinkering with the fungal-powered machinery, their multiple limbs surprisingly deft and precise. They seem unfeeling and automatic, but if one watches closely, you may see the complex calculations of their antenna.

The worms, phosphorescent and sinuous, are the recyclers and the veins of this underworld. They maintain the nutrient cycles, consuming waste and decaying matter and transforming it back into usable form. They burrow through the fungal matrix, their bodies leaving trails of glowing slime, marking the paths and the flows of resources. They are a living sewage systems, a living compost heap, and the hidden arteries of the fungal town. Touch them, and they might give a gentle shock - or the rotting material.

And at the heart of it all, the Queen-Stems. Not beings, but engines of growth and order. They are the source of life, the anchors of each district, pulsing with a slow, steady rhythm. The "rules" may be simple, but within, is a complex and sensitive web of chemical and electromagnetic communications. They decide the shape of the architecture, the allocation of resources, the direction of growth. The health of Stem is survival for its district.

Each creature seems to exist in symbiosis with the artificiality of the place. They do not question the scraps of technology that have been cobbled together, the repurposed machinery, the strange fusions of flesh and metal. It's become part of their existence, an extension of their own biology. When a creature dies, the death is just as symbiotic. Nothing is wasted. The body becomes sustenance for worms, the shell becomes part of a wall, the organs are used for processes - even the thoughts become energy within the queen stem.

There lurks a feeling, however, that the integration is not yet complete. A restlessness, an unease. The constant hum of machinery is the heart beat of industry, but also is the groan of some organism that doesn't quite know that it is. The organic and the artificial are still at war, a constant struggle for dominance.

They are a bizarre, beautiful, and terrifying organism. They are survivors, thriving in the face of adversity, and have adapted in ways that defy imagination. They are building a future in darkness, and that future seems unknowable and inevitable. There is still the question, though, of who is leading who. As the fungus subsumes the scraps, are the creatures the stewards of a biomechancial organism? Or is the machine leading them to fates unknown?

And deeper down, toward the source of the original infection, one wonders what might be.
