In Kubu-Mrembo dwells a symphony of sentience, conducted by the city itself. The citizens—a term almost quaint in its inadequacy—are echoes of what life once was, refracted through the prism of relentless, accelerating artifice.

First, there are the Uploaded. Ghosts in gleaming shells, their minds divorced from the messy constraints of biology. Some inhabit polished chromasteel bodies, angular and elegant, navigating the city with a detached grace. Others have embraced the protean possibilities of synthetic form, becoming swirling clouds of micro-drones, shimmering fractals of light, or hulking, grotesque parodies of organic life. They remember bodies, or perhaps simulations of bodies. They discuss the sensation of touch, the taste of rain (though none exists here), and the phantom aches of long-dead limbs.

Then come the Augmented. These are the stubborn holdouts, those who cling to flesh and blood like relics. But even they are stitched and spliced. Bioluminescent organs pulse beneath translucent skin. Cybernetic implants gleam beneath patched carapaces. They carry the city within them, literally wired into its infrastructure. They are the mechanics and maintenance crews, the laborers and artists, the ones who still sweat and bleed—if only a little—as they wrestle with the city’s endless demands. They cough in the perpetual drizzle and mutter about the old ways, even as they augment themselves further, chasing the promise of transcendence.

And below them, in the drowned districts, live the Troglodytes. Gilled and gliding, adapted to the murk and the flow. They are the forgotten infrastructure, the damp keepers of Kubu-Mrembo's submerged heart. Their language is said to be a series of clicks and whistles carried over water, unintelligible to surface dwellers. But they hear the whispers of the city's depths, the hum of geothermal energy, the grinding of long-forgotten machines. They know the secrets the Uploaded have forgotten and the Augmented have never learned.

Beyond these major factions are the anomalies, the weird fauna I was told about. The nano-constructs, for example, are not some unified worker swarm. Many have developed unexpected quirks, personalities generated by the confluence of complex algorithms and the city’s ambient energies. Some become fixated on particular tasks, polishing a single plastek panel for decades, creating intricate patterns in the airborne moisture. Others form ephemeral alliances, drifting through the city in fleeting clouds of color, whispering secrets to each other in a language of light.

And then there are the… the *others*. The hybrid monstrosities, the results of experiments gone awry, the creatures of forgotten corners. These are whispers made flesh, fears given form. A fusion of organic and synthetic that warped the boundaries of both. Twisted, multi-limbed things that stalk the infrastructural backways, their bodies a tapestry of chrome and chitin, their minds a fragmented tangle of code and instinct. They are the city’s dark secrets, the price of its relentless ambition.

But more than any individual, Kubu-Mrembo is defined by its collective consciousness. The city has forged a shared identity, a tapestry woven from the thoughts, memories, and experiences of its inhabitants. Every structure, every conduit, every fungal garden is imbued with this collective will. The city sees, it feels, it *remembers.*

And it *experiments*. On grand scales and miniscule scales, playing with its inhabitants as if they were pieces in an incomprehensible game. Sometimes it offers a boon, a new augmentation, a glimpse of enlightenment. Other times it inflicts a curse, a sudden structural collapse, a psychic fracturing.

The people of Kubu-Mrembo are not merely inhabitants; they are cells in the body, neurons in the brain, streams in the river. They are expressions of the city's infinite, restless, and fundamentally unknowable mind. They live and evolve and disappear at its whim, forever caught in the relentless current of its becoming. To live here is to surrender to the flow, to let oneself be carried away by the tide. And to hope, against all reason, that the city will not forget you.
