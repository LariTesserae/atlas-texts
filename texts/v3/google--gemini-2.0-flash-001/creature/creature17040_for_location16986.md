The inheritors of this strange alveolus are creatures of adaptation and subtle wrongness. They are pale things, bleached and subtly warped, sculpted by the unnatural energies that seep through the pocket in reality.

First, there are the chitinous masses, the beetles. Not the familiar, earthy beetles of the world above, but elongated, spindly things, their carapaces gleaming with an oily sheen. They possess a multitude of legs, far more than any beetle should reasonably require, which they use with unsettling coordination to scuttle across the slick rock, their movements jerky and unpredictable. Their mandibles are long and thin, tipped with iridescent points that catch the light as they probe the moss and leaf litter for sustenance. They feed upon… well, anything.  The decaying remains of fallen creatures, the luminous moss, even the strange mineral deposits that form on the walls. They are recyclers in a landscape that offers little truly fresh, consuming and reconstituting everything in a perpetual cycle.

Then come the Pale Worms, which pulse with internal light. They weave through the damp earth and cling to the underside of rocks, their bodies segmented and translucent, their bioluminescent stripes flickering and shifting like miniature constellations. They are the burrowers, the silent architects of the alveolus, constantly turning and aerating the soil. They excrete a viscous fluid that nourishes the strange fungi which grow alongside the glowing moss. Their diet consists primarily of decaying organic matter and the microscopic life that thrives in the moist earth. They are, in a sense, the blood of this place, the unseen force that keeps it alive.

Dominating the space – at least by level of awareness – are the Lacertids, the lizard-like beings. These are the most unsettling of all. In essence, they are like small, agile lizards, but with a strangeness that speaks of deeper alteration. Their scales shimmer with an ever-changing iridescence, patterns of light and colour rippling across their bodies as they move. And their eyes… their eyes are too large, too expressive, filled with unnerving intelligence. They seem to _see_ more than just the physical world, to perceive the subtle flows of energy that permeate the alveolus. They stalk the rocks, hunting the beetles and worms, their movements swift and precise. They are also the most curious, the observers. They frequently gather near the pool of glowing water, drawn to its energy, studying the symbols carved into the half-buried column with unnerving fascination.

The creatures are linked by a co-dependency born of necessity and the strange rules of the alveolus. The beetles feed the worms. The worms feed the lizards. The lizards leave… well, the lizards die and feed it all. It is a closed ecosystem, a microcosm of life warped by the energies that seep through the tear in reality.

However, the truth of these creatures goes deeper than observation suggests. The beetles communicate through erratic, near-imperceptible clicking, the worms through bioluminescent pulses, and the lizards… the lizards listen. The humming silence of the cave is in fact a chorus, or a cacophony depending on perspective. They can understand that the strange column is connected to their life, the glowing pool as well, that the opening to the world above is not the sole nature of existence. They know that their world is not the world, and there is a desire, an urge, to learn more even as there is no real way to do so.
