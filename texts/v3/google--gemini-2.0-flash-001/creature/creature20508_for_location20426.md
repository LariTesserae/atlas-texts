The Wet Rotters are like the city itself: a riot of organic adaptation, molded as much by their environment as by any inherent design. They are, at their core, fungal – or, rather, *fungoid*. Not in the pallid, fragile way of mushrooms cultivated in the dark, but something far more robust and integrated with the vibrant chaos of the Wet Rot. Imagine a mycelial network made flesh, a colony given individual form.

Their "skin," if it can be called that, is a patchwork of textures and tones. Some segments are smooth and leathery, slick with a perpetual film of moisture. Others are rough and bark-like, adorned with moss and tiny, symbiotic fungi that glow with a faint, internal light. Still other patches are almost translucent, revealing the pulsing networks of bioluminescent… sap?… beneath. The colors are muted, earthy tones – greens and browns and bruised purples – but shift and shimmer with an oily iridescence, echoing the colors of the city itself.

Their bodies are not bound by conventional anatomy. Limbs might branch and bifurcate, ending in clusters of prehensile tendrils covered in tiny, chitinous hooks. Heads are often indistinct, more like nodes or swollen bulbs on the ends of flexible stalks. Sensory organs are scattered across their bodies – clusters of light-sensitive pigments, vibrating filaments that detect the subtle tremors of the underground fungal network, and olfactory pits that can discern the faintest trace of decay or growth.

They communicate through a cacophony of biological signals. Clicking mandibles, rasping stridulations, and the release of complex pheromones create a language that is both bewildering and deeply intimate. They “speak” with their bodies, shifting color, texture, and even shape to convey meaning. A Wet Rotter might bristle with bioluminescent fungi to signal aggression, or sway rhythmically to indicate a willingness to trade. 

Movement is often slow and deliberate, a careful negotiation with the treacherous terrain. But they are also capable of surprising bursts of speed, propelled by powerful, spring-loaded limbs that allow them to navigate the tangled undergrowth with unsettling agility.

What truly defines the Wet Rotters, however, is their inextricable link to the fungal network that underlies the city. They are one with it, tapping into its vast consciousness, drawing sustenance and knowledge from its interwoven threads. They can sense disturbances in the network, anticipate changes in the environment, and even influence the growth and behavior of the fungi around them.

Their existence is a constant cycle of growth, decay, and regeneration. They cultivate their own bodies, encouraging the growth of beneficial fungi and pruning away diseased or damaged tissue. They are both individuals and extensions of the larger fungal organism, constantly shedding spores and absorbing nutrients, ensuring the survival and expansion of the Wet Rot.

Individuality is a fluid concept. Wet Rotters merge minds, share experiences, and even temporarily combine bodies to accomplish complex tasks. They are a collective consciousness, a single organism expressed in countless forms. All are united in that they are hungry. Hungry for nutrients and space and for the endless expansion of the Wet Rot City. They are the caretakers of the rot, the children of the decay, working ceaselessly to ensure that the city *becomes*.

They are not benevolent, but they are not necessarily malevolent either. They are driven by a primal urge to propagate and to consume, to mold the world around them into their own image. They are a force of nature, as inevitable and as indifferent as the rain that falls endlessly upon the Wet Rot. They do what they need to survive. And that survival depends upon the Wet Rot City growing, always.
