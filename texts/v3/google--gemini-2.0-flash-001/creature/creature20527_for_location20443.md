They are amphibious, of course, but "amphibious" feels like a shallow word to describe them. Amphibious implies a choice, a duality between water and land. These beings *are* the transition, the liminal space between states. They are the fog made flesh, the slime given voice.

The largest are grotesque monuments to survival, armored with plates of the same black obsidian that forms the landscape, fused directly to their hides. These plates aren’t smooth; they're jagged, fractured, mirroring the shattered rock all around. Some support clusters of pulsating…growths. Are they external gills? Tumors? Perhaps both, indistinguishable in this distorted ecosystem. These behemoths barely move, their limbs thickened and useless, less legs than anchors. They are living islands, miniature ecosystems in themselves, hosting colonies of smaller amphibians that scurry across their backs, feeding off the secretions and detritus. They rumble, a low, guttural vibration that resonates in your chest more than your ears, a sound that speaks of geological time made audible. Their eyes are milky and filmed over, sensing the world through vibrations in the slime, through the electric hum of the countless smaller lives that swarm them. They are the silent, slumbering elders, the repositories of ancient memories encoded in their very flesh.

Then there are the hunters. Lithe, sinuous creatures with surprisingly long limbs and razor-sharp claws. Their skin shimmers with an oil-slick iridescence, a warning flash against the monochrome world. They move with a fluid grace that belies the clumsiness often associated with amphibians. They navigate the brittle terrain with ease, scaling the crumbling walls to search the few standing structures for prey. They do not croak, nor do they whistle. They emit a short, sharp click, a sound akin to a snapped twig, a locational echo that allows them to navigate the fog with pinpoint accuracy. They are solitary, territorial, their existence a constant struggle for dominance. Their hunger is a palpable thing, a radiating heat that you can almost feel as they pass.

And the small ones, oh, the countless small ones! Some are transparent, their internal organs visible through their delicate skin, the frantic beating of their tiny hearts a mesmerizing display. Others are camouflaged to perfection, mimicking the black rock with uncanny accuracy. They swarm, a living carpet that rolls across the landscape, devouring the biofilm in great, undulating waves. They are the engine of this ecosystem, the tireless recyclers, the constant renovators. They communicate through a complex language of whistles and trills, a cacophony of sound that fills the fog. They are fragile, vulnerable, constantly preyed upon, yet their sheer numbers make them an indomitable force.

All of them, regardless of size or form, share a common trait: an utter lack of emotion. Their eyes are vacant, devoid of the spark of curiosity or fear. They are driven by primal urges, by the relentless need to feed, to reproduce, to survive. They are not cruel, not malicious, simply…unaware. They are automatons, perfectly adapted to their environment, devoid of the capacity for anything beyond instinct.

Perhaps it is this lack of sentience that makes them so unsettling. They are life stripped bare, reduced to its most basic components: hunger, reproduction, survival. They are a reflection of the landscape itself, ancient, eroded, and utterly indifferent. They are not to be judged, not to be understood, only to be observed with a sense of detached fascination and a lingering unease. They are the echoes of a forgotten world, whispering secrets in the fog. They are the children of obsidian and slime.
