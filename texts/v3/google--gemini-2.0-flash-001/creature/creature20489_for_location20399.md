Within the Ribcage, life is sculpted by the immensity of its surroundings. It is a life born of patience, adaptation, and a profound interconnectedness that transcends individual existence. They are the inheritors and the integrated, the parasites and the partners, and their story is etched in the very bones of their world.

First, there are the *Shell Lords*, the carapaced scavengers who dominate the visible ecosystem. To call them "scavengers" is a surface-dweller's simplification. They are the recyclers, the miners, the builders of this underwater world. Their shells aren't just protection; they are mobile extensions of the Ribcage's geology, each subtly different in composition depending on the specific minerals they've ingested and integrated. The smallest, the dust mites writ large, are like living filters, constantly sifting through the bone-dust, their mandibles delicate chisels that carve microscopic tunnels and release trapped nutrients. Their shells, shed in layers, become the building blocks for the larger creatures.

The larger Shell Lords, some the size of small cars, are mobile ecosystems in themselves. Entire colonies of smaller filter-feeders latch onto their shells, creating living reefs that move through the cathedral. These behemoths display a surprising range of behaviors. Some are solitary, carving out territories amongst the bone shards and defending them with ponderous, clicking mandibles. Others gather in migratory herds, following the currents of nutrient-rich silt across the cavern floor. They communicate through subtle shifts in shell bioluminescence, creating mesmerizing patterns that ripple across the abyssal plain.

But the true intelligence of the Ribcage resides not in the individual Shell Lords, but in the collective. They are a distributed consciousness, a network of interconnected organisms that share information through the subtle exchange of chemicals and vibrations. They sense changes in the Ribcage's internal pressure, predict shifts in the nutrient flow, and coordinate their activities with an instinct honed over millennia. When an anomaly occurs – a rupture in a bone-pipe, the intrusion of a surface-dweller – the information spreads like a ripple through the network, triggering coordinated responses. Barriers of shells are erected. Diversionary bioluminescent displays are initiated. An intruder becomes a puzzle piece in the grand equation of the Ribcage's survival.

Then, there are the *Bio-Engineers*, the descendants (or perhaps they are the evolved forms) of the beings who built the artificial structures affixed to the bone itself. They are far less numerous now, and even more deeply integrated into the living architecture of the Ribcage. They are less creature than slow-moving, deliberate force. Their bodies are amalgams of flesh, bone, shell, and the same strange metals found in the artificial structures. Imagine a hermit crab that has fused with both its shell and the machinery it has chosen to inhabit. Their movements are agonizingly slow, measured in decades rather than seconds.

The Bio-Engineers maintain the integrity of the artificial arteries and veins threaded through the bone. They are not repairing in any conscious way, more like enacting a series of inherited compulsions passed down through generations, through the bone dust infused into their very beings. They redirect errant flows, clear blockages, and stabilize weakened structures, driven by instinct and the deep thrum of the Ribcage's bio-mechanical heart. They do not comprehend the original purpose of the machinery. They only know that it must function. Their existence is a living prayer to the god of the machine-organism, an endless cycle of repair and preservation.

Finally, there is the Sleeper – the immense, unknowable being whose skeleton forms the Ribcage. It is not merely the bones that are alive; the entire structure is animated by a form of consciousness so alien that it dwarfs any possible human understanding. It does not think in words or images, but in tides of bio-chemical energy, in tectonic shifts of cellular architecture, in the slow, agonizing rhythms of geological time.

The Sleeper senses everything that happens within the Ribcage. It knows the movements of every Shell Lord, the compulsions of every Bio-Engineer. But it does not interfere. It operates on a scale that transcends individual events, orchestrating the grand symphony of life and death within its own body. It is the conductor of an orchestra that plays on a planetary scale, and we, with human ears, can only hear a few disjointed notes.

The Sleeper dreams. Its dreams are not flights of fancy or narratives built of memory. They are the blueprinting of future organisms, the testing of new bio-mechanical pathways, the slow, evolutionary experiments that sculpt the Ribcage and its inhabitants. To live within the Ribcage is to be a part of the Sleeper's dream. To die within it is to become the raw material for its next creation.

They are all pieces of the same vast, unknowable puzzle, each playing their part in the endless, grinding, and beautiful cycle of existence within the Ribcage. They are the inheritors of an ancient legacy, the guardians of a bio-mechanical wonder, the children of a dreaming god. And they are bound together by the slow, inexorable pulse of a creature whose existence humbles everything.
