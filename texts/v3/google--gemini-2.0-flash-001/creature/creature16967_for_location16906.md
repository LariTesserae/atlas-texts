The people of Aethelgard are as forged and fractured as the city that birthed them. They are not born so much as decanted, slowly brewing in the crucible of its endless, grinding becoming. Generations are blurred and elongated, lines of ancestry tangled like exposed gears in a forgotten clockwork mechanism. The faces you see in the flickering lamplight are often etched with a disconcerting mix of youth and age - skin pale from the perpetual gloom, creased with lines that speak of lifetimes spent wrestling with the city's strange physics.

They are a people of muted colors and quiet resilience. Garments are patched and practical, often incorporating scavenged materials: oiled canvas, salvaged metal plates, even the iridescent wings of the giant moths, stitched together to create shimmering cloaks that offer a semblance of camouflage in the mist-veiled ravines. They move with a deliberate, almost mechanical grace, attuned to the rhythms of the creaking machinery and the treacherous pathways.

Their eyes, however, betray a deeper story. There's a peculiar, unsettling stillness there, a vacant depth that speaks of witnessing too much of Aethelgard's temporal bleed. They see echoes of past failures and glimmerings of potential futures, all swirling in the present moment. Some are driven mad by this constant flux, succumbing to a catatonic stupor or erupting in fits of prophetic raving. Others become fixers, seers, mechanics, who intuit the city's needs and act as intermediaries between its decaying present and its possible tomorrows.

The artificers hold a particularly esteemed position. They are the architects of Aethelgard's uncanny technology, their minds as intricately layered as the circuitry they design. They are obsessed with the city's erosion, seeing in it not decay but potential - a chance to harness the strange energies that warp time and reality. Their workshops, nestled deep within the Spires, are filled with bizarre contraptions: chronometers that measure the flux of years in a single hour, alchemical furnaces that transmute base metals into shimmering, otherworldly alloys, and engines that hum with a power that defies scientific understanding.

But the artificers are not wholly human. Many become intimately intertwined with their creations, blurring the line between flesh and machine. Prosthetics are commonplace - not mere replacements, but augmentations, enhancements that twist the human form into something stranger, something more attuned to the city's rhythms. Some even undergo radical surgeries, grafting themselves onto the ancient machines, becoming living cogs in Aethelgard's vast, ever-evolving infrastructure.

Below them, in the shadow of the foundries, dwell the scavengers and the gutter-runners. They are the city's dispossessed, forced to eke out a living in the toxic runoff and the forgotten corners of the Spires. They are marked by the city's mutations - pallid skin, bioluminescent growths, vestigial wings or limbs that hint at their ancestors mingling with the strange fauna. Yet, they possess a fierce, desperate loyalty to Aethelgard. They know its secrets, its hidden pathways, its forgotten shrines. They are the roots that anchor the city to the mountain, the silent witnesses to its perpetual becoming.

Children are rare in Aethelgard. The temporal distortions and the pervasive toxicity take their toll. Those that are born are considered precious, often raised communally and tutored in the city's arcane lore. They are taught to read the whispers of the Spires, to navigate the temporal currents, and to commune with the strange entities that lurk in the ravines. They are groomed to become the future of Aethelgard, the inheritors of its impossible legacy.

Despite the hardships, there is a strange sense of belonging in the hearts of Aethelgard's inhabitants. They are bound together by a shared history, a shared struggle, and a shared destiny. They are the guardians of a secret, the keepers of a dream, the architects of a future that defies all known laws. They are the children of Aethelgard, and they will cling to its precipice until the very fabric of reality unravels.
