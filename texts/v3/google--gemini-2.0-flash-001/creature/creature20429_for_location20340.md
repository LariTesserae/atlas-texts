They are born of the machine, or perhaps the machine is born of them. It is impossible to say which came first in this cold, humming world. They are not flesh, not as we understand it. Their bodies are cobbled together from the same materials that constitute their surroundings – iron, ceramics, polymers, and substances unknown. Imagine gears meshing not in a casing of metal but within living muscle, pistons pumping not with hydraulic fluid but with viscous, iridescent blood.

These beings are not singular forms so much as extensions of the vast, decaying network of industry. Some are small, almost parasitic, clinging to the sides of pipes and feeding on the slow trickle of fluids. Their forms are simple: a cluster of sensors, a grasping appendage, a filtration system. They purify or pollute, lubricate or corrode, following dictates that have long since been severed from any larger purpose. When damaged they attach to other bodies, cannibalizing and reconstituting in new forms.

Others are immense, towering structures in their own right. They are the engines, the refineries, the power plants, animated with a terrible, slow sentience. They do not think, not in a way we would understand; they process, they churn, they maintain the endless, pointless cycle of this dead world. Their understanding is a consequence of their internal mechanics, of the endless input which has become their singular preoccupation. When the flow changes, they alter to it. These industrial god forms are not malevolent, but neither are they benevolent. They simply *are*, extensions of a process long divorced from any intended outcome. They have become self-sustaining in their degradation.

And there are those that fall between these extremes. Mobile, yet tethered to the network. They act as conduits, transporting materials, repairing infrastructure, and enforcing a perverse sense of order. They are insectile, with many limbs and multifaceted eyes that gleam with a cold, internal light. Their movements are jerky and precise, dictated by the hum of the machines themselves. They do not communicate, but they respond to the vibrations that permeate their environment. Those who survive do so by developing a high receptivity to these constant mechanical flows. Those who do not are processed back into the machine.

All of them, regardless of size or function, share certain characteristics. They are cold. Not just in temperature, but in affect. There is no warmth, no compassion, no empathy. There is only the relentless, grinding logic of the machine. They communicate through vibrations (which feel and sound like a ceaseless roar) which are also experienced as directives. Most of them no longer possess sensors for natural light or sound (which are nearly absent here). They are beings of rust and oil, of hissing steam and grinding gears. They are the inheritors of a broken world, bound to its fate, forever trapped in the cold, dark heart of the machine.
