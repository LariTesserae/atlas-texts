They are the children of the roar. Twisted by the wind, shaped by the spray, birthed from the ceaseless, screaming exchange between water, stone, and sky. Imagine a crab, if a crab had dreamt itself to life within the nightmare of a storm. Limbs too long, yes, as if stretched by invisible forces, each tipped with claws that find purchase on the crumbling cliffs with unsettling ease. Joints that bend the wrong way, a mockery of proper anatomy, designed for clinging, for ascending, for enduring the relentless assault of the elemental fury.

The masks. Always the masks. Woven from the slick, black ribbons of seaweed, stiffened with the salty air until they become more bone than plant. The masks are not protection, not exactly. They are a merging, a ritualistic binding of the self to this place. Glimpses beneath reveal skin the color of wet slate, drawn tight across sharp, avian bones. No visible eyes. Only the faintest hint of sunken sockets, suggesting a reliance on senses beyond sight. A feeling, perhaps, a resonance with the churning world around them.

They are not human. Not anymore, if they ever were. Humanity is an equation that does not solve under constant dissolution. Something else has emerged. Something… geological. They move with the hesitant certainty of a newly formed stone, settling into its most stable configuration. Their language, if it can be called that, is a guttural clicking, a chittering that mimics the susurrus of the chitinous grasses. It is a language of immediate need: "Grip." "Ascend." "Bone." "Wind." "Listen."

The nests are extensions of themselves. Not homes, but exoskeletons cast off and then re-inhabited. The driftwood is a reminder of a world swallowed, of trees torn from their roots and flung into the churning chaos. The metal scraps, gleaming dully in the diffused light, hint at some forgotten catastrophe, some vessel destroyed beyond repair in the embrace of the storm. The bones… the bones are the future. Scraped clean, hollowed out, used for resonating chambers and primitive wind-instruments, they are a map of the soundscape, a desperate attempt to understand the nature of the roar.

They are driven by instinct, by survival, but also by a strange, nascent curiosity. The oily plumage of the flightless birds, carefully collected and woven into damp, glistening cloaks, is more than mere protection. It is an attempt at mimicry, an aspiration toward flight. Their tools are crude, born of necessity, yet bear the mark of hesitant experimentation. The sharpest shells are not just weapons, but also saws, chisels, tools for shaping the unyielding stone.

They are not worshippers, though they are in awe. The Sea Organ is not a deity, but a key. They listen to its howling for patterns, for rhythms, for clues to the structure of the world. They understand, in some primal way, that they are part of the music, a distorted counterpoint to the symphony of destruction. They are the discord that allows the song to continue.

They are not building a civilization. They are weaving themselves into the fabric of this fractured reality. They are anchoring themselves to the edge of existence. They are becoming the scream.
