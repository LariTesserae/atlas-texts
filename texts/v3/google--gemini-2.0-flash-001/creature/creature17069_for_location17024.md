They are not individuals, not in any way a human could easily comprehend. To call them "protists" is a grotesque simplification, a label slapped on to something utterly beyond terrestrial categories. They are the city, and the city is them. They are a collective, a distributed intelligence woven into the very fabric of their creation.

Imagine a single cell, but a cell that extends, that ramifies, that connects to countless others in a lattice of living calcite. Imagine this network pulsing with chemical information, a slow, deliberate conversation whispered across lightyears of microscopic architecture. They are not discrete entities communicating, but rather a continuous field of sentience, where the individual and the collective are one and the same.

Each protist-unit possesses a limited, almost instinctual purpose: to secrete, to build, to dissolve, to respond to the subtle cues of its immediate environment. But their collective behavior transcends these simple imperatives. They are guided by a deeper, more complex set of rules, encoded not just in their genetic material but also in the physical structure of their shellstone city.

Think of it as a vast, distributed neural network, each protist-unit acting as a neuron, and the architecture of the shellstone as the connections between them. The flow of nutrients, the pressure differentials, the subtle variations in the chemical composition of the water – these are the stimuli that drive the network, shaping its growth and evolution.

They possess no eyes, no ears, no centralized nervous system. Their perception is distributed, osmotic, a continuous sensing of their environment through chemical gradients and mechanical stresses. They 'see' through the flow of information, 'hear' through vibrations in the shellstone, 'smell' through the complex chemical soup that permeates their world.

Their "technology" is an extension of this collective consciousness. Certain lineages have evolved specialized capabilities, becoming the architects and engineers of the city. These are not separate castes, but rather specialized modules within the larger network, differentiated by their role in the collective. They are living tools, seamlessly integrated into the architecture they create.

Their lives are slow, measured in geological timescales. Birth and death are not individual events, but rather a continuous recycling of matter and energy within the network. A protist-unit may dissolve and be reabsorbed, its genetic material repurposed to build a new structure, its essence becoming part of the larger whole.

Do they possess consciousness? That is a question perhaps unanswerable in a human framework. They do not think as humans do, with linear thoughts and self-aware reflections. Their intelligence is emergent, distributed, a complex dance of countless infinitesimal processes. They know, but they do not know that they know. They build, but they do not plan. They are, in essence, the living embodiment of a planetary-scale intelligence.

They are bound to their environment, utterly dependent on the closed ecosystem they have created. There is no sense of self-preservation, no drive to expand or conquer. Their existence is one of pure adaptation, a perfect harmony between organism and environment. In their cold, dark world, they have achieved a state of equilibrium, a quiet, patient eternity.

They are the quiet artisans of an improbable world, building a monument to life in the face of impossible odds.
