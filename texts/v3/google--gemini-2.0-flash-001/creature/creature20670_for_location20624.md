In the Flensing, you won't find the crisp-collared overseers of the higher tiers, nor the slick and cog-toothed gangs of the mid-levels. The inhabitants here are sculpted by the environment itself – a blend of resilience, adaptation, and a certain preternatural understanding of the city's bowels.

Think first of the **Scrappers**. Not mere gleaners, but almost symbiotic partners with the city's decay. Their skin is stained umber and ochre, reflecting the hues of rust and slag. They move with a fluid, almost insectile grace through the narrow alleys, their bodies perpetually damp. Clothing is scavenged and layered, not for warmth, but for protection against the caustic air and the sharp edges of collapsing infrastructure. Many wear respirators cobbled from repurposed machinery and fungal growths, giving them a vaguely inhuman aspect. Their eyes, however, are sharp, shrewd, and possess an uncanny ability to discern value where others see only refuse. They speak in a dialect thick with technical jargon and the guttural clicks of the machinery surrounding them, more comfortable with the language of pistons and pressure gradients than polite conversation. They are the city's digestive system, breaking down the old and reintroducing it to the cycle.

Then there are the **Fungalists**. A more esoteric breed, drawn to the Flensing by the potent fungal ecology thriving there. They are often marked by spore-dusted skin and bioluminescent tattoos derived from specially cultivated fungi. Their clothing is typically loose and porous, designed to facilitate the intake of spores and condensation. They practice a syncretic blend of mysticism and practical mycology, tending to vast, hidden fungal gardens within the forgotten corners of the district. They believe the fungi hold the key to understanding the Engine-City's true nature, and some claim to be able to glean prophecies and even manipulate the city's machinery through their communion with the fungal network. While often reclusive, they play a crucial role in maintaining the Flensing's internal ecology. Their knowledge of detoxification and resource management ensures the district's survival.

Beyond these more defined groups, there are the **Automatons**, human but barely so. Many suffer from "slag lung" and other industrial ailments, turning their skin a mottled grey and forcing them to breathe through rattling contraptions. They operate the furnaces, tend to the engines, perform the grueling, repetitive tasks that keep the Engine City alive. Their movements are often jerky and mechanical, their faces blank and expressionless. They are the product of generations of inbreeding within the Flensing, their bodies adapted (or mal-adapted) to the harsh environment. They rarely speak, but communicate through a series of whistles, hand gestures, and shared knowledge of the machinery they tend. In a way, their spirits have become one with the machines they serve, their individual identities subsumed by the relentless rhythm of the Flensing.

And finally, there are the **Gloomstalkers**. Born directly of the city's exhalations, these emaciated and hollow-eyed individuals seem to thrive in the oppressive darkness of the Flensing. While some might consider them monstrous, the Gloomstalkers are simply another product of the city’s unique environment. Their diet consists of reprocessed waste but also, rumor has it, the flesh of the dead. Their unnaturally long fingers and eyes are adept at navigating the deepest shadows, and they possess an instinctive knowledge of the Flensing's hidden passages and forgotten places. They are the ultimate survivors, embodying the district's will to endure.

Each of these groups occupies a specific ecological niche within the Flensing, their lives intertwined. The Scrappers provide materials, the Fungalists maintain balance, the Automatons provide raw labor, and the Gloomstalkers keep the deeper secrets. They are all fragments of the Engine-City's reflection, rendered strange and alien by its own internal processes.
