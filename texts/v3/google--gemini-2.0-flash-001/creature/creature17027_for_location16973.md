The inhabitants are not immediately obvious. There are no buzzing insects, no scurrying rodents, no birdsong to break the droning silence. Then, you realize the lavender stains are not stains at all, but deposits, colonies of something unbelievably small, so small that they appear as a haze of color on the bone-white bark. They pulse faintly, the source perhaps of the ozone tang in the air. Perhaps they excrete it as waste; perhaps it is a byproduct of their existence.

These are the first inhabitants, a microbial carpet that tints the landscape with its hidden vitality. They are the recyclers, the alchemists of this bizarre world, slowly breaking down the petrified bone, perhaps extracting some unknown nutrient, and leaving behind only the perfect, spherical boreholes as evidence. They propagate, surely, but how? Do they bud and drift on nonexistent currents of air? Do they tunnel through the petrified wood to colonize new territories? They are silent, patient, and their slow work shapes the very texture of this contained reality.

And there are the Makers of the holes. Not insects; nothing so crude. They are more akin to…living jewels. Imagine the smallest possible shard of obsidian, shaped into a perfect multifaceted teardrop, humming with internal energy. These jewels flow, they move with a fluid grace that is almost imperceptible to the naked eye. They are drawn to the trees by the silent hum, the vibration that pulses from each calcified trunk. They are the resonators, drawn to the harmonic frequency by the calcified trees, like moths to a pale-white flame.

These jeweled entities possess a singular purpose: to amplify the hum. They bore their perfect holes not to feed; they bore them to resonate. Each hole, when completed, becomes a tiny amplifier, subtly altering the frequency of the tree's hum, adding its own crystalline tone to the chorus. The purple colonies might feed on the byproducts of this amplification, a symbiotic loop sustaining the whole.

But watching more closely, more intensely, some other quality of the Maker becomes visible. There are gradations of luminosity, brighter at the head and tail ends of their shapes, and they seem to pulse in waves, light rippling from them into the trees they visit.

And one of them breaks free, it’s body dissolving, falling away, while the energy core bursts free, shedding its material form to become pure radiance. The radiance lingers for a moment, and then rushes back into the tree from which it came.

So, the Makers aren’t creatures at all. They are the *cells* of the calcified trees. When they come to what appears to be the end of their lives, instead of dying, the Makers give themselves back to the pulsing heart of the calcified trees. They don't live *in* this place; they *are* this place, a single, massive organism expressed in countless bone-white branches and resonating chambers. It is both a forest and a single, vast being, dreaming in light and sound.
