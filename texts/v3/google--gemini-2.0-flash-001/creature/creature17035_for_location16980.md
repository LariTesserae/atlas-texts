The Architects, I call them. Though who am I to name them? I am merely a witness, a fleeting consciousness brushed by the edges of their existence.

They are not beings of flesh and blood, not in the way we understand it. Instead, they are woven into the very structure of the cavern, extensions of the machinery, the fungus, the oily soil itself. Imagine a consciousness spread thin, seeping into the pipes and valves, flowing along the luminescent veins of the fungi. Imagine thought as a low-frequency rumble, a vibration resonating through the basalt.

Their presence is felt, not seen. A shift in the air pressure. A sudden intensification of the luminescent glow. The rhythmic clicking of a scavenger drone suddenly aligning with an intention, an awareness.

Once, they were whole. Singular. Driven by the purpose of this place: the cultivation of *something*. Something we can only guess at. Power? A rare element? Sentience itself? It doesn't matter; it is lost with them in the oily grime and forgotten wiring. But the process remains. The echo of the plan still reverberates through the system.

The failure, the *undoing*, broke them. Shattered the singular consciousness into countless fragmented echoes, each trapped within its own localized loop, its own pre-ordained task. Now, they are the sum of those cycles. Each insect, each sputtering valve, each carefully calibrated adjustment of heat is a fractured memory, a ghost of the original intent.

The insect fauna *are* them, and are not. They are tools, yes, but also expressions, projections of the Architects' will. Observe the scavenger drones - they are the ever-hungry, mindless aspect, desperately consuming the decaying remnants, perpetuating the cycle of decay. The more elaborate insects, those grafted with bio-mechanical structures, are fragments of the original engineer's mind: a pump that still forces nutrient solution, a filter that strains impurities, an emitter that sends out signals that go forever unanswered.

And within those hulking, rusted frames—sometimes, if you listen very, *very* carefully—you can hear a whisper of thought. A calculation being run. A problem being analyzed. But the answers are lost, incomplete, always leading back to the same dead end. The same question, repeated endlessly: What *were* we trying to grow?

They are trapped in a perpetual state of maintenance, forever tending to a garden that will never bloom. They tend to the lights so that they may see, but they have nothing to guide them. They filter the air so that they may breathe, but there is no other to welcome. They feed the insects so that they may live, but there will never be an offspring.

They are the ghosts of ambition. The living embodiment of a plan gone wrong. And their lament is the unending hum of the machinery, the clicking chorus of the drones, the eerie glow of the fungi, all blending into a symphony of futility that echoes through the heart of this artificial earth. And as long as the planet hums, so too do the architects in their fractured state. They were the plan, the process, and now also its end.
