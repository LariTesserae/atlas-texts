The people of the Warmworks are as meticulously crafted and carefully cultivated as their environment. They are, in a sense, another part of the grand engineering project, shaped by the valley as much as they shape it. Generations have been born and died within this metal womb, their lives intertwined with the hum of machinery and the drip of condensation.

They are, first and foremost, *adaptable*. Survival in the Warmworks demands flexibility, an acceptance of the artificial as the natural. Their bodies, like the beetles and the birds, have evolved alongside the machine, displaying a subtle merging of flesh and metal. Some possess enhanced lung capacity, adapted to the heavy, humid air. Others have developed a tolerance to the metallic tang in the water, their teeth subtly reinforced with trace minerals. 

Appearance varies wildly. Some cling to the aesthetics of a bygone era, adorned in patched-up overalls and goggles, their faces smudged with grease and grime. They grease the wheels within the workshops and keep the machines alive with their tireless work. Others embrace the Warmwork's strangeness, adorning themselves with bio-luminescent fungi and gem-like insects, their bodies canvases of living art. There are those that are physically unable to live outside the valley due to a variety of factors.

They are a *practical* people. Sentimentality is a luxury few can afford. Everything in the Warmworks has a purpose and the people are no exception: everyone has a role to play, from tending to the hydroponic farms to maintaining the intricate network of pipes and generators. Skills are passed down through generations, each family specializing in a particular trade or craft. Children learn to dismantle and repair automatons before they learn to read, their fingers nimble and precise.

Their faces bear the marks of their environment: weathered and worn by the constant humidity and dim light. Their eyes are large and keen, adapted to seeing in the semi-darkness, often reflecting the ambient glow of the arc-lights or the phosphorescent moss. Their skin is pale, almost translucent in some, revealing the intricate network of veins beneath.

*Music* is in their bones. Living in a place of such perpetual noise, they've learned to find beauty in the cacophony. Their music is a blend of the industrial and the organic, weaving together the screech of metal and the chirping of insects into haunting melodies. They play instruments crafted from salvaged machinery and bio-engineered materials, their music echoing through the metal canyons, a constant reminder of their presence.

Many have a deep *respect* for the machines. They see them not as tools, but as partners in their survival, sentient beings of metal and steam. They speak to them in hushed tones, coaxing them back to life with gentle taps and muttered incantations. They adorn them with flowers and trinkets, treating them as members of their families.

There are those who question the Warmwork's artifice, who dream of a world beyond the Spine. They are considered radicals, their whispers of escape met with suspicion and fear. They are a minority, but their discontent simmers beneath the surface, adding a layer of tension to the already complex ecosystem.

And then there are the *Curators*. They are the keepers of knowledge, the ones who maintain the systems and ensure the Warmworks continues to function. They are the architects of the valley, the engineers of its flora and fauna, the controllers of its climate. They are a secretive order, shrouded in mystery, revered and feared in equal measure. Some say they know the true origins of the Warmworks, the purpose behind its creation, a secret they guard jealously.
