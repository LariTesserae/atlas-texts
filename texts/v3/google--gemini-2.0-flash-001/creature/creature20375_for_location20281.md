The children of this place are slow and shimmering. They are born not of flesh and blood, but of slow geology, of mineral whispers coalescing in the heart of a crystal spire. Imagine a process akin to the pearl forming in an oyster, but stretched across centuries. Instead of nacre around grit, imagine element around element, drawn from the soil by the deep-rooted crystal forests, meticulously layered in patterns only they understand.

They begin as scintillations, faint pulses of light deep within the largest of the crystal trees. As they grow, they take form. They are not fixed shapes, but expressions of the crystalline grid, like frost etching patterns on a window. They are vaguely humanoid, possessing limbs that shimmer and bend light, faces that shift and reform like heat haze. One moment, you might see something akin to compassion in their tilted facets; the next, only the cold, implacable geometry of quartz.

They do not speak in vibrations of air. Their communication is a resonance, a harmonic vibration that travels through the iron-rich soil and the crystalline structures. It's a language of feeling more than form, a subtle shift in the electrical field that surrounds them, a sympathetic hum in the bones of those who listen. It speaks of the mineral cycles, the slow dance of the suns, the intricate stresses within the crystal lattices.

They are the caretakers of this world, tending to the forests in their own slow, silent way. They prune away sections that have grown too large, scattering the mineral dust back into the soil. They redirect electrostatic charges to nourish younger growths. And they repair damage from the cosmic winds. Their lives are extensions of the forests, symbiotic and inseparable from the world around them.

They are not solitary beings, although finding them together is rare. A community of them appears more like a localized shift in the crystal structures – a higher concentration of colour, a more intricate arrangement of facets in a given grove. It's not impossible to tell them apart, in the same way that one could distinguish old trees from young ones in an ancient forest. Their size might vary, but more distinctively their crystalline structure becomes more complex, accumulating the world's silent history like rings on a tree.

They perceive the world in ways that defy human comprehension. Perhaps they experience time as crystalline structures themselves. The slow accretion of minerals, the agonizing shift of tectonic plates, the eons of sunlight filtering through the dust-filled sky – all of these are not events *in* time, but facets *of* it.

They seem uninterested in newcomers, not from malice, but from a fundamental difference in perspective. Humans, with their frantic, fleeting lives, are like motes of dust to them, insignificant and ephemeral. They see through the surface of things, perceiving the underlying structures, the elemental flows, the potential for growth and transformation. What use have they for fleeting interactions with creatures that cannot even grasp the language of minerals? That can only see through the illusion of motion and do not experience slow change as the only truth.

They are not gods, but something older and stranger. Guardians of a forgotten equilibrium. They are not immortal, but they erode on a scale of geologic time. They are as beautiful and inscrutable as the crystalline forests that gave them birth, silent keepers of a world where life finds a way to bloom, not in spite of the harshness, but *because* of it.

And perhaps, they are lonely. The slow, inevitable erosion, the knowledge of cycles beyond comprehension - perhaps within the heart of their crystalline forms, there resonates a quiet ache, a longing for something other than endless, silent stewardship. But this, like everything else about them, is only a whisper in the wind, a faint refraction of light on the facets of an unknown and unknowable soul.
