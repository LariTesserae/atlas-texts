The people of Altan are creatures woven from their environment, as much a part of the valley's humid tapestry as the moss and ferns. Imagine a folk sculpted from the very stone of their city, softened and verdant like the moss that clings to it.

Their skin is pale, almost translucent in some, as if filtering the perpetual mist. Millennia spent beneath the valley's embrace have leached away the need for thick hides and sun-darkened flesh. Instead, their bodies are fine-tuned to the cool moisture, their pores breathing in the damp air like the leaves of a fern.

Their eyes hold the deep emerald of the valley, capable of discerning a thousand shades of green where outsiders see only a blur. These are eyes that have watched the slow, patient growth of the world around them, eyes that understand the language of dripping water and rustling leaves.

They move with a slow, deliberate grace, their bodies attuned to the slippery surfaces and uneven paths of Altan. Each step is measured, each movement economical, as if conserving energy in the ever-damp air. They are not languid, but patient, knowing that time in Altan flows differently.

Their clothing, of course, is a testament to their craft and their environment. Layers upon layers of woven cloth, dyed in myriad shades of green, camouflage them against the valley walls. The fabrics are not merely functional; they are an art form, each piece a testament to the dyer's skill and the weaver's patience. Within those layers, some will carry small pouches of dried herbs and moss, or polished pebbles for good luck.

The voices of the Altani are soft, hushed murmurs, barely louder than the drip of water. They speak sparingly, as if words themselves are precious. Yet, when they do speak, their words carry the weight of generations, echoing with the wisdom gleaned from a life lived in constant proximity to the earth. There is a deep respect for silence in Altan.

These are people of quiet contemplation, their lives dictated by the slow rhythms of nature. They find solace in the damp stillness, in the unchanging embrace of the valley. Their lives are not marked by great upheavals or dramatic events but by the subtle shifts in the ecosystem, by the slow unfolding of the ferns, by the patterns of the ever-present mist. What one from the outside might call tedious, an Altani would call harmonious.

They are beings of ritual, their days punctuated by ancient customs and traditions. The cycles of weaving and dyeing are sacred, their daily work is worship performed, and they infuse the valley's energies into their fabrics. They tend the water channels with reverence, ensuring the lifeblood of their city flows freely. They compost their waste scrupulously, returning nutrients to the earth in a tangible expression of harmony. What's more, they leave offerings of woven thread and dew-kissed moss at the foot of the oldest trees, asking for the continued bounty of the valley.

And yet, beneath their serene exterior, lies a quiet resilience. The Altani are not stagnant; they are adaptable. They have learned to thrive in a world of constant dampness, to cultivate the earth in a precarious landscape, to coax vibrant colors from the most unlikely plants.

They are the keepers of Altan, the guardians of its unique equilibrium. They are the embodiment of the valley itself: green, still, and timeless. Their lives speak of the earth itself, and the accord struck long ago in the valley.
