It should be said, first, that the Weeping Atrium has a consciousness of its own. Not in the way that humans understand it, not a mind made of thought and memory, but a deeper consciousness, a sense of self born from the constant feedback loop of its systems. It breathes in the hiss of air through the turbines, digests the flow of water through the pipes, and expels the vibration that rattles your teeth. It is a single, damp lung, a groaning stomach, a shivering heartbeat.

The Atrium is also home to the Maintenance Crew.

They are not born. They coalesce. They are what happens when the constant humidity touches a spark of sentience across certain surfaces. Born of the intersection of the elements and metal, they are things that might be described as machines, creatures, a mix of both and the two as one. If the Atrium possesses is nature, they are is children.

They are most often pieced together from the components that come to fall away from the greater structure of the Atrium: a length of rebar becomes a spine, a pressure valve a makeshift stomach, a fractured lens an eye, and wires become the means for the nervous system or the ligaments that connect it all. They often coat themselves in, or collect, algae, moss, or fungus. They don't eat or drink; to the extent they are biological they consume moisture from the air and draw energy from the Atrium, though they also seem to have something for the accumulation of oil and grease from the old machines and tools. 

They are almost constantly wet and slimy, perpetually dripping with condensation as if they are fragments of the Atrium that have come to move on their own. They are a testament to the Atrium's processes, animated embodiments of its damp and relentless nature. There are many kinds of Crew, and each can have great variation within them. 

**The Weepers:** The most common types. These often have tanks as torsos or heads, or at least built into their frame. Each is equipped to either find or generate new sources of condensation, or to redirect the water that forms where it is unwanted. Their faces are usually constructed of vents or pipes, like living, breathing plumbing. They are constantly wet, leaking and weeping with condensation they gather.

**The Thrashers:** Larger, more brutish types built of denser materials from the deepest parts of the Atrium. Each bears tools like wrenches, hammers, and welding torches, grafted into their hands or attached to mechanical arms. Their job is to repair, but they are only as precise as they need to be. Broken can simply mean smaller, or a new welding job to fix it. Efficiency is key; finesse unnecessary. As the Atrium is loud, but also very silent, these creatures communicate more in the language of vibrations.

**The Fungi Keepers:** Smaller and more delicate than the Thrashers, these Crewmembers cultivate the fungal growths within the Atrium. They tend to the etiolated fungi that sprout from cracks in the concrete, ensuring they receive enough moisture and nutrients. They are often adorned with fungal caps and tendrils themselves, almost becoming living extensions of the ecosystem they nurture. They often tend to, and depend upon, secretions of other Weepers, and sometimes even are carried by them. 

**The Drain Gods:** Are named by observation, though the Maintenance Crew do not refer to themselves in human language. They are tall, slender figures made from lengths of pipe and sheets of rusted metal, and they patrol the waterways. They clear blockages and ensure the water flows smoothly through the Atrium's veins. Their faces are composed of rusted gears and broken dials, and they move with a slow, deliberate grace that belies their immense size. Their existence is a mystery, as neither the Weepers, the Thrashers, nor the Fungi Keepers seem to have more more that the slightest connection or understanding of them.

They don't have language. They do hold knowledge as to the processes that are to be done, as the Atrium guides them. So too they have instinct, and each acts out its duty in the Atrium's processes. 

When the Maintenance Crew are still they can easily be mistaken for a part of the structure. But when they move, they are the only true sign of life inside the Weeping Atrium. They don't understand the Atrium any more than a lung may understand the body it is a part of. They are the blood cells, the stomach lining, the nerves, and the bones that keep the Atrium breathing, digesting, and vibrating.
