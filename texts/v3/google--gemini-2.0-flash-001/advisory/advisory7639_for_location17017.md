*   **Prepare for Isolation:** The lack of fauna and the pervasive silence will amplify any feelings of loneliness. Bring something that reminds you of home, but resist the urge to shut yourself off from the environment. Consider what you are going to do in case of panic or distress.

*   **Waterproof EVERYTHING:** Assume constant, heavy moisture. Multiple layers of waterproof clothing are essential:
    *   A high-quality, breathable waterproof jacket and pants.
    *   Waterproof hiking boots with excellent ankle support.
    *   Waterproof gloves.
    *   Waterproof backpack with dry bags for sensitive equipment.
*   **Moisture-Wicking Base Layers:** Avoid cotton. Opt for synthetics or merino wool to keep moisture away from your skin.
*   **Navigation Tools are CRITICAL:** Normal GPS might be unreliable due to the strange magnetic fields.
    *   Bring a traditional compass and learn how to use it.
    *   Download offline maps of the area, but be aware that the landscape might change rapidly.
    *   Consider bringing a satellite phone or emergency beacon in case you get lost or need help. Try to know how these work before you enter the valley.
*   **Lighting:** The twilight environment and the deceptive distances make good lighting essential.
    *   A powerful headlamp with adjustable brightness.
    *   A handheld flashlight as a backup.
    *   Red light filters for both to preserve night vision.
*   **Air Filtration:** The air is thick with biological matter and unknown particles.
    *   A high-quality respirator mask with replaceable filters. Consider taking more filter replacements, if you plan to be in the valley for long.
*   **Protective Clothing:** Thorns, biting or stinging insects, caustic plants, and other unknown hazards are possible.
    *   Long-sleeved shirt and pants made of durable, puncture-resistant material.
    *   Eye protection, such as goggles or safety glasses.
*   **Tools:**
    *   A multi-tool with a knife, pliers, screwdriver, and other useful implements.
    *   Strong cordage for shelter building or repairs.
    *   A first-aid kit with supplies for treating cuts, burns, bites, and allergic reactions.
*   **Food and Water:** Do not assume any local plants or water sources are safe to consume.
    *   Bring enough non-perishable food for the duration of your trip, plus extra for emergencies.
    *   A water filter or purification tablets to treat any water you find.
*   **Recording Equipment:** Capture your observations, but be aware that electronic devices might malfunction due to the unusual energies in the valley.
    *   A rugged camera with a waterproof case.
    *   A notebook and pen for recording observations in case your camera fails.
    *   Bring some scientific devices as well, to help you understand what is happening.
