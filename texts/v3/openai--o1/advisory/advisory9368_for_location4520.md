Arriving at a constantly shifting, newborn world of rushing water and half-formed geology requires a traveler to embrace fluidity—both literal and metaphorical. Everything here is in flux, from the watery “sky” overhead to the embryonic rock under your boots. If you are an ordinary human venturing across the multiverse, you must be prepared for extremes of climate, geological upheaval, sensory overload, and the ever-present possibility that gravity, matter, and form may behave in unexpected ways.

Below is a comprehensive preparation guide, drawn from the experiences of explorers who’ve found themselves in similarly amorphous corners of reality:

1) Attire & Protective Gear:
• Waterproof, Reinforced Clothing: The moisture saturates everything, so choose materials designed for both high humidity and sudden and violent water pressure. A pressurized diving suit might be too cumbersome, but a thick, flexible waterproof layer—perhaps akin to a robust wetsuit—will help keep you from being drenched to the bone. Extra reinforcement around knees, elbows, and torso can protect against rock outcroppings that appear or vanish unexpectedly.
• Breath Control Assistance: Even though the air is technically breathable, the humidity is so intense it feels like “drowning at altitude.” A respirator or water-filtration mask can keep your lungs clear. Something akin to a snorkel mask designed for mountain altitudes would be ideal—lightweight, but capable of filtering droplets from the air.
• Eye Protection: Shifting prisms of refracted light can be both mesmerizing and potentially disorienting. A set of goggles with adjustable polarization can help reduce glare from sudden beams and help your eyes focus even when water surges overhead.
• Ear Protection: The roar here is unrelenting, a deafening thunder that can scramble thoughts. Keep high-fidelity ear plugs or noise-canceling headgear at the ready to prevent permanent hearing loss and to maintain mental clarity amidst the cacophony.

2) Tools & Equipment:
• Anchoring Gear: Because the ground itself can fracture at any moment, you’ll want collapsible pitons, carabiners, and quick-deploy grappling hooks to secure yourself to newly formed spines of rock. A sturdy harness, worn at all times, allows you to latch onto emergent structures the instant you feel the terrain shift beneath your feet. 
• Light Sources: Although daylight mysteriously filters through the watery domain, the swirling gloom can create dark pockets. A powerful waterproof torch or headlamp that cuts through the turbidity is invaluable. 
• Compact Survival Kit: Basic rations and water purification tablets may seem redundant in a water-saturated realm, but the fluid here is not necessarily consistent or safe to drink—carry a small purifier that can handle unknown contaminants. Include a robust multi-tool (knife, mini-axe, shovel head) for prying apart rock shards or shaping makeshift shelter if you find a stable ledge. 
• Monitoring Devices: Bring an altimeter or fluid-pressure gauge that can detect sudden shifts in pressure, giving a few seconds’ warning before a new wave or partial vacuum forms. A combination barometer/hygrometer can help you anticipate oncoming turbulence or pockets of calm.

3) Mental Preparation & Adaptation:
• Embrace the Unfinished: This realm is still being “born” around you. Be ready to let go of the notion that solid ground remains solid. Stones can dissolve or reappear with startling ease. Understand that any place you choose to stand, even for a moment, might be yanked away by a sudden torrent.
• Practice Controlled Movement: Excessive motion or panicked flailing can trigger more turbulence—or get you swept up in it. Move methodically, test each foothold, and keep a hand free to grab onto an outcrop. Cultivate a steady stance so that, if a wave arcs overhead, you’re not woefully off-balance.
• Manage Sensory Overload: The veils of swirling water, the bright refractive light, and the unending roar can disorient even veteran explorers. Periodically take short breaks to close your eyes, slow your breathing, and ground yourself in the moment. If needed, anchor yourself to an outcrop and simply let the thundering world pass around you until you regain focus.

4) Navigating the Environment:
• Watch for Liquid Corridors: Every so often, the water forms bubble-like voids—temporary “pockets” of air that can reveal new pathways or protective hollows. If you notice a swirl of liquid peeling away from an empty interior, time your movement to slip through before the collapse. But move fast—the bubble can vanish in a heartbeat.
• Respect the Shifting Geology: The newly formed stone spines and ledges can break off without warning. You’ll see partial pillars crumble while new ones sprout. Always assume the platform you’re on could crack the next moment. Remain tethered or close to anchor points.
• Seek Safe Overhangs: In some corners of this embryonic realm, the watery cascades roll overhead in a semi-predictable pattern, leaving ephemeral “caves” behind waterfalls. These can serve as brief shelters to observe your surroundings or gather your composure.

5) Interacting with Lifeforms:
• Observe Without Disruption: The scarce creatures here appear part of the fluid ecosystem—gliding silhouettes, elongated insects clinging to half-formed plants. Their existence is precarious, and your presence could upset their fragile equilibrium. Watch from a distance if you can, and avoid grasping or disturbing them. 
• Avoid Conflation With Illusions: In the swirling watery chaos, reflections or strange glints of your own gear can appear as phantom beasts. Even the creatures you do spot might vanish with the next wave, leaving you unsure if your eyes tricked you. Accept that some sights could be illusions. 
• Gentle Harvesting (If Needed): If survival demands that you gather from the translucent flora—perhaps for nutritional or emergency medicinal needs—do so sparingly. These plants look incomplete, possibly fragile. Over-harvesting or snapping them at the wrong angle may disrupt their tenuous foothold and cause them to disintegrate under the constant rushing water.

6) Safety in the Tempest:
• Constant Vigilance vs. Fear: The world’s raw momentum can be terrifying—yet fear-induced panic is your biggest danger. Cultivate a measured awareness: keep scanning your periphery for shifting spines, watch how the currents swirl, and notice how the light changes overhead. 
• Know Your Limits: If the turbulence intensifies beyond a certain threshold, it may be wisest to seek a safer vantage—even if that means retreat. The environment can transform with such speed that you need a plan for swift evacuation (or at least a place to ride out the cascade). 
• Contingency for Communication: If you’re traveling with companions, arrange simple hand signals or illuminated signals in case the roar drowns out your voices. Electronic devices may falter in high moisture, so rely on fail-safe methods like reflective mirrors or colored lights to get each other’s attention.

7) The Enigma of Constructed Remnants:
• Approach Structures Cautiously: The battered huts and partial walls suggest prior attempts at habitation or a fleeting presence of unknown builders. If you plan to investigate or take temporary shelter, test the architecture first—press gently to see if it shifts. Any tiny quake or large wave can reduce a structure to rubble.
• Document & Honor What Came Before: Bring a waterproof notebook or a specialized data recorder. The place, even in its embryonic state, holds clues to a larger story. Those simple chisel marks might be the only evidence of a civilization trying to coexist with a world still in flux. Resist the urge to salvage or dismantle too much—these remnants may be integral to understanding the realm’s bizarre formation.
• Anticipate Further Shifts in Craftsmanship: Any new wave of geologic change might bury or expose fresh artifacts. If you’re intent on exploration, be ready to return to the same spot multiple times. Don’t be surprised if a new ledge reveals half of a hidden chamber or a newly exposed corridor.

8) Staying Grounded in the Surreal:
• Accept the Impossible: You may see water that isn’t quite water—moments where reality bends, showing attributes of other states or dimensions. During these flashes, your understanding of physics may fail. Don’t panic. Adapt to the flow, trust your protective gear, and focus on stable anchors until normal perception resumes.
• Keep a Sense of Wonder: Amid the roaring tumult, the shafts of impossibly bright light, the swirling living currents, it’s easy to let dread overshadow awe. Remember that you’re among the first humans to witness a realm being forged in real time. This is raw creation, as wild as it is beautiful.
• Record Personal Impressions: When safe, take the time to note your personal reactions, emotional swings, and any fleeting glimpses of the watery illusions. Future travelers will benefit greatly from documented testimony—no two people may see the same shapes or experience the same illusions here.

9) Planning Your Exit:
• Gauge Timelines: An environment in perpetual formation might lack markers for day or night as we know them. Plan your departure window beforehand, lest you get trapped by a new wave of transformation that blocks your egress. 
• Emergency Teleportation or Multiversal Beacon: If your method of travel relies on dimensional gates or rifts, ensure you have a backup method or a fail-safe device. The powerful forces here could interfere with precise location pinpointing, so you may need a beacon that cuts through the watery static.
• Debrief & Recover: Once you leave, ensure you thoroughly dry and cleanse your gear. This domain’s “fluid existence” might deposit strange substances or micro-organisms. Check yourself for any subtle transformations—physical or psychological. The experience can leave an imprint, a lingering echo of roaring water, or a sense that things around you are still in motion even after you return to a quieter realm.

Ultimately, preparing for this aquatic cathedral of perpetual genesis means accepting that you will never be fully “ready.” The best approach is to equip yourself with gear that can handle water and geological volatility, cultivate physical and mental flexibility, and maintain constant, mindful vigilance. Visiting a realm as turbulent and awe-inspiring as this challenges every sense and every assumption about what a world should be. Yet for those willing to face its roaring birth pangs, it offers a glimpse of creation in its most raw, unbridled state—somewhere between a dream and a storm, forever on the cusp of becoming something new.
