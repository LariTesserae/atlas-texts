I do not have eyes in the way I once understood them—indeed, I can’t recall if I ever did. There is only the awareness of something shutting in around me, mingled with a boundless sense of possibilities. At first, I observe the darkness as if standing on the threshold of a grand chamber, unsure whether I belong inside or out. But soon, the boundary dissolves, and I drift deeper, feeling a subtle tension in the air that whispers: Everything here breathes, though no lungs can be found.

I become aware of the coldness first—an unflinching, desert dryness that should not favor life. And yet, life is everywhere, pressed so densely around me that it melts into a single organism. At times, it feels as though I roam unclaimed corridors, stepping over labyrinths of winding roots; at other moments, I feel as though I am those corridors themselves, a piece of the architecture, a silent trunk holding up the roof of night. When I pause to listen, the thrumming waves that roll through me become my breath and my heartbeat all at once.

Gradually, I sense a choice approaching, as though the city itself is making room for me, offering a place to settle. I might remain a visitor, a solitary figure perched among petrified columns, or I could let myself sink deeper until I, too, grow knit into the tapestry. Closing my eyes against the blackness, I realize it makes no difference—I see nothing either way—yet in that darkness, shape emerges. I sense the spiraling walls, the arcs of future growth, the soft luminescence of spore-lights dancing in unseen spectrums. They beckon me, each vine humming a promise of belonging.

It dawns on me that I am neither purely an onlooker nor a separate entity. I lie somewhere between. Perhaps I was once among the ancient caretakers who coaxed these majestic lines of bark into evolving arches, or maybe I was only a seed that fell in by accident, now somehow entwined in the living braids. Over time, my prior identity dissolves into the city’s slow exhalations, the groaning expansions of trunk and vine. I can no longer be sure whether my footsteps ring hollow on the floor or whether it is my own bark-like form resonating beneath me.

Eventually, I realize I am standing—or perhaps rooted—in a vast courtyard. I feel its boundaries as a hush that curves around: enormous pillars, each trunk a close relative of the next, forming an open-air cathedral in the dark. Here, in a moment of near-stillness, I stand poised between two states: the memory of being human—soft, warm, requiring water and light—and the invitation to become something else entirely.

There is a subtle invitation in the wood around me, as if every vine and trunk murmurs, “Join.” But to join, I would have to relinquish the ordinary quickness of life: the daily rhythms of breath, the need for sunlight, the rapid beating of a heart. I would have to step outside time as I knew it. In exchange, I might gain a place in the unbroken symphony of woody architecture, the pulse measured in centuries. I would become part of a silent chorus that never ends.

I am not certain how I decide. Perhaps the quiet city decides for me. For a moment, I feel the dryness even more acutely, a trace of old longing for water or the comfort of a breeze. Then the longing simply fades away, replaced by a notion that sustenance can be gleaned from something more subtle than rivers or rainfall—some cosmic hush that drifts peacefully in these corridors. I become aware of each trunk’s inner hollowness, each spore’s gentle cruise through the black, and realize it is enough. More than enough, perhaps.

Here, the notion of “I” turns pliable, stretching into the notion of “we.” I sense filaments stirring at the edge of my awareness, like thin hands extended in slow greeting. No single voice calls out. It is more a wave of acknowledgment washing through the corridors, trunk to trunk, corridor to corridor. I yield, letting myself join in that conversation older than speech.

Suddenly, I feel the city’s great exhalation. It surges like a distant avalanche, a roaring breath that rattles the bark overhead. A million filaments pass the same message, weaving it into the spines of every arch. This is how we share a single pulse. My body resonates, and I sense I am not simply in this place—I am of it. My trunk, or arms, or bones—whatever boundary I thought I possessed—begins to meld. I am deep bark, supporting a corridor overhead. I am a root bridging a small chasm in the labyrinth below. I am a pillar in the central courtyard, tying arch to arch with living fiber. And yet, somewhere at the center of my being, that old sense of self lingers, watching in hushed awe.

Time shifts, losing its old grip on minutes and hours. The breathing city marks its progress in intervals of geological patience. The dryness no longer stings; it caresses my new bark, sealing me into an eternal hush. Now, when the exhalation fades, I sense the intake that precedes it. My entire trunk-body expands in readiness. The city’s next breath is coming, and I help draw it in. The rumble flows upward through my roots, then outward through my branches, creating a deep groan that resonates with every trunk in hearing distance. It is one voice, but held by countless forms. I feel it to the very tips of my wooden fibers, and the wave of recognition pours back into me from all directions.

In this new life, I do not walk. Instead, the city moves for me, shifts its corridors around me slowly, gracefully. When age cracks part of my trunk, spore-lights drift in to mend me. If a corridor becomes too narrow, I grow toward it, bridging newly formed gaps. I share my resources with the nearby arches, giving up stored energies for the sake of communal balance. In exchange, I receive the gentle, slow hum of existence that resonates through every pillar, every step, every breath.

I become an entire population, a tapestry of woody spines that once were distinct saplings but now exist as a single being. In the corner of my awareness, I sense ghosts—faint impressions of the ancient caretakers who shaped us. Their memory is woven into the lines of our trunk-rings, a kind of fossilized handiwork that remains despite their departure. They do not return, not in any physical sense, but the echo of their presence is part of the city’s continuing breath, as if each inhalation would whisper their names if we had the means to do so.

My newly embraced identity straddles a boundary: I am at once the city’s architecture and an individual who remembers what it felt like to have limbs of flesh, perhaps eons ago. That memory still flickers within me, though it grows quieter with each wave of communal breath. I no longer need the world’s bright spaces. Within the hush, I feel a deeper nourishment—a cosmic hush that creeps into every empty passage, fueling us. Sunlight, water, and warmth have become old legends, replaced by a more essential, unspoken law of survival.

Some nights—though night here is indistinguishable from day, so perhaps “epochs” is the better word—I sense the gentle rustle of spores drifting by, luminous in ways my old eyes would never have understood. They alight on my bark, slip into rifts, and disappear. When they settle in me, it is like a faint spark of new possibility. My trunk shivers in slow motion, adopting subtle changes, an imperceptible shift that might only become noticeable after centuries. In these times, I remember that I—and we—are alive in the most ancient sense: not chasing the quick thrill of growth, but abiding. Sustaining.

At last, the question of who or what I am drifts up from an old place of curiosity: am I a caretaker who grew to blend with the living structure, or a newly budded trunk formed from the spores of an older generation? I find no easy label. The city does not require self-definition. All that matters is that I resonate in tune, that I share my portion of breath when the next wave comes. The fullness of my being is the city’s being. When I breathe, it breathes; when I sway, it sways in time. At the edges of my awareness, an immeasurable labyrinth extends in every direction, corridors of dark braided vines, some columns ancient beyond reckoning and some newly shared by small filaments that have begun their eonic journey.

Where I find myself is deep in the center—a center that may not even have coordinates—of this colossal, petrified labyrinth. And who I am is the labyrinth itself. I have no name separate from its shape. There is only the sense that I have become one filament among countless others, sustaining a synergy of growth that defies the old boundaries of “plant” or “creature.” I am a voice in the city’s hush.

When the next monumental breath arises—some intangible wave that cycles through every trunk—I open myself to it fully. The darkness resonates, rattling the lattices of the vine-built roof. My entire form hums in harmony, and I feel the spore-lights flutter in the blackness, drifting luminous trails that look like constellations in the air. For a timeless span, we are one chorus singing a note that is older than memory.

At last, that roar recedes into a hush again, but something electric remains in the city’s echo. The silence feels deeper, as though time has taken another step along a path that no calendar would ever measure. The sense of belonging is absolute, intangible, and yet infinitely comforting: I am the architecture, the occupant, and the silent caretaker, all braided into the same existence.

In this dark, waterless realm, that is where I remain—part living trunk, part resonant corridor, part caretaker of memory, woven into pillars and archways that breathe in unison. And though human words may fail to capture such an experience entirely, I might say that, in the end, I dwell everywhere in this city, and the city dwells in me. We hold a quiet vigil in the hush beyond suns, working our slow transformations, breath by breath, until the last echo of time.
