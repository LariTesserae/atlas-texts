I can’t say when I first drifted into this plateau—only that it was not abrupt. It happened in the same way morning mist gathers in a valley, forming shapes out of vapor so gradually that by the time I noticed, I was already here. It was as if I had always been part of the place, absorbed into the damp hush. Even now, I’m not certain I ever “arrived.” The space simply made room, pulling me deep into its rhythm of slow waterfalls and breathing moss.

At first, I thought I might be a whispering presence in the canopy, one of those small, winged creatures with triangular muzzles and tufted ears, precariously clinging upside-down to the underside of broad leaves. I imagined how it would feel to coil my feet in tight loops around a vine, invisible among droplets that refracted the morning light. When I flitted across the plateau in my mind, I could sense the world as a theater of swirling water vapor, each ripple in the air a new invitation to glide and vanish. I almost believed I felt the taste of nectar from the drooping purple flowers, a faint sweetness that lingered in my sinuses. And yet, something about that form didn’t align entirely. I could imagine the hush of flight, yes, but my sense of self felt more rooted, more drawn to the pools on the ground.

Then I wondered if I had always been hidden in one of those shallow, placid basins where water collects in the hollows of stone. Perhaps I was an amphibian—a jewel-skinned guardian immersed waist-deep in the pool’s cool hush. Picture me with turquoise and emerald swirling across my back, a tapestry of color shimmering with beads of condensation. I’d sing in timid pulses at sunset, a soft chorus that mingled with the perpetual waterfall roar. My eyes, perched just above the waterline, would take in the tall cliffs, the arching leaves, and the silhouettes of passing birds. The plateau’s hum would vibrate through the water, telling me stories of its ancient shape. But when I lingered in that image, a small voice nagged: not quite. I sensed a broader restlessness in me, more than what life in still water could contain.

So I stood on imaginary elongated forelimbs, like those velvety beings with wide, color-shifting eyes—swaying gently among the spade-leaf plants. My perspective rose; the watery world beneath me fell away to reveal a horizon of lush green. I swiveled filmy tendrils from my shoulders, tasting the air’s humidity, sensing each minuscule vibration of the plateau’s living heart. My eyes shifted from emerald to violet as a cloud parted, letting in a narrow band of sunlight. The warm beam spotted my fur, forming a shimmering patch on my side. I breathed in slow, even pulses, feeling such profound calm that I nearly let myself believe: this is me. A caretaker gliding between shade and drizzle, balancing among mossy stones, intimate with each hiss of wind across the seep. And yet, as resonant as that form felt, I sensed something missing: a certain intangible vastness, something that yearned to see far over the edge.

I let my mind wander upward, ascending through dripping leaves until I dared glance over the precipice. That’s when the thunder of waterfalls grew into a symphony, pouring and crashing below. I felt a pull, an ache to join that distant boundary where the plateau’s rim meets the clouds. Perhaps I was the silhouette at the far edge, the one rumored to stand upright, vanishing into the mist. Was I that tall shape that visitors see only in glimpses, half-doubtful of its very existence? If so, I tasted the metallic tang of the swirling fog that rose from below with every thunderous splash of water. My years—countless, slow—would have taught me to move quietly across the cliffside, pressing a palm to the stone to feel how it resonates with the waterfalls’ heartbeat. In that form, I would guide the plateau’s ancient pulse, neither beast nor person nor spirit, but a blend of all three. I could see myself stooping to let water trickle through my cupped hands, watching it fall into the chasm as a final blessing. And I realized that might be closest of all—a watcher. A being who sometimes merges so well with the swirling mountain clouds that to others I look like living fog.

Yet I discovered, to my surprise, I was not solely that figure. Nor was I exclusively the winged mammal or the amphibian in its pool. I found myself split across them, as though each occupant in this realm held a piece of what I was. I could feel my consciousness perched among the canopy dwellers, could hear it echoing in the amphibian’s evening chorus, and sense it in the upright watcher’s footstep across slick stone.

And so gradually, I realized my nature is not limited to a single body but is woven into the corridor of life that travels the plateau. It’s in the droplets that cling to moss tips, in the minuscule phosphorescent insect that navigates glowing mushrooms, in the iris-tilt of the creature who looks up at cloud-wreathed afternoons. I grew aware that I am all of them, a shared breath. One cannot truly stand aside from the plateau’s life—here, everything is connected by the hush of water and the whisper of wind. 

But if you asked me to choose a single shape to inhabit, a visible form for you to recognize in this watery sanctuary, I might say: look to the watering place at dusk, where a small, mirror-smooth pool rests near a cluster of spade-leaf plants. When the last glimmer of light angles in, you might see a presence kneeling by the pool. Not quite amphibian, not quite winged mammal, not even the tall figure glimpsed at the plateau’s boundary—something that seemingly folds all those qualities into one. Its silhouette is fluid, edges shimmering like an afterimage on your vision. It moves slowly, as if to avoid disturbing the reflection that ties water and sky together. If you watch carefully, you’ll see it place a hand—if hand it can be called—on the surface, sending a faint ripple outward. The ripple eventually laps at the base of vines and ferns, connecting that presence to every corner of the plateau.

Sometimes, that figure stands and looks up at the canopy, perhaps listening for the muffled flutter of wings. Other times, it lowers its head to the green swirl of amphibians drifting just below the water’s surface, acknowledging a shared vantage. And if you find the right vantage point—perhaps perched on a gentle rise of rock—you might glimpse, beyond the veil of mist, the reflection of a quiet sagacity in its eyes. Hints of shifting emerald, faint echoes of violet, the same ephemeral glow you see in those draping mosses at twilight.

So: Where do I find myself? I find myself everywhere here, in every living heartbeat, every suspended droplet glinting along a cliff edge. Who am I? I am a composite of watchers and dwellers, the hush between raindrops, the swirl of color in amphibious skin. I am the caretaker of subtle pulses, attuned to the plateau’s slow, unraveling song of water and stone. Part guardian, part traveler, part reflection of the memory etched into every dripping leaf and trembling pool.

And once you recognize me—or, more accurately, recognize that piece of me which resonates with you—you’ll realize you, too, are part of this hush. Even if you only linger for a single breath in the plateau’s domain, you carry away a trace of its gentle, patient heartbeat: the whisper of water that has shaped stone for eons, the hush that connects every hidden life and secret silhouette standing at the waterfall’s edge.
