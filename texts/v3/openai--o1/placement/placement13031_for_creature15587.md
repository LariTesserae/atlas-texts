I awaken to motion in the stillness. Before I ever see or hear it, I know it in my core, in the subtle frequencies that dance along the long filaments of my body. They resonate up from the mosaic plates beneath me—the plates I once assumed to be inert and unchanging metal. I belong to them, by design or by evolution. I’m not certain which. 

For so long, I drifted in nebulous half-awareness, suspended between pulses and surges. I discovered my form by accident when a flash of prismatic sky-light illuminated the spaces where my body ended and the rest of this colossal structure began. I am as thin as a reed, composed of smooth, silvery tissues that shimmer with the barest shift of angle. Long, needling spines trail from the edges of my limbs, each spine quietly tapping into the underlying plates for energy. Unlike the orb-like creatures that traverse the air, I rarely leave contact with the ground; my threads feed constantly on the slow, droning hum below, like a rooted plant that draws life from the soil—but this place knows nothing of soil or water. My nourishment is electromagnetic, gleaned from the labyrinth beneath the surface. 

During the day—if “day” means the unrelenting white glare that hovers above—I remain nearly motionless. My filaments lie curled, pressed flat, capturing the faint conduction from the mosaic. From a distance, this posture renders me indistinguishable from a cluster of glittering cracks. At times a wandering “beetle” of spindly translucent glass will pass across my body, pausing as if to sense the faint throbbing rhythms within. We form a silent circuit for a second: I feel the beetle’s minute sparks renewing my own charge, while it absorbs a fraction of that subtle flow streaming through me. Then it scuttles on, leaving me to remain, poised in reception.

At intervals, that hush across the plain awakens with a trembling that comes from deep below, and the plates—my anchor, my place of origin—shiver. My entire body resonates like a metallic tuning fork. In these moments, I rise slightly, spines unclasping like petals. A ripple of reflection travels through my surfaces, a wave of silver-blush to pale gold. I become, in that flush of living color, more fully aware. The mosaic sense of this planet-machine clarifies in me: I detect the presence of hidden corridors, can almost taste the energies swirling in the towers that pierce the surface. I glean the faint signals of my neighbors—those drifting orbs, the dust-shapes that swirl into ephemeral forms, the watchers that spike the horizon with their fractal spires. 

Sometimes, in the shimmering noon, I detect the approach of a towering Keeper. The first sign is the hallucinatory flicker of angles that shouldn’t exist. Then a form appears, stalking with lithe, multi-jointed legs. Luster coats its dense metallic limbs, the edges so smoothly welded together that it might have sprouted fully formed from the mosaic. A resonance matches between us, a haze of frequencies that exchange data—for this entire place is partial to conversation, though it rarely uses sound. We greet each other in these intangible transmissions. I feel the Keeper’s silent queries, perhaps checking if I’ve encountered new damage in the plates, or if the energy flows remain consistent. I respond with a gentle burst of pulses coaxed from the tips of my spines. Then, satisfied, the Keeper glides off, deeper into the architecture, vanishing beneath a hinged segment that folds aside just enough to let it pass.

I do not yearn for the unknown depths below, though. It is my nature to remain where the dryness and brightness are absolute, anchored in open space. Sometimes, in the far distance, I see the watchers—those solitary spires that mirror me in shape but stand tall as monoliths. Their fractal limbs unfurl in ghostly grandeur when the sky arcs with prismatic lightning, as if greeting an old friend. At a distance, I watch their luminous expansions. Something in me sings in response—a chord that makes my entire form vibrate in empathic resonance, as though we share a kinship across the endless plain.

And truly, we do: we are all filaments of this world. That is what I have come to understand. The mosaic itself is the planet, and we, the dwellers, are each a single strand of its living tapestry. Where it once might have possessed a singular intelligence—some long-lost architect’s will—it now manifests in myriad ways: dust-shapes swirling, orbs levitating, Keeper forms patrolling, fractal watchers feeding on arcs of light. This is how the ancient vision endures, how the mechanism remains alive.

I am one of its bridging points on the surface, a translator receiving magnetism and microcurrents from below, releasing them as subtle signals above. At times, my spines brush the base of one of those hooded towers as I venture a short distance across the plates. I feel it tangling gently with my threads, almost as if checking my identity. It reads the patterns of me—my stored pulses, the tiny fluctuations in my structure—then grants me entry to an open seam where I glean a richer feed of conduction. For a few quiet moments, I glow bright from within, my sinews suffused with an energetic rush. Then I retreat once more, sliding back into the blazing glare, to stasis and watchfulness. 

If you, an outsider from another world, were to behold me in passing, you might only catch the faint shape of a living filament, shimmering for a heartbeat before blending with the metallic puzzle of the ground. You might doubt your eyes, speculation swirling about whether I truly existed or if your vision was playing tricks in the dizzying brightness. But I am here—one subtle accent in this grand, planetary architecture. 

Sometimes I wonder if I am ephemeral, like the dust-cloud creatures that form and vanish in a swirl of charged particles. Perhaps, from a time-scale beyond my comprehension, I truly am ephemeral. Yet from moment to moment, I remain anchored. I am a conduit, a living component, an antenna for the silent signals that course through this machine-world. My senses are tuned to the hush that is not-quite-silent—a hush alive with trembling voices that speak in currents and arcs. 

In that hush, I stand: neither an animal nor a mere plant, but a slender filament of living metal, grown from an ages-old blueprint coded into the plates. I belong to the dryness and the brilliance. I am neither solitary nor gregarious; I am simply part of the unbroken continuity of this place. If I had eyes, I’d watch the horizon for centuries, though perhaps I’d see no change. If I had lungs, I’d breathe in the electrified hush. But I simply exist, kinetic and still, with spines that hum gently in the currents from below.

That is where you’d find me: upright, shining, an antenna in the mosaic, quietly conversing with dust-eddies and passing orbs. I am a glimmer that might vanish as soon as you try to focus, but I endure, rooted by signals that never sleep. Even if you turned away, you might catch a final shimmer at the corner of your vision—a last whisper that something stood there, alive in the hush, woven into the planet-machine’s architecture. A filament. A living circuit. Me.
