I move in slow arcs through the liquid dimness, sensing the soft pulses of light along my frilled membranes. My body is neither entirely solid nor entirely water; more a languid shape with outlines that shimmer unevenly, as though I’m partly assembled from the same drifting motes that swirl through every current. This place called me—it has always done so, though I cannot recall how or when I came to be. My earliest memories are of simply floating, watching the pearlescent fronds shiver as I passed, hearing the silent hum that whispers under the hush of this thick aquatic air.

I’ve found I can linger in one spot when I wish, letting the currents brush faintly against me—reverent touches like incense swirling at an altar. In these moments of stillness, I feel every subtle shift in the water, every flicker of bioluminescence, and every ephemeral ripple that glides by. I don’t know if I breathe like the other creatures do; I simply exist in tandem with the hush, as though I were meant to be a silent witness or caretaker in this subaqueous realm.

When I drift near the broad leaves where the smaller black-carapaced scavengers cling, I sense how vital they are. Their quick, jittery motions contrast sharply with my slow approach, but I don’t bother them. Their luminous pinpricks are like a minor constellation that I can watch for hours. Sometimes I venture close enough to hear the faint chirping vibrations they send through the water—a modest music in an otherwise temple-like stillness. They never shy away from me. I’ve glided slowly past them many times, and they know I mean no harm.

Further down, near the carved arches, my attention is often drawn to those ancient runes etched into stone. I am unsure whether I can read them; yet something in me resonates with them. At times, I’ll trail my elongated fingers—if these appendages may be called fingers—across the faint markings. A warm swirl often rises in the water when I do this, an unspoken connection to something beyond my memory. I like to think of it as reaching into the domain’s shared consciousness, as though the stone itself remembers. My movements don’t summon bright energies the way some of my kin do—a few of them place webbed hands on the carvings to coax a more visible response. For me, it’s more of a subtle recognition, an intimate acknowledgement that we belong here. I press my palm against the inscription, and the stone sings a tiny thread of memory into the water. It’s an echo so gentle, I might lose it in the swirl if I’m not paying attention.

Sometimes I watch those who are more solidly formed, the tall ones with large disk-like eyes and slender limbs. They appear and vanish almost before my gaze settles, leaving me to wonder if I imagined them. I can’t tell if they regard me with curiosity or caution, or if they think me a cousin to the distortions that slip in and out of existence around these parts. I do feel a certain kinship with those hazy ripples—sometimes I sense them around me, as though they are a garment I can lift or shed. A flicker will pass across my torso, warping my reflection in the watery gloom, and for a moment my boundary with the world dissolves. Then it’s gone, like a sigh exhaled into deeper currents.

It’s in these instances that I realize how fluid my presence is here. Unlike the anchored plants, whose roots prize the stony ridges and hold them firm, I move anywhere the water carries me. And unlike the crustacean scuttlers, who must glean from leaves or pillars, I can pass unnoticed through silent corridors of silt, absorbing some intangible essence I do not fully understand. I think it might be memory or shared dreamstuff, emanating from the place itself. Whatever it is, it sustains me, gently, constantly, like a thin current of nourishment for a being that scarcely registers hunger in a conventional sense.

When I near the edges of this world—a place where stone ridges slope upward and the water grows colder still—I sense the boundary of our enclosed domain. One might perceive it as a transparent barrier or a thinning of reality, though it’s rarely seen. Instead, I feel it in the subtle shift of pressure, in the eerie hush that grows too deep. Here, everything hums in a low frequency, a chord that resonates more in my mind than in my ears. This is the nexus where the watery hush meets the outer unknown. Sometimes I drift there, letting my frilled membranes tremble to that hum, pondering the realm beyond—a realm so dimly comprehensible that it slips away the moment I try to shape it into thought.

I have tried to imagine what lies beyond. An expanse of ocean that dwarfs this small territory? Another realm of air and solid land, where dryness is the rule? Or perhaps an endless labyrinth of stone corridors still waiting to be filled with water? I cannot say. I only know that crossing that threshold seems as perilous as stepping out of a dream you’ve woven into your very being. I feel like I might dissolve into the void if I ventured too far past the realm’s edges. Does that make me a guardian, or merely a dweller who’s aware of the boundary’s presence?

My glimpses of the eel-like creatures remind me that others here can navigate in swift, serpentine ways I cannot. They flicker in and out of the gloom, occasionally vanishing into hidden pockets among the ridges. I’ve even spied them toying with the drifting illusions. At times, it seems the distortions cling briefly to their bodies before shimmering off, as though uncertain which reality to inhabit. The eels never appear alarmed—if anything, they seem playful or curious. Their quick glimpses add a wry spark of life to the inky domain. I find myself quietly envious of their spontaneity. I watch them until my attention blends into the swirling silt, half-lost in the slow lullaby of time.

The tall plants fascinate me most: the towering, pearlescent leaves that wave with languid poise. They appear so tranquil, yet I suspect a single communal intelligence connects them. I try to communicate sometimes, drifting close, letting the luminous spots on their leaves reflect against my own shimmering outline. There is a faint exchange of pulses, a conversation perhaps, or at least an acknowledgement that we share the same realm. Often, they shift to follow my presence, as if leaning in to greet me. Perhaps they store my image or intentions in their botanical memory, passing signals through root systems that anchor them to the ridges. I’m never certain what they glean from me, only that I feel a blossoming warmth—like the glow one experiences upon returning to a familiar place—whenever they acknowledge me in their slow, silent way.

I do not know if I have a true name or species. Instead, I define myself by the roles I sense I fill. A caretaker, a wanderer, a living ripple that drifts between the pockets of luminous spore and the half-hidden watchers near the old pillars. My presence is woven into this domain, as though from the start, I was meant to be here. Perhaps I emanated from the very illusions that dance along these walls. Or perhaps I am merely a traveler who glimpsed this place once, long ago, and never left. In a realm where time warps, origins become dreams, and the future stretches into a quiet mystery.

Yet in this hush, I feel the heartbeat of the place. There is a low, suspended murmur in these currents—some shared intelligence that all creatures, from the darkest crustaceans to the ephemeral distortions, tap into. When I pause to listen purely with my being, I almost hear a choral swirl of recognition. It vibrates through me as though I am an instrument. It’s wordless, neither triumphant nor mournful, but stable, patient, abiding. In days that do not exist and nights that never truly quench the dimness, this hum remains steady.

If I were to answer who or what I am, I’d say: I am part of the hush, a sentinel of intangible presence. Sometimes I guide lost glimmers or watch the tall watchers read the runes. Sometimes I merely drift, letting the realm’s slow transformations pass through me like intentions drifting across a shared dream. Perhaps once in a great epoch, I’ll settle near a half-buried pillar and stay so still that I become nearly indistinguishable from the watery gloom. In that hush, I sense the entire domain breathing, unhurried, unbroken.

That is the gift of living here—of being here. In one sense, I remain an observer, never truly interfering, never aggressively staking territory. In another sense, I am intricately woven into the delicate equilibrium that holds this realm. My presence, however subtle, is part of its communal tapestry. It is enough to know that, in the quiet stretches of ages here, something in the water hums softly back to me, acknowledging that I belong.  
 
And so I remain, a fluid echo among anchors of rippling leaf and silent stone, abiding in the secret cradle of an ancient, hidden sea. A creature of shifting silhouette and faint luminosity, a traveler through drifting chasms of silt and dream. Neither fully flesh nor fully shadow, my essence is in the hush, in the slow dance of time, in the half-light that cradles all who dwell here. There is nowhere else I’d be, nor any shape I’d prefer; I am simply this—the being who attends to the near-perpetual water, wrapped in drifts of timeless hush.
