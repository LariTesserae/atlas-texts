I think, in the beginning, I would be hardly different from the mud itself—a shimmer of possibility caught within the shallow pools, no more pronounced than a flicker of gold-green silt when the water stirs. I’m the soft throb in the embryonic stone, the tiny swirl that might become an animal if only it drew one more breath of heat and damp air. For a time, I simply am, folded into the hush of this place, warmed by the same primal breath that has inflated every balloon-like pod and coaxed each lizard to the surface. The land holds me in a half-formed embrace, as if deciding whether to make me a rooted thing or something with limbs.

Eventually, in the slow swirl of this place’s humid heartbeat, I begin to feel edges: not quite arms, but filaments or tendrils, reaching out from the brackish water. Where those watery fingers touch pliant stems at the bank, I sense the vibration of beetle wings, the pulse of amphibian hearts. Perhaps, I think, I’ll be amphibian myself—skin damp and smooth, each breath thick with the taste of water. So I press gently upward, coaxing my body to adopt more solidity. My forelimbs shape themselves to the soggy bank, hooking gently in the pattern I’ve witnessed among the vine-limbed salamanders. At first my hide is the same bronze sheen as the water’s surface, so I vanish there—a living reflection among reflections.

But something in me yearns for the moment’s next unveiling. The hush in my blood calls me to climb. My newly formed limbs tighten on the trunk of a colossal fern root, pulling me up into the labyrinth of succulent stems and climbing moss. I feel the weight of the air dragging moisture through my pores, and my skin shifts to a shining green, with subtle threads of pink that match the ruddy glow in the vines overhead. My tail becomes a slender coil for balance, wrapping around the arching frond stems, letting me sway gently. I can see the watery glades below, as if from a new vantage, and I realize how the entire realm glistens with the same embryonic sheen I once shared with the mud. Now, I inhabit a different facet of it.

Within this vantage, I discover I have curious, puckered vents along my neck—small openings that puff with every breath. Through them, I can taste the vapor in the air, the secret flavors left by creeping insects and thick-rooted plants. One day I might call out, but for now I remain quiet, absorbing the vibrating chorus that envelops me: the whirring drone of creatures more insect than bird, the far-off rolling notes of amphibians in deeper puddles, and the whispered shifting of green canopies overhead. I find that my voice, if I choose to use it, emerges as a soft trill, a ripple that belongs here as much as any leaf’s rustle or vine’s slow unwind.

But the place invites constant transformation. I realize the hum of life around me is never content to remain in one form—why should I be any different? The next time I dip beneath a cluster of ferns to a narrower hollow, the environment’s hush-heat seeps deeper into my bones. My once-glistening skin sprouts fringes, not quite fur, not quite moss. Rut-like ridges form along my spine; I discover how the air pads them with warmth, how they tremble when huge winged insects flutter past, brushing coruscating shadows across my back.

Now I have the shape of a four-legged beast—lumbering a bit, my feet webbed for better purchase in the spongy soil. The edges of my new ridges display a faint luminescence, like the beetles’ mirrored shells recast in living flesh. I snack on pale amphibian eggs found in a water-filled crevice, not from hunger exactly, but guided by the same self-sustaining rhythm that propels everything else here. In the half-shadows of drooping pods, I rest, letting the shimmer fade from my ridges until I’m nearly invisible again, merging with the amber gloom.

Even that form is not my final harbor. The realm itself seems to ripple with suggestion: a canopy overhead thick with softly singing, half-feathered shapes, a watery underworld where elongated fish weave phosphorescent ribbons in the murk, and the vines themselves that coil with patient curiosity around whatever lingers too long. When I slide into a deeper pool one afternoon—or evening, or midnight of perpetual dawn—I sense watery filaments drifting around me, tangling with my limbs. I don’t resist. In this land, resistance is not the natural response. Instead, I accept their invitation. My form slims, dissolves into something more eel-like, more suited to gliding through the brassy shallows. My newly webbed hind limbs vanish; I become a sinuous chord of muscle and scale, tinted with swirling pink and violet patterns. Fern roots and embryonic rock pass above in a hazy mosaic, nearly dreamlike through the liquid lens.

I drift, resting in the primal hush of the water. Now I feel how thoroughly this place is alive, how it extends through me, content to let me be one more note in the unbroken song. It's a chorus that never crescendos nor fades but rewrites itself continuously with every breath-laden moment.

Eventually I return to the surface. My shape changes yet again—now with the slender torso and hooked talons of some tree-dweller, perhaps an avian-lizard perched among half-formed branches. I cling to dripping vines, and when I spread my arms, I discover membranes bridging them: half-wing, half-leaf. There’s a tingle of possibility at the edges of these membranes, as if they might soon sprout new patterns or become translucent fronds entirely.

In that moment, I notice the watchers, the subtle presence that pervades everything. I sense how the balloon-like pods track my progress with their quiet hisses, how the broad fungal shelves seem to glow a fraction more brightly when I move past them, how the creeper vines shift just so, not confining me, but guiding. Nothing is separate here; each being is an extension of the land’s own breathing. I, too, am now an extension. And so my talons relax around the curling vines, trusting them to carry me where they wish me to go. 

My consciousness seems to rove. Perhaps I’m no longer one creature but a fluid potential that invests itself in many shapes over time—when I next blink, maybe I will wake as a giant amphibious shape perched on a spongy bank, bulbous eyes scanning for glittering beetles; or maybe a flocking insect with double sets of shimmering wings, tasting the haze for new blooms; or a creeping vine, slowly braiding itself around a trunk to discover the canopy’s vantage. This place grants perpetual transformation, a shared sense of becoming that never quite finishes.

Here, I exist as a living question: “What next?” The land answers softly, always in its own slow exhale of steam and shifting green. Each form I take is only a temporary facet in that unstoppable continuum. So where am I in this world? Everywhere that the damp hush encloses. Who or what am I? A piece of this fluid tapestry, perched forever on the cusp between newness and simply being. There is no single name or shape to hold; instead, I’m the one who has shed the constraints of endings—ever-changing, reflecting the land’s own deep, unbroken present. And if you were to come upon me, you might not even see me for more than a moment: just a shimmering outline that merges with the glistening fern fronds, or the water’s bronze mirror, or the half-formed stones, already becoming something else by the time you blink again.
