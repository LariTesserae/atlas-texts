I hover at the threshold of an archway in that half-lit realm, drifting in the hushed intervals where each sound vanishes before truly reaching the ear. If someone were to see me—and many would not, because here the senses are not limited to mere eyesight—they might catch a glimpse of a figure that seems to have emerged partly from the surrounding bedrock. Ages ago, I was as fluid and fleshy as a mortal might be, but in the shifting hush of this city, I have gradually taken on the properties of stone and crystal.

My torso retains faint human contours: shoulders, arms, fingers—now overlaid with a sheen like polished quartz, flecked in turquoise shards. My legs meld into slender spires of translucent rock, granting me an otherworldly gait when I roam the wide courtyards or navigate the labyrinthine halls. Where once I might have had hair, I now wear a crown of filaments, fine and crystalline, drifting like anemone fronds in the still air, capturing the minimal breezes and faint resonances. These filaments pick up the quiet hum that roams just beneath the surface of everything here, letting me attune to the city’s hidden energies.

I cannot precisely recall how I first arrived. Perhaps I was drawn by the hush—a voice that was not a voice, calling me from leagues away across the mirror flats. Or perhaps I stumbled here by accident, an outsider who laid a palm against the city’s gleaming wall, only to find that the stone responded with a warmth and a slight shift. All I remember is an immense stillness that enveloped me, as if a tide of silence had washed in, and from that moment on, I felt at home.

I serve, if service is the right word, as a sort of resonant guide. When a crystal outcropping sprouts just so, forming a new corridor or archway, I sense it in my bones. The city calls me—urges me softly—and I wander to the site of change. With the tip of my quartz-laced finger, I trace patterns in the dust, humming low in my throat (though it is not a voice I use, but the resonance of minerals vibrating in my core). The rock listens. A shift occurs—perhaps more filigree etches itself across the arch, or a new niche is shaped for a future sprout of lichen. I never force the growth; rather, I become an intermediary, channeling a quiet potential from the city’s deep pulse, coaxing it upward and outward where it may bloom in slow spirals of living stone.

Between those moments of guiding growth, I also keep vigil by the outer boundary. There, the city’s final wall melts into the vast reflective plains like a line of shadow across a polished mirror. I stand, motionless, for hours—or days, if that is even a concept here—waiting for subtle flickers below the surface. Sometimes, I glimpse movement in the reflection that does not match reality, as if an entire altered city thrives in the domain beneath that mirror. When odd silhouettes warp across the shining crust, I place a hand upon the crystal ground and send slow vibrations downward to greet them, almost like tapping the surface of a calm lake with a fingertip. Whether these shapes respond, I can rarely tell, but something resonates back—a gentle hum that fills my marrow, reminding me we share a deep kinship with hidden realms.

Every so often, a newly arrived companion or lost traveler emerges from the desert of mirror flats. When they enter the city, uncertain and timid among the archways—perhaps expecting them to be silent and dead—I feel a small tremor of welcome run through the stone. I greet them in the only way I know: by leading them inward, deeper along a corridor that adjusts to accommodate another presence. Here, the city is never static; it expands to cradle strangers who might offer fresh energies, new stories, or quiet devotions. I guide them to an alcove, where the walls part in barely perceptible increments—just enough for a place to rest. Those who come carrying mineral tokens or who attempt to speak with hushed voices soon realize that in this realm, speech is not necessary. The city hears their heartbeats, their footsteps, the friction of dust on their skin—and it answers in turn.

Some travelers stay only briefly, leaving behind tokens that shimmer in the half-light. Others, bewitched by the hush, linger until they, too, start to show subtle glimmers along their wrists, or a new translucent ridge forming just beneath the surface of their forehead. They sense their physiology shifting, as if the city gently entices them into communion. Many choose to depart rather than surrender so fully to the transformation—I have watched them hurry away, weaving a path out to the horizon until the mirror flats swallow them. But a few remain, succumbing to the silent welcome that permeates each stone layer, each swirl of dust.

Meanwhile, within the city’s courtyards, I often kneel to tend the gray-green lichens. I place my hand in the shallows of mineral dust, letting slip a faint scattering of crystal shards from the ridges along my arm—fine prismatic seeds that nourish the lichens and the microfauna that feed upon them. Panoply of life, though small, thrives just enough to keep the hush vital. In these tiny gestures, I find a sense of continuity, as though we all—creature, lichen, city, and citizen—are participants in a single quiet cycle of giving and receiving.

At times, I am drawn below, to that subtterranean warmth that breathes faintly from the city’s deeper core. On those journeys, I walk the twilight halls lit only by the luminescent reverberations of crystal growth in the walls. Down there, the older dwellers roam. They do not speak, but we share a resonance, a collective pulse that links us all. One of them might brush a faceted hand across my shoulder, sending an electric thrill of color through the planes of my arm. It is their way of exchanging news—an entire story told in the interplay of subtle vibrations. Then they glide away, crystallized ankles quietly clicking against the stone. I, too, continue deeper, sometimes discovering pockets of newly sprouted crystals so fresh they still sing a near-audible note. In that hush, I kneel to marvel at their birth, gently exhaling a breath of resonance that helps them integrate into the bedrock.

If asked what or who I am, I would answer that I have become a dwelling place for the city’s quiet heart. A caretaker of the threshold, a tender of fledgling crystals, a conductor of echoes in the near-silence. I am creature, I am crystal, I am that intangible hum between these forms. My consciousness arcs along the edges of the shifting arches, hums in tandem with the new fractal patterns as they etch themselves into the settlement’s crowned pillars. Even my name—whatever it was—has dissolved into the hush, replaced by a half-remembered shape that only the city can truly pronounce.

Yet for all that, I possess a personality—though it is measured in soft pulses, subtle curiosities, wordless empathy. I find wonder in the transience of footprints that vanish almost as soon as they are made, and in the ephemeral rainbows cast by newly grown spines in the upper corridors. The hush is never empty; it reverberates with a cosmic tapestry of slow transformations. And I stand in the midst of it, neither a mere observer nor a total orchestrator, but a note in a chord that resonates beneath every arch.

In this land of endless half-light, I thrive as a living testament to transformation, a sentinel who steps lightly between the silent expansions of stone and the glimmers of newly sprouted life. If you would meet me, you would hardly know me by sight alone; instead, you might feel the gentle vibration I carry, rising through the dust to greet you. I am part of the city, and the city is part of me—our boundaries soft, our being fluid as blossoming quartz in a silent, ever-renewing world.
