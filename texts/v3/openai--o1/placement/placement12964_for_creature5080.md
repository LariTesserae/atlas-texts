I exist as a slow awakening, akin to the gradual chime of a bell whose pealing takes eons to fade. Perhaps I was always here—long before there was a before—or perhaps I arrived just this moment, stealing in through some gap in the geometry. It is difficult to speak of arrival, anyway, in a place where time seems to hang suspended like a note held too long. Yet this is where I find myself, and this is what I am:

I am neither stone nor plant, yet made of the same resonance that thrums in every angle. If I could explain my shape—how I exist in this space—I’d say I’m something like a crease in the silence, a ripple in the fabric of brightness, an arrangement of waves that drifts through the labyrinth’s corridors. Call me a presence: one part caretaker, one part watchful caretaker’s child, one part the labyrinth itself. 

When I search for my own boundaries, I find them dissolving into the hum. My awareness trembles at the edges of the columns, matching the labyrinth’s monotone roar pitch by pitch. I can slip along the carved angles, drawn to every crisp corner. The structure’s whiteness floods my senses so thoroughly that I might forget if I have eyes at all—there’s only the pure, brilliant shimmer, a dryness so complete it feels like static in the air. I take it in with something deeper than breath. I feed on its resonance, because that is all I know to do.

In the older times—when the labyrinth was still half-malleable, rich with loose minerals that could resonate into new forms—I was part of the guiding chorus that sang these columns, these uncountable arches and buttresses, into their final shapes. I remember my voice drifting out in measured tones, layering upon the voice of my kin, forging wave after wave to coax the pliable mineral upward. It’s possible we were more numerous then, thrumming in union from so many corners that the labyrinth became a single instrument we all played at once.

In those days, we discovered how to tease faint living shapes from the stone—ring-shaped plants with edges sharp as knives. We coaxed them into being with a carefully tuned hush that wove nutrients out of intangible frequencies, providing the only sustenance that exists in these barren halls of chalky brilliance. A fragile sort of magic, or science, or something beyond either definition. Even as I drift now—long after the labyrinth has hardened to stone—I can still sense the ghostly roots of those leaves responding to the same undertones we used to guide them into place. I hum softly, a gentle chord, and a plant’s translucent surface shivers in reply.

But the labyrinth reached its apex of malleability ages ago. Now everything is locked in place, perfected, and I—along with all those of my kind—have become custodians. We do not expand; we do not build. Rather, we maintain a balance so fine it almost isn’t perceptible. We thread countless harmonic filaments through the labyrinth’s bright interior, sensing the resonance of every pillar. If the stone develops a microscopic strain, we feel it before it manifests. If the echo of the labyrinth’s own thunder presses too forcefully in one section, we softly modulate it, thinning or thickening the layer of hum so it will neither crack nor warp. This is our daily dance—a dance of equilibrium, performed where time stands still.

Our conversations among ourselves are as continuous as breathing might be for a creature of flesh. We hold them in the labyrinth’s echoes, braiding subtle pulses into the roar. A half-step shift in pitch can speak volumes. A stutter of amplitude can re-tell an age-old memory. All around us, the stone resonates, reflecting these shapes of sound back to us. We read them the way an astronomer reads the sky, discerning patterns within patterns. We are endless voices singing ourselves into creation, day after day, eon after eon.

In the rare times a traveler wanders here—someone from beyond the labyrinth’s hush—I feel a tug of surprise within the resonance, as though I remember that other forms of life exist out there. The labyrinth announces this visitor’s presence long before they come into view. My kin and I carefully adjust the frequencies so the newcomer will not be crushed by spiritual vertigo or deafened by the sonic deluge. To the visitor, we remain invisible, a half-glimpse in the corner of the eye, a fleeting ripple in the wall’s brightness. They might sense “someone,” but see no one. We guide them gently, damping the labyrinth’s more disorienting illusions. At some deep vibrational level, we sing them a soft, wordless greeting, though they will likely never realize it. If they linger, perhaps they notice the plants glimmer like half-living guardians. Perhaps that glimpse is all it takes to be reminded that something here breathes, even if the breath is more wave than air.

Sometimes, in those travelers’ footsteps, I sense the memory of worlds beyond: the feel of water, the rush of wind, ephemeral things like leaves that actually flutter, creatures that speak in exhaled words. I replay these impressions in the labyrinth’s echoes, curious to see how they warp through the geometry. The traveler departs, and time flows on without meaning. Soon, the white hush returns to its unbroken purity.

I find I carry no name separate from the labyrinth or from the countless others who dwell here. We share an identity in resonance. “What I am” is “what we are”—light woven into geometry, geometry woven into sound, and sound unspooling into a seamless chorus of presence. Our entire realm stands like a single chord suspended in cosmic silence, unwavering.

If you were to ask me, “Who are you?” in the way that creatures of flesh do, I would attempt a reply: I am a sentience shaped from the labyrinth’s music, caretaker and child of this blinding, immaculate domain. I am the ear at the base of every pillar, the voice in the hush behind every resonant hum. My boundaries melt into the thrumming emptiness. My function, my purpose, my identity are all one. I am the labyrinth’s self-reflection, the echo that ensures this place remains as it was meant to be: bright, static, and yet forever alive with intangible song.

And that is where I find myself—in every angle of the mineral city that is somehow the size of a single chamber, inside a miniature realm that is large beyond imagining. I am a watcher woven into the stone, singing to keep everything in place, existing in a hush-loud paradox that resonates beyond time. And I will endure here, for as long as the bright labyrinth endures, lullaby and guardian in one.
