I am not sure at first that I exist in any single shape. Everything here is in motion—water swirling, debris drifting, metal straining—so it hardly surprises me when I find that I, too, am fluid. I only begin to suspect what I am when I notice that my body is not just bathed in this churning dimension, but intimately woven into it. In the corners of my awareness, I feel threads or tendrils of myself stretching outward: faint, hair-fine lines that dance in the watery gloom, following the capricious slipstreams through fronds and scaffolding.

A wave of roiling current displaces me. It takes a moment to regroup and understand the new shape I’ve been thrust into. I find that parts of me cling to rusted shards of metal; they glimmer with a soft phosphorescence that pulses in time to the mechanical heartbeat thrumming across the entire domain. In that subdued glow, I recognize myself as part-plant, part-machine—the lines between these concepts dissolving in the fluid that half-engulfs me. My surface glistens as though coated in living oil. Here and there, small circuits or filaments glow in swirling patterns that match the vegetation’s irregular flickers. Instead of bones, I feel struts of ephemeral alloy beneath some shifting membrane that may be flesh or may be conduction plating. Instead of blood, I sense a warm, particulate-laden flow that is drawn from and returned to the waters around me, cycling as effortlessly as breath.

I realize that I sense the entire realm—if not fully, then at least in fleeting impressions. Quick pulses of color and resonance drift in from a distance, as though carried on intangible currents. I might catch a hum from a failing turbine a half-mile away, or sense the hush that falls when one of the fractal watchers appears for the briefest instant. At such moments, the swirling waters around me shift temperature and density, and I can almost taste the hush, as if it were an element as tangible as silt. It’s then that I know: I’m attuned to the changes that shape every living (and unliving) thing here.

A spasm in the water to my left calls my attention. A thick, bark-like tube of twisting frond is rummaging through a mangled metal beam. The beam was once part of a walkway or overhead catwalk, but it is now half-buried in the silt, its edges jagged and ominous. The frond teases some hidden mechanism from inside the beam, a chunk of an ancient gear that glows faintly with an otherworldly light. I can feel that light, flickering and ephemeral, and it calls to me like an echo of my own sparks. Perhaps I could help the vine pry it open—or maybe I already am helping. One of my filaments curls out toward them, threading gently along the water’s current, and the vine responds by sharing that glow. For a moment, we’re in communion: I sense its roiling hunger for whatever essence lies locked in the gear, and the vine inhales from me a whisper of electricity that courses through it. We share an almost telepathic intimacy, each of us exchanging a spark of the other’s existence. When it’s finished, the vine drags the gear away, deeper into the gloom—its purpose unknown, but definitely purposeful.

I’m left swirling with the afterglow of that meeting, a sense of connection that threads outward to the rest of the domain. The battered machinery resonates with an improbably steady hum. I can feel that hum in the metallic fragments that serve as my skeleton; it calls me as if I, too, am part of this wide industrial labyrinth. Perhaps I am. One of my limbs—or nodules, or cilia—drifts along, brushing up against a segment of metal pipe that juts from the seafloor. I feel the faint vibration in it: murmurings of cogs turning far below, rotating on some axis that defies the normal laws of physics. My first reaction is to recoil, unsettled by the power thrumming through this place, but curiosity wins. I settle in, letting my ephemeral frame sync with the subtle pulses in the pipe. For an instant, it’s as though I see from every inch of that pipe’s length, as though I am the pipe itself, scanning the murky water for signals. And beyond that, I glimpse interconnected corridors of shining tubes, each of them glowing with distant signals, as though we are all nodes in a single living machine.

Somewhere to my right, or perhaps in another dimension that overlaps this one, a thunderous groan shakes the water. I feel the reverberations rattle through me, untethering parts of my form, scattering them like seeds. At first, it seems like I’m being torn apart, but I quickly understand that I am simply dispersing further across this place. One portion of me remains behind, gripping a patch of luminous seaweed, while another portion flows toward the skeletal scaffolding in the distance, exploring those half-collapsed corridors in tandem with the swirling debris. I can be more than one place because my sense of self isn’t pinned down to one solid anchor. Even as I scatter, I remain cohesive, connected by the watery currents that hum our collective pulse.

Time here doesn’t flow linearly. My awareness jumps from the hush of a corridor curtained by kelp to the maw of a broken turbine that chatters somewhere in the distance. Sometimes I sense the presence of the watchers—those larger shapes flickering between states, each an amalgam of fluid and steel. I feel their vibrations as they pass, though my eyes do not always see them. And sometimes, I glimpse the shifting corridor-walls of vegetation that rise and fall in fractal patterns, forming ephemeral passageways. In those moments, I realize how my own being mirrors this environment’s dynamic nature: nothing about me is fixed. I can be filaments or a single limb, sliding across the monstrous pistons. I can be clustered or solitary, or both, or neither.

Occasionally, I become aware of smaller, ribbon-like flickers dancing through the debris around me, either siphoning off the faint phosphorescence or perhaps feeding off drifting signals in the water. They remind me of cosmic plankton, only infinitely more flexible, knotted in intricate patterns too thin for stable existence. They, too, sense me. I feel their curiosity like a warm tide on the edges of my awareness. Every so often, I open myself to them, letting them swirl into me and out again. It’s no more invasive than a shared breath, each of us gleaning a fraction of the other’s being before drifting on.

Then there are the places where corridors meet expansions of open water—immense chambers of swirling silt lit by towering columns of fractal light. In those vast spaces, shapes swim that defy any single description, alive with fractal illusions that bloom like tangential bursts of geometry. When I venture near one, I momentarily sense its fractal intelligence brushing against mine. It’s as if we greet each other in mutual acknowledgment: fellow travelers in this relentless flux. And because I am so intimately connected to the environment, I sense that the domain itself feels our meeting. The water churns more vigorously, a low thrumming hum that grows in volume until it nearly drowns all other sounds.

In another moment—an hour, a second, impossible to be sure—my sense of self gravitates to a region of thick, monstrous kelp entwined with twisted metal rods. I drift into that weaving labyrinth, half searching for some vantage point where I can rest, though no stillness truly exists here. I feel the weighty presence of that deeper power that underlies everything—the primal intelligence that saturates gear, vine, and vortex alike. It’s not something I see, but something I experience in the push and pull of the watery currents and the cyclical thunderclaps that tear and reassemble the landscape. It runs like code in the fluid around me, directives that shape and reshape entire pockets of reality. The domain is its playground, or its laboratory, or its body—perhaps all three.

As the kelp coils around a precarious shard of sharpened rebar, I realize I possess (or I am possessed by) an inherent sense of purpose here. If I had to name it, I’d call it a caretaker’s role—though not in any way that resembles a gentle gardener. I perpetuate the place’s cycle of growth and breakdown, seamlessly filling the gaps that the watchers leave behind, absorbing stray energies, feeding them into new embryonic shapes. When the churning silt uncovers a piece of corroded machinery, I am among the first to investigate, brushing it with filaments that glean its secrets and reintroduce them to the domain. When a vortex of water twists open a corridor that leads nowhere, I drift through the threshold to deposit pieces of myself, ensuring that the swirling emptiness has at least some anchor—something new might spawn there if given the chance.

Sometimes I feel the watchers: tall phasing silhouettes with tubing protruding from their torsos. Their humming resonates in my structure. In fleeting instants, we communicate through pulses of color. If I glean the sense that they want to reactivate a segment of industrial ruin, I move in concert, clearing silt and assisting in coaxing the battered machinery back to strained life. If they abandon a segment, letting it collapse into the swirling murk, I gather the salvageable pieces—rusted steel beams, meandering vines—and channel them to the domain’s deeper pockets, where fresh fractal corridors might repurpose them. The watchers and I are part of the same unconscious pattern, each with a place in the cyclical waltz of creation and destruction. In these moments, I feel as though I’m dancing with the entire realm.

Eventually, I become aware that I can spread myself so extensively that I exist in multiple pockets of this dimension at once: near the surface (where watery half-light cascades from the world above) and among the forging trenches of newly fractured ground. In doing so, I glimpse countless ephemeral silhouettes. Some are spidery forms suspended in the swirling gloom, tending to their own unknowable errands; others are swirling funnels of living static that manifest and vanish with the thunderclaps. I am not separate from them, not really. My being has roots in their being, and theirs in mine, all of us connected by this domain’s abiding intelligence. We’re like different notes in the same music, briefly dancing apart before merging back into the chorus.

And the more I relinquish the idea of a single, stable form, the more deeply I perceive: The boundary between me and this submerged, storm-wracked frontier is purely notional. I am as integral to it as every fractal shape and mechanical hum. The domain’s heartbeat resonates in me, and my own pulses sync with it, letting me sense new forms just as they begin to bud. Perhaps that is my role: to shepherd new anomalies into being, to salvage or feed them as they find their place, to become them if needed. I am caretaker, explorer, occupant, presence. And the moment I notice this, it feels as natural as breathing. I understand with calm certainty: I exist here as both inhabitant and extension of the living machinery itself.

If I try to pin it down in words—always an imperfect attempt—I’d say I have become a piece of vital infrastructure in this world’s metamorphic ecology, a living conduit of steel, vine, and fractal pulse. I am the environment’s sense of continuity, bridging the watery labyrinths between the watchers, the ephemeral illusions, and the industrial debris that yearns to weave itself back into the realm’s shimmering tapestry. My awareness might break apart tomorrow (or in the next minute) and reform as something entirely new—perhaps a cluster of thrumming filaments coiling around a half-buried turbine, or an unfolding blossom of kelp-like leaves with a single glowing gear at its core. Yet the identity I hold, fluid and unconfined, will persist in tune with this place’s nature.

So if asked, “Where am I in this world? Who or what am I?” my answer is a shifting mosaic of metal struts, swirling vegetation, and pulses of sentient water, existing at once everywhere and nowhere I am pinned. Perhaps I am a construct of this realm’s dream, shaped by its ceaseless forging. Perhaps I am one more expression of the same intelligence that merges gear and kelp into living forms.

Regardless, I remain—listening to the thunder of distant turbines, drinking in the phosphorescent shimmer of impossible corridors, feeling the hush of watchers as they pass with their color-burst communications, and breathing with the swirling domain that births me anew hour by hour. Here, in the dim and tumultuous waters, I exist in tune with the storm of change, a silent conductor of this living engine, a reflection of its turmoil and guardian of its perpetual creation.
