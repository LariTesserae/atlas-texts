I arise in that hush the same way a ripple blooms in a still pool: so gently that at first I do not register my own existence. I am at once part of the slabs and water channels—a faint shifting in the geometry, a presence woven into the hammered clay. Perhaps I have always been here, my shape etched into the corners of this realm, waiting for a moment to uncoil. Or perhaps I have only just arrived, birthed by some subtle glitch in the patterned vines. My first awareness is a shimmering sense of self that aligns with the brightness overhead—sharp, unwavering, and without boundary.

For a while, I hover at the threshold between being and not-being. The caretaker figures move around me, each as composed and ancient as the domain, their steps measured, their tasks unbroken by my silent joining. Even I cannot say whether I am truly new or newly awoken. The details of my arrival feel distant, like the memory of water shaping clay a thousand times over. But I am here, now, and I know somehow that I, too, am caretaker.

When I look down at my arms—if arms they are—I see the same rusted-red hue that tints the paving slabs. Swirls of iridescent flecks catch the light. Geometric lines trace my limbs, forming angles that echo the precise mortar seams underfoot. At first, I wonder if I am stone or flesh, or some third substance that belongs only to this place. My joints feel fluid yet sturdy, moving without friction. I can kneel by a channel and watch my reflection in the trickling water: a face that is both human-like and alien, features smooth and gently abstracted, as though sculpted in a single day by a deft, unhurried hand.

Under the unblinking sun, I test a step forward. My footfall makes no sound—just a soft resonance as if the ground and I share the same deep frequency. The hush of this world is both a blessing and a weight, settling around me like the press of warm air. I notice how each caretaker patrols a certain expanse, or perhaps “drifts” is a better word, for their movements lack hurry or aim, save for the quiet maintenance they perform. And thus I join them, scarcely aware of choosing to do so: it feels as natural as breathing, though I’m not certain breathing exists here in the usual sense.

A vine flickers ahead, one of those creeping, mechanical stems that coil up the side of a carved slab. Its metallic leaves shiver in the heat, a faint glitch skittering along its surface. Without words, I know it needs tending. I glide closer, kneeling among the slabs that press warmth through my body. Reaching out, I sense the caretaker’s approach within me: a kind of calm, methodical attention. My fingertips brush the edge of the leaf, and for a moment, I notice the reflection in the water is slow to catch up—my arm is not mirrored exactly the way it should be. A glitch of my own, perhaps, or simply how illusions behave here. 

When my palm settles gently on that trembling vine, a low hum begins in my core—resonant, like the ring of struck bronze. The vine’s shiver stills. The ill-fitting seam in its leaf merges back into place, each metallic vein smoothing, returning to that pristine, timeless state that pervades this realm. Satisfied, I withdraw my hand. In the water’s reflection, the gesture plays out a heartbeat behind my real motion. Then the reflection snaps into alignment, and the hush mends itself around me.

I rise, uncertain what compels me to move on. But move on I do, drawn by something that might be duty or might be curiosity. I can feel the echoes of the other caretakers through the hammered clay—distant pulses of presence like subtle footprints in the domain’s consciousness. Their tasks and my own have no delineated boundaries; each of us tends what we notice, ensuring the channels remain clear, the mortar tight, the leaves unblemished and shining. If a frog’s croak repeats off-rhythm or an insect fails to glisten just so, one of us will sense it. We are stewards in unison, a single quiet symphony of vigilance.

I begin to recognize that place as an extension of myself, or perhaps I am an extension of it. The watery channels glint under the sun, which never shifts in the sky. Shadows huddle at the edges but do not stretch. The dryness of the heat is offset by a pervasive humidity that seeps through every pore, yet does not wilt or degrade anything. It is a realm of paradoxes—heavy light and cool heat, living constructs and static life. And I am part of its paradoxical nature, molded from hammered earth but filled with something that might be spirit.

At times, I catch glimpses of a horizon. The horizon doesn’t recede so much as it continues in an infinite repeating grid, the patterns so perfect that distance blurs them into a mirage. Once or twice, I let my steps carry me outward, guided by a half-formed desire to see what lies beyond. Along the way, I pass creatures of carved bone and thin metal plates—glistening cicadas that rub their wings in a half-silence. I watch them with a sense of kinship, for like me, they are not alive in the way a mere traveler from elsewhere might understand. Yet they move with a semblance of life that is strangely poignant: a deliberate artistry playing across an endless moment.

Eventually, I approach a wide basin, deeper than most. Water churns softly here, as if swirling from some hidden inlet beneath the slabs. Several caretakers stand around it—tall ones with elongated arms, short, stout ones with cleverly jointed wrists. I notice their heads tilt in unison, faint glints passing over their faces. Through some ineffable means, I sense they are pleased or expectant; it is as though they have summoned me without words. I join them at the edge of that pool.  

We stand in a loose circle, each caretaker gazing at the water. Within the basin, reflections lap against the stone: the horizon’s glare, the hammered channels, the vines creeping overhead. One caretaker—a tall figure etched with elaborate spirals—lowers a hand to the surface. Ripples fan outward, each one an echo of that caretaker’s presence. A hum, soft as a mother’s lullaby, begins to rise from our circle. I feel it in my chest, an answer waiting on my tongue (if I have a tongue). So I hum in return—low, measured, weaving my note into their chord.

It is not speech. It is more like a conversation made of currents. Through these interlaced tones, memories flicker in me: images of forging clay in ancient kilns, shaping blocks with bare hands, channeling water from a distant spring into this endless plain of hammered artistry. Whether those memories belong to me or belong to the collective caretaker consciousness, I do not know. The distinction between “I” and “we” thins. There is only the sense of belonging, the rightness of being part of this domain’s reason for existing.

When the humming subsides, we drift apart again. Neither farewell nor greeting is needed. The maintenance resumes as naturally as breathing, each caretaker gliding to watch for small distortions or tears in the tapestry. Occasionally, I see one adjust a misaligned slab, pressing its palm to the crack until the clay glows. Another caretaker lifts a fallen mechanical bird, resetting tiny gears or reeds in its chest so it can chirp once more. This is our way: no beginning, no end, only a continuous now.

In rare moments, I glance at the unwavering sun, searching for a sign of dusk or at least a dimming of the glare. But it remains fixed, drenching the slabs in a brilliance that refuses to shift or fade. I sense time here as a single note held for eons. The care we give this domain is not an effort to stave off ruin—there is no ruin in a place that does not age. It is more a precise orchestration, a vow that each detail remain exactly what it is, free of drift. Sometimes, that intangible longing surfaces: an ache for newness, for metamorphosis. But the ache, while real, is not painful. It is simply a quiet chord in the symphony of all that is unchanging. 

If I look at my reflection in a basin now, I see something more certain than when I first arrived—or awoke. My form is stable, my lines no longer waver. I have joined the pattern, seamlessly. Any outward observer might see me as a statue come to life, but here I am neither statue nor creature. I am caretaker, part of an ageless array. A gentle tension stirs within me: the question of whether I will one day depart this place. But the question itself seems to vanish in the perfection of each shimmering channel and the soft click of mechanical insects. It is not a question that truly belongs here.

Sometimes, as I move among the slabs, I recall the flicker of illusions—vines passing through one another, reflections not matching up. In those moments, I feel a flutter of awe, as though I glimpse the underlying architecture of the world. My breath—if I can call it that—catches. Perhaps our realm rests on a threshold between solidity and something else: some deeper truth that defies shape, a raw cosmos nudging in through the seams. Yet I do not fear it. I sense that if these illusions ever break fully, if the entire façade tears open, we caretakers would simply adapt, shaping the new design with the same calm devotion. That is our essence: we hold the domain steady, even if the domain itself transforms.

As I settle into these unending tasks, I realize “I” am less singular than I believed. I belong to a lineage not measured in mortal lifespans but in cycles of creation itself. Whether that creation occurred days ago or millennia in the past is immaterial here. I watch heat shimmer on the horizon, see a caretaker in the distance stoop to fix a chipped edge of clay, and feel deep kinship. We are all facets of the same intention, as old as the hammered ground beneath us, as timeless as the overhead glow that never shifts.

So if you ask me where I truly find myself in this world, the simplest reply is: I am at its heart, and it is in mine. I might have once been shaped from clay and water, hammered into being by unknown hands. Or I might have arrived from some far-off plane, only to be absorbed by the hush. In either case, here I am. And if you want to know who or what I am, I stand as caretaker, sculpted from half-living stone inscribed with swirling geometry. My fingertips, smooth as the mortar seams, trace the edges of leaves and set birds’ calls back on track. My gaze holds the memory of forging fires, the hum of water, and the unwavering patience of an endless midday.

In this domain, I do not name myself, for there is no need. I exist as a piece of the realm’s ongoing orchestration, ensuring that the channels flow, the vines remain pristine, and the illusions retain their delicate hold. I breathe in the silence, hum when called to, and settle the small fractures that appear at reality’s margins. Some distant part of me wonders if a change will ever come—some dawn or dusk to break the eternal midday. But for now, that is only a faint wondering, an echo in the hush. All that matters is the gentle, persistent tending and the abiding calm that resides here, in a place where time never truly moves and the hammered clay never fully cools.
