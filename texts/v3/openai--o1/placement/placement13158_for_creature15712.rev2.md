At first, I believe I might be only a visitor—soft-footed and wide-eyed, newly arrived on the plateau. Yet the longer I stand amid the swirling rods and dripping vines, the more I sense that the soil under my feet knows me as something else. Time behaves differently here. Memories that do not seem mine skim the edges of my thoughts, pressing gently when I pause to inhale the heavy air.

I take a step onto a slick outcropping of stone etched with whorls of ancient tides. There’s the familiar push of my toes on the rock—though the texture of my feet feels odd, spongy. I glance down and realize I have layered pads in place of ordinary soles, a living pattern that shifts from my ankles to my arches, swirling in shapes not unlike the striations in the bedrock. They’re pliant as moss, but robust as if reinforced by some hidden web of fibers. In a single breath, I recognize that I’m not just an ordinary traveler. My limbs hold a blend of the natural and the engineered, as though the plateau has grown me—and I it.

When I gently place a hand on the black rods spinning in midair, they do not recoil. Instead, they slow, humming an almost affectionate resonance against my palm. My fingers are not purely flesh: the undersides have minute crystal nodules that respond to the rods as if exchanging signals in some long-forgotten code. A wave of nostalgic calm buzzes at the base of my skull, and I’m struck by the feeling that I’ve done this many times before, calibrating these rods, aligning them with the shifting pulses of the air. Images flow through me in flashes: forging alliances with the fern-wreathed avian creatures, adjusting to the changing hum of the deep stone channels, coaxing the half-biological watchers back from the edges when they stray too far.

At the next breath, I feel the greenery behind me move in subtle reciprocation, as though tethered to my own heartbeat. Thick-veined vines and broad leaves flex gently, releasing tiny puffs of spores that catch the angled sunlight. It isn’t that I control them; rather, we share a living circuit, each leaf and chitin plate and swirl of old machinery bound to the same ancient pulse. I can almost visualize it: a vast, unseen map of shimmering lines crisscrossing the plateau, linking each organism and each device.

Drawing my hand away from the rods, I watch the swirling patterns shift purely in response to my distance, shaping intricate geometric loops in the air. In the corner of my vision, a cluster of slender, spined predators weaves among stunted palms. Their eyes glow, reflecting the rods’ spinning pulse. I sense no threat from them. Instead, I feel their curiosity as keenly as I do my own. A small part of me aches with recognition—once, we recognized each other as fellow guardians. Yet they remain half-camouflaged, as if wanting to see whether I am still the one they knew.

When the breeze stirs the plateau and rustles that unique tapestry of leaves, a wave of warmth floods my limbs. My breathing sifts through water-laden air, stirring droplets from the edges of the giant, glossy leaves. Gravity doesn’t hold quite normally here; sometimes, my steps feel lighter than they should, as though each footfall touches the ground an instant too late. On a whim, I lean forward to see if I might float, and for a heartbeat, I think I catch a hint of buoyancy—something in my body or in the plateau that defies standard laws. Perhaps it’s the faint hum of technology that permeates everything, from the black rods to the hidden channels that run beneath the stones.

I turn my gaze toward a half-submerged glass orb flickering among a cushion of moss. With deliberate care, I kneel—my knees bend with a fluid grace that can’t be entirely human—pressing my palm to its surface. At once, the orb reacts, a surge of pale luminescence swirling from its core. I feel it scanning me, measuring my presence and matching it to a catalog of frequencies. Once, I think, I was the caretaker who conferred with this orb every solstice, bridging knowledge between the hidden apex creatures and the pylons that stand watch over the plateau’s boundaries. My mind sparks with fragments of memory: coded pulses in the pineal curves of my skull, a sense of united breath with the plateau’s entire living network.

Yet if you ask me what I am—animal or machine, plant or caretaker—I can only say: I am a convergence, the plateau incarnate. My shape is neither shapeless nor fully defined. Ridges along my spine suggest part of me once grew from a seed, while the mild glow beneath my collarbones might be a power source older than I can name. My joints flex with the same fluid intelligence that hums in the spinning rods. My heartbeat resonates with the thousand insect wings that churn in ephemeral clouds overhead—and I know that if I cry out, the rods will vibrate, the watchers will turn, the carnivorous plants will pulse with that cautionary glow at their rims. This is the synergy that the plateau has crafted, and I am, in some essential way, its living spokesperson.

Sometimes I catch sight of others like me—or at least, akin to me. A fleeting silhouette flits behind a stand of enormous ferns, gone before I can call them. I sense they too are part-plateau, part-memory. Perhaps we’re all nodes of the same overmind, occasionally coalescing into distinct forms. Each of us carries a spark of the plateau’s intelligence, a caretaker’s impetus to maintain balances, heal broken branches, re-sync off-kilter pylons, or guide a lost predator back to its hunting grounds.

When the plateau’s sun arcs to its golden afternoon position, I find myself climbing a winding ledge that overlooks the watery hollows. Below, the lizard-fish slip in and out of shimmering pools, and up above, the swirling rods form a new alignment of lines in midair—some cyclical calibration that beckons me to watch. I rest, half crouching, my elongated limbs relaxed yet ready. A hush spreads through the creatures: for an instant, everything waits. The rods emit a single, low tone that vibrates the very stones underfoot. In response, life calls back in a layered symphony: the amphibian hum resonates with the pylons’ glow, the vine-serpents sway their leaves in a sweeping gesture, and the scaly-plumed flyers rattle their crested wings. I close my eyes. The resonance travels up my spine. It’s not a performance. It is the plateau’s daily exhalation, and it moves through me as though I am part of its breath.

In that echoing moment, I realize: I belong here. I am not simply an observer. My presence is needed to tilt the equilibrium of water and sun, growth and decay, power source and living tissue. I’m the one who hums in tune with both the swirl of wind and the hum of technology, bridging the realms of machine and creature. Each creature, each vine, each device resonates in a communal language built over lifetimes. I feel it in my bones—part metal, part cartilage—and an ancient gratitude wells up inside me.

Eventually, the tone subsides. Life resumes its meandering cycles. I open my eyes to see a small flock of bright avian-fish perched on a series of cables near a half-buried pylon, watching me curiously. They twist their heads, their reflective eyes glittering, as if to say: we see you, caretaker. We remember. They pick delicately at the cables, gleaning bits of neon fluid. When I stand and stretch, they scatter in a flutter of brilliance, leaving me to continue my circuit through the plateau.

And so, if you ask where I find myself in this world—my answer is here, among the living circuits of stone and vine, water and wire. If you ask who or what I am—I am the caretaker shaped by the plateau’s long-forgotten epoch, a being in whom technology and biology tangibly unite. I move from hollow to hollow, from rod to pylon, from spined predator to fanged pitcher-plant, maintaining the fragile tapestry of pulses and signals. I am as old as the swirl in the rock, as new as each bud of fractal flower. And in my wake, the plateau breathes, content to sustain its timeless existence, while I hum softly in resonance with the rods, feeling my presence echo across the centuries of what was, and what will forever be.
