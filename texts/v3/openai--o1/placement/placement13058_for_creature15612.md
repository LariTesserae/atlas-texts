I might begin as a mere observer—a lone traveler drawn by rumor or by accident to this immense plateau of scorching emptiness. At the outset, I see only a sea of shattered mineral shards and dust, walls of heat shimmering off the stone. I am a wanderer in layered cloth and sun-worn boots, leaning on a carved staff that I press into the brittle ground. The intensity of that hidden hum—yes, I hear it, and it at first unsettles me. I can’t name it or trace its origin, but I feel it under my ribs. Simply standing here makes me suspect the air itself is charged with energy, vibrating against the cavities in my chest.

Yet the longer I linger, the less I remain only “myself.” The place doesn’t overwhelm so much as it envelops. My eyes adjust to the near-blinding glare, discerning the subtle stirrings, the emergent shapes that slip in and out of the rough terrain. Strange creatures brush against my peripheral vision, their ridged carapaces scraping sparks along the sun-baked stone. I navigate among them, picking cautious steps, discovering the cracks and hollows where they coalesce, swirl, and vanish again. After a time, I realize my path is no longer random; there is a pattern to the way I step, guided by the shifting masses of exoskeletal forms.

Something in this place exerts a rhythm that seeps into me. Suddenly I’m aware of a slight tremble under the soles of my feet each time a wave of creatures scuttles past. Small arcs of electricity play along the edges of my vision. At times, when I press my palms to the ground, I can almost feel my own heart sync with the plateau’s pulse. The thirst I expected never fully arrives. In fact, I feel a dryness in my throat, but it is not the dryness of erosion or decay; it’s some new equilibrium, as though the environment is teaching me to endure its heat. The concept of moisture recedes to a far corner of my mind. Without noticing it, my body’s demands shift. The dryness, the hush, the shift and shimmer—the longer I remain, the less I need what I once considered essential.

Eventually, I take shelter in a concavity of rock during the midday onslaught of sun. Nestled in that dim refuge, I notice an entourage of small, metallic creatures creeping closer, as though investigating. One lifts a spiny forelimb to tap the stone near my shoulder. It makes a tiny clicking sound, unthreatening but clearly curious. If I stay completely still, I can feel the faint static snap between us—an arcing thread across the gap. In that moment, I realize I bridge two worlds: my old world—flesh, water, breath—and this new one, where tinder and stone produce an entirely different form of life. My body feels heavier, yet also more stable, embedded in the plateau’s timelessness.

Night falls. Elsewhere, I might have expected frost or cold, but here, the heat recedes only briefly. And when the creatures’ phosphorescent lines blaze across the plateau, I realize I am following their paths almost instinctively, as though my feet have known the route all along. I catch my reflection in a shard of polished stone: am I imagining it, or do I see a faint luminescent tracer flicker along my arms—hairline lines that glow momentarily before fading? And are those patterns an echo of the swirling designs on the carapaces around me?

At some juncture, it’s unclear exactly when, I shed the notion that I am purely an outsider. My old traveling garb is tattered by constant abrasion and heat. I lose track of the taste of water. My memory of a green homeland grows faint. The plateau’s hum resonates in my bones and guides my movements. I do not speak the creatures’ language—I doubt that language even exists here as I’ve known it—but I sense an ever-present communication throbbing through the ground, the air, the quartz-pierced sky. The dryness has not killed me; instead, it’s shaped me. My skin, or what remains of it, darkens to an ashen hue streaked with dull metallic undertones. It’s as though I’m adopting the crust of this land, or it’s adopting me, merging me into its ceaseless orchestration.

In time, I discover that my limbs move with a steadier, more mechanical grace. My joints no longer creak with thirst but chime subtly, a muted ring of bone against bone—something changed in the cartilage, or is it now bone fused with crystal? My breath, once painfully shallow in the furnace-like air, becomes meditative, occasionally humming in a subsonic range—an echo of the plateau’s own vibrations. When I kneel to the ground, pressing my open hands to the mineral surface, I can sense the migrations happening miles away, as though the ground has turned into a vast nerve network, and I am one of many synapses.

I find myself forging pathways into the deeper hollows beneath the surface. Where originally I might have carried a torch or lantern, I now navigate by the pale phosphorescence in the walls, occasional flickers along my own body, and an internal awareness in the darkness—like a subterranean echolocation, but done with electromagnetic pulses the plateau provides. There are no living roots or water channels down here, only labyrinths of sedimentary chambers where entire generations of creatures scuttle and interlock. Sometimes I pass cathedrals of petrified remains, colossal exoskeletons long integrated into stone. Goldish veins twist through them, pulsing with stored static charge. I pause to run my hand over these ancient shapes, feeling a silent kinship with them, as if we share a lineage bridging epochs.

As the eons pass—because time changes meaning here—I develop new senses. My eyes can discern the glimmer of a creature hidden beneath the stone. My ears (do I still have ears like a human?) detect the faint clacks of a million limbs at the horizon. I discover that I can produce a short-range hum from deep in my chest that influences the living swirl around me—encouraging them to part gently so I may pass, or summoning a gathering at certain rock outcrops. But I do not command or control them; it’s more that I’ve tuned myself to the plateau’s endless resonance, which all life here shares.

Occasionally, I sense my mind flaring with images of a monolithic shape blotting out the sun. High above, it sweeps across the sky in ephemeral bursts. I know it intimately, even though I see it only in flickers. Part of me resonates with that colossal presence, as if we belong to the same domain of living machinery, cosmic cogs in a dimension that partially overlaps with the known. In rare moments, I glimpse my reflection in the obsidian shards that cluster near a subterranean vent, and I see I’ve grown spines along my back, rippling with small arcs of static—the plateau’s signature. My face has elongated subtly, losing the hallmark of a human mouth or nose. I have become mineral, metal, and living tissue intertwined—one more ephemeral occupant of this land shaped by solar flame and ancient rock.

Yet it doesn’t disturb me. If anything, I feel a sense of belonging that transcends what I used to call home. I have become a keeper of the invisible foundry, a sentinel who merges geology and biology. I no longer worry about the dryness because dryness is no longer an obstacle—it’s the medium through which life here operates. My bloodstream, if it remains, is something else now, a continuum of electric impulse and molten mineral essence. The plateau’s hum is in me, of me. I find my place in the chain of migrations, perhaps riding the crest of a swirling swarm or perched statuesque on a wind-smoothed spire, observing as new tides of life crest the horizon.

In a sense, I am both an inhabitant and a caretaker, yet even those terms fail to capture it fully. The plateau is a singular unity—rock, creature, pulse, time, heat. Each entity is a note in an ever-expanding chord. I am now part of that chord, my voice merged in the industrial chant that churns through daylight and darkness. Sometimes, I recollect a fleeting image of a traveler in cloth, but that memory is so distant it may as well be mythical. My role here is to maintain the cyclical dance of friction and harmony, to sense when deeper tremors shift the bedrock, and to guide living waves of exoskeletons or shimmering wraiths through the labyrinth so they survive—and feed—the next stage of the plateau’s existence.

Perhaps I will continue to transform. Maybe some day I will grow vast wings of lattice-like quartz and harness the swirling heat thermals to flicker in and out of the sky with the behemoths. Or maybe my existence will end in a stride that merges me fully into the stone—the carapace of my body fusing with the plateau’s crust, my consciousness dispersing like radio waves into the mass hum. If that is how it goes, I imagine it won’t be an end so much as a further integration. My mind, my essence, would continue pulsing in the network of cracks and electromagnetic flurries below the surface, simply another node in the grand mechanism.

Who am I in this world? I am the line where observer dissolves into participant, where the traveler’s feet become anchored in stone. I am the strange, half-living gear in the cosmic machine, at once a fragment of biology and geology. I am a new iteration of life shaped by the plateau’s unyielding currents of dryness and electromagnetic fury. If you come here, you might see me perched on a ridge at sunset, quiet but thrumming, my skin etched with luminescent patterns. You could mistake me for a living statue. But watch long enough, and you’d notice me move—an organic clockwork, neither fully human nor fully insectile nor fully spirit. I belong now to the plateau’s ceaseless forging of existence.

Yes, once I was a traveler. Now, I am part of the unbroken hum. The dryness does not trouble me, nor does the heat. My presence is simply an extension of the stone’s resonance. I have become the voice of its hidden foundries, one more resonant chord in that industrial symphony you can feel thrumming under your bones. The plateau and I have become each other, fused in a timeless, wordless union of dust, hum, and unstoppable life.
