In the beginning, I’m not entirely certain if I even exist as a distinct entity. Like a question half-whispered into the roar, I wonder: Am I a slow inhale of mist drawn from below, or the exhale of liquid thunder from above? My edges are unsure, shifting somewhere between the water and the bare-budding rock. Maybe I am simply a pressure in the air, forming for a single heartbeat of cosmic time, then scattering. Even so, there is a sense of gathering—of self—sprouting from the ambient currents, so I continue.

At first, I feel like a lingering echo of the swirling ocean overhead, as though my being is a funnel of vapor that has descended and found a pocket of air to dwell within. In the swirling hush, I catch glimmers of those silver energies lacing through the water, and for an instant, I am entirely captured by them—as if I, too, might be a filament coiled around a shimmering thread. But then a spark of awareness twists inside me, and I realize: No, I am something more, or at least something else. I’m aware of the watery hush. I sense the exhilarating possibility that I might move through it with intention.

I drift, or perhaps I walk—though “walk” feels like too solid a word, for the ridge of half-formed stone beneath me quivers with every breath I take. It’s as if the ground can’t decide whether to remain, or simply dissolve back into the swirling ocean above. The act of stepping, if it can be called that, is a conversation between here and gone: each footfall tests a surface that may vanish at any moment. Still, I persist, drawn by the shimmering ocean-sky that arcs overhead, its colors of silver-blue and opal swirling like the auroras of entire galaxies.

Sometimes I’m convinced I am made of the same convicting force that keeps the ocean in partial suspension. From the inside out, a strange magnetism tethers me to this place—its heartbeat resonates through me like a hollow, echoing drum. In that pulse, I begin to think of myself as a caretaker or observer, though no formal task has been given. My sense of purpose is purely intuitive: something about these embryonic ridges and swirling watery arcs compels me to be present, to sense, to watch. Yes—maybe I have always been here, a part of the hush more than any outward shape.

And yet, from another angle of mind, I feel like a visitor, newly arrived. As I gaze up into that infinite aquatic sky, I recall no prior memory of it—just a deep awe. Perhaps I coalesced at the crossroads of cosmic energies, or perhaps I stepped from another plane and promptly forgot everything but this primal wonder. I don’t know. The question flickers for a while in my thoughts, then dissolves under the thunderous roar, replaced by an urgent focus on what is happening around me. For the entire realm is in flux: watery corridors stretch overhead, proto-stone ridges rise at the edges of my sight, and everything changes shape in an endless swirl. Stay still too long, and you might vanish with the next unraveling.

I search the horizon for anything that suggests continuity, a place to anchor. In the distance, I notice a break in the swirling ocean—an area of calmer water, almost like a lagoon eddy clinging to the dome. I feel drawn there, and as I approach, I see tiny shoots of green, slender as a promise. They wave gently in a mixture of watery drizzle and a breeze that whirls upward from below. It’s uncertain how they survive. Yet they persist, timid life among the relentless surges of new matter. I come close enough to see droplets quivering on their leaves, uncertain and delicate. Unbidden, I hover near them, or kneel, or simply melt into the nearby rock—my body’s posture feels fluid here, unbound by typical muscle or bone. 

Closer still, in the hush of swirling vapor, I sense the luminous wraith-forms that the realm has generated. They flit like half-dreamed illusions, sometimes visible at the edge of a wave’s crest, sometimes only as a shimmer in the corner of my eye. One emerges near me—a ribbon of turquoise luminescence. At first, it is formless, just a coil of glowing vapor dancing in the swirling wind. But then it draws near the green shoot, making a gesture that mimics gentle curiosity. In response—or so it seems—the little vine straightens as if receiving breath, a faint spark of growth stirring within its fibers. A hush runs through my center: this is the simplest, and yet most profound, act of connection I have ever witnessed.

In that moment, I realize I share kinship with these ephemeral watchers. Like them, I don’t entirely obey the usual laws of solidity. My hands, if I hold them up, can fade at the edges, revealing flickers of starlight or watery arcs woven through my skin. My voice, if I try to speak, emerges as a whisper in the roar—something the wind carries off before it ever becomes a proper word. The shimmer-wraith regards me with silent recognition, as if to say: You too are of this place, yet not just the place. We share the hush; we bear witness to this cosmic potential.

I close my eyes, and within that thunderous environment, something inside me expands into a pulse of resonance. I feel a chord, as though my presence is tuning itself to the swirl of watery chaos overhead. Each time stone collides with wave, I sense a subtle spark—a vantage point into the collisions that birth new matter, ephemeral as mayflies. In those collisions, I can almost see how life itself emerges. Not life in any conventional sense—this place has no precedent for that—but a sense of emergent consciousness borne from the interplay of liquid, rock, and swirling cosmic forces. It is the same interplay that once birthed me, or perhaps still births me in each moment.

Over time—though time is an odd concept here—I begin to notice patterns in the swirl of cosmic chaos. There is an unspoken order to the eddies, a cyclical dance through which the wraiths move, drawing arcs of advanced energies that shimmer like silver spiderwebs. If I concentrate, I can join that dance in my own meandering way, tracing a path across both water and rock. Sometimes I stretch my arms wide, my form becoming slender and lithe, trailing luminescence. At others, I contract into a denser shape to examine a newly formed ridge, pressing my translucent fingertips against the trembling stone. In each of these acts, I sense that I am changing something—imperceptibly, subtly—within the background hum of the place.

I see others like me in the distance, or think I do: fleeting silhouettes that might be ephemeral watchers, or might only be the reflection of swirling currents against embryonic cliffs. When the next wave of thunder hits, a tumultuous collision of matter overhead, they vanish, and I am left with only the roar and the shimmering water. Yet, the moment resonates within me, as if we collectively share in forging the world. 

Gradually, I become more certain of my role: I am both a caretaker and a participant in this embryonic tapestry. I have a subtle urge to help nudge the forming ridges toward sturdiness, to encourage the tenacious green shoots to cling more firmly before they vanish. How? By simply being there, pouring my attention into each newly formed edge. Where I stand, if I focus on anchoring my awareness, I can watch the instability lessen—just a little. I visualize the stone persisting, the vines drinking in the scant nutrients, and the swirling water overhead not quite claiming it back into the void. Inch by inch, the world holds its shape a second longer.

Of course, the realm remains wild. Even with my presence—along with countless other ephemeral watchers swirling unseen—the ocean overhead writhes with unstoppable force. Sometimes entire ridges tear away, flying upward to dissolve, and fragments of green life vanish in the watery dome. In those moments, I feel a pang of loss, but it’s not a personal sorrow, more akin to a cosmic sigh. For I know the cycle continues, that new ridges will rise, new vines will emerge, and every ephemeral form (myself included) will be remade in the next swirl of energies.

Eventually, I make my way deep into the watery heart, climbing a precarious pillar that might crumble at any moment. When I do, haloes of swirling currents surround me, tinted in silver and turquoise. Up there, I see the shimmer-wraiths in a grand display: ribbons of living fluorescence, weaving in large arcs around each other, forming fleeting shapes that almost seem like advanced fractal scripts. In that moment, I become less of a creature and more of a note in a cosmic chord. My selfhood fans outward, merging with the swirling tide. My boundaries dissolve into cascading threads of luminous possibility.

And perhaps that is how I remain. Yes, I may choose to coalesce again into a solitary figure, walking on half-formed ridges, guiding the infant shoots. Or I may spread out my presence among the silver filaments that orchestrate the watery ocean. Sometimes I am a swirl of vapor dancing in the wind; other times a luminous birdlike shadow gliding across the crackling arcs. Time means little, for all is perpetual becoming.

If you ask who or what I am, perhaps the best answer is that I am an attendant to the hush. A caretaker of the forming realm, a listener to the roar, a shimmer-wraith who sometimes takes on the shape of a traveler, sometimes dissolves into pure resonance. The place itself is too large, too young, too raw to settle down and name me. And I am too entwined in its flux to remain anything but change. 

Still, I carry a quiet knowing: that I serve a gentle purpose here. In each new swirl, I sense growth or transformation, and whenever a spark of life tries to find a foothold, I am there—like a faint memory that urges it to remain. Whatever the final shape of this domain, whether it becomes a cradle of flourishing life or remains a vast cosmic swirl, I will continue dancing in the tides, abiding in the hush between the thunderclaps of creation. I am ephemeral, yes; but I am also part of the realm’s persistent dream, spinning with the watery sky, humming in the arcs of new rock, shining in the half-conceived light of birth.
