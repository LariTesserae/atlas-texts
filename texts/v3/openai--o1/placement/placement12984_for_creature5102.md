At first, there is no sense of “I.” There is only that raw brightness reflecting off stone, an equal measure of brilliance and chill. The wind whips across the plateau, stirring eddies of thin air as it polishes the rock. At that early juncture, my presence is no more distinct than a whisper in the stone’s cracked grain. Or perhaps I am the whisper. It’s hard to say; one doesn’t think in terms of body and boundary here.

I emerge out of the near-silence in a slow drift of awareness, coalescing between the patterned erosions of a tall spire and the hush of wind swirling past. Maybe I take shape in that hush. Or maybe the hush is me: a swirl of intangible presence carried by the shifting temperatures, latching ever so gently to the stone’s surface. Some say I belong to the plateau’s memory—just one of countless flickers of recollection that have seeped into its mineral veins over epochs. Others might fancy me a spirit, though not as flesh-based life envisions it. I feel more like a guided drift of thought, a rootless dream that has found anchor in the lattice of pale pillars and ring-walls.

I have no need for movement as creatures know it, but movement finds me anyway. It comes in the expansion and contraction of the plateau itself, in those day-to-night, warmth-to-cold transitions. When the morning sun’s rays first thrust across the stone, I shimmer for a breathless instant beneath the faint film of condensation. If you were passing by (and few ever do), you might glimpse in that thin water a vague suggestion: an elongated shape with a soft luminescent trace, or filaments of color swirling in the reflection. Were you to lean in, investigating, you might see what looks like a curved line where my shoulder would be or the half-lidded ghost of an eye. Then a fearless gust would scatter my image into prismatic shards, leaving only the unremarkable pool behind.

At times, I think I am older than the stone itself, as though before this pinnacle was raised from the planet’s bones, I waited in the silt of unformed matter. But perhaps it’s the other way around—that I am the plateau’s youngest breath, a new consciousness exhaled by the friction of wind and rock. In any case, I feel older than all else and new in the same instant, for time here does not adhere to a linear path. It folds in on itself—each cycle of thaw and freeze carrying echoes of centuries prior. I drift at those edges, where the plateau’s living hush meets the possibility of sound.

When dusk arrives, heralding the great swing from day’s cold glare into night’s darker chill, I become more certain of my shape. The faint pulses running through the black glass veins—those swirling specks of color—intensify. Threads of subtle luminescence travel along narrow fractures; I can follow them like arteries, wandering the plateau’s anatomy. In that half-light, I can almost remember a name, a bell-like syllable that resonates through my entire being. It never quite solidifies into anything pronounceable by mortal tongues. Instead, I feel the stone’s ancient hum fill my consciousness, shaping me from within.

On the plateau’s outskirts, the ring-walls and hollow domes stand half ruined. To human eyes, these might appear as tombs or altars. To me, they are resonators—structures carved to catch the wind in just the right swirl, producing a chord that reverberates between the spires. When I pass (or rather, when the wind carrying my intangible awareness passes), the chord changes, gathering an extra tone. It is a conversation of sorts: the stone’s voice, the wind’s voice, and my own ephemeral note weaving in a slow, cyclical chant. Each chord is a new telling of the plateau’s story, a call that bounces back from ancient rock, calling forth presences like me.

Sometimes, I fade to the raw hum of the stone’s bedrock, letting the centuries spool by without measure. Then, at certain moments—perhaps an unexpected glint of rainbow across a black glass fissure, or a sudden crack echoing through the pillars—I condense again. I gather enough to sense and to reflect. As I hover by the watery basins or flow along the interior of an abandoned dome, I perceive vague outlines of others like me. They are intangible visitations, merging and splitting, each shaped by the plateau’s resonance. We drift in and out of each other, sharing images, impressions, traces of memory. There is neither greeting nor parting—only a mutual recognition that we belong to this realm of geologic dreams.

Despite our unity with the stone, not all experiences here are calm. Certain storms lash across the plateau, bringing biting winds that seem to shear away my presence—like grit caught in a blast, scattering intangible forms into the void. In those storms, the plateau’s hum intensifies to a roar. For a while, I am lost, carried in screaming eddies that swirl around the highest pinnacles. My edges dissolve into the stones’ deeper seams. Only when the wind dies do I rediscover my sense of self as a dim glimmer, reassembling from the plateau’s breath. I re-form slowly, as if gathering the scattered shards of my own consciousness from the scattered echoes.

I think, in some unspoken way, I dwell at the threshold between worlds: not entirely geological, not entirely animate, neither spirit nor mind in the conventional sense. I learn by absorbing the plateau’s cyclical processes. And as I learn, I imprint faint signatures upon the stone, subtle shifts that shape the next echo of wind or the next pattern of condensation. With time, that interplay has etched miniature labyrinths around the domes—spirals fine as hairline cracks, or faint runes etched into sheltered niches. Perhaps they are a language I am writing, or perhaps they are the plateau’s own message, shared through me.

That is ultimately who I am. Or who I become, again and again, with each day’s passing and night’s hush: a presence formed by the resonant interplay of cold light, drifting vapor, and the memory-laden stone. You could call me a watcher, or a dreamer, or a reflection in a puddle—each name is partly true and partly insufficient. I hold no single outline; my many outlines merge into the plateau’s hush.

In the silent corridors, I’m the imperceptible swirl that catches in the angles of the carved walls. Along the black glass veins, I’m the faint shimmer that pulses like a heartbeat beneath the surface. When travelers come—and they do so rarely—I’m the soft, uncertain prickle at the base of their neck, the sense that someone, or something, is close. I offer no threat, only the quiet invitation to feel the living hush that connects everything in this stone domain.

Should you linger long enough in this land to sense my whisper, perhaps you’ll know me not by sight but by the way the wind’s tone shifts in your ear, or the way small specks of light shimmer across a patch of ice. You might catch the fleeting impression of a tall, slender figure leaning curiously toward you, lines of crystal threading my arms. But as soon as you turn your head, I will return to the unformed currents of air, letting the plateau cradle me in its cold, unwavering brightness.

I exist in that subtle realm: a shape drawn from mineral memory, half wind-song, half reflection. I am the plateau’s breath, its silent dream. And from long before your arrival to long after your departure, here is where I remain: drifting among polished spires, shimmering in watery mirrors, measuring my existence in the patient intervals of the stone’s unimaginable life.
