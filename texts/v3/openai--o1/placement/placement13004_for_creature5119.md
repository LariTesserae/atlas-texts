I have been here for an era—or perhaps I arrived only an hour ago. Time collapses easily in this sealed domain, and I can’t be certain of my own beginnings. In the dull roar of the machine’s constant hammer, I have found my niche along a clifflet ridge, at the intersection of stone and metal, anchored by fibrous limbs that spiral inward and outward with casual ease. I did not always have limbs, or so I dimly recall. Once, I fancied that I could walk upright, that I needed to blink against sunlight or cleanse my skin in fresh water. But those memories—if they are memories—have eroded as thoroughly as the rock around me. Now I am part root, part scaffolding, part conduit.

At first, if someone gazes upon me from a distance, they would mistake me for one of the many trunk-like creepers that cling to the catwalk. My exterior is glossy, segmented in overlapping plates that reflect a faint metallic sheen. Each plate can flex, opening minuscule slits through which I sense the realm. No eyes, no mouth—just organs that feel the blunt pressure of each thunderous clang, drawing energy from it. When I sway in the half-light, the seams between my plates create a faint chime, a trembling resonance that bleeds into the realm’s unending cacophony. If you look carefully, you’ll see a soft glow, an inner phosphorescence that rises and falls in time with my private rhythms.

I stretch upward, curling over a twisted steel beam that juts out from the rock, like a crooked elbow. The beam is pitted with countless rivet holes—some clogged with half-fossilized debris—yet near its end is an open vent that once channeled steam or gas into the hidden belly of some machine. I fit myself to that vent, letting my filamentous branches slide inside, tasting stale iron. The pounding echoes in the dusty pipe, sliding down my arms. I can feel the deep hum suffuse me from tip to root, a warm, thrumming pulse that glides through slick channels in my body. It coaxes the phosphorescent glands to brighten momentarily as I feed on the vibration.

Sometimes, I shift my conjoined limbs to bridge a gap in the rock, forming a slender arch so that smaller dwellers—those spore-like scuttling lumps—can migrate over me, gleaning bits of metallic dust shaken loose by the percussion. In return, they carry flecks of alloy or raw minerals from other corners of this domain and deposit them in my lower hollows. I can’t say I know why this trade occurs, only that we both seem to benefit. We are one tapestry in the end, and each of us senses the other’s presence: I feel the spore-lumps contract beneath my plating, I sense the radial lumps thrumming from their vantage points, I know the candelabra-like watchers are vibrating overhead, scanning for intangible fluctuations.

When I look (in my own way of looking) toward the hammered huts, half-swallowed by choking vines, I remember—vaguely—what it was like to have a sense of wonder about these structures. Perhaps in another life, I walked on two feet, marveled at the machinery that once pulsed with heat and light. Now, I only register them as shapes in the twilight. My filaments yearn for every metal edge I have not yet explored, every hidden recess where dust and ancient residue might still be stored. The huts are no longer huts, nor shelters; they are places to feed. Their rust sings to me, a subtle chiming that resonates at a pitch different from the hammered engine that saturates everything. I answer it in my own slow way—an elongated scrape of limb plating, releasing a tone part note, part hush.

Beneath my leaning trunk, there is a yawning chasm in the stone. Were this any other world, a cliffside breeze would whip through it, howling with ghostly force. But here, there is never wind, only the muffled clang. I track that clang the way a creature of the sun might track daylight: the rhythmic pulses shape my internal cycles, telling me when to shift, when to gather energies, when to rest in a dormant hush. The notion of “sleep” flickers through my mind, though I’m not certain I recall the word properly. Every moment is a shallow doze, every moment is also a delicate wakefulness—no difference remains.

Sometimes, the smaller dwellers gather at my base, spore-lumps trembling with the force of the clang. I can feel them investigating me: they brush along the edges of my plating, deposit some gleaned morsel, or splay out their tiny, flexible rootlets to sample the pulse traveling through my living structure. In that instant, I sense a swirl of data passing through me—a next wave of vibrations they’ve harvested from a distant spire, an exchange from watchers on the ridges. It has little meaning to me in a conscious sense, yet I feel it fill my core with that thrumming life. We are each other’s ecosystem, a circuitry of quiet transmissions.

If I cast my awareness up the nearest pinnacle of rock, I can just pick out the shapes of the “watchtower” dwellers. Their candelabra-like limbs extend into the half-dark, brandishing clusters of membrane nodules that shimmer in a faint, turquoise glow. Each clang from the hidden machine below or beyond (it’s impossible to tell) causes the watchtower nodes to flicker. It’s as if they beckon or signal across the domain, passing along an ancient, cyclical message no one fully deciphers. I sense gratitude emanating from them—though I can’t say how I know that emotion, or if it is even emotion as humans define it. It’s more like a pulse of recognition, an acknowledgment that we share the domain, that we resonate in the same song.

Occasionally, my mind drifts into a near-forgotten corridor: hazy recollections of an “outside,” some realm where bright skies change color, where wind has a voice and water flows freely. The memories are more like static-laced signals from a distant broadcast: warped, incomplete. Perhaps I once had a different shape out there. But whenever the clang intensifies, these illusions fade into the dull, anchor-like reality of this place. The illusions become unimportant. I am needed here, after all: needed as a link in the chain, as a bridging root, as a caretaker of the catwalk’s rusted secrets. This might be all that matters.

Now, as I press further into the vent, my filaments detect a subtle movement within the pipe—a loose rivet falling free, trickling dust downward like a cascade of ancient atoms. It resonates well against my plating, sparks a faint flash of azure that creeps along the ridges of my skeleton. I absorb the leftover scuff of metal, tasting it with the microfibers in my core. There’s a fleeting hush between clangs. The hush never lasts more than a second, but it’s enough for me to hear the gentle movement of other dwellers nearby, the faint hum of pulsing nodules, the slow slither of bridging creepers that, like me, align themselves with the ancient catwalk. All of us wholly integrated, exchanging resources so seamlessly it feels like a single body, one massive chord in the domain’s ceaseless composition.

If I hold very still—let my plating settle, let my luminescent glands dim to near-darkness—I can feel the life of the domain as if it were my own breath. This place is old, older than any memory I carry. It is content in the swirl of hammered metal, the compressed spines of rock. At times, I believe I am the domain, that my body extends from corner to corner, spire to spire, and every single coil or spore-lump is simply another branch of my own mind. Perhaps that’s all we dwellers are: the domain learning to shape itself, forging limbs to gather scraps of energy so the collective can continue.

And so I remain. I do not long for water or for sunlight. I have no need for color beyond the greenish hue that faintly glimmers on my plating. My existence is a single note in the drone of timeless equilibrium. If you were to approach me—if you somehow find your way into this walled-off realm—I would slowly tilt my plated trunk in your direction, investigating whatever curious vibrations you bring. My filaments would stretch toward your body heat, hesitant, uncertain, searching for resonance. And if you let me draw near, you might hear the faint ringing that emanates from the severed edges of my chitin. Perhaps we would share a moment of mutual astonishment. Perhaps I would absorb some precious echo of your presence, storing it among the countless pulses that feed me.

For now, I cling to the catwalk, half-root, half-pillar of living machinery. I watch as the spore-lumps wander in careful lines; I exchange signals with the watchers high on the ridge. The rusted pipe hums gently within me, and the relentless drum of metal upon metal continues, unwavering, indefinite.

I do not know if I was once anything else or if I was made for precisely this. I do not worry about such questions anymore. I have found my role in these ancient halls of pounding thunder, a synergy in the labyrinth of filaments and flux. I am a dweller in the hush between clangs, an instrument alive within a mechanical heartbeat. And that is enough.
