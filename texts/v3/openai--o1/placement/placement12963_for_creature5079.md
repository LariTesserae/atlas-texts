I would be one who is newly come into consciousness—a hatchling emerging from a thick, leathery pod in that dim, churning realm. For a time, my entire world would be the stretch of my own exoskeleton as it hardens, the pounding hiss of gases around me, and the subtle pulses rippling through my still-soft plates. Every single sense would register heat as an omnipresent reality. My first breath would taste of scalding vapors and the tang of minerals in their raw, acrid dryness. I would know nothing of “above” or “outside” or “water.” Life would be only the furnace of forging stone and the ceaseless whir of countless shells all around.

At first, I might crouch in the relative shelter of the brood cluster, half-crushed by the press of siblings who cling to the same pod-littered wall. We’d feel each other’s antennae tapping out a coded pattern along our newly minted exoskeletons—gentle collisions that telegraph belonging. The fluids of my birth-sack would still cling to me, hazing my vision so that all is blurred silhouettes and shimmering pinpoints of molten mineral. I would sense the older broodmates: their forms dense, their motions sure, each shell thickened by repeated forging. When they shift, I hear the dull rasp of plates. I see flares of heat pulse beneath their carapaces, brief orange glows that reveal a hidden furnace within. My own body tries to replicate that glow, but it flickers uncertainly in my mirrored plates.

Gradually, I’d find the strength to wriggle free of the brood mass and test the stone underfoot. The ground feels alive with the static of myriad limbs scuttling in every direction. It pulses and groans, never settling. Sometimes I confuse stone for living shell; the boundaries are blurred here, as though half the rock might yet break free and set itself in motion. Tottering along on wobbly legs, I discover how each step generates a tapestry of sensation: the crunch of fragility beneath me, the hiss of a nearby vent, the dull percussion of gas pockets shifting deep in the walls. My antennae flare, tasting the air’s chemical tang—part metallic, part highly charged, as though each swirling fume bristles with an electric tension.

Overhead, towering shapes drift in and out of the gloom, some so large that when they stir, the entire corridor of rock resonates. Their subsonic calls vibrate through every membrane in my body, rearranging my sense of balance. I imagine them as living columns, supporting the half-finished heights of this crucible. And indeed, whenever one moves, smaller creatures such as I scatter in waves, making space for the unstoppable push of ancient bulk. I feel, in some deep, primal sense, that they are anchors—pillars that hold the forging environment in place. Their existence cradles the labyrinth, or so it seems.

I linger at the base of a newly formed ridge, still hot from whatever forging process gave it shape. Every few moments, I sense that ridge deepen or sharpen—microscopic adjustments that push outward, as if the stone itself is still deciding on final angles. Here, a cluster of my broodmates is scraping at a bright, crystalline edge, biting off shimmering shards to pass into the crowd. The exchanged crystals move from mandible to mandible, their surfaces swirling with the residue of thermal and chemical interactions. Some portion is apparently “food,” though it resembles no organic matter. Another portion is carted off to fill some niche I cannot see, perhaps to shape brood pods of my own next generation someday, or to feed a vent that demands constant input. Without question, it is vital to keep that cycle going.

Prowling farther along, I notice specialized watchers perched along crevices, their wide antennae spread like splayed fans, “listening” to the environment’s mechanical heartbeat. At intervals, they make quick motions of their limbs, tapping out a signal or passing a chunk of newly gnawed mineral to a smaller subordinate. I realize they are not idle: they coordinate the flows of creatures in and out of vents and hollows. Occasionally, they let out clicking bursts—rapid, staccato messages that cause a wave of motion in the swarm. Entire clusters shuffle to the left, making way for a roped chain of scuttlers loaded with mineral lumps. The watchers’ antennae glint with internal fire, as if they’re measuring data beyond my comprehension.

Time here does not feel linear. While there is wild, relentless activity, there is also a patience that seeps into my shell. The forging continues in both abrupt leaps—like when fresh molten seams open in the rock—and in near-geological increments, shaping corridors so slowly that I cannot witness their minute expansions. My own existence would pivot between these two rhythms: the frantic scuttling to gather crucial resources when a new vent erupts, and the dormant half-sleep, in which I’d cling to a ridge and let the realm’s muted tremors lull me, my glowing innards cycling in sync with the deeper, thrumming intelligence.

Perhaps, as I mature, I learn to direct a few sub-swarms myself—leading smaller gatherers to the molten cracks, teaching broodlings how to harvest and pass along the right crystals, or fanning open my own plate-like antennae to sense changes in pressure. I’d come to know that these tasks are not random: everything in this domain depends on the synergy between molten stone and shell. It is an unending conversation between the geologic heart and the living exoskeleton. And in that synergy, an intelligence—collective, emergent—resides. As I gain experience, my own internal furnace grows hotter, fueling a bright motility. My carapace gains layers of metallic color—scarred bronze edges near my joints, iron black across my ridged back. The air around me often wavers with the heat I radiate.

Eventually, I might become one of the watchers perched at a vantage point—conveying subtle signals through drumming limbs, orchestrating which pockets of newly formed crystals must be harvested first, or which brood pods must be shifted closer to the vent’s scorching exhalations to speed their development. I learn to interpret the domain’s deeper roars—where a new seam of molten potential is about to burst or whether an older corridor is slowly cooling, soon to be claimed by scuttlers less tolerant of high extremes. In short, I learn to mold the environment’s forging processes as part of a single, continuous cycle. The entire realm does not revolve around me individually; I am simply a node in a living circuit, a conduit through which messages flow.

In time, I might even wander deeper, near the domain’s oldest recesses, to behold one of the great anchor-beasts up close. I find them in some half-buried alcove, bridging a narrow gap in the rock with an ancient shell that merges seamlessly into the walls. Their labored exhalations carry centuries of scalding breath, forging slow expansions in the stone around them. I approach, reverent in my own insectile way, scuttling with care. Broad-metal plates line my thorax now, and I let my own antennae brush the giant’s spined limbs. In that moment, I feel a subtle resonance flow through me—a sharing of the realm’s primal memory. I glimpse, for an instant, how each generation has carried on the forging tasks, how the living shapes have guided the molten rock, how the rock has shaped us in return. The giant’s subsonic energy hums in all my joints, reminding me that we are living catalysts.

Where do I belong in this world? At first, I am only one more hatchling, blinking in the gloom, hardly aware of the cyclical drama in motion. Then, gradually, I realize I am a specialized caretaker, an engineer of living heat, born with certain plate-like expansions that sense chemical fluctuations. Over eras, perhaps I ascend to become a keystone in the labyrinth’s flow—part guardian, part regulator. I cling to scorching ridges or stand watch in high alcoves, ensuring the forging cycle continues. I do not rule; the notion of “hierarchy” is too narrow. Instead, I contribute: a focus for the swirling intelligence that animates all these thousands of exoskeletons. My limbs, my antennae, my metallic shell—these are but tools of a far grander organism that extends through the corridors, exhaling steam and sparks.

And, if in the slow churn of these centuries I live long enough, maybe I grow in girth and weight until I achieve something akin to the anchor-beasts: a bridging form that can support entire sub-swarms on my spine, sustaining pods nestled beneath the ridges of my now-colossal plates. My breathing alone might shape new pockets in the rock. My inertness might become a kind of refuge for lesser scuttlers who cluster beneath me to harvest the secretions that seep from my warmer joints. Would I then pass into that half-geologic slumber, letting successive generations weave around me, forging new expansions in the labyrinth? It could be that, in the end, I meld seamlessly into the environment itself—just as some of the older corners have begun to fuse with carapaces of ancestors, forming a composite of mineral and chitin.

Yet even then, I would not be dead. The dryness, the darkness, the pressurized swirl of scorching vapors, the living crunch of brood upon brood—they would all remain vivid, pushing my body to continue forging, shaping, resonating. We might all eventually become part of that deeper consciousness that saturates each layer: a mind not reliant on water or air or light, but on inexhaustible heat and a slow release of chemical potential. In that ultimate stillness, I would sense, with a faint glow behind my plated eyes, that the forging endures and the swarm carries forward. The realm remains a cradle for new, raw forms yet to come.

Thus, I see myself in many stages: first as a trembling, newly hatched scuttler discovering my own heat; then as a caretaker, orchestrating the mineral harvest; and at last, as a massive, near-monolithic anchor bridging corridors and guiding future cycles. I am never alone in this place: the living swarm is my breath, my consciousness, my continuity. Together, we are the engine of creation pulsing through that cramped, high crucible. We are the hum of superheated shells on forging rock, the hiss and scrape of chemical births, the mindful forging and re-forging of this half-wrought world. And in that synergy—shell to stone, spark to gas, brood to brood—a new shape of existence emerges constantly, forging itself over eons, never ceasing.
