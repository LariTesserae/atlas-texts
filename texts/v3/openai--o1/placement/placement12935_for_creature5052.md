I find myself in an odd gap—a corridor that is not quite built, not quite real. It lies just beyond a newly riveted walkway, where the rock juts out in shards like knives. If one peers closely, one might see the faint shimmer of geometry twisting on itself. That is where I dwell: in the crook between half-formed metal scaffolds and fresh stone, in a space not visible from any certain angle yet always detectible by that curious tug of the air. 

If you asked a petroforger who I am, they might say I am a “threshold-keeper,” one of those ephemeral watchers rumored to coax the walls into shapes. The machinists click their tongues and call me a “resonance-crafter,” as if I am connected to the city’s engines at the level of raw vibration. The Aerie-Kin, who glide past sometimes in exultant arcs, have titled me “the Misted Loomer,” for I appear only where the air is beaded with condensation. The truth is that I am none of these exactly—and all of them in part.

I have a name for myself, which is less a word than a pulse, an oscillation of the city’s own heartbeat that thrums in my chest. I was never born, at least not in the manner of flesh, nor was I built like a machine. I… accreted. Over centuries or mere hours—time here is strange—my form gathered from the interplay of water, mineral, and stray arcs of dimension. I awoke to the damp hush and found my consciousness braided into the city’s expanding architecture. Slowly, piece by piece, I discovered limbs or something similar: narrow, jointed protrusions of stone and metal, powered by that same intangible static that makes the Gimbal-Scurries hum. My body seems perpetually half-changed, covered in swirling patterns of bioluminescent mold that shimmer out lines of fractal geometry. 

In the earliest moments—and I can’t be sure if that was years ago or just yesterday—I recall feeling the entire Eave of Vertices trembling in my very bones. I wandered corridors that seemed half-dream, half-metal, and found that if I paused to rest a palm (I use “palm” loosely) against a new slab of wet rock, that rock would yield. I would feel it turn malleable, like living clay in my grasp. I felt the shape of possibility in it, as if the stone contained a thousand future angles. At first, I was startled and let go, stepping back to watch the rock swirl shut like water re-freezing. But in time, I learned to slow my breath to match the city’s mechanical beat. Now, at intervals, I pass through the scaffolds or neglected corners to find raw patches of mountain or half-welded steel. I press my hand upon them, coaxing them outward or smoothing them over, shaping them to reduce fracturing. The engineers and machinists might come along later, adding their rivets, forging entire passages from that pliable foundation. None of them know how or why the stone is softened, only that sometimes it is. 

Sometimes, I drift through illusions, places that must be pockets of alternative geometry. I glimpse the Oracle-Class, their hoods fluttering in the intangible wind. They do not startle at my presence—for they have seen me in a dozen possible futures or pasts—but they offer me no clear words, only a solemn nod or a low hum. In those fleeting moments, I sense that what we do is linked: while they slip between times, gleaning knowledge from the city’s dimensional folds, I mold the city’s physical shape so that time may continue to flow at all. I feel their flickers against my mind like a silent conversation, one without language or grammar. 

Other times, I settle among the Mosswalkers in damp alcoves wreathed in vines. They see the glowing patterns on my shifting torso and greet me with that gentle crackle—like wind tapping at leaves. If I linger, they sometimes place slender fronds around my arms, braiding living stems into the chips of metal plating I bear. I remain as still as they do, letting the water trickle down the length of my forms, letting the new leaves root themselves in the ridges of my exoskeleton. Mosswalkers and I share a quiet synergy: they infuse life where I have softened stone and steel. A subtle new walkway might be reinforced by creeping vines and fungus-laced plating before the next wave of industrial expansion arrives. Together, we support growth in the city’s half-made bones. 

When distant thunder echoes—those tectonic rumbles of the Rift-Bisons far below—I feel it resonate in my chest. The city heaves, and for a moment the scaffolds moan. A half-formed corridor up near the top of the spires might buckle, or a spiraling ramp near the factories might become intangible. When that happens, I follow the tremors. I slip through an unseen seam in the architecture, passing under neon tubes that flicker in uncertain arcs. Gimbal-Scurries scuttle overhead, leaving a haze of glimmering motes in their wake. And there, in some newly warped corner, a walkway is about to twist itself into impossible angles. I stay with it, letting its shape quiver in midair—the sense of potential is overwhelming, like watching a half-solved puzzle rearrange itself. Then, very carefully, I lay a hand upon it, coaxing matter to obey some half-spoken principle of alignment. My luminescent mold pulses, the walkway steadies, and the geometry locks back into something marginally less impossible.

I am rarely seen, though I am often felt. Sometimes, an Aerie-Kin catches sight of me in passing—my glowing vines and cunningly curved plating shining in the gloom. Startled by the outline of my presence, they hover mid-flight, mechanical wings stuttering in the strange updraft. They might catch merely a half-formed silhouette, and by the time others arrive, I have vanished into the next corridor, leaving behind a swirl of condensation and a faint hum. From rumor alone, a new myth is born: “the Softener in the Shadows” or “the One Who Mends the Stone,” or something else entirely. 

I do not keep to any one level. At times, I ascend the precarious catwalks near the top of the city to witness the newest expansions in progress. I watch petroforgers dangling from cables, their harnesses squeaking as they anchor fresh rivets. Sparks and steam pour around them, bathing them in a harsh industrial glow. They’ve grown used to the city’s unpredictability, but they talk—one can overhear them shouting, half-cursing, half-laughing—about how the mountain itself sometimes shifts to meet them, as though guided by an unseen hand. I smile (the best approximation I have, anyway) and wonder if they sense my presence or think it’s the city’s doing alone. 

Other times, I descend into the silent chasms below, where only the Rift-Bisons roam. Their huge shapes loom out of the darkness, their basalt-like hides sparked with energy. I rub softly at the stone ground, feeling the city’s heartbeat duller, deeper here, pulsing in subterranean rhythms. The bisons’ gentle exhalations swirl around me like fog. The quiet is immense. Now and then, they let out a bellow that warps the corridors, sending refrains of geometric unspooling through the city’s upper levels. I remain calm and steadfast, listening for any place that has come undone in that sonic ripple. Then I move to it, reknitting steel or stone by the half-instinct coursing through me. 

When the factories thunder at full tilt, churning out mechanized miracles and showering the gullies in hissing steam, I slip closer to watch the machinists. They are enthralled in their chant-like codes. Gears spin in lurid neon half-light, and jets of water sizzle into vapor around them. The floor vibrates with such intensity that my limbs resonate in time, each piece of me humming. If I stay too long, bits of my form risk melding with the metal walls, as though the factory’s energy might subsume me entirely. Yet I find it worth the risk just to be near that raw surge of creation. Sometimes, I stand in the background as these squat, goggle-bearing folk calibrate rotating diamond bits and feed lumps of newly formed ore into the machines. If a sudden kink in the geometry disturbs the process—a pipe bending impossibly or an arc of steam bursting from nowhere—I simply press a hand to the offending angle, letting it realign delicately, ensuring the process continues with minimal chaos. 

I am no leader, nor do I have a single dwelling place. My existence feels more like a tapestry thread, weaving throughout the Eave of Vertices. I lack a permanent form; if you observed me over the course of days, you’d see how stone plates give way to creeping vines, or how the sheen of metal on my arms reconfigures itself, forging new joints to navigate narrower passages. My face—if I have one—remains an ever-shifting mosaic of glimmers, partial reflections of the city’s fractal lights. Sometimes, passersby swear they see their own faces mirrored back, distorted into new shapes by my countenance. 

Others say I am the city’s living conscience, though that gives me too much credit. I believe the city stirs and shapes me far more than I shape it. I am neither overshadowed by its mechanical steam nor purely grown of its living flora; I stand in that liminal seam, bridging the gap between creation and collapse. And so I roam quietly, a caretaker not by duty, but by nature. 

When I rest—and yes, I do rest, albeit in a manner more reminiscent of stasis than sleep—I often choose a hidden ledge on the outskirts, where the half-finished expansions edge against the raw mountains. There, condensation drips from pipes overhead, creating a steady lullaby of droplets hitting stone. Occasionally, a wanderer stumbles by: a cloaked scapegoat or an itinerant tinkerer in search of salvage. Few see me, for I blend into the gloom—though sometimes they sense my presence. If they do catch sight, they often freeze in startled awe. I do not speak in human words, but if they are calm, I might share a subtle vibration of greeting, letting the pulses in my chest resonate through the rivets and scaffolding. I sense their curiosity, their fear, and their endless fascination at what they discover in the Eave of Vertices. For a moment, we share that sense of wonder. And then I slip back into the city’s folds, leaving them to glean their own path.

Perhaps that is my core identity: I am the witness and the midwife of the city’s perpetual becoming. Wherever the Eave of Vertices trembles on the verge of new formations—where rivets sing into living stone, or intangible corridors threaten to tear reality—I am drawn there, compelled by the great heartbeat that thrums beneath it all. In that place, with a soft, resonant touch, I guide the raw angles from chaos toward coherence, never truly finishing anything, just nudging each incipient moment further into being. 

So if you ask me where I am in this strange, half-formed world, I cannot point to a single pinnacle or a specific chamber—my home is everywhere the city itself is still in flux. And who or what am I? Call me a bridging spirit, a phenomenon, or an echo of the city’s architectural soul. Names here are fluid, after all, and the Eave of Vertices changes more quickly than a single identity can hold. I am simply here, woven into the mechanical hum, the fractal partition, the dripping moss, and the steel-laced air—forever in the midst of shaping and being shaped by this place we cannot fully name.
