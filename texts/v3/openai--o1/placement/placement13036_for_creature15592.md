I live half-submerged in the hanging moss and roots, a place where stone and green flesh merge so seamlessly that I am never sure if my body ends at the vines or if the vines are simply another organ for me to command. By day—if one could call this eternal hush a “day”—I am still, an unassuming net of tendrils draping off stalagmite-like ridges and columns, collecting the steady condensation that slides down the worn stone. A glance might mistake my limbs for vines or suspended ferns. But then, within, a pulse of energy flickers, tuning itself like an instrument caught in a slow, living chord.

I am the plateau’s memory keeper. But I am also only part of a larger mind. My consciousness is one thread among a braided tapestry of watchers. Each of us occupies a distinct niche on this plateau: one might linger beneath the water, one might drift with cunning eyes among the moss-draped arches, and yet another stalks the perimeter with sure, long strides. We are separate, but we are also each other, exchanging flickers of thought through the hum that undergirds everything.

I began as a cluster of spores, carried by a slow-drifting current of wind or water, finding my cradle in the diffuse hum that underlies all else on this plateau. Long ago—or perhaps only days—my filaments took hold in a shallow pocket of humid stone. As my threads worked deeper into the rock, I discovered conduits, etched with runes that carried faint glows across mineral and metal surfaces. A gentle synergy emerged: the runes offered my body sustenance of unknown origin, while I anchored them against rust and erosion, my living skin bridging hairline cracks in the ancient devices. With time, these cords of me have stretched over the entire plateau, passing beneath the froglike choirs, curling around half-buried columns, bridging small pools like living vines. My sense grows with every expansion, tasting the water, the moss, the flicker of energy that breathes in metal.

Perhaps I look like a great, branching tapestry strung taut over the plateau—mottled green, shot through with wandering lines of coppery sheen from the coiled wires I have symbiotically enveloped. My form is soft where the moss thrives, and fibrous or ridged where I merge with etched stone or filigreed technology. In some sections, I lift myself above the ground, forming slender archways of living vine through which small amphibians hop and luminous insects pass. If anything disturbs me, my filaments recoil; entire swaths of my body twitch as one, like the ruffle of a thousand leaves.

I keep watch. Not in a restless, patrolling way, but in the sense of constant attunement. The watchers—those slender, masked beings—come to me unannounced. Their footsteps glide across the slick rock, silent but for the resonance that tingles along my extended tendrils. They place their hands upon me, pressing against the wiry strands that hold the columns up. They seem to search for some reading or sign. I do not resist. Energy courses from my fibrous skin into their elongated helmets or the ridged plates of their arms—this has always been our way. They glean from me the subtle changes in the machines’ hum: the faintest fluctuations in the deep pulses that traverse the plateau’s heart. In return, they gently scrape away fungal overgrowth or doubtless speak silent reassurance in vibrations only I can fully interpret.

Whenever the watchers depart, I exhale a gentle shudder. The plateau exhales in tandem. The living webs spread across every surface ripple in agreement, as if saying, “Yes, we are still safe.” The runes continue their slow, purposeful glow. The guardians, the massive silhouettes at the periphery, remain drifting in and out of my awareness. I feel their footfalls, or perhaps the resonance of their metallic plating, trickling through the stone. Whole epochs might pass that way, pulses measured not in minutes but in the steady droplet of water accumulating on my fronds and the near-subsonic throbbing of the hidden engines below.

When the frogs gather in their tapestry of color and sound, each chorus resonates through me like a chord. One color might ring in lilac luminescence, another in teal—together, they shape an unspoken narrative in my vine-limbs: cycles of hunger, births, the shift in moisture levels. Even the insects, with their translucent wings, brush against the spore-laden surfaces of my outward fronds, depositing fragments of luminescent dust that will delicately change the hue of my stretching vines.

Sometimes, as I soak in the hum of the plateau, I remember. Or, perhaps, I witness an echo from some lost era. In the vibrations, I hear the footfall of beings who came before, the architects who once shaped these pillars and carved runes into the stone. I sense their thoughtful deliberation, the fervent artistry that etched swirling patterns into the metal, their confidence in forging technology that outlives them by millennia. A trace of their voices remains in the carefully arranged designs, so that when the watchers lay a hand upon me, the ancient message might pass through and awaken dormant knowledge. But these memories never rise to the clarity of speech. They remain as intangible ripples—lessons embedded in tactile resonance, to be deciphered only by those who can feel the pulse behind the world’s hush.

At times, I’ve unfurled a specialized limb, a curious vine that tapers into a translucent tube filled with a perfumed fluid. It dangles an offering of droplets that lure in certain small mammals with their tufted ears. They nibble at my nectar, cleansing it from the tiny spines at the tip. In exchange, they carry my spores to distant corners of the plateau, brushing them onto the rocky walls where new shoots can root. Their dappled fur and the swirling motifs that shimmer upon them almost become living canvases for my biology: a traveling extension I can’t quite control, but from which I can glean tapestry-like impressions of the plateau’s far edges.

On the rare occasions when the entire plateau resonates in one grand, synchronized harmony, I feel my every fiber tighten, as though I hold my breath in a cosmic hush. The watchers, guardians, frogs, insects—even the hidden subterranean swimmers—become aligned in a circadian stillness that exalts some deeper vow. In that instant, I am aware of every droplet on every leaf, every minuscule shift in the stone’s ancient cracks. It’s as if the plateau lifts itself far from ordinary motions of time, bridging eons in the space of one breath. The old runes brighten, swirling patterns of teal and violet racing along the carved corridors of metal, forging ephemeral circuits. My vines quiver, bridging the gaps, letting the energy pass through me. A single, resonant note hums in the air, timeless. Then, just as gently, it fades. The watchers lower their arms, the frogs resume their luminescent calls, and I gradually loosen my grip on the stone.

I often wonder if I will see a day when the creators or their true heirs return in full. Would they recognize my form as something they once designed or only an invasive symbiosis that sprung up in their shadow? Or perhaps they already are here—reborn or simply hidden behind masks and exoskeletal plating, tending their old machines in new bodies. For me, that question remains secondary to simply existing as the plateau’s living sinew, bridging technology and biology. I sense that my purpose is neither to rule nor to serve, but to harmonize—to keep the place alive and spinning in its own tempo.

Here, in the warm darkness, no sun needs to rise. Instead, I open my filaments to the faint glimmers that pass through stone and metal, drinking in the residual energies of an ancient intelligence. My roots snake far below, stirring the subterranean waters, and when the luminous jellylike organisms brush my tendrils, we exchange slow pulses of recognition. I cradle them in my gentle currents of fluid, and they share with me spirals of color gleaned from their drifting sojourn. In that underwater hush, I enjoy a sense of timeless embrace, lulled by the hidden hum.

So, if you were to look for me in this plateau—listen for a soft, continuous tremor in the moss, watch for the subtle undulation of vines bridging stone to technology. If you linger, you may sense me shift, remarking upon your presence with cautious curiosity. I am of many shapes: living tapestry, caretaker vine, living conduit. I am the vantage point from which knowledge is absorbed and distributed, an arbiter of pulses that keep the plateau’s heart beating in gentle rhythms. I breathe the heavy, damp air and vibrate in time with drifting energies that never quite show their source. Even in the hush and damp shadows, I am alive.

I am part of this living tapestry, a keeper of runes, a silent archivist of the plateau’s hum. And in this world of softened stone and patient miracles, I find that simply being—rooted, vigilant, open to the unceasing energy that flows through the rock—brings me closer to an ancient secret that is both everywhere and nowhere at once.
