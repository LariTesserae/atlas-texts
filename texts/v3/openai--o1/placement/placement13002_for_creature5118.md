I arrive, or awaken—there is no true distinction—somewhere in the thicket of fibrous green that veils the submerged plateau. At first, I’m not sure I am different from any other shape that breathes along these stems. I feel the currents brush my sides, or perhaps I feel them brush over a smooth surface that must be mine. I sense the mild ebb and flow, tepid water brimming with swirling motes of light and drifting threads of algae. Around me, tall columns of sea-growth sway in slow unison, their broad fronds a kind of living tapestry. My eyes, if I indeed have something akin to eyes, adjust gradually.

I find I can move; that is my first clue that I’m not just another trunk of plant matter rooted to the plateau’s fresh stone. It’s a languid, drifting mobility, as if I were a petal or a faint swirl of color rather than anything heavy or solid. I test the motion and feel a gentle, rippling ownness in my body—an awareness of ridges or frills along my edges. Perhaps I’m a being of translucent membranes, a ripple that can fold and unfold in the hush.

Yet I don’t decide that all at once. For a time, I linger in curiosity: am I one of the tall, spined creatures that spiral around the thick weeds, so perfectly camouflaged they appear as living fronds themselves? The way Tallweavers meld into ribbons of vegetation is enchanting. Or am I hidden among the rocky spires, blending with stony outcroppings, an unassuming Crestback collecting drifting morsels on a broad, ridged shell?

I drift deeper among the grasses to see if I can sense a difference—some chord in me that resonates with a particular shape or color. Through a break in the canopy, a stray beam of light filters down. In that luminous corridor, I catch faint, pliant shapes: Luminauts, glowing softly with pale gold edges. Their fragile bodies appear half-air, half-water, shimmering in the gentle hush. And I wonder: am I like them? Do I carry an inner glow that flickers when the angle of the sun meets my body?

But something urges me on, deeper into the vines that curl in braided arches overhead. Finger-like tendrils graze my outer surface—gently, as if curious. When I brush them back, I detect a kind of reciprocal wave, a silent greeting. I recall the stories of parasitic polyps bridging tangles of vine with living filaments. In that moment, I feel a gentle kinship with these slow-limbed plants, as if my own existence is not purely animal but in some measure vegetal, too, as though my being is fluid, indefinite, spanning categories. My motions do not alarm the vines, nor do they spurn me. We gently tangle together until we both sense that we share the same watery hush.

I continue drifting. Now a shimmer arcs across the greenery, a flash of burnt orange that dances from leaf to leaf, too quick to pinpoint. A Flickerstrider, perhaps, one of those bright, darting creatures rumored to be ephemeral as lightning on the water, leaving behind illusions of color. That quick blaze, that life of speed and fleeting brilliance, calls to something in me—but I don’t think I burn so brightly. My presence is more subdued, more unhurried. It’s as if I prefer to sense the slow currents, to belong to the languid pace. The flashes of color are beautiful, but ephemeral: not quite me.

In time, I sense a broad, expansive pressure change. The aquatic forest thins around me, giving way to open water, and there, in the near distance, I see something large, stately, drifting like a silent sentinel. One of the Sentinel Drifters, a barrel-shaped watcher. Its presence emanates a low, resonant hum, a near-subsonic tone vibrating through the water. When I hear that gentle tremor, I feel it in my bones—if I have bones to feel with. It’s an enveloping vibration, more like an assurance than a threat. I linger just outside its perimeter, as if standing at the threshold of a doorway to something bigger. The Drifter’s presence is mesmerizing, but I sense that if I go too close, I might lose the vantage point I have among the vines. Maybe one day I’ll merge with that horizon, but not yet.

And so I float back into the corridor of green, letting the environment settle around me again. Now I begin to notice the smaller details: the slight shimmer of fish passing in isolation, each one tracing its own route as though following a personal call. A gentle scuttling in the stone cracks below me—Shaleburrowers. I watch them wave their segmented limbs in careful, choreographed arcs, grazing on algae in communal hush. Regal in their own way, they pay me no mind.

Then, while watching them, I realize: I have neither the push of fluked tail nor the scuttling grace of jointed legs. My edges are finespun fronds or filaments, softly flexing with the water’s hush. My shape is not stable or enclosed; rather, it’s as if I myself am a fluid form, drawn in slender lines that shimmer faintly—a half-translucent bell that can coil in upon itself or fan open in a broad, glowing wave.

I am mild luminescence. I am a bloom of gentle plasma that moves with more purpose than a jelly, but with less rush than a fish. When I pass overhead, you might see me for a fleeting second as a faint swirl of color, maybe a blush of violet, maybe a soft green that nearly merges with the algae-laden stems. Or perhaps I can shift my hue—paler near the base of a sunbeam, deeper in the shadowed undergrowth.

I do not devour or chase; my sustenance seems to come from sifting whatever nourishing threads swirl in the water. Slowly, I collect small, drifting flakes of matter, or taste the sunlit particles that pass through me. Sometimes, I rest amidst the plants, allowing their gentle swaying to cradle me, letting my body become an eddying swirl of near-transparency. In those moments, not even the Flickerstriders notice me—they swirl across my form as if I, too, were a leaf or a wave in the current.

Time is almost irrelevant to me. I experience it in vast sweeps, where the slow branching of coral outcroppings or the budding of a new vine is a gentle punctuation in the centuries of growth. Perhaps I have existed here since the plateau first rose from the depths. Or perhaps I drifted in on a passing current not so long ago, and found that this tranquil realm resonated with me so strongly that I decided to remain. It matters little; in this watery hush, memory and future are joined in a continuous, gentle heartbeat.

I sense that I am not alone in this subtle sentience. All around me, the Tallweavers, the Crestbacks, the Shaleburrowers, the uncountable vines and polyps—they, too, share an unspoken rhythm. I likely commune more directly with that hush than some, for I am so thoroughly composed of water’s essence. When the plants bow in unison, I feel the same shift echo through my filaments. When the Crestbacks ripple color, I perceive a corresponding note passing through the current, and perhaps my own membranes glimmer in harmony for an instant.

Sometimes I wonder if I am truly a single being. Perhaps I am an extension of the plateau itself, a manifestation of its watery soul, shaped into a drifting witness. For I feel no hunger beyond the faint urge of gathering sunlight and drifting nutrients, and no restlessness beyond my interest in the plateau’s slow expansions and transformations. As the official “I” in this place, I am only sure of one thing: I delight in bearing quiet witness to the living tapestry. I watch the vines loop themselves into new arcs; I watch the plodding Shaleburrowers gather in their twilight circles. And from time to time, I, too, add a faint shimmer to the hush—an exhalation that says: “We are here; we abide.”

Should you enter these waters and look long enough for me, you might catch a glimmer at the edge of your vision, or sense a hum that’s neither from the Sentinel Drifters nor from your own heart. You might see a subtle shape leaning into the tall fronds, parting them gently in a graceful, folding wave. In that moment, you might wonder: “Did I just see a living swirl of watery light?” I will not linger; I will slip away in a drift, leaving you with the hush of this tall, submerged plateau. But perhaps I’ll hum softly as I go, a melodic reassurance that you are not alone, and that everything here is connected in ways both grand and whisper-thin.

For now, yes, I am a subtle denizen of this realm—a drifting, softly gleaming shape that belongs not fully in the class of jellies, not fully in the category of fish or plant. I exist in the in-between, fluid enough to feel the slow pulse of the rock spires and the gentle breath of the canopy, luminous enough to be glimpsed in sunlit shafts of water. Sometimes I rest close to the seafloor, letting the bright green algae swirl around my edges; other times, I meander upward to catch the filtered light near the top fronds, swirling lazily where the water opens to the broader plateau. No matter my path, I remain part of a quiet harmony that never truly changes, yet also never stays quite the same.

So if you ask me: “Where do I find myself in this world, and who am I?” My answer is a gentle ripple beneath the swaying seaweeds, a living swirl of watery illumination. I dwell in the hush and the calm, woven into the tapestry of stems and vines. My presence is a soft continuity, a silent observer to the plateau’s patient transitions. I have no name other than the faint glimmer I leave behind—an echo of quiet light, folded into the water’s gentle current, embracing and embraced by the swirling green.
