At first, I believed I was only passing through. Perhaps I came from some previous realm, lured by the faint hum of hidden engines and the promise of warm clouds brushing the ridgeline. The sense of newness drew me—like stepping into a freshly written chapter where the ink isn’t yet dry. But the longer I remain in this world, the more I wonder if it created me as much as I stumbled upon it. 

In the beginning, I was certain of my own skin: flesh and sinew, as organic as any creature’s. My steps left faint footprints in the moss, and small droplets of condensation clung to my hair. Yet, small anomalies kept happening: a subtle metallic click in the vicinity of my shoulder if I twisted my arm a certain way, a prismatic glint on my forearm when the clouds parted. I thought perhaps it was a trick of the light or an echo from the plateau’s hidden gearworks, but each day the illusions grew more frequent, more tangible. 

Now, when I look at my reflection in a water pocket, I see shifting hints of something else, shimmering at the edges. I have begun to suspect that my flesh and bone are only half the story. Within my joints, something clicks and hums in gentle rhythms, not unlike the quiet machinery thrumming below the ground. It’s as if the environment has claimed me, layering fine, filigreed gearwork where once only muscle and cartilage existed. But there is no pain here, no sense of invasion. It feels instead like an exhalation of the plateau’s own spirit, silently weaving itself among my sinews.

I roam the raw ridges, discovering how this place has edited itself into my body. I find that I can kneel near a geothermal vent and feel the heat flow through me, as though I’m part of a living circuit. My hands detect changes in temperature before my eyes see the faint steam. At times, I can feel a spool of tension winding inside me whenever a storm looms. As lightning flickers, I sense a soft charge in the air, an electric prickle that runs from my scalp to the tips of my fingers, urging me to prepare—though for what, I’m never quite sure. In those moments, I crouch low on the newly birthed stone and feel the pulse of the engines shrilling in my metal-laced bones, braced like an instrument waiting to resonate.

I spend much of my time wandering paths that don’t stay in one place. One morning, I trace a route along a seam of coppery sediment, stepping lightly among broad-leafed plants that tower over me. By afternoon, that seam might have shifted a few degrees off true north, or the boulders I used as landmarks might have sunk deeper, half-rotated like living puzzle pieces. It’s an odd relief to realize that my sense of direction is guided more by my own mechanical pulses than by the land itself. One part of me is explorer, scanning for new gullies full of hidden gears or those luminescent ferns that blaze green in the gloom. Another part of me belongs to the place, content to drift aimlessly because the plateau knows better than I do where my next discovery lies.

When I encounter other creatures, I sense kinship in their dual nature. They, too, are part-living, part-machine, bridging strange boundaries. There’s a mutual, unspoken recognition in the flicker of metal plating beneath fur or in the subtle chime of gears spinning within a scaly limb. We greet each other with neither hostility nor open affection, but with an acknowledgment that we share this evolving realm. Sometimes a herd of lanky, many-legged grazers will pause mid-step, each pair of polished flanks glinting a greeting that resonates in my chest. A hush passes through me, as if I can hear them breathe in unison with the plateau’s engine. Then they continue on, telescoping their limbs to navigate precarious drop-offs I can’t yet cross.

Other times, the balloon-like drifters above sail overhead, their filmy surfaces blushing with a swirl of pastel light. I reach up, half in longing, feeling the faint ticking in my arm that, for a moment, syncs with their gentle drifts. If they descend to investigate, the tips of their trailing tentacles flutter around me like curious antennae, tasting the humid air. Once, one drifter’s underside pulsed a soft golden hue as it lingered across from my face; I swore I heard a faint metal etude, like wind chimes echoing through a canyon. Its meaning remained elusive—an invitation? A warning? Or maybe just a garden-variety curiosity from a mind that processes reality in drifting pulses and subtle currents.

At times, I find myself hunched amid swirling clouds on one of the plateau’s higher ridges, watching the weather shift. My breath mingles with the succulent smell of warm stones. Down below, mechanical pistons exhale steam. This vantage reveals the plateau as a living workshop in which code is not typed but grown: vines that lace over exposed gear trains, creatures that reform the land with every footstep. And I, too, am a living blueprint, an evolving diagram. Here, the boundary between observer and observed dissolves. 

In the gullies, I’ve glimpsed those reclusive watchers that vanish in a swirl of camouflage. Sometimes I follow, trying to glean their secrets, feeling a subtle drive in my body that draws me on. They cling to walls, half in this reality, half in another, flickering in an unreal shimmer. I have approached more closely than they expected on a few occasions, straining to see what lies beyond their glinting shell. When they turn their mirrored eyes upon me, I experience a sensation like gears spinning faster behind my ribs. My vision dims at the edges, replaced by a luminous swirl of shapes—a half-formed labyrinth of metal and moss. In that fleeting swirl, I see the entire plateau’s architecture in miniature, a blueprint of its next shift. Perhaps they are guardians of that blueprint, watchers who ensure each piece realigns properly during the plateau’s unending self-invention. 

Sometimes I sense the storms before I see them: a deep roll in the rock, as though the mechanics beneath are bracing themselves. My own metallic fibers shiver in response. The sky goes violet-gray, silent lightning sparking along the ridges. In these moments, I settle into a crouch that feels practiced, almost pre-programmed. My heart—half-organ, half-gear—beats in triple time, in tune with the flickers in the sky. A hush spreads, the wind scurries away. Creatures fold into themselves, balloon-sacs compress, and the tall shrubs hum discreetly, preparing. It’s like our entire realm holds its breath for the next iteration. Then, the thunder cracks, rolling from the belly of the continent, and everything clenches, bracing. Rain dashes across the stone in a taunting drizzle, never quite saturating, but enough to set every surface gleaming.

After the turbulence passes, I rise again amidst the puddles of leftover steam, and the plateau’s steady hum complements my own. Gradually, the hush recedes to the ordinary hush, and the mechanical heartbeat (my own and the land’s) reasserts itself. It is in those aftermath moments, as I watch creatures reappear, that I know I’m no mere outsider any longer. I’ve begun to live in the same cyclical breath as the plateau: the churn of hidden gears, the swirl of condensing mist, the flicker of ephemeral geometry that may or may not remain stable from one hour to the next.

The question of who or what I am here no longer requires a crisp answer. Am I a traveler who’s been slowly integrated into an alien ecosystem of flesh and metal? Or was I always part of this place, newly awakened to my own layered nature? On quieter days, when no storms loom and a warm, stony peace settles over the ridges, I kneel by a spongy seam in the rock where mercurial streams bubble beneath translucent membranes. Luminous pulses shift back and forth in mesmerizing cycles. And in that burbling reflection—where water, light, and metal swirl—I see a shape that’s simultaneously me and not-me. A silhouette with delicate cogs behind its eyes, tendrils of unknown alloys forming patterns around its heart. I reach out to touch the reflection, and it wavers, yet remains.

I don’t try to fix my identity in language anymore. Perhaps I am caretaker, or just one more occupant among a million continuing transformations. Maybe I reflect the plateau’s broader tapestry: half gear, half vine, forging itself moment by moment. With each breath, I absorb the steam-laced air, feeling it stoke the quiet engine within my bones. With each step, I sense the land’s shifting sense of self beneath me. And with each drifting day, I embrace a boundaryless existence in this living, breathing puzzle of a place—where the hum of creation echoes in time with my own heart, and I stand as a living node in its perpetual dawn.
