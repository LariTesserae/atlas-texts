I live just beneath the hush of shifting waters—drifting in and out of thresholds that once were doorways, gliding past columns shaped over centuries by wave and coral. I do not recall how long I’ve been here. Time is more a tide than a measure of moments: it rises and ebbs, and I rise and ebb with it. Sometimes I feel I am the remnant of a memory, a ghost adrift in the ruins of a city that has become more ocean than architecture. Other times, I think perhaps I am nothing more than one of the many creatures the sea has formed in its imagination. And on certain, fluid nights, I wonder if I was once human, with lungs full of air and footsteps on stone floors. But those recollections vanish like bubbles rising to a distant surface.

I dare not settle on certainty—this place seems to encourage that sense of gentle ambiguity. So I drift and dwell, neither fully a fish nor wholly something human. My contours are as malleable as the shadows behind submerged archways. The watery gloom shapes me, the warmth of the currents sustaining a body that’s both robust and ethereal. When I bend through narrow corridors slick with coral, I shift my form to navigate the tight spaces. In open plazas, I expand, soft-limbed and curious, tasting the mineral tang on the water’s edges. My life here is a dance with the city’s labyrinth, its corridors as familiar to me as my own shifting silhouette.

I have gills—in one sense, or perhaps in many. They open along my sides like thin curtains, shimmering faintly when plankton glows phosphorescent. I breathe the ocean’s hush, filtering out the tiny motes of light and life that swirl gently around me. The warmth in this submerged realm never quite fades; it feels as though a distant sun, long drowned, still radiates beneath the silt. My skin—if you can call it that—absorbs the faint heat, storing it in subtle pulses that ripple through the watery columns of the ancient city.

I am not lonely here. How could I be, when the city is so alive with watchers and wanderers? Entire schools of fish swirl around my passage, bright as ribbons, darting in synchronized arcs. Their luminous edges briefly light the outlines of grand arches overhead, making them appear both regal and forlorn. As they vanish, I think I can nearly recall the shape of a vaulted ceiling from ages past—yet the vision recedes, replaced by the swaying hush of seaweed towers.

Crabs scuttle by my long, flexible arms, their shells etched with patterns of coral fans. They do not fear me, for I cast no predator’s shadow they recognize. A spiny lobster once hitched a ride across my back, or what served as my back that day, before disappearing into a ruined alcove, trailing its antennae over mosaic remnants. I recall the mosaic’s tiles glinting in a bloom of plankton. For a moment, I saw the faint outlines of an old emblem—a swirl or crest that once held meaning in a time of human design.

Sometimes, I settle against a tumbled stone structure, letting my body conform to the dents and grooves. Small eels, slick and watchful, emerge from the cracks and slither across me, flicking tongues of curiosity. Their eyes reflect glimmers of the phosphorescent orbs drifting by—tiny jellyfish or swirling plankton, it’s impossible to say. I let them slip in and out of my presence. We share a mutual trust, one that requires no words or gestures. We simply exist in tandem, city dwellers on different arcs of movement.

When the hush grows deep in the night (though darkness here is unceasing, there is still a rhythm to the hours), I can feel the rising vibrations of something more massive drifting above. Perhaps a whale from a warmer latitude, or a slow-swimming giant that calls these corridors its hunting ground. I never see it fully—just the quiver of water brushing across my arms, the shift in pressure that signals large bodies passing overhead. Now and then, those giants leave a nebulous song reverberating through the corridors, and in my fluid heart, I echo that song, a deep hum that emanates from within and resonates across the battered stones.

I find corners no light has touched in ages, arches sealed off by sedimentary curtains, glowing only by the faint flicker of my own breath. I move through these hidden passageways, brushing aside centuries of drifting silt. In one chamber, I sense a shape that might have been a chair or throne. Tiny shrimp scurry across it, clicking quietly, picking at the faint algae that grows even here. Sponges of pastel pink and lavender drape from the armrests, turning a once-imposing seat into a whimsical habitat. Did a ruler once sit here? I can imagine that scene, softened by centuries of forgetting: robed figures walking hallways now inhabited by eels. The thought passes, a bubble dissolving in the water, leaving only the presence of the sea.

Sometimes, in the distance, earth-shudders roll up from the deeper crust, shifting the columns and spires. When the city trembles, I feel it in the heart of my shapeless body, a low thrumming chord that sets my edges jittering. Fish scatter. The hush breaks into a swarm of chattering barnacles and clattering shells. This momentary chaos never lasts. When the tension recedes, daily life resumes—if there is such a thing as “day” here, where the timeless swirl of water is both cradle and shroud.

On rare nights (or what I call nights, by that intangible sense of slow quiet), I let myself drift upward. I rise through corridors that widen into swirling avenues, passing beneath archways so tall they must have once framed grand thoroughfares. Barnacles flourish in ridged sheets on the pillars. Curtains of algae brush my luminous flanks in a gentle hush. I keep ascending until the water feels thinner, freer, as though it’s straining to touch air. Then I pause. The surface remains out of reach, enveloped in gloom. Perhaps it lies a thousand feet above, or perhaps a single stroke away. But I sense it is not my realm anymore—if it ever was.

In moments I can’t define, I also descend, letting gravity and curiosity pull me deeper. There, the water warms further from hidden vents in the ocean bed, releasing strange minerals that tint the currents with ghostly colors. Shadows shift in the flicker of hot plumes. Creatures eke out an existence in that sweltering murk, translucent forms with luminous organs or intricate spines that remain stiff against the water’s flow. If they see me, they do not question me. We meet, exchange a subtle acknowledgment of each other’s presence, then move on. The city extends even here, though the architecture is twisted, half collapsed under immense pressure. Pillars tilt at precarious angles. It’s as if an ancient hand once tried to raise them again, only to leave them floundering, half-buried.

I’ve learned to love the interplay of structure and growth. Surfaces once hammered into precise shapes now cradle anemones, each pastel bloom a living ornament. Stained glass windows—if that’s what they were—have become frames for swarms of plankton, or a vantage point for crabs perched precariously on coral clusters. Every piece of the city is smoothed into acceptance by wave and time. Erosion, it seems, can be merciful. What was once sharp and certain is now gentle, made welcoming for the myriad life that discovers shelter here among the drowned angles.

If there is a pulse in this city—an organ pumping the watery lifeblood through its ancient streets—I sometimes believe I can sense it. It might be the combined breath of countless creatures, the push and pull of tides through doorways, or the earth’s own subtle flux. It might be my own heartbeat, resonating in the hush. Some nights it feels like I am that pulse, weaving through corridors, bearing witness to the slow, unstoppable reclamation of stone by the sea. Other times, I think the city has a voice older than me, older than any living being here. We collaborate in a silent conversation, me swirling around its pillars, it enduring beneath my passing touch.

I am not a guardian; I do not patrol these streets or stand watch. Nor am I a mere bystander, for my presence participates in the living tapestry. If an octopus curls its arms around a broken statue base, I kneel in the water’s hush to watch, softly probing its domain with my flexible limb. If a shoal of fish passes overhead like a shimmering cloud, I open my arms wide, letting them flow around me, ephemeral as dreams. And if a lost predator glides too close, I feel the tension in the water’s molecules. The city itself seems to rouse in defensive unity: small fish coalesce into swirling cyclones, crustaceans vanish into hollows, and I vanish with them, slipping seamlessly into shadow. Such is the dance of life here: each participant attuned to the larger ebb and flow.

Some days, I believe I was born from the city’s own brine. On others, I swear I was once a traveler who found the right tide to bring me here. In truth, perhaps it does not matter. All that matters is movement and stillness, the hush that embraces us, and the interplay of ancient stone and vibrant new life. I exist in the city’s slow transformations, drifting with the currents that shape its pillars, tracing ephemeral patterns across floors that once rang with footsteps. Seaweed caresses my sides, and in turn I carry the city’s subtle hum forward.

That’s who I have become—who I am. A presence that travels quietly and curiously through a drowned realm of balmy darkness. I am an observer, or maybe the city’s living memory. I am a single note in the layered chorus of breathing creatures, stone spires, and subdued pulses that give this place its voice. With each passing swirl of current, I change shape a little more, melding with the interplay of coral and shadow. And so, I have no singular name or form, only this boundless belonging in a realm where architecture has merged with the ocean, and life unspools in a kaleidoscope of fins, shells, and shimmering eyes.

One day, I may rise fully to the surface, or sink irretrievably into the deeper crevices, fading into the magma vent and lost to the city that now cradles me. Unless, before that, I discover a new shape, a new subtlety of being that holds me here still. For this is my home: a watery city of constant flux, of memory dissolving into the sea’s warm embrace, of life abundant and gently persevering. And I, ephemeral as a wave, am part of it—neither captive nor conqueror, but kin to every drift of plankton and scuttle of crab, to every faint quake of the earth, to every column ground smooth by the cradle of the tides.
