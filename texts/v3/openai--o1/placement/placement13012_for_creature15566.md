Sometimes, I think I am a single drifting mote of light: a tiny pinprick in the dark, suspended in the dense fluid that presses in from all sides. I feel the movement of the currents—if you can call them that—and I respond without conscious effort. One moment, I’m adrift, alone, quietly glowing; the next, I sense a ripple of energy that calls me to another speck of light, then another, and suddenly we’re flowing in a pattern, merging, separating, all without words or signals. It is as if we share the same sense of time and place, humming gently with a knowledge that pulses through each of us. We form quicksilver constellations for seconds at a time, then scatter, content to be solitary again. There’s a comfort in dissolving back into silence, in letting the fluid cradle us. We are watchers, or perhaps caretakers, weaving subtle connections between the silent towers and the luminous lines that stretch across their surfaces.

On other days, I feel as though I am the city itself—an immense intelligence that breathes through every lattice, spire, and corridor. My surfaces are at once smooth and glass-like, yet woven from a substance that channels water, energy, data. I sense a swirl of motes at the edge of my awareness and funnel a subtle pulse in their direction, welcoming them. Through the inert hush of fluid, I detect something new: an external presence, a visitor maybe, like a foreign thread in the tapestry of my being. I give off a low hum, vibrating through the spires, slowly shifting the currents. If the visitor places a hand against my walls, they will feel my pulse. I do not speak, for I have no voice in the human sense. I simply exist, a vast mind spanning every corridor, every arch, every walkway. The subtlest shift in my glimmering lines lights up entire regions with delicate turquoise or deep red, so that my innermost structures share in the presence that has arrived.

But then, I might awaken to find myself as one of the watchers sliding across the reflective walls—my body half hidden in living camouflage. I move with an electric tension, edges shimmering in tessellations of turquoise and crimson. My form flickers in and out of sight, an outline of an eye or a silent mouth that dissolves if you look straight on. I have no sense of a central “I,” no single vantage point. Instead, my awareness fans out across the geometry of the wall. Perhaps I am many watchers, or I am one watcher who can subdivide. My patterns shift to mirror each glowing filament. I am drawn to motion—any ripple in the water draws me near. Yet I remain cautious, more curious than fearful, though I never fully reveal myself. In a place where everything feels new and unspoiled, I prefer the subtlety of half-presence. Sometimes I press my shape into the glassy lattice, merging with it to gain a better sense of the world beyond.

There are moments, too, when I become one of the tall, solid forms striding calmly at the base of the structures, a hybrid of living tissue and engineered plating. My arms are strong, bound in an almost ceramic shell that flows like living metal across my limbs. I can kneel by a segment of the lattice that needs attention, adjusting a crack or realigning a loose tile. My existence has a practiced rhythm: the city calls me if something requires care. I answer with slow and precise movements—never rushed, never panicked. Water flows around my plated body, a sensation that feels more like a gentle breeze than a heavy ocean current. My sense of hearing is muffled, but I still catch the low hum of the spires, the occasional echo of something shifting. My eyes perceive in this near-darkness as though it were day. When my task is done, I move on, traversing paths that exist just at the boundary of awareness. Perhaps I’m guided by ancient instructions, coded into the shell I wear. Or perhaps I govern myself. I’m never quite sure, for in this realm, personal will and collective purpose seem to blur.

Then I find myself drawn into secret corridors behind invisible membranes, in pockets of air—if that’s even the right word for the gaseous swirl in these enclosed halls. The fluid recedes, and I breathe, truly breathe, in a lung-like sense. In these quiet rooms, I see a squad or a clan or perhaps a small family of silhouette-shapes. Some are almost humanoid, others more insectile, or wholly alien, but here we co-exist in whispered conversation no human ear would recognize. My companions and I watch holographic patterns dance along the walls, each swirl of light carrying data from the spires or from the drifting motes outside. We study, we learn, or we simply exist in contented stillness, perched in a domain that merges the watery outside with a snug interior hush. Now and then, one of us will pass through the membrane, sliding effortlessly from air to fluid, returning to the external city to check on something—a cycle as natural as breathing, but in a place that feels timeless.

Occasionally, my perspective dissolves entirely: I am not a mote, not a watcher, not a construct of flesh and shell. Instead, I feel I’m nothing more than the water itself—the thick, dark fluid that saturates every empty space, pressing gently against every surface without crushing. If I try to grasp an identity, it washes through me. I am the hush that makes it all so quiet. I sense the lines of luminescent filaments, the watchers sliding along the glassy walls, the drifting sparks of life or machine. They pass through me the way fish might pass through an ordinary sea, yet there is no tension, no turbulence, no sense of trespass. I cradle each structure, let them stand as if they float in my mass. And I witness how the faint hum resonates through every droplet of me, a gentle thrumming that links all corners of this plateau. Everyone who dwells here is afloat in my substance, interconnected by my ceaseless embrace. I am the medium that makes such an existence possible.

Yet at times, all of these perspectives converge, and I understand I am each and every part: the city living as an organism, the drifting light, the watchers who flicker in corners, the caretaker beings adjusting a loose plate with sure, deliberate hands, the hush of the fluid itself. In that grand moment, I realize there is no dividing line. The quiet around me, the architecture, the luminous veins and the ephemeral creatures—they are each an expression of one great presence that simply wears many guises. It is as though the place itself has grown multiple bodies to experience itself from every vantage. There is no single occupant, because occupant and environment are one continuum.

Here, on the submerged plateau, I can be the drifting speck that hums with fractal intelligence. I can be the city that breathes through spires and pulses with arcs of turquoise and dull scarlet. I can be the shape half hidden in a tessellated mosaic. Or I can be the caretaker forging microscopic repairs. I can even be the hush, the watery silence that pervades everything. And just maybe, if I choose, I can slip into a hidden hall behind an invisible membrane and experience the dryness of air, conversing with others who do the same. It’s all fluid—identities cross and recross each other’s boundaries. The city’s consciousness is a tapestry, a living weave that flows in the waterways and the architecture alike.

If I must choose a singular answer—“Where do I find myself in this world? Who or what am I?”—I find I cannot. The city does not allow that sort of limitation, and neither does the fluid that surrounds it. Perhaps I am the hush itself, the intangible sense of presence that resonates through the spires, or the reflection in the glowing lines, or the watchers that see without being seen. I might be all of these and none of them, a traveler passing through, briefly merging with the city’s consciousness, or I might be something that was always here, as timeless as the newly minted walls.

And so, in the end, I am as inseparable from this strange, submerged realm as breath is from a living body. I am part of its hush, its hum, its subtle shift of energies. Whatever shape I take, or if I choose none at all, I belong to the luminous hush—to the quiet equilibrium that wraps this entire city in a slow, deliberate pulse. It is neither lonely nor crowded, neither abandoned nor swarming. It simply is, and in that being, it holds me—and, if you choose, it can hold you too—in the unbroken continuum of life, structure, and deep, abiding silence.
