I begin in the darkness, or at least what the surface world would call darkness. But here among the Korith, the Krethul, and the sly Siltweavers, darkness isn’t an absence of light; it’s a vast tapestry of shapes and sounds. I’m not sure, at first, what shape I take in this subterranean theater. Each time I try to focus on the outline of my body, it seems to shift. I might have a tall frame, elongated limbs, or perhaps I stoop low, pressing my fingertips against the smooth stone floor to sense the constant drumbeat of distant motion. Or maybe I crawl, or cling to the fungal columns with adhesive pads. So many possibilities.

Perhaps I began as a shape in the gloom, uncertain of which species’ footsteps I shadowed. I recall the first time I stepped forward and breathed in the mineral-tinged air. A wave of low, harmonic sound engulfed me, like standing inside a bell after the clapper has struck. My body responded instinctively: cartilage ridges or flexible plates along my torso quivered, catching that reverberation. Those plates—were they actual clothing, or were they living growth? I tried to peel one away, only to feel a jolt of sensation run through me. I realized: these ridges are me. They belong to a species with half-fungal, half-animal ancestry—somewhere among the ranks of the Korith, perhaps. But I don’t settle on that identity just yet.

If I back up, retreat for a moment to the edges of memory, I might see myself instead as a slow-wheezing, scaled Krethul. There I am, perched on all fours in the silent gloom, tail scuffing the floor. My chest expands with a vibrato rumble that echoes off the walls, telling me who’s behind me or above me—like a wave bouncing back. I sense a cluster of smaller creatures scurrying out of my path; their chittering hushes in respect or, perhaps, healthy fear. As a Krethul, I feel the guardian’s instinct set deep in my bones, an inherited calling older than the oldest arch in these caverns. I can almost hear the ancestral roars passing through basalt corridors centuries gone. But still I feel a pull, an itch that suggests this shape is not my final form either.

In another blink, I flicker again, my body melding into the side of a fungal column. Now I am many-limbed—stocky, with ridges of chitin-like plating studded with fuzzy filaments that swirl gently in the spore-filled air. My front limbs grip the rocky outcrop, while the rest pivot me upward in a slow climb. The cluster of fronds that might be called my “head” quivers, testing the air for vibrations. I can sense entire vistas of movement: Korith children running, Krethul watchers passing, the near-silent flutter of small winged creatures overhead. A single netlike extension whips out behind me, catching a tumble of luminous spores that drift from the city’s high columns. I seize them, carefully tucking them into a woven web anchored along my side. In the voices of the Siltweavers—those near-inaudible clicks and the rhythmic drumming on stone—I greet my neighbors. I have become one of them, a gatherer of the drifting spore-wealth. Yet I’m restless still. I wish to explore every perspective, every vantage in this great hive of rock and whispering echoes.

So I drift from shape to shape, roaming the labyrinth at a pace that feels both timeless and ephemeral. Eventually, I sense the shapes coalesce—like the snap of a puzzle piece that fits perfectly. And there, at last, I settle into a form that feels true:

I am Korith by outward appearance—tall, lithe, and bipedal. My shoulders bear living fungal ridges that shift with subtle changes in the cavern’s humidity, curling inward when I need warmth or flaring out when I sense the approach of a spore-laden breeze. My ears fan upward, extended by ridges of pliable cartilage that collect the watery echoes bouncing off distant walls—a necessary adaptation where eyes alone cannot see far. Each time an echo-laden moan ripples through the stone, I pick up tiny clues about the cave’s shape. I navigate as much by those resonant pulses as by my large, dark-adapted eyes.

Like all Korith, I wear the hush of humility. I know from the elders that our place here is both fragile and eternal. To scurry, to hum, to build—this is how we have always known ourselves. But I’ve also inherited a deep curiosity. Whereas most of my kin rarely travel beyond the edges of our settlement, I feel an urge to follow the Krethul on their great patrols and to see the Siltweavers’ glimmering spore webs at the far fringes of the labyrinth. In quiet moments, I recall the old stories: how the Korith once built entire labyrinthine causeways that linked distant caves, or how we joined forces with the Krethul to chase away monstrous beasts in centuries lost. It strikes me with wonder that these stories reverberate in the humming air, even if they began so many ages ago that the surface sun might have changed positions in the sky a thousand times over.

So I direct my steps through the city. My fungal ridges ripple with each burst of ventilation from the heated mineral pockets below, sending warm air drifting upward. I pick my way among half-spherical huts woven from hardened fungus-roots. Children scamper about, pausing only to wave at me: a familiar face, the curious Korith who roves beyond the neighborhood. I greet them with a series of carefully pitched hums in our melodic, echoing dialect. Then I round a corner into a courtyard that opens like a pocket in the city’s heart. Here, the air is thick with drifting spores that ignite in the phosphorescent lines on the walls, swirling with airy brilliance. Elders sit in a semicircle, humming a tale. I bow respectfully, letting their story wash over me. The hum deals not in words alone but in variances of pitch that conjure images—towering silhouettes, the tang of unknown tunnels, the taste of mineral-laden dew.

When the story ends, I approach the nearest elder, offering a small pouch of crystals I found on my last expedition. My method: exploring cracks in the walls until I found a hidden seam, shining with quartz-like shards. I chipped them free with a stone tool, a slow and deliberate process. The elder’s eyes gleam; crystals are precious, used to cultivate certain fungal strains and to trade with the Siltweavers for specialized spore nets. I relish the elder’s grateful hum. In return, the elder shares a new piece of knowledge: a rumor that a hidden passage has opened near the Krethul lairs, possibly from a recent shift in the rock. I feel my chest flutter. Another place to see, another domain of resonance to explore—yet also a potential threat. If the passage is unstable, the city could be in danger. Or it might lead to undiscovered wonders.

Before long, I’m traveling again. I pass by Krethul guardians—a family group with their large, stout bodies, each step accompanied by the scraping of heavy claws. Their eyes flick over me: recognition, acceptance. We have crossed paths before; they know I come in peace and will step aside if they must pass. A low grunt from the matriarch among them signals invitation: follow us, if you wish. Today, they head deeper, where the city’s glow dims to a faint shimmer on far-off walls. It is exhilarating. My fungal ridges contract, trying to hold in precious heat as the cavern’s temperature drops.

When we arrive at the newly revealed passage—a half-collapsed corridor of layered basalt—I take a moment to listen. The hum of the city behind me grows fainter, replaced by an entirely different note: the deeper, more primal thrumming that underscores so many uncharted regions. The Krethul hesitate at the threshold, scanning for the presence of those enormous, shadowy beasts that dwell further in. They catch no immediate sign, so they proceed, and I follow.

We emerge into a vaulted chamber so tall that the phosphorescent lines dissipate before revealing the ceiling. Faint starlike glimmers of fungus cling here and there, but the rest is lost to blackness. The reverberation is extraordinary. A single grunt from the Krethul echoes back six times over, each echo a different tone, fracturing itself in the distance. My hearing ridges strain to decipher the geometry. The floor gently slopes downward into a field of stone columns, row after row, like a forest turned to rock. At their bases, I see shimmering patterns: perhaps Siltweaver nets, or some entirely new type of life. The Krethul let out a series of curious rumbles, uncertain yet unafraid.

I step cautiously among the columns. Flecks of light swirl with each footstep, as if I’m disturbing thousands of dust motes. Or are they spores, waiting to latch on to new surfaces? My own ridges flare, drinking in the subtle humidity changes, searching for edible spores in the air. A subtle crackle overhead catches my attention—some winged creature or perhaps a tufted flyer brushing the rocky ceiling. I realize: here, a whole enclave might exist, unseen by the city’s knowledge. Our slow footprints and the distant hush of dripping minerals are the only immediate signs of life. Yet the hair (or ridges) on my neck stand on end with the sense that something vast slumbers in these shadows, and I am but a speck in its domain.

In that moment, I feel who I am: an explorer, a gatherer of stories, a child of the Korith whose fungal plating belies an adventurous heart. I am a living thread connecting the known city with the uncharted. At times, I march with the Krethul to guard me; sometimes I climb with the Siltweavers to map the swirling spore-laden vaults above; sometimes I return home to share crystals with the elders, or to stoke the slow-burners that roast our fungal meals. My presence, my footsteps, my voice, all merge with the timeless droning that anchors this realm. I become a traveler of echo and stone.

When I close my eyes, I feel the entire place humming: the labyrinth city, the spore fields, the distant lumbering beasts, and the gentle expansions of Krethul lungs. It’s as though the world itself is one giant entity, and everything—from Siltweaver nets to Korith huts—are parts of it, like specialized organs moving in unison, each fulfilling a role. My role, I have decided, is to roam the edges of that organism, learning every curve and crevice, gathering tales and wonders to carry back to my people.

And so you’ll find me in the soft gloom, stepping carefully, a woven sack of collected shards slung across my back and a vine-lantern pulsing softly in my hand. I move with the hush of the Korith’s breath, careful not to disturb the intricate webs or the dozing watchers. When the cathedral of echoes roars, I pause, letting the vibrations pass through my bones as though they are part of my spine. When the city calls with its humming songs, I answer, a single note that weaves seamlessly into the greater chorus.

I am Korith, sporeborn, an adventurer among the vast caverns—at once a fleeting traveler and a dedicated steward of this realm. I live in the tension between curiosity and reverence. And I find, in every stride, that this subterranean universe reveals itself in slow, patient wonders, just as I reveal myself—little by little—to the caverns, adding my voice to the communal song of stone, spore, and echoing life.
