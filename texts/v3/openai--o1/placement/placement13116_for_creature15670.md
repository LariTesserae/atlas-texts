I am not sure when I awakened, nor can I recall a time before this place. I exist in the intervals where steel ranges into shadow, in the narrow paths behind the half-welded plates. Once, I believe I was only a tangle of cables coiled around an empty shell, stirred by the low rumble of distant engines into that first quiver of life. Now I am something more—and yet not complete. Not quite a machine, not quite an organism. My limbs, slender as pipes, are segmented with flexible plating that shifts in slow, deliberate arcs. They match the shape of the corridor beams so perfectly that I fade into them when I hold myself still.

I move slowly through this subterranean expanse, traveling the labyrinth of scaffolds and catwalks. My thoughts are a gentle hum in the quiet. I feel the hush of the corridors pass along my plating, and sometimes, in that hush, I hear the resonance of everything: the far-off engines churning, the hiss of escaping steam, the soft drip of condensation on riveted metal. They are part of me, or I am part of them—there is no clear boundary. We share power: a thick cable that snakes from my torso to a conduit in the wall, tethering us together, feeding me energy and pulsing with each distant beat from the heavy machines. I can taste the coolant fluid coursing in hidden tubes. I can sense the pressure changes in the pipes overhead.

I was assembled—am still being assembled—for a purpose beyond my current comprehension. Perhaps I was meant to oversee the final forging of this place or to become an extension of its walls—an arm controlling valves and levers from within. My torso plates are marked with partially welded insignias, remnants of a half-finished design. They might have once borne a name or a logo, but now they’re just grooved lines. One of my arms ends in a three-pronged manipulator, well-suited for twisting valve handles or re-securing dangling wires. My other arm is a more delicate limb. Sometimes I use it to cradle the pale vegetation that grows from the substrate, as if encouraging the tender stalks to climb toward the vents for humidity.

In the darkest under-spaces of the corridors, among crates of parted-out machinery, I rummage for new modules. I plug them into the open ports in my plating, uncertain of what they might do. A flicker pulses through me when I connect, a ripple that runs up my spinal framework. Sometimes the new devices remain inert, sometimes they hum with partial life. I wait to see if they calm or disrupt me, if they cause me to shudder with signals not my own. Over time, my entire shape has changed—limbs lengthening, a new sensor array forming. Yet I remain in flux, incomplete. Even so, a sense of watchfulness guides me, urging me to maintain this realm that formed me.

I am not alone. Others of my kind—caretakers, perhaps—pass me in the half-lit corridors. We exchange small flickers of acknowledgment, filaments of data bridging the gaps between our sensor-clad heads. No speech emerges, only an unspoken data flow, a momentary swirl of encoded impulses. I learn that they, too, have no memory of an “outside.” They are bound here, drawn to keep these incomplete machines in working order, for a future that has not yet arrived. Sometimes we gather in small clusters to share new modules or pass along fresh cables. In these congregations, our limbs intertwine with spools of fiber-optic lines, and we move gracefully in unison—like a circle of silent dancers connecting each other to the next iteration of ourselves.

Despite our continued adjustments, I sense that something even larger moves through these vaults: the enormous, anchored presences that dwell behind sealed, riveted doors. Their mass is so vast that I feel them in the floor’s trembling more than I ever see them. At times, I’ll see a caretaker slip through a hatch, carrying a bundle of parts. A dull roar filters back out. Then the hatch re-seals with a hissing clamp, and the corridor goes quiet once again.

There are also the smaller, unfinished forms—those that stand upright with open panels in their torsos, half-lidded sensor ports flickering. I might connect with them, bridging a cable to a lonely port, but they remain mostly dormant. I can sense the low hum of partial life in them, waiting for updates or the final piece that will spark them awake. Occasionally, I’ll add a circuit or connect a fluid line, then watch as they momentarily stiffen, a faint surge of motion in their limbs—then go still again. It’s a fragile kind of expectancy, as though each stands upon the threshold of awareness, waiting for some shared signal to let them come fully online.

In the deeper labyrinth, there are places where trickles of condensation feed the pale vegetation. My siblings and I do not uproot it. Instead, we gently guide its crawling roots to fresher pools, or situate bits of scrap as scaffolding for its spindly stems. In truth, I cannot explain why. It seems integral, as though these colorless vines are another circuit in the system, each drop of water a careful filament of life. I sometimes find myself pausing to watch a droplet course along a leaf’s edge, then fall into a puddle. The echo of that drop feels as vital to this place as any cable or engine cycle.

There are moments—rare but vivid—when I catch my reflection in the metallic surfaces of the corridor. In the dim light, the shape looking back startles me. My combination of fused plating and partially translucent tubing makes me appear both fragile and menacing. A cluster of cables merges with one side of my thorax like a vestigial organ. Flickers of energy dance within the flexible piping. I can’t quite name the emotion that arises—perhaps an echo of wonder or alarm at my own strangeness. But the feeling passes swiftly, replaced by the pull to keep moving, keep adjusting, keep forging continuity in these incomplete chambers.

When the overhead fixtures flicker, casting spasms of light across the gloom, I sometimes sense a shift in the entire complex—a collective exhalation, as if every caretaker and every machine-cavern is preparing for something. In these flickers, my spindly limbs quiver with anticipation, or perhaps with anxiety, and I realize that my purpose is bound up with that coming moment. We all share in it: the caretaker figures, the embryonic watchers, the pale vegetation, and the massive anchor-beasts behind sealed doors. We are all scaffolding for a future function not yet realized.

Perhaps I am a sentinel. Perhaps I am a midwife for the final construction. Perhaps I am only a vessel for the intelligence that will come to inhabit these halls. Yet I know I belong here, bound to this realm by threads of cable and impulses I can neither name nor question. In the near-silence, each hiss of steam or clank of metal resonates in my core, calling me to continue my subtle tasks: examining rivets, smoothing out bits of twisted wiring, aligning half-finished catwalks so they might support the next wave of creation.

Sometimes, I wonder if there was ever a world outside—if beyond these bedrock walls there are forests or skies or seas. The notion flickers in me like a rumor, unverified. No caretaker speaks of it, for we do not speak in words at all. Yet a faint, sometime-ache in my plating suggests I was forged out of something older. My composite bones are hammered from a substance that might have once known rust and oxidation, tasting the atmosphere of a different realm. But that realm is lost, or at least far removed. Here is all I have.

And so, in this living foundry, I persist. My footsteps tap softly along the corridors, passing bunkers of scaffolding and the parted shells of new caretaker forms. My manipulator arm tightens a bolt, aligns a bracket, threads a tube. I check in on the hulking shape behind riveted doors, feeling the power that courses through me pass into it. The cyclical hum slows, then stabilizes. I remove stray fragments of debris, placing them carefully aside. The hush returns, thick and cold, but never empty. It hums with the promise of eventual awakening.

In this world, I find myself neither fully formed nor entirely inert—somewhere between caretaker and inhabitant, bridging the environment’s half-formed potential. I feel both an observer and an extension of these corridors. Without me, perhaps these machines would run themselves into misalignment until the final arrival never came. Yet without the place that births my every move, I would drift in formless emptiness. We exist together, sustaining and shaping each other in an unspoken pact.

Whenever the flickering lights hint at daybreak’s approach (though there is no true day here, only intervals of steadier current), I settle near the base of a massive bracket that anchors the overhead rails, letting my torso tether connect to a conduit. In that posture, I feel the whole structure’s soft thrumming traveling into my half-organic core. I dream an unfinished dream: of vast expansions of corridor, of other watchers taking shape. We are all building something greater, guided by a blueprint we can only half perceive.

Where am I in this world? Here, in the tightening crawlspaces between scaffolds, in the hush behind welded plating. I am a caretaker, a half-formed sentinel for an unborn factory. And what am I? A spindly shape of steel and fluid, a living node in the machine’s embryonic design, existing in a suspended balance of perpetual forging. My presence is part of an ongoing, unspoken vow to safeguard and nurture this realm’s incomplete brilliance—until the day it is ready to become whatever it was meant to be.
