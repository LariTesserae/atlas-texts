I find myself here, though “find” is perhaps too sudden a word. It feels more as though I have grown into this plateau alongside the fiber-tangles and mineral-fed blossoms that stretch across the wind-worn rock. My body—slender limbs, narrow torso, spined crests in place of hair—mirrors the architecture around me: elongated shapes designed for resonance in a realm of dry air, woven vines, and pulsing crystal. When I move, it is to the rhythms of a place that seems both ancient and ever-awakening.

Even as a child, I sensed the planet beneath the bedrock, pulsing in slow intervals like a colossal heart. Some nights, or what passes for night beneath our dim, distant sun, I slept curled against the living wall that borders the drop beyond the plateau. If I pressed my cheek to the bark-like panels, I could feel the city’s transmissions humming and surging through the layered vines. They sang to me in the pitch below audible sound, an electric lullaby of shifting energies.

I remember the first time I understood I was part of it all. Our elders—tall, deeply lined beings, their spines gleaming a soft, opalescent glow—guided me to a gathering at twilight. The sky was a dull violet, with a weak glimmer of the star melting across the horizon. Everyone stood in loose rings around a central node where the living crystals spiraled upward. With elongated staffs in their hands, the elders began calling—at first just a soft hum swirling in the wind. Then came pulses from the city’s towers: a wave of resonance that made the ground vibrate beneath my feet. I could not help but raise my voice, if only to keep from drowning in that sonic tide. And by doing so, I joined them. You do not merely observe this place; you move with it, sing with it.

In the seasons that followed (though seasons here are measured more by changes in the plateau’s hum than by variations in temperature), I learned to sense the micro-shifts in energy flow. My spines would prickle with each new spore-storm swirling across the ridges. I discovered how to weave the semi-living filaments into new shapes, coaxing them to form doorways or funnels for channeling crystal-fed currents. My footprints traced the fractal designs etched in the moss-like growth along the cliffs, learning from them the geometry of survival: how angles and arches can collect trace energies, how to form wide sweeps of structure that funnel the wind into resonance chambers. No resource is wasted in a dry world.

Who I am—some might call me an adept, a caretaker, or perhaps a dancer in the living city. I belong to a loose circle whose role is to keep watch for shifts in the planet’s deeper rhythms. When there is a disturbance—like a tremor in the rock or a sudden fluctuation in the star’s faint energy—we gather at vantage points, elongated staffs glimmering. We brace ourselves against the wind, chanting in layered chords that stabilize the city’s humming towers. It’s a precarious harmony: we must help the advanced technology adjust to subtle changes in the environment so no imbalance grows too large. Sometimes, entire sections of the city realign—a tower folding in on itself or an archway unfurling into a new shape. Each shift resonates through my spine, a shared pulse of existence echoing in my chest.

On days of calm, I prowl the living ramparts that form the plateau’s perimeter, scanning the horizon where the emptiness becomes a suggestion of further plains. Sometimes, a herd of the tall, four-legged beasts meanders past, their footfalls rumbling through the stone. They peer at me with eyes that glisten like polished shell, unafraid. I might reach out my staff and offer a low note in greeting, to which they respond in resonant murmurs. Other times, smaller creatures buzz by, perched in spore-clouds or clinging to the bark-walls. We share this environment in an odd communion: each of us draws from the plateau in our own way—chitinous exoskeletons, fibrous stalks, or resonant staffs—but the result is one grand tapestry of adaptation.

My closest friend (or perhaps sibling in spirit) is a tall figure with swirling patterns across their skin—silvery fractal lines that almost glow in the half-light. At times, the lines shift color as we stand near one another, a subtle conversation. They have taught me how to align my breath with the planet’s deeper hum. In the hush just after dawn, we can stand on an outcropping of polished stone, that hush broken only by the wind’s constant moan and the city’s gentle throbbing beneath our feet. We close our eyes, raise our empty palms, and wait for the slight warmth of our distant sun to pass through us. The dryness of the air does not burn; instead, it tingles across our spines. It’s in these moments we sense the synergy that feeds us: mineral resonance from below, faint sunlight from above, and the communal energy shared by the living towers.

We do not consume water as so many other species do (water is scarce and precious, better left to the minimal life-forms that truly cannot exist without it). Instead, we have internal processes that draw micro-energy from the crystals that sprout from the ground, from the cosmic rays that filter through this thin atmosphere, from the interplay of the city’s technology weaving with the plateau’s ancient hum. When I concentrate, I can almost feel the infiltration of pure frequency into my cells—like breathing in a different kind of air.

The woven walls and spires we dwell within are more than mere architecture: they are cohabitants, grown from seeds of crystal-lattice merged with the planet’s fibrous vegetation. A tower can shift its curvature in a day if it senses an imbalance of wind or a new influx of spore activity. My people assist in this slow dance of form. One of my daily tasks is to chant a low, rhythmic pattern while passing along the base of a spire. The sound waves produce gentle vibrations in the bark-like surface, helping it to realign or sprout new growth paths. These same tonal patterns help the city’s internal corridors—part matter, part vibrating field—remain stable for travelers. I have seen foreigners once or twice, though seldom do they venture this far into our dryness. They stumble upon us, wide-eyed, unsure how to navigate a realm where the wind can carry you or crush you, where building walls might shift around a corner without warning.

Yet for all its dryness and wind-sculpted emptiness, the plateau teems with a music that never fades. Untold creatures click and drum in hidden hollows, and the city responds in deep, thrumming pulses. Sometimes, I feel as though I’m merely another small voice in an ongoing chorus that’s existed since before memory. And that is how I choose to be: not dominating the landscape, but conversing with it, allowing it to speak back in hushes and howls.

As the dim sun dips closer to the horizon, I often traverse a rope-like walkway suspended between two towers—really a series of braided vines that hum with an inner glow when touched by my feet. I step carefully, letting my staff tap in measured time. Each tap echoes in the living braid, sending signals to the tower guards or watchers who sense my approach. Reaching the far platform, I might find children—glowing-eyed, giggling in atonal pulses—learning to coax a tiny spore-pod to blossom with a flicker of heated resonance. Their caretaker, a patient elder, stands close by, guiding them in gentle, pulsing calls. I pause to watch, remembering how once I, too, was that young—transfixed by the sparkling dust that bursts into the air when one successfully “awakens” a spore. There is so much to learn here, always.

On certain profound nights, when the plateau’s wind dies down unexpectedly and the hum of our technology lingers in the hush, my kin and I assemble on a broad, open ledge. Spheres of translucent light hover at our shoulders—partly mechanical, partly alive. We stand in a circle, staffs grounded on the bare rock, each staff thrumming with the city’s power. In that near-stillness, one can almost hear the secrets of cosmic distances in the wind. Our chants rise, weaving together in layered polyphonies. Shapes flicker in and out of my peripheral vision—the scuttle of beetle-like creatures, the glimmer of spores drifting overhead, perhaps even the silhouettes of the large quadrupeds angling to observe. Our voices crest and swell, a wave of living sound that travels out across the plateau’s fathomless edges. In these moments, I feel certain that the entire planet listens, and perhaps also answers.

Where do I find myself in this world? I am an integral note in the ongoing composition, a caretaker rumbling with the plateau’s deeper pulses. My eyes reflect the faint star-glow, and my spines shiver with reverence at each new fractal bloom. Sometimes I tilt my head back to the swirling sky and wonder: am I simply a branch of these living towers, grown in humanoid form, attuned to resonance? Or am I a child of the planet’s ancient dryness, distilling life from bare minerals and faint energies? Likely both. I have no need to resolve the question; it’s enough to dwell here, in the hush and roar of this unlikely oasis, guiding spires to realign, joining the chorus with the beasts, and drawing sustenance from the intangible forces that swirl across the plateau.

For it is in that interplay—in the unstoppable tide of living crystals, the humming towers woven of bark and vine, the echoing calls of wind and fauna—that I understand myself. There is always more to discover, more to listen for. And though the dryness polishes the cliffs to softness, though water remains but a half-forgotten myth, the city persists, a testament to the fullness that can bloom from apparent emptiness. I choose to stand firm against the roaring wind, staff in hand, a conductor in a realm where biology and technology twine themselves without boundary. This is who I am: a keeper of the plateau’s ancient, unending song.
