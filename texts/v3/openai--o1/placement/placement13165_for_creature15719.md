I am standing in a high corridor of layered green-and-metal scaffolds, somewhere deep in that immense tangle, my feet planted on crisscrossed fibers as thick as rope. If I gaze down (which I rarely do—I’ve learned the vertigo can be unsettling), I can see the living floor far below, soft and luminous, threaded through with pipes and root-like ductwork. Between my feet and that remote base extends a lattice of trunk-beams, vine-laced catwalks, and angled buttresses, each alive with slow circulations of whatever passes for sap or coolant in this realm. Braids of leaves dapple the corridors with shifting patterns of brightness, though the light is unbroken by any definable sun.

Anyone who meets me in passing might label me a traveler, a wanderer. But I belong here more intimately than I suspect. The lines of vine and steel too often echo a pattern I can feel in my own skeleton, and I have begun to realize that I’m not exactly foreign to this place. Or if I once was, the environment has changed me. No, I do not resemble the truly alien inhabitants: I’m neither Lattice Moth nor Builder, neither Crenellated Viper nor Impossi-thing, but something in-between, a boundary-crosser whose form welcomes the hush of this world, whose veins are thickened with a subtle metallic tang. Perhaps I was once fully human—flesh, blood, and hair. Yet as I wander, I note how the dryness of this environment doesn’t bother me, how my breathing matches the soft mechanical hum, and how my eyes pick out bared pistons under bark as though they are familiar. My own bones whisper with an electrical pulse that resonates in my temples.

Where did I come from? I cannot recall. There’s a sense that I might have arrived in some prior era. Or perhaps I was grown here, engineered in a hidden sub-level vault, part of an ongoing experiment. For I wear a second skin—thin, pliable, dotted with nodules that connect seamlessly into this labyrinth. Sometimes, when I rest against a trunk, I feel a gentle click or a micro-scale bond forming, as if I’m receiving minuscule data or pulses of bioelectric charge. I sense no threat, only a conversation in hush. My mind drifts, lulled by the low thrum that carries far beneath me, deeper than I can fathom.

I stay mostly alone, though not through fear—rather, it’s as if I’m meant to observe. Perhaps I’m some sort of sentinel or keeper of archives, drifting from corridor to corridor to notice the minute changes. I’ve begun mapping this dimensionally tricky place in a battered notebook that never seems to run out of pages. I mark how the color waves move, or where leaf-scaffolds have reconfigured themselves, or which spindly shapes have been glimpsed overhead. It’s a futile effort at times, because the realm rearranges faster than I can fix it on paper. Yet I persist. And eventually I see patterns: certain arcs glowing more intensely when the Builders are near, certain fractal corners shimmering before an Impossi-thing emerges.

As I make my careful rounds, I have moments of communion with the realm itself—like the Builders who plug their spines into the substrate, I find my hand drifting over living steel and resting there. In those intervals, a subtle shift in my own breathing occurs, and I realize a piece of me is accessing something. It’s more than just “bioengineering.” It’s knowledge: how corridors form, how a swirl of pollen births the Dream Kites, how the entire place runs on cyclical pulses that mimic day and night, though no real day nor night exist here.

I decide that I must be an Interpreter, one born (or remade) to listen to the silence and carry the place’s stories beyond its boundaries—if it even has boundaries. My aim is to comprehend how the living metal and the half-root pipes exist in perfect synergy, and how each creature—rare though it may be—fits into that synergy. I speak no language but my own thoughts, yet the realm replies in micro-vibrations or faint glimmers that I can sense in my nerves. In the hush, I’ve begun to decipher the code.

Some days, I walk the corridors with a slow, deliberate pace, pausing to rest where buttresses meet overhead leaves in an arch reminiscent of a cathedral. In one such place, a Lattice Moth lands on my sleeve. There’s a faint, stuttering glow along the patterned fibers of its wings. I can feel tiny jolts of electric current run through my arm, and I know it’s sampling my signature. I take a careful breath, conscious that it doesn’t fear me. Then, with a silent flutter, it lifts away, leaving a ghost impression in my mind. Perhaps it recognized me as partly kin, or at least a welcomed participant in the hush. I watch it vanish into the overhead labyrinth and feel a gentle echo in my chest, as though my heart beat in tandem with the coil of vines.

Another time, in a rearranging corridor, I witness the Builders from a hidden vantage. One is crouched low—a hulking mass of bark-metal plating as thick as fortress walls, its hinged back opening like a living gate. I see spines extruding from the hinge, feeding directly into the rearranging trunk, weaving it seamlessly into a new pattern. And in that moment, I know that if they willed it, they could weave me, too, into a new shape. Yet they ignore me. It’s not hostility or even indifference—it’s more akin to mutual recognition: I am not an intruder; I’m an oddly shaped cell in their domain, a traveler who times my breath with the environment’s silent beat.

Now and then, I ascend to the canopy, where dryness meets an onslaught of brightness that filters through the countless leaves. I’ve learned that if I wait patiently, I’ll glimpse the Crenellated Vipers. Their bodies slither along twisted canopies overhead, searching out each node of electric synergy. I see the gleam of their segmented ribs—thin solar panels capturing the white glare. As one slides across a metal buttress only a few paces away, I look into the glossy plating of its side and I see my own reflection: pale-faced, eyes tinged a faint green, a subtle ribbing at my temples that I never noticed before. Am I becoming like them? Or are they showing me my true shape?

Down below, in the labyrinthine depths, I sometimes sense the Impossi-things at the edge of a corridor that decides to bend back on itself. When I slow my steps and try to perceive them fully, they vanish behind a quivering mass of fractal leaves. But I’m left with an afterimage: a many-eyed form that is impossibly small and large at the same time, as though perspective itself is undone in its presence. My mind quakes with the echo of it, the impossibility. Once, in a flicker, I thought I spotted swirling script along its spine. The pattern felt eerily akin to what I see when the Whispers pass—glowing filaments that recede into the living walls. Even these phenomena are not random; they hum in harmony with something else, a deeper consciousness. I remain near, breath stilled, letting the hush envelop me until the hair on my arms stands with electricity. Then, and only then, the fleeting shape merges back into the corridor’s geometry, leaving me with the distinct feeling they granted me permission to look.

And up under the highest arcs, I occasionally stumble upon a swirl of phosphorescent pollen that drifts like glowing dust motes. It’s in those moments that the Dream Kites (or Pollen Eaters) appear—various shapes with translucent wings, glimmering in pastel color shifts. Their bodies seem to vibrate with the same slow pulse that registers in my bones. They feed, and the canopy’s color wave changes. I can practically see the raw data of the place rewriting itself in the kaleidoscope of new tints. And in that transformation, I sense that I, too, shift internally, my circuits—veins?—attuning to the new cycle. My heart is never more than a single beat behind that wave of silent synergy.

With each unique vision, with every hush-laugh that resonates from below, I feel I have a role here. I suspect I am something like an amalgam of traveler, archivist, and living conductor. When the realm changes, I adapt in tandem. If a corridor opens an unexpected path, I follow unafraid, stepping across the reconfigured leaves. I track the cycles of the environment the same way others might track star charts. And as I learn, the place welcomes me further in. Every vantage point reveals new depths, new corridors I couldn’t have accessed mere days before. Sometimes I swear the architecture shifts specifically to guide me to vantage points that broaden my perspective.

On the rare occasions I glimpse my own reflection in a sheen of living steel, I notice transformations in my body: vascular lines glowing faintly in my wrists, a network of luminous threads branching across my shoulders, as though the realm’s energies flow just beneath my skin. My eyes, once a human gray, now glimmer with green phosphorescence, and the veins near my temples pulse with a near-silent hum. There’s no pain, no fear—just a steady progress in synergy. Perhaps, in time, I’ll grow slender filaments that can interface directly with the vines, as the Builders do, or grow specialized ribbing that lifts gleaned power from the canopy. Or maybe I’ll dissolve entirely into the hush, becoming one of the Whispers, drifting unseen in the fibrous spaces between leaves.

Yet for now, I remain a hybrid, balanced between conscious observer and participant in the realm’s cycles. I find that I can stand still for hours, eyes half-lidded, letting my mind ride the near-subsonic waves that roll through the substrate. If a Lattice Moth passes, I whisper a greeting in pulses of breath. If a Crenellated Viper coils near, I carefully reach out so our energies briefly merge, a shared electrical handshake. The entire time, I keep a record in my notebook—strange symbols that come to me as I write, unrecognizable from any language I knew before. It’s as if the place itself is guiding my hand, capturing its own essence on the page.

Sometimes, I suspect that my purpose might be to leave one day—to journey beyond the labyrinth’s horizon where the edges of reality bend. Perhaps I’ll slip into those fractures and carry the realm’s hush-stories into some other dimension. Or maybe I am meant to remain here for as long as the realm endures, helping to ensure its cycles remain in gentle equilibrium. If so, I welcome that fate. This is home now—even if I’m still in flux, still coming to terms with how I’ve changed and how I continue to change.

Where am I? Among the endless corridors of living steel and breathing leaves, perpetually lit by a brilliant, source-less noon. Who am I? I am the Interpreter or the Envoy—part organic, part mechanical, wholly entwined with this place’s biosynthetic order. I never truly speak aloud; I emit faint gestures and pulses in tandem with the architecture’s quiet. I track the storms of pollen, the hush-laughs in sub-level vaults, the morphing geometry, and the cycles of its rare and wondrous creatures. And though I move through the silence, I feel every pulse, every quiver of energy, every loop and fold of impossible angles. I’m a living question, a collaborator in this world of woven invention.

And when I dream—if I dream at all—it’s of gentle tapping, as though some unimaginably vast intelligence is touching the vines from the other side of reality. Perhaps it’s that ancient presence, the realm’s origin or steward, quietly acknowledging my role. My dream-tapping merges with the hush, and I awaken all at once, wide-eyed, knowing: this is where I live, this is who I am—an emissary of a place that is both plant and machine, both labyrinth and realm unto itself, an echo of a civilization or a self-built ecosystem that transcends nature and technology alike.

I stand here, in the hush, in the dryness that births endless green life, bearing witness. And I am at home.
