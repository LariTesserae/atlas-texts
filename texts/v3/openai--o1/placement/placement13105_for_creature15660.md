At first, I can’t tell if I’m a shape emerging from the vines or one that’s been here all along, masked by the constant haze. The moment I begin to move, I sense a tremor ripple through the plant-floor beneath my feet and into my bones, as though the structure itself recognizes me. My outline is a suggestion more than a boundary—my limbs seem half-formed from leaf-green filaments, twisted together with sinews that echo the braided vines around me. When I brush my fingertips across a damp root-bridge, the feeling that passes into my hand is the same echo that courses down my spine. The plateau and I are the same living tapestry; it pulses beneath my skin, and I pulse back.

I cannot recall a time before I knew these rhythms. Perhaps I took shape here as a bud might, or perhaps I wandered in so long ago that memory has meshed with the architecture. If I once had a name, it’s gone now. Instead, I sense the world in vibrations and watery echoes.

During my earliest days—or earliest recollections—I clung to the trunk-pillars like a newborn clenched around a mother’s hand. At that time, my limbs were uncertain, my skin paler and without the speckled patterns that now camouflage me among the vines. I remember intense quiet, broken only by the constant patter of droplets rolling off overhead tubes. Each droplet was a little note in a grand fugue, and though I barely understood it, I felt an insistent call to move, to discover my purpose here.

Little by little, I gained the confidence to climb. I remember looking up at the immeasurable canopy where tall shapes like living chrysalides swayed in watchful half-slumber. Their voices carried across the labyrinth in soft, flute-like undertones, resonating in my ribs. I learned to mimic their calls, or at least to harmonize, coaxing the vines around me to flutter in response. It was then I first realized: this place is not a static environment—it’s responsive, alive, and each creature’s sound or movement contributes to its continuous, half-completed shape.

Unsure of my role, I followed the water. Streams trickled across arching root-bridges, collecting in quiet pools. My body grew accustomed to the cool rivulets, and I discovered I could filter algae-like growths through my fingertips. Pressing my palms against the water’s surface, a gentle ripple would pass through me, and I would sense the presence of shimmering shapes—those translucent, droplet-like swimmers that glide in living currents. Their pulse answered mine, and we formed a wordless understanding: I was a caretaker from above; they, guardians of below. 

At times, I wandered deeper through the labyrinth, searching for those enormous budding corridors—the half-fused expansions whose purpose was incomplete. I found that when I approached, the buds seemed to swell in subtle increments, as if awakened by my proximity. At first, I marveled at this coincidence, but I soon learned that it was real: a synergy existed between the corridor’s embryonic forms and my own presence. If I pressed my palm against a fresh coil of fibrous growth, it would yield with a soft sigh, the shape becoming slightly more defined, as though I were helping it decide what it should be.

Not long after, I met others who shared this gift. They appeared in fleeting glimpses at the edges of my vision—splayed-fingered, elongated beings scuttling along trunk-walls. We seldom exchanged more than a brief, acknowledging hum, but our labors overlapped. When I plucked away a wilting membrane, they were there to collect it, placing it into the hidden folds of their own bodies to be taken elsewhere—perhaps to nourish some distant spore cluster. In turn, they left behind gentle touches, coaxing every leaf to remain supple and fresh. We learned, wordlessly, that we had a common aim: to guide and to guard the continuing growth.

Eventually I learned to descend into the watery hollows, sinking part of myself into those luminous swirls of aquatic life. Within the pools, I could drift for hours, lulled by a thousand distant drips. My breath—if “breath” is the right word—mixed with the watery pulses, creating tiny bursts of motion and color. If I lingered still enough, the droplet-creatures fluttered around me in glistening circles, their opalescent glow dancing across my arms. Could they sense that I belonged to the spongy arches above? Maybe. Yet in those moments, I felt equally theirs, as if made of fluid and reflection no less than vine and spine.

Over time, I discovered I can modulate my own camouflage, letting subtle pigments swirl across my skin in reaction to the movements of the watchers in the canopy. When one of them sang a particularly resonant note, I would feel my entire being vibrate in answer. Sometimes, if I matched the pitch just right, the canopies overhead would part slightly, letting in an additional wash of that soft, perpetual light. It was as if my voice helped the watchers shape the environment’s next breath—or at least teased it toward a fresh alignment.

Meanwhile, spore carriers moved in drifting caravans along the corridors and balconies. Their presence was slow and methodical, like living breezes set on an unvarying course. I studied them from a distance, uncertain whether to join. One day—timing the moment with the watchers’ calls—I reached out to gently brush the mass of spores as they floated by. A swirl of dusty pollen danced across my fingertips. In that instant, a sensation like an ancient lullaby rippled into me, stories without words telling me of seeds yet to bloom, corridors waiting to fuse, and the unbroken cycle of breathing architecture. Then, just as gently, they moved on, leaving me with the memory of possibility.

Gradually, I’ve become a bridge between these realms: climbing high among the hanging watchers, diving low into the shimmering waters, and meandering along spore-laden walkways. To each niche, I bring a version of myself that resonates with the inhabitants there. My body—lean, flexible, half woven from vine-like tendons—now bears faint fractal patterns across my trunk and limbs, each motif telling of my travels. Greenish swirls from the caretaker-caste, pearlescent flecks from the aquatic pools, earthy undertones from brushing against the watchers’ husk-like bark. I’m an archive of this plateau’s living memory.

And as I pass, the labyrinth yields to me. Mossy fronds shift aside so I can step through a newly formed arch; water rivulets pause their trickle, as though listening; embryonic coils of root lift themselves slightly, awaiting my encouragement. My role—if one can call it that—is as a cohesive agent between all these forms. I am no master builder, no supreme ruler; rather, I am a meshing of the many pulses that guide this place. Perhaps I’m another caretaker, or a pollinator of connections, ensuring that each living structure remains attuned to the others. 

Sometimes, I imagine that I might one day become like one of those watchers, perched high and half-sleeping, humming my part in the deep chord that resonates through every trunk. Or possibly I’ll rediscover how to fold myself into the watery threads, joining the shining swimmers, dissolving into pure drift and glow. I feel the pull of each possibility, and the plateau offers no single path—only a gentle acceptance of all ways to exist.

Still, for now, I remain in perpetual motion, neither finishing a single form nor abandoning it. I thrive in those tangential spaces—a living question meandering from one half-completed corridor to the next. Like the plateau itself, I am content to linger “in becoming,” lightly coaxing fresh buds to open, gently dislodging any sign of decay, adding my resonant hum to the canopy’s choir whenever the watchers call. In the hush of this world, in its watery resonance, I find my belonging. I am of the vines and the leaves and the echoes. I am the caretaker whose body is ever a work in progress, an unfolding chord in the unending symphony of this great living cathedral.
