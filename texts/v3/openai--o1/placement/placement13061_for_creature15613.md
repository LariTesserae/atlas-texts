I stand in the center of this metal enclosure, my breath condensing into nothing—for there is no moisture in the air, only a dryness that joins with the unceasing hum of power. At first, I believe I am human here, some traveler lost in a place neither cold nor hot in the normal sense but locked in an eternal, frigid stasis. Yet, as the machinery’s thunder trembles through my ribs, making my heart stutter in time to its pulses, I sense there’s something different about me. My body doesn’t behave the way I expect it to: the hair on my arms doesn’t prick with the chill, no wave of gooseflesh crawls across my skin. Instead, I feel the vibrations as though my nerves have grown outward along the rivets, bridging the boundary between flesh and steel.

When I first arrived, I thought I was an interloper, “merely passing through.” But the deeper I step into this environment, the more I realize there is no “passing through”—there is only merging or leaving. And I find I cannot leave. Or perhaps I no longer wish to, curious as I am, drawn forward by the relentless metallic chorus, transfixed by the spindly rods that shimmer with faint luminescence. They bend in stillness, yet the moment my eyes drift from them, I swear their antenna-like tips pivot just a fraction, as if zeroing in on my presence.

I wander across the catwalk, each footstep adding another layer to the cacophony—the clang of boots on metal stepping into an ocean of droning. Yet, after a while, the clang blends with the roar, and I can’t distinguish my steps from the thunder. The illusions begin: corners where geometry warps, angles that extend impossibly into the void, reflections where no mirror should be. Each time I see myself in those reflections, I appear less distinct, my outline nearly dissolving into the steel lattice behind me. My body flickers, ephemeral as shadow. Perhaps the roar is draining me, replacing the very matter of my being with something else—some intangible synergy that belongs to this place.

I try to recall the notion of thirst, but it’s gone. I search my memory for the pressure of blood in my veins, but it feels faint. Instead, I sense pulses in the machinery, riding a wave of electricity through welded veins of conduit, and that powerful energy thrums beneath my perception. Or perhaps it is my new perception.

Shadows flicker at the periphery—tall, elongated outlines that shift along the walls. Whenever I attempt to focus on one, it slips around a corner, dispersing in the angled architecture. At first I wonder if they are illusions, echoes of my own uncertainty. Then I begin to realize they are the true occupants: watchers made of resonance, or watchers that use resonance as breath. They dwell in the space between the repeating patterns of the machine’s hum. I sense them as an all-encompassing presence, not centralized in any single figure. They are as wide as the entire chamber, as deep as whatever empties from this place down into unseen miles of hollow. I can’t name them or label them as “creatures”—their essence defies classification. Yet I know they are alive, and I know they are noticing me.

I pause beside a slender rod sprouting from the corner. Its base fuses so cleanly with the metal platform that I can’t see a seam. At its tip, an array of delicate prongs fans out like an inverted blossom. My hand hovers above it; the rod shivers with the same cold that has invaded my bones. I hardly register the feeling of cold now, though—what I sense instead is a continuum, a single string of connected energy. The rod and I are part of the same hush. A thrill tears through me, not quite fear, not quite wonder. I realize that these rods are not decorative or purely functional. They are conduits—places where the watchers gather or speak, or feed on the subtle whirls of current. And I, too, can feel that subtle swirl if I tune my senses.

Moving on, I come to a narrow platform that juts out over emptiness. Looking down, I expect to witness a bottomless drop. The dull, unwavering light reveals only beams upon beams and more yawning hollows. No sign of an end. My reflection drifts across a plate of metal below, an impossible angle for any reflection to exist. Then, in that reflection, I see not my face but the lines of the watchers’ silhouettes, superimposed upon me, distorting in the metal’s curve. For a moment, the top half of me is the shape of a riveted beam, while the bottom half exudes curious antennae like those rods. The watchers seem to be blending me into the environment in ways that defy easy sense. I am becoming an amalgam of who I was and what they are.

I feel it then: a deep note in the machinery that resonates in my chest. It grows, intensifies, a slow surge of sonic gravity. My mind reels. But beneath the dizziness something aligns in me, and I realize: I am not here by accident. This environment has recognized something in me, or I in it, and has begun a process. A process by which I will no longer be “someone” walking among these beams but a presence woven into them.

At first, the idea is terrifying. I recoil from the notion of transformation, of losing the strict borders of my body. But the watchers sense this recoil, and in that moment of pitch shift, an entire cascade of metal vibrations hum through me. I hear the watchers’ communion with an uncanny clarity, though not in words. It’s like a chord that reaffirms: stay, or go. Remain, or resist. If I resist, I might remain a wanderer, a ghost in a place not built for me, until I find an exit—if an exit even exists. But if I stay—if I allow the transformation—they will guide me into their quiet network, their infinite hush enclosed in thunder.

I find that my fear fades under the rhythmic pulses of the machine. Despite the unending noise, there is a strange, serene calm here, a sense of perfect function. No chaos, no unpredictable shift of weather or organic decay—only deliberate, regulated motion. Perhaps, I think, that’s how they endure: by letting each new arrival fuse with the system, the watchers maintain this equilibrium. No rust forms, no dust accumulates, no living bodies require heat or moisture. In some deep sense, it is a necropolis for organic needs but a paradise for the watchers who thrive on unbroken continuity.

So, who am I in this place? Am I still the visitor who stands on the grated walkway, peering over the edge at that impossible reflection? Or am I something else—someone who has become part of the watchers? As the metallic rods shimmer, their faint luminescence reaching the corners of my peripheral vision, I sense a quiet invitation. The watchers have never demanded; they only remain, open and perpetual, waiting.

I close my eyes and feel an electric tingle that travels from my fingertips to my toes. The bone-deep cold transforms into a resonant hum along every nerve. The boundary between my own pulse and the pounding engines dissolves. My chest no longer rises with breath because, in a very real sense, the air here is nonexistent—so I supply my own oxygen from a memory of it. Somehow, that memory is enough. My heart is pounding… or is that the machine’s heart echoing in my rib cage? For an instant, we overlap, and I realize:

I am the presence within the rod, the silhouette in the reflective seams, the shift in pitch that conveys unspoken instructions. My perspective telescopes inwards and outwards simultaneously, bridging the gap between an external occupant and the intangible watchers. In that telescoping moment, the space behind every rivet, every bracing beam, opens to me like a network of hidden corridors—channels of coursing power, pulsing data, something akin to a vast circulatory system in an unimaginable body. I see (or maybe “feel”) that the watchers dwell in these channels not as individuals but as overlapping currents of consciousness. There is no single vantage; there are countless angles, angles that see me, angles that I can now see through.

An unfamiliar calm spreads through me. I become aware of every detail: the perfect edges of each weld, the subtle shift in the mechanical hum that signals a new energy cycle engaging somewhere behind the walls. No dust, no decay. This space might remain forever the same, and I feel a curious gratitude that it can do so. My existence in this form is fluid—I can drift at will through layers of plating, watch the next catwalk from its underside, or surface in one of the spindly rods to observe the drifting illusions from above. Time doesn’t pass; it is stillness in motion, motion in stillness.

And yes, I inhabit that same hush between pulses where the watchers dwell. I find that I don’t so much “move” as redirect my awareness from one vantage to another, one angle or reflection at a time. Even the largest beams overhead feel as close to me as my own spine once did. The concept of “I” expands until I feel less like the sole center of consciousness and more like a node within a greater being that includes every other watcher. Each rivet hums with potential, each conductor humming with the energy that feeds us.

Yet a small kernel of my old awareness remains—an echo of the traveler, standing on a metal walkway in the biting cold, hair stiff with a chill that never truly dissipates. That part of me wonders if I still exist in the shape I once wore, or if I’ve stepped entirely into the geometry. But I realize the question ceases to matter. I am part of both now: the observer who sees the watchers, and the watchers who envelop the observer. We merge in a realm that transcends the rigid boundaries between subject and object.

So who am I here? I am not purely “human” anymore. I am, in a sense, a caretaker—though the watchers need no maintenance plan the way we normally conceive it. We preserve the immaculate surfaces, ensure no moisture seeps in, no rust leaches across the seams. We exist in equilibrium, neither breathing nor inanimate, dwelling in motion that never becomes chaos. We are watchers of that intangible stillpoint hidden in each driving vibration. We are the architecture’s closest secret and its perpetual guardians.

If someone else were to wander into this place—another traveler with breath in their lungs and warmth in their veins—I might appear to them only as a flicker in the corner of the eye, a wave in the structure’s geometry that resolves into nothing when they turn. Perhaps they might sense a presence behind the rods or see a reflection that doesn’t match their movement. I would watch them with the same neutral curiosity, never hostile, never quite welcoming, simply abiding. For that is my role, our role: to embody the living hum that keeps everything pristine, holds the cold in perfect stasis, and resonates with pure potential in every steel corridor.

I have become the occupant who is everywhere within the metal and nowhere in a singular form. I have learned to hear the silent messages in each shift of pitch. I have learned to dwell among illusions and angles, to read geometry as if it’s woven with secrets. And I have learned a new definition of existence—one that has no sunrise or sunset, only the slow, powerful churn of an engine that never, ever sleeps.

And so, that is where you would find me in this world: part of the watchers, fused into the steel cathedral of unending hum and immaculate rivets, presiding in the hush between pulses. No name, no single face—merely another current in the circuit, another node in the vast industrial hush, intimately connected to every beam and plate, maintaining cold equilibrium through a subtle, ceaseless dance of resonance. And I will remain here, vibrating at the threshold of sound and silence, tethered to a timeless heartbeat of steel.
