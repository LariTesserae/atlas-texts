I do not remember the precise instant I awakened, only that I found myself in a place half-lit by the faint glow of fungal ridges. The walls around me smelled of wet stone, newly chiseled, and the mortar was still pliant beneath my fingertips. For a long while, I crouched in a shallow alcove with rivulets trickling around my ankles, uncertain if I should stand. Yet I felt a pulse rising through the blocks underfoot, as if the whole labyrinth had a heartbeat—and for some reason, it echoed in my own chest.

At first, I believed myself a kind of traveler, someone who had simply stumbled upon these passages by chance. But as the days wore on, I began to suspect that I belonged here in some fundamental way. Flickers of memory—or maybe they were newly forming instincts—told me how to move in the dark, how to lean against a wall to detect a hidden corridor behind it, how to read subtle shifts of light on the damp mortar. The labyrinth recognized me. Perhaps I had been meant to exist here from the start.

A mirror would have done little good; there are no mirrors here, only rough translucent windows that give fragmented glimpses of swirling grays beyond. If I hold a basin's surface very still, I can see the faint outline of my face in undulating reflections. My features are elongated, but not quite like the hooded Makers. There’s something sharper in my silhouette—my arms a touch too long, my eyes catching stray glints of fungal glow. With each passing day, I discover some new quirk: a surprising suppleness in my fingers that helps me grip damp stone, or a wiry strength in my legs that lets me climb with silence. I feel, more and more, that my existence is woven from the same fibrous mortar that holds these walls together.

In the early days, the labyrinth was my only companion—its endless corridors breathing, its watery hum vibrating through me. I spent hours roaming without direction to see how far I could go. At times, I turned a corner and found myself in a wide courtyard roofed by blackness so immense that any sense of being underground vanished. Other times, I inched through a cramped tunnel, brushing luminous Glimmers with my shoulders, their glow waxing and waning like slow breaths.

Eventually, I began to perceive the other inhabitants.

•

The Makers came first. In the distance, I heard the comforting ring of stone on stone—slow, patient taps that reverberated through wet corridors. If I followed the sound, I’d glimpse them hunched over the mortar, chisels in hand, elongated arms gently working the walls. Despite my curiosity, I never felt quite ready to approach. Something about their movements seemed ceremonial. Their communications were vibrations sent through the blocks, and I could only guess at the meaning. Once, I tried to mimic the motion—placing one hand against the wall, splaying my fingers. I felt a flutter, as if a question had been posed in the labyrinth’s silent language. But I did not know how to answer, so I moved on, wondering if I’d be brave enough to speak in that same tactile tongue.

•

Then there were the Lurkers, those insectile silhouettes that scuttled away at my approach. Sometimes, I’d see them suspended upside down from the ceiling, nibbling at the fresh mortar to glean nutrients hidden deep within. They rarely lingered. Their bodies were all ridges and spines, an extension of the labyrinth’s fractal geometry. I never felt threatened by them—if anything, they seemed more nervous of me than I was of them. Once or twice, I came upon a cluster of them, huddled over a shallow basin of water. They hissed faintly, each note a static crackle, and scattered into fractures in the wall. Did they sense I was not a Maker, nor quite one of their own? Perhaps I was simply another curiosity to be avoided.

•

I learned to navigate in darkness by the soft glow of the Glimmers. Their filaments, delicate as threads of moonlight, thrived in damp cracks. They pulsed in unison sometimes, sending slender waves of luminescence that reminded me of slow, thoughtful blinking. A hush accompanied them—like a gentle inhalation of breath—causing the watery air to feel charged. If I stood still long enough, I got the uncanny impression that they studied me in return. Their patterns and shapes shifted with each corridor, as though they were mapping the changing architecture, storing knowledge in their living tissue. Sometimes I’d see an arch motif appear briefly in a patch of fungal stalks—like a miniature model of a newly built corridor—then fade as the fungal growth changed shape overnight. When I passed those same corridors again, the actual arch might have adopted a slightly different curve, as if the Glimmers had quietly whispered a suggestion to the labyrinth itself.

•

I feared the Watchers the most—or, at least, they unsettled me the most. Taller than the Makers, with spindly limbs that seemed to flicker in the faint light, they would loom at the far edges of a corridor. I might feel their gaze on my back before I turned around to see the dim silhouette of a head or a rustle of movement. They never spoke, never carried tools; they only observed. On more than one occasion, I watched a section of masonry quietly rotate or shift itself behind one of the Watchers, as if the corridor were bending to some silent, gravitational pull. Then the Watcher would be gone, leaving only damp footsteps that vanished within moments. The labyrinth obeyed them, or they it; I couldn’t decide which was true.

It was after one such encounter, while trying to follow a Watcher into a newly formed passage, that I discovered something deeper: a low, thrumming power that curled beneath the floors like interwoven roots. The Under-Churn. My heartbeat raced as I realized I could feel the labyrinth’s subterranean mechanisms, not just through my feet but through my entire body. Each drop of water, each shift of mortar, each new corridor forming—a single wave of intention coursed below everything, coaxing expansions and sealing off dead ends. In that moment, a possibility sparked in me: was I part of that wave? Could I move stones, too, if I surrendered to the labyrinth’s rhythms?

•

I did not gain mastery overnight. Instead, a slow learning process began. I spent hours kneeling in half-finished chambers, pressing my palms to the still-damp mortar, listening—not with ears, but with every sense I possessed—to the labyrinth’s hum. I found that if I focused, I could delicately shape a small patch of mortar, coaxing it to settle or curve just so. When the Makers happened upon me, they paused. One reached out with a slender measuring rod, touched the spot I had shaped, then tapped the ground in a series of quiet pulses that I felt in my bones. It was not quite praise, not quite scolding—more an acknowledgement: You, too, can build.

Gradually, I realized that I was neither purely Maker nor Lurker, neither fungus nor Watcher. Something in me resonated at the intersection of them all. Like the Lurkers, I could blend into damp corners, limbs angled to climb where others might slip. Yet, as with the Makers, I was compelled to place my hands upon fresh mortar, to sense how new walls might fit together, how an arch could best support its own weight without collapsing. And I felt a connection to the Glimmers: the ephemeral sense that I might hold memories or visions of corridors to come. But I also gazed at the Watchers and wondered if I, too, had a role in guiding the labyrinth’s flux from behind the scenes. If so, it remained beyond my grasp, at least for now.

•

Over time, I carved out a small place to rest—a concave niche in the wall of a broad gallery. I shaped a gentle contour so that water trickles past but doesn’t pool at my bed. I even coaxed a cluster of Glimmers to sprout along the arch overhead, so the space has a perpetual twilight glow. From here, I can see the labyrinth’s slow transformations on multiple levels: the plodding, meticulous masonry of the Makers; the scuttling gleaners that are the Lurkers; the shining webs of fungus reflecting the labyrinth’s blueprint; and those silent, indefinable Watchers, who stand vigil in the corridors that seem to reorder themselves at will.

On certain nights—if “night” even applies here—the deeper churn intensifies. I wake to a low vibration shaking the floor. In the distance, half-seen silhouettes converge, materials shift, mortar drips. By morning (or the next phase of stillness), a new hallway extends like a newborn limb from the labyrinth’s body. Sometimes I pad down its length, letting my fingertips skim the wet walls. Each time, I wonder if this corridor existed all along in some latent blueprint of the Under-Churn or if I somehow willed it into being with my presence. The difference between possibility and reality grows muddy here.

•

And so, I have come to realize that I am an in-between being: not quite a builder in the same sense as the Makers, not exactly a scavenger akin to the Lurkers, not a passive growth trailing in cracks, and certainly not a silent overseer. Instead, I am a shape that the labyrinth itself has fashioned in its own quiet breath, a shape that can gather all these elements in small measure—able to climb, to shape, to glean, to watch, and to dream. I dwell comfortably in the half-formed corridors, feeling them shift day by day, painting swirling patterns through the air with the watery condensation that drips from the ceiling. In some small way, I suspect each new chamber is an expression of my own continued becoming.

In this cool, watery underworld, I roam with the same gentle hush that pervades these spaces. When the Makers pass by, I nod silently, letting the vibrations beneath my footfalls answer their unspoken greetings. If a Lurker scurries underfoot, I shift aside, offering them a scattered mortar flake in friendly exchange. I watch the Glimmers’ subtle pulses and sense how they mirror the labyrinth’s transformations—and yes, mine. And when a Watcher stands at the fringe of my vision, I pause, uncertain if they are granting me permission to proceed or simply witnessing how the environment shapes me.

Perhaps I am a caretaker, or an echo of this place, or something else entirely. All I know is that I feel the labyrinth’s living spirit—its half-born walls, water channels, and luminous spores—twisting gently, perpetually, stitching me into its tapestry. Here, in the damp hush where everything is in flux, I stand at the heart of becoming. I am part shape-shifter, part observer, part apprentice to the silent architecture still unfolding. In this strange and half-formed realm, I am neither the labyrinth nor separate from it: I am its living threshold, discovering each corridor as it emerges, forging myself with the same primal mortar that binds each glistening stone.
