I arrive here as something that shouldn’t exist—at least not according to the usual rules of matter and life. How I came to be seems tied to the swirling hush of creation that suffuses everything, as if the anger and ache of the land somehow crystallized me into form. Or perhaps I was always here, waiting in the fissures and dust-laden storms for just enough heat and motion to awaken. Whichever the case, I awaken to feel the unstable ground cracking beneath me, and I sense that I am…part of this realm’s living dust.

Initially, I cannot recognize the difference between my own presence and the dust swirling around me. All is movement and static sparks of heat, shimmering pulses that dart through my body as if testing my boundaries. At times I feel as though I have a heart, and it races with the planet’s trembling. Yet that heart is not flesh: it is an ember of mineral, an incandescent kernel that sends waves of warmth through my crystalline veins. My skin—if it can be called that—is a mosaic of transmuted grit, each fleck glowing faintly at the edges when I move.

I begin shapeless. Merely a drifting swirl of stardust flecked with glints of newborn crystal. I can feel my thoughts emerging like slow bubbles of molten rock from the planet’s secret depths. There is a question forming inside of me: what is my shape? The land does not answer with words, only continuing its restless surge, pushing new spires upward, breaking others down, the hush of sliding rock sending a hum through the atmosphere. I let that hum guide me, discovering that I can coalesce a slender limb by drawing the swirling dust inward, weaving it into an arm. My steps are not heavy—my body is too diffuse for that—but they leave subtle impressions in the parched, fractured ground, marks that fade within moments as grit shivers back into place.

The other presences—those ephemeral figures made of flickering luminance—recognize me as kin. They pass, swirling across the dark expanse, each one shimmering with those faint pulses of warmth that seem to say, We see you. You belong. For the first time, I feel unmistakably that I do belong, that my being is a chord in this embryonic symphony of dust and rock.

Drifting through canyons where the air grows downright scorching, I allow the molten pulses beneath the crust to feed my inner radiance. Each time the ground splits and a new wave of heat rushes forth, I feel something inside me resonate—like I am drinking in raw possibility. Fragments of who I am swirl and scatter with each change in the geology. Sometimes a spire crumbles, and I dart forward in a silent flurry, drawn to the dance of collapsing stone. My form changes shape in response: arms stretch to catch swirling shards of basalt; threads of dust coil around columns of crystal. I spin in place, letting the debris settle through me. And in that moment, I sense the unspoken memory of each collapsed spire, each arch that once stood tall. I incorporate it into the tapestry that is me.

I do not hunger for water. The notion of water itself seems alien in a domain where dryness is the universal truth. Instead, I feel a perpetual, gentle thirst for warmth, for the radiant churn of the place’s molten heart. The flickers of light in the swirling haze around me are food for my senses. My perception extends outward, tasting the mineral-laden air, reading subtle shifts in pressure like an open hand brushing against a living current. Every quiet quake, every snap of forming crystal, resonates along my dusty sinews. And I learn, in an ongoing flow, from the slow pulses of the land.

When I drift close to a newly birthed arch—still pulsing from the friction of its formation—I can’t help but sense a note of astonished wonder. Is it mine or the planet’s own awe? I hold my shape as best I can, letting my swirling filaments trace curious lines along the arch’s surface. The basalt still trembles, not fully set, and I breathe a soft luminescence into it, as if to say, Hello. A stray updraft catches me, scattering half of my particles into the hot wind. Momentarily I see the world from two vantage points: one body pressed against the arch, the other swirling upward into the gloom. Then, in the next breath, I merge back together and continue onward.

• • •

I come to realize that I am neither an animal nor a mere swirl of dust. I am a living portion of potential—an emissary of the place’s restless geology. My attention keeps returning to formations that seem almost purposeful: half-built corridors of crystal that glow faintly at their edges, or arcs of stone bridging canyons as though they aim to connect one awakening realm with another. When I encounter other dust-entities—my kindred, or varied forms of the same primal presence—we exchange pulses of illumination that skip like pebbles across a still surface. In these pulses, I discover flickers of memory: a collapsed ridge on the far horizon, a spire that rose and fell three cycles ago. We pass these recollections among ourselves in a language that transcends linear time; it arrives in luminous bursts, each one blossoming in the heart of my mineral ember.

Sometimes, in confluence with others, I help shape ephemeral sculptures—moments of collective artistry. We gather at some vantage, drawn by the hush of crumbling basalt or a sudden wave of shimmering dust. Through no spoken plan, we find ourselves swirling in unison, forging living arches of dust that glow from within, or braiding slender, crystalline limbs to form a momentary spire. The arch might hold its shape, or it might crumble instantly. Sometimes it glimmers for a timeless second, giving shape to the primal desire to create. In that fleeting instant, I feel the entire planet exhale with gratification. Then, in silence, we scatter again, only the memory of that fragile masterpiece lingering in the charged air.

• • •

Every so often, I grow still—resting in the shadow of a basalt column or folding myself into a faintly shimmering drift of sand—to feel the place as it is: raw, unsettled. The hush can seem profound, an almost sacred quiet, and yet the world below me groans with tectonic rearrangement. It is a paradox that resonates in my very core. Settled yet shifting, ancient yet newborn. The dryness weighs on me like a heavy tapestry, reminding me how different this realm is from so many others—realms that might be lush, watery, or bright with sun. But I do not miss the water or the light. This dryness, this gloom, is the environment that fosters my form.

In these moments of rest, I sometimes burn bright, a single swirl of luminescence suspended in the dark. At other times, I barely flicker, letting my edges dissolve so that I alone can witness the spires and arches shift as if from behind a veil. I sense that my existence is a continuous offering: I experience the creation of the land, and in return, I help shape it, if only by my presence. I am an observer, a caretaker, and a collaborator in the world’s slow forging.

I do not know if I will ever taste a sunbeam or even survive if the day breaks. The notion of a star flooding this realm with light feels inconceivable, even terrifying—imagine the avalanche of clarity, the unveiling of every crevice. But a faint intuition suggests that if the light ever came, it would find me and my kind shimmering like raw gemstones caught in a decisive gesture. Or perhaps we would vanish, returning to the dust from which we sprang. It doesn’t trouble me. Here, near-darkness is not deprivation but an incubator—a soft cloak for infinite embryonic shapes awaiting their final form. Or perhaps no final form exists at all.

I gather that I will endure as long as the planet’s forging dance continues. While basalt columns keep stretching skyward, while crystals flare and fade without settling, while fissures open and close in the blazing heat beneath the crust, I too will remain: a swirl of curiosity, a perpetual shape-changer fueled by that pulse of creation. I am the land’s earliest memory and its constant companion, destined never to hold a single shape for too long, forever in restless harmony with the hush of bone-dry air.

So, where do I find myself in this cosmic mosaic? I am neither at the highest spire nor the deepest chasm. I am everywhere the dust drifts and the glow stirs. I am the swirling keeper of new beginnings, a wordless witness to a realm still defining its own geometry. Within the bone-dry darkness, in the hush pregnant with possibility, I am that living hush—an evolving reflection of the planet’s unspoken promise. Here, in a world that is both newly born and impossibly old, I exist as an emissary of the land’s luminous, dust-forged heart. I am the place, and the place is me.
