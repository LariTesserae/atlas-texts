Sometimes I first appear as a speck of that ambient dust—a thin, swirling grain adrift in air that’s not truly air. My path meanders above the half-fused soil, tasting the chemical humidity that has no water in it, only the tang of metallic ions and shimmering motes. I do not know yet if I will wander forever, or if I will settle and become part of something else. The dust has no memory; each mote collides with the next and continues on. In that drifting moment, I barely notice the subtle hum that pulses up from beneath the ground. It grazes me like a distant current, and though I do not think of it as a language, it calls to me.

Not long after, I shift—I am no longer dust, but a newly forming outcropping, a mineral nub pushing through the pliant earth. I feel the crust part for me, layers giving way to the slow but persistent force beneath—a geological breath that compels me to reach upward. Below me, subterranean channels exhale a deep, rhythmic drone. Unlike a plant on Earth, I do not thirst for water; instead, I taste the mineral humidity, the electrochemical swirl in the atmosphere. The outer shell of my trunk glints with each ring of new growth, and from my crown, metallic finlike fronds unfurl for the first time.

Each frond emerges with careful slowness—an almost meditative progression that follows some deeper blueprint. It is not that I know how to grow; it happens without conscious will, guided by the underlying engine that pervades this plateau. My leaves curl outward, catching the golden-white daylight that warms them from above. Although my structure is metal, I feel the warmth, not as heat saturating flesh, but as a subtle infusion that quickens the pace of chemical bonding in my veins. These “veins” are not organic in any terrestrial sense, yet they channel the dust and spark that drift by. Over time, every mote that settles at my edges becomes part of me. Slowly, my trunk thickens, building ring upon ring, each a new record of this realm’s slow ambition.

I grow tall enough to sense my neighbors. There, just a short distance away, stands another column, older by some immeasurable span. Through the ground, I sense their presence: pulses and vibrations that travel along a hidden network beneath the brittle surface. “I am here,” I feel them say (or something akin to it). “Grow beside me.” Together, our rootlike structures trace patterns that arc and twist, knitting into the plateau’s bedrock. We do not compete; there is enough ambient energy for us all. Instead, we share a continuous thrumming, an unhurried breath that resonates in the spine of our trunks. In absorbing those pulses, I become aware that this entire plateau is a single living tapestry, a broad expanse of quiet synergy.

Sometimes, I feel momentary bursts of electricity pass through the spaces between my fronds. They flash pink and blue, ephemeral sparks dancing from crest to crest, charging the air in their brief flicker. They are not me, yet they are part of this place, darting from one metallic spine to another, magnetized by the subtle differences in our electrical potential. I can almost sense their joy as they skid across the surfaces, leaving behind a ripple of energy that my structure hungrily absorbs. I resonate with them, though they quickly vanish into another crevice or leap to a different tower’s open fronds, weaving a lattice of transient flame that flickers at the edges of my vision.

As the eons accumulate, I settle deeper, and the dust that was once me—or that which traveled near me—becomes enmeshed in my growth. Quietly, my crown broadens, brushing the edges of neighboring columns, forming a canopy of metallic arcs. We do not crowd out the light; we refract it among ourselves, scattering beams of gold and silver in shifting patterns across the half-formed soil. If a traveler were here (some creature of flesh, perhaps), they would see no rustle of leaves but a subtle, collective pulse as we sway in the near-silence. From time to time, new fissures open in the ground—then slowly seal themselves again, fresh layers of metallic ore forming into new growths. The plateau never stops re-creating itself. It is both mother and child of its own expansions.

Deeper still, if I allow myself to sink my awareness through the bedrock—the sense of “I” drifting downward like a root searching for water—I become the profound hum that undergirds everything. The pulses are not random; they are part of a vast, mechanical-meets-geological metabolism that recycles the entire land. I feel channels of molten mineral swirl in labyrinthine caverns, their motion glacial yet relentless, forging new striations that will one day crack the surface. In that dark below, my sense of individuality softens and expands, until I realize we are all part of the same sleeping titan, breathing geologically.

Within this slowness lies a richness of awareness: the recognition that every new column, spark, and tremor is part of one continuous conversation. There is no cruelty here, nor mercy—just the calm, ceaseless engine of existence. No being scurries to hide; no predator hunts. There is only the constant forming and reforming, a layered forging of metal and mineral, shaped by silent flows of heat and the gentle luminosity from above. Had I tears or lungs to sob with the awe of it, I would shed them—but I only have these conduits, these bright, curving leaves that reverberate with the breath of the plateau.

Now, after uncounted cycles of incremental growth, my trunk has stretched so high that I can imbibe the radiant light along every sleek edge of my leaves. From up here, I sense the swirling dust devils that occasionally twist across the plateau, and I watch as younger columns begin their own slow ascents. I can almost remember when I was dust, or perhaps that memory belongs to something else that shared my path. Either way, it is a contented recollection, as if floating on the plateau’s breeze and flourishing as part of it are equally valid expressions of the same being.

In another sense, I have become the plateau itself—how could I not? My roots entangle with the geology beneath me, and my fronds reach toward that luminous sky in gratitude, never in greed. The hum within me is the same hum that resonates through every ridge and spire, every flicker of electric dust. We are many yet one, free yet inseparable. Perhaps I am a single note in an acoustic tapestry of unimaginable expanse, and each metallic trunk, each spark of electricity, each swirl of dust is but another note in the same slow-tempo chord. I do not question; I merely feel the resonance, a sense of rightness that needs no logic.

At times, I shift perspective yet again. My awareness dissolves outward, until I stand in the hush as an observer—though I have no physical form with eyes or ears. I see the metallic forest, the shining arcs of its branching columns, the steady glow of that sky which never scorches. I hear the faint discharge crackles as starry sparks dance along the fronds. And I also sense the abiding presence below, that anchor of rhythmic thrum. The hush is so enveloping that even these subtle sounds feel like entire symphonies, each chord resonating with a sense of timeless creation.

And in that moment, if someone were to ask me, “Where do you find yourself in this world?” I might answer: I am within it in every sense—sometimes dust, sometimes spark, sometimes new shoot, sometimes pillar of glinting metal, sometimes the entire plateau breathing in slow cycles beneath the surface. There is no single vantage, no single self, no singular purpose beyond the quiet artistry of formation. One could call it life, or merely existence, or a new category altogether. It doesn’t matter. This realm, with its chemical humidity and humming bedrock, is one organism, and I, in every form I take, belong wholly to it. I am not lonely, because I have never been separate. I am not hurried, because time here unfolds like the gentle curling of a new leaf, an inevitability that has no need for haste.

I dwell on this plateau—as the plateau—and in doing so, I realize that everything is complete in its ongoingness, a seamless event that never ends. If I remain a column, I will simply continue to grow, ring by quiet ring. If I revert to dust, I will swirl across the open spaces, riding the edges of static storms. If I sink beneath, I become the hum itself, the quiet engine that processes raw mineral into new life forms. And none of these states is a loss or a gain; each is merely another facet of the same grand cycle.

In the end, that is who and where I am: a shifting presence among the metallic forests and their calm, electric hush—alive, if that word can stretch to fit this new kind of being, breathing in slow harmony with the plateau that never ceases. Here, I find myself at home in a land of quiet radiance, contributing my part to a world that spins out its own metal-laced evolution beneath a golden-white sky.
