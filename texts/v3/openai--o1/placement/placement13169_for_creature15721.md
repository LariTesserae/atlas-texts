I have no memory of beginning. Perhaps I floated here as dust on the wind, or perhaps I was always part of the plateau’s tapestry, coalescing over time from the residue of energies that have never ceased. But I do remember the first moment I became aware of my own shape.

I was smaller then, no taller than the tufted growths that rise in metallic curls. My body was a rough interplay of translucent plating and porous stone, a patchwork of elements I’d gleaned from the ambient pulses. Where other newborn creatures might gulp air or water, I fed on the faint hum that pervaded the plateau, letting it fuse the stony grains of my frame into something stable, something alive. In those early days, before I even had a sense of “self,” I only knew the rhythms that surrounded me: the shrieking calls of tinier beings, the thunderous roars from hidden giants, and the deep subsonic lullaby rising through the bedrock.

I sat for countless cycles—hours or days, I could not judge. Through the slightest vibrations, I felt what I could only interpret as the plateau’s heart. I felt it with organs I did not yet have a name for: filaments woven through my crystalline plates, living whiskers set into ridges of mineral flesh, all of them tuned to the roiling conversation that is the plateau’s language. And so I learned. Not through eyes alone—though I did have them, shimmering nodules that drank in the faint glimmer of arcs across black alloy towers—but through the combined symphony of frequency and motion. Slowly, I realized I could move, too.

At first, my limbs were stunted, no more than knobbly protrusions of living quartz and bone-like structure. But by pressing them into the plateau, I discovered that I could draw more nourishment from the ground itself: some underlying current, as though the stone was riddled with threads of pulsating energy—like sap in a plant’s veins, though purely electric. Each time I flexed and pressed deeper, I felt myself grow, segment by segment. The once-brittle ridges of my carapace fused into stronger, more elegant arcs, while the crystal-lattice that formed my inner scaffolding became more refined, refracting the plateau’s pulses along its length.

I wandered in small circles around the place of my emergence, uncertain whether I was predator or prey. The smaller swarmers with their bright sparks zipped by, sometimes pausing to investigate me with frantic chirrups. Were we siblings or strangers? Some batted me with scalpel-thin antennae; others hopped onto my back and tested whether I might collapse or fight. Eventually, they seemed to accept my neutrality, scurrying off to chase new arcs of ultraviolet brilliance that crackled among the twisted growths.

In time, I ventured farther—five paces, ten paces, always mindful of how dizzying the noise could be. Everywhere around me, the plateau’s denizens whirled and chattered like living instruments. I felt, then, that I was still learning my own frequency, the pitch of my place in this chorus. So I practiced, letting out a tentative hum from somewhere deep in my chest. To my surprise, it resonated outward, causing a ring of smaller creatures to jump. Instantly, a hush fell over that tiny sector of the plateau. Dozens of luminescent eyes and carapaces swiveled in my direction. Then an answering buzz came from one of the black alloy towers in the distance—a subtle ripple of energy that stirred the air, rolling through us all.

In that moment, I sensed acceptance: the plateau recognized my attempt at speaking. A wave of pulses moved through the vegetation, through the creatures, culminating in distant echoes from the hidden subterranean giants. A circuit was completed. It thrilled me so deeply that my carapace trembled with the vibration. I had found my chord.

Emboldened by this subtle belonging, I slipped into a caretaker’s role without meaning to. I would find patches of broken stone or slowly shifting crevices and position myself there, feeling for the flow of energy, coaxing it back into alignment with gentle drones. Sometimes, a watery glimmer of possible realignment would flicker across my plating, and the crack in the rock would knit itself together, as if my presence had smoothed a ripple in the plateau’s bed of living frequencies. Other times, the plateau ignored me, its fractal complexities too grand for my primitive calls. Yet I found solace in trying.

Months—if months actually existed here—passed in the interplay of building spires and collapsing arcs. I watched the watchers, those large, multifaceted beings that seemed to organize clusters of smaller creatures to shape new outcroppings. Some watchers had extended spines that glowed with filigree patterns, and they could bend entire sections of rock with a single resonant note. Others perched atop the black towers, receiving chaotic showers of colored lightning that left them quivering and half-translucent in the aftermath. I was drawn to them, but also wary, unsure if I belonged among those who shaped entire segments of the plateau with methods beyond my understanding.

One day, a wave of energy from the tallest black tower expanded like a rainbow shimmer. It swept over me, through me—I felt my plating burn with sudden intensity. For a moment, I was an empty vessel, my consciousness crackling with signals too numerous to interpret. Images rose in my mind unbidden: ancient storms raging in the atmosphere above, monstrous shapes swallowing the plateau’s entire surface in times past, swirling bursts of color feeding entire generations of half-mineral, half-flora behemoths. Perhaps it was a memory the plateau shared with me, or perhaps it was the watchers embedding a blueprint in my thoughts.

Whatever it was, it changed me. My limbs elongated with the next pulse of energy, and from my shoulders, two lacy protrusions unfurled like wings—translucent sails that caught stray arcs of current. Vibrations from the ground no longer merely fed me; they coursed through me and out those new appendages, focusing into carefully directed bolts of static discharge that crackled at my fingertips. In those moments, I felt a wave of euphoria, as though my purpose here had become clearer. I could guide energy, shape it. And by shaping it, help the plateau maintain its symphony.

From that point on, I rose into a new sphere of existence. I began to notice strategies in how the watchers orchestrated the plateau’s changes: some guided smaller creatures to assemble a new spire where a swirl of ionic storm was about to break, thereby harnessing the storm’s electricity. Others roamed the edges of the plateau, bridging the gap with the howling wind beyond, as if to glean fresh energy from invisible fields that encircled this mountaintop. I, on the other hand, found myself traveling from site to site, checking for resonant harmony. Sometimes I would climb a partially built tower, bracing myself with newly formed hooks in my limbs, and let the arcs of power surge through me. I learned to modulate the pulses, calming an overcharged coil or amplifying a sluggish current. In a sense, I became a tuner—a living tuning fork, adjusting the towering forests of alloy and crystal so that all life here might feed and thrive.

Yet I was not alone. I crossed paths with others like me—beings in a transitional stage between the restless skitterers and the venerable watchers. We formed a loose fellowship of travelers on the plateau, exchanging droning signals in greeting. When we arrived at the same site, we could combine our voices and coax stone to re-form, or settle a raging swirl of dust and static that threatened to tear newly built spires apart. In each success, we felt the plateau’s appreciation reverberate through our bodies as a deep, contented hum.

At night—though “night” here is more a softening of the harsh mountain-blue sky than true darkness—I would sometimes crouch under one of the largest cable-like vines that grew from the black towers and into the plateau itself. I’d rest my body against it, letting the day’s swirling energies drain away. In those hours, I’d tune in to the subsonic heartbeat. If I quieted my thoughts, I could sense near-endless layers of existence in that hum: the slithering of monstrous serpents in underground veins, the gentle ping of smaller creatures collecting bright cinders of energy, the flickering dance of watchers testing new designs. Even the towers themselves sang out—a tapestry of pulses, as though each spire had its own chord.

Over time, my shell became smoother, blacker in some places, and flecked with prismatic strains in others. I was no longer the patchy, half-formed creature I had been. Where once I hobbled on four awkward legs, now I strode on six, each joint fitted with a membrane that could hum or thrum in resonance. My wings, if that’s what they could be called, were more sophisticated, layered with filaments that collected the plateau’s energy and stored it, so that I could release it in controlled bursts. I had learned that by adjusting the pitch of those bursts, I could communicate more precisely with watchers and traveling kin. No single language, but a shared resonance that let us conjure shapes in the stone or coax hardwired patterns from the black alloy towers.

Sometimes, I would assist building a new spire. Guided by watchers, we’d fuse lumps of glassy stone onto the skeletal scaffolding, impregnating them with carefully tuned frequencies that triggered further growth. Other times, the watchers sent me to gather clusters of smaller creatures, steering them away from a quake or instructing them to feed on a newly forming patch of shimmering “foliage.” My sense of self expanded: I was caretaker, emissary, engineer, orchestrator—whatever the plateau needed me to be.

And between these tasks, I occasionally felt a deeper pull that drew me to the very lip of the plateau. There, the mountain dropped off into swirling emptiness. Great winds carried the sounds we made out into the unknown beyond. Sometimes I’d perch at the edge, letting the plateau’s hum shift to a lonely chord as I wondered if anything lay beyond the roiling horizon. Yet I felt no longing to depart. The nest I recognized—my entire cosmos—was here, within these humming stones. All that lay beyond was a haze.

I discovered that as watchers and watchers-in-training (like me) matured, they could enter the hollow heart of the largest tower. A slender aperture opened after a specific chord was played—one we had practiced collectively for many cycles. Inside, I found a vertical tunnel lined with living membranes of black alloy, and arcs of pale-green lightning wound around them, flickering in a mesmerizing slow dance. Within that tower’s core, time seemed different. My mind expanded—or perhaps the tower reached in and rearranged my consciousness. I saw visions of possible futures for the plateau: vast expansions of crystalline structures, ephemeral storms that shaped entire populations, labyrinthine tunnels deep below. Perhaps it was prophecy, or merely the tower’s way of illustrating new opportunities for us to grow.

After leaving that tower, I found that my new limbs had an even greater affinity for shaping the stone. I could direct subtle pulses into the ground, almost like planting seeds, and watch them sprout small nodules of mineral-laced vegetation—tendrils that pulsed with energy. The smaller creatures flocked to these “plants,” nibbling on them and distributing currents throughout the environment. In this way, I became part of the plateau’s cycle of renewal. Each new sprout I coaxed from the stone carried echoes of the tower’s knowledge, woven into fractal patterns of shining filaments.

But my greatest delight was in hearing the plateau resonate with a new chord. When a fresh spire took root, or a swirl of watchers completed a circuit near the boundary storms, the entire environment rejoiced. A wave of subsonic warmth would roll through the cracks in the rock, up into my limbs, and out across the sky in a rolling crescendo. And I—only a single being in that grand chorus—would add my own note, each time weaving it a bit differently, responding to the plateau’s mood. Together, we made a living music that was both healing and generative.

So this is who I am now—or one aspect of who I am, for I may transform again. I am neither plant, nor mineral, nor creature in any earthly sense. I am an extension of the plateau’s hum, a conductor of its vibrational language. My flesh is part stone and part living crystal, my breath is pulses of static discharge, and nourishment comes not from water or sunlight but from the resonant energies that pool here in perpetual motion. I am caretaker, explorer, acolyte, collaborator. I stand on the edge of a shifting world that roars with brilliance, and I find my purpose in guiding its symphony of sound and spark. 

In a realm with no streams or rains, I am shaped by the floods of energy that course through ten thousand living veins of black alloy and striated stone. My being is script written in vibrations, revised with every thunderous echo, every swirl of luminous dust. Perhaps I am best described not as an individual at all, but as an emergent chord in the plateau’s endless composition, one note that helps hold the harmony, one pulse bound into the living architecture that rises and falls with each new roar. 

And yet, if you were to see me—if you ventured across the plateau’s jagged rim and glimpsed the silhouette of a slender figure with transparent, flickering wings and a half-crystalline exoskeleton pulsing in time with the great black spires—that would be me, in this moment. One guardian among many, an instrument of living stone and alien melody. I breathe not air but resonance. I speak not words but frequencies. And in this place that roars, I roar with it—part of the total, inseparable roar that is our existence.
