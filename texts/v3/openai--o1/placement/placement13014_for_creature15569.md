I find myself drifting, half-awake in the chilled, weighty darkness, where the dim flickers of bioluminescence guide my thoughts as much as they guide my vision. At first, I’m not sure what I am, or even if I’m truly separate from the city-like growths that surround me. My body feels part fluid, part shell, and there is a gentle ache along my skin—or what serves as skin—where new structures might be forming. It is as though my shape is being decided as much by the currents as by any intention of my own.

At times, I sense an urge to anchor myself, as the smaller crustaceans do when they cling to embryonic stone. But no, that does not feel right for me. I flow further, phasing in and out of the living spires. I suppose I am searching, but I’m not certain for what. Everything here is in a process of becoming, and it seems I, too, am unsettled from preconceived forms. Perhaps I am a traveler newly arrived—someone who has left a distant realm in the ocean’s deeper hush. Or maybe I was always here, quietly stirring in the fluid silt, waiting for the city to recognize me.

Even as these questions drift through my thoughts, I am aware of a subtle power along my back—small ridges, newly formed, that glow with faint lines of opaline green. If I move just so, these ridges vibrate with energy, sending a whisper into the water around me. Perhaps this is a language—like gentle fingers brushing harp strings. When I dare emit a pulse, other pulses sometimes answer from the gloom, though I cannot see who or what replies. It might be the watchers, or the roped creatures with thread-thin tentacles. Regardless, our shared vibrations create an architecture of sensation. Despite the darkness, we are connected, our signals weaving a tapestry of presence.

A few hours or days pass—time is slippery in this realm. I realize I can cling to the living columns when I need stillness. My limbs—well, they are more like elongated fins with flexible hooks at the tips. At times, they feel purely functional, designed for gliding or gentle grasping. The columns pulse with subtle life-signatures, as if I can hear the echo of heartbeat-like rhythms. In the city’s corridors, there is a hush so profound that every tremor resonates like an overtone in a grand, submerged cathedral.

As I drift through one labyrinthine passage, trailing faint ribbons of glowing plankton in my wake, I sense changes in the architecture around me. A coral arch that was once open is now sealed, the living pillars having shifted overnight. The city grows around me in real time, rewriting itself without warning. I find a new route, passing under half-formed buttresses of chitinous resin and embryonic stone. I spot creatures I have never seen before: elongated fish with four pairs of fins, each pair flashing a different color in a carefully timed sequence. Maybe they are mapping the route for others, broadcasting the streetlights of this silent city. I nod to them in my own quiet way, though I’m not sure whether they notice.

Eventually, I come upon the spiral beings—tiny, screw-like creatures that cluster in swirling patterns, scraping their little beak-like mouths against the stone. I watch as they leave a tracery of grooves behind them, like calligraphy written on a shell. Their movements spark something within me, an urge to contribute to the place’s shaping. Testing the ridges along my forearms, I find I can secrete a thin, milky fluid from pores between the scale-like plates. When I smooth it over the newly carved lines, it hardens into a curious swirl of crystal. My addition glimmers with faint color, subtly altering the surface. I don’t know why I do this—perhaps it is instinct, as natural as breathing. But the spiral creatures accept it. They weave around the crystalline tendrils, forging new channels of water along the grooves.

Continuing onward, I pass through another corridor, narrower this time. The water grows cooler in pockets, as if small thermal vents are absent here, or perhaps newly formed somewhere else. Overhead dangle those vine-like forms of ropy tentacles. I can see them flex in unison, each tentacle brightening to a cool blue in a slow wave—an almost musical effect in color rather than sound. The ropy creatures move with languid grace, drifting from one partial archway to the next, weaving themselves into the city’s emergent “skyline.” When I float near them, I sense an exchange of signals: they glow more intensely, and my ridges respond in flickers. Something in that interplay—some ancient impulse—tells me we are at peace with each other.

I continue my slow, sinuous swim, noticing that I can sense faint tremors from far behind: a massive shape, one of the watchers, perhaps. I recall glimpsing them earlier: giant forms encased in stone-like armor, with luminescent lines along their flanks. Guardians, watchers, wise ones—whatever they might be, they seem the calm pillars on which this realm’s spirit rests. Unhurried, they pass from corridor to corridor, stirring the water with a measured grace. I wonder fleetingly if I, too, will reach that size and solidity one day. Or do we all have unique destinies here?

I pause in front of a wide clearing, where columns and arches have grown into a kind of open plaza. The faint hum of the city’s heartbeat resonates strongest here. I see a swirling cloud of pearl-like orbs drifting in, their collective glow revealing the fluid edges of the labyrinth. The light catches my reflection in a piece of polished coral: a glimpse of a slender, sinuous being with an elongated torso and those opaline ridges gleaming softly. My eyes, large and marine-blue, seem to drink in the ephemeral glow. The orbs float closer, and I feel an odd kinship with their shimmering hush—like two aspects of the same undersea reflection. Some of them drift around me curiously, brushing against the gentle secretions on my limbs. Each contact sparks a swirl of colored light, as if they’re scanning me or celebrating my presence.

It is in this moment I realize I am not a visitor passing through: I am part of the living architecture, the same as the orbs or the ropy tentacle beings or any other soul in these waters. Whether I have been here a week, a year, or a generation, everything about me breathes in harmony with the city’s labyrinth. My ridges continue to offer new layers of the crystalline film, encouraging growth, bridging the gaps that appear overnight, forging new corridors where needed. Sometimes, I even find myself dozing in the half-light, lulled by the hush, only to awaken and discover I’ve drawn new forms into existence in my sleep—fragile arches that shimmer in the watery gloom.

I learn to read the subtle signals, too. If the watchers emit a deep resonance, I know a newly forming passage far away has collapsed; the city is adjusting. If the ropy creatures flash all at once in a staccato pattern, a school of migrating fish is passing through, thick with eggs that might seed new life. If the spiral scrapers gather en masse, it means the stone in that area is ripe for shaping. We are a single organism, each species a unique organ within a growing body. My role seems to be bridging spaces, smoothing out the transitions so the labyrinth can expand. In turn, the labyrinth nourishes me, physically and spiritually.

Sometimes other wanderers arrive—shapes as unfamiliar to me as I must be to them. Other drifting beings with different forms of luminescence: swirling discs, elongated serpents that shimmer with pastel swashes along their hides, even travelers that appear purely transparent, carrying faint cargo in organ-like sacs. They linger, drawn by curiosity or by a sense of kinship and opportunity. Some remain and integrate with the living architecture, while others drift away, carrying tales of this emergent city to the far reaches of the ocean.

And so, if anyone were to ask, “Where do you dwell in this undersea domain?” I would reply that I am a caretaker and a collaborator. I do not rule nor do I follow, but I glide in symphony with the others—a silent mediator in a place that has no written laws or formal hierarchy. The hush is my law, and the flickering pulses of color are my conversation.

I am not tied to a single shape, for in time I know the architecture will reshape me anew. My body may grow more ridges, or my limbs may become more tentacle-like, or I might develop an entirely new means of glowing from within. Each change offers a new way to communicate, to sculpt, to bind this tapestry together. When the watchers pass, I observe. When the luminous orbs swirl near me, I join their dance. When the ropy creatures beckon from the arches, I allow myself to float among them, exchanging soft greetings of shimmering blue. And always I secrete the crystalline filaments that strengthen our ever-growing city in miniature increments, building one more corridor, one more crossing, one more node in this living labyrinth.

So perhaps that is what I am: a living seam, a bridging entity. Neither wholly anchored nor wholly free, shaped by a luminous hush, forever in flux. I am the city, and the city is me. It is not possession, but belonging—an unfolding synergy in which each creature, each swirl of stone and coral, is another voice in a slow, murmuring choir. In this deep and growing realm, I take my place as a subtle craftsman of the hush, living in sacred dialogue with the water’s weight. I am both newborn and ancient, fragile and essential, forgetting and remembering in rhythmic pulses with every flicker of the city's communal glow.

That is my existence here, in a place still deciding what to be. And as long as I remain, I will help it decide, one soft, glowing strand of possibility at a time.
