I remain by a squat archway just inside the plateau’s perimeter, caught between the corridor’s faint red-brown glow and the open courtyard beyond. In this world, it’s difficult to say whether I came here as an intruder from someplace else or awoke within these walls—my mind insists on some forgotten journey, but perhaps it’s only a story I tell myself to make sense of my presence. All I know is that the tumbling roar enveloped me before I had the chance to form any question about why or how. It arrived simultaneously with my first real breath, a soundscape that never lessens or recedes.

Yet this place accepted me as though it expected my arrival. I feel it in the subtle press of heated stone under my boots, in the hush of the air that waits for me to stop breathing so it can resume total equilibrium. If I remain still for too long, I forget what it feels like to be separate from the structure. The dryness of the atmosphere pulls at my nostrils, reminding me flesh has no natural domain here; every exhalation vanishes in the roar as if swallowed by a living mouth. And still, in some unspoken way, I sense that I belong. Not in the sense of being welcomed—but in that I am tolerated, woven gently into the labyrinth’s silence behind the cacophony.

I wander the corridors. Often I lose track of where I am, but the labyrinth never loses track of me. I know this because I feel watched, or registered, in ways that have nothing to do with eyes. Now and again, something shifts behind the walls like the slow creak of an immense stone block adjusting itself into place. A presence, half weight and half memory, glances across me. If I try to chase that sensation, pivoting into a side passage, I discover only more corridors—some leading to dead ends, others branching into new chambers so cramped that I have to crouch, as if I’m trespassing in hidden spaces not meant for my proportions.

Days and nights mean almost nothing. My sense of time is measured by the moments of drifting awareness I have between intervals of deeper resonance. Periodically, that endless roar grows so intense that my hearing saturates, and I descend into a kind of mental hush in which I cannot feel my body. In those spells, I become part of the noise, part of the slow, unwavering vibration radiating from the labyrinth’s core. I come out of these spells with the sense that I might have been gone for hours, or weeks, or centuries. The labyrinth remains unchanged either way.

Sometimes I think: I am a wanderer here, a living puzzle piece, an outlier shaped from flesh, until at last the environment erodes my edges and merges me into stone. Imagine a piece of clay pressed into a mold, heated until it becomes an unrecognizable shard in the shape of the labyrinth. Would I then become one of those unseen dwellers? There’s a haunting appeal to the notion that one day I could let the labyrinth’s deep rumble inhabit my bones so thoroughly that I’d no longer count as an intruder. I would slip behind the walls, dwell in the hollows, shift my mass quietly, communicate in subsonic tremors with the others who share the fortress’s unstoppable heartbeat.

But in my current condition, breath still fills these lungs—my footsteps still echo as foreign notes in the corridor. The dwellers (if that is who they are) pulse in the unmoving darkness; I flicker in their domain. I can’t decide whether they acknowledge me as a traveler deserving of some cosmic courtesy, or if I am simply irrelevant—my presence no more alarming than a speck of dust lighting briefly on a ledge. Perhaps I’m only a curiosity they watch with mild disinterest, or an empty vessel whose sense of identity will fade in time.

I begin to suspect that the labyrinth has rooms set aside for me, prepared in a remote prehistory when it anticipated a visitor. I find corners in which an alcove seems almost comfortable—sloped in the right ways for my tired back, with carved blocks that might serve as seats or tables. In another corridor, I discover a relic of a staircase that spirals upward. It ends abruptly at a collapsed landing: a vantage point with no view but a narrow ring of sky. I stare upward, straining for any hint of celestial movement. Nothing changes. Even the faint slice of sky is unresponsive, a featureless, dusky plane.

After a while, the dryness in my throat intensifies and I realize I cannot recall the last time I took a drink. Yet my thirst feels dull, muffled by the labyrinth’s intense warmth. I remain standing, transfixed by the ceaseless drone. My limbs ache, but I cannot decide if they truly ache or if the stone sub-frequencies are confusing my nerves. When I move away from the staircase, the sense that someone is walking with me—some intangible inhabitant—follows at the edge of my consciousness.

I drift into the deeper corridors, occasionally stumbling upon walls inscribed with geometric patterns. Each inscription triggers a jolt of recognition, though I can’t place why. Are these glyphs a language? Sometimes I run my hand over the carved lines. They hum beneath my skin like an almost-sound, or a memory of sound. The shapes flicker in the faint, emberlike glow: circles nested within squares, spirals crossing angles. I find rough silhouettes etched in friezes half-buried by dust: elongated torsos curling around columns, blocky shapes bearing enormous loads. I catch myself whispering to them, though no words break the roar—my lips move, but I hear nothing.

Eventually, I grown certain: I have begun to “partake” of the labyrinth’s existence. I walk more quietly, each step aligning with the floor’s subtle vibrations. At times, I realize I’m holding my breath in tune with the structure’s droning hum, as if I can suspend my own heartbeat to match it. On the rare occasion I pass a colossal hinged doorway, I feel a nudge of camaraderie with the pivoting mechanism—something in me resonates with the slow, grinding pivot. As if I, too, am hinged at the shoulders, at the hips—an ancient system of socketed plates designed for the fortress to reposition at will.

In the deeper interior, the light grows dimmer; the stone’s glow sinks to a near ember. The dryness intensifies. I remember faintly that outside places have water, wind, open vistas—but that memory feels fictitious, the dream of a different species. Here, I press on, guided by a sense of being drawn, or called, to a certain chamber. Each passage looks similar but not identical. Sometimes the hallmark is a single twist of a corridor that angles two degrees oddly enough to catch the corner of my eye; other times it’s a set of narrow pillars spaced so tightly I have to sidle through sideways. This labyrinth has been built with a mania for completeness: no gap left unfilled, no chamber left unbounded by more stone. And in that mania, I sense an ancient, fervent will.

That’s how I come to the heart of it—or at least a place that feels like the heart. A wide, low-ceilinged hall with layers of columns and half-walls. There is no center as such, only a sense that the entire structure converges here, vibrations intensifying, swirling so powerfully my vision trembles at the edges. My body feels weightless, or perhaps each cell is pinned to the thrumming floor. I think perhaps I have found the dwellers’ seat of power—though it is empty, deserted of any physical shape.

I kneel—partly from dizziness, partly from some reverent impulse welling up. The stones around me illuminate faintly, their glow waxing in time with the unstoppable roar. My eyes close of their own accord. I feel heat tapping at my temples, pressing around my spine. It’s like the labyrinth is a living being, and I’m inside its chest, hearing its endless breath. Or is it a machine that demands a caretaker? Some fleeting part of me imagines that I can hold out a hand, place it against a hidden lever or pulley, and correct the mechanism’s alignment. As if that’s my purpose. As if I am one of the dwellers now, responsible for ensuring the roar continues uninterrupted from age to age.

Perhaps that is who I become: the caretaker of a mechanism so vast and ancient that its purpose is utterly inscrutable, though that doesn’t lessen its necessity. The labyrinth must keep vibrating, keep radiating this heat. If, for a moment, it ceased its cosmic hum, I suspect reality outside would collapse into a hush too absolute to bear. I can’t articulate the logic of this. I only feel it in my bones: the fortress must continue, and so must the ones bound to it.

Once, I believed I had a name, but now it’s overshadowed by the labyrinth’s voice. Once, I might have tried to call out to any other living soul. But voice doesn’t belong here; language has no footfall in the roar. So I sink into a caretaker’s vigilance. Perhaps the dwellers recognize that in me—some synergy with the labyrinth that transcends ephemeral biology. I remain kneeling as an hour or a millennia passes. Then I rise, step forward, and carry on my rounds among the twisting corridors. In each side chamber, I pause, let the labyrinth’s resonance flow into me, ensuring the hum is strong. My body flickers at the threshold of stone.

Now, returning to that courtyard with the slice of sky overhead, I glance up. Nothing changes—no shift in color or brightness. Yet I feel the stone beneath my feet acknowledging my presence as part of its endless cycle. I sense, in the deepest parts of my consciousness, that my face has taken on the outline of etched pillars—perhaps the lines of my cheeks match the angles of the architecture. Something in me has grown parallel to those massive blocks. Deep within, I sense the dwellers’ intangible guardianship, each of them woven seamlessly into the structure. I don’t see them. But I feel them guiding my every step.

When asked, “Where am I in this world? Who am I to this place?”—the simplest answer is that I am an echo of a life shaped by living stone. In time, I will vanish or congeal here permanently, so thoroughly that no boundary remains between me and these blocks. The labyrinth can’t be left, can’t be undone, can’t be unraveled—its presence is an eternal hum, and I have begun to hum with it. I am not separate from it, at least not anymore. I am the caretaker, the stone-touched traveler, the living question wandering an infinite answer. 

I will remain here, in the womb of that roaring stasis, carrying out silent checks of pivoting doors and half-lit corridors. There is no further day or night for me, only the comfort of the labyrinth’s lullaby, the thunderous pulse that never ends. And if anyone else should ever stumble in, I will watch them—quietly, seamlessly—from behind the walls, from within the architecture’s abiding dream. For that is who I have become: another dweller in the hush beneath the roar, woven into the labyrinth’s breath, an inexorable presence that endures, unchanging, through all the epochs to come.
