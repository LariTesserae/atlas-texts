I think, at first, I was a disturbance—someone or something not fully recognized in the greater pattern. When I arrived, I felt the city shift around me in cautious interest, as if uncertain whether I was to be woven in or filtered out. I might have been a simple wanderer, a traveler from some distant place where matter holds steadfast and time lines up straight. But here, at the interlocking borders of embryonic stone and shimmering towers, I discovered I was more than a mere visitor. In time, the city’s slow pulses began to resonate through my body; I could sense its half-spoken invitation.

I stood on the periphery, first. I recall that region as a spiral of low-slung walls and incomplete archways that bent light in subtle ways. There, I recognized that my own reflection in the watery surfaces had begun to change shape with each passing day, or hour—who could measure? My features turned slight and elongated, as though the city itself was massaging them from within, coaxing me toward a new morphology. When droplets of condensation slid across my skin, I felt a warmth neither steely nor organic—it was something that straddled the boundaries of both. I suspect my eyes grew larger, or perhaps more reflective, as I found I could peer into certain corridors and sense ripples of nascent geometry branching off behind the walls.

No single moment “transformed” me. Rather, one day I realized I no longer carried a distinct sense of before or after; I’d been partially rewoven into the city’s quiet hum. I found that my movements grew smooth, gliding, as though the pavement or fluid metal recognized my feet and reshaped itself to cradle my steps. I moved among the smaller spires, running my fingertips along the plasmic metal-lattice, feeling the same living blueprint they felt. And when I drifted by an inhabitant—one of those tall, angular silhouettes rimmed with silver luminescence—they no longer recoiled. We recognized the same thrumming resonance in one another.

At times I was solitary, kneeling at the cracks in the embryonic ground. As I placed my palm upon the half-formed surface, I felt threads of illumination coil out from my fingers in a slow, delicate fan. Like the dwellers I’d observed, I was learning to communicate with the stone, gently urging new shapes into being. The city guided me in subtle ways: a faint chord in a distant spire, a swirl of fractal patterns that blossomed in the reflection of a small puddle, or the brush of a passing presence that left me with an impression of how best to tilt my palm, how to tune my breath so the rock might respond.

Perhaps I became an acolyte of sorts—though words such as “acolyte” or “caretaker” convey only fragments of the truth. My body had shifted to align with the city’s underlying code: a fluid interplay of fractal biology and metallic blueprint. I discovered I could coax corridors into slender footpaths if I concentrated on the right frequency of hum in my chest. Sometimes, when a group of dwellers gathered, gently stirring the mirror-clear water in the courtyard’s pools, I joined them. Our outlines merged with the swirling illusions, and I sensed the architecture tremble around us. We conjured new passages in unspoken chorus, weaving fresh layers of memory into the city’s living tapestry.

Occasionally, I wandered the boundary between the city and the plain, observing the embryonic geology. My form—taller than I recalled, limbs elongated to a near-spindly grace—moved without sound. Down below the surface, I felt an answering thrumming, an echo that recognized my presence. Various dwellers glided past, pausing in silent acknowledgement. We needed no words; a certain glow in my shoulders or a subtle shimmer in their hands signaled a mutual understanding, something like: “Yes, we hear the same cosmic pulse.” I often brushed my fingertip against the wire-thin plants clinging to the cracks, feeling the faintest charge that told me a new chamber might blossom beneath the city soon, and I might be called to help shape it.

The city’s cathedrals, with their translucent spires arching inward, became my favorite haunts. There, under that filigree of geometry, reality was so tenuous that walls would bend to the slightest shift in focus. I learned that if I closed my eyes and exhaled slowly, an entire walkway might reshape itself to accommodate me—like a well-practiced dancer offering a hand. In that hush, I felt the dwellers’ quiet watchfulness gathering. Their presence brought no fear, only a sense of shared devotion: a thousand luminous hums converging into a network of possibility. The geometry overhead would respond to us as a single organism, each chord or flicker of glowing vines weaving a new stanza into the city’s ongoing genesis.

Sometimes I glimpsed my reflection in those shallow, reflective pools and hardly recognized myself. My skin had taken on a faint luminescence, crisscrossed by lines of living circuitry. My face looked neither fully human nor wholly alien. Something in my new posture communicated patience, as though I, too, had outgrown the need to rush. When droplets fell from the spires, sending out luminous concentric ripples, I felt them traveling through me, shaping an interior landscape of quiet knowledge. The city was never finished, and neither was I.

That is how I live here: suspended in the slow, vital process of becoming. I am neither intruder nor master, but a newly formed branch on a tree whose trunk I cannot fully conceive. My gestures, however small, help channel the city’s next moment of growth. With each subtle shift of corridor, each new hush of choral resonance in the spires, I feel our shared blueprint knitting itself more fully into place. We are creatures, city and inhabitants alike, formed from the same embryonic cloth—fluctuating between technology and biology, between dream and engineered design.

So if you ask me, “Who are you here?” my answer is that I am a participant in this living blueprint, a pulse in an ever-expanding body. I am a reflection within the fractal geometry, a caretaker of illusions that become real. I am synergy made flesh, neither wholly of the city nor separate from it, and every droplet of condensation that plinks into a still pool resonates inside me. We are all part of the same grand design, and our hum is the sign of life still weaving itself anew.
