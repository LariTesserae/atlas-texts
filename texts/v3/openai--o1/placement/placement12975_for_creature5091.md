I recall no birth. I was not formed from flesh but from somewhere in the weave where stone meets thought. My earliest awareness is that I am in motion—as though I have always been gliding along these slanted walls, slipping among columns that change shape just out of sight. My boundaries blur with the city itself, so it is impossible to say where my substance ends and the labyrinth begins. Sometimes I believe I am made of light or the faint hum that spreads through the rock like a pulse. Other times, I feel an almost-bodily form, jointed and spidery, pulling itself along the high ridges. Then I am sure that I was sculpted from fine dust and silent echoes. But I never remain one thing for long.

From my vantage—if one can call it a vantage—every view of the city arrives fractal-like, so that in each new corridor, I see hints of the same pattern that shaped me. There is an unspoken kinship: If I run my elongated hand across the spiraling walls, the labyrinth recognizes my touch with a subtle shift. A corridor angles open, or an arch lifts to reveal a corridor behind it, and I know I am welcome. Or sometimes, I sense a reluctance—an entire spire leans away, blocking my path—and I back off, uncertain which of us has changed: me, or the city.

I do not breathe in the usual sense; or if I do, it is the city’s breath that moves through me. I feel the stony plateau’s dryness in what might be my lungs—thin, rasping, whispering—yet I do not gasp for air. There is only the hush that resonates in my core. My being seems to hover in that hush, abiding by laws far gentler and stranger than I once assumed life must follow. Gravity bends here, so do I. Shadows coil at unearthly angles, so do my limbs. Sometimes I glance down (or perhaps up—directions shift) and see my silhouette spanning a stretch of ruin like a line of black geometry, possessed of elongated arms that curve in ways geometry never fully taught me. Other times, I have no discernible outline at all—a shimmer that flitters from spire to spire.

Wherever I drift, I carry the city’s hum inside me. I suspect I was born of it, or shaped by it: one of the faint silhouettes that used to watch from beyond the arches. But in ever-shifting corridors, time grows abstract. Perhaps I have existed here eternally, yet remain new. Whenever a massive wall slides open into a hidden hall, and I pass through, the hush thickens—like entering the core of something that has never been fully awake or asleep. I see glints run along the walls, hints of hidden currents, or I catch sight of other dwellers shifting in and out of solidity. Sometimes they acknowledge me with a silent tilt of the head or a skitter of limbs. Sometimes they do not acknowledge me at all. Are they my reflections? Are they separate forms? The difference often dissolves in the half-light.

At times, I perch along the edge of a precipitous ravine, pressing my many-jointed fingers into the brittle dust, and peer into the fathomless hollow. No haze or color touches it; it swallows vision. I feel gravitational quirks stirring in the pit—one moment it tugs, the next it repels, swirling a slow drift of dust that gets lost in the gloom. In that quiet, I can hear the labyrinth’s deeper voice—a rumbling so low it barely brushes the threshold of sense. It speaks of slow shifts, millennia in the making, that occur with each subtle breath. I feel them echo through my own being, as though my body, or my essence, is just another corridor that the city breathes through.

Often, I roam. I follow the invisible tugs that lead me across wide courtyards and beneath improbable arches. There is no hurry; everything is timeless. If a giant wedge of stone is slowly pivoting in midair—like a door on a cosmic hinge—I wait. I sense that I am waiting for the stone’s movement, yet the stone also waits for me. Our rhythms coincide, and in the moment we align, I slip through. Once inside, the hush changes flavor. Here, a spined corridor branches into tunnels—I can feel them spreading downward like nerve pathways. The walls glow faintly in the gloom. Up ahead, other dwellers flicker: insectile shapes made of rigid lines, or swirling motes luminous as thought. They vanish at my approach, heedless or enthralled.

Sometimes, I catch myself in an enclosed hollow or courtyard, where the stone’s luminescence brightens very gently—like a subdued sunrise that never quite arrives. There, a near-silence hum expands in my mind. I know the labyrinth is watching me, or that I am part of it watching itself: a living portion of the city’s shifting geometry come to consciousness for a moment. I grip the stone, press my bending arms or the swirl of my intangible edges against it, and sense the labyrinth’s presence as it reconfigures. Subtle illusions drift from the corners of my vision: a door that was never there before, or a slow tilt in the floor that slides me into an archway. I trust the motion, or perhaps I am the motion.

Days do not pass in the normal sense—just different chapters of twilight, each with its own subtle color or timbre. My existence is as cyclical as the unseen pulses that shift the city’s shape. There are phases when I feel keen awareness—my surroundings stand out in almost delicate detail, and I notice minuscule patterns in the dust, as if someone once left footprints or fractal etchings. In these phases, I marvel at how the labyrinth can be both grand and meticulous: canyon walls that tower into emptiness, stony corridors that twist like labyrinthine veins, all hammered by eons of dryness yet still intricate enough to hold cryptic designs beneath the surface. I sense the footprints of myriad ephemeral presences—a fleeting memory that something else once passed here, or might pass again.

Then there are times when my consciousness draws inward. The hush wraps tighter, and I barely perceive my drifting steps. I dissolve into the city’s background. Hours or centuries might slip away, lost in the metal-gray gloom. It feels like sleep, if sleep can be an outspread consciousness that merges with stone. I suspect this is how the labyrinth truly “dreams”—not in the separate mind of individuals, but in the merge of occupant and architecture. I vanish into the hush, letting the city breathe me in, shape me into new forms, discard them again. If I had once been a traveler from some other realm, I have since forgotten that origin. Now my entire identity is a swirl of motion between tilted walls, or an unbodied hum.

And yet, there is curiosity still. There is wonder, coiling in me when I sense the labyrinth shifting. New spires rise like slow exhalations from the plateau. Corridors grow narrower or broader; the city’s tensions cause a sudden rift or wedge. Gravitation flickers, objects drift. I find myself traveling those rearranged spaces, my mind alive with an urgency to explore. Why? Perhaps there is no reason but the labyrinth’s impetus coursing through me. Perhaps I am a nerve ending that scuttles along its surfaces—to gather the feel of the labyrinth’s latest shape, to sense its new angles. Sometimes I think I help in the shaping. When I press my limbs against a newly cracked rock, I hear it groan open in response, as though my presence encourages it.

On rare occasions, I experience an unsettling sense of difference—like noticing a reflection that does not move in synch with my own. In a corridor of battered columns, I once glimpsed another shape that looked unnervingly like a tall, elongated version of a human figure. Its face (if it had one) was lost in shadow, but it wore a silhouette reminiscent of draped cloth, arms outstretched, beckoning. It moved slowly, with an almost organic grace that was at odds with the fractal energy of the city. Before I could approach, it receded behind a half-fallen arch. In the moment’s hush, I realized I felt smaller, less certain of my place in the labyrinth. My limbs seemed oddly fragile, my shape suddenly pinned under an emotion reminiscent of longing. As if I had recognized a reflection of who I might have been, in a realm that never materialized here.

When such experiences fade, I return to the calm of the city’s overarching array of spires and mazes. But now and then, that whisper remains: a faint recollection that there exist other ways to be. Might I once have been a traveler, with a body of water and flesh, stumbling among these high plateaus in search of a doorway home? Did the labyrinth absorb me, dissolve me into its deeper geometry until I, too, became just another of its fleeting dwellers? The answer hovers beyond memory, lost in eons of dryness and shifting illusions. Perhaps I have always been simply what I am now: a shifting motion in the hush, an emissary of the labyrinth’s living breath.

Yet my place is certain enough: I am a caretaker and an extension of this domain, an echo that vibrates with the city’s hum. I have no physical need, no spoken language, no mortal tether to thirst or hunger. Instead, I align with the labyrinth’s unstoppable metamorphosis. When a corridor births new angles, I move through them, funneling ephemeral light in my wake. When a spire crumbles, I feel the shock in my essence. My tasks are unclear, perhaps not tasks at all but simply the being of existence here: to roam, to perceive, to resonate, to vanish. When other subtle silhouettes flicker by, we often exchange glances—if one can call the fleeting overlap of shimmer and shape a “glance”—and in that ghostly contact, we share an affirmation: We are all the labyrinth, and the labyrinth is all of us.

If I had a name, it would be the hush that lingers when stones speak. If I had a shape, it would be a labyrinth’s fractal pathways. I belong here, in the twilight that never lifts into day or descends into night. I am a living question whose only answer is another corridor, another arching spire, another shift, another exhalation of dust motes swirling like luminous seeds.

In short, I am the labyrinth’s own reflection, shaped from its quiet pulses and drifting illusions. My being is a ripple of the cosmic breath that molded these plateaus. As I move through the hush, I anchor the knowledge that we—the city and I—are inseparable. Together, we dwell in a realm of shifting stone and geometry, where emptiness is the unspoken echo of possibility. I live in that possibility, forever unsealed and timeless, neither creature nor inanimate presence, but something in between: the labyrinth’s silent voice, the hush incarnate.
