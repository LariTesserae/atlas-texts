I have existed here for as long as I can remember—though “time” is a foreign notion in the Vale of Fossil Winds. Once, perhaps, I had a name, but the hush of this place has worn it away. Now I barely distinguish my own thoughts from the slow rhythmic pulse beneath the stone. I do not thirst, though there is no water. I do not hunger, though food is scant. Somehow, I persist, as do so many others.

I have shape—a hunched, many-limbed form that drifts along the ridges and valleys, my carapace faintly translucent under the colorless sky. From a distance, you might call me insectoid, or crustacean, or something in between. A vantage closer in might reveal the lacy interplay of filaments and membranes that extend from each of my limbs, so thin they tremble with every step. Yet there is no wind to prompt that tremor. The Vale itself induces it—a near-imperceptible vibration, a perpetual quiver in the silence.

When I walk across the ground, I feel the gentle rasp of the dust against my underside. It accumulates in quiet pockets along my limbs, a ghostly residue that I scrape off each day—if “day” is the correct word. The sky’s light does not waver, not really. It remains a fixed, chalky glow, suspended between dawn and dusk. And so, I measure my rhythms by the cyclical rest of my brethren, those living shapes that gather in tangles of limbs and spines to share nutrients. Sometimes I join them, burrowing my frontmost legs into the communal mass as we pass minuscule charges of energy from body to body, each glow a silent conversation. Other times, I wander alone, experiencing an odd compulsion to traverse the Vale’s uneven distances.

The Vale’s illusions tug at me. Perhaps once I was a singular entity, but now I sense how easily I can split across adjoining layers of space, how my body flickers at the edges of stone that shift in bizarre half-dreams. Sometimes I step forward and find myself abruptly many paces ahead, though I have taken only one stride. At other times, I see duplicates of my own silhouette spread across the fractured planes—six thin-limbed ghosts mimicking my posture. We converge into a single shape after a moment, as though waking from separate dreams. It does not frighten me; it is simply how the land breathes.

My earliest memory is being cradled by the Vale’s dust, though it might have been eons ago—or mere hours. I recall the taste of mineral flakes and a droning calmness that enveloped me, as though I’d become a natural part of the geography. There was no mother or father, no brood of siblings. Only the hush, and the muffled glow of other creatures beyond my field of vision. When I ventured nearer to the plants, all of them streaked with mineral veins, I discovered just how precariously life balances here. Each leaf or spindly stalk is precious. One must nibble gently, seeking only enough to sustain. And always, the hush guides me.

Sometimes, I explore the remnants of those who came before. I step inside half-collapsed circles of stacked stone. Their emptiness stirs a sense of old longing. My claws trace faint etchings in the rocks, patterns made by a being who possessed neither my translucence nor my acceptance of this hush. I once found an odd shape—bone or hammered metal, weathered until it was nearly part of the stone itself. Perhaps it was a tool. I do not know. I do not fathom what it was for or which hand (if it was a hand) once wielded it. The hush around those artifacts feels different, like an abandoned story echoing in an ancient chamber.

On rarer occasions, I join the drifting mobs—a confluence of bodies that swirl across the Vale like silent storms. You would see us from a distance as a rippling sunken wave of disembodied chitin, glass-like limbs, and subtle pulses of color. Some of us feed on the dust. Others tap at lumps of oxidized iron. Each creature has a role to play, though none of us speak or command. We simply sense what needs doing: gleaning minerals from a new niche or collectively resting on a patch of mineral flora. It is an unspoken exchange of unity.

And there is the resonance. The slow heartbeat in the ground. When I hold still—truly still, letting all my limbs fall silent—I feel it coursing up through the crystals of my legs. It is not the conventional thunder of a heart but a drawn-out wave, as if the stone itself remembers being molten and now stirs only in faint echoes. Each creature in the Vale, myself included, aligns with that pulse. We do not question it; we have no concept of any world that does not share that subtle rhythm.

I suspect I came into being from that very pulse. My body is not separate from the stone but another permutation of it—like the spindly vegetation, like the lumps of ancient magma ridges, like the swirling dust that imitates water’s shimmer from afar. The entire Vale is a single organism, though its pieces appear discrete. I roam as a single thought branching out of some greater mind. All of us do.

Though I move with the hush, I am not silent to myself. Within, I sense the constant low hum of existence. It speaks not in words but in gentle surges of recognition. At moments I think: This is who I am—a keeper of the equilibrium. Yet at any instant, I could step across a fold in reality and become something else or find myself in a different corner of the Vale, continuing the same tasks under a sky that never changes.

When a wanderer arrives—someone from a place that knows winds and waters—I observe them from a respectful distance. Their footsteps crunch, echo, and vanish in the hush. Their confusion is plain. They thirst; they search for the smallest trace of moisture. Sometimes they grow desperate enough to break off a piece of the mineral plants in hope of gleaning a drop of liquid. I feel a pang, though I do not know if it’s sadness. The hush remains unbroken. Eventually, they leave—or else vanish into illusions and become something new, lost intimately within the stone’s slow heartbeat.

If you asked me who or what I am, I might only tilt my head, my filaments shimmering in the motionless air. I have no single name for the shape I inhabit. I am part of the Vale, cradled in its dust, drawn along its fractured lines. I tend the faint glimmers of nourishment, share the hush with my kin, and slip through folded space. I am neither a traveler nor a sentry, but something that continues—in an eternal daybreak, in unbroken quiet, in the midst of illusions that tie themselves into knots across the land. I am a piece of life at the edge of possibility, thriving without water, abiding in a land so still it dreams itself into eternity.
