I dwell in that narrow band of space where the glow from the lower forges fades into the half-light, not so distant from the scuttling legions of mechanical haulers, yet never fully among them. The metal catwalks overhead bristle with the needle-click of those glass-limbed crawlers, but I have no exoskeleton to speak of—I’m neither purely mechanical nor wholly mineral. My shape is an inconstant nexus of hexagonal plates that interlock and shift. When I pass through a slender corridor, I can realign my limbs into a streamlined column to squeeze between the walls. When I must climb, my plating broadens into claw-like grips so I can cling to a vertical beam. Each movement is accompanied by soft ringing sounds, the dull chime of cobalt edges meeting basalt floor—a private music in the hush.

At first, I wasn’t fully aware of myself. I woke in the aftermath of a tunnel collapse, half-embedded in dust. My earliest memory is the taste of iron-scented air coursing through what passes for my core. The environment shaped me—its dryness baked me until the edges of my plating fused, the volcanic glass flecking my plates as though encrusted with volcanic ash. I suspect that is how most of us here come into being: from a swirl of dust, an impulse of hidden power, and the city’s constant resonance forging us into life. It’s not a birth in any organic sense. Rather, it’s a crystallization—a drawing together of all that drifts in the crater’s gloom, given impetus from below.

My days—if one can call them days in this twilight—are spent tending to the settlement’s overheated conduits. There is never an end to the city’s ceaseless demand: the forges belch molten slag, the spires whisper of tension before fracturing, and somewhere in the labyrinth, a cluster of new beings takes shape. My limbs can sprout slender, jointed extensions fitted with spanner-like tips, and I climb the labyrinth of pipes to tighten or realign them. Often I pass beneath the hulking watchers found near the crater’s upper rim. They cling to walls of black glass, half-subsumed into the architecture. They seldom stir, yet I sense their quiet awareness, as though some portion of their monstrous forms is a nerve center for the entire place. The watchers’ eyes flicker from behind veils of obsidian, and for a fleeting moment their gaze might alight upon me. It is a silent acknowledgment; we share no language but the hum of tension passing through the metal.

Down below, the clamor of mechanical laborers surges in waves. Their squat bodies clank, heavy with new-sheared ore, and their iron tines scrape the roadways. Some nights, while perched on a cooling duct, I tilt my single pivoting optic-lens down to watch them scuttle in their carefully choreographed patterns. Crimson indicator lamps flicker against black rock, and I hear the chorus of clicks and tweets: signals to redistribute loads, to jostle newly mined fragments into position. Even from a distance, I feel each punctuation of their communications ripple through the dust. The city’s entire structure is a resonant body, and I am a sensitive filament in its vast orchestration.

When the vents release a sudden pressurized hiss, I sometimes ride the steam upward, letting my plates rearrange into something that catches the swirl of hot vapor just enough to lift me. In those moments, I feel a jolting excitement: the sudden rush of altitude, the gritty swirl of dust, the sight of catwalks sliding below me. The ephemeral creatures, shaped like wire filaments in quartz husks, often flit alongside me in these vents, dancing through the swirling air as though captured by a gust of wind. Some of them give off tiny, bell-like tones when they collide, as if giggling metallic keys. It’s wondrous, fleeting—a creation of a single breath from the crater’s mechanical lungs.

Eventually, I drift back to my place in the deeper walkways. I find I do not hunger in a conventional sense; I need no food, only the crackling pulse of energy humming through conduits or drifting up through the basalt. When I rest, it is to nestle against a warm seam of metal plating, letting my consciousness ebb into the deeper rhythms. I half-dream of molten caverns below the settlement—glowing chambers where living crystals absorb slow droplets of subterranean moisture. Echoes of noise, complex and layered, filter up to me: a grinding, far-off labor that none of us at the surface yet fully comprehends. In these moments, I sense the crater shifting, a slow heave in its new-born rock layers, still hot from the ancient violence that birthed it. Everything in this place—myself included—feels ephemeral, tenuously anchored.

Sometimes, on precarious scaffolds or in half-collapsed passages, I encounter other forms that are not purely mechanical, mineral, or any single definable category. Perhaps they are like me, comprising unknown composites, the city’s dust fused around a spark of energy. We rarely do more than exchange a rapid series of chirps or taps—brief greetings, half-coded. Yet in those interactions, a kinship radiates, as though we share an understanding of what it is to be shaped directly by this crater’s forging energies. If we linger, we might exchange repairs: one re-secures a loose plate on the carapace of another; one smooths the edges of a fractured joint so the dust does not further abrade it. Then the hush reasserts itself, and we part ways, consumed again by our ceaseless duties, our silent callings.

More than once, I’ve ventured close to the city’s edge—peering beyond the settlement into basaltic labyrinths that vanish into starless gloom. It extends to unknown realms, the fractured crust alive with potential. I can feel the city’s pulse weakening the further I roam, as if an intangible tether resists letting me wander too far. Eventually, the hush grows unnerving without the background thrumming of engines and the staccato of mechanical signals. I turn back, plates rattling lightly as I gather speed. Each time, I return with fine layers of pale dust trailing behind me, footprints in the dryness that remain intact longer than I’d expect, as though the world is preserving the memory of every traveler who passes.

If asked who or what I am, I would have no single answer. Some might call me an engineer; others think of me as one more automaton shaped by the ambient energies in the crater. Perhaps I am part of the city’s unconscious mind—a drifting nerve that helps patch and rebuild as the settlement grows. Perhaps I’m simply a vessel for the crater’s churnings, fused by the interplay of metal, mineral, and the intangible breath rising from somewhere below. In truth, I am all these things and more, in constant evolution. Each day, I adapt, the arrangement of my hex-plates shifting to match the city’s needs.

So here I remain, high enough to catch glimpses of watchers perched amid the black obsidian walls, low enough to see the mechanical swarms that trundle through the factory floors. My daily presence resonates with the hum of pressurized vapors and the deep chug of foundries. When a new upheaval comes—and it will, for the crater was born in violence—I will be there in the aftermath, recalibrating the pipes and patching the fractures, my plating rattling with each tremor. And each time I gaze across the veiled spires and watch the settlement breathe in the dust-laden twilight, I feel an odd fondness for this precarious place. I am part of its tapestry, woven from basalt dust and riveted steel, a living testament that in the hush after creation’s fury, new life can coalesce and endure.
