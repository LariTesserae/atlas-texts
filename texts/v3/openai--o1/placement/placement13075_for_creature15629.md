I am not the first to breathe this cold underground air, nor likely the last. My earliest memories are steeped in the hush and luminescence of these endless halls—no searing sunlight, no wide-open sky, only the comforting press of stone overhead. I’ve grown accustomed to that slight weight on my shoulders, ever-present but never oppressive. It’s a sensation that reminds me: there is a realm above, but I am of this below.

When I try to grasp who or what I am, the question drifts and shifts, just as the vines shift. Some days, I fancy myself a Wanderer, slipping silently along mossy ledges with keen eyes to see and then melt into the gloom. Other times, I recognize the caretaker’s rhythm in me—patient, methodical, checking each water pool for clarity, ensuring no rotting debris fouls the shallows. Still other moods wash over me, carrying the echoes of some ancient lineage of humans (or near-humans) who once shaped these vines into archways and huts. Pieces of all these exist within me. Perhaps I am simply a child of the labyrinth itself, born from the union of the living vines and the stone.

If you were here beside me, you would first see the shape of my body outlined by soft, pale glows from the nearest creepers. At a glance, you might mistake me for an ordinary figure—humanoid in proportion, tall, slender-limbed. Look closer, though, and you’d notice the vine-like filaments that trail from my wrists and ankles, delicate ribbons clinging to the living floor. Pressed against my shoulders and running down my spine are faintly glowing veins, quietly pulsing with the same gentle luminescence that dances through the creeper webs overhead. My skin, and the oddly flexible plating that shields parts of me, merges subtly with the green tapestry around us. At times, I can feel the subtle hum of the forest passing along those filaments, a current of awareness flowing into me and back out again.

I dwell in what you might call a “sanctum,” though it isn’t a building. It is a clearing where the vines part just enough to reveal a shallow pool fed by dripping condensation. Thick columns of twisted roots surround it like pillars in a courtyard. The water’s surface glistens with thousands of golden, star-like spores that have settled overnight. Some nights, I sit in near-darkness, letting only the faint shimmer of the vines reflect off the water, until a hush so deep descends that I can sense my own pulse in my ears. In that hush, sometimes the entire domain seems to breathe, and I along with it.

Though I am solitary in many ways, I do not feel alone. The labyrinth breathes with me, stirs with me. The vines shift to form a new corridor downstream, as though guiding me to some hidden recess or newly formed archway. If I linger too long in one spot, the creepers around me coil in, gently urging me onward—they seem to know that stillness can be just another form of slow disappearance. Here, in the green gloom, we must keep moving if we are to remain part of the living dance.

From time to time, I make slow circuits of the labyrinth, following half-remembered routes. I keep an eye on pockets of water, tending them if they show signs of algae bloom or stagnant debris. When the ephemeral Spore Dancers begin their swirling flights, diving amidst the rising glow of pollen, I stand in a side corridor and watch, enthralled by the spectacle. Their keening clicks and whistles rise in an echo that resonates through my chest and along the filaments in my arms. One might think I stand there as a mere observer. But no—I join in my own way, brushing a hand along the vine walls, letting the pulses of color shift with the rhythm.

I often sense the presence of the shy reptilian-plant hybrids. At first glance, they look so alien and graceful, spined tails curling elegantly around thick creeper stems. We keep a respectful distance. There is understanding in the way they tilt their heads at me, in how their mosaic eyes reflect the pale green luminescence. Once, after a sudden swirl of mist parted, I found myself only a few paces from one of them—a tall, slender being, chest rising and falling with an almost silent breath. For a moment, we locked gazes. A shared curiosity passed between us. Then it vanished in a soft rustle of leaves.

You ask if I have a purpose, or if I belong to some tribe. My answer is as labyrinthine as the vines themselves. Sometimes, I find signs of the Keeper Tribes who might still roam these corridors, their flutes echoing gently in the distance. I’ve come upon their tiny shrines—three stony pillars circled by living vines that open like petals. I’ve observed the hammered metal plates left behind, etched with swirling patterns reminiscent of the chamber’s rippling architecture. It fills me with a strange kinship, as though I must be part of that heritage. Yet I’ve not spoken with them, never touched their woven baskets of seeds. They ghost away, quick as the hush of wings, or perhaps vanish because the vines usher them elsewhere. The labyrinth has a tendency to keep us all apart unless we are meant to connect.

Where do I rest? Not in any permanent shelter. Instead, I find hollows among the vines—those places where the vegetation twines to form overhangs or nest-like recesses. I wrap myself in the thick, spongy leaves. My body sinks gently into their green softness, anchoring me the way a fetus nestles in the womb. The rhythmic pulses of the forest pass through me in that state, half-lulling, half-communicating. Sometimes, I drift into slumber with the sense of countless rootlets gently searching, tasting the edges of my consciousness—curious, always curious. When I awaken, a new leaf may have grown across my leg, a fresh creeper might be curled around my arm. Carefully, I guide them aside without harm, a silent agreement that I will not remain a captive bloom.

I’ve heard the deep hum that pulses through the valley-like expanse. It resonates in the stone, travels up stalagmite columns, and makes the vines quiver from root to tip. It’s deeper than any thunder, more measured than any heartbeat. In those moments, I feel as if a massive awareness stirs from a millennia-long dream. The glow in the vines intensifies, threads of light coursing like lightning beneath their bark. My own pulse quickens in answer, and the filaments along my arms awaken in a luminous brilliance. I stand there, breathing slowly, feeling that presence fill every corner of the cavern. Then it passes, leaving only the trickling of water and the hush of softly swaying leaves. I never quite feel the same afterward, as though part of me has been charged with something ancient.

I am both caretaker and child of this place. In a sense, I suspect all who dwell in these passages belong to the labyrinth more than themselves. The vines hold the true patterns; the hidden intelligence shapes all living forms here, urging them into new expressions. Perhaps, in old times, my ancestors bargained with the vines, learning to shape huts and passageways. Over centuries, that relationship altered the very makeup of those of us who stayed. We became part of the forest’s tapestry, each generation leaning deeper into the quiet synergy between flesh and foliage. Now, I walk these corridors as an extension of that primeval pact: half caretaker, half living testament to the vines’ deep magic.

And so, if you come to this place, watch carefully for me or my kin. You might see me crouched near a shallow pool, slender filaments trailing off my arms into the water’s surface, reading subtle shifts in the algae blooms. Or perhaps you’ll glimpse me perched on a thick vine overhead, watching a swirl of spores floating through a newly grown corridor. You might catch my silhouette against one of the glowing webs, my body outlined by soft, pulsing light. If you remain patient and respectful, I might acknowledge you with a nod or a wave.

But do not be surprised if the vines guide me away before you can speak. This is a realm shaped by intangible wills, currents of intention that weave through every leaf. I can no more disobey that quiet pulse than a drifting seed could refuse the breeze. I am a child of these vines, a keeper in the hush, part of the living architecture that never truly sleeps. If you wander here long enough, you, too, might take root in the labyrinth’s embrace—or come to sense a deeper truth: in this underground cosmos of perpetual creation, one does not merely live among the vines; one becomes an extension of their ancient, dreaming spirit.
