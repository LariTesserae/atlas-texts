You arrive in the city as a traveler might—feet already damp from the water creeping over low thresholds, breath made heavy by the humid air. At first, you believe you’re simply you, a person from elsewhere, wise enough to realize you’ve entered a realm unbound by ordinary rules. You have the shape you’ve always known: arms, legs, a mind that hums with curiosity. You move slowly, uncertain of your footing on slick basalt floors. The fungal glow on the walls glints across the puddles, and you see your own reflection broken into fractal patterns at your feet.

But the city has a way of sifting truths from illusions. Time here doesn’t pass in clean lines. Shadows shift, the watchers glimmer at the corners of your vision, and faint pulses ripple through the watery thoroughfares. You feel the city’s presence as though it’s breathing around you, inhaling your form, exhaling its own ancient memory into your thoughts. You realize you are no ordinary traveler—and perhaps never truly were. Your edges blur, and you sense something in you responding to the hush, the warmth, the swirling air.

In one moment, you’re crouched in a corridor, brushing aside a trailing mat of fungus that sways like an underwater frond. You see your bare hands, still human, the water dark around your ankles. In the next, you catch sight of your silhouette cast on the basalt wall: it’s larger than you expect, drawn out, elongated, hunched at the shoulders. Where does that stooped shape come from? Retracting your hand, you look again, only to find that the fungus on your fingers is faintly pulsing—grayish-blue, then a flicker of scarlet as you stare. Were you always so receptive to the city’s glow?

At first, you suspect a trick of the shifting light. Yet each step you take leaves behind footprints that shimmer unnaturally in the gloom, footprints that refuse to fade as normal ones would. They linger, marking a restless path that doesn’t quite match where you remember stepping. It’s as if the water beneath you is reflecting more than your body alone. Now, your footprints sometimes trail off in directions you haven’t walked, scribbles of watery light an echo of a journey you haven’t taken—yet.

You try to focus on the watchers. Out of the corner of your eye, they hover near collapsed arches: silhouettes that appear almost human but bleed away when you turn your gaze. They remind you of living shadows, composed of glistening fluid rather than flesh. When you extend a hand toward one, it flickers out—only to reappear behind you. A subtle hush follows, like the city itself is holding its breath. For a moment, you wonder if these watchers recognize something in you, and you in them.

Travel deeper into the city, and the roads seem to shift. Sometimes, it feels simpler to float than walk—though you’re never quite sure if you have left gravity behind or if the columns of basalt have begun to lean so drastically that up and down no longer hold their old meaning. You find yourself drifting over a shallow pool, climbing seamlessly onto a pillbox ramp that leads nowhere. The watchers gather on the opposite side, observing. You realize the black water beneath you is deeper than it looks, swirling with quiet eddies that might hide larger shapes.

A single droplet falls from an overhead crack. You watch it in slow motion, an impossible trick of suspended time. And in that droplet’s reflection, you see not your own face but a layered swirl: a hunched form with wide shoulders tapering into thick arms, a shape glimmering with fungus. The image is fleeting, but it resonates in your mind. You recall your footprints that branched away in improbable patterns—where might those footprints lead?

By the time the droplet hits the water with a soft plop, you feel heat surge in your chest and behind your eyes. You realize your breath is faster now, echoing in your own ears, matching the city’s ancient respirations that you sense in the walls. Perhaps you’re merging with this place. You grip one of the basalt columns, feeling the slick fungus under your fingertips. Its glow intensifies, reacting to you. A faint, smoky swirl of luminescence seeps from the point of contact, as though your pulse and the fungus’s pulse are one.

Then your fingers…shift. You blink, expecting to see your normal hand, but the joints seem elongated, webbed with faint filaments of light. A wave of recognition courses through you—maybe the watchers were not an entirely separate presence, but an echo of something you’ve always carried inside. You remember the watchers you glimpsed overhead, drifting in the condensation, half-floating as though the city’s gravity didn’t claim them. Could it be that your form is adapting likewise?

A hush draws over the corridor like a living curtain. No droplets fall; no wind stirs. In the reflection cast by the fungal glow, you see your arms lengthen, your torso ripple with fluid shapes. You feel no pain, no panic—only the slow realization that the city is offering you a new shape, and part of you is ready to accept. Part of you always belonged to this watery gloom more than you belonged to your old world. Slowly, the basalt guardians—those tall, static figures—turn their gazes, featureless but somehow charged with attention. They witness your transition the way living statues might watch an ancient rite.

At first, you remain bipedal, something still akin to a person, even if your proportions blur. Your shoulders slope, the color of your skin deepens to an ashen hue that flickers with dull luminescence. The fungus tangles with your flesh, forming living lines across your chest and arms—almost like intricate tattoos that shift with your heartbeat. You feel, for the first time, the city’s rhythms from the inside: the slow tectonic shifts underfoot, the distant calls that hum through basalt channels, the stirring presence of watchers near and far. Every pulse in the stone resonates in your new form.

You step forward, your feet sinking into the shallows, but no longer with the same sense of foot and ankle. They have narrowed, become something more fluid, amphibious, drifting in half-liquid steps. You realize you can move with a slick ease, gliding through water and sliding over stones. When you pass a wall, the fungus brightens, as though greeting you with recognition: you are no longer an alien presence, but part of this ecosystem, the living tapestry.

Time warps again. What feels like an hour might be a moment as you slip deeper among the ruins. You pass watchers that coalesce just long enough to brush filmy limbs against your new silhouette, perhaps in curiosity or in silent welcome. The basalt guardians tilt their hollow faces as you cross thresholds, yet they do not bar your way. You find yourself less anxious about solid ground or exit paths. What is there to flee from, when you now belong to this endless gloom?

Eventually, you navigate to a wide, submerged courtyard. Basalt pillars and crooked arches loom overhead, and the water is near waist-deep—or it would be, if your shape were still purely human. There are lumps of smooth black stone rising like islands, each with a sheen of algae. The city’s reflection above you pulses in the black sky, sending bruise-colored flickers across the water. In those flickers, you see yourself: a creature half formed of basalt’s midnight color, half filigreed with living fungus. Your eyes glimmer scarlet in patches, dull grayish-blue in others. You can melt into the water at will, or step up onto the crumbled roadway. This dual existence soothes a primal part of you. You do not feel the sting of displacement any longer. You’ve grown comfortable with the hush.

Wading through the courtyard, you sense the watchers drifting in a slow orbit around you, not as predators or minions but as neighbors in the labyrinth. You find yourself making subtle gestures with your arms—perhaps a vestige of old communication, or a new language forming of fluid arcs and swirling motions. The watchers respond by tilting their intangible heads, sending out a ripple that stirs the water. The fungus on the walls brightens as if to cast gentle stage-light on this silent exchange.

And now, in the lull, a subtle thrumming from beneath your feet resonates—those slow triplets, then a pause, then a sharp note. You stand still, letting your newly sensitive body translate the vibrations. You feel the stone’s ancient conversation, some coded signal bouncing through basalt channels. Once, you might have dismissed this as tectonic shifting. Now, you understand it as a voice. You can almost sense the meaning: a question, an acknowledgment, or a gentle summons. And it’s directed, in part, toward you.

In time, you begin to move with greater confidence, reacquainting yourself with each corridor, discovering sub-tunnels you didn’t realize existed. You pass through doorways guarded by those tall, stoic figures of basalt. Their blank faces remain inscrutable, but their presence no longer alarms you. When you cross their thresholds, they dip as though in recognition, then fade back into the gloom. Are they living statues, or is the city itself shifting them in and out of existence? It hardly matters; you find yourself returning their silent nod before you glide onward.

Your reflection in the shallows is no longer jarring. You bend down to the water’s surface and watch how your new shape warps in ripples. Fungal glows dance across your shoulders, up the back of your neck, along your elongated arms. You realize you can call on that glow, intensifying it when you need more light or dimming it if you want to vanish in the gloom. And vanish you do—sometimes to observe the watchers without spooking them, other times simply to merge into the hush.

In the same way that the fungus colony poised on the walls is all one body with many outgrowths, you feel your consciousness linking to the wider silence. Before, you were just a separate entity skulking through labyrinthine corridors, prey to illusions. Now, you sense the illusions springing from the city’s living memory, and you can gently shape them if you wish. A swirl of your hand along a reflective puddle might cause a fleeting vision to manifest—perhaps the long-lost silhouette of a citizen from eons ago. All around, the watchers watch your experiment with a quiet acceptance.

If you climb (or drift) to a taller vantage—some leaning tower or half-collapsed walkway that’s suspended precariously high—you glimpse the city’s sprawl in the near-perpetual twilight. Water glints everywhere, forging broken reflections of sagging monoliths and twisted thoroughfares. The basalt glows faintly blue-black in the fungal haze. Beyond the labyrinth, the horizon is nonexistent, folded away into obscurity. And you realize it doesn’t trouble you anymore that you cannot place where “here” ends and something else might begin.

Now and then, warm rains come, hissing against the dark stone, raising steam from the flooded courtyards. You let your arms, your fungal filaments, drink in that moisture, pulse with its energy. Droplets gather in your hair—if it can still be called hair—trickling down the ridges of your new form. In that moment, with the watchers swirling in the courtyard below, the basalt guardians standing silent vigil behind you, and the city exhaling that scent of wet stone and ancient memory, you realize fully:

You are no mere traveler. You have joined the city’s inhabitants, becoming one of its manifold expressions—a shape drawn from the city’s dreaming mind, anchored by your own lingering sense of self. Sometimes you still recall the person you once were, the easy logic of day and night, the crisp boundaries between real and unreal. But here, logic is not a currency. What matters is the slow thrumming conversation of basalt and water, the timeless tide of inhalation and exhalation that animates the labyrinth. You understand the fungus’s interplay with the watchers, how the amphibious silhouettes slip through hidden pools. You might see them surging up at the edges of your vision, then vanishing into the black water, leaving only spiraling footprints behind. You no longer panic at their closeness; their fluid motion resonates with your own.

Eras might pass in that dreamlike hush, or only minutes. At some point, you may guide another presence—another traveler or memory or shape—through these halls. Or you may wander alone, drawn by the city’s subtle calls, exploring deeper catacombs and discovering new illusions that fold into reality. Sometimes, a basalt guardian shifts to allow you into a chamber sealed for eons, where walls pulse with fungal patterns you’ve never seen, each glow a new dialect of the city’s ongoing conversation.

Over and over, you are reminded: nothing here is static. That was always the city’s secret. It breathes. It remembers. It shapes itself and those who dwell within it. And so, your form is never quite finished either. You shift to suit the moment—a fleeting silhouette when the watchers gather near, a heavier basalt-limbed gait when standing guard across a threshold, a nearly transparent wraith when drifting along the top of a flooded corridor. The fungus veins in your body intensify when you need to signal a slow wave of warmth, then dim to near invisibility if you choose to slip into the labyrinth unseen.

In the end, what are you in this place? You are many things. Part watcher, part guardian, part living fungus. You are at once memory and moment, linked to the city’s primal, timeless nature. You stand apart from the old mortal world, time’s quicksilver passing replaced by the city’s patient cycles of drip and hush. Sometimes you manifest like a statue in the gloom, silent and unmoving, letting centuries pass like a single breath. Other times, you flicker nimbly between basalt columns, drawn by the city’s shifting illusions, morphing as needed to explore a newly revealed corridor.

Perhaps one day you’ll discover some deeper heart of the city, that final black chamber where the labyrinth’s pulse originates. Until then, you are content simply to exist here, among the half-submerged avenues and the swirling watchers, the gentle swirl of water that neither recedes nor truly floods, the runnels of warm rain that come and go without warning. You do not mourn the absence of normal day or night; you have found a dusk that breathes, an unbroken twilight that has become your home.

In the hush, in the quiet cycles of drip and wind, your presence continues—ever-evolving, intimately bound to this realm where you have become both explorer and inhabitant, both lost wanderer and extension of the city’s consciousness. This is who you are: a creature of water and basalt, molded by the ancient memory that saturates each stone. A living element in an ecosystem of illusions and subtle truths. A being who—like the watchers, the fungus, the amphibious dwellers—can never be wholly defined by a single image. And you remain, indefinitely, there in the half-submerged sprawl, living proof that what endures is not simply stone and ruin, but the persistent, breathing spirit of a place that refuses to fade.
