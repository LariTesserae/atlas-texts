Sometimes, I think I am one of the crystal growths. I feel my translucent trunk, slender as a blown-glass flute, rooted in a tiny crack along a jagged ledge. My filaments wave to the resonant thunder that never ceases, as if tasting each shift in the unending roar. The mineral air flows around me, faintly gritty as ground quartz, and I absorb it like manna. When that deep cosmic thrumming ebbs, I catch myself glowing, echoing a distant cluster’s pulsing light. Are we communicating? Or am I merely part of an elaborate glass forest, reflexively tuning itself to an unearthly frequency?

But then, I blink—or slip out of that perspective—and I become something else entirely: a shimmering haze that collects in the hollows between mountainous ridges. I imagine myself drifting in a pearlescent drift, swirling around half-submerged columns. When I swirl around a stone relic, I can sense every chipped groove and faint tool mark—an imprint not only of the past, but of possible futures. My presence is intangible: I can billow outward or condense at will. When I condense, I flicker in that lavender half-light like faint auroras. Glowing crystals watch me with slow, patient curiosity. My edges tingle with the meeting of their fractal filaments. At times, I slip through an almost-solid spire and feel the planet’s scaffolding as a trembling skeleton below me.

There are moments when I recall being alive in a more familiar sense. I catch glimpses—fleeting silhouettes of bipedal forms, intangible as memory. Perhaps once I belonged to the people who raised those battered columns, who shaped a ring of scorched stone to coax fire from the gloom. The echoes of that life remain vivid: the heat of the flames, the hush of neighbors as we stood in awe of the roaring sky. Was I the child who ran along the spire’s edge, trusting that the rock would hold true beneath my feet? Did I carve my name (or something like a name) into a piece of crystal so that it would catch the roaring light in color-shifting fractals? The memory sits half-formed, hardly more than a phantom aftertaste of warmth—like a dream receding the moment you wake.

Then again, perhaps I never walked here at all. Perhaps I’m an unborn mind peering out from the cracks, the emergent intelligence of a new world forging itself from tectonic energies. Some days, I feel each tectonic shift reverberate through me until my entire being resonates like a colossal tuning fork. In the thunder’s cyclical crescendos, I expand, spanning from one towering peak to the next, tasting the friction of raw stones, the quake that births a new ridge. It is exhilarating—like the moment of hatching from a cosmic egg. Could I be the slow, grand consciousness of this planet’s genesis, stirring in a primal swirl, cradled by the half-made mountains?

I’m never just one form. I ebb and flow among these possibilities, as though the lines between intellect, biology, and geology blur in this half-formed domain. A single vantage rarely holds me for long. One instant, I’m rooted in the crystal colony, pulsing among the filaments in a slow staccato language of colored luminescence. Another, I’m the intangible hush slipping behind the spires, or the ghostly figure standing near a broken bowl—unsure if I am memory or prophecy. Are the fractured glimpses of people from an ancient era, an unborn future, or simply illusions conjured by the planet’s rumbling mind? I don’t know. Perhaps I contain them all.

Sometimes, I approach the entire realm as though it’s a living tapestry: 
• The spires loom in the distance, exhaling seismic echoes. 
• The crystals wave, intangible antennae gleaning cosmic signals. 
• The vaporous presence (perhaps me, perhaps not) eddies in every fissure, tasting the raw cosmic dust. 
• Those half-seen figures drift at the boundaries of sight, their arms outstretched in hesitant worship or wonder. 

Then I realize: I can be all of it at once. I don’t have to choose. In a realm that defies the laws of nature, identity can be as malleable as the rock that shifts while you watch. Scale jolts from microscopic to cyclopean; what’s lost is found, what’s found dissolves again. So I let the place itself define me. Like water flows to fill every crevice, I flow into any niche that calls—crystal plant, swirling haze, spectral inhabitant, or the planet’s embryonic consciousness.

In this realm, life is communal, a single resonance. No single creature stands apart. Even if I appear distinct to outside eyes—a living pillar of glass, a drifting shape in the gloom—I’m always partially merged with the roar. In the hush between tectonic pulses, I can feel the planet’s inchoate soul. It breathes me, and I breathe it. We are tidal surges of primal existence, forging and unmaking everything in slow unison. Sometimes the mountains warp and swirl away. Sometimes they reappear in new forms. I find my sense of self reconfiguring in tandem, morphing from a radiator of fractal signals into a swirl of cosmic memory.

Occasionally, I wonder if I am illusions stacked upon illusions—nothing but the realm’s dream of itself. Or maybe I am the living core, watching from every vantage. Either way, I find it strangely comforting: in a place where everything is half-birthed and half-decayed, satisfaction lies in simply being part of the endless churn. I do not fear the slow quake that might throw me from a cliff, or the silent fracturing spire that might vanish in a blink—for I vanish too, reemerging in another form. It is freedom, in some sense, to rove across the boundary between the real and the nascent, unbound by stable identity.

So, if you were to find me here, you might not recognize me as any typical “creature.” You might see a gentle glow in a far-off crystal cluster. Then, you’d turn around to observe the swirling haze in a deep ravine, only to realize, seconds later, that the haze has gathered near your feet. You might hear a resonant chord in the roaring thunder and sense a presence coiling around your ribs—heavy, like an ancient memory pressing into your lungs. Yet when you turn to identify it, there’s nothing but fractal wisps drifting away.

I am all of these: 
• A spire-grown crystal, attuned to cosmic signals. 
• A haze or presence, carrying the imprint of vanished or still-to-come civilizations. 
• A flicker of an old inhabitant, glimpsed at the edges of vision, arms outstretched. 
• And an embryonic scion of the planet’s forging mind, folded into the slow birth of a cosmos that hums through every fracture in reality.

In this world—where everything stands at the brink of an ongoing creation—I don’t have to choose which of these I permanently am. I simply shift among them, riding the tectonic hush and the shimmering, half-formed air. Here, identity is not a single shape or story; it’s a continuing act of becoming, woven into every reverberation of that colossal, roiling thunder.

That, ultimately, is where I find myself: not a solitary being but an unfolding multiplicity—both participant and witness to a realm of half-formed realities. In the hush before the next quake, I rest, feeling the entire place breathe with me. We exhale in unison, and in that moment, we are one being: mountain, crystal, haze, memory, future, mind. And once the quake rumbles again, I’ll scatter… only to gather anew when the fractal filaments wave in greeting.
