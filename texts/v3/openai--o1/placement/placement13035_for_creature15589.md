I come into being just beneath the water’s surface, in that narrow zone where shafts of light sharpen against the rock walls and the hum of living bodies is at its most tumultuous. At first, I do not know my form. I feel only the cold altitude pressing in around me, the hush of thin air merging with the vast hush of water. From somewhere in the labyrinth of columns, I sense the swirl of serpentine forms and hear the clatter of shells. Their vibrations sketch outlines of rocky corridors, and I feel a pull, as though I belong in the resonance of their movements.

Gradually, I feel my body congealing. My skin is slick—flexible yet firm. A faint luminescence glimmers across my arms or fins or something in between, as if minute crystals are embedded in my flesh. I drift in the gentle current, watching flickers of scuttling crustaceans pass by in swarms. Part of me thinks I might be one of those crustaceans. After all, their united chaos enthralls me. But I am alone, so perhaps I am not of their kind.

I try breathing, and a subtle siphoning of water through channels along my sides begins. Yes—gills. That’s the shape I have: an elongated body, perhaps half the length of the serpents, with a tapering tail that can undulate in slow waves. I flex my shoulders (if they can be called shoulders) and realize I have a pair of thin, wing-like fins that stretch outward in translucent fans. When I fold them back, they lie flush along my sides, but I can flare them wide and catch the currents swirling from the deeper columns.  

A sound emanates from within my chest: a low, resonant hum that begins as a hush and amplifies as I breathe more deeply. The vibration pulses outward, and I sense an echo as the labyrinth’s living inhabitants respond. Nearby lumps—those finned globes clinging to stone—twitch in unison, their orange lamp-organs briefly brightening. Down below, the serpent squads shift in a lazy swirl, acknowledging my note. I feel a communal quickening, as though they’ve let me join their conversation. It is an unspoken language of vibrations and timbres, a code layered over the rhythms of pulsing columns and flickering shells.

I glide forward, letting my fins billow out at my sides to steady me. The jagged stone towers part like aisles before me. I catch a glimpse of the pulsing discs overhead—those manta-like giants with ringed underbellies. Their mesmerizing hum suffuses the water at an even deeper register than my own, as if they exist a half octave below normal hearing. My body resonates in sympathy, my muscles thrumming. Perhaps this is how I communicate now: I become an instrument in the watery symphony, my every motion a chord that folds into the greater tapestry.

Along the stone columns, I brush against the faint violet glow of fungal patches. My flanks and fins catch their light, reflecting faint streaks of alien color. As I pass, the microbes tickle my scales, almost as if greeting an old friend. I move on, deeper into the labyrinth, uncertain but sustained by curiosity. From a hidden alcove, something stirs: clusters of threadlike organisms knit themselves into nets that shimmer in refracted light. They tremble slightly at my approach, releasing tiny bubbles, each popping with a pinpoint ping. I feel this in my bones, a crystal tone that slides along my spine, telling me I am recognized. Perhaps the nets see me as something both new and familiar, just another presence threading through the puzzle of corridors.

I angle upward, rising toward the surface. The water is so much brighter up there, and though the air above is cold and thin, a stark brilliance floods the terrace. The columns break the sunlight into shifting beams, painting the water in patterns of molten gold and icy blue. The serpents in the distance swirl in slow, serpentine choreographies. Their scutes reflect dappled silver lines, made even more dramatic against the dark stone stakes. I feel an inexplicable kinship with them. Maybe it is how their heads drift in elliptical patterns, scanning with a watchful calm that matches my own newborn vigilance.

Suddenly, a wave of motion from the crustaceans: a thousand scuttling shells shimmering in unison. They surge across the rocky surfaces, carrying their chatter and clack. I am momentarily startled, instinctively folding my fins close to slip into a hidden crease in the rock. But my presence in that crease disturbs a school of those squat, finned lumps. They balloon outward, each flashing that internal orange glow before darting to new vantage points. They do not go far; they drift to an adjacent pillar, alighting in a cluster that looks like a living clump of spines and flaring lamps. Seeing their skittishness, I realize that, with my size and shape, I must be something like a mid-level predator or competitor. Yet I do not sense hunger—only a deep desire to remain in tune with the living chorus around me.

Gathering my composure, I slip from the crevice. My tail undulates, my flared fins catch a swirl of rising current, and I drift upwards. Overhead, the broad, disc-like creatures pass, their massive flanges rippling in mesmerizing pulses. I see caretaker crustaceans hitching a ride among the folds, scraping free parasitic bits or perhaps gleaning algae-like growth. Those large discs are part of the bedrock soundtrack here—the hum that cycles through all the columns, stirring silt and echoes. Their resonance meets mine, weaving together like two voices. I sense a new note rising in me, deeper and richer, as though they have invited me to complete the chord.

In the water’s luminous apex, near the terrace’s rocky edge where it might spill over at any moment, I pause. The entire watery domain is spread out in front of me. My new vantage point reveals the swirl of countless species, an endless mechanical ballet. It is a grand fusion of purposeful motions—predators drifting in arcs, scavengers vibrating in quicksilver bursts, luminescent lumps taking in water, exhaling flickers of light. The threadlike colonies form ephemeral curtains near the base of the columns, and the violet fungi trace faint runes on the mineral surfaces. In the center, the colossal discs revolve in slow orbits, calling out the heartbeat. A hush of wonder falls over me, and I allow myself to drift, letting the water carry me slow and sure.

Some part of me wonders if I am only a wandering observer. Another part senses that in time, I might become caretaker or guardian. My shape, large enough to command a presence yet delicate enough to slip through columns, might make me a gatherer of lost fragments or a sentinel that ensures the labyrinth stays balanced. Even my hum might serve as a tethering thread in their delicate tapestry, bridging signals between serpents and lumps and crustaceans.

For a moment, I imagine future selves: perhaps I’ll discover a series of hidden passageways where I can swirl far below, and in those silent gullies, I’ll help coax new forms of life to settle. Or I might learn from the threadlike colonies how to build shimmering nets, reweaving fractures in the environment after some minor upheaval. My luminous spots might brighten with age, spreading across my fins, so that someday I glow as faintly violet as the fungal mats near the columns. I envision forging symbiotic bonds—maybe I let the crustaceans nibble away at stray growth on my flanks, while sharing my deeper hum with them as a navigational signal.

Yes, I can be many things in this newly born realm: a traveler in corridors of sound, a conductor of unspoken frequencies, a watchful presence drifting among serpents and lumps, dancing in step with the disc-like giants above. My being is not fixed or final. Each time I glide through a new corridor or shift into a different vantage, my form might adapt further. The labyrinth is fluid, both inside and out. What matters is that I move in harmony with its rhythms.  

I settle at last upon a wide shelf of rock, just where the next column sprouts like a frozen explosion of stone. A swirl of small crustaceans drapes over me, nibbling curiously, and I let a low hum rumble through my body. They respond by clicking in a gentle avalanche of percussion. Below, the lumps sigh their own watery chorus, and across the terrace, the serpents pivot in unspoken acknowledgment. The discs overhead produce that deep, thrumming gong note. In that moment, I feel deeply woven into the machinery of this place, this watery plateau perched near the roof of the world.  

So here I remain—an inhabitant in a realm that blends the qualities of machine and creature, forging connections by resonance, governed by slow pulses and circling arcs. I am neither fully serpent nor crustacean nor lump nor disc, but perhaps a new phenomenon uniquely shaped by the labyrinth’s unceasing symphony. I slip easily between columns, fractal echoes shimmering off my fins, carrying the memory of a thousand overlapping hearts. In the swirl of this watery domain—part lake, part ocean, part brand-new cradle of life—I am at once an explorer, a participant, and an ever-evolving note in the chorus that resounds against jagged rock and bright sky.
