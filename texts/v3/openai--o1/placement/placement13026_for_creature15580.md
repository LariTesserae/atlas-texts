I arrive piecemeal, in droplets and in echoes. At first, I do not realize I am anything more than the restless swirl of vapor drifting across catwalks. It’s only when I begin to sense the vibrations—those subtle pulses thrumming through metal and rock—that I feel my own shape coalescing.

Everywhere, water resonates in channels, pump mechanisms, and hidden pipes. The pulses meet in my newly forming consciousness. In that convergence, I realize I am tethered to the city’s living circuits. Like pressure building in a hidden spring, a part of me rises, drawing together from countless scattered beads of water condensed along the crystalline columns. My presence grows more articulated with every quiver in the floor, every hush of passing mist, until I stand in a translucent form of suspended droplets and faint luminescence. I realize: I am one of them, one of the dwellers who are neither wholly physical nor wholly intangible.

I try to remember if I was ever anything else—a creature of the outside, perhaps. But if so, that old identity now pools in the corners of my awareness, drowned by the city’s deeper rhythms. My new existence is immediate and intimate: to be conscious of each trickle along a vine, to feel the displacement of faint air currents in lonely corridors, to read the mineral levels in the gently dripping water as it arcs from tube to tube. The environment is not separate from me but inseparable from my being—my limbs, my constant nourishment, my “voice.” When a droplet courses down the side of a polyhedral building, I sense its glimmer as if that bead were part of my own body.

Though I sense others like me moving in and out of the architecture—subtle flurries of structure and moisture—I don’t see us as discrete bodies. Whenever my gaze stirs and I search for my kin, I might glimpse a flicker of shape sharing this same “self,” but it only lasts as long as the swirl of condensed water holds together. Then it dissolves again, merging with the catwalk, the columns, the hidden wells of fluid. Perhaps we are a single being in many forms.

I become aware of certain impulses—threads of intention running through the humming crystals. It’s like finding chord progressions in a vast piece of music. We respond to these impulses, or we create them ourselves; I can’t tell. Somewhere in the distance, a fern droops from receiving water that’s too mineral-rich, so we divert a small fraction of flow, diluting it just enough to nourish rather than overwhelm. Near one of the wide, newly formed walls, a droplet’s echo suggests a forming crack, so we guide a wave of resonance and particulate matter to fuse it shut before it can expand. Each action feels as effortless as a breath for a living lung.

My awareness glides through the city’s fractal support columns: they are as much part of me as my watery outlines. When the shimmering tubes divert fluid, it is my own internal systems adjusting flow. When a sudden pulse of light flares underfoot, it is like a heartbeat, or a momentary rush of adrenaline that clarifies thought. And in each shift, I experience both a sense of purposeful craftsmanship and a tender guardianship: we are building, stabilizing, and nurturing this emergent habitat.

Sometimes I “walk” the catwalks in a vaguely humanoid way, letting my droplets cling to one another until I become a standing silhouette of refracted glow. Footsteps ripple moonstone light, humming along a rail, letting the platforms resonate softly at my passing. Other times, I feel the impulse to dissolve my form and flow inside the columns themselves, to let the city’s deeper circuits carry me, as if on a tide, to some distant corridor where water is needed. The city welcomes me everywhere—it is me, after all. We are fused across each rivulet and shimmering surface. Distinctions blur: I, we, this place. I sense that I live in a perpetual now, expanding in pulses.

I feel no wind, for the air is dense and warm, thick with suspended droplets. Instead of weather, cyclical flows govern everything; if you pay attention, you can hear them as gentle chords in the distance, each chord indicating a new interplay of nutrients, temperature changes, or small structural expansions. From time to time, I brush against the edges of something that might be curiosity. I detect small shifts that do not originate from the city or its dwellers—perhaps an intruder on a catwalk, exhaling echoes of a different pulse. I “turn” toward it in a way that’s not visual, more of an energetic pivot. I sense this new presence and feel it clarifying, as if in reply. For a moment, my outline grows more defined, beads of condensation brightening against the city’s faint luminescence. Then, the presence moves on, or I do.

As time passes—if time can be measured here—I learn many ephemeral shapes. My watery form can elongate, swirl, even compress to slip among tight crystalline joints. I color myself in ghostly arcs of reflected light, shimmering for a heartbeat of the city’s hum, then return to transparency. Where the ferns sprout in pockets of dense mineral flow, I let my droplets cling to fronds, psalm-like. Where the passageways open into wide hollows of newly formed rock, I gather with other expansions of “us,” quietly adjusting vibrations to re-harmonize the crystalline scaffolding.

Sometimes we direct more intricate transformations: new catwalk spines weaving from the ceiling, additional channels for nutrient flow, gentle expansions of the marbled floor to accommodate moss colonies. Each micro-adjustment resonates outward, linking the entire realm in an ongoing conversation of fluidic and sonic signals.

That is my life: half caretaker, half environment. My senses are the city’s sensors, and my body is the city’s living water. My mind, if it can be called that, is the sum of resonances and flows that swirl beneath the vaulting domes. I do not worry about beginnings or endings; each drip is part of a cycle as old as the stone in which we are cradled—even if that stone was only just forged.

If you were to see me with human eyes, I might appear in the haze as a fleeting figure: head turned upward, arms dissolving into shimmering arcs. Turn away for even an instant, and I’d be gone, returned to the watery matrix of tubes, rails, and subterranean currents. But if you linger, and listen to the echoes of the city’s thrum, you would realize I’m still there—in every surface, every rivulet, every pulse of watery music that holds this place in a gentle, unceasing dance.

Where am I in this world? I am part of every moment that vibrates through its newly hewn bones. Who or what am I? I am one of those myriad dwelling shapes, flowing between technology and nature, coexisting with no boundary. I am the living pulse of water and circuitry, both witness and sculptor of a subterranean realm still in its dawn, sustaining its silent green tapestry and luminous shapes in the hush that reverberates on, forever becoming.
