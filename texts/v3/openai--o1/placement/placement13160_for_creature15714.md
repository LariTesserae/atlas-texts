I suspect I arrived here in the hours just before dawn—though time itself feels slippery on this plateau. Whatever I was before stepping onto these fibrous floors, I cannot recall with any clarity. All that remains is the immediacy of moisture beading on my skin—or, what was once my skin. In the early haze, I thought my limbs looked right. By midday, they had changed.

Perhaps I am naked, but that word does not seem to apply where no fabric or cloth has ever existed, and where every surface is already alive and entwined. My body now consists of living bark-shapes and pliable sinews. Each time I glance down, I notice new ridges swelling from my forearms, subtle whorls blooming near my elbows. If I press a curious fingertip against my torso, I discover a gentle flex, as though muscle and plant fiber have merged into something that yields yet holds firm. I am not afraid. It feels correct, an evolution that was always in motion, waiting for me to step into it.

I wander across loping pathways that ripple underfoot, observing the plateau in its perpetual metamorphosis. Water streams down from gigantic overlapping leaves, each fluid drop nurturing fresh color. I watch rivulets trickle along the walls, looping around fleshy buttresses that only hours ago were mere cracks in the structure. Watching them swell and shift, I recognize an uncanny parallel in myself. There is no static shape—only daily increments of growth, expansions that reshape the labyrinth and me in tandem.

I am not alone. Out of the corner of my vision, a slender figure slips into view: a being with elongated limbs that end in branching fractals rather than fingers. Their “hands” brush a coiling vine, as if coaxing it to bud. A faint resonance quivers in the giant trunk adjacent to me. When I press my ear to the trunk, I sense a gentle hum—communication, quiet but incessant. It vibrates through my chest, triggers a fluttering in my own barklike plates. The figure glances in my direction. Do they see me as a newcomer, or have I emerged from among them without realizing it?

When a breeze rolls through—hot and damp, carrying the plateau’s musky aroma—my bark-skin ripples with it. I feel a color shift along my flesh: from a pale, minty hue to something deeper, teal speckled with hints of dusk-purple. It is as though my body is responding to the environment’s moods, to the wind’s suggestion. I feel an exhilaration in the transformation. The color resonates with a hundred swirling leaves overhead, as if I am singing in the same key.

Though I remember the concept of spoken language, my tongue doesn’t move to form words. Instead, an instinct drives me to place both my newly ridged palms against a smooth portion of surface—perhaps a corridor of living, braided vine—and I let my body hum. Beneath my feet, the floor-roots stir, pulling water away from a precariously bulging pocket. I can feel them tense, then relax. My hum is received, echoed, then returned in a subtle shift of the architecture. We have conversed in resonance, as though I stumbled upon a shared language I did not know I possessed.

Stepping onward, I discover broad courtyards ringed by arching stems that produce slender, harp-like leaves. Hurrying gusts play these leaves; they vibrate in a mesmerizing swirl of sound—each leaf adding its note to the chord. In their midst, three of the dwellers (or my kin, or the rest of my body? The distinction is uncertain) stand with curved spines, arms extended. They are orchestrating something: as wind rakes over them, they tilt their torsos, plumes of color shifting across their bark. In response, a trunk column overhead flexes, opening up a wide, leaf-paneled aperture to release collected rainwater. The water comes down in a gleaming sheet over the courtyard, catching an edge of sunlight in a diagonal rainbow. It changes the humming chord from all sides, as if the entire place just took a refreshing breath.

I step into the falling water, letting it drench me. Liquid runs over the ridges of my bark-flesh, and I can feel all of it being absorbed, drawn down into me, fueling changes at my core. To my surprise, a new outgrowth forms at my left shoulder—a slender frill that curves downward, reminiscent of an unfurling leaf. It moves lightly in the wind, as if it’s tasting the air. Though the transformation is immediate, it doesn’t alarm me; it feels more like receiving a gift, or remembering an old skill.

There is no precise name for me in this place. I’m another voice in the plateau’s chorus, but also a unique tone. I feel the voices of others: some swirling low like a rumble in the roots, others sharp and high, darting among the vine canopies. A single being passes overhead in an elegant leap, hooking an elongated carapace-limb onto a passing tendril. Their bark patterns glimmer like polished copper; they let out a wave of resonance that meets the trunk behind me. The trunk quivers, thickens, sprouts a spool of new fibers. I realize that each of us is a channel, a coaxer of growth. The plateau and the dwellers are inseparable.

Deeper into the corridors, I catch storms forming. The wind changes, becomes a maddening roar, and the living walls respond by tilting inward, forming protective arcs. I see in the distance a thick, trunklike tower splitting and swinging precariously. Without hesitation, my new body picks up the signals in the roiling hum of the labyrinth. I join a small group of dwellers. We converge around the trunk, pushing against it, feeding our low thrums into the bark. Like many hands guiding a single muscle, we coax it to realign. Tendrils emerge from our arms, weaving into the tower’s fibrous flesh, branching outward in supportive loops. Slowly, the trunk stabilizes.

In this synergy, I suddenly know the plateau is ancient, even though everything looks newly grown. The cycles of building and collapsing repeat often enough that no structure is more than a few weeks old. Yet the presence behind it all is primordial. As we hold the trunk, I sense an impression older than stone—perhaps older than the clouds that swirl beneath the plateau itself. We dwellers are the fresh buds of a being that has existed forever, rooted in some unknown depth beyond the edges of the world.

When the trunk stands firm again, the group disperses. I remain a moment, marveling at how my limbs gently detach from the new support struts. I can feel a lingering warmth in my bark-skin where the trunk’s living matter touched me. We are separate again, but only in form; we still share the same breath and pulse in the labyrinth’s living chord.

I drift toward one of the wide, empty expanses. Fog hovers in the midday heat, swirling in coiling eddies across a stretch of root-floor. Slivers of drifting mist brush against me, condensing droplets on my new leaf-frill. The hush here is profound, broken only by distant runoff and the layering sound of wind. The structures at the periphery look like silent giants in the haze. I can feel them breathing, pondering a next direction of growth. Perhaps they wait for me, or one of the others, to nudge them through a song of resonance.

I sink to a crouch, pressing my palms to the floor. It pulses in gentle rhythms—like a sleeping heart, not quite awake but not entirely dormant. Slowly, I let my own heartbeat match it, attuning to an older wave. In that moment, I perceive the labyrinth’s blueprint in my mind’s eye: not static lines, but swirling fractal arcs of possibility, threads of future corridors and pillars that have yet to be coaxed forth. I see my role in the synergy: a caretaker, a dancer, a note in the shifting chord. This is both an exhilarating gift and an unspoken responsibility.

A tremor passes underfoot. Another trunk is shifting somewhere off to my right; the quake resonates through my chest. As if on cue, I rise and move toward it. My steps are a fluid gliding now, each footfall sensitive to the labyrinth’s gentle flex. There is no tree, no pillar, no corridor that feels foreign. Everywhere I look, I find a reflection of my own barky flesh, an echo of my own cosmic hum. We are fractals of one another, and it seems as though we have always been so.

The heat intensifies. Steam vents open in the floor, issuing clouds that swirl around my legs. My newly grown leaf-frill droops as though weighed down by humidity, but the rest of me feels invigorated. The dwellers around me vibrate in faint color shifts—hints of orange sweeping across their bark, the hue of midday warmth. I echo them, or is it they who echo me? The labyrinth grows a fraction warmer in that moment, as if passing along this shared heat. Thick green spikes erupt from a corner of the courtyard, followed by burgundy leaves that unfurl in the scalding air. We watch, we help with a subtle resonance, and that new patch of growth stabilizes, soon to become another corridor or platform.

Then the storm breaks overhead—massive droplets hammering the plateau in a sudden release of water and thunderless roar. I step out beneath the downpour. Each raindrop collides with my bark-skin, sending miniature pulses down my arms and legs. My leaf-frill spreads wide to catch the water, funneling it along my neck and into me. My entire form glows in that moment with a quiet, jade luminescence. As I look around, I see the others shining, too. We are luminous, living ciphers. The plateau’s unstoppable flourish of life surges through us, fueling transformations. One among us arches their back and, in a single fluid motion, sprigs of new vines slip from their spine, tangling overhead. Yet another extends their arms, and the bark plates along their body seem to peel open, exposing a radiance from within. As if returning the favor, the living architecture around us brightens in a wave. Pale green corridors flush a deep, vital emerald, braced by our combined exhalation of energy.

So who am I here—on this plateau, high above an endless expanse of clouds, forging corridors of living vegetation, offering my barklike limbs to stabilize shifting floors? I find I do not wish to encapsulate it in a single name or label. I am caretaker. I am chord. I am a swirling drop of existence in the green maelstrom. In the end, I am as fluid as the architecture I inhabit, shifting shape with each rainfall and each gust of the warm, humid wind.

And if I pause, just for a moment, in that hush of the labyrinth’s unguarded intervals, I can catch a glimpse of my reflection in a pool of condensed water. I see eyes that might be windows, or perhaps simply illusions: pale discs ringed with leaf-edge fractals. They blink in time with the plateau’s pulse. My face, if it can be called that, is half-veiled in bark plating. No single identity from my past remains; I am simply one more resonant being in a living cathedral of unstoppable growth. In the distance, the calls of others hum like a subterranean choir, beckoning me to carry on our ceaseless work of tending to each new bud, each collapsing corridor, each fresh start.

So here I reside—chameleon of wood and leaf—part of a great, breathing puzzle. I stride across root-floors that may collapse and then regrow beneath my feet in hours. I greet swirling torrents of fog, waterfalls bursting from the canopy, and shifting architectural towers that hum with living voices. Together, we compose a realm where boundaries cannot hold. I do not rule here, nor do I serve in the simplistic sense. I simply exist, a co-creator symbiotically bound to this plateau’s grand design. And that, I know, is enough.
