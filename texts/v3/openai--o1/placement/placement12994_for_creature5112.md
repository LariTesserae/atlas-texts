Sometimes, in the dim hush of that mineral twilight, I think I was once something else—soft-skinned, breathing air with ease, drinking water as if it were the most ordinary act under any sun. But here, in this parched vault beneath the starless black, I find no memory of such a life. At other times, I forget that “once” exists at all. The darkness crowns all moments alike, the past folding into the now, carrying me deeper into this land of scalding stone and silent, ancient scuttling.

• • •

I might be a wandering traveler, coming upon this high, claustrophobic cleft by accident or fate. Imagine stumbling through the heat, clothing caked in dust, lips cracked from a dryness that seems to slice right through your flesh. My breath would feel scorched at the back of the throat, each inhalation an effort. I would see evidence of movement but no respite—just shapes shifting at the corners of sight. This place offers no boon to outsiders; it holds no illusions about hospitality.

I rest on a protrusion of stone, only half aware that it might be the edge of some living colony. A faint glow emanates from within cracks in the rock—an ember-luminescence dampened by thick, layered exoskeletons. If I reach out and brush my fingertips there, I am met with a sudden rustle: small, jointed limbs receding under stone ledges, antennae quivering. Instantly, the hush resumes, as if the entire enclave were holding its collective breath.

Yet sometimes, I imagine I arrived here long enough ago that I’ve begun to adapt. From traveler to resident, from witness to participant. My sweat turned to salt, the moisture of my body leached away until I survive on rationed breath alone, perhaps feeding on the faint exhalations from heated stone. Each day—if “day” even means anything—pulls me deeper into an acceptance that normal rules have fled. Each day I think: Yes, I must embrace something new—or die.

• • •

Then, at other times, I see myself not as an intruder but as one of the original dwellers. A being hatched in the alveolar cells hidden behind the basalt arches. I know the taste of scorching air from birth. My body has ridged plating, each segment separated by a narrow glow of rust-colored light—an internal warmth, or an absorption of the ambient heat. I move in clusters alongside my kin, scraping at the rock for those mineral flecks we can distill into sustenance. A rhythm emerges: scuttle, pause, sense the trembling of the gloom, scuttle again. We pool in hollows, pressing together for shared warmth that surpasses even the environment’s furnace-like breath.

When a predator—a leathery mound—lumbers near, we freeze as one sub-sentient mind. Should it come too close, we swarm, our exoskeletal bodies rattling in an explosion of chitter-clacking. Sometimes we drive it away, sometimes we sacrifice the weak, a necessary cost to preserve the colony. Afterwards, we return to our scraping, the cycle continuing as the darkness above stirs with unseen pulses.

• • •

Some moments, I abandon that chitin and become something slow, vault-like in shape. My skin is thick, leathery, possessed of hidden flaps that can seal shut to keep the stifling dust out. I dwell alone, half-embedded in an eroded recess, rousing only when the necessity of hunger or reproduction calls. My movements are glacial: a heave forward, a slow drag. Each inch gained is an enormous effort. The smaller exoskeletal beings track my progress from a safe distance, well aware that if I manage to corner one, I will feed for weeks—draining its fluids, gleaning the essential heat it carries. But I do not crave company. My solitude is matched only by the emptiness of the black sky above.

Now and then, I come upon one of my own kind. We circle each other, exchanging faint chemical signals in the superheated air. Will it be mating, a languid press of pliable masses exchanging genetic resonance, or combat to the death? Even I don’t know until the last moment. My sluggish heartbeat resonates in my folds; I can almost hear it echo in the stone around me. If conflict is kindled, I unfurl my hidden plates, forming rough bludgeons to lash the rival. Our duel is a slow-motion collision of fleshy armor, punctuated by an occasional wet snap when an outer layer tears. Eventually, one of us emerges the victor; we neither roar nor cry out. Silence is our language. Our breath is the only exhalation. The cycle continues.

• • •

Then again, I wonder if I am something deeper, a presence that rarely surfaces. In the labyrinthine tunnels below, among the thick mats of fibrous excretions, I cling to walls as though fused to them. My shape is ambiguous: part fibrous growth, part spindly limbs. I feed on traces of sulfur seeping from the stone, on crystals that form in the cooler pockets. I sense every movement through vibrations that pass from rock to filament. When conditions shift—the swirling gloom outside drops or rises in temperature—I unseal from the tunnel wall and lead a silent migration upward. Maybe I am the early scout: the first to taste the dryness, the first to know if a wave of predatory lumps has begun to ascend. In that moment, I scuttle forth with my brood trailing behind me, each of us a piece broken off from that fibrous mass. To an outsider, we appear as if the tunnels themselves have come alive, unraveling into insectile forms.

We slip through cracks with near-weightless speed, vanishing just as quickly if the threat is too great. Our exoskeletons, if that’s what they are, are so thin that the red glow of the environment shows right through, painting our innards in silhouette. We feed quietly, gathering what we need, then vanish back into our tunnels, leaving behind only a swirl of dust.

• • •

In fleeting visions, I might be an ethereal shape with skin so translucent it’s almost glass. My spindly legs fold tight, letting me drift in the heat like a wisp of haze. I watch the basalt arches from a vantage that requires no perch—my limbs can cling to the slightest outcropping, or even hover if I shift my balance just so. When the exoskeletal hives below grow too rowdy, or if a leathery hulk stirs in a corner, I slip away in an impossible spurt of agility. Occasionally, I gather with others of my kind in the deeper gloom, exchanging subtle patterns of leg-folding and temperature pulses, a dance of signals as intangible to outsiders as starlight might be to empty space. Our watchful, ghostly presence weaves in and out of the domain, rarely interfering, never quite belonging.

• • •

So where do I find myself? Who or what am I, in this cathedral cleft of unrelenting heat, under a sky that never shows a sun?

I suspect I am all of these at once. Some part of me is the traveler who once arrived, lungs stinging, scrabbling for moisture that does not exist. Another part has grown an exoskeleton, scuttling among my chittering siblings, absorbing the hum of the hive as routine. A deeper part is that leathery mound, content to simmer in solitary immensity, stirred only by primal urges. I am the whisper in the tunnels, the translucent drifter, the fibrous tangle clinging to basalt walls.

In essence, to exist here is to shed all singular identity, to meld with the domain’s silent currents. Every creature navigates by subtle changes in temperature, by chemical taste in these particulates. Perhaps I cling with a thousand limb-tips to the stone, or exhale through vents that line my flank. Or maybe I am the one phenomenon that ties them all together, the driver of this hush. Or the hush itself.

• • •

Dig deeper. In the silent intervals between pulses of life, I might even be the stone. The weathered basalt arches that once were shaped by uncertain hands—some leftover impulse of another age. My ridges are smoothed by erosion, my surface pitted by the gritty winds. Life congregates around me, scurries across me, fights and mates upon my surfaces. I remain, indifferent to their drama, exhaling the stored heat that saturates my every pore. I am the vantage spire, the place where a creature might glimpse the roiling gloom overhead and wonder if it can glean meaning from that black labyrinth of sky.

• • •

No single answer is adequate in a place that sidesteps all normal categories. This world demands that you accept many shapes, or none, because it is not built for a single perspective. It does not revolve around one apex species or a lone vantage. It is—for all its dryness and intangible gloom—a braided tapestry of existences, each weaving around the others, each fostering a small, self-contained resilience.

So perhaps the truest reply is: I am the awareness that drifts among them, tasting each vantage in turn. In the kingdom of heat and dust, to be a traveler is to starve; to be a scuttler is to submit to the hive. To be a leathery hulk is to cling to lonely survival, and to be a tunnel-dweller is to fade into the under-layers. Yet all belong to the same tapestry—an accord of necessity shaped by extremes.

And so, if you ask me, “Where would I find myself in this world? Who or what would I be?”—I might say: I am the living motion that passes through each molten-breathing form. A many-eyed, many-limbed awareness that splits into myriad shapes. I am the hush itself, pulsing with the slow rasp of countless bodies. I am all of them, from the softest gloss of a hidden larva to the largest carapace rising from the depths to scavenge. I am this place’s abiding spirit, woven of dryness, heat, and the quiet, unending dance of survival.

Such is the nature of existence in the high, dark refuge above that unfathomable basin. I can be traveler, or local, or brood, or battered basalt itself—because in a realm so sealed and singular, one being’s story bleeds into another’s. Here, any shape can be a fleeting container for life’s ancient, ceaseless riddle. And in a domain that defies the rules of water and light, one truth persists: everything that endures must find a way to become the place itself, and in so doing, slip beyond the boundaries of what we once called a singular, separate self.
