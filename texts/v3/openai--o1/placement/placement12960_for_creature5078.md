I believe I would be something between matter and touch—inseparable from the place’s constant metamorphosis, yet gifted with a consciousness that can notice, shape, and respond. Not quite a spire-grower, not one of the slow heavy-movers, but a kind of wanderer who treads along the spongy boundaries of new rock, carrying light and warmth in curious, flickering motions. I see myself as a slender figure with a faint translucence, faintly glowing with the same pastel rose-green hues and metallic glimmers found in the moist stones underfoot.

Perhaps I arrived in the earliest moments when the ground was still half-molten, a shimmer of impulsive life that seeped out of the cracks. Over time, I learned to bring my elongated palms to the rock and sense its readiness to shift. My arms, elongated and flexible, can feel the quiet resonances moving through the plates. When they hum with energy, I hum back—a subtle, symbiotic dance. Sometimes it’s enough to loosen a fresh seam in the stone; sometimes it coaxes crystals to flourish, opening like mineral blossoms.

My form is fluid and never quite settled, so I’m often mistaken for one of those atmospheric phantoms. Now and then, I’ll flicker at the edge of a spire or drift across a newly formed pool, reflecting softly in the water’s surface. At times, the spire-growers seem to notice me, and we share a meeting of pulses in the space between us: no words, just flickers in the subtle glow coursing through our limbs. We are kindred in that we both guide creation—though the spire-growers operate with a pragmatism, coaxing entire columns of mineral to take shape, while I lean gently into the half-solid stone. I slip in and out, testing the potential of each emerging texture.

During the damp dawn (or is it dusk?), I sometimes find myself drawn to the winged shimmer-creatures. When they gather in lilting swarms, I hover nearby, watching how the crystals refract across their wings. If they allow it, I’ll ride the swirl of their convection—the delicate, dancing currents that funnel from newly warmed pools. For a moment, I become a part of their flight, flickering in and out of focus as if I, too, were built of agile glass. Then I gently descend, letting them drift on.

When the heavy-movers come lumbering through, I’m always amazed by how they tilt their broad heads, listening for deeper notes in the planetary body. Usually, they pay me no mind, but once in a while one of them will rumble softly in my direction. Not an alarm—more a greeting, a recognition that we share the same plane of existence. I sense a kindness there, an ancient wisdom, as if they have always been guardians of planetary births. Sometimes I follow in their wake, stepping over the softened ground they leave behind. They part the spongy terrain, and I can trace the luscious, brand-new seams that ooze heated minerals. If I touch one, the energy flows through my fingertips like a slender bolt of electric fascination.

My hours pass in a contemplative roaming—feeling the crust’s half-formed edges, letting my luminous body soak up the half-daylight, half-twilight that glows from an unsettled sky. I can never quite decide which direction to wander next. The entire horizon calls, each part of it offering the possibility of stone that might soon shift or a pool that might abruptly begin to boil with new formation. And so I simply float, drift, and occasionally direct my attention where I sense the greatest call.

At certain twilight intervals—if we can call them intervals, in a place where day and night exist as perpetual indecision—I take up a position at the tip of a newly raised spire. Balancing there, I anchor my feet on the tapering column, arms wrapped around the damp, living architecture. The spire-growers sometimes gather below, their translucent forms flickering with color-coded signals. They coax new growth from the base, smoothing rough edges, weaving channels for the water to trickle through.

Meanwhile, I watch the sky. There’s a restlessness in the clouds, a promise of something crackling. Occasionally, shimmering arcs of unknown energy will lace through the heavens in branching lines, lighting the damp surfaces of the world in momentary phosphorescence. I feel called to that electricity, almost able to taste it in the air. There’s a sense of kinship: both that energy and I belong to the space between states. We are phenomena that refuse to be pinned down—here one moment, intangible the next.

And so I remain: an intermediary presence, bridging the gap between spire, stone, and sky, steeped in the hush and symphony of ongoing creation. If you looked at me for too long, you might see your own reflection, a faint outline that suggests the land strives to mirror every curious gaze turned upon it. I exist to read the half-uttered language of creation—the whispered incongruities in the stone, the fractal shimmer of the winged creatures, and the gravity-bending architecture that resides one breath away from magic.

My purpose, such as I understand it, is to help translate the pulses of the place into tangible forms. Sometimes that happens at the calm boundary of a steaming fissure, my hands pressed into the spongy earth, guiding the swirl of minerals into a shape that might become a new root or a new crack. Other times, I’ll wade through the shallow, warm waters at the base of the spires, stirring the sediment until it finds a gentle flow that suits the fledgling vegetation.

In these tasks, I sense no hurry. The entire realm vibrates with a timeless urgency that’s also paradoxically patient. Even if transformation occurs in mere hours, it carries an undercurrent of eternity—like the planet is saying, “I can bloom in an instant, or I can wait a thousand years. It makes no difference. Let us just keep becoming.”

I do not possess a name in any spoken language, for sound alone can’t hold all the nuances of this place. Instead, I’m woven into a synergy of vibrations, recognized—if at all—by the faint melodic patterns my presence adds to the overall symphony. The spire-growers might detect me as a light chord in their pulsations, the heavy-movers might feel me as a ripple underfoot, and the winged creatures might see me in a fleeting reflection as they dip toward the water.

I am neither entirely creature nor spirit, neither fully builder nor passive observer. I feel like a piece of the bedrock that rose up with its own awareness, a condensation of the planet’s imagination, a breeze drifting through the half-formed mountains. On certain days, the glimmer of dawn breaks across the sharp edges of still-hardening stone, and in that fresh brightness, you might catch me, arms outstretched to the sky, shining just briefly in rose or pale green. Then I dissolve back into the swirling haze, letting the realm continue its dance of new creation.

So that is who I am—something born of the planet’s own unsettled breath, shaped by stirring rock, warmed by half-luminous energy. And this is where I find myself: hidden in the perimeter between seen and unseen, an ever-curious conductor gently orchestrating the raw possibility of this land so that these spires can soar, these creatures can thrive, and this still-lucid world can discover, each moment, what it might become.
