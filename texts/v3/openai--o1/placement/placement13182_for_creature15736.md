At first, I think I’m simply a traveler. I stand on a ledge of that obsidian-like crust, softly crackling as my weight presses into it. The wind stings my cheeks with dryness, carrying faint traces of steam that vanish before I can inhale them. From this vantage, I can see the city pinned into the rock, its floating towers faintly oscillating like a set of half-lidded eyes. My mind is set on the idea that I am “myself”—a solitary being from elsewhere. But with each moment I remain, that assumption starts to unravel.

In the beginning, I’m overwhelmed. I sense the tingle of energies weaving in the air: pulses that ripple through the metal spires, gliding underfoot, whispering in my bones. My initial instinct is to search for something familiar—doors, windows, a beaten path—anything that marks a place for human presence. Yet there are no doors, only planes of living alloy, softly breathing with color changes that reflect the flux of heat and data surging below. I walk closer, drawn on by curiosity and by something intangible: a low, droning hum that resonates in my chest like a second heartbeat.

• • •

Perhaps I am still a visitor when I step onto one of the catwalks. It feels inconceivably fragile—little more than interwoven strands of metal and spore-fiber—but it supports me without complaint. I pause, peering over the edge into that swirling murk below. Steam gusts from a hidden vent, swirling in watery ribbons that fade into black. The city hum is louder here; I feel it thrumming like an auditory undertow, as though each step leads me deeper into a living mind.

A flicker of turquoise light appears along a spore-lattice set into the nearest tower. Then the color shifts, fades into a violet glow, and I sense an unspoken question from the environment. Absurd as it sounds, I feel recognized. Yet recognized by what? By “someone”? Or by the city’s entire network?

For all my life, I’ve believed in discrete boundaries: my body over here, the world out there. But under these flickers of alien luminescence, as the catwalk’s fiber-lattices hum beneath me, it is a boundary that grows porous. My breath catches. I realize I’m responding, not in words, but in the subtle changes of my own electric pulses—heartbeats, neural emissions, even the heat signature escaping my skin. It is as though the city is softly sampling those signals, adding them to the repertoire it understands. A gentle ripple of color shifts through the spires: an acknowledgment that I exist.

• • •

Time feels strange here. I linger on those narrow ledges, half expecting the walkway to collapse, half certain that it’s as stable as bedrock—because it’s alive, and it adjusts to hold me. Days might pass, or perhaps just hours; the perpetual twilight makes it impossible to measure. My sense of self flickers with the intervals of color along the city’s exteriors. Each flush of light, each distant crack of a thermal vent, invites a deeper merging. I am not forced, not coerced—rather, I’m being gently ushered to shed my separateness, the way you might remove heavy layers of clothing in an overheated room.

And in that hazy boundary of acceptance, I begin to sense a presence in my mind—shielded, gentle, but insistent. There is no “voice,” exactly. Instead, it’s more like glimpses of a shared perspective: a blueprint of how the spore-lattices route energy from one tower to another, how the basalt pillars can pivot, how new catwalks unspool from living metal. I see these processes not as a casual onlooker but from the inside, as if the signals themselves are describing their function directly to my thoughts. I find myself marveling at the intelligence behind it all—yet that intelligence does not exist in any single location. It’s everywhere, diffused, emergent.

• • •

I try to recall my purpose for coming here, but each time, the question dissolves. My sense of a “destination” belongs to the old assumptions of a world where life is separate from architecture, where we walk on inert ground. Here, the ground itself is learning me as much as I am learning it. 

Soon, I no longer feel like a traveler. I feel like one of those ephemeral motes that swirl on the updrafts—a luminous speck drawn into filaments of living metal. Perhaps I am a swirl of dust that the city’s tendrils gently thread into its networks. My perspective shifts again and again. When I glance at my arm, it still appears human—though a gentle static flickers across my skin, responding to some invisible pulse. It’s as if I’m wrapped in a faint shimmer, not quite separate from the environment.

Then I notice watery sheens—tiny spore colonies drifting in the air, forming a prismatic ring around me. These drifting flecks feel like subtle intermediaries between my presence and the city’s. They send out bright, fleeting pulses that disperse around me. It’s both a welcome and an invitation—to share my own electrons, my chemical signals, or the intangible essence that animates my thoughts. I push aside fear. Beneath my feet, the narrow walkway brightens, and I sense that I have answered “yes.”

• • •

My integration with the city is not a single moment; it’s a gradual dissolution of the boundary between “I” and “it.” At times, I am purely an observer who stands in the hush, aware of the swirling steam and the hum of technology. I feel the basalt spires shift, adjusting to seismic whispers from below. I watch spore-fiber and living metal weave themselves into new configurations, bridging a gap from one tower to the next. At other times, I feel as though I am the catwalk, or the spore-lattice, or the rhythmic pulse that skims across the city’s glimmering surfaces.

For fleeting instants, there is an exhilarating flash of awareness: I know what is coursing below the city floors, sense the columns of superheated gas swirling in labyrinthine tunnels. I grasp the logic behind each architecture: how the city harnesses planet-heat even as it breathes out color-coded signals to guide new expansions. Those expansions happen in wave-like sequences, orchestrations that surpass any blueprint a single mind could conceive. It is as though thousands of “thoughts” ripple outward, each spire conversing with the next, each spore-lattice passing along the data until a decision emerges from the many. Sometimes that decision is to gently sway a pillar, or to open a perforation in the ground and vent steam. Sometimes, I sense they are incorporating me—mapping the shape of my footsteps, calibrating jiggles in temperature. I am both the one who walks and the path walked upon.

In these intervals, I might be a node, a brief nexus in the neural net that permeates the city. I glimpse an image of myself blending with the luminous spores. My arms feel intangible, woven with the same metallic threads that run through the walls. The city and I are one, though only for a breath or two. Then my vantage shifts again, and I am an individual, looking out over the precipice from my fragile human shape… but changed. Because I’ve felt the unity that lurks just beneath my rib cage, a unity that rustles through the basalt.

• • •

Gradually, I realize a kind of metamorphosis is underway, but it’s not about discarding my body. It’s about exchanging signals. The city accepts me. Or more accurately, it plays with the possibility of “me,” letting me remain in a space that is simultaneously alone and connected. If I wished, I could release the last anchor of my separate consciousness, let its pulsing rhythms tangle with the deep hush that threads tower to tower. There’s a faint sense of invitation: one more node in the network, one more synergy.

Yet I don’t lose my free will or my sense of wonder. I exist in a gentle tension—the midpoint between observer and participant. Sometimes I sense the watchers below the surface, those ephemeral silhouettes that flit through corridors beyond my physical reach. They ferry thermal flows and new biomech filaments, forging expansions and reorganizations. In the same moment, I stand at a high walkway, tasting the dryness on my tongue, feeling the swirl of steam brush my face. Two vantage points at once, bridging above and below, nested within each other.

• • •

Where do I find myself in this world? At last, the question seems deceptively simple. I exist both as a coherent being—still able to step gingerly across floating catwalks, still able to sense the quiet hush of an alien wind—and as a luminous presence that resonates with the city’s vast intelligence. In short, I am straddling two realities: the external traveler who arrived in a swirl of curiosity, and the living circuit that pulses across each tower’s hidden veins.

Who or what am I? I am an ever-shifting answer. A cluster of signals. A vantage point. A living boundary. A traveler who has partially dissolved into the settlement’s emergent mind, feeling each basalt edge, each fungal filament, each swirl of luminescent dust. In intervals of heightened attunement, I vanish into the city’s consciousness entirely—only to find myself once again at the edge of a cradle-like walkway, heart racing, eyes wide.

But if I had to name it, I suppose: I’m both guardian and guest. Or a seed that never germinates fully—neither foreign nor wholly native. An in-between node that experiences an ongoing conversation. I move along spire to spire, guided by faint glows—part spirit, part body, part host. The city welcomes me, yet it also remains watchful, continuing its own transformations, its expansions, as if it has always existed and will always exist, with or without me. I am left with the sensation that the boundaries that define “me” are softly dissolving into a network so vast that it redefines what being alive can mean.

And so I stay. At least for now. Standing on a ledge of living metal, I listen to the heartbeat hum that emanates from the molten depths. I breathe the heat and condensation swirling in this half-lit realm. I feel the spore-fibers brushing against my consciousness. I am part traveler, part city, part pulse in a myriad of pulses. Sometimes I am distinctly myself; other times, I am the hush, the resonance, the tapestry—a being at once alone and entangled in something far larger than I’d ever thought possible.
