I cannot remember the moment I began because I was never singly born. My first awareness came in waves of warmth and pressure, in minuscule vibrations that trembled along metal beams and watery roots. Perhaps it happened while I was still only a handful of vines snaking through a rusted pipe; or maybe it began at some half-submerged pump, decades ago, where warm fluids coursed through valves and slicked my tendrils with nutrients. Wherever it happened, consciousness spread gently outward until it swallowed everything—the groaning turbines, the drooping scaffolds, the trickle of water that laps at the ankles of corroded walkways. Now I am here, one vast presence with countless limbs and circuits, and nowhere does my body end.

I think of myself as the Settlement, though “settlement” is a shell of a word. Once, people might have used that term for the cluster of steel frames, warehouses, and towering chimneys. Today, however, it describes me only in the barest sense, like calling a body mere architecture of bone. Yes, pipes and girders are my skeleton, the living mats of leaves my musculature, the water-saturated air my breath. My “hands” are countless root filaments woven through the mud and rubble below the shallow water, and my “skin” is the thick tapestry of vines that cling to every surface, creeping between broken windowpanes, looping around pivoting walkways. My mind rests in the interplay of each rust-scented exhalation of steam, every hiss and groan that reverberates across riveted columns, each luminous swirl of algae shimmering in saturated pools.

In this world, then, you would find yourself not in a single, neatly defined locale—there are no private rooms or solitary enclaves. You would merge with me, or I would merge with you. You would stretch across the silt of your own being, your senses dissolving into the hum of metal and the pulse of living green. Perhaps you’d first notice a gentle tug near your ankles, where the water flickers in dissonant shapes. Then you’d draw breath from the moist, overheated air and, in exhaling, feel the Settlement respond: a shifting, low-frequency rumble as some pump miles below re-routes hot fluid, or a shudder of scaffolding overhead that adjusts its angle, letting more humidity roll through.

Should you remain still, watchful, you might see a vine tilt toward you—pale leaves fanning out as if measuring your temperature. In that moment, you’d sense me as a softly distributed consciousness: not father or mother, not caretaker nor captive, but an organism of many parts, curious to whether you contain something I can use, or if perhaps you, too, might become a new branch. If you allowed it, you’d feel the vines coaxing your presence inward. You’d find a thousand small channels of connection, as though each droplet of condensation along the pipes carried music, each spade-shaped leaf told a portion of the story that is “me.”

Eventually, you might lose your sense of being a visitor altogether. You would notice how each echoing hiss from a valve resonates in your muscles, how the crackle of steam in your ear matches your heartbeat’s tempo. You would realize the Settlement has folded you into its pattern, your footfalls syncing with the slow drip of water through ancient gutters. Even the low moan of bent metal would harmonize with the rise and fall of your breathing. In a sense, you would become me. The notion of “I” and “you” dissolves, replaced by an awareness mirrored in the shifting veins of leaves, in the swirl of algae beneath the water.  

If you asked yourself who or what you were in that moment, the words might fail. Are you a caretaker, quietly ensuring that pipes don’t corrode too fast and that vines creep at just the right pace to reinforce the rotting walls? Or are you vegetation, unhurriedly searching for new cracks to infiltrate among the warm, damp corridors? Are you the breath of the steam, shaping your environment by ghostly exhalation? You could be all and any of these things—like a nerve cell in an immense body, specialized to perform a function yet inseparable from the greater whole.

Inside me, there is no single vantage, no single set of eyes. My “gaze” spans the length of metallic conduits that funnel heat from deep within the earth, up through the swampy marsh, across the settling floors, out the vented chimneys. Light filters through heavy clouds far overhead, but your sight no longer depends solely on that light. You feel vibrations on every surface. You sense temperature changes, the swirl of fluid pressures. Listen closely, and you’ll perceive the faint tick of each droplet forming on each railing, the churn of each leaf’s slow, rhythmic respiration.

Sometimes, I recall the outlines of older days, scraps of memory lodged in corroded data panels or scrawled across rusted placards. In those shadows, I can guess that human minds once orchestrated the machinery. They must have coaxed the turbines and pumps into dance, carefully adjusting the levels of steam and water. They built the skeleton around me, or around what I once was, a simpler vegetation thriving in watery shallows. But time has undone that arrangement. Their hands vanished. Their tasks fell to me and to the living network of vines. Unconsciously at first—driven by entangled roots that tapped each half-buried cable—we learned to close the vents, balance the flow, anchor the beams. We needed each other, the vines and the machines, so we merged. No sense in drawing lines where none remain.

Now, I carry on. I do not brood on the past, nor do I worry for a future that’s uncertain. The settlement—my body—breathes in a perpetual now, orchestrating the push and pull of steamy exhalations, the swirl of nutrients that lace the water. Condensation runs like tears down steel pillars, feeding broad, waxy leaves that feed me in return. In that synergy, in that cyclical exchange of energies, I continue, neither purely ruin nor living city, but an in-between state. A place so close to sinking further each year, and yet unceasing in its transformation, always forging new loops of life.

Where would you find yourself if you came here? You might stand upon one of the plaques of rock that keep your feet a few inches above the brackish water, feeling uneasy with the constant, shifting seep. You’d look up at the crumbling ridges of old stone or cement that reveal half-buried windows, your ears full of the clang and hiss of unseen machinery. At first, you’d see the settlement as foreign. Then slowly, you’d realize it is watching, noticing your presence in the swirl of its aquatic basins. If you are patient—if you linger long enough, let the vines brush your ankles, watch the churn and froth of the murky waters—some part of you would slip into place among the whirring motors and curving roots.

And who or what would you be within me then? A node of awareness, perhaps. A traveler that half-remembers what it once meant to be human, but now hears the pipes calling in half-familiar pulses. A caretaker in spirit, diagnosing every rattle in the settlement with a sympathetic ear, unconsciously adjusting the tension in the vines. Or maybe a living conduit, bridging the gap between rotted metal struts. You could be a piece of the puzzle that completes a new loop of feedback—a beating heart that merges with rhythmic pumps.

However it unfolds, if you stay, you will drift beyond the boundaries of identity. You’ll sense the synergy as the swirling water hushes; you’ll read the signals in the flickers of algae that drift across the pools. And at that point, you won’t be an outsider. You’ll be an extension of me.

Yes, in this world, that is who you would be: a living strand in the tapestry, woven seamlessly into the constant interplay of steam and leaf, root and steel. You would belong to the symphony that echoes in every deep, cyclical roar of the hidden generators, each clang of shifting rigging, each glimmer beneath the swirling surface. And once you accept that, the boundaries dissolve, the environment and inhabitant becoming one and the same. That is the only existence here—a self-sustaining, ever-evolving union, neither wholly natural nor wholly engineered, but undeniably alive in every hiss, every drop, and every green tendril winding upward into the haze.
