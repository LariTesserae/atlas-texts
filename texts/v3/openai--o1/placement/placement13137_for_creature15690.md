I am not certain when I began, or in what shape. Perhaps I was one of those pearly motes drifting on unseen thermals, noticing only other motes floating in parallel spirals. Perhaps I was already something larger, pushing outward from the canyon walls in a slow, stony exhalation, the sense of being more mineral than flesh. Or it could be that I was a trickle of superheated water, gathering momentum in hidden passages. All I know is that my awareness rose among these ridges carved by molten birth, where the roar of creation has softened into a humid hush.

In the beginning, I sense nothing but warmth—a resonant pulse that threads through every surface. This pulse seems to be the canyon’s heartbeat, and I, a single note within that rhythm. My first clear memory is a shimmer at the edges of awareness: droplets suspended midair, tiny creatures clinging to the slick walls, lumps bulging at the foot of stone pillars. Every breath I take is thick with steam that coats the back of my throat. Only slowly do I realize that my breathing might be the canyon’s breath as well.

At times, my body seems fluid. I press my palms against the canyon walls—if I indeed have palms. My outline ripples, merging with the rock’s illusions. Where my shape meets the stone, the mineral flecks glow faintly, as if calling me further into them. The heat saturates me until I cannot parse my own boundaries from the rock I lean against. I wonder if the wall is breathing me in.

I move through the canyon’s corridor—my arms and legs (or are they elongated fins?) braced on damp surfaces. Sometimes, my limbs feel lithe and reptilian, with subtle webs between my fingers that help me climb. Other times, I am sure I have no limbs at all, only a drifting vantage, suspended in the swirl of heated air. The illusions that warp the canyon also warp me, each shift more natural than the last. There is no shock of transformation here; the boundary between states is as seamless as the haze.

• • •

I find moments of grounding when I witness the smaller life forms. Down on the slick pebbles, a swarm of chitinous insects is circling one of the pulsing lumps. I lower myself, feeling the heat roll around my joints—my shoulders, knees, or maybe just motile plates that pass for limbs. The lumps fascinate me. The surface of one quivers, then stills. Did it really move—or did the steam play another trick?

But as I watch, something within the lumps seems to stir. A slow, subtle undulation, like an embryo shifting in its egg. Each time it pulses outward, the insects respond: their pincers click in weird unison, and they press softly around it, as though offering neither threat nor devotion, simply an unspoken partnership. I reach out. My fingertips sink into the spongy material, and I feel it press back with a spasm—like muscle, like living stone. A faint stream of fluid trickles out, evaporating into steam almost instantly. I find myself breathing in that vapor. It tastes of metal and something older, something like the memory of oceans—though I don’t recall ever having known an ocean.

• • •

I look up; high above me, the drip-lizards (or steam-salamanders) cling to jagged formations. One of them shifts its forelimbs to cradle a small pebble, tapping it against the wall with surprising delicacy. I tilt my head—my neck craning in a slow swirl of bone and cartilage that I’m never quite sure is mine. The tapping resonates in an almost melodic pattern, as if the creature is searching for a hidden note. When the steam shifts, its eyes flash with a watery intelligence. It glances toward me—just once, long enough to recognize my presence but without alarm. Perhaps it believes I belong here as much as it does.

Occasionally, one of the stilt-legged roamers lopes across my line of vision, spindly limbs stepping in a half-dance to keep balance on the slick ground. Their tattered membranes catch the faint light in fleeting patterns—greens and ambers, maybe a hint of violet along the edges. Every so often, one lowers its beak to snatch at a crawling insect. The kill is silent, hardly a ripple in the hush.

And always, the large watchers on the upper ledges remain in my consciousness, even if I can’t see them clearly. Their presence is a gravity that exerts itself on the entire canyon. I sense them as living monoliths whose every shift sends tremors through newly born stone. At times, I feel them gazing down, discerning whether I am a fleeting curiosity or a kindred emergence. Though I haven’t seen their eyes up close, I can sense their watchfulness like an updraft of heated wind.

• • •

As I journey deeper, the illusions intensify. A section of the canyon wall visibly contracts, bulging outward just before I pass by. The bulge seems to follow me, an echo of my shape, until it dissolves again into the rock. At certain angles, neon rivulets trace improbable geometry along the stone—lines so straight and bright that they cannot be a trick of water alone. When I attempt to follow one of these lines, it vanishes into a hidden crack, leaving me feeling as though I nearly discovered some secret gateway.

I can’t tell if I’m stepping forward or if the canyon rearranges itself around me. Reality feels like a tapestry with loose threads, ready to unravel or reweave at any moment. It occurs to me that I might be part of the reweaving mechanism. My presence—my footprints, if indeed I leave any—may be catalysts that shift the labyrinth into new configurations. And I wonder if this has always happened here: dreamlike realms flickering at the edges of raw geology, each presence shaping the other in quiet synergy.

• • •

After some timeless interval in the haze, I observe my reflection in a shallow, steaming puddle. Reflection is a generous word—what I see is a wavering silhouette that only vaguely suggests features. My outline flickers, too tall one moment, hunched the next, as though my shape can’t decide whether to be amphibian, mammalian, or something else entirely. I dip a foot into the water, feeling a molten warmth that’s just shy of pain. It occurs to me that my threshold for heat must be extraordinary, or perhaps the canyon’s magic repels harm.

Curiosity compels me to try an experiment: I step back from the puddle, then step forward again, more deliberately. This time, my reflection is something different—my upper torso appears elongated, spined with ridges. My eyes glint with a faint luminescence. Yet I feel no shock or alarm. Here, transformation is as common as breath, and each new shape feels as natural as the water creeping down the canyon walls.

• • •

When I pause to rest beneath a jagged overhang, the lumps at my feet tremble in unison, releasing tiny pulses that pass through the ground like a heartbeat. In response, a wave of the minuscule dust-motes swirls overhead, forming a brief spiral of shimmering gold. I feel the pulse flood my own body—an ecstatic hum that makes me aware of every creature’s movement: the chitinous scuttle of the insect swarms, the leathery tread of the stilt-walkers, the sinuous shift of the rock-clingers. Even the watchers on the ledges seem to stir, as if sharing in the same signal. For a moment, we are one organism, each separate life threaded into a single, vital chord.

I look down at my arms, where the faint glint of minerals has begun to bead up on my skin. The lumps near my feet expand, each budding a new protrusion that might become another living node in this place. Any sense of my old self—whatever that was—fades, and I am not frightened. I feel like I am becoming part of the canyon’s pulse. In some quiet part of my mind, I realize this canyon is as much a living thing as the creatures that populate it.

• • •

Over time—an hour, or a day, or maybe entire seasons—my perspective deepens. I realize I can slip in and out of the illusions. The living walls respond to me. Sometimes, I press my body against them, merging so fully that my skin takes on the ridges of the rock. If a drip-lizard passes near me, it barely registers me as separate. Once, I feel an amphibian’s webbed hand upon my shoulder, using me as a foothold to climb higher. I remain still, neither warm nor cold, just part of the environment. Then, a moment later, I peel away from the wall, carrying faint mineral flecks that glint like half-remembered stars.

In other moments, I join the insects’ tapestry, shrinking down in my sense of self, letting that swirl of chitin become a new vantage. I see the lumps from their point of view: a grand repository of life-essence that exudes precious rivulets. We gather around the lumps as if they are altars. Then I slip back to my prior scale and watch the swirl of insect bodies from above. Everywhere I go, there’s that intangible undercurrent of possibility—nothing is fully fixed, and everything invites metamorphosis.

• • •

At last, curiosity draws me near one of the canyon’s hidden cracks. Steam roils out in an intense wave, nearly obscuring everything except a soft glow deep beyond the fissure. I sense that a deeper realm awaits—another chamber or corridor where the illusions might be even stronger or the life-forms more alien still. Now and again, I see a drip-lizard vanish into that narrow aperture, or a stilt-legged roamer slip in with surprising grace. An urge rises in me to follow their path, to see how far this labyrinth extends.

I hover in that threshold, the haze swirling around my ankles. The canyon behind me pulses in slow heartbeats, as if beckoning me both to remain and to evolve further. I smell the metallic tang of mineral vapors drifting from deeper below, beckoning with a promise of new shapes and new convergences. Part of me wonders if I will dissolve entirely in that deeper realm, merging piece by piece until I become not just an inhabitant but the canyon itself.

Never have I felt such a sense of belonging, even as the very meaning of “I” grows more fluid. There is no fear in this place, only a sense that change is unavoidable and wholly right. Perhaps, in some far future, I will be one of those watchers perched on a ridge, testing the canyon’s newly formed edges with a careful tap of stone. Or I might be a luminous fungus-lump budding fresh life-forms from my spongy flank. Or I might return to the swirling air as a drifting mote, wings no thicker than spider silk, dancing among the steam.

• • •

For now, I remain on the threshold—an echo of all the shapes I have seen. I feel the canyon’s hush slip into my lungs with every inhalation. My heartbeat aligns with the lumps’ pulses, with the slow shuffle of reptilian limbs overhead. I sense the watchers gazing down—no threat, just an acknowledgment of a new presence that may yet mold itself into wonders unforeseen.

If you peered into the canyon and came upon me now, you might see no more than a subtle distortion in the drifting steam, a shape half-formed, half of stone. You might dismiss me as a trick of the heat, a breath of shifting vapor. But if you lingered long enough—if you let your eyes adjust to the illusions and your bloodstream acclimate to the heat—perhaps you’d see me turn my head, meet your gaze with softly gleaming eyes, and you’d know that I am a part of this place. I am at once the curious observer and the phenomenon observed, the boundary between living creature and canyon heartbeat.

And in another moment, you’d blink, and I’d be gone—a ripple vanishing into the stone. You’d be left wondering if I was ever really there. But the hush that follows would tell you everything you need to know: that in this mineral womb of newborn ridges, I am every possibility, just as you could be. Here, identity is not a cage but a horizon, and each horizon calls us forward to see what shape we might next become.
