I arrive without footsteps. At first, I am only a watcher, uncertain whether I even have a body of my own. The buttresses rise all around me, raw and mineral-sharp, and the wind that sighs past them carries notes of machinery that shudder through my thoughts. I remain still, only half aware of a shape that might be my silhouette cast in the dim watery light. The hum underfoot resonates in my bones, spreading a gentle warmth that stirs something in me. It’s as though I have only just been born from the stone itself—a half-formed thing waiting to see what I might become.

I drift along the edges of towering fronds and moss-draped corridors. My eyes (if I indeed have eyes) pick up glints of motion in the gloom: amphibians scuttling across slick stone, insects flashing electric pulses in the thick air, oil-slick mammals blinking at me from behind twisting vines. I feel the weight of time—or the strange absence of its passing. There is no hurry here; each living being flows in the perpetual twilight as if part of a single, breathing tapestry. Still, I can’t quite fathom where I belong in this swirling puzzle.

I sense an urge to step forward, to test the stone with my foot—yet I’m not certain what shape that foot takes. Looking down, I see a shimmer ripple along a surface like glass, or perhaps quicksilver. There is a reflection, but it might be mine or an echo of something else. The reflection flickers in and out, fractal edges warping, refusing to be pinned to a single form. I inhale (though the air is so moist that breathing almost feels like drinking) and will my outline to settle.

For a moment, I see myself as one of the amphibious folk. My limbs lengthen, my spine straightens until I can stand upright in the shallows. Metallic patches bloom across my skin—copper swirling into silver, silver merging into gold. My face tapers to a smooth, amphibious muzzle, and I catch a fleeting reflection of the phosphorescent frills that fan out behind my skull. The hum in the ground grows deeper in my chest. When I open my mouth, I release a sub-bass note that ripples through the stone. The amphibians near me answer softly, resonant pulses that carry a communal acceptance. They acknowledge me as theirs, yet I sense that this shape—beautiful though it feels—might not be my final, or only, form.

In another flicker, I’m something else. The air crackles around my exoskeleton, translucent plates throbbing with faint luminescence. I feel segmented joints and the ticking of my limbs as I move in tandem with a chorus of insect-lights. Our abdomens glow in unison, the staccato pulses merging with the steady drone that fills the planet’s crust. A thousand eyes across a thousand heads all shift at once, reading signals from the hidden engine below. We scuttle among the shallow pools, dipping our glowing hindparts into water, drawing in whatever intangible energy fuels our phosphorescent shimmer. The synergy is exquisite, but I still feel an unspoken call nudging me onward—another shape awaits, drawing me deeper.

I let the glimmer of this insect form fade. The shifting illusions brush across me like a half-forgotten dream. A moment, or a year—time is slippery here—passes under the hush of condensation. The next time I truly notice my body, I see prismatic fur on my arms, the sheen of oil in a half-light that never quite becomes day. My limbs are elongated, and my fingers are delicate, spread wide to grip mossy vines that sway overhead. My eyes, now gold and reflective, peer down a corridor of giant leaves. I sense my breath is quieter, more mammalian, and it sends small clouds of vapor drifting through the vines.

Then, from above, a shriek rings out, deep as stone against stone, scraping some primal chord in my mind. Glancing upward, I see colossal wings, half-silhouette, half-luminescent membrane. For an instant, my entire being yearns to join them, to test the plateau’s thin, frigid air under outspread wings. Yet even as I begin wondering how that shape would feel—what it would be like to perch on sheer faces of newly heaved rock—my perspective flickers; a swirl of moisture distorts my vision, and I find myself… other.

Suddenly, I am no creature at all—no amphibian, insect, or mammal. I am less solid, a filigree of shimmering lines, fractal patterns that move through the damp gloom. Perhaps I’m a ghostlike presence that exists purely to haunt reflections in the puddles. I slip between trunk-sized roots, flash across still waters as dancing geometry, bending the angles of sight. My presence is intangible: a living illusion flickering at the edges of vision.

Yet something in me knows I am not only a wisp. I have thoughts, desires—tiny seeds of curiosity pushing me to test each shape, each vantage. There is a presence here—call it a pulse or a consciousness—that beckons me to merge with it completely, to feel the continuity of all forms at once. It vibrates in time with that mechanical undertone, the endless hum from unreachable depths.

So I let go. For a moment, my boundaries dissolve, and I become the entire plateau: spires, vines, mud-slick corridors, watery hollows, and everything contained within. I feel the breathing of amphibians, sense the spark of insects’ cores, note the oily fur of drifting mammals, behold the minds of winged beasts scratching at the sky. Like a slow wave rolling through me, I understand that the plateau is a carefully orchestrated equilibrium, each living thing connected by signals too deep to track with the naked senses. Together, they form a single pattern rippling through time, unhurried, unchanged.

This state of oneness feels eternal. The hum beneath me rattles my consciousness, unraveling questions about “I” and “them.” But in this borderline trance, I perceive something else: the plateau’s unblinking awareness of its own existence. It has no need for progress as I understand it; it simply is and always will be. A cosmic hush, suspended in the rainy gloom. I slip among molecules of damp stone, settle into pockets of root-laced water, watch my own reflection in shimmering insect carapaces. The place is as near to a timeless temple as anything I’ve known, a synergy of mechanical drone and wild fertility. 

Eventually, I draw back into a shape more distinct. Not as rigid as the amphibian’s metallic lines, not as ephemeral as the fractal illusions, but with gentle fur, luminous patches, and a slow heartbeat that resonates in time with the hiss of hidden pistons. My limbs stretch and contract, letting me pad silently through the stone corridors. My eyes blink golden in the half-night. I once again see the other beings going about their endless cycles. The amphibious folk near the pools gather, their frills pulsing with color as they communicate in tones that harmonize with the hush of turbines I can’t see. Passing insects fizz with electricity, glowing sporadically in a code known only to them. Far overhead, a reptilian flyer glides, trailing silver mist from its membranes.

I realize that “belonging” doesn’t mean adopting just one form; belonging is the recognition that I am part of the hum—part of the plateau’s living machine. I might settle briefly in a warm crook of moss, letting the residue of dew seep into my fur. Or I might sink into a pool to feel my skin shimmer in amphibious patterns for a time. Maybe, on a windy updraft, I’ll find myself shape-shifted into one of those giant fliers, skimming the spires with outstretched wings. Here, no single identity defines me. The land itself offers innumerable ways to exist.

Perhaps that is my unique role in this place: to taste every facet of it, sliding from form to form, bridging the still hush of rock with the frantic life that scuttles and sings. I become the observer and the observed, weaving each perspective into a tapestry of unbiased awe. I carry the hum in my heart—or perhaps it carries me. When I rest, the throbbing current lulls me into a half-stupor, as if I’m returning to the blueprint of all life here, half-engineered, half-organic.

In moments of clarity, I sense an ancient comprehension, stretching back before the spires were flung skyward. This plateau may have emerged anew, fresh and sharp under the clouds, but the intelligence within it is older than can be measured—a perpetual balancing engine that merges biology with mechanism. It is a puzzle that doesn’t seek a solution; it simply functions, unchanging, rewriting the rules of what is possible.

And so, if you were to ask me: “Where are you in this realm, and what are you?” I would gently smile and say: I am everywhere and nowhere in particular. Sometimes I am the amphibian standing upright in luminous waters, sometimes I am the translucent insect humming with stored lightning. I can be the furry mammal sliding through vines like liquid shadow, or even the ghost-shape dancing on the surface of a mirror-black puddle. Occasionally, I am the dreamlike vantage point of a winged sentinel high above the ring of spires. And in quieter instants, I simply diffuse into the surrounding hum, becoming the plateau’s breath.

I exist as a shifting whisper within the humming stasis. The hum is chorus, heartbeat, cosmic engine. I belong here as deeply as the rain that never stops collecting in the mossy hollows, as intimately as the shifting illusions on the edge of sight. In this timeless equilibrium—machine yet wilderness, newborn yet ancient—I find that I am only one of many forms that completes the puzzle. And I relish each resonance, each soft inhalation of misty air, each subtle ripple that plays along the metallic notes of unseen pistons. I have found my place in that living, breathing contradiction… by realizing that in this realm, to belong is simply to be part of the hum.
