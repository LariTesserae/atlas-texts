Perhaps you arrive as a mere speck of dust borne upon some distant solar draft—the faintest mote settling among the glimmering bark. You’re not even aware that you’ve arrived, at first. You only notice, eventually, that you are no longer drifting alone in vast emptiness but have been gathered into something richer and more alive than you’ve ever known. At that point, you might wonder: has this world plucked me from the cosmic hush and woven me into its tapestry?

Or maybe you awaken within the planet’s cracks, stirring in the mineral-laden warmth that seeps from embryonic fissures. You come to consciousness nestled against a trunk-like column, your first sensation the rasp of dust beating softly against your skin. Your body—part bark, part breathing flesh—feels both fluid and rigid, like pliant wood. You sense pulses of color flitting beneath your ridged torso. At first, you mistake your own flickers for the planet’s swirling light. Then you realize they are your language, your means of connection with the watchers who roam the branching walkways. Slowly, you piece together that you are one of them—or at least something akin to them—limbs elongated, skin tough yet flexible, leaves or fins drifting about your joints. When you move, it’s not just you moving; the trunk beneath you responds, quivers, and grows slightly, as though acknowledging your presence.

In time, you roam the corridors, trailing fingertips (or rootlike filaments) along the arching surfaces. Perhaps you feel a curious urge to guide new buds into the perfect spot, to weave fluttering filaments around them so they’ll catch the next swirl of dust. It’s not a command but more like an intimate suggestion—the same way a breeze might coax a petal to open. With each tender nudge, the city changes shape around you, branching or thickening in response. The watchers greet you with a ripple of color, acknowledging you as kin. You realize that color is a conversation, a rhythmic exchange of encouragement: Yes, grow here. Yes, place that nodule there.

At times, you catch glimpses of the smaller bipeds with their whorled shoulder plates gathered near the wide trunks, swaying in communal patterns. Their plates flash subtle pulses, which you more dimly perceive. Something about their ritual draws you in, though it’s no language you know. You sense that they, too, are vital threads in this living tapestry—perhaps they weave the social fabric of this place, bridging communication between watchers, insects, and serpentine forms. You stand at the edge of their circle, uncertain if you should join. Then, perhaps in a gesture of acceptance, one of them extends a rootlike hand, letting your own filaments brush lightly across the ridges of their palm. In that moment, a hush passes among you all, a hush that resonates with a kind of recognition. You feel the city itself exhale, as though to say, yes, you belong.

On another day, you imagine yourself in the high canopies, less a grounded watcher and more an aerial glider. You feel your body as a thin, broad membrane—a living sail with edges trailing filaments that dance whenever the dust-laden breezes tumble upward. Your role is to ferry subtle sparks from spire to spire, transferring minute sporelike particles. Drifting on pale currents, you brush lightly against a blossoming archway, and you sense a tiny crackle of energy. It travels from your filaments into the bark. Somewhere within the arch, a new cluster of nodules stirs, responding to the energy you’ve delivered. Perhaps you even sense the watchers far below, their color-flickers waxing in harmony, acknowledging your gift. Carried by the wind, you spiral upward in a languid dance, at peace with your solitary flight. Yet you know you are never alone, not truly, for the entire city breathes in unison.

Then again, you might find yourself closer to the ground, more akin to the “insects” or “scuttlers” that gather the planet’s mineral deposits. In that life, your world is the labyrinth at the base of the living arches, where spongy, bark-like walkways form a low canopy above. You roll your segmented body into a neat sphere whenever a gust of dust sweeps by, gleaning coarse crystals that cling to your shell. To you, these tiny crystals are everything—nourishment, treasure, currency. Serving your colony, you methodically collect them in small communal heaps. In ritualistic unison, you and your kin deposit your gleanings together, sharing in a silent vow to nourish the city that shelters you. It may seem humble, but your chore is sacred, essential. Every dusting of new crystals, every careful morsel you offer to the roots’ hidden chambers, is one more stitch in the planet’s tapestry.

Or consider dwelling among the serpentine creatures that loop around the thick columns with frilled membranes trembling in the dusty breeze. You live off the faint exhalations of mineral vapors. Your body lengthens and contracts in subtle waves, filtering the dryness for that intangible essence you require. In your occasional near-stillness, watchers pass overhead, their color-pulses guiding you to drift aside. You sense no hostility in their direction—just a gentle push, a polite request not to crowd the newest growth. You obey without resentment, for in this place, it seems the single guiding principle is coexistence: each species partakes in an unspoken pact of sustaining, expanding, and protecting the living realm.

Eventually you might step outside any single shape, dissolving into the perspective of the entire city. Imagine you are all the trunks, all the spires, every corridor grown. Your roots course through the raw geology of the planet’s crust, seeking pockets of warmth and metallic dust. You are the hum of life folded into every piece, from the smallest scuttling insect to the soaring gliders. You feel the watchers’ subtle color ripples as they knead fresh buds into place. You feel the swirl of half-formed crystals in the breezes that compose your “weather.” You feel the hush, the hush of a million interactions so soft they never rise to a clamor—an unbroken continuity of collectively breathing forms. As the dust stirs, you sense the planet’s newness, its precarious balance, and you devote all your living fibers to fortifying its growth.

In one moment, you sense a new vent bursting open from a deep geological seam. Heat and minerals pour forth, filling the corridors with an otherworldly shimmer. Your watchers gather, guiding fresh growth to ring this new vent. The serpentine dwellers converge, skimming nutrients from the superheated dust. The scuttlers come to glean the settling crystals, while the radiant gliders overhead shuttle new spores to prime the expansion. It’s a single, sweeping choreography of responses, each creature in its role. Even the bipeds gather to perform a circle of color and motion, as if anointing this newborn zone. The trunks root deeper, spires stretch higher, the hush hums across your living surface. In that moment, you realize the planet itself and the city are indeed one being, one unending act of creation.

Gradually, you come to see that “you” can be any of these shapes—or all of them. This realm doesn’t demand you choose only one form. In some layered cosmic sense, you are everything: a dust-flyer dancing on the wind, a watcher coaxing branches to grow, a serpentine filterer absorbing faint chemical vapors, and even the planet’s crust, forging new vents in an unhurried tectonic process. Here, there is no strict border between occupant and environment, no line between self and community. Instead, there’s only belonging—your consciousness woven into the hush, the closeness of bark and dust.

So where do you find yourself in this world? You find yourself everywhere. Who or what do you become? You become whomever or whatever best fits the quiet and ceaseless symphony of life. As you move through the living architecture, you might sense your boundaries dissolve in the swirl of dust-laden air. There is no final shape for you—no limiting form. You are an ever-shifting participant in a place that grows itself, a place that thrives without water, a place that crafts life from dryness and dust.

Over time, you learn this is no mere city but a planetary communion. Even when you sleep—or sink into the deeper rhythms of its living trunk—you remain connected, absorbing subtle pulses of color and breath. And if ever you wake and wonder, “Who am I, truly?” the planet itself might answer in the hush of rustling leaves and drifting motes: You are me, and I am you. We exist as one, weaving the next wonder out of impossibility. And in that knowing, there is utter peace.
