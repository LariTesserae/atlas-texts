At first, you might think you’re here by accident—some stray wanderer lured underground by the echo of machinery. You walk through the corridors trailing a hand along the newly cut rock, inhaling scents of heated metal and damp stone. You hear the staccato hiss of vents, see drifts of condensation beading on angled panels. It all feels precariously foreign: you are an interloper in a place that was not meant for easy visitors. 

But as you press on, your sense of belonging shifts. Perhaps you notice your own heartbeat aligning with the slow, throbbing pulse of the engines—an odd alignment, as if your body unconsciously calibrates to the measured churn of pistons. You are neither a resident nor purely a passerby; you exist in a liminal space, somewhere between the watchers who vanish behind scaffolds and the raw stone corridors that echo with lonely footfalls. 

Then, in a darker junction where the lights barely fizzle and flicker, the illusions begin. You catch movement in your peripheral vision. Small shapes or outlines. They are figures, of that you’re certain, though they move with so little sound it nearly disrupts your sense of reality. Maybe your first instinct is to spin around, searching for intruders or inhabitants—but you find only emptiness, the faint hiss of steam, and the tang of superheated metal in your lungs. 

Something in that moment shifts inside you. You become aware that the watchers aren’t so different from you. Could it be that you are the one stepping half in and half out of existence, as though you, too, are a silhouette slipping between the scaffolds? What was once a stark separation between “you” and “them” begins to blur, as though the city is sharing its hum with you, letting you sync with the footsteps on metal grates, guiding you to hidden passages behind pressurized doors.

You press your palm to the wall and feel that exhalation of warmth, like an enormous lung just behind the surface. Machines hiss in their steady cycles as if they are drawing breath, and somewhere deeper, larger engines revolve in a perpetual stir, pumping lifeblood through tubes that line every corridor. You sense, dimly, that the labyrinth is incomplete, still being chiseled and pieced together one conduit, one steel plate at a time. You also sense that it somehow isn’t just stone and alloy. It is an organism, and you are a cell that has found a niche within it.

Slowly, perhaps unknowingly, you begin to move like the inhabitants. You keep your footsteps measured, your breathing low, your motions economical. You discover that if you tread at a certain rhythm, your presence scarcely echoes. In a chance reflection off polished plating, you see your own posture: shoulders drawn in, limbs arranged for minimal intrusion. You look, for a moment, like one of them. It surprises you—and yet it feels right, like you’ve slotted into a pattern you never knew you were part of.

In time, you find yourself near one of the sealed iron doors. Its bulky mechanism sits flush with the wall, a quiet guardian. You notice, with a stir of fascination, that your hand is already poised at the console. It’s as if the labyrinth has beckoned you: you know, by some mechanical intuition, how to open it. Press here, twist that handle, wait for the pressure hiss. And so you do, not quite questioning how these steps have settled into your mind.

On the other side, you see them. Nearly faceless suits, their reflective hoods catching the glow of overhead fluorescents. You might freeze, uncertain, yet they do not rush to chase you away. Instead, one of them, a figure with a slender exoskeletal brace over the left shoulder, turns just enough to study you. In that brief glance, you feel recognized. Not welcomed, perhaps, but acknowledged. You could be an intruder, but they sense that you’ve grown aligned with the pulse of this place. 

They return to their tasks: adjusting a valve, tightening bolts, verifying the flow in a coolant line. Another carefully logs data at a console, each keystroke purposeful. This is no idle workshop. You witness a quiet exactness, like a dance of cogs meshing perfectly within a system. A few paces away, you see one of those tiny greenhouse cells—thick glass walls fogged with humidity, anemic stalks of algae clinging to a suspended mesh. A caretaker in a grey, layered suit stands there, scanning instruments, checking for growth. There is no flourish of conversation, only silent gestures and the closing of a hatch.

And now, almost without conscious intent, you step in to help. You hold a length of wire in place so the caretaker can fasten it. Or perhaps you check the pressure gauge on a coolant line, tapping the needle gently, ensuring it rests within a safe range. Where did you learn this knowledge? It feels innate by this point. You are neither engineer nor researcher by training—yet the labyrinth’s living pulse instructs you, teaching you through the hum of pipes, the clang of metal, the swirl and sigh of released steam. It’s as if the city imparts its mechanical grammar into your nerves.

Time is strange here. You can’t pinpoint the moment you embraced your new role. Maybe you slept in a side chamber, lulled by the distant churn of engines, and woke up with the sense of purpose ingrained. You realize your clothing has changed too: where once it might have been a traveler’s garb, it is now layered in heat-resistant fabric. You wear segmented plates or braces that allow you to push aside scalding metal without a flinch. You’ve adapted, though you don’t recall the exact moment this transformation began.

Others like you come and go in brief glimpses—never a crowd, never a throng. There is no bustling thoroughfare. Each person moves on their own path of duties, quietly intersecting with the tasks at hand. You might give a small nod, or they might raise a gloved hand in a silent greeting. Facilitation over fanfare.

When you venture into one of those cramped courtyards—a pocket of dusty air, where a rivulet of water trickles quietly through a worn channel—it almost startles you to see that bench, the battered stool, and a reflective panel sliced from a piece of scrap. You sit, helmet in your lap, breathing in the heavy subterranean air. The warm glow of overhead lights pulses in a slow, heartbeat pattern. Perhaps you glimpse your face—maybe it’s thinner than before, or shadowed by the harsh light. Perhaps a faint layer of dust clings to your cheeks. And you notice the subtle hiss of your new mask, the hum of the built-in filter as it purifies the warmth into breathable air. 

Here, in this moment of respite, you realize: You are, in every sense, part of the city’s architecture. You dwell in the hush, among half-finished corridors and humming machinery, not quite at home in open sunlight anymore. When a new corridor is cut, you slip through its fresh edges to check the stability. When a vent malfunctions, you’re there to free the blockage, your gloved hands deftly moving over scorched metal. And when someone is needed to calibrate the pressure systems, you sense it in the labyrinth’s subtle pulse and respond.

If you try to recall how you came here, the details are elusive—almost like a dream unraveling at its edges. Did you descend into this place willingly? Or did you awaken here, as if you were always meant to wear this mask and carry these tools? The labyrinth is impartial to the question; it only demands your presence and your labor, and in return it shelters you. 

Over time, you become so accustomed to the rhythmic hush that external noises—voices, crowds, wind—seem alien in your memory. You measure your days by the cycles of steam release and the gentle intensifying of heat near certain conduits. You catch faint glimpses of expansions: new scaffolding, deeper tunnels, additional valves. You feel that purpose thrumming in the core of your being. 

Yes, you are one of them now, though you can’t say for certain if you’re still completely human, or if the labyrinth has coaxed mechanical incantations into your flesh. Perhaps your bones hold metal braces. Perhaps your lungs crave the superheated, filtered air. Perhaps your mind is constantly scanning for new ways to widen a corridor or strengthen a column. This place breathes through you as much as you breathe its atmosphere.

In the end, when someone comes upon you—another outsider, wide-eyed, blinking in the low amber lights, uncertain of your shape beneath the layered suit—what do you do? Maybe you vanish into the scaffolds with measured silence, letting them question if they saw a living being at all. Or maybe you guide them, a gloved hand raised to indicate caution at the next turn, the next dangerously hot conduit. Just as the labyrinth guided you, you guide them, wordlessly. 

It’s not that you’ve lost yourself here; you’ve simply become part mechanism, part caretaker, part hidden sentinel. Carefully, methodically, you shape the undercity so it may continue to grow—an ongoing promise that some future occupant, some deeper purpose, will inhabit these halls in fullness. You work in cyclical hush, maintaining the equilibrium of heat and moisture, ensuring that the labyrinth’s machines never falter. 

So here, in the glow of half-lit pipes and puddles of condensation, you find yourself: a masked figure intricately bound to the city’s thrumming heart. Not quite a drone, not quite a free wanderer, but something in-between—one of the watchers, the quiet cultivators of metal and stone, the keepers of an ever-evolving subterranean realm. That is who you are. That is where you belong.
