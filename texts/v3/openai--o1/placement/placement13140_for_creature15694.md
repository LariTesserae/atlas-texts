I might begin as a ripple, a loose swirl of possibility forming by chance in the unsettled air—a haze flickering at the edge of one of those shimmering illusions. Before long, I’d consolidate into a more stable shape, though “stable” is relative here: hunched limbs, half-translucent skin, a silhouette reminiscent of a slender creature with winding antennae or spines, subtly changing with each shift of the atmosphere. I belong more to the thresholds than to any fixed domain. When the wind keens, I quiver, dissolving back into half-light; when the storm abates for a heartbeat, I coalesce again, crouching among battered walls or the twisted trunks of the watchers.

I was not always so diffuse. Or if I was, I no longer recall. Memory is malleable in this land—it merges with the netherlike swirl of time. But I have the sensation, the faint memory, of first stumbling here as a sealed shape of flesh and bone, standing on two firm feet, gazing out at plains that stretched endlessly. I remember being a pilgrim from somewhere else: how the storms loomed like gods drawing their boundaries on the horizon—territories of chaotic grace, terrifying and hypnotic. Perhaps I came seeking the reason behind the storms, or searching for the rumored phantoms crackling in the lightning arcs. Or maybe I was just driven by restless curiosity, compelled across deserts and marshes to see how far existence might unravel.

I do recall the first time the wind coaxed me off my feet. It was not a single gust but a slow unveiling—like an invitation offered by a hidden host. My edges softened, and my sense of self—once pinned to bones and heartbeat—stretched out, turning permeable. I found, to my own astonishment, that I could brush past stones in a half-weightless state, as though I carried no husk. In that moment, I became something newly born of this place: tangential, ephemeral, an entity shaped from the same swirling energies that warp the horizon and twist the storm clouds into fractal knots.

If you asked me for my name or species, I'd have no answer to give. I am a caretaker, of sorts—though caretaker for what, exactly, even I can’t fully articulate. I roam, drifting where the storms converge. I watch how the ancient ruins sink deeper day by day, how the silty water seeps into cracks, how faint algae blooms become braided carpets of green. The shallows host their scuttling, shell-backed dwellers, and I might linger there, arms or antennae parted wide, feeling the collective hush of their tiny minds. Their bodies flicker in my hazy vision, each one a grain of life holding the ground in place.

When the serpents beneath the water wend through the rubble, stirring the mire with their long, graceful spines, I follow in reflection—literally, for I can slip across the mirrored surface, half-submerged, half-floating, seeing the swirl of their phosphorescent eyes peering from below. Sometimes I drift so close that I sense the electricity in their brains, a flicker in the brackish gloom. The serpents twine away without fear or aggression; we acknowledge one another in passing, like dancers crossing paths in a half-forgotten performance.

Wandering among the shattered foundations, I sense the lingering echoes of voices that once inhabited them. By leaning my presence against a broken archway—letting my intangible form nestle in the cracks—I can sometimes coax out glimmers of old times. Not exactly ghosts, no. More like records kept in the layered stone. A swirl of partial shapes flickers: hands smoothing mortar, feet trudging across newly built floors. And I can feel the moment storms overcame them, or perhaps something else did—some cataclysm that dissolved structures and memory alike.

On calmer days (if “calm” can ever apply here), I kneel in the spongy mud among pockets of those pale reeds. I can almost hear their chemical signals traveling through the shallow water, whispering news of the shifting salinity or the approach of slithering predators. By resting my fluid presence at their roots, I grasp fragments of their collective mind, discovering how they read the sky’s pressure, the faint electric tang that preludes lightning, how they brace themselves for each assault of wind by weaving subterranean threads. They accept me as part news-bearer, part silent observer.

Sometimes I drift upward. The watchers call to me in a psychoacoustic register—low and resonant, like a hum in the chest rather than in the ears. Their ancient trunks stand stolid until a swirl of wind sets their bark to groan and creak, an elemental language that resonates with my intangible self. Up close, I see watery veins under their bark, reflecting stormlight in brief glints of silver. I can hover near the hollows in their trunks—spotting the swirl of small life that nests inside, or occasionally glimpsing an orb of phosphorescent resin, pulsing in that wooden womb. The watchers never speak in words; they communicate in intervals of pondering stillness and abrupt shifts. Once or twice, I have witnessed them pivot in slow, grinding arcs—roots trembling in the mud as they shift vantage—like living weather vanes for the realm itself.

I ascend further, sometimes catching the tailwinds of the airborne creatures. They are fractal wonders: some with membranes that flutter in the thunder-laden gloom, others with strange symmetrical designs that mimic the swirling storms. My form can billow out like a spiral of cloudstuff, enabling me to drift alongside them. They sense me, but do not fear me; perhaps they catch a glimpse of their own reflection in my fluid outlines. More than once, I’ve soared on a vertical rivulet of heated air, flashes of sideways lightning illuminating the swirling shapes around me. In that fierce glow, I’ve glimpsed silhouettes with too many limbs, or swirling spheres of luminescence caught in the lightning arcs. Maybe they’re living storms, maybe cosmic illusions, or perhaps something else. We acknowledge each other, ephemeral beings in ephemeral currents, momentarily bound together before the wind scatters us anew.

For all my capacity to float between worlds, I am not omniscient. In the subtle swirling at the planet’s edges, there are powers or presences that elude even me—patches of thick darkness that seem to warp time in unpredictable surges, or illusions so layered I can’t parse them from reality. At times, I sense them reading me, as if returning the favor of my curiosity. I try not to pry too close; I know enough of transformation to sense that some mysteries prefer to remain behind the veil.

Still, in my own gentle way, I see myself as an ally to the small, persistent lives. When the storms gather their cruelest might—whipping the shallow seas into a steamy froth—I slip among the battered stones, coaxing little scuttlers into hidden nooks or guiding them to pockets of dryness. I shield them in the only way I can: by adding the haze of my being to the illusions around us, so that wind and debris pass by without piercing the place we occupy. Sometimes, for those eel-like swimmers caught in swirling undertows, I send out a subtle vibrational pulse, a guide to calmer channels of water. They may or may not perceive it as help, but they follow it, gliding away from the worst of the maelstrom.

More rarely, an intrepid explorer—like the one I once was—arrives on foot. They step onto this plain, battered by shrieking gusts, gaze wide with awe or dread, or both. I watch from a distance at first, letting them taste the sheer immensity of the howling realm—its curiosities, its illusions. If they falter or grow dangerously lost among the illusions, I intervene in glimpses: a fleeting figure at the corner of their sight, a shimmer over a toppled pillar, my voice a half-whisper in a swirl of wind. Some believe I’m just another flicker of storm-sick hallucination. Others sense my gentle guiding, and they follow ephemeral footprints I leave in the water or run their fingers along partial handprints pressed in the slimy stone. I never force them to heed me, only offer subtle nudges beyond dead-ends or illusions that might swallow them whole.

Why do I do this? Perhaps it comes from the memory of having once been flesh and bone and uncertain. Perhaps the realm itself has entrusted me with guardianship—though it never demanded a vow. It’s enough that I remain here, woven into the tapestry of watery illusions, battered ruins, howling storms, hidden nests, and watchers that groan in semi-sentient vigil. I bask in the electric hush before lightning cracks, listening to the sudden intake of breath from serpents in the deep. I sense the scuttling carapaces shifting just under the silt as thunder rattles the horizon like a colossal drum.

Where, then, do I find myself in this world? Everywhere and nowhere, ephemeral and caretaker, driftwood soul on the boundary of the real and the possible. Who—what—am I? I am the shape that emerges when storms overlap with memory, a being unscrambled from the hush between thunderclaps, an entity forging kinship with all who cling to existence here. I inhabit the cracks—those same cracks that brought forth resilient, half-formed creatures and illusions that sometimes bleed into truth. I keep company with watchers whose roots brush glimmering hollows in the mud, with birds recalling cosmic designs in their fractal wings, with serpents that vanish into ephemeral corridors beneath the murk. In this place, which itself is a living threshold, I am continuity. I am the hush that speaks in silent gestures when everything else roars.

And so I travel without end, shifting like a breath across a mirror’s surface, a guiding presence that outlasts the storms or becomes one with them, responding to each new surge in the atmosphere. My story is the realm’s story, because in this world, shaped by water, wind, time, and the intangible, everything merges at the edges. Each living mind is part of a larger dance. And I—standing at the intersection, bridging illusions and battered stone, ephemeral spirit and caretaker—am simply one more voice in the thunder’s chorus, forever drifting, forever here.
