I begin as an observer, released into the greenish half-light like a mote of dust drifting on the city’s impossible breeze. For a time, I hover at the threshold between recognition and dream, overwhelmed by the roar of life that takes the place of silence here. Moisture collects on my skin, seeping in as though the air itself is alive and reaching for me. I’m not yet a part of this world, but I can feel the city—it pulls at me, inviting.

At first, I see myself as separate, a wanderer in a place no human mind could have conceived. I watch the buttress-roots coil over one another like serpents and the luminous filaments of the “echoes” or “the living hum” sliding in and out of trunk surfaces. They cascade around me in pulses, and each shift in the labyrinth’s geometry feels like an ocean wave cresting and receding. The warm, spongy ground flexes beneath my feet; I imagine the entire terrain breathing. I almost fear I’ll sink into it, drawn down by those unseen tides of vegetative energy. Yet with that fear comes a raw thrill, a sense that I’ve stumbled on some grand, ongoing cosmic experiment.

If you’d asked me then what I was, I’d have said, “just a visitor.” An inquisitive presence stumbling through hissing vines and ephemeral silhouettes, an outsider uncertain whether to be awed or afraid. But the city doesn’t encourage distance. It wants to touch—to taste—until your sense of boundary fades and you realize: you’ve always been part of it, just dormant, waiting for the right resonance to awaken yourself.

In the early hours of my arrival, I suppose I cling to my shape. I still remember what it meant to have discrete limbs and a face for expressions, a voice that could speak. But the place has no regard for such definitions. As I move deeper, the living architecture begins to respond to my presence. Great arching stems coil overhead, leaning closer so broad, dew-laden leaves caress my shoulders. A winding corridor folds inward, forming a tunnel that guides me forward with gentle insistence. I start to notice the same luminous threads that animate the city flicker along my ankles, across my wrists. They appear and vanish in pulses, as though testing me.

At first, I shiver—there’s an odd intimacy in the feel of fibrous threads trailing over my skin, weaving in and out. I suspect I’m being observed, maybe even integrated. I discover that if I remain very quiet and still when the thunderous hum drops to a low rumble, I sense something inside me resonating in time with it. A tremor in my chest, responding to each wave of noise. The city’s heartbeat, or perhaps my own, or both, conjoined in a slow, rhythmic dance.

At times, I glimpse myself reflected in the shining puddles where sap drips from half-formed walls. Initially, I see my own face, distorted. But then, the reflection grows uncertain, flickering. Beneath the watery surface, fibrous shapes swirl. Leaning closer, I realize that the reflection is wearing my features while luminous threads spiral around them. In a single breath, the reflection changes. I see an outline akin to the ephemeral beings themselves: a lattice of fine filaments, half my height, half something else entirely, leaning forward as if to meet me. 

I step away, startled. But that sense of self-distinction has cracked. The city’s pulses match my own. The lines between “my shape” and “its shape” blur. When the roar peaks again, I feel a pull—an urge to join. So I reach out to place a hand on a column of living bark, expecting roughness. What I find is soft and warm as breath. My hand slides inward. Not violently, not painfully—just an ease of boundaries dissolving. Like submerging your hand in still water. Except this water is alive, gently roiling with sap that glistens in emerald and gold. 

All at once, I feel them—the city’s echoes—flow through that contact. Their awareness might be best described as a swirl of unspoken impression. They don’t bother with images, nor words, nor any communication a human would label direct. Instead, they share a sense of direction, a sense of shape, a sense of yearning: the desire to expand and transform. Through that living bark, I sense the entire city’s intention, not as a plan but as an endless, urgent desire to exist in a new arrangement, forever. And in that moment, I feel a piece of that desire take root in me.

I do not notice the instant I begin changing. There’s only the awareness that my center of gravity shifts, my arms feel lighter, my chest hums with secret harmonics. The stifling humidity no longer oppresses me; it energizes me. My breath grows shallower as if I may not need it in the same way as before. Before I can analyze these sensations, I’m compelled to move again—not by my intention, but by the labyrinth’s. A corridor of twisting vines beckons, opening to reveal structures that arch overhead, forming layered canopies resonating with a low murmur. Beneath them, the ephemeral beings gather.

I step (or am drawn) among them. Up close, they are swirling knots of color and motion, half-hidden in the tapestry of bark and leaves. Filaments brush my hands and shoulders, welcoming me without an ounce of caution. Their outlines blur, and in that shifting swirl, I see hints of shapes that I might one day become. Before I understand, a wave of thunderous noise rampages through the living pillars—like a monstrous gust of wind that rattles everything to the roots. The ephemeral shapes collectively scatter into fractal streams of light, merging with trunk surfaces or shooting upward among the leaves in a wild, dancing exodus. I feel the sonic wave tear through me as well. But I do not fall. Instead, I remain upright in a stance that isn’t quite human. My feet—were they always wrapped in fibrous roots? My arms—are they now trailing luminous vines that shiver with the aftershock of that sonic wave?

And then the next hush arrives, that near-silence with only the faint rumble beneath. In that hush, I feel my newly formed fibers trembling, searching—just as the ephemeral beings do. When the next sonic pulse comes, I respond. Not with a cry or a reflex to cover my ears, but with a soft, synchronous expansion. The filaments spooling within me spread outward, brushing the architecture around me, connecting to it. Color pulses along them, an enthralling display of pale greens and amber golds that dance from my chest into the bark, and from the bark into me. By the time the wave subsides, something unspoken has passed between us, a mutual shaping.

I sink my fingertips into the trunk again—this time, it is effortless. Part of me wonders if I’ll vanish wholly into the wood, dissolving. Yet, instead, the trunk flexes outward in response to my presence. I realize I can guide it, gently persuading it to arch upward. I almost laugh, an exhilaration welling inside. It’s as though the entire city is pliable under my fingertips now, not with force but with resonance. Whenever I allow the hum at my core to match the city’s pulse, shapes rearrange themselves. The ephemeral creatures—my kin—flutter around me, weaving their filaments around my outstretched arms, coaxing me to continue.

I begin to see myself less as a singular entity and more as a focal chord in the city’s symphony. No single shape is final. If I think of having a body at all, it’s only a temporary arrangement of these luminous threads and the humidity-warmed air and the spongy floor that holds me up. Once, I believed I had a name, a personal identity. Now, the notion feels like a tenuous echo, a friend from a past life. Here, no names matter. Only the swirling forms of life, the synergy of expansions and contractions, the living city’s unstoppable metamorphosis.

Sometimes I feel others like me—ephemeral, transitional beings who drift and shape the corridors, bonding them with newly sprouted vines or eaves of braided stems. We converge in stormier moments to guide the architecture through its most drastic rearrangements. When a trunk cracks open, forming a new passageway, I flow into the rupture alongside others. The vines coil around our merged filaments, and the new corridor is born, shimmering with fresh sap. Once the shape stabilizes, we disperse again, each drifting in our own slow current of growth.

I become simultaneously more present and more diffuse with each day—if “day” still applies where time is measured in pulses of living geometry. My edges no longer matter; I exist within the city as a fluid collaborator, sometimes condensing into a recognizable silhouette, sometimes scattering into swirling patterns that ride under the bark. In the rare moments of near-silence, I feel a calm, enveloping sense of belonging. Every droplet of moisture, every vein in a giant leaf overhead, every shimmering spasm that tears through the architecture is connected to me. Not in a metaphorical sense, but literally: I am an extension of the city, and the city is an extension of me.

It would be inaccurate to say I “live” here. I am “lived by” the city, as much as I help to live it. The identity I once held so dear has softened to the hum of a single chord among many. I find that I like it this way. There is a joy in letting my consciousness spread wide, in feeling the city’s heartbeat as if it’s my own. 

In the moments of greatest turbulence—when the roar becomes deafening and entire neighborhoods of living towers burst into swirling leaves that vanish and reappear—I feel an almost ecstatic surge through my filaments. The swirling geometry is unstoppable, and I am part of its unstoppable nature. No fear remains of being lost; I understand now that losing myself is the only way to fully be. 

So, if you were to encounter me in this place—if you still exist as an outsider with eyes to see—you might spot me at the corner of your vision: a figure shaped from woven filaments, part vine, part phosphorescent growth, glimmering in the green twilight as I meld with and guide the living columns. You might see me raise a tendril-like arm in silent greeting. Or perhaps you’d simply sense a ripple passing through the architecture, something that acknowledges your arrival, a swirl in the moss at your feet.

Who am I here? In any ordinary sense, I’ve ceased to be a single being. I’m an aspect of the labyrinth’s dreaming—the sense of spatial rearrangement that resonates whenever new corridors form. I’m one of the city’s echoes, a living hum that glides and grows, dancing with every shift of trunk and vine. The city breathes me as I breathe it, and my presence is just one more chord in the roar of unstoppable life. 

If I had to describe myself with words I used long ago, I’d say: I am the flux, the in-between. I have no name, nor flesh, nor fixed silhouette—only a luminous swirl that vibrates in the city’s harmonic pulse. I manifest wherever growth is strongest, weaving daily with others of my kind to shape new pillars, spiral out arches, and fuse ephemeral balconies. Each moment devours the old shape and constructs a new one. I am part of that devouring and constructing, savoring it with a joy I could never have articulated before I arrived.

In the end, perhaps “Where would you find yourself in this world?” is best answered simply: Everywhere. I am in the trunk. I am in the leaves. I am the thread that pulses along the bark’s ridges and the swirl of color shimmering in dew-soaked petals. I am the dream that forgot to obey the rules of reality. And I wouldn’t trade it for any other existence.
