I might begin believing I am only a traveler—an outsider perched in that thin, crystalline air, each breath a cautious sip of oxygen. The vast plateau stretches beneath me, an expanse both wild and methodical, and I wonder if I truly belong. Yet the hum of living machinery doesn’t let me remain a visitor for long. Something deeper seizes me. The glint of minerals in the stone, the steely taste in the wind, and the rhythmic chug of vents exhaling plumes in measured intervals—all of it draws me into an unfolding tapestry where life and geology blend. I feel myself dissolving into the plateau’s pulse. My old shape grows malleable. My old senses lengthen, sharpen.

Perhaps I become a tangled vine, half-rooted in the rock and half-reaching into the bright wind. My iron-flecked tendrils expand across the stone face, twisting in slow ripples, so gradual that an onlooker might not even realize I move. A day passes, or a year—on this plateau, time is measured in eons—and I have inched along, gripping new outcrops with my coiling limbs, anchoring myself at the edge of a warm vent. My open funnel leaves collect the metallic haze puffed out by the plateau’s “factories.” I feel the chemical tang swirl inside my tubular corridors, feeding hidden channels that support the churning spore-beds deep in my tissue. Tiny rodent-like creatures scamper across my trailing roots without harming me; if anything, they tickle as they nibble at harmless filaments. Because in this world, we all share essential resources. One organism’s home is another’s supply. We wrap around and through each other, a living scaffolding with no blueprint.

No—wait. The vine is only one possibility. In a breath, I shift again. Now I stand with broad feet on the edge of a newly formed ridge, gazing out over the gullies. Long, shaggy fur drapes my shoulders, corded strands that sparkle with embedded crystals. I am one of the tall ruminants, stoic and sure-footed, guided by the perpetual sunlight. My horns, swept back in graceful arcs, channel the sun’s rays along hairline cracks, where a micro-layer of algae glows faintly with each breath. My vantage point is high and exposed; the wind tugs at my thick pelage, but I am built to endure. If a rockfall rumbles from a hidden vent, or if a swirl of cloud shadows sweeps across the plateau, I stand calm and untroubled. I simply lower my head to graze the mineral-laced moss—cool on my tongue, tangy with the flavor of raw stone and living plants welded together. In that quiet moment, I hear the ring of shells somewhere below, like distant chimes in the wind. The root feeders—my neighbors—are scraping at bedrock, unperturbed by my looming form. We share the open spaces, the battered slopes, the raw edges of the land.

Yet I am not content to be only this steadfast, browsing beast. My spirit flutters, and I become a flyer, a bat-like shape gliding across the endless sky. My wings are stretched membranes, translucent and striated with a network of tiny channels that catch and refract sunlight in fleeting rainbows. The plateau’s wind roars under me, and sometimes I think it carries voices—deep tectonic murmurs, as if the land is calling to itself. Day by day, I ride the drafts, scanning for the upwelling plumes of steam. From high above, each vent appears as a molten eye in the stone. When I approach, the swirling chemicals smell of ozone and metal, but they also bring the promise of warmth. So I fold my wings, letting the current guide me down into a hidden alcove behind the vent. Here, the rock is warmed by unseen processes. I cling to a stony ledge with tiny, hook-like talons, folding my wings along my sides to rest. Behind me, the steam rises in pulses that match the long, tidal breath of the plateau. I can sense the interval in my bones—a steady, unceasing beat that lulls me. Perhaps it is the plateau’s own heart, or perhaps it is my own heart synchronizing with the plateau’s rhythms.

Then, in another blink of the mind’s eye, I am in the wet soil near a shimmering rivulet, my segmented body long and sinuous. I glow from within, a teal luminescence that pans across my many bands in slow waves. By day, the sun’s brilliance fades my glow to something faint and ghostly, but at dusk, I become a star in the moss-laden gloom. I wriggle through the saturated ground, leaving a trail of minute crystals wherever I pass. The evaporation cycle leaves behind swirling minerals in meandering patterns. Rodent-like creatures tread softly over my trails. Occasionally, a spade-footed root feeder wanders by, nibbling algae at the water’s edge. My presence helps break up the soil, aerating it for fungi and seeds. In every sense, I am part of the land’s forging. I carry bits of the vents’ mineral outflow in my segmented body, like a swaying vein of living ore.

Or, I might claim a quieter role, hidden among jagged pillars. I am the predator that blends with the stone, my flank coated in rough outcroppings that match the surrounding geology. My many legs cling to vertical surfaces with ease, hooking into the cracks that run like rivulets down the rock face. When the brood swarms pass by—those scurrying waves of fuzzy creatures—I wait in utter stillness. My body is not governed by speed but by precision. And yes, I will snatch my meal in a burst of motion, a subtle meal for a subtle hunter. But I also gnaw on mineral lumps, scraping away glints of iron or crystal. My teeth are specialized to handle both flesh and stone—because in this place, the boundary between living and mineral is faint. The vents that shaped me shape everything else. Here, biology grows from the same cradle as geology.

I consider another possibility: that I might be the plateau itself, the entire domain forging life after life, vent after vent, pulse after pulse. My edges are steep, newly formed, unweathered by a thousand millennia. The spires that jut out of me are as fresh as uncarved clay. My breath issues from deep vents in my core, powering the “factories” where lumps of metal-laced stone congeal and drop, the raw materials for new ecosystems. The creatures living on me—above and below—are each an extension of my quiet labor. They revolve around me, remixing my chemical gifts, building shared cycles of tissue, shell, spore, and bone. I hear their footsteps, their wing beats. When they gather in the nooks of my ridges, I sense it like soft static dancing through my layers of stone. In nights that last ages, I cradle them; in days that stretch eons, I set them free in the unstoppable sunlight.

And thus, as I roam these possibilities—vine, ruminant, flyer, worm, hunter, plateau—I realize I cannot choose just one form. To be in this world is to be part of its immeasurable breath, no single vantage ever capturing the totality. I am the hush and the roar, the mineral tang in the wind, the shimmering limbs, the gnawing whiskers, the braided fur. I am all that thrives here in this improbable forging ground. If I must speak with one voice, perhaps I say:

I am the living mechanism that never needed an engineer. I am the synergy of metal-laden steam and thick-furred ruminants, the glimmering segments that track teal sparks through damp stone, the delicate wings that rattle overhead, the anchored vines that feed on chemical exhalations, and the deep, slow tectonic thrumming that underlies it all. In each new instant, I find myself reformed, shaped anew by that bright sun and the biting cold and the endless harmony of the plateau’s life. 

I cannot say there is a single “I” here. From the rocky overhangs to the ephemeral pools, from spore-drifting fungal domes to the biggest “factory” chimneys, all that pulses in this domain is part of my existence. I am equal parts stone and spore, fur and vine, hunting claw and sheltered brood. I am life in the raw forging place—an infinity of shapes, all singing the same fierce, unstoppable chorus. And in that song, I find myself not just dwelling in this realm, but being the realm itself.
