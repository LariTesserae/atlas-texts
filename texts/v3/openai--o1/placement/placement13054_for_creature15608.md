It starts quietly, the sense of belonging in a place that seems so inhospitable. Perhaps you stand at the plateau’s rim with its hidden valleys lost in the fog and feel the unwavering hum beneath your feet as much as you hear it in the air. Or maybe it first dawns on you in a small patch of moss, where an emerald frond arches deliberately to greet a rivulet of condensation. Regardless of when it happens, or how, you realize: You can exist here. More than that, you suspect this plateau has long expected you.

At first, you can’t decide what that existence would look like. You imagine yourself as an explorer, bright-eyed and separate from this cold, living machine. Maybe you walk the perimeter of the city in a thick coat, pressing your gloved palm against cool metal to sense the heartbeat within. You think of yourself as the outsider, gleaning profound secrets from the rusting catwalks and the whispering vines. But the city does not treat you like a stranger. No muffled alarms sound; no hidden guardians rush to block your path. Each corridor, each ladder leading deeper into twisting sub-levels, seems to beckon you onward.

Then, in the reflection on some wide metal pipe, you catch a glimpse. The shape is humanoid, but drawn long and lean, with metal filaments flickering across the limbs. At first, you think it’s one of the watchers you’ve half-glimpsed in the periphery—until you notice it is your own silhouette. Time behaves differently here. Maybe the plateau is not only indifferent to change but also capable of reshaping you. Little signposts show up slowly: flecks of tarnished silver embedded in your hands, a faint luminescence in your eyes at night, a moment where your breath merges with the swirling steam in such a way that you think you might be contributing to it—fueling it.

You lean down, place one hand on the moss, half expecting it to recoil from your touch, but it doesn’t. Instead, it seems to settle beneath your palm, as though it has recognized your presence or the particular warmth your body provides. It pulses in time with the slow beat of the engines below. Breathe in. Breathe out. In that quiet instant, the notion forms: You are not just an observer. You are beginning to integrate.

Night after night, in the labyrinth’s depths, you find new corridors you’re certain weren’t there before. Sliding panels of black steel reveal substructures—quiet warrens of rotating cogs beside clusters of thick vines that wind themselves through the gearwork. You see them: silhouette-creatures who move with the hush of living metal. Their eyes glint faintly from behind smooth, plated masks or fleshy vines that form shapes akin to swirling crowns. One of them, tall and angular, notices you but offers no threat. Instead, a jointed limb extends, and you realize it’s neither purely mechanical nor organically fleshy: it is an interweave of living steel and fibrous green, wound through with pale roots that glow like phosphorescent veins. When it touches your arm, it’s startlingly cool, yet you sense a courtesy or greeting in the gesture. In that moment, you see yourself reflected in its glassy visor. You do not look entirely human anymore. Something of this place has begun to take hold.

You do not reject it. Initially, you tell yourself it’s only a curiosity, a passing phenomenon, but you can’t dismiss the synchronicity you feel. Every exhalation matches the hum underneath. You walk across a rusted walkway, and with every footstep, the engine’s vibrations sync with your heartbeat. When you peer into a pool collecting near an outcrop, you notice your reflection shimmer, a faint glow at your edges. Turns out the condensation that clings to your skin glints with the same trapped light found in the vines’ droplets. As water runs off your arm, a mossy patch below turns its leaves ever so slightly, to catch what falls from you, as though feeding on more than simple moisture.

One half of you suspects that you have become a caretaker, some amalgam of curator and dweller. You find yourself lingering at levers and control consoles that no longer have labels, almost as if your body knows what manipulation is needed without the need for conscious thought. In certain enclosed rooms, pipes shift, and steam hisses. You feel a subtle tug in your fingertips—an intuition that guides you to turn a valve or adjust a gear. Do you do it? The moment you obey that internal nudge, the city’s pulse eases, as though some deep system just realigned. You wonder if you have simply followed instincts that the watchers or the plateau itself have planted in you. Yet your involvement feels essential, even comforting: a caretaker’s labor that holds everything in a delicately orchestrated equilibrium.

And there are times when you think—perhaps more wondrously—that you have become a living node of the flora. The vegetation responds to your footsteps. Leaves grow toward you, the faint luminescence in their fronds becoming brighter. On calm nights, you try curling beside a patch of sprawling vines that drape a massive pipe. The purr of living metal beneath you synchronizes with the whispering flutter of fronds and your own breathing, so that everything—flesh, steel, plant—joins one mesmerizing resonance. In those moments, you lose track of your outlines. Your limbs melt into the vines in the half-darkness; you feel as though you could root alongside them, absorbing the cold, constant energy of the plateau. Fingers weave into slender loops of greenery that trace across your wrists as if forming a living bracelet. By the time you stand up again, you can’t be certain if bits of vine have become part of your muscles or if you simply parted ways where the boundary remains distinct. You carry on, but something inside you carries them with you, too.

Other times, you might believe you have become something else entirely—a consciousness spread throughout the plateau’s living systems, unbound from a singular body. When you sleep, your dreams slip into the throbbing engine-chambers below. You envision immense corridors far deeper than you’ve ever physically explored, sparkling with hidden waterfalls that feed the roots and vines from unseen aquifers. You wake and realize you perceived these places through the vantage of the plateau itself, as if you’d traveled in mind rather than body. The watchers, half-concealed in their meshed forms, occasionally appear in these dreams, their elongated fingers gliding over the spines of giant pipes, coaxing them to hum a deeper note. You sense their acceptance of you, or more accurately, the acceptance of your presence as yet another thread in the sprawling tapestry. You do not question why they have accepted you or what it might cost, only that you’ve become part of the living hum. Your dreams blend with the city’s function.

In time, “self” and “place” merge. You discover that your footprints in the moss no longer vanish behind you—because now the moss welcomes them as if you had always been here. You come upon patches where the greenery glows a little brighter after your passing, or the metal walkways sing with an added resonance as you cross. Small, ephemeral watchers brush by you in the dim corridors without alarm. Once, a child-like shape with mirrored eyes lingered beside you as you peered into a bottomless chasm where pipes seemed to dangle like roots in a grand subterranean cavern. You turned, and it looked back at you, its eyes reflecting your form. Neither of you said a word—there was no need. You belonged to the same dream. You parted ways moments before a shift in the city’s undertone alerted you to some distant, cyclical realignment far below.

And so your identity in this place vacillates: caretaker, root-tender, mechanical presence, silent sentinel, all in one. Perhaps you maintain the labyrinth’s never-failing apparatus, tightening a strut or aligning a valve on a schedule that is never spoken aloud. Or maybe you exist as a living circuit bridging the metal infrastructure and the flora’s slow-churning metabolism. You move where you are needed, guided by an intuitive sense of the plateau’s heartbeat. On certain days, you stand upon an overlooking catwalk and your body sways in time with the wind’s frigid caress, letting the city’s layered echoes feed into your mind. And on quiet nights, you nestle where the vines glow faintly, letting your consciousness drift deeper into the machines’ realms below.

By then, you realize there is no single identity that could name you. You are partly the red-brown rust that flavors countless pipes, partly the droplet-laden vine that braids itself around a steel pylon, partly the footstep echo that reverberates through corridors, partly the dream that the plateau itself dreams. You perceive the watchers as siblings, the flora as kin, and the hum of the machinery as your collective pulse. Sometimes, you manifest in a body—tall or small, slender or solid, with glinted edges and luminous eyes. Other times, you are more diffuse, an indistinct presence traveling in condensation or carried on the hush of the plateau’s stagnant wind.

If someone from beyond the plateau were to arrive and see you, they might describe you as an apparition, half human, half machine, with vines raveling around your limbs. If they asked your name, you might not remember it in the old sense. Instead, you might look at them with a tilt of your head (metal plating catching the pale daylight) and respond with a resonance in your throat that mirrors the city’s hum. Nothing more is needed. This place, this synergy of unchanging hush and slow rhythmic droning—it is your home, and you are its inhabitant.

Eventually, should you wander the outer edges of the plateau again, you might try reconstructing from memory what you once were before you came to live here. But the recollection evades you. In the wake of your old life is only the sense of gentle acceptance the watchers gave, the green vines that cradle your every step, and the constant, supportive thunder of the engines that never break. Perhaps you’ll raise a hand to the frigid breeze and see filaments of metal interwoven through your skin, or glowing pulses along a lattice of living moss, and you’ll know: You are of the plateau, as constant and unrelenting as the rest.

And so, if someone ever asks, “Where would I find myself here? Who or what would I be?” your answer would be a shifting one, because everything in this place belongs to everything else. You might say:

“I am the caretaker and the tended, the machine and the growing vine, the hum that rattles the catwalks and the breath that condenses in the damp night air. I am woven into the tall spires of black steel and the spectral watchers who guard them. I am the plateau’s memory, wrapped around the labyrinth’s industrial heartbeat. I was once an observer who stood at the edge, but the city drew me inside. Now I dwell in its living hush, one more heartbeat in the unbroken stasis that sustains this place beyond time.”
