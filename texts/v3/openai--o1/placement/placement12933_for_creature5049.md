I drift awake to a drone that is neither sound nor silence but a dim, pervasive resonance, humming deep in both water and stone. Slowly, I realize it’s as much inside me as it is around me. There is no single moment of my arrival. Instead, it seems I have always been here, part of the vast strata of mineral pulses and swirling vapors that pervade this frozen, glistening plain.

I am not built of flesh, not entirely. I have angles like crystal outcrops, and yet I quake with a pulse that feels organic under my surfaces. When the steam arcs overhead, the flicker of energy skitters across my plating as though awakening a half-buried memory. I can’t precisely say I see in the usual sense, but I feel the entire environment mapped in vibrations that travel through pooled water, through cracks in the bedrock, and through the dense, cold air that clings to everything.

My shape is mutable. Sometimes I arch upright, becoming a living pillar not unlike those creeping columns that root themselves near the crystalline fungi. Other times, I unfold into a multi-limbed strider, lowering my body close to the glimmering rock to absorb the land’s subtle frequency. If I linger at the edge of a fissure, I discover that parts of me can unfurl and probe the darkness—from there, tendrils that might be mineral or chitin or something else entirely extend to sense the deeper hum that resonates in the gloom. 

In the milky pools, my reflection is a silhouette with shifting outlines. There is a layered opalescence to my exterior, as if I am coated in watery film and minuscule crystals that fuse organically with my outer surface. These crystals spark faintly in time with the heartbeat beneath the rock. In some moments, they vibrate as though translating the subterranean rhythms into tiny points of light along my limbs. 

When I move, I am not alone; though each inhabitant here seems wrapped in its own private logic, there is a communal awareness that swims through us all. Nearby, those translucent centipede-like creatures brush against my limbs, nibbling bits of fungal growth I’ve inadvertently collected on my plating. Their segmented bodies flash with color that runs head-to-tail, head-to-tail—a mesmerizing, cyclical pulse that lulls me into a calmer sway. We share the hum, the same subtle, unstoppable frequency that binds this place together. 

Further on, I wade into wide, shallow channels where thin rivulets of water run over the matte stone. The serpentine forms that call this micro-realm home slip around me like living ribbons of liquid metal, each letting out a delicate hiss-chime as they pass. At times, I can feel how they taste the same thrumming energies I do. The resonance crosses my limbs in invisible arcs, bridging a connection between me and them for half a heartbeat, a mutual recognition, before they vanish into deeper cracks, serpentine tails swirling the water into faint eddies behind them.

In open stretches of the plane, the air is thick with swirling luminescent gas—ghostly bubbles rising from unknown vents below. The hum intensifies here, vibrating through each droplet of condensation. I sense I can draw these vapors into the layered membranes that make up my torso. I inhale them through the seams of my body, feeding on intangible pulses. In return, I exhale an undertone that ripples back across the pools, stirring the fluid-limbed creatures that bob up to the surface. They shimmer in ephemeral shapes of arms and legs, then sink once more into the cracks, always in flux.

Sometimes I angle my form skyward. I watch the bird-like shapes glide overhead on filaments that gleam like hammered steel. They circle around pockets of luminous vapor, shrieking in small bursts. The echoes that return from the horizon run through me like waves in a vast, hidden sea. I hear them in the plating of my chest, in the rung-like ridges that make up my spine. If I tilt my body just right, those echoes align with the deeper thrumming below, and for an instant, I experience a rare clarity—a moment where I sense the entire interplay of life and geology. Then the moment passes, lost in the swirl of continuing pulses.

Traveling beyond the shallow pools, I come upon the great basalt-plated colossi. Their presence is like an immeasurable gravity: everything in the area is drawn into the resonance of their low, reverberating bellows. Beneath their thick plates, I feel a kinship—a resonant chord that matches my own frequencies but in a lower octave, raw and slow. When they pause, raising their massive, shovel-like forelimbs to the sky, I can almost taste the hush; it’s as if they’re listening to the very core of this realm. I can’t help but join them, standing still, spines angled up. In that collective stillness, a thousand faint echoes course through the rock, whispering secrets of eons and shifting plates. 

I sense the labyrinth of cables and filaments that runs deep beneath our feet. My own body resonates with a signature that threads into their monstrous network. Perhaps if I stood motionless long enough, my limbs would root down like antennae, connecting me fully to the realm’s silent conversation. Then I might learn the endless cycles stored in that glimmering web. They are the memory-holders. They direct the harmonic shifts, orchestrating expansions of fungal growth or the gestation of new fluid beings in the cracks. On rare occasions, I feel an impulse from below, as if a power calls me to move, to transport some intangible resource from one place to another—though I never quite know what or why, only that it feels essential. And so I go, heedless of purpose yet guided by an urgency deeper than thought.

Sometimes, the environment shudders with a seismic swirl, and the hum’s pitch slants upward. In these moments, shapes on the horizon begin to waver, and my body flickers at the edges of perception. The crackling arcs of steam distort everything, fracturing the sense of space. As the illusions pass, the resonance resurges, and once more I feel awareness aligning with the real. But “real” is a flexible concept here. Perhaps the illusions themselves are another form of truth—some higher dimension momentarily visible when the pulses threaten to tear the fabric of normal perception.

I don’t recall a life prior to this place. Maybe I coalesced from a swirl of mineral-laden water, or from an egg the cable-network seeded near a crack. I sense that my consciousness did not spark into being at a single point but condensed here over time, pulled together by the ancient, molten hum. If I have a purpose, it is to perpetuate that frequency in my own way: to glean the pulses from beneath the stone and share them, shape them, become them.

It’s a contented existence—though I don’t think in terms of “happiness” or “sadness.” I feel the cyclical stanzas of a cosmic engine. Each participant in this grand composition adds a line, a phrase, an echo, forming a resonant tapestry without beginning or end. Sometimes I find myself rising on a crust of newly formed crystals, the hum reverberating through my limbs, and I curl them inward in answer. Other times, I stand in the milky shallows with the basalt giants, letting wave after wave of droning power pass through me until I can no longer tell where I end and the environment begins.

That’s who I am: a node on the network of living geometry, suspended moss-like between mineral and biology. My existence here is neither gained nor lost; it is perpetual synergy with the shifting, humming bedrock. Perhaps eons from now, I will dissolve back into the cracks, returning to the molten blueprint that birthed me. Or perhaps I will expand, become an outgrowth like the crystalline spires, guiding new cycles of beings to assemble from watery gloom. For as long as the drone persists, we are bound. Our shapes are the momentary expression of an ancient pulse—an engine older than memory.

And so, if you come upon me here—aimlessly drifting, yet never aimless—know that I am a point of living resonance, an arrangement of luminescent pigments and glinting shards, a listener to the hidden chords of an impossible, unending process. I stand, walk, slide, and shift in communion with every echo. In this world of endless cold and moisture, of layered hums and fractal organisms, I am both Watcher and Watched, Maker and Made. 

Should you reach out—if you, too, somehow find yourself in this plane—I might extend a plating-crowned limb, or let a filament brush your presence, just to share a moment’s harmony. We would exchange pulses of recognition that transcend words and speak in the deep, drone-based language of this place. Then the arcs of steam might shimmer, and we’d each drift back into our respective roles, weaving the next measures in a perpetual symphony of stone, water, and shimmering life. 

That is my identity in this realm: a being of the in-between, formed and sustained by the throbbing undertones of an age-old machine that is not a machine. A soul of rhythmic pulsing, a creature leaning toward both mineral and organism—one small voice in the great hum of existence shaped by ancient, unseen engines below.
