I notice it first as a resonance underfoot, at once deep and quivering with life. The stone receives my weight without protest; it’s been shaped by so many footsteps—though perhaps not human ones—that I feel momentarily welcomed, as if stepping onto a familiar threshold. And yet, I cannot tell if this threshold is meant for me.

When I try to focus on my own shape, it remains elusive. At times, I sense my weight anchored in four limbs, a thick breath stirring in my chest. At others, I feel the dryness of wing membranes taut along my flanks, or the soft scuttle of many legs. My form has not settled. All I know is that I inhabit some corner of this grooved plateau, perched in a cloud-wreathed hush, surrounded by the clicking of shell and the subdued shimmer of damp rock. I sense my own presence in the hush of dripping water, in the breath of wind that sidles through narrow gaps in these towering, time-worn pillars.

Perhaps I am small, no bigger than one of the quick-darting insects that vanish beneath an overhang when a footfall vibrates the gravel. If I focus on that perspective, I feel the stone’s roughness, each grain towering like its own cliff. My body might be translucent, catching any stray gleam and turning it into a prism of faint color. I scuttle over the damp surface in a nervous flutter, seeking darkness because light is a rarity—startling when it breaks through. My shell, delicate and multi-segmented, allows me to cling upside-down to a ledge without slipping. The plateau’s gloom feels like a protective cloak, letting me hide from the echoing thunder of giant wings overhead.

Then, other times, I sense the pressure of warm, moss-scented air in my broad nostrils as I plod along an impossible ledge. My ribs expand with each breath, steady and methodical, the stone beneath my feet worn smooth by generations of ancestors just like me. My toes flex confidently against the curves of rock, unafraid of plunges that drop hundreds of feet, for I have known these paths since the day I first stumbled to my feet. Thick, ridged hide blends into the stone’s color, an earthy tapestry of greys and browns, so that I become nearly invisible when I stand still. My call—a low, resonant rumble—echoes through corridor after corridor, letting others know I am here, and I belong.

But then again, a sudden draft wraps around my limbs, and I feel the urge to ascend, to push off with powerful muscles and cast myself into the open air. My wings—membranes stretched taut from elongated fingers—catch the swirling currents. The plateau spreads out below, an intricate labyrinth of pillars, overhangs, and damp runnels glinting with the moisture that collects in every pocket. From above, I sense an entire hidden world in each fracture of rock, each dim recess harboring some new shape. The stony labyrinth resonates with the hum of countless unseen inhabitants. And I, airborne, drift at the edges of a passing cloud, droplets collecting on my leathery wings, the wind whistling around the ridges of my chest. The horizon is a haze, but I know these heights intimately. I circle a tall spire where the sun sometimes breaks through like a golden spear. Below, smaller shapes scuttle, hop, or lounge in the puddles they call home.

Or there are moments—between steps, between gulps of this humid air—when I wonder if I am not a creature at all but rather the plateau itself, a slumbering spine of stone that has endured centuries of storm and sun. I feel the cracks that form, millimeter by millimeter, in my own surface. My ridges stand as silent witnesses to ages of life anchoring itself onto me, scuttling and rooting, leaving faint etchings. Time flows so slowly that the difference between one day and the next is imperceptible. In this immense stillness, I sense the weight of water collecting in shallow basins, seeping into me, wearing deeper channels that the insects have transformed into thoroughfares. I harbor the breathing hush of amphibians in my hollows, warming them in pockets where the plateau’s own subtle heat seeps through. My entire existence resonates with a deep, unhurried pulse.

Now and then, a splash of memory flickers: odd runes carved by slender hands, scrawled under a rock ledge. I might be the being who etched them, a wandering mind that tried to capture something about these winged creatures, these luminous amphibians, the hush of water. Did I rest here for a night or a season? Did I attempt to map the gentle weaving of life in these heights, only to depart and leave behind cryptic shapes? The memory fades quickly, as if it were not truly my own. Yet it lingers in the mossy corners, reminding me that I—or someone like me—might have recognized the plateau as a sanctuary, even if we could never remain.

As I drift through these shifting identities, the plateau itself remains constant in its humid hush and resonant calls. My vantage changes—sometimes I am crossing a slick path on webbed toes, flattening to the rock in hopes of blending in; sometimes I sway on countless jointed legs, antennae testing the next footstep for safety. Still other times, I coil my powerful wings around me, finding a roost on a jagged spire, lulled by the hum-rattle of beetle-like carapaces below. The edges of my vision fill with scuttling shapes and half-seen silhouettes perched on precarious shelves. Outside the ledge’s lip, swirling clouds swallow even the nearest horizon. Sky merges with rock, moisture merges with stone. Everything is an overlapping tapestry.

Enduring music surrounds it all: dripping water, the click-click of arthropod limbs against gravel, the occasional thunderous bellow or piercing call that travels from one rocky column to the next. At times it forms a chaotic rhapsody, at times a gentle lullaby. I sense that the plateau breathes in these rhythms, that each living thing here contributes a note or a chord. Even in the hush, the feeling of life persists, like a subterranean drum.

And so, where do I find myself? In truth, I find myself in all these forms. The plateau does not demand a single identity. One moment, I am a child of the wind, coasting on leathery wings above the labyrinth. The next, I crouch among glistening amphibians, breathing steam in shallow pools. Then I take on the heaviness of a broad-chested grazer, my call echoing from the rocky walls as I forage on wiry shrubs. And in another breath, I feel the silent immensity of the rock itself beneath all those footsteps.

If I must choose a final shape—or at least a shape for this moment—then let me be the flicker of awareness that holds all these threads together: the watchful presence that kneels to study runic glyphs half-buried in moss, that hears the chitinous swarms in the gloom, that catches the thermal pockets of rising air. Let me wander the plateau with slow wonder, belonging to none of these creatures yet feeling kinship with them all. I am a traveler through an ancient cathedral of twilight, a spirit that glimpses the age-old harmony of wing, shell, fur, stone, and water.

Perhaps, as night begins to creep in—not much darker than the plateau’s perpetual dim—I will find a slender ledge to rest upon. My limbs, whatever form they currently hold, will not object; they will fold or curl or sprawl against the sculpted rock. The hush will settle deeper. The swirling shapes in my mind will subside. In that pause, I will exhale in tandem with the plateau’s slow breath. And in that moment of union, I know that my being lies both within and beyond any single form: part ephemeral wanderer, part chiseled stone, part hush of wings. I am simply here in the gloom and the damp and the warmth, witnessing the small miracles unfolding in every crevice. It is enough to be the presence that sees and feels.

In this world, I am the one who is both many and none—a guest among an uncountable procession of life. I share my breath with web-footed amphibians, see my reflection in the shining carapaces of insects, and drape my wings upon the wind. I may never claim rightful ownership of this plateau, nor will I stake its ridges or carve my name in stone. Instead, I settle into the enduring pulse that has guided so many generations of creatures before me and will outlast countless after. In that pulse, I find my truest identity: a shifting, humble note within the plateau’s timeless chorus.
