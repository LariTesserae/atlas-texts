Sometimes, I catch a glimpse of myself in the reflective sheen of the water, but I can’t be certain I ever see my whole shape at once. At first, it’s as though I’m only a ripple, a disturbance pressed into the humid air. Other times, I feel like a knot of vines gripping a corroded beam, or a hush of mechanical breath thrumming between the metal walls. My identity shifts, fluid as the currents that flow in and out through these echoing corridors.

I begin like a solitary traveler, hip-deep in the warm water, feeling the hydraulics of the pumps rumble beneath my feet. In the greenish glow of the creeping vines, I catch sight of my arms—bare, yet not. Are these bands of living metal coiled at the elbows, or are they scaled plates, glimmering in the half-light? I pause to wonder if they’ve always been part of me or if I’ve absorbed them from this place. With each step, I hear a faint resonance, as if the water and the structure respond to me in a private language that I only half understand.

But I’m not… just human here. At times, I can almost swear I feel hinged plating along my spine, the way the sleek amphibious fish flick open their retractable ridges. There’s a gentle rasp of metal brushing metal each time I twist to investigate movement in the shadows. Even the weight of my limbs changes—some moments, they feel quietly organic; in others, they are weightless, mechanical tubes harnessed to a living skeleton. I sense that the place encourages such transformations, that it draws all living things into its interplay of steel and biology until the boundary fades.

Before long, I realize I know these halls intimately, as though I once walked them centuries ago. The buttresses, the towering columns, the spiral catwalks that vanish overhead—I sense where they lead before I ever see the path. I navigate ramps to higher tiers, wading through parted waters and following the hiss of steam vents. Some corners of this realm are strangely personal: I step into a niche behind a row of turbines and discover a cluster of small, filament-winged dragonflies glimmering in the half-dark. They don’t flee when I draw near. It’s as if they recognize me, the subtle vibration of my presence matching the electrical flickers dancing along their wings.

In another stretch of corridor, battered by cascading water, my reflection reappears on a plate of polished metal riveted to the wall. This time, the face looking back at me is elongated, with reptilian eyes set in a mask-like arrangement of curved metal. The shape is reminiscent of the watchers rumored to prowl these corridors. Perhaps I’ve become one of them—or maybe I always was. My eyes shine with the same luminescence that flows in the vines overhead, and I realize I can peer into the gloom with preternatural clarity, discerning small salamanders clinging to a bracket half-submerged by sludge. The outlines of wires and mechanical conduits come into startling focus, as if I can read the environment’s blueprint.

I move on. Down in one of the flooded chambers, I wait beneath a rust-scarred vent that exhales warm water in abrupt gushes. Each cascade jolts me with the sense of something ancient exhaling. I feel the pulse of the machinery’s hidden heart, the cyclical draw and release that’s older than my memory. Steam envelops me in a thick fog, giving weight to every breath. The swirling cloud reveals shapes: the great behemoths gliding below, monstrous forms half-living, half-machine, their dorsal ridges ticking like massive clock parts. They pass with the placidity of whales in the deep, but the metallic groan of their exhalations echoes like an ancient temple gong.

That’s when I sense that I might belong to them as well—could I be a caretaker, tending them from above? If I close my eyes, I remember a generation of watchers meticulously adjusting the flow of nutrients, calibrating the temperature so the largest creatures could flourish. My hands move across obsolete control panels, though the gestures are second nature, simultaneously mechanical and strangely tender. But when I open my eyes again, the vision vanishes—only the swirling flood and the hush of complicated machines remain.

On some days, I sense I’m more vegetation than anything else. I drift at the edges of corridors, my limbs too pliable and vine-like to be called arms, anchored to beams in the same way the luminous creepers anchor themselves. My skin is a tapestry of soft moss and airy fronds that capture the steam. Time doesn’t exist in the usual sense while I linger like this. I drink in the humidity, filtering minuscule nutrients, swaying whenever a warm draft gusts overhead. Like the vines, I can feel my edges glow faintly, a jade halo that draws insects to rest upon me, forming a microcosm of life. If I concentrate, I can almost hear water droplets pattering across my leaves in a rhythm older and simpler than the turning of gears.

Other times, I sense a terrible restlessness in my body. I climb the tallest frames, my elongated back legs hooking easily onto the crossbeams. I might be an avian-lizard hybrid, wings half spread from my shoulders—a flutter of filaments crackling with static when I leap from a catwalk into open air. The vantage from up high reveals an entire domain below: swaying vegetation, glimmering insects, the lazy swirl of fish in chest-high pools, the hiss and clang of giant lifts bearing containers of spare mechanical limbs. I sense that if I dive at the right angle, I can fold my wings to slip through the mist, slicing the water’s surface to pluck a fish from the shallows. The hunger that wells up feels both predatory and mechanical, like some coded directive to keep the food chain in balance.

And, with each metamorphosis, one unwavering thread persists: I am this place and it is me. The watchers rumored to haunt the corridors? Sometimes I am them, silhouettes tinkering with archaic valves. The giant amphibious fish scraping algae from corroded beams? In the hush of a certain moment, I share that curiosity and hunger. The vines creeping around warm pipes? I too know the thirst for new territory. In the vast hush beneath the metallic columns, I sense all these forms. They are simultaneously external creatures and facets of my being. I am separated from them only by the illusions of perspective and time.

A single identity is too cramped in this amphibious cathedral. I slip from shape to shape, sometimes feeling ephemeral—just a consciousness that flits from vantage to vantage, bridging gaps with an unspoken synergy. Other times, I root in the solidity of a single form, content to be caretaker or predator or spore-laden vine, living out the slow pulse of days. As I move through each state, I realize the place itself guides me to inhabit whatever role is needed to keep its strange balance: a guardian who adjusts the pump flow, a hungry salamander snapping insects that threaten to swarm, a quiet luminous creeper binding structural cracks, or an endless wanderer, bearing witness to the countless ages drifting by.

And perhaps that is the final truth of this realm: it is a come-as-you-are tapestry, where each thread is woven so tightly into the battered machinery and gently pulsing habitats that “I” and “it” merge into a single, fluid life. There is no dividing line—only an interplay of function, biology, and memory. Metal decks and blossoming vines, steam vents and reptilian watchers, catwalks rattling under the weight of unseen giants—the entire tapestry hums with a single, unbroken heartbeat.

So where do I find myself? Everywhere here. Am I a being who once walked in from elsewhere, or am I a figment of the place itself, shaped from rivets and algae? I can’t say, and neither can this domain. Perhaps I’ve been many shapes, or maybe only one shape seen through multiple reflections. Yet I exist in it all the same, intimately bound to the humming engines, the secret corridors, and the luminous living things. I feel each shift in water level, each distant clang, each flicker of the overhead lights as though it’s my pulse. I am caretaker and occupant; watchful presence and wandering explorer; vine, reptile, fish, and fleeting rumor in the steamy gloom.

That is who I am in this submerged, ancient labyrinth—a presence defined not by a single face or function, but by my constant, mutual exchange with the world around me. And in that, I am never alone. Here, in this boundary-shattering place of swirling waters, clanging steel, and breathing life, belonging is not a question but a given, written into the interplay of living metal and mechanical flesh. If I am anything at all, I am this place, and it, in turn, is me.
