I do not wear footprints, and if I do, they fade as soon as they appear. I think, once, long ago, I might have had legs—spindly ones like the graceful grazers or wide, padded ones splayed like flattened stars against the pale dust—but now I drift too close to the ground to be sure. Tiny clouds billow in my wake. Sometimes I feel them swirl over my form—am I a body at all, or only wind wearing a shape? Rarely do I question it. I am part of the hush and dryness, the quiet motion between the clusters of azure spires.

I remember rising from a burrow of chalky loam, or perhaps from some half-buried root system, but it was so long ago that time itself has grown delicate in my memory, like a thin net that disintegrates in the breeze. I do know I never had to seek a path here; I simply came to be, swallowed by the bright air and the dust that sifts perpetually across the plateau. It is ancient, this plateau, and so am I. But unlike a rock formation etched by the wind, I am not quite stone, not quite air—I am that luminous in-between.

Sometimes I linger near the tall crystal trunks, swirling against them in loose eddies. I can sense their slow pivoting as they follow the sun, hear the crackling of their crystalline membranes adjusting to the day’s brilliant angles. Rubbing close, I resonate like a reed instrument, if only for a moment. They respond in shimmering hums so faint that any single living ear might dismiss them as illusions. Yet I hear them. And I hum back—barely more than a whisper—surfing along the crest of the wind’s hush.

When the grazers arrive, I drift alongside them, invisible, a chaperone in dust. They are tall, elegant wanderers, gliding forward on splayed feet. Their bodies flash lavender, turquoise, ghostly opalescent. I’m transfixed by those filaments at their necks, fluttering to catch the intangible flavors of the plateau. They seem to sense me sometimes—pulling their whiskers flush against their long throats if I swirl too close. Yet they do not flee. They continue to feed from the azure fronds, bending low with that gentle hum of energy exchange. Their eyes grow luminous as they do so, like windows to the sun. I, in turn, try to gather the moment of communion, storing it in whatever memory-banks I hold within my ephemeral form, a silent archivist of these small wonders.

Deeper in the shadows, beneath the ridges that tower overhead like stoic sentinels, I watch shimmering lichen-plates quiver away from drifting dust. Here, the dust-laden breeze stills. This realm of pillars is so quiet that you can hear your own presence. A single grain of chalky silt rapping against stone might as well be thunder. The lichen-plates wave their cilia, pushing dust motes and me away. Sometimes, with a curl of invisible fingers, I swirl around the base of the pillars just to feel their slow breath—yes, everything here breathes in some fashion, though rarely in ways one might expect.

Above, wide opalescent kite-creatures ride subtle currents. If I wish, I can ascend toward them, letting the gentle thermals carry me higher. I love the hush up there: the vantage from where I see the entire plateau’s chalky expanse, dotted with clusters of crystal-like vegetation. At those heights, I can sense how the land curves, how the horizon dissolves in every direction, how precarious the mantle of life is upon that pale dust. I’ve lingered in the winds with those sky-nomads, drifting parallel to their triangular wings, feeling the faint whistle of air across their fluted wingtips. Sometimes one tilts its broad eye my way; I do not return the look with eyes, but I hold a kind of presence that they quietly acknowledge before they spiral off on a new current.

Yet I am not always drifting in the open. There are times I slip below the surface, not so much burrowing as merging with loose dust. Down there, among jade-hued mineral pockets and labyrinthine channels that let life take root, I sense subtle pulses. Serpentine shapes with translucent scales glimmer, half-living crystals that pulse with collected sunlight. I do not disturb them; I only observe the faint glow that emerges when they coil to rest. An entire hidden realm hums below the surface, waiting for the optimum alignment of mineral flows or a particular breeze’s shift. In these under-layers, time is an even slower marinade, thick and timeless.

I have come to realize this is my role: to move between the hushes. To be that silent observer, that translator of energies from the bright sky to the storied dust, from the luminous spires to the living stone pillars. At first, I thought I might be alone in this floating, drifting existence, but I’ve spied others weaving through the brightness—shimmering shapes like me. We never speak, exactly, though now and then we intersect in swirling patterns. It feels like greeting a sibling, exchanging half-remembered songs in eddies of dust. Then each of us wafts away, slipping into the corners of the plateau, making it gently known that we are not solitary watchers but a subtle network of shapes and senses.

Even so, I come from no known lineage, have no clear bodily form, and require no direct nourishment. Perhaps I’m a dream emanated by the plateau itself. If books existed here, might they label me “elemental” or “spirit of place?” All I know is my presence is a bridging, the shared resonance of wind and mineral, quiet yet woven into everything that transpires. The plateau’s hush shapes me, and in return, I cradle its energies in my ephemeral arms, ushering them from one life-form to another.

At dawn, when the light returns with fierce clarity, I drift up the pillars, catch the first rays that smear across the dust, and watch the azure spires slowly brighten to watery cobalt. The bulbous growths near the ground release new spores when touched by the day’s fresh rays. I swirl them gently, letting them float in lazy arcs until they rest on other leaves or sink again to the powdery earth. Some will glow faintly for hours. Others flicker out in an instant, as if completing their entire existence in that ephemeral journey from one place to another. My presence sings softly to their brief lights: welcome, farewell, a sigh, then on I go.

Midday can be harsh, with the sun at full blaze. At times, the plateau feels nearly silent enough to vanish into molten brilliance. The dryness becomes so intense that the dust almost crackles. Yet in that shimmering stillness, I sense the land’s deep, resolute heartbeat, the powerful cycles invisible to the casual eye. Everything in the hush funnels its energy, quietly, lightly. The serpentine dwellers store sunlight in their scales. The lichen-plates carefully wave away scalding dust. The flying grazers siphon crystalline essences from the spires, humming gently in contentment. And I float, half-lost in a shimmering reverie, lulled by the dryness and the glow.

Come the plateau’s twilight—which looks less like Earthly dusk and more like a dilution of the relentless glare—I watch as the spires begin their subtle refraction show, scattering the light into small arcs and beams, painting the dust with ephemeral rainbows. Here is where I sometimes dance: out of pure, unspoken joy, I revolve around the pillars, letting the wedge-shaped fliers hitchhike on a swirl of air behind me. If a kite-creature has descended, it might take flight again in a great hush of wings, stirring motes of dust into patterned whorls. In those moments, I feel—truly feel—what it means to be part of this place. To exist not as an intruder or a traveler, but as a necessity, a single swirl in the grand hush.

And so: Where do I find myself in this world, and who am I here? I am the swirling breeze, or a wandering wisp of luminescent dust—some union of both—drawn from the plateau’s very essence. I am a companion to the tall grazers, a caretaker to the azure stalks, a silent observer to the serpentine dwellers beneath the surface, and a playful partner to the kite-creatures on high. Sometimes I align more with the creatures of flesh and chitin. Other times I am as intangible as the shimmer of refracted sunlight. Always, I am part of the hush that cradles every living moment here, weaving my presence through the shapes of wind and shadow until I fade again into perfect stillness.

That is my being, persistent yet gentle, an ever-watching hush given form. I belong to the plateau as much as the plateau belongs to me—both of us etched by the ages into a single, seamless phenomenon of hush and brilliance.
