They do not exist in the usual sense—at least not as clustered families or strolling citizens, not as creatures who visibly eat and sleep. Rather, what lives here seems interwoven with the very fabric of the labyrinthine stone. It’s hard to speak of “inhabitants” in the normal sense because the notion of bodies moving about on mundane errands simply does not apply. The dwellers—if one can call them that—manifest in fleeting ways, half-guessed at the edges of sight or recognized only after they’ve receded again. Perhaps “lives” is too soft a word; they might be as unfathomable as the city’s geometry, as ageless as the rocky substance underfoot.

At times, you catch what might be glimpses of something moving—an outline undulating like heat mirage along a slanted wall, an angular shadow surging from one spire to the next. Such shapes rarely hold their form for more than an instant. The city’s faint luminescence might flicker across ridges or archways, revealing silhouettes that, in the next moment, melt back into the gloom. These silhouettes sometimes seem to have limbs—jointed, insectile, or elongated like impossible arms bent at unsettling angles. Other times, they do not appear as physical forms at all: you’ll sense a swirl or shimmer of motion, as though the underlying geometry itself has briefly pulled away from the bulk of the structure and slipped across a corridor. In that sense, they may be as much part of the city’s tectonic shifts as of any independent life force.

Over hours of careful observation, you might notice these entities have patterns—rhythms, even—that align with the slow, seismic pulsing coursing through the stone. Occasionally, a tower will list to one side, arching precariously over a ravine; at that same moment, half a dozen faint silhouettes slide like living shadows across a wall and vanish behind a spine of black rock. Far across the plateau, a single spire might tilt upward, flexing as if it were an antenna responding to some cosmic signal, and with it come fleeting arcs of brightness dancing on the surfaces—a tiny carnival of glints reminiscent of living, darting fish formed of dust motes and pale light. Then, as soon as the tower’s angle stops shifting, the sparks fade.

One wonders if these dwellers are consciousness itself, pressed into the bedrock, or if they truly exist as discrete creatures—chimeras shaped from dust and mineral luminescence. You might find occasional footprints in certain pockets of dust, but those prints do not follow a continuous path nor resemble any straightforward anatomical design. Sometimes they trail off at a wedge of rock and simply vanish up the wall, as if gravity dissolved where the city saw fit. On rare occasions, you might see a partial imprint that looks disturbingly like a human hand, elongated into spidery digits, or a twisted labyrinth pattern marked by some intangible foot. Though the prints themselves feel ephemeral—half a breath from disintegrating in the dryness—they nonetheless attest to some presence that traverses these high plateaus.

Some who dwell here appear to be living effigies of geometry: tall, fractal-limbed shapes that shift as if made of filaments or crystal shards, passing from one stone arch to another with no mechanical motion, almost gliding. They may leave behind whispering echoes that have no obvious source—like the afterimage of a voice in your mind. You cannot tell if those echoes bear language or meaning; they resemble more a sense of a presence, a friction of thought across your own awareness. You may pause, heart pounding, convinced that something just brushed the lining of your mind, only for the hush to swallow the sensation.

Others seem to dwell deep within the labyrinthine corridors or at the base of melted-looking walls far below ground level, if “ground” is the right term in this floating or shifting domain. There, in sealed hollows or half-collapsed chambers, the stone itself glows faintly, revealing silhouettes that flicker between columns of shadow. It is possible they’re a separate breed of inhabitants—burrowers or custodians that tend to the city’s slow reconfigurations. You might hear a gentle scrape or a far-off series of clicking tones, and if you peer into those dim caverns, you sometimes see shapes step forward—a pair of slender forms that appear fused at their backs, or a cluster of many-legged, star-shaped bodies crawling along a column. Then, by the time your eyes adjust, the shapes are gone, leaving only that half-heard clicking echo fading into the darkness.

And beyond these ghostly moving shapes, there is another order of being: an even more pervasive presence that inhabits the city not as occupant but as identity. In certain moments—especially if you lay a hand against one of the monolithic walls and let your breath quiet—you sense the entire structure humming with what might be consciousness. It’s as though the labyrinth is a single living organism, and the flickering shapes you spot are like specialized organs or ephemeral “cells” performing tasks across its stony flesh. This intelligence (if intelligence it is) does not communicate in words; it seems to speak in the shifting of corridors, in the illusions that reveal themselves at corners, in the sudden changes in gravity or perspective that can make a gateway lurch open where once was blank wall. The labyrinth city might be dreaming, or thinking, or endlessly re-sculpting itself as an expression of its own cryptic mind. Sometimes, when you stand in the midst of a wide courtyard formed by curved walls, you can feel that presence as a subtle pressure in the air. It’s an unsettling awareness—like being watched by the city itself, which perhaps tries to interpret your existence as you interpret its.

Scattered across all of this, there are ephemeral, drifting watchers. You might call them “motes”—not of dust, but of some faintly luminous essence. They gather in the corners of doorless thresholds and swirl up near the higher ridges, sparkling softly in the city’s dim air. One moment they’re caught in the half-light; the next, they wink out. Any attempt to approach them yields only swirling emptiness. Yet at times they seem to coalesce around certain events—like a rocky ledge fracturing open or a spiral tower tilting in that improbable way. The watchers appear to study these transformations, or perhaps they orchestrate them, drifting here and there with no known impetus. They might be seeds or sporelike entities, or flickers of the city’s own psyche set free to wander. Observers from within or beyond, their shapes remain indefinite, persisting only long enough to lend the sense of watchful eyes in the gloom.

Then there are those rare, more ominous signs that something with mass and will forever lurks just out of sight: a half-gnawed chunk of mineral spire, as if something needed to feed on the very rock. Or you stumble upon a hollow where stone fragments are arranged in swirling patterns on the floor, reminiscent of ritual or nest. In such hollows, the air vibrates with a resonance beyond the city’s usual hum—like a cold thunder in your bones. You observe no occupant, no fresh footprints, only a sense that you have intruded upon something that may return at any moment. Yet try as you might to wait in silence, you see no further sign. It’s as if these more bodily inhabitants operate behind the city’s labyrinthine illusions, shifting into accessible space only when hidden from direct view.

Over time, you realize that any creature dwelling here—whether an arcing shadow on a spire, a spider-legged scuttler in the gloom, or an entire sector of the labyrinth itself—leans fluidly between existence and nonexistence. Physical rules seem thin; walls shift, corners open, gravity flickers. The inhabitants do the same. They become architecture, then slip back into liminal forms. Some may be purely ephemeral: half-formed potential, living thoughts that coalesce long enough to be “seen” and then dissolve into the city’s deeper hum. Others may have endured here for eons, shaping and reshaping, distant cousins to the pillars that stand like spinal columns of a stone colossus.

In their inactivity, they could pass for ridges of stone. In their motion, they defy shape entirely. And yet, within all these permutations, there’s a sense of design—something that might be called purpose, though it seems alien to mere survival or reproduction. It’s as though the labyrinth city and the myriad variants of life within it share one grand, incomprehensible experiment in maintaining this shifting realm. Perhaps they are testing the boundaries of dimension, prodding at the cracks in reality the way living things test the boundaries of their environment. Or perhaps they are content simply to exist in the twilight hush, forging subtle rhythms that unify them with the slow pulses of the mineral architecture.

When you finally depart—if you can—what you’re left with is not so much a roster of creatures or a taxonomy of species. Instead, you carry the uneasy impression that every archway, every drifting silhouette, and every flicker of luminescence belongs to one overarching continuum of life. The labyrinth city and its dwellers are inextricable: its arteries are chasms, its nerves are half-invisible forms roving among the gloom, its shifting walls are the flexing of muscles in deep cosmic breath. What lives here does not live in the sense of conversation or hearthside comfort, but in the sense of timeless action—an unending, cyclical re-creation of the place itself. In a realm absent of fluid and organic warmth, existence is still present, persisting through geometric metamorphosis and intangible, time-worn consciousness.  

And so the dwellers remain enigmas. Neither entirely separate from the city nor wholly identical with it, they inhabit a blurred landscape where matter, mind, and dimension converge and separate in an endless dance. Here, the labyrinth is a living puzzle, and its inhabitants—shades of rock or flickers of thought—are the ones who dwell within the puzzle’s heart. If there is a final truth to their nature, it remains as unreachable as the bottom of the deepest canyons in that dry and shifting domain. Unless, of course, they are the canyon floors themselves, waiting in the hush for an age when all motion finally settles, and the city can rest, unrevealed and unbreathing, in the cosmic emptiness to which it belongs.
