They are not creatures as we would recognize them, for they wear no soft flesh, possess no beating hearts, and speak with no voices. And yet, in the stillness of that plateau, they thrive in a way that only this strange realm could allow. These “inhabitants” meld seamlessly with their environment. They appear as mineral columns rising from the brittle soil, metallic fronds curling and re-curling at their crowns. They do not shuffle or step, but grow quietly as if time were a slow river constantly nourishing them.

Look closely at one—and it’s difficult to do so without feeling a hush in your own breath—and you may see slight, methodical expansions along each finlike “leaf.” Fine lines of shimmering chemical dust accumulate at the edges, absorbed by the structure in filigreed patterns that could be taken for veins. This dust fosters minuscule changes, so punctual in their quiet progress that you can trace the growth ring by ring if you stare long enough. In that sense, they are something akin to plants—yet they have no bark or chlorophyll, no sap or moisture. They appear to be woven of raw, metallic threads, stiff yet bearing a delicate pliancy that sways minutely in the faint waves of heat.

Many of them seem solitary at first glance, each a pillar of its own constitution, but there is a subtle communal bond. If you kneel between two clusters and press your ear close to the ground, you might hear a shared vibration passing back and forth between nearby trunks, carrying silent pulses that ripple through the rootlike substructures. Though they bear no eyes or ears, it seems they communicate in subterranean tremors. Perhaps these vibrations are like primal messages: calls of growth, chemical exchanges that say “I am here—grow beside me.”

If you wander farther, you find clusters arranged in irregular arcs, almost like groves of a metal forest parted by wide lanes of unformed ground. Some appear newly emerged: small nubs of shimmering ore breaking through the crust, each ring still forming around a core. Others stand tall, spined towers bristling with symmetrical growth, their underlayers glinting with an inner luminescence. Hard to say whether they took days or decades to reach that height—time seems as malleable as the half-fused crust beneath your boots.

Underneath the forest canopy of metallic spires, a different sort of resident makes itself known only in occasional flickers: sparks dancing in the hollows, the air briefly igniting with pinkish-blue static. These flickers come and go, magnetically drawn to certain lattice points within the columns. You can almost imagine them as the souls of the place, ephemeral and electric, following some coded directive that only they understand. They dart in silence, leaving the faintest crackle of discharge that resonates like voices pitched too low—or too vast—for human ears.

Deeper down, layered beneath the plateau’s topsoil, there is another form of life altogether—if “life” is the right word. The low hum you perceive is, in a way, its breath, pressing up through the bedrock like the pulse of a colossal circulatory system. It is neither plant nor animal nor conventional machine, but something that merges geology with a slow, mechanical metabolism. It is the plateau itself: a living foundation that settles and reforms in a constant state of becoming. This deep-lung engine sifts mineral fluids through unseen channels, forging new surfaces at a rate so gradual we scarcely recognize the surge. Yet the evidence is there—new metallic columns forming, fissures sealing themselves, fresh arcs in the newly cast ground. The entire realm is a single organism shaped by mineral flows and electrochemical beats, continuing a timeless cycle of self-extension.

Within that hush, the brilliance of this place is that there is no hurry. The energy overhead is gentle and constant, bathing the plateau in a luminous wave that fosters this slow growth without scalding it. The inhabitants—these mineral forests, these dusty electric sparks, and that vast engine-like presence below—settle into a stable equilibrium. They have no names, no classification that fits into Earth’s taxonomy. They simply are, existing without the impetus to travel or devour or flee. They reflect their environment perfectly, shaped by the same process that forges them.

In that sense, nothing in this realm is lonely, for every column is connected by that resonant hum, every spark responding in synergistic arcs. A silent interplay persists among them. And so they live—if live is the word—like a chorus of slow harmony, each newly grown panel or sparking filament a subtle variation on the same theme. The plateau cradles them all, an ongoing experiment of chemical humidity and inorganic fertility, persisting in the golden-white light that never burns out. Together, these columns, sparks, and subterranean processes compose the only possible form of “life” in this silent land, building toward an existence both ancient and perpetually new.
