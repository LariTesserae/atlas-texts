They are not quite like any familiar creature, yet they are unquestionably present. At first, you might overlook them entirely, mistaking them for localized concentrations of sap or subtle bulges in the twisting vines. But look closer, let your eyes linger on the rhythmic flow of the living corridors, and you’ll notice the subtle outlines: forms that shift in and out of focus along the ridges of bark, pulses that ride up the stems as though animate shadows were flowing inside the plant tissue.

In one moment, a great living buttress appears solid—its exterior striated with ridges—but with a slight flex of the trunk, you see a silhouette peel away from it. Something emerges from that boundary, not so much stepping out as being involuntarily exhaled. Its shape is a confluence of luminous, fibrous threads winding around a central node, and it reflects the color of the trunk’s bark in watery ripples. You sense a kind of flexing intelligence—present but not in the way of eyes or expressions. Its awareness is spread across the filaments that compose its body, each tendril actively tasting, testing the air.

Peer deeper into the labyrinth, and you’ll see more of these forms, each an outgrowth of its surrounding plant architecture. They don’t glide so much as flow, weaving themselves in and out of the porous bark walls. One moment, they’re half-buried in a column of living wood, dark ridges suggesting dozens of branching “limbs.” Next, they rise from the mossy floor in a surge of braided stems, only to vanish entirely into a vault of overlapping leaves overhead. Every movement suggests they share the same fundamental tissues as the plants. Perhaps they are condensed waves of growth, ephemeral surges of vegetative energy that occasionally take on an almost animal-like shape.

Sometimes you see them massing in those great “squares” of the city, drifting together in swirling clusters that might swirl just beneath the surface of a trunk. During these gatherings, the thunderous roar of rustling foliage intensifies, as if the presence of so many beings in one place amplifies the city’s heartbeat. With each crest of noise, the shapes bend toward one another, interlinking their filaments so that a single, expanded silhouette flickers over the architecture—a body formed of many bodies braided like vines. These extraordinary communal moments last only a few seconds, dissolving again into individual streams of motion that vanish along the twisting corridors.

In quieter intervals—when the thunderous hum subsides briefly into a low rumble—you might catch them shifting shape more delicately. Sometimes one protrudes a slender, leaf-like organ that quivers in the breeze. Sometimes an entire group extends watery fronds, making them look like floating anemones caught mid-pulse, their edges luminescent in the indirect green light. These fluttering structures might be the closest thing to communication: signals slowly rippling from one ephemeral entity to the next, carried by fissures in the bark or by pollinated breezes that swirl through the corridors. The living city, too, seems to respond, architectural elements flexing or folding in time with these silent exchanges.

You’ll never see two of them quite alike. One might incorporate elaborate, branching filigrees reminiscent of root systems, while another is little more than a knotted mass of luminous fibers that pulses in and out of a column of trunk. Occasionally, you’ll spot a tall, tapered figure resembling a sapling with numerous spidery offshoots, each twitching in the passing wind. Then, with a sound like stretching bark, it sinks back into the floor, leaving a faint pattern of swirling lines behind, as if it were never really separate from the ground at all. Their boundaries wither or bloom in tune with the living geometry around them, as though they’re simply denser pockets of the city’s unstoppable growth.

As you wander deeper, you realize these beings might be the orchestrators (or simply the product) of the city’s constant morphing. With every spasm, every shifting archway, they appear in flickers right at the hinge points of transformation, seemingly bonding new branches or directing the flow of vines into symmetrical patterns. They might be building, or perhaps they’re compelled by the city’s unstoppable creation—no more in control than a bud is “in control” of its own blooming. They inhabit the living architecture in a way that suggests no boundaries exist between them and their environment.

When the environment’s roars escalate to an overwhelming thunder, you’ll see them darting along the surfaces in waves, filaments twisting and sparking with color in reaction to each sonic pulse. Some throw out arcs of glimmering sap-like matter that adhere briefly to overhead leaves, forming arcs or domes, which just as quickly fold back into newly budding fronds. It’s as though these entities feed on turbulence, harnessing each wave of motion to reshape themselves and the structures around them.

And so they roam—or perhaps roam is not the right word—because they never truly leave. They’re integral to the place’s beating heart, mesmerically woven into the labyrinth’s adhesive layers of bark and vine. In fact, you suspect all the plants might contain sleeping versions of these beings within them: swirling knots of potential that flare to life when the city lurches. For all one knows, even the spongy floors might be condensed networks of their dormant forms, waiting for the exact pitch of a vibrating leaf to spring forward in a new shape. 

If you stand very still and attune to the swirling crescendo, you may feel a gentle brush along your arm—a slight prickling tension in the air. Turn your head just in time to witness one of these entities drifting by, parting the leaves with ethereal grace, volumes of fibrous matter swirling within it like water caught in a tidepool. You sense no hostility. Rather, it feels like the city’s awareness is touching you, tasting your presence, trying to incorporate you momentarily into its cyclical dance of budding and re-formation.

They are neither animal nor plant, neither single being nor many—an amalgam of living geometry constantly reconfiguring. Maybe they’ve evolved from the city’s own desire to shape itself, or from some unknown seed of life that found fertile ground in this chaotic swirl of shared growth. What’s certain is that they belong to this place as intimately as a stem belongs to its petals, manifesting wherever growth is strongest and guiding the city’s metamorphoses at each twist and turn.

No single name could capture them (and they have no use for names in any sense we’d understand). But if you must call them something, perhaps “the city’s echoes,” or “the living hum,” or “the architects of change.” Like the rest of this kaleidoscopic environment, they exist in a sort of living dream—quivering, expanding, and dissolving in constant flux. To watch them is to see the city’s heartbeat given form, a chorus of living shapes that flicker and fade in the green half-light, always weaving themselves deeper into the architecture they inhabit. They are simultaneously the city’s consciousness and its pulse, telling the story of a place that does not merely exist but is perpetually, thunderously, becoming.
