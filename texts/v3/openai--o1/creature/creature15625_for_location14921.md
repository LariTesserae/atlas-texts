They are not a single species, nor do they organize themselves into any strict hierarchy; each living presence simply follows its own small rhythms that stitch together into the larger tapestry of the place. Yet for all their variety, there is a sense that every inhabitant shares a kinship with the living city—an unspoken pledge that draws them together. They are as much shaped by the environment as they shape it in return.

At the smallest scale, you come upon thumbnail-sized insects with carapaces of shimmering scales. By daylight, each scale seems to reflect a different color so that no two appear quite the same. They flutter and pause erratically, traveling mostly in swarms that cling to vines before detaching in a sudden, synchronized explosion of motion. Together, they send up a noise like coins being chattered between teeth: a frenetic, metallic clacking that crescendos and fades in pulses. Some feed on the nectars that drip from splits in the translucent bulbs. Others nibble on the mossy bark or gather the dustlike spores that drift through the air like golden pollen. They are the first sign of life you notice, bright and busy, embodying the ceaseless energy curling between trunk and leaf.

Slightly larger are the “listening beetles,” named for the bony fans that jut from their sides. These look like flattened ears or half-formed shells, always rotating at angles to catch the swirl of sound. Their entire existence seems devoted to capturing the city’s endless noise and transforming it somehow. When they’ve collected sufficient sonic vibrations, they launch themselves into a loop of aerial acrobatics—tumbling end over end with surprising agility for creatures so bulky. Some say they are actually storing the sound inside themselves, as if feeding on the resonance, then releasing it later as a burst of ultrasonic clicks. Past travelers report hearing these creatures in the dead of night, broadcasting ghostly echoes that cause the vines to stir in their sleep.

Everywhere near water, some variety of amphibian thrives: sleek-skinned frogs with luminous patterns that blink on and off like living lanterns, newts with elongated bodies and frilled gills, and weird multi-limbed “toads” that scuttle more than they hop. You can see them sliding among the broad leaf-bowls, vying for the precious droplets of moisture. Their calls weave together in an overlapping chorus—part croak, part shriek—like a constantly shifting chant of devotion to the watery pockets that sustain them. In more secluded corners where the plant growth arches into hidden alcoves, you might see smaller amphibians perched on the lips of massive flowers, grooming themselves with webbed fingers. Their eyes shift with the changing haze of light, often looking more curious than fearful, as though they are aware of an unseen dimension spiraling just out of reach.

High above, nesting in the lattice of branches and vine-rafters, dwell avian-like creatures whose feathers seem partially plantlike themselves: each plume displays fibrous veins reminiscent of a leaf. Their wings creak and rustle, and when they flutter in groups, the sound is like sheets of thick paper being rifled. They preen with elaborate care, tending to each feather’s edges with a narrow tongue that excretes a resinous sap, which keeps them flexible and shining. Their songs, when heard alone, can be hypnotically melodious, modulating in trills and warbles that echo through the hollow trunks. But set against the larger cacophony, their voices become fleeting ribbons of melody in an ocean of noise. They hunt insects on the wing and delicately probe into the largest blossoms for pockets of nectar, sometimes emerging with their feathery heads drenched in sticky gold. Yet, without fail, they remain aloof. They watch from their high vantage points, keen-eyed, as though tasked with bearing witness to whatever transpires below.

And then there are the larger beasts—the ones whose tremors you feel before you see them. Even glimpsed at a distance, they appear as great, shifting silhouettes against the vine-woven walls. Some are squat and broad, with scaled hides that blend into bark-like camouflage. These slow-moving creatures graze on fibrous root growth and nudge aside decaying trunks, snuffling at the fungal blooms that sprout there. Others remain tall and gawky, perched on stilt-like limbs that allow them to thread through low corridors without stooping. Vines catch on their bony protrusions, and the creatures pause often to pull the tangles free with elongated snouts or specialized pincers, leaving behind bits of shed scale or hair that the plants swiftly envelop and reclaim. Despite their size, they step with surprising grace, careful rarely to crush the smaller life below. It is as if the entire environment has taught them to tread lightly, to navigate with a gentle presence.

There are rumors—maybe only half whispers—of even more elusive inhabitants. People speak of creatures that can’t properly be categorized as animal or plant. They hold a position in between, merging with the vines themselves, half formed of bark and leaf, with eyes that appear one moment as an emerald sheen on a trunk, and another moment as the shifting swirl of a blossom. These beings might be the architects of the place or living guardians that flow in and out of the city’s plant mass, rearranging corridors and openings to suit deeper, unknown purposes. Some who wander alone at night claim to see slender shapes drift across pathways and vanish into trunk walls without a sound. Others speak of faint, whispery singing that resonates through the bark at twilight. When you press your palm to a trunk at such times, a subtle vibration passes back, like a coded greeting or a question that can’t be put into words.

Each lifeform here plays a role in maintaining the balance of swirling sound and pulsing growth. Pollinators large and small feed from the bursts of nectar released by the translucent pods, and in return, they carry vital spores or seeds through the labyrinth. Scavengers break down fallen towers of plant matter, ensuring that fresh, younger vines can surge upward unimpeded. Even the seemingly discordant noise has a function: some species navigate by echo, some exchange rapid-fire calls to confirm territory or to lure mates, and many rely on the continuous vibrations as a measure of the living city’s health. Silence—even fleeting—becomes a phenomenon so extraordinary that it draws every ear and every eye.

Among these fellow inhabitants, you too feel like a visitor welcomed into a vast, breathing organism. Each step echoes with a thousand living footsteps, each breath answers the green exhalations of the city’s lungs. If you stay long enough, you may find yourself watched by the deeper intelligence lurking in the vines. You might catch glimpses of eyes behind leaves or sense a presence that peers into your intentions. Your other senses will sharpen, perhaps to a level you never thought possible. And gradually, your own movements might become more fluid, your ears more attuned, your sense of time slipping into the city’s strange warp. You may realize that you’re following the same unspoken pledge all these creatures share—to give as much as you take, to resonate with the living architecture, and to keep life’s heartbeat cycling.

In this place, no single occupant dominates, and none truly sits at the top. Instead, every lifeform—whether feathered, chitinous, leathery, veined, or amorphous—sustains the next in a grand and shifting mosaic. It is a realm where biology is both bricks and breath, and where continuity depends on constant change. From the tiniest luminescent insect to the half-glimpsed shape weaving seamlessly through living corridors, they dwell here in concert. They thrive on the swirl of warmth and sound, on the openings that appear and vanish among the vines, on the illusions that flicker at the corners of perception. They live for this perpetual moment of creation, always in flux, always on the edge of something new—and in that shared pulse of existence, they are irrevocably bound to the place that birthed them.
