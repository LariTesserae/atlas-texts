At first glance, you might think that nothing at all resides there—that whatever carved outlines of civilization once emerged from these dripping walls has long since vanished. Indeed, with no birds or beasts, no tracks, no shadows of movement, how could there be any inhabitants? But if you linger, the plateau itself begins to suggest presence. It’s in the way the vines tighten over stone arches like sinews—mechanical yet alive. It’s in the way echoes from the chasms seem to answer your footsteps, and in how the colored ribbons along the cliff faces shimmer before your eyes in silent conversation. Something is here, though not in any form you’d easily name.

Perhaps there are many things that dwell together, layered upon each other like sediment. One possibility is that an ancestral being—the creative genius behind nature’s twisted architecture—lingers in the humid air. It’s not an entity of flesh, but rather a force of constant growth, a caretaker manifested in every droplet of water. This presence seems to guide the vines to arch in just the right shapes, coaxing roots and stems to lace together into corridors that defy normal reason yet never fall apart. The city could be its living blueprint, an organism with slow pulses measured not in heartbeats but in centuries.

Then again, there might be watchers in the seams of reality itself—those flickers of disjointed geometry and the faint glow in the cracks. Imagine beings who slipped through from a dimension just half a step off from our own. If they have bodies at all, they’re made of bent perspective: edges that warp, corners that do not align. They live in the warp and weft of the city’s illusions. Their breathing is the hushless wind, tugged back and forth by temperature gradients at these extreme heights. Their voices might be the roar of the waterfalls, resonating so low in pitch that you feel it more than hear it. And when you glimpse a tear in the distance like a seam in the sky, perhaps that’s a half-seen doorway they use to move deeper into or out of the plateau’s hidden angles.

Or instead, consider that the city is itself the inhabitant, and all you witness—every curtain of moss, every trickle of condensation, every thundering cascade—are its organs and fluids. Roots stretch where limbs might be, columns stand in place of bones, wet stone corridors keep rhythms like veins. In this view, it’s not deserted at all; the stone towers, the vine canopies, the labyrinthine waterways are the body and mind of a colossal, slow-wheeling life-form. It does not speak because it has never needed a voice. It simply endures, rising from the depths like an impossible fortress. The overlapped echoes, wind, and water may be the echoes of its all-encompassing respiration. The flickers of warped geometry might be how it perceives itself—shifting in ways a conventional life-form never could.

Others might suggest that the city’s true residents are the illusions themselves—those bright ribbons of color swirling across rock, those subtle seams in the air. They are ghostlike: intangible to your touch, yet fiercely anchored to the plateau’s form. They slip across surfaces with a sense of purpose, as though choreographed by an unseen hand. At certain moments, you might sense them coalescing into vague shapes in your peripheral vision: half-formed silhouettes drifting around the dripping grottos. Yet the moment you try to look straight at them, they vanish. It is as if they feed on whatever tension holds this place together—space-time thrumming with possibilities.

Imagine, too, that the roaring waterfalls are not just water but a chorus of echoes from some hidden population. Picture ephemeral creatures shaped of living sound, swirling up in the spray and descending again into the caverns below. Their entire existence is an eternal resonant chord, joining in the cacophony where cliff meets torrent. They flicker into near-solid forms at the edges of your hearing, only to recede in synchronized waves. You never see them, because their shape is purely acoustic. Yet you might feel them brushing past you whenever the waterfall’s bass note momentarily reverberates in your chest.

And still, there is another explanation: that the plateau is a grand reverie, preserved by time’s trickery. Its “inhabitants” are memories of a civilization that once lived in a deeper, older era. Now, these presences are suspended in a dimension where chronologies loop back on themselves. They don’t move forward, so they don’t evolve or deteriorate—they just persist at the threshold between existence and oblivion. Glimpses of them might be found in the fleeting corners of your vision—no more than fractal shapes, repeating across stone and water—like the afterimage of something never truly there. If you ventured far enough into the city’s labyrinth, perhaps you would find the memory of a banquet hall or a council chamber still echoing with the silent debate of vanished voices.

In the end, none of these facets fully excludes the others. The plateau could be home to all of these presences at once—a braided tapestry of improbable life forms and intangible echoes, overlapping so seamlessly that the city appears to be empty at a casual glance. You see only water, rock, and vine, but behind the veil there is an immeasurable congregation of forces. They are the hum within each droplet, the shimmer in the cracks of reality, the vibrating chords in the dense, wet air, the fleeting illusions on the horizon.

And so you find that it is not deserted after all. Quite the opposite. Something, or many somethings, abide here in eternal stasis, coaxing the vines to twine, shaping the waterfalls’ thunder, warping the geometry at the edges, and haunting the place with a breathless, living silence. They need no shelter, no tools, no languages or roads. They simply exist, bound to the plateau’s ancient heart—whatever that core may be. Whether they are the city’s architects, its ghosts, or one and the same, they occupy the ephemeral spaces where reality grows thin. Their unchanging realm carries on, as loud and lonely as the day it was first (and timelessly) formed.
