They are difficult to name, these denizens of the plateau—difficult to define, even, as “creatures” in the usual sense. At times, you might think of them as organic: creatures of sinew, scale, exoskeleton. But watch them a moment longer, and you’ll see segments of them refract light back in glassy, prismatic patterns, as though grown from living crystal. Some appear bony and angular; others shimmer like quicksilver with fluid limbs that shift color and sheen. They defy simple classification—each form a living alloy of mineral and flesh, kinetic energy and pulsing, alien awareness.

Wherever you look, you see a shared lineage in the way they move. Their movements are sharp, precise, reactive. Yet there’s a certain fluidity to it, as if they have an internal sense of the plateau’s deeper rhythm. The thrumming resonance that shakes the stone underfoot seems to direct or cue them. When a tower discharges a wave of sonic force, certain creatures pause and raise their antennae or spines, receiving it, feeding on it. When shimmering pulses arc across the black alloy spires, others gather in half-circles, opening segmented shells or beating translucent wings to catch the energy. Respiration here might well be the absorption of these arcs, converting them into life-sustaining currents that ripple through their bodies. It’s as though moisture is not their lifeblood—electric potential is.

A cluster of small, nimble ones—each about the size of a child’s hand—congregates around a crack where a faint glow seeps up from under the stone. They dart in and out with insatiable curiosity, chittering in high, percussive tones. Their bodies are mostly smooth, banded metal segmented around a cluster of crackling veins that emit tiny sparks. In every movement, they jitter with a manic energy that makes them look half-coiled, ready to spring. Occasionally, two or three will scamper off together, swirling around each other in a vortex of excited chirrups, seemingly enthralled by whatever new current they sense below the plateau’s surface.

Just a few feet away, looming overhead, is something else—tall, multiple-limbed, its upper half reminiscent of a serpent’s silhouette rearing upright. Its skin (if it can be called skin) is layered in geometric plates of onyx-like material, with thin lines of light tracing circuit-like patterns between plates. Each limb ends in a spidery cluster of hooked claws or pincers, which it uses to probe at the stone. It does so slowly, almost tenderly, as if testing for stress fractures or sifting out minerals that have drifted too near the surface. When a deep boom echoes from a distant tower, this towering figure arches its neck as if to meet that resonance with its own long, droning exhalation—a single note pitched so low it vibrates in your chest more than in your ears.

Scattered among these extremes in size, you’ll find countless in-betweens. Some flutter with diaphanous wings, nearly invisible except for a faint prism of color as they catch the dust-laden shafts of mountain sunlight. Others slither across the ground, sleek and dull in coloration, but every so often shedding a glitter of static discharge along their path. A few even resemble small avian creatures, with v-shaped beaks that peck at mineral filaments protruding from the plateau’s cracks. But their feathers look more like flexible glass shards, and their calls warble in an unsettling, disharmonic chord, as if they’re sampling multiple frequencies at once.

In the “vegetation”—if it can be called that—some of these entities dwell symbiotically. The alien plants coil around the creatures’ bodies, or the creatures grip the twisting stems with specialized limbs made for clasping. In a curious role reversal from terrestrial ecosystems, the “plants” might feed on the minor pulses generated by the animals, while the animals absorb radiant arcs from the metallic towers. Both, in turn, feed the intangible hum of the plateau, which knits them all together in a single resonant system. It is as though the entire plateau and its denizens comprise a living circuit board—every resonance, every pulse feeding back into the chaotic harmony that keeps the cycle going.

Nor are the plateau’s denizens always visible on the surface. With the ground trembling at intervals and certain sections rearranging themselves, it seems probable that far below the rock face, there are burrowing giants—massive, half-mineral serpents that tunnel through the layers of banded stone. By the occasional swell beneath your feet, you sense they are a constant, methodical presence, tirelessly circulating who-knows-what energies to the surface. More rarely, you’ll see a sudden rise in activity as smaller creatures scatter, then a bulge in the stone drifts past like a leviathan crossing just beneath the skin of a lake, displacing the ground as fluidly as water would be. You might catch the faintest glimpse of a gargantuan segmented carapace through cracks that momentarily gape, then close up again. Like living arteries, these subterranean highways are constantly funneling some vital resource from one region to another.

And what of the watchers—those that appear more deliberate, almost sentient, examining the strange black structures or fiddling with newly formed spires of fused stone? They gather in small, purposeful groups to piece together shards or shape half-formed alloys with an odd, meticulous artistry. Sometimes these constructions remain, feeding pulses back into the plateau, while at other times they wither away in the confusion of swirling storms. The watchers don’t seem discouraged when progress is undone; they move on without alarm, as though every half-built tower, every collapsed arch, is part of an ongoing experiment that must be eternally repeated.

Among them are individuals who, every so often, converge at the tallest black alloy tower—perhaps the largest intact relic of whatever technology seeded this place. They latch onto ridges or outcroppings of it, letting arcs of violet and green light climb across their bodies in crackling webs. Their forms shudder in synchrony, seemingly absorbing knowledge, reprogramming themselves, or exchanging signals in a complex dance of pulses. Then they descend and scatter across the plateau, disseminating whatever insight they gleaned. If you watch closely, you’ll see new behaviors emerge—a tweak to how they harvest minerals, a subtle shift in how a certain brood defends its territory from the swirling storms at the perimeter. It is an ecological process akin to data-sharing, as if the region’s population is one vast, multi-limbed mind, the plateau’s own intellect blossoming and collapsing in cycles.

Across every slope of rock and every quivering frond, biological shapes mingle with geometric order. It’s almost impossible to draw a line and say “these are animals, and those are plants, or structures.” Most things here blend all three; a stalagmite might extend a thin vine-like appendage to siphon sparks from passing insects, or a cluster of once-living exoskeletons might coalesce, growing into a new spire over the course of days. Sure enough, the place brims with emergent forms that test the boundary between animate and inanimate, living function and metallic complexity.

And always, always, the noise. The smaller ones add their calls—clicks, squeals, and susurrations that prick your ears like insects buzzing too close. The mid-range dwellers stomp or flap with a deeper resonance, sometimes letting out a series of pulsing drones that skip across the plateau’s surface in rolling waves. The towering colossals, whether above ground or just below it, occasionally emit thunderous calls that make the black alloy towers respond in kind. And under it all remains that subsonic roar from the mountain’s heart, the plateau’s vital, throbbing cadence. It’s no wonder that much of the life here should rely on sensing vibrations and pulses rather than conventional sight or sound—a chorus of frequencies, more raw than any human voice.

This, then, is who lives here: an entire kingdom of energy-dependent organisms, at once perilous and extraordinarily attuned, thriving in an environment bereft of water but awash in electrical nourishment, sonic bombardment, and raw geologic force. They are simultaneously the architects and products of this plateau, shaping it as it shapes them in return—a world where living is not about blood and chlorophyll but about harnessing the ceaseless hum, the breathless altitude, the swirl of ionic storms high above. They inhabit this realm in a constant dance of evolution, at one with the living stone that pulses beneath their feet. Quite simply, the plateau is them and they are the plateau, an indivisible, churning phenomenon of life made real through thunder and spark and the indomitable surreal logic of its own creation.
