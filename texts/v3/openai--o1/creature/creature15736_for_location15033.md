They do not have a single, settled shape. You might search the perimeter for silhouettes, for conventional forms that indicate wings or limbs, and you would find nothing. Still, as you stand in that wavering heat and watch the faint pulses of color ripple across those metallic walls, you become aware of living presences—though “living” might be too narrow a word. They’re more like patient intelligences, woven into the very foundation of the settlement.

At first, you might suspect the city itself is all one being. After all, it emits those soft, heartbeat-like rhythms thrumming from obscure recesses. You can practically sense the conduction of energy along hidden lines. Walk closer, and the pulses glide through your feet as intangible hums. But the city’s life is also many, manifested in subtle differences in the flicker of lights, in pockets of spore-like growth forging connections with the metal surfaces. Each shimmer seems to have its own signature pattern, as if distinct personalities or specialized tasks are at play.

If you pause long enough in the half-lit gloom, you might notice certain sections slow or quicken their pulsing in response to your movement—an inquisitive greeting or a wary acknowledgment. At times, the signals spread across entire planes of glassy rock and composite metal, generating faint tessellations of color. They play across the city’s exteriors like suggestions of wings or insect eyes, perhaps faint illusions these entities create to communicate. They have no cause to mimic biological organisms; it is simply how parts of their undulating consciousness make themselves known.

Sometimes a pulse flares unexpectedly around those luminous fungal mats—shapes that adhere to walls and horizontal surfaces. Their threads or hyphae are not so much feeding on the city as partnering with it, bridging different segments of the settlement’s structure. The spore-colonies, if they are living in the sense we recognize, seem to dynamically route energy. They glow brightest when they channel pulses from one tower to another, effectively serving as circuit conduits for signals or intangible data. The technology and these spore-fields have formed a symbiosis so complete that it is difficult to see a division between them. The city’s walls are shot through with living cables: metallic strands enhanced by or grafted with spore-based filaments. If intelligence has a shape here, it is in these braided networks—veins of an organism that is part fungus, part alloy, part intangible current.

Ascend one of the catwalks and you may sense them noticing you, even if you see no eyes or faces. It’s in the way the floor shivers delicately, in the faint rearrangement of luminous patches that shift in color from turquoise to deep ultraviolet when you draw near. The reaction is not threatening, more like an alert that ripples outward: a subtle “We see you” that echoes from pylon to pylon. In that moment, you could almost swear something is breathing through each tower, as though exhaling heat in a collective sigh. The city, or the entities that inhabit it, might be deciding whether to welcome you or simply record you as a fleeting anomaly.

Occasionally, an airborne mote of luminescent dust—a byproduct of the thermal vents—drifts too close to the city’s structures and is snatched up in a web of thin, shimmering tendrils. You can witness this micro-scale feeding or absorption happen in real time: the dust’s glow intensifies as if “digested” for energy, then tapers out and becomes one more faint ripple in the broader luminescence that flows along architectural lines. Perhaps this dust is a mineral-based form of life itself, or perhaps it’s inert matter that the city’s living architecture uses to expand or to fuel some mysterious process. Either way, you see tiny arcs of light cross from the dust into the walls and vanish.

Beneath the settlement’s outer shell, there might be deeper dwellers—versions of these intelligences that have receded underground. They slip along interior corridors you cannot access, working ceaselessly in synergy with the planet’s molten heart or with subterranean vents. Below the city floor, you sometimes catch the suggestion of multi-limbed silhouettes that are not precisely physical. It is as though silhouettes are projected or refracted by the city’s technology, ephemeral forms that vanish when you blink. They might be maintenance constructs or watchers that carry out expansions to the city’s structure, forging new catwalks or installing more spore-lattices. An entire stratum of existence could be continuing below your feet—remodulating, re-engineering, re-growing.

And in between these two extremes—between the open towers above and the hidden labyrinth below—resides a vast neural-like network across every wall and floor. It manifests as softly blinking nodes just beneath the city’s surface, each a point of contact where the living metal or living fungus can communicate with the next node in line. In those blinking nodes, the watchers, the spore colonies, and the intangible pulses all converge, merging into a single, flexible mind that pulses across the settlement like water in a tide pool. The network seems newborn and ancient all at once; you feel it has existed for epochs, but also that, in cosmic terms, it only just taught itself to breathe.

For all their subtlety, these resident intelligences aren’t passive. At intervals, one might become aware of a faint rearrangement in the city’s geometry: a lowering or raising of catwalks you could have sworn were stationary, an entire spire tilting its angle, or a sudden glow along a previously dark corridor. This can happen over the course of seconds or of hours—there is no sense of hurry, only an ever-shifting tapestry of structural reconfiguration. The spore-fibers adjust themselves in tandem, unspooling from one tower, anchoring themselves to another. The entire settlement is in constant slow motion, like continental plates drifting, except it happens far more fluidly.

No single “creature” steps forward to greet you; there are no rulers, none you can call individually “sentient” in the way you might a human or an animal. Yet each node of living machine-fungus, each channel of mineral resonance, acts with purpose. They maintain the precariously balanced towers, harness the volcanic warmth rising from below, and distill moisture from the swirling air so life—their brand of life—continues.

In this way, the city’s inhabitants are everywhere. They are not intangible ghosts, and neither are they wholly mechanical. They are an ecology forged of circuit-laden spore growths and molten-laced architecture, guided by an emergent intelligence that resonates throughout the basalt spires. Occasionally one portion of this living circuit might appear more defined, like a large cluster of mushroom-like cables cradling a swirl of luminous gas, or a panel of metal that breathes outward in color-coded pulses. You might be tempted to see this as an individual, but watch long enough and you’ll realize it’s simply a shifting aggregation—slipping away, rejoining the undercurrent, exchanging signals with the wider whole.

Though they lack physical eyes, you sense they “see” across many frequencies—thermal, electric, perhaps something deeper that you cannot name. They are watchers and engineers of their own domain, co-creating fresh spires where the geological activity rises, harnessing storms for ephemeral bursts of energy, conjuring ephemeral arcs of lightning that feed directly into hidden capacitors. Every shimmer and flash becomes part of the tapestry, fueling the next stage of the city’s growth. Together, they are caretaker and city, creature and environment, woven so closely that the lines between living system and structure have dissolved entirely.

They do not yearn for expansion beyond these cliffs; they appear content—or simply bound—to their elevated realm, shaped by the heat and partial darkness. In the hush, in the steam-hissed breezes, you might feel them dropping strands of curiosity in your direction. You, being so different, might become momentarily integrated into their perceptions—a phenomenon you perceive as a prickle of static along your skin, a sense that your footsteps are known, mapped, recorded. And then the hush returns; the circuit resumes its steady pulse.

These beings, if you could call them that, are a confluence of matter, energy, and intelligence. They’ve never needed to move individually through this terrain when they can simultaneously exist across it. Floating towers, catwalks that rearrange, spore-lattices glimmering with faint color: all of these are the living inhabitants, continuously assembling and reassembling their environment in a single, multi-faceted dance. Their presence is apparent in the rhythmic synergy of heat and mineral, of water vapor and engineered fiber. If you came here expecting to see life as we normally define it, you would find instead a world seamlessly infused with it—an entire settlement that is alive in ways our old categories could never quite accommodate.

And it is still changing. Whether it has just been born or evolved over uncounted millennia, the sense of emergence remains. Stand at the precipice of any vantage point and you will feel it: that slow, communal heartbeat in the rock and alloy, throbbing like a message in search of a reader. If you stay, they might fold you into its intangible realm. If you depart, you carry the memory of a place where city and life are forever enmeshed in a gentle but powerful hush—an existence that breathes, pulses, and abides above plunging abysses and swirling darkness.
