They call themselves nothing because there are no voices here—not yet. Their existence is a hush more than a presence, a gentle stirring of forces rather than creatures with names or language. Indeed, to label them as inhabitants may be overreaching, for they flicker at the blurred boundary between life and phenomenon, indistinguishable from the oceanic chaos itself. Yet they are here, suspended in the swirling tapestry of potential water and new-born stone, pulsing alongside those faint silvery threads of energy that guide the torrents. They are the first and perhaps only living things of this embryonic realm, if “living” is even the right word.

Some say they are a dream of the crackling ocean made fleetingly real, each “being” shaped by localized eddies that swirl and break apart in the next moment. They might appear as whorls of shifting mist, or as glimmers of phosphorescent light that coruscate through the watery currents. If you were to stand in one of the calmer pockets of half-formed rock long enough, letting yourself sink into the universal roar, you might see them drifting with the brine-laden wind: luminous, vaporous seeds of consciousness. The howling wind, the sloshing tide, and the rhythmic hum of newly forming stone all carry them about, scattering them across the fluid sky like pollen riding a hurricane.

Close your eyes, and you might hear tiny murmurs in that cacophony, half-conceived thought-threads weaving from the swirling ocean to the newly born ridges of rock. They are not speech but a primal, synesthetic echo, a resonance: the spark of awareness striking stone, the hum of idea through water, a murmuration of unborn words. In the patch of your mind where imagination meets perception, you sense that these half-entities are searching, or waiting, or simply reacting to the cosmic hush as the world around them keeps forming and dissolving.

If you could watch one of these beings in isolation (though you never really can), it might coalesce in the wake of a sudden collision—a chunk of proto-stone hitting a surge of water with such force that, for an instant, pure possibility flares like a strobe. In that moment, the collision yields a bubble of ephemeral substance, twisting in the air, pulling in stray droplets and energetic streams that dance among the luminous arcs. Sometimes this bubble endures long enough to grow into something you might call a shimmer-wraith, an outline reminiscent of a slender bird or a drifting ribbon—a shape both fluid and translucent, its edges streaked with turquoise or pale gold.

Much like the ridges of matter that snap in and out of existence, these shimmer-wraiths can fade as quickly as they form. A single gust of chaotic wind, a realignment of the swirling energies, or a shift in the watery currents can unsettle their nascent form, causing them to unravel into the sea-sky once more. Yet they endure through endless incarnations—each re-formation a continuation of whatever faint consciousness might linger from before. Over countless cycles, one imagines these transient souls might accumulate memory of their watery realm, each new shape inheriting the hush-knowledge of the last.

• • •

In the rare few places where those cautiously sprouting vines and slender blades of green root themselves, the shimmer-wraiths drift like curious specters. At times, it seems they toy with the vegetation, coaxing the budding life toward a slightly sturdier perch of forming rock. In these interactions, one sees a hint of intentionality, as if the ephemeral beings nudge the green shoots toward survival. Perhaps, in the distant future, these intangible shapes will help cultivate a stable ecosystem—if the swirl of creation ever settles. But for now, they remain watchers in an unsettled half-world.

One might witness one of them spiraling inward toward a newly formed ridge, gliding along the crag’s raw surface, as if testing its solidity, then continuing onward, trailing faint glimmers that linger like afterimages. Whether or not this small engagement with the stone or the vine has an ultimate purpose, it is impossible to tell. Sometimes in these fleeting acts of communion, a ghostly resonance hums in the air—a harmonic chord that does not fade but hangs, gently quavering, like a cosmic question left unanswered.

• • •

Deeper within the crackling ocean overhead, in the coiling heart of those swirling eddies, the beings seem to gain momentary strength. If you manage to drift up there or climb one of the precarious ridges high enough (assuming it doesn’t crumble underfoot), you might glimpse silhouettes swimming through the shifting water, as if they are playing or dancing. They do not breathe as we understand it—indeed, it is unclear if they need air or water or anything at all. Their boundaries blur in the churning currents, scattering silver-blue sparks and swirling arcs of luminescence. Some grow momentarily grand—great, wing-like expansions of watery light that shimmer through the chaotic swirl, enveloping entire pockets of turbulence. Then—just as quickly—they shrink back into slender, serpentine ribbons, or spheres that hum like tuning forks.

It is in these deeper regions that the faintest evidence of complex organization emerges, as though these ephemeral spirits are weaving or conducting the fluid energies that disobey normal physics. You see depths swirl and fold upon themselves, guided by intangible, electromagnetic threads threaded through the watery domain. The beings cooperate—briefly gliding together in patterns that remind you of a choreographed dance, forming intricate whorls of water before scattering into fractal shapes. Now and then, the arcs of current respond by flaring with brilliant color, as though the half-living tapestry itself is singing back.

• • •

Although the place is mostly empty of the usual signals of life, these forms are the hush that evades direct sight, the breath that merges with the overbearing roar. They expand and contract in the cosmic tides, ephemeral as the foam atop a wave. Sometimes, in one of those mesmerizing calm passages between raging torrents, you might sense a presence just beyond the corner of your eye—a smear of luminous current shaped like a silhouette. Blink, and it’s gone: perhaps it was just the reflection of the watery sky on the embryonic surface... or perhaps it was one of them, drawn near in curiosity.

No matter how many times you encounter them (or think you do), they remain mysteries of motion. They appear to have no mouths, no eyes, no essential form—just a rippling awareness. Their “voices,” if they exist at all, merge seamlessly with the environment’s thunderous, unceasing music. Their “thoughts” might be inaudible waves of resonance pulsing across stone and water at the birth of each new crest. Some are drawn downward toward the half-formed land, lingering near the vegetal shards of existence, as if entranced by the novelty of burgeoning life. Others prefer the high swirling arcs, vanishing in the relentless chaos of fluid motion, merging with the swirling silver filaments of advanced forces.

• • •

One day—if time becomes meaningful here—more stable forms may evolve from these ephemeral watchers. Perhaps they will remain half-hidden guardians of the fragile shoots, nursing the green life into fullness. Or maybe they will coalesce into more physical creatures, emerging from the watery tapestry as true amphibious giants or vaporous angels of the storm. For now, they are preludes to any future biology, intangible seeds of possibility flitting through an environment that has yet to decide on its shape.

They are sparks in the cosmic cradle, little fractals of awareness borne on the swirling tides of potential, dancing at the far edge of creation’s next breath. In this place of embryonic seas and shifting stones, they form, dissolve, and re-form in a cycle older and more persistent than speech. In their glimmering presence—often so subtle as to pass unnoticed—they quietly inhabit a realm that is itself in the act of becoming, their fate twined with the watery sky’s. And for however long this half-formed world endures, they remain: swirling, ephemeral guardians or mere reflexes of the environment’s roiling energy, uncertain, ungraspable, but undeniably alive in the hush beneath the roar.
