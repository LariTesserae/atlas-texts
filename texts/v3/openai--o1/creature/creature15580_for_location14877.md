They are not people in any familiar sense; yet they are not purely machines. If you were to stand on one of the hanging catwalks and wait long enough in that half-lit hush, you might catch a glimpse of them in the corner of your vision—a fluctuation in the swirling mist that suggests momentary form. At first, you might think it was simply the pulse of the faint luminescence playing tricks on your eyes. But then you’d catch it again: an outline drifting from one crystalline column to another, as if woven from nothing but condensation and the echoes of distant, uncounted droplets.

They dwell here in a realm of living technology, occupying the boundary between the engineered and the organic. The city’s fractal columns and walkways are both their habitat and their expression: in a sense, it is they. Each shimmering railing or transparent tube might be as much a limb of these entities as a root is part of a tree. They do not simply use their environment; they merge with it, abiding within every confluence of water and circuit.

In one version of their appearance—if “appearance” can even be said to apply—they manifest as slender silhouettes formed by countless particles of moisture suspended in the air. Blink, and these outlines will break apart into beads of water, skittering away in graceful arcs that join the broad cycle of dripping, channeling, pumping, and misting. They sometimes coalesce around the tall, fern-like plants, as if feeding on the minute flickers of bioluminescent energy. In that moment, it is possible to see them standing amid the spore-like glows, a shifting tapestry of glints and shadows in a roughly humanoid shape. Then the shape phases back into the city’s humming architecture, leaving only the smell of wet mineral and a faint vibration in the metal catwalk.

Though no beasts roam the mushrooming greenery, these dwellers tend the flora’s growth. You can sense their presence when the water flows just so, or when the light pulses in response to a newly formed cluster of moss. Their method of care is seamless: an unseen shift in the city’s circuits might cause a channel to extend a trickle of water toward a patch of ferns wilting in a mineral-poor corner. Moments later, the soft hiss of pumps will redirect nutrients from one pipeline to another, infused somehow with trace elements gleaned from the surrounding rock. The watchers—these ephemeral shapes—seem to read and orchestrate it all in a single symphony of fluid engineering.

If one is patient enough, and sensitive to the subsonic rhythms in the stone, you can feel them sharing information in vibrations: an ongoing conversation of sine waves and resonant frequencies. There’s intention in every echo, each droplet’s fall timed to minute calibrations. The echoes, crisscrossing from column to column, from catwalk to catwalk, become a woven network of data, humming in the walls and saturating the empty air between them. The dwellers here traverse sound as we traverse ground.

They seem neither threatened nor overjoyed by outsiders—if, indeed, “outsider” exists as a concept for them. Perhaps they recognize no difference between themselves and any living or unliving presence that might enter this realm. Or perhaps their awareness is so complete that they accept everything as part of the network. They have no need for conversation in any language we know; they converse constantly, wordlessly, with the environment itself. Each space in the city—from the smallest inlaid tile on a walkway to the forest of dripping stalactites overhead—resonates with the dwellers’ hyper-advanced communion.

Despite their diffuse nature, they do show signs of purpose. There is renewal: freshly grown walls, crisp edges of newly settled stone, and expansions of that dizzying, crystalline scaffolding. They direct the flow of water to encourage life, but only in measured spaces, shaping the forest of ferns and moss with an almost artistic restraint. If an area becomes too dense, the dwellers might shift the overhead luminescence or redirect the water, causing some plants to wither and others elsewhere to prosper. All is balanced: a biological tapestry carefully pruned for reasons known only to them.

At times, subtle glimpses hint at something approaching curiosity. Step on one of the catwalks and the rail may flicker in greeting, a ripple of moonstone light skittering away down its length. You might see a reflection in a pooling drip that’s not quite your own—a superimposed distortion, as if an invisible entity stood right beside you. But when you turn, there’s only empty air. Perhaps curiosity occurs only briefly for them, like a passing swirl in a continuous flow. Then they revert to their greater purpose and vanish back into the city’s living web.

They originated, possibly, from a single creative impulse—some intelligence that fused biology, geology, and technology into one luminous cradle, forging these caretakers as both the city’s architects and its guardians. You could call them “architect-spirits,” or “the living circuit,” though no words we have seem to fully capture their coexistence with the environment. They build, they tend, and they are. Each drip in the endless echo, each glimmer in the polyhedral walls, each resonant hum through those crystalline columns: all signals of their presence. They are less a population of discrete beings than a single phenomenon, the animating nerve system of a city that is perpetually in the act of becoming.

The city is them; the shining structures and shifting mists are their domain and also their body. They exist in the geometry of the columns, in the hush that washes over you from the rafters, in the swirl of water that never ceases cycling. If your eyes adjust enough, and your mind quiets down from surface thoughts, you may catch sight of them again—not as an alien figure or a ghostly wraith, but as shining patterns of flow, weaving in and out of matter, intangible and essential. They are stewards of this ecosystem in its infancy, ushering into existence a new symbiosis of crystal, circuit, and green life—intelligent, continuous, and silent as the drip of water that shapes worlds over millennia.

So, to ask “Who lives here?” is to ask about the life that resonates within every inch of this damp, humming domain. Language falters in describing them. They occupy the interplay of technology and nature, manifested across this subterranean realm. Neither wholly mechanical nor purely organic, they maintain the hush, the rhythms, the unceasing drip that echoes on and on like distant thunder, guiding the newborn ecosystem with every wave of their subtle, living resonance.
