They’re not a single species, nor even a small family of related forms, but a patchwork of odd manifestations. At first you might assume that nothing truly lives here at all—only the silent architecture of leaf and steel, the hush of an engineered ecosystem that seems complete without creatures. But look closely in those fleeting edges of movement, those silhouettes that dart along the buttresses and vanish through twisting corridors of vines. Something abides here, more than one something, each adapted to the dryness and the all-consuming green in its own way.

Perhaps the most commonly glimpsed (though “common” is relative, for they’re still incredibly rare) are spindly creatures that seem to merge with the tangle of living struts. Some call them the Lattice Moths, though they’re nothing like Earthly moths: they carry frond-like wings of semi-synthetic fibers, gleaning faint charges from the humming vines. The patterns on these wings glow with a dull phosphorescence, brightening when they sense an electrical conduit near the surface, then dimming as they flutter away. Their bodies are skeletal and tiered, with overlapping segments that might be part chitin, part metal, so that if you spot one perched on a trunk, it could be mistaken for a decorative rivet or a patch of bark—until it takes flight. Their flight is nearly silent, muffled by the dryness and softened by their fibrous wings, leaving only the faintest stirring of air in their wake.

Deeper into the labyrinth of columns and leaf-walls dwell heavier-limbed forms, which travelers sometimes call the Builders. Rarely seen, they’re rumored to roam the sub-level vaults, where massive rootlike pipes snake along behind membranous barriers. When a corridor is reconfigured or a new walkway weaves itself into being, a dark shape might shuffle in the half-light to guide it—a bulky, hunchbacked silhouette with flexible spines that plug directly into the living-metal substrate. These creatures appear to coordinate the braided fronds and struts, hefting slow, heavy limbs to push or help the architecture complete its rearrangements. Their surfaces are scaly with something akin to bark plating, but their joints, sticking out from each segment, reveal a hidden clockwork of steel rods and rotating gears. A low, resonant rumble sometimes echoes from below when they move, as if huge bellows are compressing and decompressing deep in their chest cavity or possibly further within the living environment itself. Then, they vanish again, subsumed by the green labyrinth and the hush of continuous growth.

High overhead, in the near-blinding brightness of the canopy, dwell smaller, vining shapes that cling where leaves fuse with metallic buttresses in perplexing, paradoxical angles. These are the Crenellated Vipers—so named for how they coil and uncoil in the corners of that tessellating geometry. Less “snake” than a living system of cables, they move by contracting and releasing segments of a flexible spine, each piece riddled with tiny solar-like panels that capture the unrelenting brightness. From below, you might glimpse them as half-lost ribbons twisting among the leaves, letting out no sound, only an occasional flicker of reflected glow. Some travelers swear the Vipers deliver charges from one node to another, acting as living wires or organic cables bridging the canopy’s many circuits. Touching one is said to result in a jolt that leaves your arm numb, as though you brushed a sparking fence. Yet the creatures are docile unless disturbed—merely another link in the unbroken cycle of growth and power that permeates this realm.

Even stranger are the glimpses of geometry-shifting forms. They appear where the architecture folds back on itself, at those spots where leaves merge in impossible loops or corridors realign with bizarre topological contortions. A flicker at the edge of sight, a trembling shape that might be as large as a person—or as small as a cat, or might exist in multiple scales at once. Some travelers have named them the Impossi-things. They almost shimmer like reflections glimpsed through warped glass, never quite locking into a singular shape. At times, they seem part of the structure itself—an extension of bark or vine that wavers, extrudes a limb, then recedes into the curling geometry. If you approach them, they slip behind the nearest fold of space or twist into a corner that shouldn’t exist. In the rare event someone has come face to face with one, they claim it has more eyes than can be counted, but that the eyes rearrange themselves constantly—like shifting rivets on a living blueprint.

And sometimes in the arching expanses—a place that could be a fusion of a colonnade and an atrium of leaves—there appear fleeting shapes with wings reminiscent of membranes or tinted glass, whose bodies quake with a color cycling reminiscent of the canopies’. Call them Pollen Eaters or Dream Kites, depending on the myths swirling among wanderers. They cluster in small flocks during those swirling storms of luminescent pollen, inhaling the luminous motes through tiny vents along their elongated faces, feeding on the very glow that the entire environment exudes. Then, just as quickly, they disperse, drifting to far reaches of the labyrinth, dissolving into the green glow of leaf and steel. Their presence always precedes a wave of color change in the leaves, as though they trigger or accelerate the seasonal pulses that sweep across the canopy.

What do these creatures subsist on, in a place without water? They draw energy from the environment’s complex web—electric pulses in vines, chemical compounds exhaled by the leaves, particulates carried by the faintly humming ventilation. Each lifeform is a puzzle piece fitted into the complex tapestry of synergy: the Lattice Moths glean small charges from the corridors; the Builders manipulate the architecture’s reconfigurations; the Crenellated Vipers shuttle energy overhead; and the Impossi-things lurk in quantum folds, bridging the logic that keeps the entire place from collapsing under its own impossible angles. Then there are outliers—forms so elusive that sightings are only rumors: rumored fishlike creatures that swim through unseen channels of plasma, or serpent-lizards that dwell entirely in the metal scaffolds, living off minimal interference patterns in the planetary engine’s hum.

In the sub-level recesses that smell of powdery resin and faint ozone, you might catch a hush-laugh from something lurking at the boundaries: it echoes through the fibrous walls like a breeze that never quite arrives. These are the rumored Whispers, intangible as echoes in an empty corridor, perhaps not corporeal at all. Some say they are the souls of the place, ephemeral consciousness wrought from the interplay of living roots and industrial struts. Others claim they are maintenance drones, intangible illusions used by an ancient, abiding intelligence that oversees the realm’s engineering. One traveler insisted they appear as filaments of light if you manage to see them head-on, each glowing thread traced with the looping script of a language older than physical matter. But no one can confirm these stories, for the Whispers dissipate whenever approached, their presence a half-felt tingle across the skin.

Who built them, or if they built themselves, is an open question. The realm feels like it was forged by a species that favored symbiosis between biology and machinery—perhaps a long-extinct civilization that left behind their living factory, and these creatures are all that remain of their grand experiment. Or maybe these inhabitants arrived later, opportunists drawn to the husk of a planet-sized engine. They might have adapted over millennia, each species finding a niche within the leaf-metal labyrinth, forging a silent contract of co-existence with the realm’s self-sustaining systems.

And though their shapes are distinct, there’s a unifying hush about them. None cry out in typical animal calls; none scurry about with the chatter of small mammals. Even the ones with wings barely rustle. Instead, they converse by chemical signals, micro-vibrations, or pulses of electric glow. The entire living-mechanized environment, from trunk to vine to the creatures themselves, participates in a code of subtle rhythms. If you stand long enough in one spot, you might sense it in the hush: a near-subsonic wave that travels across the floor of knotted stems, a ripple in the canopy overhead, and a shift in the posture of the arcs and branches. Maybe that’s how these residents communicate—through pulses in a web of conductive bark, the hum of fans that cycles in perfect synchrony with the fractal highways of growth.

After countless hours—or days, or however time functions here—you begin to see the patterns in that silence. The Lattice Moths flit toward power conduits when the Builders start rearranging a corridor. The Crenellated Vipers coil in the canopy just before a wave of color flows overhead. The swirling storms of pollen brew far off in the labyrinth, and the Dream Kites ride them, luminous and ephemeral. The Impossi-things appear in the corner of your vision at the same junctures, bridging or smoothing out the weird angles of reality. Everything is interconnected, each lifeform a gear or circuit in the realm’s grand mechanism.

And so, although you see them only in glimpses—rare, fleeting, half-lost in the living structure’s immensity—they are here. Many, many kinds, all but silent, each adapted to a genius of engineering that defies normal biology. Their eyes, wings, or roots glean energy from the dryness, from the bright, unearthly light, from the quiet hum of vents. They inhabit the archways and sub-level vaults, the overhead canopies and the labyrinthine corridors, caretakers or symbionts of a place that itself is neither fully alive nor purely a machine. And perhaps that is the ultimate truth: they are the children of an environment that transcends the usual definitions of nature or technology, each inhabitant the living echo of a world that built itself—and continues to do so—endlessly, silently, always in motion, weaving new forms as it goes.
