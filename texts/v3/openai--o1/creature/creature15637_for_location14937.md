They move in small clusters that break and rejoin as their paths thread through the long, vaulted corridors. At first, you might mistake them for shadows thrown by the flickering lights—tall and willowy, with limbs so spare they nearly vanish in profile. Yet each figure is a solid presence, draped in layered cloth or flexible metallic wraps, hooded in folds of faintly gleaming material that helps them blend with the black stone and shifting gloom. The cloth breathes, sighs, and seems to ripple with the city’s hum, acting like a second skin to shield them from the clinging moisture and drifting fungal spores.

When they stand still, they seem to merge with the ancient architecture, becoming yet more pillars among the columns and spires. But pause long enough, and you’ll see them peer from under those hoods—large, luminous eyes that reflect the embedded lights threading the walls. Their gaze glitters, a subtle sign of life in the hush of drifting condensation. They rarely speak aloud; you might only hear a medley of clicks or softly modulated hums if they need to communicate across the gloom. Otherwise, they converse through the tapping of rods or scepters—thin tubes hooked into the city’s power conduits, passing coded signals you can half-feel in your chest as pulses.

These beings—call them the Attuned, for that is how they seem to one another—are not the only residents of the sprawling labyrinth. You’ll see mechanical silhouettes drifting along walls or vaulted ceilings, each suspended on an invisible lattice of magnetics. Think of them as the city’s caretakers: sleek, multi-jointed constructs whose every movement is measured and quiet. Their bodies are plated in dark metal or resin-like coverings, a patchwork of smooth curves and sharper edges. Spidery limbs end in delicate pincers or optical sensors, ever scanning surfaces for stress fractures, clearing fungal overgrowth from vital joints and vents, or delicately redirecting rivulets of water when the equilibrium in some corridor tips askew. If glimpsed from the corner of your eye, they could be spiders… or, perhaps, robed attendants performing a silent vigil. Only their humming circuits and the faint red or blue glow of optical arrays give away their non-organic nature.

Side by side, these two groups—the Attuned and the caretakers—navigate the city’s corridors in a choreography that suggests ancient partnership. The Attuned stride with spindly grace and often seem free to roam, investigating chance phenomena (an unexplained swirl of watery mist in the rafters, or a newly formed crystalline deposit glinting in the corner of an unused walkway). The caretakers, perennially unhurried, are seen more often near the heart of the labyrinth, where the technology runs hottest and tightest. Occasionally, you’ll witness one caretaker approach an Attuned figure, stretching out a narrow limb to deliver something: a crystalline tool, perhaps a spool of flexible piping. No words exchanged, only a faint spark or nod. Then they part ways, each returning to their appointed tasks with an unspoken mutual awareness.

Lower down, near the swirling columns of geothermal extraction, you’ll find a different shape of life altogether—small, scuttling creatures that cling to the city’s warmest surfaces, feeding on the fungal growths in the half-hidden basins. These creatures are reminiscent of rodents, but elongated, hairless, and so pale they almost glow. Their eyes have narrowed to glittering black beads: they prefer darkness, and they flee from direct contact with the city’s mechanized spaces. They shy away from the steady radiance of the caretaker’s sensors, retreating into cracks and small burrows carved into the underside of the walkways. Rumor has it their whiskerlike antennae can detect subtle changes in the city’s power fields, allowing them to anticipate surges of energy or abrupt gravitational shifts. Locals refer to them as glow-rats or pale mice—though in shape they’re neither fully rodent nor insect, but an odd bridging of the two. 

Only the boldest or most desperate of these glow-rats dare to poke their snouts into the busier thoroughfares. They do so when some trick of the city’s machinery has produced a new water source or exposed fresh mineral deposits. In the shimmering gloom, you might see them gnawing on mineral-laced fungus, eyes darting around for passing Attuned or caretakers. But rarely do either group interfere; it’s as though an unspoken truce exists—a quiet acceptance that each occupant of the city has a role to play in the precarious ecosystem.

And then there are the watchers in the domed doorways. You’ll only see them in near-silhouette, standing behind translucent holographic script that flickers in the threshold of their chambers. These watchers are seldom seen outside their sanctums, and if you do catch a glimpse, they appear wreathed in streams of luminous code that arc over their shoulders and around their torsos. Even the Attuned, with their reflective eyes, approach these doorways with caution or reverence—rarely crossing the boundary uninvited. Some watchers appear fully human in outline, others elongated or strangely segmented, their limbs elegantly twisting like the city’s cables. Their role seems to be the interfacing of mind with machine. The watchers are, at once, archivists and communicators, bridging the ancient technology with the living presences who walk the corridors.

Behind the watchers’ thresholds, you might hear layered pulses, quick staccato bursts, or the distant whir of data-laden fans. In those private domains, ideas become tangible illusions drifting through the air like glowing filaments. Those illusions mimic spiraling starfields, or geometric designs that overlay the city’s plans, or cryptic images of past eras. A hush surrounds these watchers: the hush of a library or laboratory. Deeper in, one might find them seated before banks of shifting glyphs, eyes closed in silent communion with the city’s archaic systems.

At intervals, larger gatherings congregate around massive, gearlike rostrums in the central hub—Attuned, caretakers, watchers, and the occasional unclassifiable figure (sometimes with unusual accessories or half-mechanical limbs). They assemble, each finding a spot on the worn stone or a jutting ledge, and let the rostrums resonate with repeating cycles of low vibrations. There is no straightforward conversation here; it’s more like a communal attunement, a combined calibration of the city’s humming heart. You might see watchers intensifying the holographic swirl, caretakers adjusting the subsonic pitch of the gears, the Attuned pressing their scepters to the floor so that the pulses travel through them, uniting them in a single resonant moment. Then, just as silently, they disperse, returning to the endless corridors and spires.

Beyond these principal inhabitants—Attuned, watchers, caretakers—one also hears rumors of solitary creatures deeper still. Some speak of a serpentine presence that hunts along the lesser-lit margins of the labyrinth, feeding on an unknown energy that seeps from the half-finished expansions. Others say entire colonies of fungus-based life-forms exist in remote pockets, grown sentient by centuries of exposure to the city’s power fields. Truth is elusive; rumor finds fertile ground in the labyrinth’s shadows. But nearly all inhabitants respect the city’s living mechanics: whether mechanical or organic, each being finds a niche within the warm, pulsing, echoing realm. 

In the end, to see “who lives here” is to see a dynamic, delicately balanced system at work: robed Attuned navigating labyrinthine walkways, mechanical caretakers scuttling overhead, watchers aligning mind and metal from their threshold chambers, glow-rats gleaning life from hidden fungus, and a swirl of more mysterious influences lurking in unvisited corners. This place is not simply occupied by a single civilization but shaped and sustained by a shared presence—technology and biology woven together in an ongoing dance, each occupant relying on the city’s elusive energies while feeding them in turn. In the hush and clangor and flicker of impossible light, life in this subterranean metropolis is subtle yet intensely real, a silent synergy bridging ancient architecture, advanced machinery, and the quiet, persistent spark of consciousness.
