They come in many forms, in many silhouettes shifting behind the drifting steam and overgrown vines. One might think, at first glance, that it’s just humans living here—stoop-shouldered laborers in heavy coats, pulling cargo across wet cobblestones. And yes, there are plenty of those, trudging through the city’s industrial districts, wiping condensation from their brows. Yet a closer look reveals that not all of them are precisely human, or if they are, then humanity here has splintered into subtle variations. Some faces bear unusual pallor, the cold-damp climate leaving them bone-white and wan; others display faint traces of hybridity, as if the city’s porous boundaries between the mechanical, the organic, and something else have begun to blur in their very physiology. 

Children run along the walkways in groups, squealing as they chase each other between dripping pipes and along slick catwalks. You see they’re smaller than you might expect, stunted, perhaps, by the heavy metals in the air or an adaptation to the labyrinthine spaces. They wear patchwork clothes—stitched leathers that look as though they’re handed down from generation to generation, with improvised hoods that can be pulled over their faces when the rains come. Their boots splash in shallow puddles of runoff, and they hop across twisted metal lumps that may once have been scaffolding. Sometimes you glimpse an odd spark in their eyes, as though the flicker of overhead cables has found its way into their pupils—charged with the city’s restless energy.

Meanwhile, in the narrower alleyways, older folk stand by barrel-fires (when they can light them) to fend off the chill. Their breath mists before them as they speak of changes in the city—sections that were there one day, gone the next. Some of them swear they’ve felt the ground shift beneath their feet. They mutter about ‘soft spots’ or ‘ghost alleys’ as if these phenomena are as ordinary as the next steam vent. You notice the metal of their everyday objects—cups, tools, even cutlery—has begun to corrode at the edges, or sprout tiny metallic filaments that coil and twist like vines. They force nervous laughs, unsure whether these growths are a sign of environmental infection or of some deeper, more arcane metamorphosis at play.

Closer to the industrial centers, specialized workers inhabit blocky dormitories attached to factories by enclosed catwalks. They wear sturdier coats, reinforced with riveted plates, to protect themselves from stray sparks. Their gloves are thickly padded, the fingertips worn shiny from handling pressurized hoses and wrenches. The factories’ constant hum thrums through their bones, so they communicate in clipped, practical phrases. Oil-stained rags hang from their belts; iron lunchboxes clank against tool sets in the gloom of half-lit corridors. Now and then, alarm sirens or a steam-whistle blast above their heads remind them that the systems require perpetual vigilance—an endless dance of checks and recalibrations, a ceaseless attempt to steer the city’s expansions before it overtakes them.

Then there are the drifters, the scavengers: individuals who see the city more like an ever-renewing puzzle box. They roam through half-buried tunnels and old construction zones, prying free scraps of metal or shards of rock newly exposed by shifting ground. Some try to sell their finds at makeshift markets that spring up under the cover of catwalks—these markets are full of improvised stalls where flickering bulbs cast everything in jaundiced light. Here, you’ll encounter all manner of contraptions: the husks of mechanical limbs, thick glass tubes filled with a faintly luminous sludge, partially living “bio-wiring” teased from cracks in the old labyrinth. Buyers haggle in hushed voices, exchanging government scrip or handmade tokens minted by local guilds. They tear chunks from a loaf of spongy bread (grown from some arcane fungal process) and observe each other warily from behind goggles that glisten with collected condensation.

Beyond the human(ish) inhabitants, the city teems with other life—some of it not obviously derived from Earth’s usual biology. In the damp cracks of cinderblock walls, squat amphibian-like creatures nestle in clusters, their skin slimy and translucent, throats ballooning as they croak in watery undertones. They scuttle out when no one’s watching to hunt the elongated rodents that leap like tiny spring-heeled acrobats from pipe to pipe. These rodents have large, luminous eyes (perhaps an adaptation to the perpetual half-light), and their limbs bend in ways that seem just a bit off. Some scavenge scraps of metal or nibble on the brittle filaments sprouting from corroded surfaces; whether they’re drawn by the taste of the metal or glean some nutrient from it is anyone’s guess.

High overhead, crouched on iron rafters, black-plumed waterbirds perch in scattered flocks. Their feathers glint with moisture, and they occasionally clack their beaks in the hush between rains. Their calls—low, resonant squawks—echo through the metal canyons. Locals say the birds are good omens; others claim they’re harbingers of structural weakness, drawn to the sound of failing beams or shifting foundations. Some swear the birds gather at sites of imminent collapse. Because the city is always moving, you can find fresh rubble and half-crushed catwalks from time to time—perhaps the birds sense the next place where the ground will buckle. Occasionally, you catch them feasting on the pale mushrooms that bloom in the gloom.

Below street level, labyrinthine waterways and drainage ducts house entire ecosystems of their own. There, you might see the glint of quicksilver fish cutting through algae-ridden runoff, or pale eel-like creatures that slip silently through deeper channels. Rumor has it some watery predators lurk further down, behind heavy sluice gates in older parts of the city—unrecorded amphibian giants whose low thrumming calls can shake the walls if you stand close enough. Engineers in watertight overalls sometimes vanish for hours or days while investigating blockages or minor floods, returning with tales of subterranean flora that glow with softly pulsing light.

And then there are those who dwell at the very edge of the city’s official maps: the rumored tinkerers, or possibly renegade engineers, who stay hidden within half-abandoned towers. They scavenge from the old manufacturing lines, forging new devices that fuse with the city’s myriad pipes and cables in bizarre, vine-like tangles. Some say these outcasts knowingly expand the city’s metal sprawl under the cover of night, leading to those sudden, inexplicable surges of scaffolding or entire districts that appear in the space of a few days. Whether they’re part of a grand design, or whether each is merely following a private obsession, remains a mystery. The results of their efforts often show up in the neglected corners: growths of twisted steel and plating that arch like living branches, opening new pathways or swallowing old ones.

In the midst of so much mechanical and biological interweaving, the city teems with transitional figures: half-starved dreamers who claim to see patterns in the drip of condensation, machinists who implant themselves with small gear-driven gadgets (supposedly to regulate body temperature), cryptic monks who study the resonance of whistling steam lines. Some proclaim an emerging religion around the city itself—treating it as a living entity that grows, breathes, and might one day speak. You might catch them chanting at the foot of new structural growths, tracing runic patterns on corroded metal surfaces, their eyes alight with the fervor of discovery. Whether their devotion hastens or hinders the city’s transformations is unclear.

Altogether, it’s a place where life—human, animal, fungal, and mechanical—melds in uneasy union. Individuals and creatures both toughen and adapt, forging alliances out of necessity, forming enclaves in the half-finished structures or ephemeral corridors. Even as the city’s design remains unsettled, so too do the inhabitants remain in flux: forging families or alliances, adopting pieces of the city’s technology into their daily habits or bodies, sometimes drifting off into blank spots on the map. All the while, new arrivals wander in from the outer wetlands, drawn by the rumor of work or the pull of curiosity. They find a place that is cold and wet, yet alive with possibility, a place that breathes steam and hums with a quiet power.

In the end, there is no single inhabitant or species to sum it all up. Rather, this metropolis-cum-wetland fosters an entire spectrum of beings—some common and everyday, some mutated or spliced by unknown forces, some entirely foreign. And each of them reflects the city in miniature: half-formed, shifting, carrying intermingled threads of industry, biology, and whispered rumor. It’s a tapestry of many shapes and voices, all of them damp and cold and undeniably alive, forging onward in this embryonic sprawl that is still deciding what it wants to become.
