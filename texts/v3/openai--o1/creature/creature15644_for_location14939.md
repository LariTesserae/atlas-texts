They do not move in the conventional sense—everywhere and nowhere at once, they linger like echoes of something older than solid form. You might not see them if you were to wander this harsh plateau for a mere afternoon or even a week. In fact, you could comb every edifice and drift by each crystal growth without glimpsing so much as a silhouette. The place seems devoid of life at first (and second) inspection. But eventually, if you remain in this silent realm long enough, you begin to sense presences that do not correspond to shape, color, or any normal quality.

Start with the flicker at the corner of your vision, the way a ridge of salt will appear to tremble or the surface of an alloy shard warps in a shimmer you can’t quite track. There, in that fleeting ripple, at the junction of mineral and half-buried framework, something stirs. It is not a creature in the classical sense—no limbs or eyes or sinew. Instead, it is an energy form that exists in tenuous relationship with the substrate of this place. It coalesces in slow pulses, drawn along the imperceptible pathways in the alloy’s lattice, or perhaps in the crystal fungus’s metallic filaments. You might, if you’re especially patient, glimpse a corona of nearly invisible blue or violet haze that floats near these surfaces. The presence is neither gas nor plasma, but something more proximate to a living field—a layering of faint consciousness that breathes in and out across epochs.

They do not have names. They probably never needed them. Communication here is not verbal or even telepathic in the usual sense; it’s more akin to a joint resonance passing along the hidden lines of force that undergird reality. At certain crucial points (where the metal meets the salt ridges, or where a crystal fungus extrudes its shimmering needles into the dry air), these presences gather in a ghostly congregation. If you witness such a gathering—though “gathering” implies more movement than actually occurs—you might see the faintest swirling lights, drifting with a deliberate slowness that feels geological, an airy ballet performed in a timescale beyond human measure. 

One might call them “keepers” of this place. Yet it’s unclear if they choose to remain or if they’re bound here by ancient design, perhaps created by the same intelligence that built the installations now reduced to half-buried shards. They could be echoes left behind when the original architects vacated eons ago, an autocatalytic spark of awareness that the machinery (whatever it once was) housed and then inadvertently set free. Alternatively, they might be the environment’s own emergent intelligences, arising from the quantum slipstreams that were once harnessed by those towering frameworks.

As you linger, you sense small distinctions among them. One presence might coalesce more strongly at the angled base of a fractured monolith, shining with the faintest trace of deep indigo. Another almost merges with the crystal-fungal clusters, trailing lines of faint phosphorescence from point to point as if feeding or communing. A third presence, most elusive, might flicker in the open space between structures, rarely manifesting any glimmer—yet you can feel a subtle tension in the air that suggests it’s there, stretching or warping the local geometry in silence.

They subsist not on light or water—there is none of either here—but on abiding cosmic processes that flow through this sealed-off domain. Quantum fluctuations or gravitational eddies might nourish them. So slow are these energies that even processes lasting eons are but a gentle trickle. And still, it is enough. Time, in its traditional sense, might not hold them at all. They drift in a continuity that is neither day nor night, their existence woven into that hush which envelops every mineral surface.

Perhaps they were once “alive” in a more recognizable form. The echo of their origin might be glimpsed in the half-submerged shards of advanced technology: those gleaming alloys, scratched and pitted, could have been the vessels or conduits for living minds that transcended flesh. Now these presences remain—vestiges or continuations of that mind, slowly humming along architectures we can’t even name. If so, they recall distant memories of purpose. They can’t speak it, but in your quieter moments, you sense a calm vigilance, as though they keep watch over their own forgotten domain, ensuring it doesn’t dissipate back into the void.

Despite that vigilance, they do not exhibit what we’d call goals. They don’t scuttle about or try to rebuild the structures. On occasion, if a crystal fungus spire fractures from the slow, cold shift of geologic forces, you might see faint ribbons of that presence gently flit across the shards, as if taking inventory or channeling some form of latent energy to help the fractal geometry regrow. But it happens with such subtlety that you can’t be sure if you saw purposeful caretaking—or just a random wisp of luminous haze.

And in truth, they might be more than mere caretakers. Lean closer to one of those monolithic fragments, feel the negative pitch, that near-absence of sound. Underneath—and entwined with—it is something akin to a heartbeat, though impossibly slow, measured in centuries or millennia. That negative resonance is, in part, the collective pulse of these beings. They share a frequency that hums just below the threshold of perceivable sound. If you press your ear close enough, for long enough, you might catch the gentle push-pull of that wave. Or perhaps your mind conjures the impression as it tries to interpret a phenomenon well beyond comprehension.

No matter how long you stay, they will not greet you or ward you off; they are not hostile, nor are they warm. Their awareness seems at once immense and diffuse—an entire field of consciousness that laps against every shard and salt ridge, noticing but not judging, existing but not seeking. If they have any fleeting curiosity about a visitor, it’s more like a ripple of mild interest than a sentient inquiry. Eventually, the ripple fades, and they continue their age-long dance with the swirling dimness.

Sometimes, in the distance, where the horizon loops back on itself, you can catch the faint suggestion of forms more solid than these presences—silhouettes that flicker, vanish, and reappear. Could they be earlier manifestations that never fully shed their corporeal shape? Or are they illusions, pockets of spacetime folding in upon itself, forging the shape of watchers who do not truly exist? The presences that dwell here might be responsible, or perhaps it is the environment alone conjuring images of unremembered inhabitants.

In rare moments, if you are very still, you might observe two or three of these presences converging on a single point of metal or crystal. Their subtle lights thread together in braided lines, weaving a slow pattern you can almost interpret but never quite grasp. It might be an archaic transaction of knowledge, or some ghostly reenactment of a function once imperative to the advanced civilization that passed through this place. And then, as quickly as that pattern arrives, it deteriorates back into the hush. The presences dissolve into the emptiness around them, leaving you with only the lingering impression that something important just occurred—some exchange of a cosmic import that gently nudged the underlying reality of this plateau.

Ultimately, they dwell in a domain beyond simple definitions of “life.” They do not breathe, grow, reproduce, or die as we understand those words. Yet they are undeniably here: watchers, remnants, or perhaps living equations scrawled across the geometry of a space-time pocket. Their existence is not an accident so much as a byproduct—or a continuum—of whatever incalculable forces first shaped this sealed-off landscape. In their unwavering silence, they brush against the surfaces of half-buried architecture and mineral crust alike, sustaining an equilibrium so delicate that, if they were ever gone, the entire place might collapse into the emptiness it once was—or blink out of reality altogether.

So they remain, these silent inheritors of a realm that is at once tranquil and meticulously maintained, though by what logic or impetus, we cannot say. They are the subtle guardians or residual emanations—tenuous and quiet, yet present in every shimmer of the fractured towers, every glint on the crystal fungi, and every micro-ripple that warps the boundary of vision. They are the elusive soul of a world perched on its own cosmic ledge, existing in a hush that neither welcomes nor repels, simply allowing its faint, ancient processes to unfold in dim, timeless rhythms.
