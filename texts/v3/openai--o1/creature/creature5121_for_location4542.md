Sometimes, at first glance, it can feel as though nothing lives here, as though the land itself has swept any would-be inhabitants out into the cold expanse. Yet if you pause and watch carefully, or if by chance you linger in the hush of one of the great courtyards, you may begin to sense presences that do not reveal themselves outright. It would be too simple to say they are just people tucked away in the tunnels or small creatures hidden among the moss. They are more complicated than that—more varied, more deeply woven into the suspicious geometry of the place.

One might imagine that the oldest dwellers venture out only on the nights when storms roll in. The distant lightning frames their silhouettes against the battered slopes, in fleeting bursts. They seem tall but stooped—as though carrying the burden of ages on their backs—and their limbs appear jointed in ways that defy an easy count. They do not hurry; in fact, they move with a patience that suggests time is nearly meaningless to them. When the lightning ceases, these figures blend back into the gloom of the city’s blocky terraces, archways, and half-subterranean passages. Perhaps they are keepers of the knowledge etched into the labyrinth walls, or perhaps they are the knowledge itself—able to shift and realign like the flickering geometry.

Yet such figures are not all there is. In the clear midday hours, when the thin sun filters through high clouds to cast an even wash of light, you might glimpse smaller, almost drifting shapes near the damp basins. They could be akin to insects, yet they move with the languid bob of floating seeds. Sometimes they hover above the reflective droplets of water, stirring the surface into tiny ripples as if tasting the condensation for minerals or else performing some quiet pollination among the translucent mosses. Soft pulses glow within their lucent bodies, each pulse a traveling spark, as though they store small charges of static from the windy air. Then, with some hidden signal, they vanish among the vines that creep along the stone, or they slip through a gap in the rock, leaving the water surface undisturbed once more.

Meanwhile, in those corridors carved deeply under the crust, you might find the city’s “architects”: not necessarily the original builders, but beings intimately tied to the structures’ continuing transformation. They dwell in the twisting alleys and tomb-like passages where flickering inscriptions line the walls. They have a peculiar habit of tracing those inscriptions with elongated fingers—claws, maybe, or chisel-like appendages—encouraging the geometry to shift again, as if they are guardians of a living text. They might speak by pressing patterns onto the stones, each tap sending out a resonance that others can interpret. If you stand in silence, your breath condensed into a faint haze, you might hear the soft staccato of this tapping as it echoes through unseen halls. It’s unclear if they understand speech in any form you’d recognize; they could communicate strictly in those vibrational pulses, sometimes crossing into your hearing range as a low hum.

Less tangible inhabitants seem to reside in the warp itself: ephemeral shapes glimpsed as reflections in the half-frozen sheets of water. Look into a dull, icy mirror of a rock pool, and you might spot motion that does not line up with the swaying vegetation behind you. A shadow that belongs to no physical creature could drift across the stone, dissolving the moment you turn around. It’s as though fragments of old illusions or parallel versions of this place bleed into the present. Call them ghosts, though they may not be spirits of the dead so much as echoes of possible realities—realities that the land, drifting at the edge of normal physics, can’t help but half-manifest.

Nor are all the living shapes in motion. Some dwell in a state of near-stasis within the thickest vines. If you were to peel away layers of fibrous growth, you might find clusters of embryonic forms: half-plant, half-animal, or perhaps neither. Suspended in the translucent pulp, they appear to rest for centuries at a time, drawing nourishment from the rich mineral condensation. A subtle heartbeat or quiver of an eyelid might indicate potential consciousness. These forms could be a future generation, gestating until the conditions shift—until the storms grow more frequent, or the temperature changes, or something triggers a collective emergence. It’s anyone’s guess what shape they might take once they wriggle free from the vines.

In certain corners of the city, deeper than the sun can reach, there are rumors of massive, slow-moving shapes that exhale clouds of vapor. They appear to be fused with the stone itself, as though they began as lumps of bedrock that somehow awakened. People who have caught a glimpse describe them as “colossal golems” covered in patches of luminous moss, as if the same vegetation that carpets the slopes has found a new host. Whether these shapes possess minds or simply uphold the architecture by propping ancient ceilings with their broad backs is unclear. They might roam along the labyrinth’s lower levels, silently brushing aside rubble from collapsed corridors, ensuring the pathways stay passable—if only to maintain this city for some purpose that has long since been forgotten by mortal memory.

Then there is the vegetation itself, which in this place seems to tread the boundary between flora and something more sentient. When the wind gusts across those plateau stretches, the vines and translucent reeds bend in a wave, releasing not just a rustle but a chord—a resonance that suggests communication on a grand, networked level. In the stillness after, the entire place seems to respond: you can almost feel a faint thrumming in the bedrock. Are these plants playing sentinel for other life forms? Are they warning of storms, or sending signals to those luminous insects, or calling to the ghost-like echoes that traverse the corridors? Possibly all of the above, or something else entirely beyond our comprehension. 

There remain tales, too, of visitors—or, at least, of wanderers who appear and disappear, leaving footprints that lead nowhere or meander out of sight, as if swallowed by the dimension-shifts in the doorways. Perhaps these are travelers from another time, or from an offshoot dimension that occasionally overlaps with this one. They might be explorers who discovered a crack in reality, stepped through, and now walk these ruins collecting relics or knowledge. Some say they vanish as quickly as they arrived, carrying with them shards of the swirling anomalies that warp the city’s geometry.

Altogether, what lives here is not a uniform population but a mosaic of existences—some fully corporeal, others perched on the edge of reality, others integrated so fully with the environment that distinguishing them from stone or moss becomes nearly impossible. They coexist mostly without strife, each sustaining its peculiar niche. The tall, stooped forms prowling the terraces step carefully around the pools of sentient seedlings. The luminous insect-like shapes flit from vine to vine, pollinating or distributing subtle charges. And over it all, the warp pulsing through corridors, doorways, and carved inscriptions reminds every living thing that nothing, not even the ground beneath, is entirely fixed.

So the place might seem empty at first, but in truth it teems with life so well-adapted and subtle that it rarely calls attention to itself. The land’s grand contradictions—its ancient, sprawling city and half-breathing geology, its sudden illusions and quiet unstoppable growth—continue to nurture these elusive denizens. Stand in a courtyard at dusk or dawn, and you might feel their presence pressing in from all around, like a collective hush. They are the watchers, the caretakers, the echoes, and the seeds of potential. They are the ghosts of vanished worlds (or yet-to-come worlds), the inhabitants who have learned to occupy a realm where vastness meets thin air and where existence itself flickers at the edges. They live here, in a place at once too big and too ethereal, fulfilling some secret role in the unending tapestry of this reality on the cusp.
