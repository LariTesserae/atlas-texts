They slip in and out of corners without fanfare, these half-seen shapes. At first, you only catch suggestions of them—a limb or two at the edges of your peripheral vision, the trailing hiss of breath echoing off the damp walls. Look again, and they’ve retreated into darkness. Yet the evidence of their presence is layered into every arch and corridor. Someone built these passages, hammered mortar into place with patient strokes, set blocks of mineral shards so that each corridor merges with the next in a grand, unfinished puzzle. The labyrinth stands as a living testament to them, to the quiet intensity of their labors.

When you linger long enough, especially in one of the broader chambers where the overhead vault feels lost in blackness, you start to sense different types of inhabitants. In some ways, it’s like an ecosystem that’s still forming along with the architecture.

—

The Makers  
They are tall but stooped, elongated arms straining under the quiet weight of their craft. Little is known of their faces—too often, they keep hoods or bits of damp cloth pulled around their visages, or they skulk within the corridors’ gloom. They walk on two legs but place their palms against the walls as they go, feeling for subtle shifts in temperature, humidity, or resonance. Much of their communication is tactile rather than vocal. You might see one press a splayed hand against the masonry, then feel a gentle thrum course through the stone around you—a message transmitted in vibrations.  

They carry simple implements: chisels chipped from the very minerals they shape, carved stone hammers with fiber-wrapped handles, slender rods for measuring and marking. Their footprints—long, narrow, and often accompanied by smeared traces of mortar—crisscross the corridors, mapping the ceaseless wanderings of those who construct and reconstruct the labyrinth’s bones. When they do speak, it’s in low, humming tones that resonate with the churn of water overhead. The echoes transform in the corridors, turning each phrase into a riddle: sometimes only the final note of a word reaches you, rising or falling to mark meaning you can barely glean.

Their motives remain enigmatic. Partly, you suspect they build because the labyrinth is their lifeblood, a communal expression of being. Each damp corridor, every newly chipped arch, is an extension of their communal identity. They appear never to rest for long; at odd hours, you might hear the dull clang of a mallet or the rasp of a chisel gnawing at stone. Night or day holds little distinction here—only the cycles of water and mortar setting determine their rhythm.  

It’s said the Makers are drawn to any corner where the labyrinth is newest: a half-built wing, an unsealed chamber. There they gather and work in near silence, calibrating each rough edge with surprising delicacy. Sometimes, you see faintly glowing spores collecting on their shoulders—residue from the fungal protrusions. They do not seem to clean it away; it’s merely part of the ongoing synergy between them and this damp world they’re constructing.

—

The Lurkers  
Once in a while, you’ll catch the scuttling silhouette of a Lurker—a creature with four, maybe six, impossibly jointed limbs. It clings to the damp stone, head cocked at an awkward angle to peer through translucent compound eyes. Its frame is all ridges and angled planes, like living fractals molded out of the labyrinth’s own mineral shards. It might hiss—a faint, static-like sound—and then vanish into a vertical crack in the walls.

The Lurkers seem less like builders and more like opportunists or gleaners, surviving off the labyrinth’s transitional energies. They gnaw at fresh mortar, extracting minerals or bacterial traces that feed the fungal ridges. Occasionally, you may glimpse them in small groups, perched around one of the shallow basins of water, drinking or lapping at the moisture with quick, needle-thin tongues. But as soon as the sound of clinking stone signals a Maker’s approach, they retreat, melding with the shadows.  

Some suspect the Lurkers are older than the labyrinth. Perhaps they roamed an underworld of ephemeral tunnels and found a new territory here, wedging themselves into the spaces between forming blocks. Others believe that the Lurkers arrived alongside the labyrinth’s birth, driven by the same impulse that churns the environment. Either way, they move in pulses—like a fluid, collectively shifting in and out of new corridors, never fully settling in a single place.

—

The Glimmers  
Those pale, bioluminescent fungal growths might not be mere plants but living, collective entities. Some watchers call them the Glimmers. At a glance, they appear rooted in cracks between blocks, slender filaments pooling with wet luminescence. But if you pause in a corridor where they thrive—and watch carefully—the Glimmers will pulse or drift in a subtle dance. They gather condensation, capturing droplets in tiny cup-like protrusions, then sway or contract to pass water along to new growths.  

Occasionally, you’ll find a patch of them projecting peculiar shapes—a half-formed swirl, a wave-like ridge, or something that seems almost geometric. In these shapes, the Glimmers might be echoing the labyrinth’s architecture, like a natural parallel to the Makers’ manual craftsmanship. Or maybe they’re just random patterns in the gloom. Whatever the case, these small effusions of pale light add a spectral glow to the corridors. The Lurkers and the Makers alike seem to treat them with caution, never hacking them away but skirting around them or gently brushing them aside.  

A rumor circulates that the Glimmers are the labyrinth’s memory—that they store, in their vibrant veins, the blueprint for every newly formed passageway. If you stare too long, you imagine you see slow wave patterns of knowledge drifting from one node to another, an organic map of the entire subterranean sprawl.

—

The Watchers  
Every so often, in the near-dark, you spot a figure of spindly design, taller even than the Makers. Their limbs are somewhat skeletal in silhouette, but the details remain elusive. They stand at the margins of a corridor or across a damp courtyard, observing from the gloom. You get only the briefest glimpse—then they fade back into blackness without a sound, leaving behind a faint scraping that might be metallic or might be the grind of shifting stones.

No one is certain if the Watchers sculpt, or tunnel, or gather. They do not appear to carry tools. Some say the Watchers oversee all the labyrinth’s processes, ensuring no corridor expands too fast, no basin overflows. Others claim the Watchers dwell deeper still, in half-lit halls next to broad pools, where they commune with the labyrinth’s hidden machinery—an ancient network of pumps or valves that coax water through the system in cyclical rhythms.

Perhaps the most unsettling theory contends that the Watchers are only partially physical, that they manifest out of the labyrinth’s architecture. When new corridors shift or entire sections realign overnight, you might catch a fleeting movement at the edges of your vision. Then the corridor ceases to shift, as though guided to its final position by these ephemeral beings. Are the Watchers illusions cast by the interplay of water, stone, and darkness? Or are they the hidden intelligence behind the labyrinth’s inexorable flux?

—

The Under-Churn  
Beneath all of them—a deeper stratum of life or mechanism—something rumbles. It could be monstrous living machines that the Makers rely upon, or a subterranean entity that spurs the labyrinth’s continuous growth. One theory holds that the labyrinth’s mortar is not entirely inert, that it’s seeded with an organic or mechanical agent that expands slowly from within, drawing power from the water’s flow. This Under-Churn remains unseen, trapped below the floors, but it’s the source of the thrumming vibrations you feel through the soles of your feet. It stirs the water in carefully orchestrated cycles, fosters new corridors to bud from old ones, and might even communicate with the Glimmers in pulses of subsonic sound.

From time to time, a corridor spontaneously shifts, as if extruded from unseen stock. Through cracks in the floor, you hear the clank of something rotating or grinding. The Under-Churn’s impetus is continuous: it neither sleeps nor pauses its grafting of stone upon stone. Some travelers suspect that should the Under-Churn ever halt, the labyrinth would collapse upon itself, starved of the energy that binds these damp, half-formed walls together.

—

Dynamics of Coexistence  
These inhabitants don’t share one language, yet they move in synergy, each taking advantage of the labyrinth’s transitional state. The Makers build, refining and adjusting the architecture’s features. The Lurkers scavenge and glean nutrients from fresh mortar seams and water channels. The Glimmers illuminate the labyrinth in hushed pulses, perhaps guiding or guarding knowledge of the structure’s form. The Watchers keep silent watch, slipping in and out like living silhouettes. Meanwhile, the labyrinth’s own heartbeat, the Under-Churn, keeps all in motion—perpetually coaxing new expansions.

You might go days without encountering a single living soul, lulled into the sense that you roam a stillborn city of rock and water. Then, suddenly, the echo of chisels and mallets arises, or the fleeting glimpse of an insectile body skittering across damp blocks. In glimpses and echoes, their story is told: they are creatures who dwell in flux, forging a place that never quite finishes being built. Every delicate bas-relief, every half-assembled arch, every softly glowing fungus is a sign of their devotion to shaping and reshaping this cool, watery underworld in an unending process of becoming.

They are part of the labyrinth, and the labyrinth is part of them—a woven tapestry of stone, water, and motion. They exist in small clusters or as solitary wanderers, each aligning with this newly born realm’s pulse. Whether or not they were here first, or arrived at its inception, or manifested from its living walls, no one truly knows. What is clear is that in every damp passage, you can feel their presence, sense their watchful eyes or the hush of their silent labors. And it is through these quiet yet ceaseless efforts that the chill, half-finished corridors continue to grow—layer upon layer, moment by moment, breath by breath.
