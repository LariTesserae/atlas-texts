At first, you would think no one lives here. On the wind-battered plateau, with its cold, wet hush and broad industrial labyrinth, there is not a flicker of movement that belongs to anything like a person or an animal. But if you linger—and that is key, to linger—you begin to sense that the city and its surrounding growth are not so devoid of occupants as they appear. There is a presence, some elusive occupant woven into each pipe, circuit, and cluster of moss. You notice it in the gaps: in the swirl of condensation that forms around a tower’s metallic spire, or at the edges of your vision when a cluster of vines quivers in a breeze that blows from nowhere.

You start by suspecting that these residents might be mechanical caretakers—machines scaled to inhabit the cavities and corridors of the factories like breath in a ribcage. Sometimes you think you catch them: a slick reflection along a serpentine conduit that recoils just as you focus, or the faint scuttling that resonates through the pipes at night. But when you hurry over to see more, you find only shining rivets and the same unyielding machinery, as though the structure itself had merely shuddered. It’s possible they are intangible or that they slip between the walls, crossing from one pipe to another with fluid ease, glimpsed only when a seam of reflected light catches them at just the right angle. These watchers, these ephemeral custodians, never draw attention to themselves. They have no reason to. The city’s processes keep running, maintained by an invisible hand.

Then you wonder if, instead, the living presence belongs to the vegetation. Look closely at a cluster of the faintly glimmering fronds in the gloom, and you’ll swear that one leaf is arching closer to you, as if bending in curiosity or for a better view. Sometimes, a patch of moss breathes—expanding in slow pulses that match the ceaseless thunder beneath your feet. It looks like the machinery’s hum has somehow synced with the mosses’ metabolism, weaving them all into a single living circuit. The vines that drape themselves over the city like living wires also seem to share in that pulsing rhythm; their roots latch onto metal and stone, not just for support but, it seems, to plug themselves into the industrial heartbeat. In the drifting night fog, these vines glow with a subdued inner luminescence, each tendril a living filament. Could the plateau’s flora be the truest inhabitants—quiet but endlessly vigilant in their slow and steady growth?

Yet, somewhere deeper, below the factories, you feel another kind of presence that defies both mechanical labeling and the notion of a mere plant. When you walk along the city’s periphery, you find sealed-off chambers cut into the bedrock. If you press your ear to a cold, wet steel panel, you can sense a throbbing not quite captured by the churn of heavy engines or the dripping water. It’s older, quieter, more measured. It’s as though the plateau itself—its juvenile crags, hidden aquifers, and labyrinthine channels—hosts a consciousness that silently attends to everything from below. The massive black-steel city, the vines, the dripping pools: all are part of its outward manifestation, an extension of something vast and continuous. Spend too long in the city’s catacombs, you might start to see flickering shapes formed by the interplay of shadows on the far walls. These might be illusions of watery reflections from the rivulets, or perhaps they are some semi-lucid echoes of this subterranean mind, casting silhouettes of what it dreams or remembers.

Stranger still, there are stories (anonymous graffiti scrawled on rusted railings, or half-buried placards in a language you can’t quite decipher) that speak of travelers who came through here long ago. These cryptic scribbles suggest that the city once had a more visible populace, or perhaps it served as a fortress for some far-flung civilization that harnessed the plateau’s unworldly energies. Yet all references to these travelers or inhabitants end abruptly, as if they vanished—folded back into the black iron and damp rock. Did the plateau subsume them, rendering them part of its sustaining cycle? Could their consciousness have migrated into the labyrinth of pipes and passages, merging with the city’s mechanical hum?

You begin to see glimpses of them in the faint shadows that shift along the catwalks. The shapes are almost humanoid, but elongated and thin, their proportions distorted by generations of living in high-altitude gloom. Their limbs blend seamlessly into the corners where metal meets stone, leaving behind trailing glimpses of half-visible joints or arc-segments of dull silver. Observing from a distance, you might catch one in a rare shaft of watery sunlight: it flashes a momentary reflection from something smooth on its body—skin, shell, or integrated plating—and then melts back into the gloom. Perhaps these figures live off the plateau’s lush greenery or from energy in the swirling corridors of steam. They drop no footprints, for the thick moss swallows any evidence of passage. Yet certain scuffs on a metal walkway, or a series of odd notches on the edges of a pipe, evoke sets of fingers or toes. Each mark suggests an uncanny melding of organic and synthetic form.

One night, leaning over a guardrail, peering into a wide ventilation shaft, you might see them more clearly. That is when they gather, they say, in the near-total darkness that only the faintly luminous flora can breach. You can sense their presence in that darkness before you see them: a tension in the air, a reversal in the soundscape, as if the eternal hum quiets just enough to let something else come forward. Then, inch by inch, the movement begins—vines curling in tandem, silent figures ascending from deeper levels of the metropolis. Shadows crossing shadows. If you strain your eyes, you may catch the glint of a metallic limb, something resembling a visor or mask, lit from within, shining gently through the surrounding fog. Though they move fluidly, one limb or torso segment occasionally reveals a rigid geometry reminiscent of the city’s architecture, so that you can never tell where the machinery ends and these beings begin.

All the while, the flora embraces them. Leaves brush against their frames as if in greeting, droplets roll downward to gather at their feet. They appear to feed the vines—possibly with chemical residues or minuscule metallic flecks they collect from the machinery—and in return, the vines sustain them with some byproduct of chlorophyll or spore-laden mist. Being entirely alive, or entirely metallic, or entirely plantlike—none of these definitions seem to fit. They are a blend, an outgrowth of the plateau’s equilibrium. Some even say they are the plateau. A species or community or single entity—no one can be quite sure—but something that perpetuates this permanent stasis, this hush that envelops the factories, the plateau, the green life, and the cosmic hum of industry.

As you stand at the edge, gazing into the city’s labyrinth, you sense that all of it—the metal, the moist air, the glistening flora, and these phantom-like shapes—forms a complete organism in slow, methodical operation. The city churns, and the plateau provides, while the watery pockets collect in rock folds, feeding the green that creeps over every boulder. The silent watchers, if that’s what they are, tend to it all in subtle ways, never drawing attention yet always fulfilling a function. Even the constant engines, that mechanical thunder in the deep, are but a heartbeat, ensuring that neither the flora nor these spectral inhabitants nor the city’s labyrinth of pipes ever falters.

So who or what truly lives here? Perhaps they are the plateau dreaming through machinery. Or perhaps the vegetation has coaxed old metal to life, forging new shapes from steel and spores. Or possibly the city’s subterranean energies have gestated entire lifeforms that maintain the place like a caretaker does a quiet estate. In the end, proof is elusive. You see no footprints in the moss, no lights burning in tower windows at night. Yet the unmistakable knowledge hangs in the fog: you are not alone. Every moss-laden pipe, every ring of glinting metal, and every hidden corridor hums with a subtle pulse. The plateau belongs to a living synergy—cold, damp, unwavering, and perfect in its intricate dance of plant and polished steel. And somewhere in that labyrinth of perpetual motion and green hush, the watchers go on as they always have, in a place beyond time, neither entirely seen nor wholly unseen—forever present in a land that refuses to change.
