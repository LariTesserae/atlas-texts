It may be that the first living forms one notices here are not separate from their environment at all. They might be the glowing filaments running like veins along the city’s walls—thin capillaries of turquoise or scarlet light, converging at junctures like nodes of a circulatory map. Upon closer inspection, these lights could prove more than simple fixtures or decorations: they breathe by slow, rhythmic pulses, and they ripple when you glide near, as if reacting to your presence. What if these glowing lines are the occupant-species themselves, their bodies braided through the architecture as living conduits of data or energy? They might experience their world through the surfaces they illuminate, in a communion of flesh and structure where wall, window, and inhabitant are one continuous form.

Others, you sense, might move through the watery depths just beyond sight. They flicker in the corners of your vision: subtle glints along the glassy surfaces. On those surfaces, you might notice an unusual pattern—tiny tessellations that shimmer and shift. In one moment, it looks purely geometric, but the next, you think you see the faint outline of an eye, or something akin to a mouth. Perhaps these creatures have learned to camouflage themselves so seamlessly against the translucent walls that their waking presence only appears for a second—like a shifting mosaic that rearranges itself as soon as you focus. They are watchers, but not in the predatory sense. Their gestures seem more curious, as if they are studying you just as you try to study them.

Then there are those who manifest in the drifting particulate. Pinprick motes of luminescence that appear to be single cells, or glowing machines, or something in between. You may at first assume they are aimless floaters, lacking consciousness. But on truly observing them, you notice patterns in their swirl: the way they assemble into swirling constellations that last only seconds, or how they respond to changes in the faint hum emanating through the city’s spires. This might be a form of communal intelligence—small, autonomous units meeting and parting in fractal rhythms, each humming with specialized knowledge. They could be the primary caretakers of the city, weaving unseen repairs, transmitting signals from tower to tower in a dance that defies ordinary senses.

In places where the walls rise into tall, slender spires, you might witness something that seems made of watery shadows. These shapes move with an unsettling grace, swirling upward like living vortices. Their edges waver between substance and emptiness, outlines shifting in ways that defy stable geometry. Yet their movement exudes a careful intention, as if each swirl is a silent conversation. They might be spirit-like embodiments of the fluid itself—residents of a dimension just adjacent, whose forms materialize in the fluid for mere instants. They never fully emerge into this world, but flicker—in one moment a swirling pattern of bubble-like shapes, in the next a ghostly framework reminiscent of wings or fins.

You might glimpse, too, more solid, robust shapes moving at the base of the city’s structures. Their outlines suggest something large, reminiscent of upright figures or massive crustaceans—patient, deliberate. Imagine a creature half cultured in advanced ceramics, half composed of living tissue, with muscular limbs extended from a shell that merges seamlessly into metallic plating. They move methodically, as though tending to tasks only they understand: adjusting a crystalline tile here, or realigning a shimmering conduit there. Possibly these beings are the architects—or their descendants—who created this place. Or perhaps they are only stewards, following innate directives bestowed by a civilization that has long since moved on.

Deeper within the labyrinthine halls that seem sealed by invisible membranes, you might find enclaves of residents who favor enclosed air and dryness in the midst of all this fluid. Whispered rumors of intricate dwellings behind the spires’ dark exteriors: corridors lined with the same translucent lattices, but filled with a gaseous atmosphere. Small collectives might gather there, their silhouettes humanoid or altogether alien, studying swirling holographic displays. Or perhaps the interiors are dormant, waiting for the day when these inhabitants step out to explore the watery realm beyond their shielded compartments. If you manage to peer through an opening or a seam, you might just detect movement inside—impressions of luminous eyes or metal-jointed limbs in the gloom.

Yet the strangest possibility is that this city’s truest inhabitants are not creatures in separate bodies at all, but exist everywhere at once. Imagine that the entire settlement, from its basalt-like foundation to its crystalline pinnacles, harbors a single, vast intelligence. This might be a mind that breathes in the watery hush, maintaining the equilibrium of these structures from within. If you place a hand on any surface, you might feel a gentle pulse, as though that intelligence is aware of you—curious, perhaps, or simply continuing its endless calculations. Then the lines of light flow a bit brighter, the hum in the water modulates. Communication, perhaps, without words.

In that sense, the city itself could be the occupant: the labyrinth of towers and open-sided spires are its limbs and organs, the faint glows its patterned nervous system, and all the drifting motes are like synaptic sparks that carry messages from one node to another. Every so often, forms come and go—reflections of that deeper presence, ephemeral manifestations of the city’s abiding mind. They need no separate classification as “animal” or “machine” because they embody both extremes. Their purpose is unknown; do they build, archive, create? The stillness suggests they have reached a perfect stasis: not asleep, but not bustling with activity. Existing in a calm, silent conversation with the watery world around them.

Yet, hints of movement remain: a swirl of luminous motes that might combine to become a living shape, an arm-like structure that emerges from the side of a tower before retracting. An echoing clunk that might be the city’s internal systems shifting. Nothing here is frantic; nothing rushes or flails. Every motion seems measured, almost ceremonial. If humans (or any visitors) wander through, they might find themselves watched in infinite ways that are too subtle for them to detect—a gentle, layered observation coming from every angle.

And so, the dwellers of this submerged plateau are manifold but never plainly revealed. Some are ephemeral flickers on the edge of vision, some are integrated so completely as to be indistinguishable from the architecture, and some might be sealed away in invisible enclaves. There may be hierarchies among them, or no hierarchy at all—only a shared communion in the humming silence. They do not appear menacing, nor overtly welcoming; they simply persist, part of a system that thrives in its own equilibrium.

When you leave, you are left with a sense that you have barely laid eyes upon them, despite their constant presence. They are watchers, stewards, ghosts, or a single entity—perhaps all at once. Like the shifting lines of light that trace the buildings, they blend seamlessly into the environment they have shaped, or that has shaped them. In their silent, measured existence, they offer neither invitation nor threat, only the promise that something profound stirs in the quiet spaces of this submerged city, something that belongs wholly to its own realm and might forever elude full understanding from those who look in from outside.
