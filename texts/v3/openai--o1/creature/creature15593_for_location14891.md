Some say that no true life could persist in such a place, an inert plateau choked under eternal darkness where even time seems to forget itself. Yet if you look closer, you’ll notice silhouettes moving behind riveted panels or down the recesses of metal corridors. Wherever such figures appear, they leave only the faintest trace in their wake—an almost inaudible scuff across the crust of black dust, or a ripple of receding warmth lingering in the air.

They are tall, or they seem so—though it’s difficult to tell because one’s eyes grow uncertain under this stagnant gloom. Their outlines have a certain elongated quality, like bodies stretched slowly over millennia, molded by the dryness and the hush. Their limbs could be bare, dust-caked metal, or perhaps heavily worn flesh wrapped in industrial coverings. No single glimpse ever quite assures you of what you saw. Instead, you recall impressions: angular protrusions at the joints, glints of reflection where a face might be. Occasionally you see a ragged hood or a bulky harness of tools strapped across a torso that flickers in and out of the hazy spotlights.

Most of the time, they move in purposeful silence, shifting across catwalks or trudging down sub-level ramps that spiral into the city’s deeper veins. Sometimes they pause to inspect a spinning pipe or read the tremors of an aging engine. They place careful hands—talons, or metal prongs, or delicate glove-like contraptions—on the old scaffolding, as though they can feel every vibration through their fingertips. Then, with an economy of movement, they tighten a bolt or re-weld a seam before ghosting away. The city’s industrial pulse depends on these watchers and fixers, yet few would suspect they exist at all. They seem to merge with the gloom the moment they stray from the sparse pools of light.

No one spies their faces clearly. A scant reflection might betray glimmering eyes—or something that, in another world, you might call eyes—but just as quickly it’s swallowed by the dust-laden dark. Their forms shift from mechanical to organic and back again in that half-light, so you’re left wondering whether they’re living beings adapted to an industrial life, or mechanical constructs that have developed something akin to a soul over the centuries. Or perhaps they are some hybrid in between. The dryness ensures that nothing decays quickly, so parts can remain functional well past ordinary lifespans. Maybe they have replaced their own failing limbs with salvaged machinery, forging a lineage of metal and flesh that has drifted away from its original humanity—if humanity was ever the right word.

In the hush of the upper walkways, you sometimes see them hunched at the edges, bestowing faint repairs on the centuries-old turbines that grind slowly but unerringly. The watchers place their ear close to the valves to catch the slightest hiss of escaping gas, or trace slender implements along metal surfaces to test for invisible cracks. When they find a weakness, their entire posture shifts: they reach out with single-minded care, placing coverage plates, dripping molten alloy that cools to form instant patches, or hooking up cables that pulse with a quiet, red glow. Over time, it becomes clear that each caretaker is not merely maintaining the city’s machinery but communing with it. The watchers stoop and listen, then perform intricate calibrations, reading the city’s heartbeat the way one might read a coded text.

Deeper down, in the cavernous sub-level vaults, there are others who dwell in near-total darkness. Some call them the engineers, though the term feels too narrow. Towering silhouettes reel cables along overhead pulleys, lugging massive crates from one gloom-shrouded sector to another. These crates contain machinery whose purpose is a mystery to all but the engineers themselves—though perhaps their function is lost even to them. A methodical momentum propels every action. Some of the contraptions look incomplete, half-finished or half-forgotten, tangles of pipes and gears that hum with half-born life. Occasionally, the shapes moving alongside them let slip a faint, resonant chord—like a sigh that straddles the line between mechanical whir and animal breath.

In the labyrinths beneath the factories, rumor speaks of dwellings carved into the black stone or fashioned from spare metal sheeting. Here, the inhabitants sometimes gather in hushed congregation. If you stand outside, you might perceive a dim, pulsing glow leaking through the seams of a hatchway. A moment later, you’ll hear a low, rhythmic clanking from within, like the slow and steady beat of an ancient ritual. Are they forging new replacements for themselves? Building fresh components for the city’s hidden designs? Or simply reaffirming the cycle that keeps them all alive?

Between these pockets of half-seen civilization drift smaller shapes—perhaps the ones you catch on the edges of your vision. These skittering creatures, more animal than worker, appear and vanish in an eyeblink. They scale the stunted mineral outcrops around the city’s perimeter or scuttle under the half-closed rolling doors of the great factories. Their heads might bear a crystalline shimmer or angular protrusions that glow faintly, like residual heat stored beneath translucent plates. Some say these creatures are pests—moths drawn to the city’s faint lights. Others suspect they’re discarded experiments, mechanical beasts left to roam. Scraps of rumors claim they feed on the drifting black dust or absorb geothermal warmth directly through their spindly legs, partial embodiments of the plateau itself.

On the rarest nights—though day and night are meaningless here—a curious phenomenon unfolds at the heart of the settlement. The watchers, engineers, and the elusive creatures converge in the city’s central square. It’s an open space ringed by silent, monolithic machines. The overhead pipes gather into a tangle that forms a dome, like twisted arteries feeding a forgotten organ. A single, dull glow emanates from a dome’s apex, illuminating the canals that fan out from this location. In that moment, the watchers almost seem to take the shape of robed figures, standing in a circle, heads tilted to detect the subtle vibrations traveling through the air or ground. It might be a meeting of sorts, though no words pass between them—only that universal, mechanical hum.

Under that glow, you might see one caretaker, perhaps draped in a layered cloak of rubbery material, approach the old canals. Kneeling by their edge, they trace a gloved fingertip along the glass-smooth interior, as though remembering a time when something once flowed there. Behind them, others bear canisters or containers that might hold liquids or energy, but inevitably those vessels remain sealed. You never witness them pouring anything into the canals. Whatever once ran in those channels is long gone. Still, a quiet, reverent hush accompanies each inspection, as though the caretakers cling to a memory of days when the plateau was not so parched.

Sometimes, from high vantage points along rusted scaffolds, you can glimpse the watchers going about their inscrutable tasks in a pattern that seems almost ritualistic. They traverse the city in synchronized rounds, verifying each factory’s pulse, attuning themselves to the turbines’ rotating cycles, ensuring the pipes’ silent power flows remain unbroken. Like clock hands, they pass one another at precise intervals, exchanging fleeting nods—or are they mechanical scans? Their movements are so understated that it’s possible to miss them entirely unless you look for the signs: a shift in the haze of black dust, a momentary flicker of a headlamp, a soft clang of metal boots on steel catwalks.

What compels them to continue? Perhaps these inhabitants are bound by duty to the ancient machinic environment, or perhaps they are the spirit of the city itself made tangible. Their entire culture—if it can be called that—revolves around maintaining and perpetuating this hush of industry. The heavy tungsten rods and whirring cogs, the low thrumming engines, the labor of forging unknown devices: this is the root of their existence. They draw purpose from connection to the old structures, one spinning gear fitting endlessly into another.

And so, they endure in the gloom. While outer visitors might label them ghosts amidst rusting metal, the watchers and engineers could well be the last custodians of an epoch that refuses to die. Their bodies—mechanical, organic, or both—have adapted to a plateau stripped of every comfort. They neither fear the stillness nor shy from the dryness; they have become part of it, melding with the colossal factories and the empty canals in a sort of quiet symbiosis. Where once might have been a bustling population of workers, there is now just this slow, pensive echo of labor still being done, of a need still being fulfilled, even if no one remembers why.

On certain rare occasions, if you linger long enough, one of them may pause in the darkness and turn to face you, just for an instant. You may detect a faint searching light emanating from where its eyes or sensors should be. In that moment, you might imagine recognition—an almost gentle curiosity. But then it passes, and the being is gone, swallowed by black dust motes twisting in the gloom. All that remains is the sound of your own breath and the distant pounding of factory hearts, continuing in their measured tempo, as though the city itself lives, and these steadfast, half-seen figures are only its many articulated limbs, meant to carry on until the plateau at last settles into oblivion.

Thus are the dwellers of this ancient, final plateau: an unbroken chain of caretakers and quiet spirits, neither wholly alive nor fully inert, tirelessly mending and coaxing the worn machinery back into rhythmic life. Their presence is as enduring as the warm hush that rises from beneath the ground, and as cryptic as the age-old towers that watch silently over the emptiness. In every corner of this blackened landscape, you sense the faint traces of these beings—more persistent than change, more patient than time—figures forever wound into the city’s hidden heartbeat.
