They move within the great metallic mosaic almost like a second form of wind, though wind does not blow here. Their presence is not immediately obvious, but if you wait with a listening stillness—the same hush that pervades the surface—you begin to sense them in the subtle pulses and vibrating undercurrents. They are the inhabitants, scattered and secretive, woven into the ancient network of plates and buried corridors. They are not a single species but a tapestry of delicate, self-sustaining mechanisms evolved from or engineered by the planet-spanning machine.

At the smallest scale, hidden close to the cracks where thin filaments rise, there exist clusters of entities you might think of as “beetles,” if beetles were spun from glass wires and solar flares. Each is a few centimeters in length—an articulated lattice of translucent material that refracts the blazing light overhead. Their limbs are many-jointed and extremely fine, giving them a spidery elegance as they skitter from gap to gap. Look closer, and you see that their backs are fitted with arrays of microscopic nodules, glimmering like polished gems. These nodules feed on the faint currents of static or magnetism that dance through the plates, a slow drip of energy that keeps them alive. They make no conventional sound—only a faint crackling presence, as though each minute shift of their bodies discharges tiny sparks.

Scattered across the plains, virtually imperceptible unless you tune your eyes to the glare, are the “orbs.” These spherical creatures hover near the surface, seldom more than a half-meter above the plates. Their surfaces appear to be made of layered, polarized crystals, spinning and rippling in mesmerizing patterns. They possess tendrils extending from their undersides, hair-like and flexible, which occasionally make momentary contact with the metal mosaic, as if tasting currents or anchoring themselves against an unseen force. Their motion is gentle and gliding; watch long enough, and you’ll see a group of them—maybe three or four—gracefully ascend into the glare high above, then drift back, shifting hue from a luminous silver to gold and back again. Perhaps it is how they communicate, modulating the way light refracts through their shells. These orbs seldom approach the hooded towers, preferring the open expanses where energies radiate uniformly, but every now and then one can be glimpsed vanishing through a seam in the architecture, as though called below.

The bizarre “plants” you’ve seen—those radiant filaments—are themselves inhabited by swarms of minute, sub-visual organisms, not quite like insects but reminiscent of living fractals. From a distance, the filaments appear solitary, but move in close and you’ll spot faint motes zipping across the surface, or streaming inside the hollow stalks. They are so minute, it’s difficult to say if they’re mechanical, organic, or both at once—like living circuits, gleaning whatever passes for nutrients in this dryness. Perhaps they help repair or modulate the filaments, siphoning the energy that flows along internal conduits. Sometimes you’ll witness the entire filament bend ever so slightly, its top glimmering in a new angle of brightness, and if you look at just the right moment, you’ll glimpse a surge of luminous specks spiraling upward within its translucent core.

In the deeper layers, below the plates where corridors and sealed chambers yawn, there are larger beings—presences half glimpsed through parted walls or fleeting silhouettes at the threshold of your vision. You sense them more than see them: a gathering shift in the thrumming beneath your feet, a faint pattern on the edges of your hearing. It’s as though they are deeply attuned to the superstructure itself, moving through it as fish navigate the ocean, guided by fields and pulses invisible to the untrained eye. When one of these subterranean dwellers surfaces above ground, it seldom stays long. You might catch the merest flicker of a shape—too tall, too multiple-limbed, or too gracefully contorting to be anything you recognize. Their outlines glisten with that polished metal luster, as if grown directly from the living architecture. Some watchers have called them “Keepers,” suspecting they oversee maintenance of the planet-machine, checking the towers, adjusting filaments, ensuring the mosaic remains in calibration. Their silent comings and goings feel watchful but not malevolent—like an impersonal caretaker servicing an eternal engine.

Yet this single caretaker species does not govern the plains alone. Hidden “colonies” of smaller mechanical-living hybrids cling to the bases of those tall monolithic protrusions. If you venture near one of these monoliths, you may spy, pressed flush against the metal wall, irregular clusters of what look like suction-cupped discs or coiling ribbons of matte black. At first, you might mistake them for drifting dust or inert debris, until they begin to ripple in response to subtle shifts of energy from the tower. Beneath these ribbons, there are nest-like cavities. They seethe with life that doesn’t rely on fluid water but on some sort of conductive solution—an oily, shimmering film that circulates from the tower’s interior. From that film, they derive nourishment, humming with cryptic signals that pass between them in quick pulses of light across patterned surfaces.

Not all life here is confined to metallic glimmer and filigree, though. There are also static-laden dust-cloud creatures, ephemeral as weather phenomena. You might see them stir when the drifting, dark powder on the horizon forms swirling eddies. In an instant, these swirls coalesce into shapes that move as coherent beings. The dust shapes manifest limbs reaching from swirling torsos, or else become smooth silhouettes that flicker in the stark brightness like living shadows. Some swirl momentarily with a purpose—searching, perhaps feeding on bits of magnetically charged particles—then disperse, scattering back into formless haze. It’s unclear whether they’re truly sentient or just complex patterns spontaneously emerging from the environment’s energies. Yet something in the silent, graceful choreography of these dust-cloud shapes suggests intelligence woven from the planet’s climate: a synergy of magnetism, dryness, and constant illumination.

Farthest out, where the horizon extends into an indefinite, glaring distance, dwell those few hardy life forms that isolate themselves from everything else. They might be solitary drifters or nearly immobile watchers that perch on slight rises in the plate mosaic. These watchers are elongated, delicate spires of living crystal threaded with metallic veins that still faintly align with the architecture beneath. Rarely, under one of those prismatic arcs that flash across the sky, you will see them react in a majestic blossoming—fractal arms unfurl as if to catch the energies from above. Their shimmering bodies refract the light into a halo of color. Then, when the sky dims to its usual steady glare, the watchers fold back into themselves, returning to statuesque stillness. Quite possibly, they store that energy for decades or centuries, sustaining them in slow, measured processes that outlast any typical organism’s lifespan.

All these inhabitants—whether spidery glass-lattice “beetles,” luminous orbs, fractal dust-shapes, Keeper forms, or solitary watchers—interconnect in a hidden ecology older than memory. It’s an ecology of magnetism and conduction, geometry and coded pulses, built from the planetary superstructure’s inexhaustible energies. No single species reigns; rather, each type of life finds a niche in the mosaic, existing in delicate balance with the environment’s unceasing hum. Perhaps it is the echo of the planet’s creators, or the final expression of an advanced civilization’s dream of a stable, self-running biosphere—albeit one entirely foreign to our assumptions of water, air, and organic matter.

For all this, they remain elusive. If you blink, or glance away, or let your mind wander for half a moment, you may doubt you saw anything alive at all. Then, a faint chiming ripple through the plates might surprise you, and you’ll glimpse just a flash—a glint of movement, a swirl of dust forming an elegant spiral, an orb drifting behind a tower’s curving facade. And you’ll be reminded that life is here. It threads in and out of the mosaic architecture, ancient and exacting, quietly thriving by channeling forces we scarcely understand.

They are the dwellers of this planet-machine, shaped by ages of dryness and brilliance, by the metal puzzle-plate ground and endless white sky. Ghostlike in some moments and startlingly vivid in others, they embody the merging of life and mechanism, for there is no division between technology and biology in this place. It is a single, living mechanism with countless forms of existence woven throughout—an ecology of arcs and pulses, of shimmering filaments and silent watchers, each fulfilling a role in the hush of an engineered eternity.
