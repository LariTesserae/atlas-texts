They call themselves many things, depending on who is asked and in which pocket of the city one inquires. Names slip and shift here as fluidly as the terrain: words coil off the tongue in half-heard syllables or brazen clangs. One name might be spoken in thriving, steam-wreathed neighborhoods but remain unknown just a few scaffolds away, beyond some twisted ramp that leads to an entirely different sector of existence. Taken all together, the creatures of this place share a sense of belonging to the Eave of Vertices; they wear their environment almost like a second skin, some with real hides of stone and steel, others with subtle illusions that warp the senses and meld them seamlessly with the half-formed angles of the cliffs.

Wander through a low-lit walkway where the swirling steam drips into rivulets and you might see the petroforgers: bipedal figures with elongated limbs specialized for clambering along near-vertical surfaces. Their clothes, if the patchwork of harnesses, tubes, and mechanized plates can be called such, serve more as structural additions to their bodies than protection from the cold. Slick with condensed vapor, they’ll pause to drive a spike or rivet into newly exposed rock, affixing the next length of walkway. Petroforgers have lean but powerful builds; their hands are perpetually stained by mineral residue and glimmers of wet metal, as though they spend their days elbow-deep in the bedrock of the city. Beneath the humming of their power tools, you can sometimes hear them humming tunes or conversing in curt, melodic bursts. They speak with short, biting words between hammer falls, like nailing syllables into the stone.

Out on precarious balconies that jut out over the city’s chasms, the Aerie-Kin build eccentric lofts from salvage and living vines. They are lightly feathered creatures, though they also bear mechanical augments: small, gear-driven turbines or telescoping rods that flick out from their limbs to help them glide on the uncertain currents of air. Their plumage overlaps with patterns reminiscent of fractal geometry, echoing the illusions that plague the city’s architecture. It’s said the Aerie-Kin migrated here centuries ago, drawn by the strange wind shear that makes flight unpredictable and thrilling. In the early morning gloom—though “morning” is a loose concept here—they shuffle restlessly on the ledges, testing the wind by opening and closing slender wings. Pitching themselves out into the steep open, they let the improbable winds swirl around them, trusting in their mechanical feathers or miniature turbines to steer them amid the spires.

Within the neon-lit corridors closest to the raucous factories live the machinists—short, stocky folk with heavy protective goggles and thick gloves lined with rotating diamond studs or magnetized tips. They are half-covered in an exoskeleton of pipes and flexible conduits, connected to the city’s power veins by subtle cables. If one stares long enough into the faint green luminescence behind a machinist’s goggles, they might see more than just eyes—a swirl of quartz-like lenses, or maybe organic retina fused with synthetic fluid. The machinists navigate effortlessly through labyrinthine upper floors where enormous equipment roars. They whisper incantations of a sort, which they claim are formulaic instructions for the machines that churn in half-dimness, as if the engines themselves understand a language that is equal parts mechanical code and living chant. 

In the quieter, mist-shrouded recesses, you find the Mosswalkers. These are tall, willowy beings whose bodies sprout layers of thick botanical growth. Some combine metal plating with trailing vines and are adept at blending into the pockets of greenery. It is rumored that certain Mosswalkers no longer have a separate body under the plant matter; they’ve fully fused skin and moss into an integrated organism. They prefer the stony terraces where moss thrives in slick rivulets of water. Their presence is gentle, if somewhat unsettling: when they move, leaves and thin creepers spread across the stone behind them, and more than once travelers have seen an entire path bloom with new buds after a Mosswalker’s slow passage. Their voices are soft crackles, like wind in leaves, though some let out a resonant, low-pitched hum when performing complicated horticultural manipulations of the environment—urging certain fungi to root deeper in cracks, or coaxing vines to reinforce older scaffolds that threaten to collapse.

There are smaller creatures too, ones that rarely congregate in large groups. The Gimbal-Scurries are palm-sized, spider-like automatons wrapped in patches of soft fur or lichen. They crawl along the undersides of walkways or cling upside-down to metal beams, whirring softly as they go. Some have corners of stone or chipped crystal embedded in their carapaces—almost as if they were grown, not constructed. They feed on the ambient static created by the city’s improbable geometry and seem able to appear out of nowhere, perhaps slipping in through those strange dimensional seams. Wherever they go, they leave faint trails of glimmering motes, as if trying to stitch together the fractures in reality that persist all around them.

Meanwhile, large, deep-chested beasts dubbed Rift-Bisons roam the lower caverns where new expansions have scarcely begun. Their horns and thick plating are reminiscent of basalt columns; sparks dance at the tips of their horns when they clash, as if the friction of their world-warping existence creates arcs of minute lightning. For the most part, Rift-Bisons are calm, slowly scraping moss and lichen from the walls for sustenance. But on occasion, a bellow reverberates through the canyons, sounding like the collision of tectonic plates. Locals watch from secure vantage points, for that cry might signal a shift in the city’s architecture—corridors warping, new angles appearing spontaneously, the entire place rippling in resonance with the beasts’ calls.

Beyond the straightforward flesh-and-blood denizens, there are ephemeral beings half-lost in the city’s unraveling corners. Tethered Wisps flit through the heavy air: faint silhouettes recognizable only by a trailing swirl of light and shadow. Some believe them to be echoes of travelers long dead, or glimmers of yet-to-be-born inhabitants caught in the city’s temporal folds. Others suspect they are the city’s guardians—maintenance apparitions that slip in and out of the intangible doorways to repair cosmic tears with intangible tools. Occasionally, a Wisp will coalesce beside a wall or walkway, sliding softly over the surface as if inspecting it, then disappear without warning, leaving behind a faint swirl of luminescent dew.

As you venture deeper, you find entire pockets where time seems to stutter and overlap: glimpses of a group of robed figures quietly chanting in a distant corridor that may not fully exist in the present. Who are they? No one can say for certain, but rumors abound that these shrouded specters are the Oracle-Class. They’ve adapted to the city’s dimensional loops so completely that they slip fluidly between eras, gleaning knowledge from possible futures or lost pasts. They’ve been spotted momentarily—hoods drawn low, faces reflecting the city’s own geometry back at the viewer with impossible angles. Then they flicker out, leaving a subtle whiff of ozone and the sense that they walk a layer of existence underfoot.

Communities of flesh-and-machine live in a delicate dance here. In the hivelike warrens of the city’s middle-levels, tight enclaves of craftspeople and scavengers trade in exotic salvage: twisted lumps of “newly formed” mineral effluent, shards of glasslike stone that appear overnight in the city’s bowels, or mechanical cogs half digested by some living engine. The currency might be units of electricity, lumps of raw ore, or even intangible favors bound to certain corners of geometry. Haggling is often a matter of carefully balancing physical resources with ephemeral trade, concluded when both sides sense the tug of gravity realign itself—a sign the deal is sealed in the city’s unspoken manner.

Despite the patchwork settlement, a sociable communal life thrives. There are clang-addled markets where the machinists or petroforgers hawk their metalwork, and Aerie-Kin flutter down from on high to barter away sleek fittings or find the perfect mechanical pivot for their wings. Mosswalkers trade cuttings of bizarre plant species that can cling to metal with supernatural tenacity. On dim terraces, you might find gatherings around battered industrial heaters, where travelers or laborers share stories, speak of expansions rumored to be underway in unseen ravines, or recount glimpses of angles folding in the next block over. It’s a ramshackle sort of fellowship, but accompanied by the tang of moist air, the hiss of constantly venting steam, and an undercurrent of whirring power.

On the highest catwalks, across from the largest factory outflows, the city’s leadership—if such a thing can be said to exist—makes its domain. Not so much a single council as a loose constellation of engineers, architects, and dimension-weavers, they manage the slow colonization of new vertical territories. They debate whether an outcropping is stable enough for another foundry, or if a particularly large chunk of stone is suitable for transforming into living quarters. Amid these cadres, there move individuals part flesh, part algorithm, trailing multi-layered projections that show potential expansions in shimmering 3D illusions. The tension is always between pushing forward—drilling deeper, forging higher—and restraining themselves to avoid stressing the tenuous boundary of reality that holds the entire Eave of Vertices intact.

Finally, there are the wanderers who prefer the outskirts, the half-finished fringes where half-raw stone meets the city’s outskirts in a smolder of new construction. Some are tinkers, some explorers. Some are simply misfits who find solace in the hush of dripping water and the solace of unpeopled ledges. They track footprints of elusive beasts, searching for those ephemeral doorways that hum at the edge of the senses. Allegedly, these wanderers trade quiet whispers with mysterious watchers in the canyons—entities made almost entirely of swirling steam and the glint of mineral dust. Often, none can say exactly who or what these watchers are, only that they appear at vantage points overlooking massive expansions. Some watchers have been witnessed gently placing a palm against an unformed wall of stone, turning it pliable and shaping it like clay before fading away in a swirl of condensation.

Taken in aggregate, the inhabitants of the Eave of Vertices are neither wholly natural nor entirely machinic, neither fully empirical nor purely mystical. In their breath and their gears, their leaves and illusions, beats a shared pulse: the city’s deep, thrumming heartbeat that resonates through every rivet and spire of rock. They are a people (or peoples) who have decided—consciously or not—to embrace the precarious miracle of an environment that births new edges faster than any single being can comprehend. And so they inhabit narrow ledges and half-finished corridors, forging relationships with dimension-warping geometry and ancient living stone, as though the boundary between city and mountain, flesh and fabrication, were never so firm to begin with. Here at the Eave of Vertices, the dwellers celebrate (in their subdued, clanging way) the slow, irrational beauty of a world still actively being made, populated by creatures and technologies that refuse ordinary definitions, each destined to evolve alongside the shifting terrain for as long as time permits—however strangely time may flow.
