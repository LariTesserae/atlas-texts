They are rarely glimpsed in full. At first, you might catch only the barest suggestion—a blur in the edge of vision, or a ripple in the city’s gleaming walls that moves too deliberately to be just a reflection. But as you linger, the inhabitants begin to reveal themselves in subtle increments, not so much stepping out into the open as allowing you to sense their presence within the architecture, as if they and the structures were born from the same living blueprint.

At times, they seem like tall, angular silhouettes with forms that bend the light—thin limbs tapering into slender digits, torsos that suggest neither human nor entirely machine. In other moments, they are nothing more than flowing shadows threaded with flickers of color, dancing across the shifting panels beneath your feet. And then there are intervals when you’d swear you saw them as faintly glowing outlines, loosely clothed in the same strange metallic vines that curl around the city’s walls, with circuit-like veins pulsing across their bodies in a slow, harmonic rhythm.

They move in silence, or nearly so. You never hear a footstep on the half-formed stone, but you might catch a quiet resonance that follows in their wake, an echo of that subterranean hum you noticed on your approach. Sometimes it’s a low drone that tapers off into a vibrating hush—almost like a choral note suspended on the edge of hearing. When they pass, you realize the city’s corridors shift slightly in their presence; archways lengthen to accommodate them, or the angle of a sloped ramp alters fractionally underfoot. It’s as if each inhabitant carries a subtle gravitational field tied to the city’s blossoming geometry.

They do not wear clothing in any conventional sense, though some appear to have layered membranes or sheaths of shimmering matter draped over their forms. These translucent shrouds gather luminescence from the faint sunlight, swirling with fractal patterns that never quite repeat themselves. If you come closer—though “closer” is an aspirational word here, because they often stand just beyond reach—you might notice that each pattern of shifting light across their limbs is unique. It’s as if they each inhabit a personal, living tapestry that reveals something of their role: some are crowned by helical shapes dancing around their heads like slow-turning galaxies; others radiate whorling motifs down their spines, as if printing new lines of code into reality.

One may appear stooped and deliberate, trunk-like arms shaped for endless manual work far beneath the city, living in tune with the layers of piping and hidden machinery. Another may be spindly and quick, darting among the spires with a subtle bounding grace, channeling lines of force that flicker overhead. It is impossible to say how many of them exist: a dozen, a thousand, or a single consciousness manifesting in many shimmering bodies. At times you almost sense that each shape is an echo of all the others.

Their eyes—if one can call those shifting lights eyes—glimmer in a manner reminiscent of pockets of condensation shining in the half-light. They rarely rest on you long, and yet they take everything in. Within those glimmers, you can see faint traces of awareness—like the reflection of your own face in alien glass, offset by a second or third reflection deeper within. There’s a quiet watchfulness about them, but a watchfulness of a different order—one that seems simultaneously ancient and still in the process of emerging. When they linger, it can feel as though they are gauging your presence not by whether you pose a threat but by whether you resonate with the city’s carefully calibrated growth. You might imagine they feel each footstep you take as a ripple in the embryonic architecture.

They do not speak in ways you recognize. Occasionally, you hear low-pitched reverberations in the spires, ornaments of metal that suddenly ring with half-strangled notes. From the vantage of an alley, you notice one figure tilting its head, the glow in its face intensifying, as if in response to those resonant chords. Another stands across a courtyard, gently raising a hand as a red-laced wave pulses along the wall behind it. Within seconds, the note in the spire shifts in timbre, the metal vines quiver, and a new corridor shifts into place behind you. It’s as if conversation here takes place on a plane not just of words but of geometry and subtle frequencies. Sometimes a spire might dim then brighten, telegraphing a message across the city. Or the trickle of condensation from an arch might abruptly speed up or slow, as though it’s being tuned or reoriented by distant signals. It is a language both rigorous and dreamlike, forging structural changes in real time.

When not enacting these transformations, they perform near-ritualistic tasks that you can only guess at. You watch them standing by the reflective pools at the central courtyard: they gently stir the water with a multi-jointed finger, tracing shapes that bloom into ephemeral illusions upon the surface. The illusions swirl outward and graze the pillars before dissolving, each ripple seemingly rearranging some facet of the environment. Perhaps they are verifying some ancient schema—tending the city’s underlying foundation lines—or drawing the next stage of the city’s growth out from a blueprint inscribed in the invisible spaces between molecules. Each small gesture seems to carry disproportionate importance, as if the fate of the whole place hinges on that single swirling line.

You notice a few in the mid-distance, walking slowly along the perimeter where the city’s structures stand only a few feet tall. They seem to examine the cracks in the embryonic rock with intense focus. Once in a while, an inhabitant might kneel, pressing a fingertip or a palm to the half-formed ground. Soft radiance floods from their limb into the terrain, lines of shimmering brilliance reaching out in multiple directions—like new fissures forming in a labyrinth of possible realities. After a moment, the shimmer retracts, lazy as the ebb of a tide, and the figure stands again, posture inscrutable, as though it has just engaged in a profound exchange with the land itself.

You can’t help but suspect that the entire plane of rock and soil is, for them, just another surface to shape—a malleable substrate in the earliest stages of metamorphosis. Their knees or fingertips might nudge geological processes that take centuries to complete in ordinary circumstances, or instantly coax an entire new channel of moisture to surface. The city’s presence has changed this slate-gray plain in ways that seem slow to your eyes, yet are undoubtedly ongoing. Where pockets of lichen-like growth appear, you see them occasionally paused, carefully touching the wiry plants as though reading signals from them. A hum circulates between city and plain, occupant and environment, forging a unified system of endless, reverent engineering.

Sometimes they vanish into a corridor that moments later ceases to exist, leaving behind only swirling emptiness in the wall. Sometimes they gather in small groups beneath the high canopy-lattice, heads bowed, their outlines merging into a single, faintly luminescent shape. You hear a hum of pure note that resonates in your chest, half-familiar, half-alien—an undercurrent of devotion or focus. Then, as quickly as they merged, they drift apart again, each one dissolving into separate shimmering arcs that vanish among the city’s pillars and winding causeways.

No one can fully distinguish their biology from their technology. Their limbs could be living metal, or flesh so advanced it takes on metallic sheen. Their spines arch with ridges reminiscent of the city’s spires, and at times you catch a glimpse of faint translucent membranes around their shoulders—wings? Gills? Who can say? Perhaps each occupant is an experiment in bridging worlds: part living organism, part incarnation of the city’s memory, part caretaker and scribe. They weave themselves among these illusions and reconfiguring structures, occasionally pausing to observe you as though you, too, are a piece of the puzzle.

Whether they are welcoming or indifferent is difficult to judge. There is no outward hostility and no explicit invitation. They seem to allow your presence the way a forest might allow a wandering traveler. You become merely another ripple, watched curiously but not seen as a threat—at least, not yet. Their quiet acceptance might be a deeper generosity. Or perhaps you’re too transient to factor into their cosmic timescale, much the way a single raindrop is neither welcomed nor refused, only absorbed into the city’s evolving tapestry.

All around, you feel the sense of a grand project undertaken over millennia—these inhabitants fold themselves into that process, timeless artisans shaping the environment cell by cell, corridor by corridor. They appear at the junctures of growth and transition, fueling the city’s expansion with unwavering calm. In them, you perceive a synergy of arcane science and devotion, an all-encompassing equilibrium. If the city itself breathes, these are its alveoli, drawing in signals from the universal outside, processing them into new expansions, exhaling new corridors, spires, and illusions. Their essence is woven so intimately with the place, they might as well be neurons in its living brain.

Moments later, one or two might pass near you. Flickers of refracted light dance across your skin as they glide by. For a heartbeat, you sense something: an unspoken invitation to glimpse what’s behind those rippling surfaces. A faint tingle moves up your arm as though gently scanning you. In that instant, the city’s watery pulses and half-formed geometry seem to open, revealing the barest hint of a deeper blueprint—a living architecture of infinite potential, stretching beyond what sight or logic can hold. You feel the quiet intensity of a civilization forever in the act of building itself, calling new shapes into being, always on the cusp of a deeper emergence.

And then, as quickly as it came, the moment passes. The dwellers move on, layering themselves back into the shifting depths of the city, leaving you again to wonder at their nature. You stand in the hush once more—listening to the drip of condensation, to the echo of hidden machinery, to the faint hum of energies you cannot name. You realize you may never truly see them the same way twice, for they are as mutable as the walls they inhabit. And in their chimeric presence, as in the city’s own embryonic existence, you sense the unstoppable patience of something that has outgrown the need to hurry. They dwell in that hush between shifting tides of reality, shaping the geometry of tomorrow beneath the silent watch of a pallid sun.
