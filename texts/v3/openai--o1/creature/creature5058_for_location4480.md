They make their homes among the welded seams and rising tendrils of vine, so inconspicuously that at first you might think no one lives here at all. There is no single blueprint of being in this place—it is inhabited by many sorts of dwellers, some more mechanical than flesh, some scarcely different from any hardscrabble gardener tending a greenhouse plot. Yet they share a mutual bond in maintaining this lofty habitat, ensuring water flows, structural beams hold, and leaves keep reaching toward the artificial suns. They’re a spectrum of engineers, cultivators, and caretakers—though not all of them walk on two feet.

If you watch the upper catwalks, sometimes you’ll catch a glimpse of them—figures stooped to examine the health of a vine or the condition of a newly welded beam. Hoods of segmented plating drape their shoulders, each piece etched in patterns that, in the shifting light, mimic the fractals found on leaves. Some wear thick, wrap-like boots with stabilizers, enabling them to climb rafters and reach hanging drip lines. Others seem more ephemeral: they don’t appear to have a distinct boundary between garment and body, as though grown or forged from the surrounding environment. At times, you see fingers that end in small tools—delicate spanners, sensors, shears—just as easily used for coaxing new shoots as for tightening bolts.

But not all inhabitants here are flesh at all. Some of the “people,” if you can call them that, are more akin to mobile assemblies. They glide on hidden rollers along the corridors and catwalks: bulbous torsos of polished metal, each with a cluster of arms that pivot gracefully. You see them exchanging cables with one another in passing, as if in greeting, or connecting newly arrived pipe segments to overhead rails. Occasionally, they pause beneath the canopy of broad leaves, letting accumulated condensation drip down onto their exoskeletons; whether this is an act of maintenance, refreshment, or some unspoken ritual remains unclear.

Near the scaffolding where fresh welds glow faintly, new faces—human or otherwise—arrive and depart in an unending dance of construction. Some are spindly-limbed, wearing harnesses of flexible tubing that help regulate their body temperatures and oxygen intake in the humid air. Others look more robust, standing with broad shoulders and thick gloves, their voices resonating when they call out measured instructions to the mechanical arms overhead. You might spy them passing seedlings and nutrient tubes back and forth, or making minute calibrations to the lighting arrays that replicate a full day’s sun cycle in less than half the real time.

There are also watchers—quiet souls who move among the greenery without disturbing a single leaf. They seem to exist for observation alone, whether botanical or structural. You can tell them by their broad, mirrored goggles, reflecting every angle of latticework and vine. No tool belts hang from their hips; no heavy packs weigh them down. They drift down corridors with a measured pace, occasionally pausing to let a mechanical bird-land—more drone than animal—alight on an outstretched arm. Perhaps they gather data: leaf coloration, water flow rates, micro-filaments of fungal networks that lace the roots in their translucent channels. Then they slip away, ghostlike, into newly opened bracings or side passages you hadn’t noticed before.

Deep within the humming lower levels, communities of more specialized beings reside. Their silhouettes are often glimpsed behind a veil of steam rising from pumping stations. Here, the name of the game is hydraulics, thermoregulation, and ensuring the entire domain’s breath-like cycles run smoothly. These subterranean dwellers—some human, some mechanical, some caught in between—oversee the great turbines, the spinning fans that flush stale air out and draw fresh air in. You hear their faint chatter over echoing walkie-talkies or feel the vibrations of their heavy boots stomping along grated floors. If you venture down there, you’ll find them hoisting massive water filters, re-routing channels to keep the ecosystem balanced. Their skin (or metal plating) glistens with condensation, and the patter of dripping water mixes with the clang of metal as they slide open valves taller than they are.

Running parallel to this caretaker community is another group, less visible but just as present: the laborers of expansion. Their domain is the scaffolding perched at the edges where new corridors and overlooks are planned. They are explorers of possibility, forging fresh arcs from raw steel and weaving them seamlessly into the existing structure. You might catch them arguing softly about load distribution or the best path for a future walkway. They are driven by curiosity and guided by blueprint-laden tablets or glowing schematics projected onto half-finished walls. Their fingertips hum with the energy of welders, their eyes bright with the promise of shaping yet another corner of this ever-growing habitat. Day and night, they merge raw materials with living greenery, sculpting a domain that breathes and thrives, piece by piece.

Yet the residents here aren’t solely cultivators and builders. Every so often, you see solitary figures who appear more contemplative than industrious. They linger in quiet alcoves tucked behind clusters of broad tropical leaves, or in the mesh-lattice corners where moisture drips down from overhead pipes. These sages—some old, some surprisingly young—are keepers of lore. They record subtle shifts in the microclimate, trace genealogies of hybrid vines, and measure the changing composition of the air. Their records might be stored on delicate data crystals or scrawled into watery inks on metallic scrollwork. They’ve studied how each new corridor affects wind currents, how every cluster of new leaves might alter the ecosystem’s intangible balance.

Stranger still are the rumored inhabitants who communicate more by presence than by speech. These nearly invisible denizens slip in and out of vent passages, gather near humming nodes of energy, and seem to exist in concert with the environment’s mechanical heartbeat. You might detect them in the flicker of a control panel’s lights late at night, or sense them in the sudden hush that descends when the automated systems pause. Though details are scarce, some say they came here to ensure the place’s guiding intelligence remains stable. Others claim they’re the source of that intelligence—a kind of living code, seeping between circuits and vines alike.

Not to be overlooked are the winged and skittering creatures—those hybridized drones, part biomechanical, part organic, which thrive in the thick air. The mechanical birds with whirring wings, darting in to drink droplets from succulent leaves, or the glint of what might be amphibians with silicone-laced skin. Did the residents create these creatures, or did they find their way in, drawn by the promise of moisture and warmth? The people here debate that quietly, never quite reaching a consensus. Perhaps the creatures emerged from some synergy of environment and invention—an experiment that seeded unexpected life in the cracks and corners. Whatever their origin, they offer a constant, if subtle, reminder that this domain fosters not only carefully planned growth but also spontaneous, unpredictable forms of being.

Above all, those who live here share an understanding that they are part of something perpetually in flux. Walls expand, vines climb, corridors spool out into new vistas. Nothing stands still for long, and so the inhabitants—the watchers, builders, researchers, caretakers—adapt in kind. Their whispers fill the rafters with plans for tomorrow. Their footprints line the steel floors with traces of yesterday’s exploration. By day, they sweat over piping systems and coax the next generation of seedlings to root. By night, they watch the glow of overhead lamps dim and shift, measuring it all to make sure the cycles continue uninterrupted.

In essence, this place shelters a community that manages the tension and harmony between the natural and the manufactured. They know the vines can’t exist without the metal columns, and the columns themselves serve little purpose without the lush canopy that purifies the air. They are bound to the ever-present hum of machinery, just as they are nourished by the drip of condensation. Each caretaker, explorer, and sentinel plays a distinct role, yet their lives mesh and overlap like the wires and roots beneath their feet. Together, they embody the living system they inhabit—a grand, evolving experiment that is part greenhouse, part factory, part laboratory, and part sanctuary in the sky.
