They are fewer in number than one might expect of a place so grand. This city—woven of leaf and metal—offers plenty of nooks and passageways, but the denizens who call it home occupy only a small fraction of the space. At first glance, one might see them as shadows crossing arching vine corridors or as silhouettes bent over metal apparatus. You might glimpse a few watchers perched in high walkways, peering down through the mist. But the emptiness of the city’s avenues suggests that most of these inhabitants stay tucked away in concealed halls, communal living lofts, or workshop alcoves so seamlessly interwoven with the living walls that it’s easy to miss their presence entirely.

Some dwellers appear mostly human, though their clothes and mannerisms have been shaped by this strange environment. Their layered garments are pieced from thick, fibrous leaves that have been coaxed to fuse into durable natural fabrics. Due to the constant damp, they wear broad hoods that extend not just overhead but down along the shoulders, patterned with living filaments that can wick water away. When they walk, thick-soled boots squeak softly on the porous vine flooring, and every so often their footsteps pause as a particularly unstable portion of the structure quivers or shifts beneath them. They place palm to wood and close their eyes, as though communing with the living material. Then, once the corridor stabilizes, they proceed with quiet confidence.

Others are far stranger. Some have elongated limbs or partially translucent skin that shimmers like a membrane—remnants of the illusions that flicker through this place. Rumors say that these translucent folk arrived only a generation or two ago and adapted in ways nobody anticipated. Their breathing is shallow and quiet, less needing the crisp air, as though they partially “sip” the ambient energies passing through the vines. When they speak, occasionally their words slip and warp, as if their voices are traveling through the same shifting pockets that distort the architecture, producing half-heard echoes that can carry from passage to passage.

Then there are those shaped more by the plant world than by simple flesh. Vines seem to coil around their arms or torso—not merely decorative but intimately joined with living tissue. Their flesh and the vines’ bark weave together, forming flexible but permanent grafts. These individuals can sense the city in ways others cannot: they feel micro-pulses of growth, detect shifts in the vine-floor, and perceive illusions in real time. For them, the city is one big living web, humming with silent signals. They often serve as harbingers of structural trouble, responding to any sudden surge of growth or stress within the labyrinth of plant corridors.

Most inhabitants, however, are less visibly altered. In dusty workshops or forging halls hammered out from thick knots of roots, you’ll find them sweating over broad anvils, coaxing metal sheets to fuse with bark plating, or checking fluid levels in scalding steam pipes. Their faces carry that air of resigned determination common to those who live in a harsh environment. They wipe condensation from their brows, occasionally stopping to peer at a pressure gauge or to run a bare hand across the damp walls, feeling for any new leaks. Here, technology is both advanced and precarious. The forging masters rely on the vines to serve as scaffolds for piping, and in turn the vines rely on what scraps of heat and condensation the machines yield. This symbiosis shapes the daily routines of the people: tasks revolve around coaxing growth in the right spots, pruning back roots that threaten to overtake vital machinery, and forging new blank plates to patch worn areas of living architecture.

Scattered among them are cunning specialists called “shapers.” Not quite engineers, not quite gardeners, these individuals coax the living substrate with gentle touches or murmured incantations that might be mechanical instructions, songs, or older whispered traditions. The vines respond by twisting into new forms, forming anything from spiral staircases to large, puckered hollows that can shelter entire families. Shapers’ abilities are as intangible as they are crucial, and many approach them with reverence: if a corridor is going to shift unexpectedly, chances are a shaper can sense or guide that movement. Were it not for their skill, the illusions that warp the city might cause structural disasters.

Beneath street level, in the deeper halls closest to the root systems, dwell reclusive artisans who craft smaller, specialized tools. These subterranean enclaves are lit by luminescent fungi that seem half mechanical themselves—fungal caps studded with tiny metallic nodes, feeding off residual steam and condensation. Within this green-glow hush, the artisans produce delicate gadgets: telescopes fashioned from hollow vine tubes and polished iron lenses, or living-wire contraptions that harness the bioelectric pulses traveling through the city’s labyrinth of roots. The inhabitants who dwell here rarely surface; they prefer the subterranean gloom, where the closeness of the vines fosters deeper connections. Some say they whisper directly to the city, listening for new growth or hidden tears in the architecture miles away, as though they have tapped into the city’s own nervous system.

Up in the open terraces, on the flatter platforms that cling to mossy ridges, are the gatherers—those who move lightly about the plant canopies, scouring for edible fronds and catching condensation before it drips away. They carry woven nets and metal sieves, which they position beneath thick clusters of leaves to catch run-off. Their entire livelihood revolves around gleaning moisture in a realm where no true rivers or lakes form. Many gatherers are lithe and surefooted, skilled at bounding across living bridges that stretch between spires of trunk-like growth. Their attire is dyed in soft greens and browns, allowing them to vanish into the leafy environment. On quiet days, you’ll see them perched at the edge of a swaying walkway, scanning endless horizons for clouds, trying to predict where the next rainfall will condense in greater volumes. They often meet travelers first, for the gatherers dwell at the city’s periphery and have a knack for spotting approachers threading the vine-lattices from miles off.

Despite the persistent hush, there are smaller creatures inhabiting these heights, coexisting uneasily with the city’s living mechanics. Thin-bodied mothlike beings flit through the corridors: nearly transparent, ephemeral, as if made of teased silver. Sometimes they circle the forging halls, drawn to the heat and the faint glimmer of metal surfaces. Others, spidery in shape, with too many segmented limbs and heads that tilt in unnerving angles, scuttle up the city’s pillars. On rare occasions, they transmit flashes of bioluminescence across their limbs, perhaps communicating in a pattern only they understand. The citizens don’t consider these creatures pets or pests so much as visitors who slip in and out of the living walls. Sometimes these insects nest behind metal plating, weaving filament cocoons that softly pulse like the heartbeat of the city itself.

What larger beings lurk at the fringes remains mostly speculation. If you ask a forging master or a gatherer, they’ll wave you off with a scowl; the big ones, they’ll say, are best left to their domain. If you press further, you might hear tales of shapes glimpsed in the swirling mists: hulking silhouettes that move with surprising grace, limb and vine indistinguishable. Some suspect that entire swaths of the city’s underpinnings are not merely plant structures but huge living beasts harbored in the woven architecture. Residents speak in hushed tones of “root-behemoths,” massive creatures that dwell in the largest subterranean alveoli of the city, half asleep and half symbiotically merged with the vines. They seldom stir, but whenever that deep, cyclical rumble echoes from below, the inhabitants cast wary glances at the floor, uncertain of which ancient presence is shifting beneath them.

Among the city’s most intriguing denizens are the watchers. No one can say how many watchers there are; they are glimpsed everywhere at once, perched on high balconies or stepping out from behind a trembling corridor just as it misaligns. Some watchers are humanoid, others more vine than flesh, and still others might be illusions themselves—beings who come and go with the city’s shimmering distortions. They do not speak often, but when they do, they refer to ancient knowledge of how the city grew or how it might someday shift again. Folks consider the watchers a kind of caretaker caste, or perhaps guardians. They are rumored to keep records: coded mechanical logs and hidden living archives that store the city’s lineage, instructions for splicing bark with steel, and cryptic references to older powers that shaped this entire region. Many watchers appear equipped with instruments like slender rods whose tips glow dully with a greenish light, reminiscent of fungal luminescence. It’s said these rods manipulate illusions, snapping them apart or merging them to maintain equilibrium.

What unites nearly all inhabitants is a combination of caution and acceptance. The forging masters, the shapers, the watchers, the gatherers—no group seems entirely at ease, yet they’ve learned to read the city’s slight tremors and illusions as day-to-day facts of life. Their economy is a precarious dance of growing, harvesting, forging, and piecing together new expansions, always mindful not to cut too deeply into the essential vines. They accept rust as inevitable, illusions as normal, and the city’s shifting angles as part of a living puzzle. In quieter moments, dwellers might gather in communal spaces—round hollows draped by broad fanning leaves—where they swap stories under the soft glow of battered lanterns. They discuss the day’s yield, puzzle over mechanical breakdowns, and reflect on the city’s deeper mysteries.

If you walk among them, you will notice an unspoken recognition that this place is more alive and more unstable than any single creature could comprehend. People speak in hushed voices about the city’s next “great realignment,” though no one can say exactly when or how it will happen. The illusions, the shifting vines, the glimpses of structure flickering in and out—these are signs, the watchers say, of a tension building. Only a handful seem to truly welcome that possibility: a chance for the city to expand or transform, or perhaps reveal hidden layers previously confined to rumor. Others regard the illusions with dread; they worry that if the city shifts too violently, entire districts could vanish or appear in new, disorienting places.

So the inhabitants remain, suspended between reverence and wariness for the living environment that sustains them. Their daily tasks—checking steam engines, patching bark, gathering the condensation—are done with a collective sense of respect for the green sprawl that shelters them. They rest in open-air lofts whose walls are half-veins of living leaves, half-welded support beams. At dawn, they rise to the faint clang of forging hammers and the dripping hush left by receding mist. At dusk, they see the corridors glisten with the last traces of daylight while the illusions softly flicker at the edges of vision. In these twilight hours, the watchers emerge at vantage points to observe the lights from the forging halls, the swirl of the city’s respiration, the hush that seeps through every vine.

In the end, those who dwell here sense that they live at the boundary between worlds—real and unreal, mechanical and living, stable and ever-shifting. They don’t seem to need a final resolution. They have learned to accommodate the city’s uncertain nature and, in turn, the city accommodates them, adjusting corridors to shield them from the cold or offering up new growth where materials have dwindled. Life flows in slow currents, in cycles of forging and pruning, in illusions that trick the eye but ensure a deeper balance.

Thus, in a place of ancient, eroded heights—where vines coil overhead in lattices the size of city blocks, where illusions shimmer and clank of machinery reverberates—the inhabitants quietly persist. Some are flesh and blood, some partially plant, others something in between. Under starry skies veiled by drifting mist, they remain guided by their instincts and by the pulsing heartbeat of the living city they call home, ever watchful for the next subtle shift in bark or the next mechanical sigh from deep below.
