They call themselves many names, for their language shifts like the subtle dust that reshapes footprints. To one another they communicate in half-heard vibrations, a pulse of resonance through the mineral architecture, as if each wall and courtyard were an instrument gathering up the faintest reverberations. They live in the stillness between sounds, in the hush behind the occasional scuffing of stone against stone.

Glimpses of their forms reveal a fluid balance of flesh, crystal, and something else—something intangible. Their physiological makeup is neither purely organic nor wholly mineral. Instead, where one might expect joints or muscle, there may be cleverly interlocked arms of gemstone, pale at the edges and showing flickers of color in the half-light. In some individuals, faint traces of vein-like channels run through scattered crystal deposits. Others show a more compact form: a nearly human silhouette whose limbs possess gently faceted planes. When they move, they move in concert with the subtle shifts of the city’s architecture. Keystones seem to realign to guide their journeys. Pillars and arches resonate—a music felt more than heard—and it coaxes them through corridors with practiced ease.

These folk do not dwell in typical homes. They recede into the thick walls, finding hollows that respond to their presence by quietly adjusting shape. Interior ledges may morph into soft, sloping shelves to cradle them while they rest. Entire alcoves become private dimly lit retreats, forming ephemeral huts that revert to smooth rock when vacated. The growing crystalline spines and elaborate fractal patterns across the settlement are as much a product of their will as the natural forces of this place. In the still watches of that half-lit existence, they guide the city to mature into new shapes, deepening corridors or raising new arches—though they do so without chisels, without clang or commotion. The transformation occurs in alignment with their collective breathing, each gentle exhalation coaxes the rock to shift, an exhalation more a subtle hum than a spoken word.

Their lives are quiet yet hardly idle. Each day—if such a concept exists in a world perpetually between dusk and dawn—some gather near the lichen-like growths in the courtyards. They kneel, running translucent frond-fingers through the dust, examining the unhurried cycles of growth that feed the microfauna. They tend to the pale sprouts, encouraging the minuscule vines or spore-laden clusters to cling to the stone. This guardianship also fosters the small creatures that, in turn, feed on the vegetation. Many of this settlement’s dwellers understand that if the lichen thrives, so does the rest of their hushed environment. Without pomp or ceremony, they sprinkle a fine dust of their own making around the roots—fine crystal shards exhaled through ridges in their skin, each shard containing minute traces of minerals that the sprouts need.

Others are watchers of the outward plains, patrolling the boundary where the city’s last ridges meet the mirror-like flats. These watchers stand at the edges with surfaces of their bodies turned translucent, absorbing the faint glimmer from the overhead half-light. They watch the weird reflections across that crystalline lakebed, letting their luminous eyes track anomalies in the illusions mirrored below. Sometimes, it is said, the watchers see entire structures shift beneath the surface, or movements that do not match any figure walking above. When these watchers return, they confer with their kin: a subtle series of touches, hand to crystalline shoulder, or a weaving of fingers that transmits meaning in the gentle clicks of joined mineral facets. These messages ripple through the settlement so that all eventually share in what was witnessed.

Now and then, a single figure might wander from the settlement altogether, venturing across the mirror-crust. This sojourner’s form becomes impossibly delicate, nearly invisible in the gloom, carrying within them an energy that can feed upon the faint sparks beneath the reflective surface. They do not simply walk; they attune themselves to the half-light like living prisms. Each step resonates, sending a low thrumming tone along the ground, searching for hidden tunnels, for the labyrinth that rumor suggests lies below. These journeys might last days, or the equivalent of whatever passes for days here; some who leave do not return in ordinary fashion. Instead, they slip back into the city’s boundaries from an unexpected alley, as though they have emerged from the stone itself, bringing tales of new spires discovered deep beneath the crust. Others shift in form by the time of their return, subtle modifications in their crystalline limbs that testify to an encounter with the unknown expansions below.

Among these people, there are weavers of color as well. They fasten themselves to the newly sprouted crystals in certain carved alcoves, coaxing them to refract and scatter the half-light into ephemeral rainbows. These fleeting sprays of color become a kind of living tapestry projected onto the city’s walls. Over time, as the crystals and lichen co-evolve, so too do these arcs of color shift and brighten, forming patterns that tell the story of the settlement’s transformations. Older inhabitants read these tapestries like archives: swirling prismatic histories that record when a new arch was raised, when a spire collapsed and re-grew, when a population of small mineral-skulled predators arrived, or an unexpected cascade of crystal growth swept across the courtyards. The weavers never speak words aloud; the city’s memory is carried in the dance of refracted light.

Despite their collective synergy, individuality still exists. Certain inhabitants favor inhabiting corners deep within the underground corridors—a twilight realm beneath the city’s visible floors, where the stone is faintly warmer. There they cultivate reclusive gardens of living mineral filaments that glisten in the hush. Many of these guardians-of-the-deep are older, their bodies more thoroughly encrusted with fine crystals. Younger or newly formed individuals move nearer the surface, brimming with curiosity, learning to mesh their vibrations with the greater symphony of the city’s heart. They trade silent lessons, their resonance weaving in and out, until one day they, too, may decide to delve deeper—or wander out over the mirror-crust.

Not all are shaped like the beings that glide through the corridors. There are smaller creatures—akin to familiars—who move in the city’s wake. Some are spidery stonelings with elongated limbs of jointed crystal, able to cling to vertical walls and scuttle across the city’s faceted columns. They feed on microscopic mineral algae and gather in clusters around the largest outcroppings, picking them clean of dusty residue. In rare corners of the settlement, a winged variety roosts among the tallest arches. Their wings, sheer and shimmering, cast glances of color across the stone. They launch themselves in near-silent flight, capturing motes of drifting dust that serve as their staple diet. To the larger dwellers, these creatures are cherished pollinators, carrying the spore-like seeds of the lichen from courtyard to courtyard.

Occasional visitors also pass by—outsiders from beyond the mirror flats. Their shapes do not mirror the indigenous dwellers so easily, and they rarely linger for long. If the city’s denizens acknowledge them, it is with a polite, almost ceremonial hush. These visitors sometimes bear offerings: small satchels of once-lost minerals or carved tokens that glow softly in the half-light. They place these items into recesses in the walls as a gesture of respect, and the city quietly absorbs them, the stone shifting to enclose each item in new crystal growth. None within the settlement publicly acknowledges that transaction, and the visitors slip away—some on two legs, some on far more. Whether these visitors are explorers, traders, or pilgrims seeking the unique energies of the half-lit city remains uncertain.

In all these mysteries, this settlement’s dwellers abide by a sense of time measured not by solar cycles but by the steady accumulation of dust and the slow, purposeful growth of stone. To them, existence revolves around the subtle interactions among minerals, light, and near-silent communion. The dryness of the air, nearly devoid of water, seems not to trouble them; they draw what nourishment they need from trace materials in the rock and from the faint hum of the environment. Their lifespans, by human reckoning, could be impossibly long. Or perhaps they operate in continuous states of metamorphosis, never truly dying so much as melding back into the structure that birthed them.

Within this half-lit domain, these crystalline kindred or quasi-humanoid stewards maintain a perpetually unfolding dance with the city’s living architecture. They move through arches that exist in one shape by morning and another by evening, weaving their presence into the bedrock itself. Their hush is not emptiness but living reverence: a vast hush that carries subtle signals of continuity, a near-silent chorus that shapes arches, grows crystals, and nurtures the fragile life that clings to drifting dust. In them is the promise that this land is not a static wasteland but a place forever in flux, a place guided by the delicate hands of its silent inhabitants who see all growth—even the smallest crystals and faintest sprouts—as part of an ongoing creation.
