They move in slow currents, like water through the city’s countless runnels—some seeing themselves as stewards of the vast metal pantheon in which they dwell, others simply eking out a living among the incessant drip-drip of condensation. The settlement’s scale and ceaseless industry shape the people (and other beings) who call it home. No single type of inhabitant can describe them all.

In the shadows of sprawling catwalks, there are those who call themselves the Rustkeepers. Stooped figures, perpetually soaked by the overhanging leaks, they wear heavy, oil-stained cloaks and broad hats fashioned from rubberized scrap. Beneath their hats and hoods, they keep an array of small chisels, picks, and brushes, tools that they use to apply protective sealants, scrape away corrosion, and shift the flow of trickling water to protect vital metal struts. Stoic in demeanor, the Rustkeepers drift through the walkways at all hours, never fully asleep: a small band of caretakers who believe the city’s steel bones have a kind of soul that must be nursed. Decades of exposure to the drizzling weather have made their skin sealed with a waxy sheen—even their voices have turned soft and watery, as if their breath is always heavy with mist.

Deep within the hum of the machinery, entire families of Tinker-Colonists live in warren-like spaces partitioned from one another by arrays of pipes and valves. They’ve built modest dwellings from old shipping containers and leftover pieces of plating, welding them to the city’s skeleton. It’s said they arrived centuries ago—when the settlement was younger—and simply never left, each generation piling on more improvised structures. The Tinker-Colonists move with an easy confidence through the labyrinth, skipping across knotted tangles of industrial belts to retrieve odd scraps and harness the city’s endless discard. Many shape metal for new contraptions, forging anything from mechanical limbs (for those injured by falling beams) to small wind turbines that capture the swirling gusts. Children of the Tinker-Colonists dangle from overhead maintenance rails with fearless aplomb, weaving vines around them to stabilize the walkways as if this is all perfectly routine.

In the warmer depths—industrial warrens close to the massive boiler-like expansions—live the Vapotouched, a reclusive group rumored to have adapted to the city’s thick fog of steam. Their small enclaves cluster around vents that gush heated mist, creating hot microclimates in which slender, spindly plants and pliant mushrooms proliferate. Because of the constant moisture and heat, these inhabitants dress light, sometimes only in wraps of woven moss fiber, and keep their hair short or shaved to combat mold creeping in. An unsettling rumor says they’ve developed a curious immunity to scalding vapors, so that they can pass through bursts of pressurized steam without flinching. In the shadows of factory turbines, you might glimpse their silhouettes, ghostly pale, gleaning lumps of fungal matter that they cultivate for both food and medicine.

Meanwhile, up in the highest spires—where the wind roars across open terraces—a different breed thrives: the Skyward Riggers. Lean-limbed and surefooted, they pride themselves on bridging the gaps between precarious steel towers with rope-and-cable walkways, sometimes spun from handwoven wire. They are the watchers who scale the city’s tallest scaffolding to predict the next roiling storm. Indeed, it’s said the Riggers read the clouds like oracles: swirling banks of slate-hued mist, sudden shears of wind, and the shifting barometric pressure that resonates through the metal struts… they sense it. When they descend from on high, they often bring news of approaching squalls, letting the entire city reel in cargo booms and secure fragile gear. Of all the settlements’ people, they possess the greatest reverence for the mountain’s shape beneath the scaffolding—they sense how the original rock is still there under the plated metal, and sometimes can be heard groaning when the city’s tension shifts in a high gale.

Not everyone who lives here is fully human. In the low-lit vaults, humming with the deep pulse of turbines and generator coils, clusters of Automaton-Brood scuttle on spidery metal legs. They’re part machine, part biological—the specifics murky and mostly unknown. Occasional glimpses reveal elaborate mechanical joints glistening with moisture, but ringed by living membranes that knit almost seamlessly with the metal. Some inhabitants consider them pests—tinkerers gone too far, or experiments left to reproduce in the city’s underbelly. They maintain hidden warrens and consume waste metal or industrial byproducts to fuel their self-repair. Though they rarely communicate, some say they’re conscious and keep a silent watch on the city’s fundamental workings, perhaps protecting it from catastrophic breakdown in ways no human caretaker fully understands.

A separate group known as the Gutter Gardeners roam along the city’s many drainage trenches and channels, meticulously guiding the flow of runoff into deeper cisterns or sending it outward to feed the vines curled around pylons. Many Gutter Gardeners wear chest-mounted canisters that drip with collected water and natural compost. They have a custom of sowing new seeds in every rusted seam, often traveling with satchels of spore pods and tough alpine starts. In the dimly lit corridors, a Gutter Gardener can be identified by the gentle clacking of their seed-pouches bouncing on plastic hip-plates. Owing to their efforts, no matter how fast the city expands, green always follows in its wake.

Past the battered bulkheads and inside echoing machine hangars dwell the Magrail Operators—often people who arrived with the cargo dirigibles or specialized locomotive lines. They live in cramped compartments near the tracks, where locomotives are loaded and unloaded in bursts of flurried motion. Some are specialized engineers who know exactly where to place the city’s newest additions, guiding elevated trains filled with supplies. Others are crane operators who maneuver freight with delicate precision onto platforms barely wider than two steps, suspended hundreds of feet above chasms that vanish in mist. Many remain sleepless and tense, as storms or mechanical failures can cause an entire load to plummet away. Yet their skill is undoubtable: watching a Magrail Operator at work is mesmerizing, as they glide metal beams along tracks that would make any outsider’s legs quake with vertigo.

Scattered among these more visible groups are countless solitary wanderers. A few are known as Drifters—travelers who pass through a labyrinth of grated floors, earning meals by performing tasks no one else wants to do: clearing blockages, collecting water samples for hazard testing, or applying protective tar to newly uncovered seams. With no real home, Drifters carry their lives on their backs, sometimes with spindly mechanical frames over their shoulders to which they can clamp containers and sleeping hammocks. Others have partial lodging in run-down corners of an ancient factory block, hidden behind coolant towers or in the labyrinth of vents. The Drifters know the city’s unspoken rules, including how to navigate fragile catwalks in a windstorm, or whom to barter with for a hot meal. Surviving on the city’s swirling edges, they embody the precarious nature of life here—no single place is stable or predictable, but a life can be knit nonetheless in the spaces between rainfall and steel.

As for animal life, it’s sparse but hardly absent. Small, cunning rodents nest in the warm pockets of newly welded beams or behind the metal siding near steam vents. Some have even developed long, water-repelling fur to better withstand the ever-present dampness. A few silhouetted birds perch in the high rafters, perhaps feeding on insects that thrive in the humid gloom. Large, flying scavengers—cliff-dwelling corvids or hawkish creatures—may circle the city in the rare patches of sunlight. Now and then, one hears the beating of heavy wings echoing across the canyons, followed by the faint caw of something that has claimed an outcropping or high stanchion as its territory. Evidence of other, more elusive creatures occasionally surfaces—slimy tracks, droppings near half-broken ducting, mysterious scraping marks in remote corridors. Tales of giant amphibian-lizard hybrids or gnarled goats perched on remote platforms spread with each rumor. But they remain mostly that: hints, rumored sightings, or half-glimpsed shapes in the swirling fog.

And across all these pockets of life, the city itself feels animate—an “organism” forged of metal, steam, and the quiet beat of rooftop rivulets. If the inhabitants quarrel, the structure’s hidden rhythms somehow reconcile them, an unspoken consensus to protect the labyrinth from corrosion and collapse. To hear the Tinker-Colonists tell it, the place’s expansions tap into a kind of collective drive, compelling them to tack on the next outgrowth of scaffolding or connect the next tower. Vapotouched enclaves re-route steam lines to cultivate new fungal outcroppings. Gutter Gardeners find space in newly bolted corridors to seed creeping vines. Even the Automaton-Brood may repurpose scraps of metal or old cables to form new outshoots. In this way, the entire settlement grows and refines itself like an unstoppable tide.

No one group runs it all. Instead, the city’s many denizens each hold some small stewardship over this or that domain: the beams overhead, the steam below, the vines that anchor cracks, the cargo lines delivering new materials. It’s a distributed consciousness of sorts—a thousand specialized tasks overlapping in function, with no single overarching authority, just an unspoken agreement that the city must keep going, must keep breathing. Storms come, wind shrieks, water cascades in angled sheets across the walkways—and yet the city does not stop. The inhabitants repair, adjust, scramble to keep the next wave of rain or industrial surge from overwhelming them.

They have shaped themselves to fit the city just as the city has shaped itself around them. The Rustkeepers speak reverently of the place’s hum, how it calls them to keep the metal from succumbing to rot. The Tinker-Colonists craft contraptions that harness the swirling air and flicker of electricity to power new expansions. The Vapotouched roam the warm steam vents, fostering the life that thrives in the cracks of steel. The Skyward Riggers carry messages of storms rolling in from the horizon, ensuring fragile structures can batten down their hatches. The Automaton-Brood scuttle silently in the dark, minds and networks woven with the city’s pulse.

They are all part of a living ecosystem. Here on these carved mountaintops, amid the swirling, damp atmosphere, they tighten bolts, lay new catwalks, and harness the unstoppable flow of water and air—a thousand different whispers and gestures weaving into one. Their motives vary: some do it for survival, some for faith, others for simple habit. Yet in each rung of scaffolding, in every courtyard turned partial greenhouse, in every narrow corridor lined with cables, you can see the traces of their work. 

And so the city’s residents keep living, breathing in the cool, moist air. Wind whips their hair; condensation trickles down their tools and clothes. Around them, more vines sprout, more expansions bolt themselves upon the old framework, and the slow swirl of clouds overhead signals the next transformative wave. The settlement is neither purely nature nor strictly machine. Its inhabitants, too, blur that line, forging new shapes of life in the watery gloom.

They are builders, watchers, caretakers, scavengers, archivists. They believe they dwell in something worth preserving, worth evolving—something that remains in flux, like the swirling vapors they breathe. And through it all, the hush persists. It’s never truly silent, but the low hum from infinite moving parts sets a kind of lullaby for these people and creatures alike. In that hush, you sense their unity, their shared purpose, the subtle knowledge that life clings on in every crevice, and that they, too, cling on—ever at the intersection of forging and being forged by the city’s living machine.
