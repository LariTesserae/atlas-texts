They might be called people, or at least a kind of people, but at first glance, you wouldn’t be sure. Wander between the living towers and woven plant-walls, and you’ll spot them only in glimpses—figures shaped by a world where water is scarce, and every resource must come from the planet’s mineral depth or the sky’s faint radiance. These inhabitants have evolved (or designed themselves) to draw life from energy fields and crystalline structures in much the same way their home’s flora does. They wear garments that mesh seamlessly with the fibrous vines and bark-like panels that form their settlement. Yet their outlines are unmistakably self-possessed: elongated limbs, flexible torsos, eyes that glimmer with an inner phosphorescence, especially in the half-light.

Still, be patient. At first, you see only movement at the edge of your vision—someone crouching behind a tendril archway or drifting out of sight around a living rampart. You might think them shy. But as you spend more time there, you realize it’s less about shyness and more about how attuned they are to the rhythms of this environment. They move in harmony with the hissing vegetation and the resonant pulses of the bio-crystalline towers. Like dancers trained to an unfamiliar music, they flow, stutter, then pause in perfect time with the place’s natural and technological heartbeat.

They speak, or at least communicate, in layered polyphonic calls—sometimes mimicking the whirring and droning of local fauna, sometimes resonating at a pitch just under the threshold of your hearing. It’s a language folded into the ambient noise of wind and machines: pulses, clicks, and deep, chest-reverberating tones. If you listen carefully, you’ll hear them respond to the environment’s background hum, weaving their communications into the cyclical calls of the towering devices. It’s not merely conversation; it’s a constant alignment, as though they are tuning themselves—individually and communally—to the planet’s unending song.

Their bodies are slender and strong, built for the plateau’s cool, windy dryness. Their hair, if it can be called that, often looks more like fine-tuned antennae or small clusters of pearlescent spines. These spines reflect the dim sunlight in shifting colors that sometimes match the glow of the vegetation’s crystalline parts, as though they can harvest minute energies from the environment. A few individuals display swirling patterns across their skin—an echo of the fractal designs in the moss-like coverings on the cliffs. Whether these patterns are genetic or a kind of ceremonial marking is hard to discern; perhaps the boundary between body and environment has blurred over eons.

In one moment, you’ll see them gliding along the suspended walkways of braided vines, carrying translucent orbs that themselves seem alive. In another, you might peer through a gap in the living wall and spot a cluster of them kneeling around a spherical device that thrums with an internal light. Their gestures are reverent, measured, almost like a ritual of communion with the planet’s energies. These are the keepers of the settlement’s technology, the ones ensuring each wave of sonic pulses, each surge of crystal-powered current, remains stable and harmonious. Indeed, the entire “city” breathes in sync—its residents adjusting living panels, polishing bark-like plating, coaxing vines to coil or uncoil—to keep a balance between the plateau’s natural processes and the advanced forces that power their homes.

A certain seriousness pervades them, an awareness of ancient age. Yet there’s wonder, too. Watch where the older ones gather with the very young: teach them how to coax new spore growths, how to harvest the glowing pods that appear unpredictably. The young let out chirruping laughter when they succeed, their voices wrapping around the wind’s endless low roar. If ever you find yourself in their presence, you feel the depth of history behind their eyes—knowledge of how to endure across epochs in a world where dryness has polished stone to softness, and where existence depends on harmony with the planet’s murmur.

They build little in the sense of solid, permanent structures. Instead, their architecture is grown: spires of living crystal and bark fused with the metallic shimmer of unknown alloys. But they also shape intangible constructs—fields of force or directed resonances—to create spaces that feel partially beyond matter. Step through certain doorways, and you sense a shift in the air, a faint tingle up your spine, as if you’ve wandered into an electromagnetic threshold or a realm of carefully woven soundwaves. Some of their greatest “buildings” are hardly visible to the naked eye, anchored to the vines and rock only by clusters of radiating nodal points. Instead of walls, you might see glimmering outlines in the atmosphere—or sense a hum that demarcates a boundary.

Culturally, they appear neither fully somber nor jubilant. Their customs revolve around cooperative maintenance of the plateau’s life-web. At intervals, you might see processions of them crossing the open rock around dusk (or that subtle shift in the dim light that stands in for dusk). They carry newly crystalized seeds or migrate to specific vantage points to perform thrumming chants. At such times, the fauna emerges from nooks and ledges: chitinous creatures skitter on the edges, while floating spore-carriers drift overhead, as though drawn by the pulses of the chant. The quadruped behemoths might pause in their wanderings to join the chorus with deep, booming calls, while fine-bodied gliders swoop over the congregation, scattering luminous pollen. Each piece merges into the whole.

This sense of symbiosis extends to the plateau’s technology. Although highly advanced, it never feels intrusive or separate. Towering structures realign with each sonic pulse, often aided by the inhabitants’ guided chants. You’ll see figures with elongated staffs that they seem to key into the living machinery’s resonant frequencies; with a push or a swirl of their staff, entire sections of the city might rotate, fold, or open like a blossoming flower. In such moments, the watchers raise their voices in unison, every note reinforcing the next, until the entire plateau feels alive with a single, unified purpose.

Indeed, they must be wise to survive here. Waterless conditions demand an unorthodox approach to sustenance, so they’ve learned to supplement themselves with the plateau’s mineral and energetic wealth. Occasionally, you might witness them meditating, posed against the wind-scoured cliffs, arms outstretched to catch the meager sunlight and swirling dust. Their tall, pale eyes close in deep concentration, and a faint luminescence outlines their forms. Perhaps they’re drawing slow trickles of energy from the star, from the core of the planet, or from a synergy of both. Science and mysticism blend into an art of survival.

And so they carry themselves across the eons. When the plateau rings with a new wave of activity—be it the activation of a colossal gate that shudders open at the city’s center or the arrival of a spore-storm that coats every living fiber in shimmering dust—they adapt, shift, and realign. No event stands isolated; it’s all part of the living geometry, the cyclical dance of being. They are watchers and participants both, guardians who understand that life here stands on a razor’s edge between abundance and disaster.

Should you stand at the place where a woven bastion meets the endless drop beyond the plateau, one of them might approach you. In their expression, you see a question that spans time: will you learn their resonances or remain an outsider? You might only catch drifts of meaning in the low hum of their voice, a mouthful of alien syllables layered over an undertone of otherworldly music. But even so, the compassion and invitation in their stance is clear. For a moment, you share in the wind’s howl, the swirl of spore-lights, and the cool hush of this ancient realm. And you realize these people do not just live here; they are inseparable from this organic, crystal-driven tapestry, a final refuge of vibrant existence in a place that seems so unlivable to naive eyes.

Such are the plateau’s inhabitants: part caretaker, part conductor of the perpetual symphony, chaotically brilliant yet orderly in a manner beyond basic comprehension. They are both proud and humble, proficient in weaving living matter with unfathomable technology, drawing power from dryness and forging civilizations from spore-laden dust. Carefully, without haste, they reshape the plateau’s surfaces and rhythms to preserve their home. Forever strange, forever vibrant, they flourish in the place where clouds seldom gather and water is but a faint myth, proving that life can root itself in any realm and sing out into the cosmic winds.
