They come in many forms, from the whisper-quiet to the thunderous, from those who scuttle among the creeping moss to those whose roars shake the ancient bedrock. If you stand in the middle of this plateau and look carefully, you can see them all, although they rarely line up for easy viewing. Lives overlap and interlock like pieces of a puzzle, bound by the slow pulse of the plateau’s ecology and the quick spark of hidden technologies.

First, you notice the smallest creatures: insects the size of your fingernail or smaller, most bearing glossy carapaces that reflect prismatic tones in the angled sun. Some flit about in swirling clouds, drifting like living motes of dust that shimmer and vanish if you move too fast. Others scuttle through the cracks in stone, weaving around droplets of condensed moisture. Their colors run the spectrum: opalescent shells flicker with pastel pinks, while others are a near-transparent green that can merge seamlessly against a thick vine. It is their music you heard upon entering: a million discrete chirps, clicks, and droning hums that combine into a single, wavering tapestry. If you crouch down, you might see the larger ones feeding on the algae in shallow pools, siphoning the film into hidden mouths, or rubbing forelegs together in a hypnotic dance. Some of these tiny creatures also follow the faint glow of the plateau’s curious machines; you see them gathering around a half-submerged orb, each insect’s eyes flickering with the orb’s shifting light.

Beyond the insects, there are amphibious lizard-fish, their sleek bodies half-sheathed in shimmering scales that ripple between bronze and emerald. They haunt the puddles, sometimes lurking beneath the surface in murky water tinted by fertile green algae. When one emerges, it reveals a fish’s elongated face but with stubby, reptilian forearms for clambering up slick stones. They peer about with wide, sideways eyes that suggest a keen awareness. Occasionally, you’ll see them flick out a barbed tongue at an unwary insect or leap from one pool to a neighboring depression, crossing open ground with an unexpected grace.

Swiveling your gaze upward, you catch glimpses of the mid-sized beasts that rule the ferns and creepers. Some sport a blend of plumage and scales so strange it defies what you know of evolution. One might have a short snout lined with small, sharp teeth and sails of feathery spines along its back—almost draconic, yet perched on thick hind limbs that bend with the precision of a bird’s. Hence the call you hear echoing across the rocks: a shrill note that warps midway into a rolling vibration, as though they combine the songs of reptiles and avians in a single, unsettling aria.
