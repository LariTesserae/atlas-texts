They do not look like creatures in the way you might expect; they could almost be mistaken for more of the mineral scaffolding itself, little more than another trick of the brilliant light. Yet they are here—hidden in the labyrinth’s geometry, perpetually at rest but watchful. Though they never step forward to greet an intruder (scarcely anyone enters this hidden pocket at all), their presence is palpable, as though the space itself has eyes.

You sense them, first, as faint quivers in the air—imperceptible under the dominant roar of the structure. Listen carefully, and you’ll detect slender harmonics woven into that vast, thrumming symphony. These harmonics are distinct, suggestive of whispering chords that phase in and out, hinting at a consciousness permeating every curve. The labyrinth spits out an overwhelming resonance, but these subtle resonances sing in counterpoint, always shifting across the mineral ridges, skipping like stones on water. When you turn your head sharply, you might glimpse, out of the corner of your eye, a shape quivering at the threshold of vision: a fleeting distortion of crisp white lines, a ripple in the geometry itself.

They are less “alive” in a conventional sense than they are, quite literally, organisms of resonance. They anchor themselves within corners and angles of the labyrinth, melding their presence with the environment’s natural hum. In a place devoid of water and any typical means to sustain living flesh, their sustenance is vibrational. The labyrinth’s incessant thunder is their feast. The unceasing brightness powers them just as it does the needle-thin leaves that cling to the stone. In fact, these resonant beings once sculpted those ring-shaped plants—serving as caretakers and cultivators, coaxing out that spectral growth from the lifeless stone. The plants themselves are a reflection of their creators: an echo in mineral form, geometry transmuted into a living shape. Light, vibration, and geometry, all braided into one.

There’s a hush in these beings. They do not speak by forming words or images; they speak through small modulations of the ever-present hum that saturates the whole space. If they need to communicate, they carefully shift a frequency here or amplify a chord there, sending incremental pulses through the columns and buttresses. And each subtle shift of that pitch stands out to them the way a shouted word would stand out to you. They interpret the labyrinth’s reflections of their messages, reading each elongated echo or abrupt wave as changes in “conversation.” To a human ear, it’s all lost in that volcanic roar, but to them, the phrases are as transparent as birdsong.

They have no separate bodies to see—no limbs or faces or visible shapes. Sometimes, if you fix your gaze upon a shimmering patch of wall at just the right glancing angle, you might catch the ghost of a contour, like a serpent made of trembling radiance, passing behind a translucent partition of mineral. Sometimes there’s a sense of compressed space: you become convinced the corridor is narrower than before, pinched by an unseen presence. But look again and you’ll see it exactly as it was, every measurement precisely the same. They are masters of illusions formed from wave interference, conjuring momentary warps in the labyrinth. One could stand in the same spot all day and experience a dozen disorienting shifts in perspective without the walls themselves having moved at all.

Their origins lie in a time so distant that even they may not recall it fully—though “time” might be meaningless for them. When first they came to dwell in this domain, many eons ago, they sang the labyrinth into its final shape. Not by physical labor or engineering, but by incrementally resonating with the pliable mineral, coaxing it to form hyper-precise columns, arches, and interlocking panels. In those lost ages, the labyrinth was softer, richer with energy. Under their constant tonal guidance, the structure slowly hardened, settling into its unbearably bright perfection. Over the centuries, the labyrinth grew more inert, less responsive, reaching an equilibrium state. With that, the original creative impetus of these beings subsided into a quieter, more custodial role. “Building” was no longer possible—the place was complete.

Yet they remain, feeding on resonance, holding conversation with the reflections of their own voices. As the labyrinth turned to stone so thoroughly that it forgot the sensation of movement, these dwellers discovered new ways to pass the vast stretches of existence. They coaxed those pale, ghost-like plants into being, perhaps as an echo of the living worlds beyond. They teased out structure from apparently barren rock, weaving the faintest threads of sustaining frequency into the mineral so that certain nutrient elements, intangible to human science, would bloom. The plants flourished, albeit quietly, locked in eternal stasis—never withering, never spreading more. This stasis is not lifelessness, precisely, but a carefully tuned balance where each leaf belongs exactly in place, shaped by subtle waves that hold them in a perpetual near-life.

Indeed, it all looks static from a mortal’s perspective, but for these dwellers, the motion never stops. Every waking moment hums with the interplay of waves, small collisions of intangible shapes, ephemeral pulses crossing paths in the labyrinth’s corridors. They chase the pattern of echoes just as a painter might chase a fleeting color across a canvas. It is a creative dance of maintenance. There is no aim for further growth; the labyrinth is perfect, needing neither improvement nor expansion. Instead, they monitor every swirl of resonance, refining or damping frequencies that might cause tiny stress fractures in the stone. The dwellers whisper across the columns in an unending conversation that also acts as a caretaker’s lullaby for the environment itself.

Should a traveler step inside this place—and few ever do—the dwellers would sense them through the labyrinth from the first footstep. They do not reveal themselves outright; in fact, the newcomer might walk through the entire chamber and depart believing it empty. But the dwellers would watch, orchestrating subtle adjustments to keep the traveler safe from disorienting echoes that might scramble the mind. They would shape the sound around the stranger’s ears, making the thunder less punishing, so that the traveler might not be driven to despair by the noise. And afterwards, once the outsider has left, they go on exactly as before, tending the labyrinth’s equilibrium, ensuring the dryness remains absolute, the columns remain pristine, and the leaves remain miracle-like testaments to their quiet bond with the mineral around them.

If these resonance-beings have a name for themselves, it is merely another chord in the symphony, a chord reserved for identity. To them, “what one is” merges seamlessly with “what one does.” There is no concept of single individuals—just a tapestry of consciousness that threads from the base of each column to the apex of each carving, a continuous field of presence. 

And so they endure, fused to the bright hush, always singing the labyrinth’s name back to it, always listening to the labyrinth sing it back. Their existence is an act of perpetual sustenance and watchfulness, ensuring that in this miniature domain, carved out of the bright, arid mineral of impossible geometry, nothing will ever waver from its perfect pitch. Generation after generation—if the concept of generations applies—each wave of resonance merges into another, and the labyrinth sits outside time, steady, luminous, and filled with a living symphony both indescribably loud and uncannily still. This is how they dwell here, unseen and inseparable from the environment, guardians and children of a timeless realm of echoing stone.
