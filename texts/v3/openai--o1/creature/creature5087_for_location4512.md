They do not announce themselves as you approach. One expects guardians or officials, or some bustling throng of factory workers, but the inhabitants slip in and out of sight quietly, with measured intent. It is only after you have stood in one place, listening for more than a few minutes, that you catch the sense of them: presences woven into the city’s fabric like threads of living metal—beings as interlaced with this sprawling plateau of constructed strata as any forest animal is with woodland soil.

You begin to see them in corners first, barely glimpsed silhouettes in the steam or flickers of motion beneath the catwalks. At first, they seem no more than elongated shapes hunched over control panels or perched along scaffold edges. Then a faint glint of eyes or a soft whir of a limb and you realize: these are not uniform beings. Indeed, they share almost no single trait except their affinity for the city’s mechanized labyrinth.

Some are slender, insectile forms, their limbs jointed at odd angles, tiptoeing across narrow rails in symmetrical pairs. When fully visible in the light, large sections of chitin glisten like tempered steel. They participate in quiet tasks—maybe cleaning, maybe repair, or simply performing inscrutable adjustments to small valves hidden within conduits. They scuttle with a measured grace, stepping over rivulets of condensation with the same care a mortal traveler might show in crossing a torrent.

Others appear more mammalian, though you can’t help but notice the metallic plating integrated into their hides. Their eyes may shine with an LED glow, or a segment of exposed tubing may pulse along what should be flesh and bone. Occasionally, a canine-like shape trots along the ridges of upper walkways, its footfalls light on the gridded metal. If you pause to watch, you might see it pause as well, muzzle tilting as though sensing vibrations in the stillness—a silent communion with the low mechanical heartbeat thrumming in the floors.

Sometimes you glimpse what might be the city’s original inhabitants: tall, spire-limbed figures that stride with uncanny poise. Cloaked in silvery robes or layered exosuits, they merge into the metallic environment as if formed from the same source. Few in number, always at some distance, these robed figures speak in hushed frequencies—perhaps subsonic communications that make the ramps and walls hum like a sympathetic tuning fork. They seem to orchestrate the city’s shifting architecture, gesturing with slender arms at a series of hidden controls or placing small devices in recesses. Walls slide apart in telling response, corridors reconfigure with incremental grace. One minute, they’re at the far end of a platform. You glance away, and they vanish when you look back, as though they stepped neatly into a space that wasn’t there a moment before.

But for every tall, regal figure, there are a hundred more ephemeral shapes: squat bipedal workers, drift-quiet watchers perched on spindly towers, half-seen flocks of mothlike drones that swirl around the bright overhead panels as if feeding on the city’s illumination. They come in all sizes and functionalities—some apparently nothing but optical sensors and sets of jointed limbs, others trailing intricate cables that spark gently against the metal rails. You get the impression that each cameo you witness is only a fraction of the population, that for every entity you spot, a dozen more sense your presence from vantage points in the labyrinthine layers above and below.

They are not a single species so much as a continuum between the organic and the mechanical. Observe the serpent-spined rodents that flicker in and out of pipe openings: they have real, fleshy bodies dusted with a faint electric shimmer, tails that might be flexible fiber-optic cables. The creeping vines across the surfaces might host tiny insects that are part plant themselves, nibbling at the vine’s metallic edges to absorb trace minerals. Sometimes you stumble on a small group of spiderlike repair drones deeply integrated into the vine’s structure, drawing nutrients or currents from it through needle-fine filaments. Life bleeds into technology; technology bleeds back into life. Exact boundary lines become impossible to draw.

Wind your way into the deeper levels, and you’ll find more massive forms. In shadowed factories or broad under-decks where barrels of surplus materials lie stacked, hulking, quadrupedal shapes prowl at the edges of sight. Whether they are guardians, cargo movers, or autonomous assemblers, one can’t say. Their torsos bristle with robust plating, and elongated arms fold out from their flanks to manipulate crates or raw metal sheets. Upon seeing a newcomer, they pause, heavy heads tilting with subdued curiosity before lumbering smoothly away, vanishing behind layers of scaffolding and humming conveyor belts.

And woven throughout them all is a curious sub-order: caretaker entities—less a distinct species and more a role that many adopt in turn. You might meet a spiderlike automaton carrying small cuttings of that spindly greenery, placing them in vents to encourage growth. Next, a lizardlike being carefully squeegeeing condensation off an intake grill, preventing rust or mineral buildup. Even the robed figures occasionally pause to repair a broken walkway or to calibrate a misaligned overhead panel. This entire place pulses with quiet devotion, as though each inhabitant accepts some measure of responsibility for sustaining the living architecture.

Then, there are the watchers—they might manifest only as outlines half-glimpsed behind a shifting wall or columns that flicker in and out of solidity. They may be partially out of phase, caught between one configuration of the city and the next. You sense they exist on some deeper frequency of alignment, akin to residents who live between the cracks, adjusting the city from within. Perhaps they watch over the anomalies in geometry, smoothing the corners where angles warp, supporting the corridors that bend in improbable ways. Their faces, if they have them, are not easily perceived, but you sometimes feel a gentle curiosity emanating from them in the silence, as if they register your presence with benevolence.

Yet, for all these inhabitants’ mechanical underpinnings or engineered traits, there’s a distinct tenderness in their interactions with the environment. Even the largest, most imposing forms move with consideration, mindful of the water channels and delicate vines. No inch of this place is left idle; no microclimate or side chamber is without its specialized occupant. Where the city’s geometry becomes particularly disorienting, you find those watchers or robed architects orchestrating subtle realignments, ensuring no traveler is lost forever in a corridor that folds back on itself.

It is not always harmonious—sometimes you see territorial skirmishes between overlapping subgroups, or witness a droning swarm chase away a curious serpent-rodent. From the marks on plating—strange burn-scars or deep claw-gouges—you can glean that conflicts have arisen before. Yet, overall, a quiet equilibrium settles over the plateau. Each inhabitant finds a niche, adapts to the shifting corridors, lingers for weeks, months, or years, then moves on or transforms. Evolution here can be immediate if triggered: a scuttling automaton might jettison a broken limb to sprout a new, more specialized tool, or a vine-dwelling insect might spontaneously develop an extra set of filaments to filter an unfamiliar chemical in the air.

Indeed, the city is less a simple dwelling and more an interwoven system of ongoing creation—its inhabitants the living (or half-living) proof that the place itself teems with an enigmatic energy, a slow pulse that fosters constant recombination. Creatures appear, shift their forms, blend influences from the next species over. Some eventually fade from view or recede behind newly welded walls, leaving only faint, cryptic signs that they existed at all.

And the question of who truly rules or ultimately built this place remains elusive. Perhaps there was once a single mind, or a collective, that initiated the design. Now, though, each being partakes of that lineage—maintenance, transformation, and caretaker roles woven into their behavior, as if every occupant is a steward of a broader blueprint. Watching them, you sense they’re all acting out a design inscribed in the very walls, following signals you cannot fully decode but can almost feel in the hush of the air.

In the end, “who lives here” is not a stable answer. It changes with each reconfiguration of the architecture, with each new vine that twines across a walkway, with each caretaker or robed figure who sets an expansion in motion. Some inhabitants might be micro-organisms making the condensation rivulets their domain; others might be massive, silent watchers drifting through interdimensional halls. The city fosters them all in a delicate, ongoing mosaic of metallic aliveness.

That is the city’s gentle, industrious secret: a place that, by intention or nature, extends hospitality not just to living organisms but to new forms of living—hybrids of gear and leaf, wire and sinew, code and pulse. A diverse, ever-mutable symbiosis. They are the caretakers, the dwellers, the watchers, the occasional wanderers. Together, they bring the artificial plateau to life, forging a meditative, humming realm where boundaries blur—and where, always, something else is ready to be born from the union of metal and breath.
