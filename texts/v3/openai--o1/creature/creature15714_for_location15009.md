They are not easily seen at first, partly because there is no standard silhouette or familiar profile to catch the eye. For a long while, one might assume nothing lives here at all, that the plateau is simply an extravagant garden without custodians. Yet eventually, a shape moves—just at the corner of your vision—and when you look, you see it: an elongated form gliding along one of the fibrous walls, as if grown from its braided texture. Then it slips away, vanishing where tendrils fold over each other like sentient roots. Turn a corner, and you may glimpse another shape drifting among wide-lobed leaves, or crouched in some curving alcove, obscured by shifting canopies of living green.

Those who dwell here—if they can be called dwellers rather than emanations—seem less like distinct organisms and more like parts of a grand superstructure. They might have limbs, or something akin to them: appendages that are neither fully plant nor fully animal, perhaps ridged carapaces grown from the same fibrous weave as the pillars themselves. Where skin would be, they have cells that resemble living bark, layered in undulating patterns. Their hues shift in time with the vegetation around them: while the plateau pulses with fresh green shoots, these beings glow a matching jade-like luminescence; when, by afternoon, the upper leaves tinge burgundy, they likewise deepen into dusky reds. They are perpetual chameleons, blending with the metamorphosis of the architecture.

At first, they move like illusions in the humid haze—skimming across vine walls, or leaning out over precarious ledges with no fear of sudden crumble. It’s as if the place were a sea of shifting shapes, and they are swimmers who have mastered every current. They climb the towering trunks nimbly, at times merging so seamlessly with the bark that they are indistinguishable from it. Sometimes they vanish into the heart of bulbous pods, letting the leathery walls close around them, only to reemerge minutes or hours later, apparently nourished or changed. Each new cycle, they might look different: taller, with a fresh cluster of leaflike frills sprouting from their shoulders; or more sinuous, their spines elongated like a series of interlocking vines. These transformations happen swiftly, often while they are out of sight, as though another hidden stage of their life cycle is at work.

No voice or traditional language passes their lips. Instead, they communicate through resonance—through subtle changes in posture, color, and the quiet, pulsing hum that quivers at the base of the architecture. If you pause in a courtyard at midday and press your ear to one of the towering stems, you might feel their presence more than hear it: an undulating tone hidden within the vegetative roar. At times, one of these beings will stand at a crossing of root-floor corridors and press both hands (or branches, or something in between) against the living walls, sending rhythmic vibrations into the structure. Moments later, a distant trunk might shift or contort, as though responding to the coded signal.

They do not appear to require conventional nourishment. Instead, they are nourished by the perpetual flush of water and heat that surges through the living labyrinth. When a water spout cascades from an overhead leaf-panel, they gather below in silent, collective ceremony, letting the droplets soak their bark-flesh. Their limbs unfurl, absorbing the moisture. Occasionally, their heads tilt back, eyes half-lidded (if they possess eyes at all), faces parted in an unreadable expression. It’s almost rapture: the plateau waters replenish them, carry them forward, sustain the next wave of shape-shift.

And these beings are architects—or at least co-creators—of the space around them. Where one stands leaning against a drooping leaf-wall, that wall might thicken, sprout a new layer of fibrous buttresses, or redirect the streams of condensation trickling down its surface. If a cluster of trunklike columns is about to collapse under its own reconfiguration, a group of these beings will assemble, gently bracing the structures until fresh support grows. They do it with the deft ease of caretakers, not so much designing as guiding a living system that cannot help but build. Sometimes they pull aside draping leaf-shingles to peer into dripping channels, checking the flow of water, then sealing cracks with a brush of their flexible digits. It is as though the entire plateau is one single, colossal organism—and these are its roving nerve impulses, ensuring balance, spurring growth, maintaining the perpetual churn.

It would be a mistake to call them peaceful in the placid sense: the environment is full of upheaval, and so are they. When storms gather in the hazy sky or when the floor-roots bulge as if about to split, you can see them moving in frantic harmony, arms outstretched, bodies performing an eerily synchronized dance. Together they coax the vines, reweave the ripping floors, sometimes letting the plant matter surge through them or cling to them until an emerging shape stabilizes. If a corridor collapses, they do not rush in panic but in a communal urgency, as if guided by a single impetus. When the threat passes, they disperse again into the swirling mist, as intangible as drifting seeds.

Their appearances vary across time and vantage point. Some share a slender, spindly frame with elongated limbs that unfold like branches swaying in a gale. Others are wide and hefty, supporting thick, trunklike torsos and layered plates of barky armor. Some seem to glow from within, the greenish luminescence pulsing along their rib-like ridges in a slow heartbeat. Up close, you might catch the delicate intertwining of veins beneath their bark, carrying nutrient fluids that mirror the water-channels in the plateau’s living walls.

They do not build shelters for themselves: their home is the labyrinth. They rest perched among spiny ridges or suspended from thick vines, letting the plateau’s gentle rocking lull them. At intervals, they vanish into the deeper corridors, where the roots churn in near darkness and the air is close with the smell of fresh mulch. Here, in the womb-like quiet, they press themselves to the pulsing floor, surrendering to the communal tide of growth that patterns the plateau’s every breath. When they emerge from these depths, they sometimes carry new outgrowths on their limbs—fleshy nubs that might later twist into fresh vines or extrude pollen-like motes that float into the humid air.

Across the wide, empty expanses—where plumes of fog swirl and the wind’s roar echoes—they glide with an unhurried confidence, sometimes stopping to sense the changes in air pressure or temperature. You might see one pause, lay a hand against the ground to feel a slow quake beneath, then alter course in silence, weaving from spire to spire until vanishing behind a wall of layered leaves. They appear to have no hierarchy, though complex patterns of cooperation arise spontaneously. If disputes occur, they seem resolved through a clash of gestures and color displays—flares of burgundy or gold radiating across their barky skin—until one group cedes or a harmonious resolution emerges in swirling shades of green.

No single name fits them—these living mind-shapers, these fluid custodians. “Symbiotes” could be one term, for they are entwined with the plateau’s continuous, restless growth. “Architects” could be another, though that suggests a plan or blueprint where there seems to be only organic momentum. “Chorus,” perhaps, for together they move and shift with a unified dynamic, each body a voice in the living ensemble. Yet each is distinct in subtle ways: patterns in their bark, the shape of their leaflike frills, or the timbre of their resonant song. They are individual notes in a greater composition.

With no animals or external predators to challenge them, they thrive in their own cyclical exchange of life energy and environment. One might assume they have no predators because the plateau itself offers none. Or perhaps those dangers exist in deeper places, unseen. But at least in these visible, swirling corridors of vapor and leaf, these choristers move with an unhurried calm, as though time cannot touch them. Each violent shift of wind or water is not a moment of terror, but an opportunity to reshape their realm, to feed its labyrinthine expansion, to build on its unstoppable flourish of life.

When storms roll in from the edges of the world and pound the plateau in surges of heat and water, the dwellers greet the onslaught head-on, arms spread wide, letting the torrent film their barky surfaces until leafy protrusions soak up every droplet. In those moments, they radiate a fierce vitality. Their luminous patterns flicker like strobe lights trapped beneath living skin, fueling further growth in the structures around them. Leaves expand, corridors elongate, colossal columns unravel into fresh tendrils. If you are present to see these transformations, you may realize that they are not mere inhabitants: they are catalysts, conductors of this grand symphony of flora.

To observe them for too long is disorienting. The eye demands the comfort of borders—of stable shapes—but these inhabitants and this living labyrinth share no such limitations. You might see one give a final, lingering glance before a wide leaf drapes over them, shutting them away like a door closing, its flexible membrane merging seamlessly with the corridor. An hour later, you return and the place is changed: new columns where old walls once stood, new drops of water bursting through fresh channels, no sign of the dweller except perhaps a shifting shadow behind a mesh of vines.

They are as new as the buds and as ancient as the plateau’s pulsing heart, forever weaving themselves in and out of living matter. Whether they were the first seeds that rooted here eons ago, or whether they spontaneously emerged from the plateau’s restless expansions, no one can say. Yet their presence resonates in every twisting vine, every echoing water spout, every shifting floor panel. They are the reason no metal or external framework is needed: each arch, each pillar, each corridor is guided by their silent interplay of signals.

So they remain: ephemeral yet essential, wholly belonging to this place of ceaseless renewal. They are its guardians but also its children, its living soul swirling in the thick humidity. Just as the plateau stands unattached to the horizon, suspended above an ocean of clouds, these beings exist beyond the framework of ordinary existence—part creature, part caretaker, part living architecture. In the hush between the wind’s bellows, you may glimpse them, in fleeting silhouette or fibrous-limbed embrace of a shifting trunk. They are elusive, unknowable, but undeniably alive, and their dance breathes spirit into every inch of this teeming, ever-transforming realm.
