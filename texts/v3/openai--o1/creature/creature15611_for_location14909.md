They call no single shape their own, for shape is still an accordion of possibilities in this young realm. Their world is defined by motion and convergence rather than by fixed outlines. Indeed, one might say that everything here is in the process of becoming, and so too are the entities that dwell within it. Far from mere static vegetation, they occupy the blurred region between plant and something more—sharing in the cacophony of dripping water and shifting earth like essential organs of a living body.

At a glance, they appear as towering trunks, tangles of vine, lamenting fronds, and pulsating fungal spires—each one part of a whole. On closer inspection, though, what looks like bark sometimes moves of its own accord. It thickens and contracts in pulses that might mimic breath. Portions of vividly hued leaves tear away from stems, drifting through the moist air, gently pivoting like living sails, before attaching themselves elsewhere—beginning a new phase of existence as an extension of some other trunk or root cluster. Even the broad shelves of fungal caps, shimmering with faint luminescence, seem to rearrange themselves by degrees whenever the living tapestries become too dense. There is no sense of rigid species boundaries. Instead, identity flows in currents across an intricately woven tapestry of green, gold, gray, and spectral light.

In that weave, one can observe a system of constant conversation—though not in any language humans would recognize. The drips of water from the canopy overhead strike these living forms with percussive force, like mallets on suspended drums. The impacted leaves answer, shifting hue ever so subtly, or else unleashing tiny spores that ride the swirling mist. The vines communicate in creeping gestures, coiling around a trunk with enough careful deliberation to suggest intelligence. Far below in the soft, embryonic bedrock, the bigger roots converge in tangles of labyrinthine shapes. Sometimes, these entangled roots vibrate in unison, producing resonant pulses—like the low, thrumming chords of an orchestra slowly tuning itself.

Perhaps the key to who lives here is that they are at once individuals and a connected collective. Imagine if a forest could alter its shape, not over centuries of gradual growth but within hours, forging new arches and bridging gaps in the canopy with living lattices. From one angle, you might see a trunk twist itself to carve out more space for a cluster of creeping vines, while those vines, in turn, tighten their coil to provide structural support against an encroaching slope. And deeper still, at the level of cell or spore, each is receptive to the signals of all the others. Nothing stands alone.

Some parts of this world’s denizens exude a bioelectric shimmer, reminiscent of the flickering static that traces certain leaves. Whether it’s a byproduct of photosynthetic extremes or a form of communication remains unclear. Delicate filaments whip out from hidden pockets whenever a droplet’s terminal velocity grows too fierce, as though to catch the water mid-fall, converting the impact into a soft glow. Here and there, pods the size of a human head dangle from vine-bridges, gently emitting pulses of color into the half-lit air. Sometimes they burst in a gentle pop, releasing swirling motes that spin off to seed some other corner of the canopy.

It’s not that these entities are devoid of ambition or curiosity—those concepts don’t neatly translate to such a place. Instead, what drives them seems to be a deeper imperative: to perpetually build and refine the living structure that spans from the forest floor to the looming altitudes. They are gardeners and garden all at once. Nowhere is this more evident than the half-formed plateaus, where the mountains and roots are locked in a co-creative dance. The “creatures” that dwell in those heights cling to the raw stone, sinking their root-legs into rocky fissures, knitting them together with fibrous strength. It’s as though they coax the very atmosphere into stone; in return, the shifting geology nourishes them, pulling up hidden minerals that feed entire networks of growth.

Sometimes, in the gloom, you might catch a fleeting silhouette—a slender column moving deliberately among the giant leaves. It sways as if guided by ocean currents, yet these are not the motions of a trunk anchored in place. No, it strolls—if walking can be done without feet, gliding across the flexible land-mass. Yet before you can focus, it dissolves into a swirl of tendrils and merges with a braided vine, as though the entire glimpse was just a momentary variant in an endless continuum. Whether these ephemeral shapes harbor consciousness in any sense familiar to us is one of the many mysteries that saturate the place. Suns and seasons might come and go without ever revealing the full extent of their awareness.

Further down, in pockets of humidity beneath the broad canopy, the inhabitants exist in microcosm—tiny leaflings that swarm together, each no larger than a coin, bending the moss-like ground inward to funnel shared water resources. Possibly they form nursery hubs for younger generations of trunk and vine. On the bark above, glimmering patches hover in an uneasy dance; these might be ephemeral seed colonies, or else some intricate spore-based intelligence that orchestrates shifts in the layered terrain. Observing them offers no definitive answers: they shift color patterns, sometimes shimmering in unison, sometimes scattering into fractal confusion, as though in mid-debate about what shape tomorrow should take.

In this place, life is not a question of isolated creatures staking out territory, nor of predator and prey. Rather, it is a grand, collective phenomenon—cooperative, improvisational, and ceaseless in its invention. These denizens, if one can call them that, are simultaneously creators of each other’s forms and results of each other’s transformations. They are the scaffold on which new life takes hold and the architects that mold it, an endless fractal of being and becoming.

So it is that, while no walking beasts or winged birds call out in the gloom, the realm is brimming with inhabitants. Each root, trunk, vine, and fleshy spore bed takes part, from the microscopic to the massive. Some are fusions of bark and fluid membrane, humming with an undercurrent of electricity. Others are more static grand pillars, anchors that hold entire sections of the canopy in place during the tremors of day and night. All together, they form a choral presence that resonates in every droplet and every shift of the embryonic stone. They are their own world, singing itself into being with every new root, leaf, and pulse of living matter, unceasing in their cosmic chant.
