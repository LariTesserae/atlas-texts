They move in the morning mists, these dwellers—and whether they’re human, a close cousin, or something altogether foreign, one can’t quite pin down at first glance. At a distance, you might only see a shifting shape, an outline half-lost against the green curtains of vine or the iron skeletons of scaffolding. They step deliberately, but not sluggishly, as if their actions carry a certain measured intent. Sometimes they glide in small groups, sometimes emerge in pairs with low conversation, and sometimes stand alone, gazing out from a catwalk or a courtyard as though taking in the entirety of this new world they’re shaping.

Their forms are as varied as the land, though not chaotic. You notice differences: some are slighter of build, wearing close-fitted garments that slip easily through the draping leaves; others are broader, clad in patched-together protective layers or hauling bundles of steam-flecked materials. Encounters with them at close range are rare unless you enter the heart of the industrial quarter or wander the muddy lanes between newly erected fences. There, you might discover someone kneeling at the lip of a stark drainage ditch, testing water clarity with a small instrumentation device that clicks and hums. You might find another perched upon a metal girder, winding wires with adept fingers, connecting them to gears that rotate gently in the thick air.

All around, their voices—if they are indeed voices—blend with the hush of steam vents and the song of local creatures. Whether they speak in a structured language or an ever-shifting code of whistles and half-shouts is uncertain, for their methods of communication are layered with mechanical echoes. You catch repeated patterns in the clangs and the hiss of compressed gasses, as though they rely in part on the rhythms of their own equipment to convey meaning. It is to them as a chorus is to a congregation: each engine’s chug, each pipe’s dull rattle, and each valve’s exhalation might serve a dual role—function and signal.

They’ve established small enclaves across the industrial district, each with a defined purpose. In one courtyard, dwellers with deft hands coax seeds and cuttings into rows of vertical planters that cling to corrugated steel walls. Here, the lines between gardener and mechanic blur: with each sprouting tendril or coil of vine, a new pipe or conduit is installed. Both the plants and the piping climb together, step by step, harnessing the district’s abundant moisture as if they had formed a pact. In another courtyard, heavier-set figures operate a slow-turning press that shapes raw ore into plates and rods. Their tools look ancient and futuristic at once—intricate gears within battered housings, patch-worked with fresh rivets. Occasionally, you glimpse a spark of what might be a low-voltage arc that helps weld and shape the metal into flexible lattices.

Elsewhere, more transient individuals roam on the outskirts, near the damp edges where the jungle claims foothold. They seem part scout, part caretaker, adjusting small markers along the fence lines, scraping away at vines that try to overtake newly laid cables. Their clothes—dark cloth, or sometimes gleaming harnesses of treated leather—are stained with green residue and flecked with the ruddiness of rust dust. When the wind bounces through the openings of those looming architectural shapes, you’ll hear them speak in hushed tones to whomever accompanies them, but from a distance, it might only be a breath against the hush of leaves.

The children—if that’s what these smaller figures can be called—dart underfoot in the open spaces, skipping between puddles reflecting the chimneys overhead. They squeal with delight at the amphibians that hop in and out of the shallow pools or the bright birds that wheel overhead. Now and then, one child stops to inspect the most mundane thing—a rivet left on the ground, a patch of moss climbing a ladder rung—and stares as though wide-eyed at a treasure trove. They gather these objects: lumps of ore, curled bits of fern, bits of chipped metal. From the giggles and the way they pass these keepsakes among themselves, you sense that collecting them is as important as play—maybe these young inhabitants are forging the next wave of tools or forging the next wave of questions.

In the machined shadows, you sense a certain reticence among the elders or leaders, as though they have no wish to rush this place beyond its current phase. They act like stewards of a slow-waking entity, encouraging the city to find its shape. Each building has a caretaker, each walkway a small group tasked with maintenance and gentle improvement. The dwellers place everything carefully: a new pipeline here, a gear assembly there, always leaving room for the vines or canopy that might come. They appear content to let flora decorate the steel skeletons without yanking it back or walling it off. Perhaps their engineering never strove to isolate nature in the first place but to form an alliance with it.

Their daily routines revolve around water and metal, soil and steam. In the early hours, they check flow meters in the newly cut drainage ditches, ensuring no harmful runoff seeps into the heart of the greenery. By midday, many cluster in open yards to hammer out fresh designs—schematics spread on wide, battered tables, lines representing expansions not yet built. The afternoons see them weaving through the deeper corners of the jungle-like thickets, pruning invasive tangles or collecting exotic spores that flourish in the newly warm microclimates around the steaming vents. Their evenings are quieter: low fires wink to life behind metal grills, warming the courtyards where they gather to share scraps of data, or meals that combine horticultural gleanings with the yield of strange mechanical contraptions that spin, churn, and process behind closed walls.

And it’s not just the two-legged dwellers who call this place home. The animals, too, have become participants in the settlement’s slow dance. Half-tame amphibians find niches in the condensation pools forming under angled rooftops. A variety of bright-plumed or dark-winged birds take to the looping cables overhead, nesting in the corners where rust meets root. Meandering lizards or small mammals scuttle across people’s boots without fear, picking through discarded scraps or gleaning seeds from nearly invisible cracks in the pavement. Even the insects, thriving in the perpetual moisture, show little preference for the dichotomy of steel versus vine—they treat it all as part of the same broad ecosystem.

There’s also a rumor, whispered among the dwellers themselves, that deeper in the industrial labyrinth—behind some half-concealed door or in the catacombs of pipe-laden corridors—there exist older inhabitants or older machines that predate the newly arrived populace. Some claim there’s a cavernous chamber where a single enormous device ticks away like a mechanical heartbeat, forging something yet unknown. Others tell of a subterranean garden teeming with phosphorescent fungi that feed on chemical drips. Whether these tales are truth or myth, no one seems worried; they only speak of them with that same calm acceptance, as though each undiscovered corner simply waits its turn to be woven into the tapestry.

Through it all, the dwellers maintain an air of serene focus. They do not scurry, nor do they idle. Their momentum is deliberate, paced like the steady march of seasons. The newness of this territory resonates with their own sense of fresh purpose. If they are forging an identity, it’s one that merges the might of metal with the fluid grace of living matter. Each day, new green shoots appear along the edges of corrugated walkways, and each day, a fresh plate of metal is riveted into place somewhere along the skyline. Bit by bit, the settlement grows outward and upward, like a greenhouse built on an engine’s blueprint.

In the small hours of the night, when the sky draws down a veil of shadow, the dwellers keep watch with subtle lights. Lanterns—some fueled by a modest chemical reaction, some electric, mounted on spindly poles—illuminate the soft haze. Long-limbed silhouettes pass between these lights, discussing the day’s efforts or lulling themselves into rest by the persistent rhythm of hiss and drip. Even then, the hush is never total—the machinery’s heartbeats and the forest’s lullabies overlap in soft synergy.

And so these beings, whoever they are, live as the place does: neither fully tamed nor wholly wild, perpetually on the cusp of forming something that is both mechanical and organic. They are watchers and builders, gatherers and planters, forging a bond that feels less like an occupation and more like a partnership with the land. From the whispers flitting through the cables overhead to the amphibians perched on valve cranks, every occupant contributes to the quiet concerto of synergy. There is a shared patience here, a reverence for the untapped potential that seeps through every puddle and powers every gear. 

In time, the settlement may flourish—factories grown into living organisms of pipes and bark, towers crowned by blossoming canopies. Or it may remain as it is, content in a newfound equilibrium of beam and leaf. Whatever shape it assumes, the dwellers embody its slow, intentional breathing, fueling and nurturing this harmonious evolution, day by day, drip by drip, clang by clang.
