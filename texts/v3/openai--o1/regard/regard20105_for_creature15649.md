They did not arrive with fanfare or cosmic heralding trumpets. Anyone watching for a grand descent or a trail of celestial lights would find nothing. They simply came—like a gentle ripple of resolve through the thick, humid air, sensed as a quiet hush among the endless roar. Not a body, not a face, but a consciousness of such limitless warmth and gravity that the plateau itself stirred in recognition. Whether they were an elder god, a primal force, or something else entirely scarcely matters. Their essence radiated compassion and wisdom in equal measures—a gentle regard for this ancient spire of dripping vines and paradoxical angles.

At first, the place did not seem to welcome or reject them. It remained what it had always been: a vertical labyrinth of water-slick stone and green swathes of life, punctured by illusions of fractured reality. Condensation beaded on the twisting roots. Curtains of moss swayed in the crosswind. The air was so thick with moisture that any mortal might choke on it, but the visitor only observed, wrapped in an eternal patience. 

As they drew closer to the plateau’s central enigma, something in the structure shifted: a vine’s tendril, no thicker than a finger, twitched nearly imperceptibly. A swirl of color that had circled the cliff face froze mid-dance. The presence of the visitor could be felt along the resonant hum of waterfalls, clarifying their tone as though a dissonant chord had quietly resolved. There was no spectacle—no parted clouds, no cracks of lightning—but a subtle sense that an ancient tension had just found an unseen hand to share its burden.

Days, or centuries (or seconds—it was near impossible to mark time in this glacial realm) passed as the wise and benevolent power simply dwelled in the presence of this impossible city. They observed each corridor and vertiginous drop, listened to the thunder of hidden cataracts that boomed like a cataclysmic heartbeat. They watched the illusions swirl at the edges of vision, gleaning the manifold secrets embedded in each flicker of warped geometry. Gently, they attuned themselves to the city’s silent watchers and quiet presences—those woven seamlessly into the architecture, those who lingered in the ribbons of color, those whose entire existence echoed through waterfalls.

Eventually, the plateau itself spoke to them, not in words but in a reverberant chord that pulsed in the rock. The message carried a question, or something like it: Why do you come? What need draws your gaze?

The power’s answer was silence. Or rather, a silent offering of acceptance, a vow to hold this place in compassion just as it was. No demand to be changed. No command to be altered. In that moment, vines and water and illusions alike seemed to quiver with a shared relief. Whether it was once a citadel of some ancient civilization, or whether it lived as a single organism, or whether it was a swirl of many entities in a cosmic tapestry, the place understood: they had found someone who would neither conquer nor disturb them, but only witness them with reverence.

Still, for all their gentleness, the presence did not come entirely empty-handed. Beyond mere observation, they brought a faculty to heal subtle imbalances—a gift not always recognized by those at the receiving end, for it could be as soft as a whisper. In the deepest chasms, where waterfalls had carved impossible angles, the illusions sometimes twisted dangerously beyond the plateau’s own equilibrium. Places where the seams of reality tore too wide, letting in fractal turbulence that might, in time, unravel the city’s delicate stasis. 

Toward these hairline fractures, the benevolent visitor lent a soothing resonance, like a guiding note humming in the key of existence. The ribbons of color stilled, aligning themselves more harmoniously. The warped geometry found a gentle nudge that encouraged it to fold inward without collapsing. The faint glows in hidden crevices brightened for a moment, as though they were exhaling millennia of tension. Gradually, as each seam found its equilibrium, the plateau’s illusions became less jagged and more symphonic, retaining their mystery yet no longer teetering on self-destruction.

One by one, the flickering, half-seen inhabitants—those strange watchers who lived in angles or existed as sonic shapes—realized the presence was not a threat. As though responding to an unheard invitation, they emerged from the corners of vision to gather in a subtle communion. There was no circle, no meeting place, but a convergent awareness: illusions, living vines, the city’s vast living architecture, and that compassionate power. Flutters in the frequency of waterfalls, coils of vines brushing stone arches as though in greeting: each inhabitant offered its recognition in the language it knew best.

In return, the mysterious visitor gifted only empathy, intangible yet immediate. A luminous hush drifted over the plateau. It did not silence the waterfalls—nothing could—but it softened the roar, turning it into a deep, resonant murmur that seemed almost comforting. It made space for the watchers in the seams, who could finally dance in gentler tides of reality. The vine-latticed corridors breathed more easily, no longer strained by chaotic leaps of dimension.

Some watchers grew bolder. A swirling coil of color, shaped vaguely like an elongated, spiraling banner, floated closer to the wise power’s core. Such ephemeral illusions usually retreated from any direct gaze, but here it found no hostility, only a silent encouragement to approach. When it did, the banner of color glowed softly, as if it had been waiting eons for this single moment of recognition. The benevolent presence let it pass through them—translating the illusions into a shape of shared understanding. In that contact, a quiet harmony sang out across the plateau, too subtle for mortal ears. 

Then, after a timeless interval, the visitor—satisfied that the subtle tears in the fabric of this place had grown calmer—began their departure. If one could call it that. They never truly came via conventional paths; they simply manifested as a diffusion of compassion. Now, that same compassion spread out, gently dissolving among the waterfalls’ steam and the tangled green corridors. The wise power did not vanish so much as they left a promise behind: that their awareness would always remain a whisper away, ready to return if the city ever needed them again.

And the plateau? It continued, of course, largely unchanged in its outward form. The towering columns still soared into the perpetual midday gloom, the water still roared in endless cascades, the vines still latched themselves, sinew-like, over dripping arches. Time did not resume in any normal sense; it had never quite flowed there in the first place. Yet something felt different—a slight ease in the tension that bound every mossy surface and twisting vine. The illusions seemed less frantic, the geometry less apt to tear itself apart in the extremes of space. The watchers, intangible and dimension-straddling, moved with a greater sense of belonging instead of unrest.

If you or I returned, climbing the ancient, flood-carved path under the relentless heat, we might notice only minute differences. The city would still seem frozen in a timeless hush. But perhaps, when the wind howled, we would catch a fleeting harmony in the roar, a ghostly chord like an echo of a cosmic kindness. Perhaps the unearthly shapes at the corners of our eyes might linger just a moment longer, as if aware of a new benevolence quietly woven into the plateau’s foundations.

No dramatic monuments stand to mark that a wise presence once visited. There are no inscriptions, no shining altars left behind. The place remains, as it always was, a grand collision of water, stone, and verdant growth precariously balanced on the edge of reality’s laws. Yet from that unseen vantage, an invisible grace suffuses each droplet and vine. The watchers continue to dwell in hidden folds of perception, the illusions still dance across the stone, and the entire citadel of overgrown wonders quietly holds a new peace in its vast, echoing heart. 

Such is the way of a power both wise and benevolent: to do only what nurtures, to heal just enough to let a place flourish in its own nature, and to depart without trace—save for the lingering sense that something has awakened into gentler harmony, one breath at a time, out of time, forever.
