Over the ages, slow as continental shift, a subtle awareness has gathered far beyond the sea’s rim and the drowned plateau’s horizon. Something patient and benevolent—older than the last days of dry land, and touched by a wisdom that does not count years in mortal terms—turns its serene attention toward the submerged city. It senses the gentle hum of conch-shell horns, the reverent weaving of kelp architecture, the measured pulse of web-fingered children learning to navigate watery passages. Like a soft beam of sun through deep cloud, its gentle perception settles on this realm, curious and calm.

In that vast hush, the wise power contemplates: Should it intervene here, where life flows at its own slow cadence and thrives in balanced harmony? Eons have taught it caution; to act is to alter. And in this place, everything that grows—every swirl of gilled cheek and every budding algae bloom—has found its rhythm, unhurried and sure. Yet it cannot help but feel a subtle pull, a desire to soothe, to guide, to guard. 

So one dawn—if the watery hush can bear such a name—the power’s presence unfurls like a warm current drifting from a distant source. Its arrival is not marked by thunder or by blazing light but by the quiet shift of water that glides along the plateau. At the edge of the city, where the kelp columns stand like silent sentinels, the people sense a gentle stirring of currents, as though the water itself savors a new vitality. Schools of glimmering fish pause in their dance, calmly parted by a force that touches without force, nudges without push.

The first to truly notice are the contemplatives in their outlying enclaves—the keepers of spore-lanterns and twilight-opening flowers. In the dim reaches, they feel a presence on the edge of their awareness that is neither predator nor passing phenomenon. News of this presence travels on the hush of water, from frill-cheeked philosopher to the swirling families who play in the city’s shallows. Horn calls begin, echoing in gentle intervals across the living architecture, signaling a rare but welcome visitor.

And at the center of the city—where braided kelp walls form a tranquil communal space—this benevolent presence seems to gather itself, as if breathing in the calm. The water grows indescribably clear for a moment, shimmering in subtle fractals. Sunlight from above glides through without any haze, casting bright motes across every emerald frond, every bit of ancient stone. The effect is subtle, a luminous hush that brightens hearts more than eyes. The people float in quiet astonishment, arching their slender necks, letting fin-like limbs brush aside drifting leaves as they look for the source.

Some of them close their eyes and feel the presence deep in their chests, as if it moves in sync with their own heartbeats. Others see, just at the periphery of vision, a faint swirl of golden luminescence that encircles them, protective and warm. The children, who know no fear of the unknown, laugh in delight as swirling eels dance with renewed vigor. The wise power has touched every living thing with a gentle whisper that says, “You are cherished.”

At first, there is no singular, grand purpose in this visitation—no demand, no sudden shift in daily life. The city holds its breath, waiting to see what this being’s kindness might reveal. And the being, for its part, watches with something akin to a parental wonder. It sees how the forested columns of kelp breathe in unison with the dwellings—how the people and creatures collaborate in a tapestry of equilibrium.

Over time, as the presence lingers, new possibilities blossom like coral on old stone. The wise power has ways of gifting knowledge that do not disrupt the flow. Perhaps it guides the dwellers to notice an overlooked species of bioluminescent algae that can help them mark more precise navigational routes through the darker ravines. Perhaps it sparks an idea in the toolmakers’ minds, prompting them to bind certain coral shards in a new weave that will reinforce kelp walls against strong currents. Perhaps it quietly nudges the philosophers to record the city’s oral lore more completely on stone tablets—a subtle impetus not to forget their stories, but to preserve them for distant kin who might wander this way someday.

None of these enrichments come with fanfare. They are gentle seed-thoughts, drifting into the minds of those receptive souls who dream as they tend sea-fruit gardens or rest in the shimmering half-light. When those new insights arrive, no one discerns them as divine revelation. They feel more like the next natural step in a conversation with the living environment. The city’s slow heartbeat remains the conductor of each fresh idea; the wise power merely provides a higher vantage, a quiet encouragement.

With its attention still present but never imposing, the benevolent force watches children who are now brimming with excitement. They have discovered the newly marked routes that descend into unseen corners of the plateau. There, they gather strange, prismatic shells once overlooked in dusky recesses, bring them back to the city’s communal gatherings, and weave them into shining tapestries that float along the currents. The shells glimmer with a rainbow sheen, adding musical clinks that chime gently in the swirling water. This small act brings the settlement a slight fascination, a sense of curiosity about corners of the plateau never fully explored.

Meanwhile, in the city square, celebrations become slightly more frequent. Each time the conch horns bellow with a gathering call, people drift from their algae gardens, from their weaving huts, from their stone tablets where they carve ancestral knowledge. They meet at the communal center, laying out woven sea mats and passing around carved shell cups. Inside these cups is a new brew, made with a beneficial strain of algae that the wise power guided them to discover: it warms them from within, and, with repeated use, even extends moments of watery endurance for those who still sometimes surface to breathe. Songs—a synergy of resonant hums, shell instruments, and the echoing calls of playful sea mammals—fill the watery air, carried far beyond the city’s perimeter.

Yet through all of this, the wise force remains unobtrusive, delicate in its influence. It understands that to elevate is not to dictate. It tends to the city’s potential like a gentle gardener who places a little extra sunlight here, a drop more water there, leaving the life itself to flourish naturally. And in turn, the inhabitants sense a deepening peace. They continue living in measured pulses, mending walls of living kelp, feeding the crustaceans that guard their perimeters, and coaxing new sea-fruits from the high canopies. But there’s a gleam in their eyes—an awareness that something kind has witnessed their existence and chosen to strengthen, not reshape, who they already are.

In the child’s laughter echoing down some corridor of braided fronds, the presence hears its own joy returned. In the artisan’s carefully woven vine canopy, it sees how creation can be an act of reverence. The city’s acceptance and wonder seep into the wise power itself, a reciprocal gift of belonging.

Eventually, seasons shift. The rains across distant mountaintops (far overhead in the shimmering sunlight) abate just a little, the storms that once roiled the surface calm for a time, and the plateau’s environment adjusts accordingly. The people adapt to changes in current and temperature as they have for generations, weaving new channels for a subtle shift in aquatic flow. The wise power senses the city’s continued equilibrium—feels the gentle interplay of daily life, the swirl of shell instruments that never quite still. Quietly, unobtrusively, it begins to withdraw its focused presence, trusting the inhabitants’ own gentle stewardship to sustain them.

But on certain dawns, when the water brightens in pale, rippled rays and the city’s horns resonate in a lilting chord, a faint glow moves through the kelp columns. It manifests as a swirl of golden motes, or a gentle ripple that passes along the seafloor. No one is quite sure how to describe it, but they smile with a deep, intuitive understanding. Children claim it tickles their fins and scaly arms, urging them to burst into new games. The contemplatives sense it as a glimmer brushing across their stone tablets, a reminder to keep writing every new chapter. The kelp-shapers feel their hearts lighten, as if they were gently reminded that growth takes time, but ultimately yields something magnificent.

And so that wise, benevolent power is never truly gone. Rather, like a distant star in a night sky, it keeps an ever-watchful presence, content to shine from afar. For the drowned plateau-civilization does not need radical miracles; it needs only the subtle assurance that it is known, appreciated, and gently guided when necessary. In doing so, the power sustains a promise as ancient as creation: that life, in its myriad forms, can flourish in ever-harmonious patterns, each unique realm treasured by an unseen goodness that passes through like a kindly current—soft and steady, echoing the city’s heartbeat in time with the deeper music of the sea.
