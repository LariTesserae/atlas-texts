They came in silence, drifting on a soft wind that rarely found its way through the canopy’s woven gloom. This presence—benevolent, ancient, and wise—arrived without fanfare. In truth, the forest’s thundering commotion left little room for ceremony, and the wandering power preferred it that way. They settled near the basin’s edge in a place of partial twilight, where the new ridges of earth cast elongated shadows. At first, the only sign of their arrival was a subtle hush in the calls of nearby creatures, as if a single measure in the wild symphony had gone quiet for an expectant beat.

Though it might be said they possessed a form, it was less a body than a concentration of will, a pooling of perception so strong it altered the air around it. Creatures passing close would pause, antennae or whiskers twitching, sensing a shift in the pattern—like a moment of calm amid an endless storm. Some circled closer, cautious and curious, only to be met by a feeling of warmth and gentle attention. Others merely felt a soothing hush brush across their instincts, then continued on their urgent scuttling or croaking or flight.

The power observed the trembling land with eyes that were everywhere. They took in the thick webs of vines, the sudden clamp of hungry plants, the swirling spore-ghosts that rose from fumarole cracks. They felt the quiet intelligence of the hominids hiding beneath logs and behind sprawling creepers. They noted how these furtive beings, huddled with flint blades and clay on their skin, survived by reading the land’s endless shifting moods. And the power observed, too, the half-formed magic seething in the humid gloom—an energy that quickened every sprout, every womb, every cracked egg, urging life to burst into being and transform again.

Once, a winged reptile with leathery flanks descended onto a vine-knotted branch near the presence. The wise being felt its suspicious gaze as it tilted its horned head, wings half-furled. The being’s attention fell upon the creature like a gentle hand, neither beckoning nor commanding, but inviting. A moment passed in reciprocal awareness—an exchange of calmness for curiosity—before the winged form launched off in a thunderous flap, returning to the canopy with only a lingering sense of peace in its wake.

Had the watcher been a mortal traveler, they might have trembled at this place’s perpetual chaos. They might have tried to drain boggy pools or carve out a path. But the benevolent power did not arrive here to conquer. They came simply because the land’s raw energies, and the creatures shaped by them, called out across the fabric of existence. This was a place of perpetual birth and re-creation. And so the power stood, intangible yet present, letting the scenes evolve in swirling tapestry around them.

Still, there is a difference between passive observation and true benevolence. The power understood that the inhabitants of this land struggled as often as they thrived, for everything here was in flux. At times, the land gave so generously—an overnight sprout of new fungi, a fresh outpouring of insects for easy feeding—that its creatures experienced bounty beyond measure. But within hours, a sudden mudslide or unrelenting rainstorm or lightning strike could wipe those same creatures away. To exist here was to embrace risk as fiercely as life. Many thrived, many perished, and the cycle continued without mercy or malice.

But the wise one sensed more than just physical challenges: beneath the chaotic churn, there existed a deep potential—a seed of emergent connection. The swirl of energies was not random, even if it appeared so. Here, the land was forging, like molten metal fusing in a smith’s fire. All these living forms—strange amphibians, scuttling arthropods, winged reptiles, creeping vines, half-feral hominids—were part of an unfurling design yet to be realized. And if one listened with a patient ear, they could sense a faint chanting chord beneath the cacophony, a thread that wove them together. The land was alive in a new, raw way, forging unity through unstoppable forces of creation. It only lacked clarity, a gentle guidance to keep that emergent unity from fracturing into destructive chaos.

So the benevolent power moved—though “movement” for such a being was more a subtle re-centering of focus—through the undergrowth, beneath massive leaves dripping condensation, across scalding fissure vents. Whenever they passed, the thrumming calls and roars momentarily softened. The air took on a faint luminescence, as if some hidden star had pooled its light among the dew. Vines that twitched in response to footsteps, closing like living gates, relaxed. Fungal spires that hissed spores at intruders stilled, letting the foreign shape drift by.

And in this drifting, they reached out—softly, slowly—to touch the hearts of a handful of creatures, not with words but with impressions: glimpses of a broader pattern, of a shared kinship that ran deeper than predator and prey. Some glimpses found their way into the dreams of the hominids. One might awaken after a restless doze in a hollow log, recalling a vision of vines twisting in unison, an unbroken circuit from root to canopy, from canopy to sky, from sky to water. Another might dream of themselves wearing the eyes of a reptilian quadruped, seeing the forest floor as unbroken territory that must be guarded or nurtured. These dreamers would stir from the gloom with a quickened awareness, an expanded empathy toward forms they once viewed only as threats.

The presence did not erase the reality of hunger, territory, and survival. They did not force cooperation. Rather, they seeded a subtle harmony: a new chord that, when perceived, reminded the dreamers (and those with open senses) that each living being was an expression of the same primal force. Over time, the wise one hoped that as the new ridges of earth hardened and the chain of life settled into deeper rhythms, a quiet sense of unity might shape how these creatures interacted. Even the scuttling hordes, upon receiving faint pulses of this empathy, might hesitate a moment longer before devouring an entire clutch of amphibian eggs, giving the caretaker a chance to defend them or safeguard a few precious spawn. The giant amphibians, newly sensitized by dreams, might adjust their migrations to avoid the rookeries of leathery flyers, reducing the violent collisions that so often toppled fragile nests. And perhaps the hominids, guided by fleeting visions, might gather in small gatherings not merely out of fear but out of a gentle desire to share knowledge and pool resources, discovering that a fallen trunk can become more than just a singular refuge.

This was no promise of peace eternally. The basin was too wild, too uproarious for anything so static. Its thrashing storms and surging quakes would persist, re-shaping the land every season, every moon, every hour. And the watchers—those who had glimpsed the presence in their dreams or felt that gentle hush in their chests—would still have to scramble for food, fend off predators, and outwit the next unstoppable flood. Yet, layered over the chaos, something new might bloom: not cities with walls, but pockets of shared purpose, alliances that lasted a little longer than a single day. Shared signals that might evolve into a more nuanced tongue. Perhaps, in time, these minds could learn to ease the collisions between species, to guide the land’s molten energies into new forms of synergy. 

In one hidden glade, the benevolent power paused to watch a freshly opened pool. Mud had slid away to reveal a hot spring where steamy vapors curled outward. Around this sudden oasis, a bizarre congregation had gathered: amphibians crooning in bright bursts, scuttlers clicking and tapping their antennae against the rocky lip, and a pair of hominids with bone tools, crouched warily. None seemed sure whether to claim it or flee. The presence drifted into that tension like a breath of cool wind, silently showing them—through a haze of shared sensing—how the warmth might benefit all if approached in respectful increments. The amphibians might lay eggs in the gentle heat, the scuttlers might feed on algae that bloomed in the mineral-rich waters, the hominids could rest sore limbs and test the waters as a source of daily replenishment. In that hush, each creature felt a faint sense of the others’ existence, glimpsing a moment of synergy beyond conflict. And for a breath of time, they balanced together in that glade, coexisting in unspoken truce.

Then, as always, the grand forces of the land reasserted themselves. A far-off lightning strike shattered the hush, and these gathered forms startled back into their separate instincts, the amphibians leaping, the scuttlers scattering, the two hominids clutching their tools. Yet they withdrew with less hostility than before, some intangible thread of shared experience lingering in the air. The benevolent one did not mourn the quick end to that fleeting unity; they knew the shape of growth here was fractal, built from countless small and repeated moments that crack the shell of fear.

In such ways, the wise power inhabited the half-formed land. They did not conquer, direct, or freeze anything in place. Instead, they danced on the edges and in the hollows, weaving threads of gentle awareness through the raging tapestry of creation. Wherever they passed, they brought a lasting whisper of possibilities—fragile, often lost in the roar, yet never fully extinct. An old seed scattered at the dawn of the world can lie dormant for centuries, after all, awaiting a single shift of conditions to sprout. So it was with these sparks of connection.

When at last the presence departed, even that was not a clean exit, for they had left echoes behind. Creatures across the basin would occasionally pause, startled by a memory of stillness, or a moment of inexplicable kindness. A hominid might share a new hand signal that meant “together” or “pause,” borrowed from a dream. A reptilian mother might tolerate a nesting neighbor a moment longer. A swirl of spores might momentarily shape itself into the outline of a passing beast, reflecting a memory of that guiding hush. The presence, no longer localized, flowed back into the deeper currents of existence, content that they had planted subtle changes in the soil of minds and hearts.

And the place went on as before—alive, roiling, half-formed, thunderous—but with slender threads of new awareness coursing through it. Call it grace, or a shift in perspective, or the faintest pulse of a collective soul. Whatever name it bore, the benevolent and wise power had left a gentler resonance in the perpetual drumbeat, an echo of compassion in a realm fueled by wild creation. And that echo—soft, persistent, unrelenting—would remind every species that even in the fiercest cycle of birth and decay, there was room for a glimmer of harmony, and for the timid beginnings of understanding among all who shared this living storm.
