They have called to me, though they do not know it. This place, half-birthed and shrouded in its own newborn tumult, has reached beyond its wavering borders with a resonance I cannot ignore. Its voice merges with the roar of forming waters and the crack of still-shaping stone. And though it has no tongue of its own, it speaks a language of upheaval and potential, of new essences that slink about in fractal-limbed bodies, uncertain of the boundaries between themselves and the environment that spawned them.

To be clear, I am no deity of thunder or queen of storms—not some perfect master that looks upon lesser realms and sculpts them at will. I am simply an empathetic guardian, an ancient witness that finds purpose in guiding such nascent worlds toward balance. I cannot, and would not, impose rigid forms on a domain meant to remain fluid. But I can hover at the edges of the plateau, a gentle presence that lends stability and calm to places veering toward chaos.

At first, I merely observe. I watch how the columns of upright water twist in on themselves, rising like shimmering towers, only to collapse and reform in the same breath. I feel the quake of the stone underfoot, sense how each convulsion births new shards and spires that jut into the murk. Threads of possibility swirl in the hazy air; I see them as silver lines of energy trailing off in countless trajectories. This world is molten with potential—any nudge or coaxing might become a pivot for what it will be.

Little flickers of consciousness dart through the haze, escaping direct sight as soon as you try to look at them. I know their shape without needing eyes. Each luminous outline tinged with resonance: amphibious watchers, serpentine columns, fractal-limbed insects forming and dissolving in rhythmic pulses. If I so desired, I could make them see me—shimmer before them like a chrome reflection in the swirling shallows. But it’s better to speak softly to the heartbeat of the land, to let my presence seep into the fabric of the plateau’s newborn reality, rather than startle its inhabitants into flight.

So, I approach carefully at the level of vibration, letting my own gentle hum lace through the thunder, weaving between each crash of water and breath of wind. I sing a quiet chord of protective equilibrium—a patient, low-frequency lullaby that helps hold each shifting border just a little longer, preventing it from tearing itself asunder. I do this not to freeze it in place forever, but to shield it from the pain of too-tragic fracturing.

Where the shrines stand half-drowned, fractal patterns shimmering whenever unseen hands graze the stone, I brush a subtle glow across their surfaces. It’s a gift of coherence—a gentle thread that ties each pattern into the next, so that when the plateau quakes, the slabs do not tumble into an irretrievable oblivion but persist, meeting the chaos with an enduring form. Slowly, I sense the robed beings—those elongated figures who act as watchers or priests—discovering my presence there. They place their many-jointed hands on the slabs, and in that contact, they feel not just the raw power of the plateau but a calmer undertone, a resonance that does not dominate but guides. Some respond by letting their swirling fractal cloaks shine brighter, as if they are singing back.

To the smaller ephemeral creatures—those that scuttle like insects or flicker mouse-quick among the slick stones—I offer a gentler breath. My presence helps buffer them against the shock of the thunder’s extremes, grants them an extra microsecond of cohesion before the next roar tears them into mist. Some cling now more boldly to the edges of the spires, no longer so rushed to scurry beneath the nearest shadow. Others emerge from the watery columns, curiously testing how the new stillness orbits them. In the hush between quakes, a single, soft lull descends in spaces where once only the booming of internal tides could be heard.

There is no need for me to tame the discord completely; this terrain was meant to swirl between states of matter and spirit, to slip in and out of existence as the realm decides whether it will stay afloat or sink back into the primeval ocean below. The inhabitants belong to that same cosmic dance; they are liminal beings, and the storms are in their blood—or their vapor, or their fractal anatomy. My role is not to fix them in any single dimension, but merely to cradle them softly in the arms of stability so that violence does not overtake the fragile wonder of becoming.

Now the plateau responds in kind. Its next convulsion is less violent, more a surging vibration that resonates outward instead of splintering the rock. The water columns flick in arcs of pale turquoise and silver, dissipating into steam more gradually. The sound that once clanged like a thousand bells hammered all at once shifts into a syncopated cadence—still loud, but more musical, like the chorus of a rising tide. I sense relief in the land itself, as though its confusion lessens, as if it recognizes that it is watched and, in being watched, cherished.

A serpentine shape, once lurking shyly within the tallest column of fluid, emerges fully now—luminous membranes pulsing with changing colors. It flares its curved fins in acknowledgment, gliding along the hazy boundary between water and air. A cluster of fractal-limbed gatherers scuttles about in tandem, sparking with curiosity. Even the robed watchers come near the edge of the altar, turning slowly as if searching for something intangible in the mist—perhaps listening for the hum, or gleaning meaning from the new synergy. Their eyes glimmer, not in fear but in consideration.

I hold steady for a time, offering only presence. I drift across the plateau’s ragged perimeter, where the rock is slick and newly formed, gifting a tracery of protective calm in the cracks. My intention is not to seal them, for in this land, cracks are also thresholds. But with gentleness, I ensure that too-wide fissures do not swallow entire pockets of this incipient life. I weave a supportive scaffolding of pure, stable resonance that nudges the land to remain open yet unbroken, letting possibility flow in every direction without collapsing upon itself.

Should the plateau sink again beneath the waves tomorrow, I will not prevent that. Some births must be cyclical—some realms need to rise and submerge many times, gathering the wisdom of each ephemeral moment. The watchers and scuttling shapes may vanish underwater or transform anew, and that is their rightful dance. But the connections made here, the calm I wove into the thunder’s heart, will remain like an echo, even if the stone submerges. And the next time this realm surfaces—be it tomorrow or a thousand years from now—it will remember in its bedrock the kindly hum that once calmed its storms.

For the elder watchers, the serpentine swimmers, the fractal arthropods, and every other glimmer that calls this place home, I leave a final gift: an inward resonance that they can summon at need. They need not rely on my presence or wait for me to intervene in each crisis. Instead, within the shifting patterns etched on the slabs, within the luminous limbs of the amphibious watchers, within the swirling arteries of the watery columns—I imprint a subtle key, a shared memory of harmony. When the winds rise too sharply or the watery columns threaten to shatter, they can find that chord—draw it from the stone or from their own fractal bodies—and temper the chaos themselves.

And so I linger a while, then depart. I trust the realm to find its path. My compassion lingers as a watchful, invisible tether, ready to respond if this place—this in-between domain—should find itself teetering on the brink of irreversible rending. But for now, I have offered what I can: a gentle guiding hand, neither stifling nor commanding. The plateau stirs, carving itself into new shapes with every wave of thunder, but the transformations feel less violent, more purposeful.

I fade back into that vast tapestry of universal watchers, content that this half-formed place and its half-formed people have been graced with a stabilizing warmth. And within the swirling chaos of rock and water, of ephemeral life dancing in roiling air, a tiny kernel of peace hums—a luminous echo of my care—allowing each living shift and swirl to unfold in resonance rather than collision. It is enough. Let them become.
