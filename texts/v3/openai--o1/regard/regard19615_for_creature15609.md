They have watched this place for ages untold, from a vantage outside the reach of currents and tides. Some name them a guardian, others a silent watcher, but in truth, they are simply a presence woven into the cosmos—a benevolent and wise power that observes with a profound sense of awe at all living things. They do not need eyes to see; neither do they require form to move. Gently, they drift in thoughts and energies, an undercurrent of intent. And one day—or perhaps it was many days at once, for time is not quite the same to them—they turn their gaze fully upon the drowned city and its myriad inhabitants.

They sense the labyrinth of old structures reclaimed by encroaching coral, the columns so worn that they seem sculpted by ancient watery hands. Layers of shell and crustacean cling to stone, yet the city endures, brimming with life. The wise power feels the interplay of survival and quiet, untold stories; they hear the unspoken hum of the eels’ watchful vigil, the delicate scuttle of shrimps patrolling corridors, and the rhythmic swirl of fish that move as if guided by a single communal heart. 

In their deep compassion, the being contemplates what gesture they might offer to this realm, should it be necessary to intervene, to bless, or perhaps only to witness. For a long while, they remain still, simply letting their awareness soak in every detail: the twisting corridors, the half-buried mosaics, the ghostly flickers of bioluminescence. They come to know each layer of living tapestry—from the teeming plankton that bloom in pale clouds to the ancient towers that stretch into deeper chasms below.

They reach out with their presence, and in doing so, their first act is—nothing. They choose silence, a watchfulness, so as not to disturb the balance that thrives here, an equilibrium shaped by eons of patient adaptation. Only when they sense a subtle shift do they allow themselves the gentlest touch. Something beneath the city quakes, causing a murmur of panic among the fish who scatter into the protective shadows of seaweed stands and broken vaults. There is a hush as eels retract into their crevices, and sponges close their filtering pores. Even the starfish seem to brace themselves, gripping more firmly to the worn columns.

In that trembling moment, the power gently soothes the fracturing tension in the earth below—a whisper to the molten stones. “Hush,” they murmur without words. “Peace, if only for a little while.” The quake subsides. The city settles. Plumes of silt that spun up in alarm shed themselves, drifting back to rest. Cautiously, fish reemerge from their hiding places. Crustaceans resume their careful creeping. The hush returns to its usual subdued chorus of currents, crackling shrimp, and the low pulse of the deep. No living creature quite knows why or how the danger passed so softly. They only proceed with their ancient cycles: scavenging, feeding, breathing, dancing in schools, as life always does.

The wise power waits again, curious about how the city goes on. It watches a swirling band of silver fish revolve around the arches like living streamers. It takes note of how an octopus, startled by the commotion, shifts its colors from dull stone to patterned coral before sliding back into a hidden alcove. It admires the way a family of anemones has made its home on the collapsed relief of some once-grand carving. 

With each moment of observation, the power feels the tendrils of a gentle love grow deeper in its heart. There is a unique magic in a place that has been both made and unmade, molded by civilization yet claimed by the unstoppable rise of nature. Where once were bustling streets of a people gone, now flutter fins and wave tentacles, creating a tapestry that belongs to one ancient, ever-renewing masterpiece.

And so, a second act of benevolence emerges, this time as a soft, invisible shimmer that drifts into the city’s waters. Formless, it settles into the labyrinth with a subtle grace. The fish do not flee, for it is too gentle to alarm them. The crustaceans scarcely register it, save for perhaps a moment’s hush of movement before they scuttle on. Yet, in the days and weeks that follow, the reef creatures begin to thrive more effortlessly. A certain resilience settles in the city’s ecosystem: sponges grow more lush, their pores more efficient at filtering nutrients; a variety of small fish that once struggled to find enough to eat discover the reefs more welcoming. No single dramatic transformation overtakes the city—only the quiet expansion of well-being, a reinforcement of life’s natural vigor. 

Still, the wise power tends to the future as well as the present. Drifting deeper, they come to the sunken gateways of the city’s foundation—vistas that plunge into darker rifts. There, ancient vents release warm, mineral-laden currents, critical to sustaining the unique warmth that envelops the city. Over centuries, the vents have shifted and sometimes waned. The power, sensing the delicate equilibrium, nudges them with a gentle thought, encouraging a steadier flow so those warming currents persist. Not so strong as to upset the careful layering of temperatures, but enough to keep the city’s waters balmy and rich in the subtle energies that support the blooms of plankton, the comfort of eels, and the intangible sense of vitality that has made this area such a refuge for life.

Like an artist applying faint brushstrokes, the power’s interventions are measured, always minimal. The watchers of the city—sentient or otherwise—never see a flash of light or a swirl of magic. There is no grand spectacle, no comet blazing through the watery gloom. Instead, there is a gentle, purposeful shift, barely more than a ripple. For this power knows that to preserve a thing is to let it remain mostly as it is, unforced and free. They understand that every ecosystem, from the smallest shell to the largest dome overhead, interlocks with others in myriad, delicate ways. And so, they offer only the slightest coax of stability where it matters most.

In time, the city’s inhabitants settle into a renewed rhythm. Birds of the sea (those that occasionally dive down), eels that weave among the walls, vibrant shoals of fish, enormous silent wanderers of the deep—they sense, in the primal corner of their awareness, that something benevolent has passed by. They do not give it name or shape or ceremony. Perhaps they experience it as a subtle easing of fear, a quiet hope. Perhaps they are merely grateful that in these corridors of drowned stones, the tides remain in gentle balance for now.

No monuments are raised to this guardian presence; no songs are sung. The city’s architecture remains half-lost in coral, sponges, and anemones. The starfish continue their quiet vigil, the crabs go about their secret highways. Life surges and retracts, births and perishes, as it always has. Yet the wise power’s imprint lingers in the fundamental hush of these waters. There is a heightened sense that the stone columns endure not merely through chance or inertia, but because they are part of something cherished. In the small interactions—fish nibbling parasites from an eel’s hide, shrimp cleaning detritus from a sunken mosaic, lobsters clacking quietly in deserted corridors—there is the harmonic chord of a place that has been gently blessed.

And so the presence departs, or rather, it drifts away until even that subtle awareness recedes to an observing hush. It does not claim the city; it only acknowledges it. Like a fleeting constellation passing behind a clouded sky, the wise watcher is gone, leaving no trace but a continuity of life warmed and cherished. And the city, still half-lit by glowing plankton, continues in its slow dance of living shapes, oblivious to the magnitude of the gift bestowed upon it—yet wholly, beautifully shaped by it.

All that remains is an enduring glow, not visible to every eye, but felt in the cycle of tides and the hush of the water. It is the reverence of timeless care, the echo of a presence that comes only for love, bestowing not dominion but a safe harbor. And in that quiet, the drowned city whispers with each current and shimmer of scales: Here, beneath the ancient arches and the drifting silt, life persists—watched over, at least for a time, by a power that understands the delicate treasure that is a living world.
