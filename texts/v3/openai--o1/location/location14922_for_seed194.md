It is dark—perfectly, almost oppressively dark. At first, it seems like a void, a space that never knew the touch of any sun or flame. Yet as you linger, you sense that it is not empty at all. The air is cool, crisp against your skin, but alarmingly dry: no hint of moisture, no trace of dew. You might imagine that nothing could live under these conditions—but there is life here, surrounding you, enveloping you, saturating every surface and corner.

It is all plant. Not ordinary flora, but a vast, all-encompassing growth of fibrous stems, bark-like lattices, and thickly braided vines. These plant-forms have spread into panoplies of overlapping branches and webbed sheets, shaped, somehow, into the outlines of walls, corridors, and vault-like ceilings. The entire space is a city built of living architecture, but it occupies only a hidden pocket of the world—a sealed chamber or dome, perhaps, though its true boundaries remain obscured by darkness.

Despite the sprawling growth, the interior feels strangely hollow. Tall, trunk-like columns loom at intervals like pillars supporting a grand temple, yet the open spaces between them are huge and resonant. If you could see, you would notice how each trunk sways minutely, how thick roots coil into braided floors, forming patterns of broad, empty courtyards. There is no water, no sign of trickling streams or condensation. Whatever fluids once might have pulsed through these plants has evaporated—or never existed at all. Instead, the stalks and vines seem hardened, robbed of all moisture, petrified in form and yet not dead.

In the hush of absolute darkness, you become aware of a curious noise. It grows in steady cycles, a low thrumming that swells to a roar before fading, only to surge again. It is uncannily loud—no melodic chord, but rather a droning turbulence, as if the entire city of plants is exhaling in a single, ragged breath. It rattles the woven walkways and sets ancient leaves to clattering against one another. No animals scramble in the gloom; there are no scuttling feet or flapping wings. Only the vegetation, singing its slow, resonant song to itself.

Despite the emptiness, there is an undeniable sense of habitation—of structures once lovingly coaxed into shape by hands that have long since vanished. Perhaps an ancient people, armed with only the simplest tools, shaped and guided the growth of these plants over centuries. Their methods may have involved chanting or arcs of primitive scythework, weaving trunks into archways, carving out halls in living tissue. Over eons, wind or some deeper planetary current has scoured these forms, smoothing edges, polishing surfaces, leaving behind an otherworldly skeleton of organic, interlinked architecture.

Everything appears frozen in this final, deeply ancient state. There are no new branches or fresh green shoots; it is as if the life that remains is in slow stasis, a never-ending twilight of minimal change and minimal energy. Here and there, faint hints of spore-like tufts cling to the bark, drifting in the swirling eddies of sound. These spores glow just enough—in some invisible spectrum—to create a diffuse sense of presence, though no human eye could truly see by them. If you close your eyes, you feel them softly pulsing, a subtle radiation of strangeness in the heavy dark.

With no sun, no water, and no creatures to stir them, these monumental plants have settled into this steady, final form: an eternal hush, broken only by that repeating wave of sound surging through the silence. In this place, time creeps. Changes unfold in the slow fractal of centuries. Perhaps the vine-walls thicken by an infinitesimal margin each year, or maybe a trunk occasionally cracks and topples, quietly rearranging the layout of rooms. Yet the city has been shaped and then abandoned, or so it appears, with no guardians to mend or alter it further.

And still the sound resonates: a deep, beating hush, rising, culminating, and ebbing. It is the only signal of motion here, a reminder that although the city lies in darkness, it is not entirely inert. Something in the plants requires expression—perhaps the slow release of stored gases, or the friction of woody fibers rubbing as the city breathes inward and outward. It is loud enough to feel through your bones, an unseen tide washing through the labyrinth of black corridors.

No single set of rules made this place; it was shaped by an ancient synergy of living growth and primitive, careful craft. Tools of bone or stone, or perhaps sharpened, petrified branches, must have once coaxed the vegetation into tall, sweeping shapes. But those who orchestrated this architecture have left no obvious trace, no runes or carvings, no relics of metal or pottery—only these silent, leafless wooden tapestries. Now, without animals to graze or people to tread the paths, the entire city remains like a hushed monument: plant upon plant upon plant, bridging vaults and catwalks of lignified matter.

In truth, it is difficult to grasp how large or small this world might be. It feels contained, pocketed, as though the city might end just beyond the next corridor—yet it could also stretch on through endless interconnected hollows, forever sealed from any bright realm outside. Here, in the cool dryness, time slides by without hurry, each century layering more dust upon the braided floors. You feel, more than see, the weight of epochs, the hush of a place that reached its final shape long ago and has only subtle pulses left to offer.

And if you stand quietly within that blackness, letting the droning wash over you, you might sense the echo of an impossibility: a metropolis of living plant in a waterless, silent cosmos, with no creatures to tend it and no starlight to nourish it. It endures on some deeper principle. Eroded by countless ages, it has arrived at this final, stable form—a city of silent, leafless trunks, weaving themselves into halls and courtyards beneath a roof of darkness, chanting the last slow rhythms of its own existence.
