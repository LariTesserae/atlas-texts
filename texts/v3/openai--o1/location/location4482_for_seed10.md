In the high plateaus where time has worn the rock almost flat, the air stays bitterly cold to the bone. Yet everywhere you look, it’s wet: drifts of vapor cling to rampart-like cliff edges, rivulets seep through hairline fissures in the ancient stone, and tangles of rooted vines weep with melted frost. The natural world here is so ancient it’s become half-fossil, half-living—scrubbed down by eons of wind and rain until most landforms lie smooth as old driftwood. Across this deep-worn landscape, rolling marshes form around dips in the rock, and the water, so near the surface, weaves constantly underfoot.

And then there are the plants—everywhere, in every possible form. Dark, moss-like growth covers entire expanses of stone, acting as the very bedrock’s living skin. Beneath the moss, giant petrified root systems sprawl out in fractal-like patterns, arching and webbing as if they once held up an ancient forest that has all but turned to stone. Yet the forest never truly died: newer layers of green upon green sprout and spiral out, forming living arches and columns, as if the vegetation itself decided to grow into vast, living architectures. It’s not thickly packed like a modern jungle—rather, it feels sparsely arrayed across a huge area, with immense pockets of open space ringed by clusters of bizarre, trunk-like vines. Twilight filters through tall, whip-thin stalks that are tangling with monstrous fungal blossoms, an endless tapestry of cold, damp greenery stretching outward.

Despite the absence of any visible manmade structures, there is a strange, mechanical heartbeat to this realm. The environment thrums and roars off and on like a distant engine turning over. At times, low metallic booms ripple along in the wind, as if some hidden machinery in the ground is propelling the water upward in roiling jets just below the surface. There are no factories or smokestacks, nothing that resembles technology in the human sense; still, the continuous hum has the texture of pistons firing and steam valves releasing. It’s like the land has mechanized itself, a colossal living contraption fueled by breath and composting vegetation, whose power resonates through the low clouds.

Those clouds shroud nearly everything in a faint twilight. Light here always seems muted—sunbreaks come rarely, and even then, you only see a silvery glow instead of a bright glare. The reflection off the wet stone and the glossy, dark leaves can be momentarily blinding, but it soon fades back into a deep, pearlescent dimness. Shadows stretch in odd directions; the swirling mists can twist them into illusions, making it seem as though the distant vines are writhing or that the very ground itself is curling upward.

Fauna thrives in this damp chill, though it amplifies the eerie vibe. Strange, pale-furred mammals dart near the pools, their feet never quite splashing, as if they’re half-riding the surface tension. Stalk-eyed amphibians slither out onto the slick stone, fiddling with tendrils of algae as if completing arcane tasks. Swarms of radiant insects swirl together, forming clouds that mimic the pressure and motion of thunderstorms, flashing with internal sparks like a swarm of tiny lightning bugs. Far-off roars or screeches echo among the marshes—some sound like howling wind, some like the grinding of metal, some like an entire menagerie in conversation.

All of it moves with a feverish energy, as if the place is permanently locked in a tumultuous state of flux. Plants coil and uncoil in real time; you can almost see them shifting positions as if they have somewhere urgent to be. Water runs freely in loops and channels, sometimes reversing direction spontaneously, as if some undercurrent or subterranean stirring sends it back the way it came. And at irregular intervals, sudden gusts whip across the plateau, toppling lesser forms of growth, tearing off sheets of moss, sending them into the air like bizarre kites.

Reality here feels unstable, as though something is quietly breaking the rules. The plants grow at impossible angles or seem to defy geometry. The cold wind can whip into a sudden near-hurricane only to vanish just as quickly. Even the ground occasionally does strange things—like flash over with a thin layer of ice that disappears moments later under the next drizzle. The line between vegetation and stone becomes unpredictable; you might step in what looks like solid stone only to have your foot sink into a thick, spongy tangle of roots, the boundary between mineral and living tissue blurred.

Volumes of sound ripple and crash across these heights, loud enough to echo into the distance. Great reverberations roll underfoot, and it’s never clear if they come from deep tectonic shifts or from the land’s own rhythmic, engine-like metabolism. Sometimes the noise grows so deafening it drowns out thought—an almost orchestral cacophony that peaks and distorts like an overworked factory line. Then it dims abruptly, leaving the plateau in a heavy hush broken only by trickling water and faint insect chitter.

Through it all, no lines or shapes betray that any sentient hands have crafted or built here. The place is untouched—raw and primeval in some ways, yet uncannily mechanical in others. It is as if the land itself invented industrial processes millennia ago, evolving internal piston-beats and gearlike rhythms as part of its own strange biology. Where the lines between life, metal, earth, and storm begin or end is impossible to say. Everything bleeds into everything else, and the sense is that the entire plateau is alive, thinking, and changing from one moment to the next.

Some days, a watchful traveler might guess the cold storms would flatten this bizarre wilderness once and for all—yet it never happens. The plateau endures, propped up by an ancient inevitability. The plants keep rising. The loud pulses keep pounding. The watery air keeps condensing. In this turbulent world of cold and wet and abundant life, ancient stone and churning undercurrents throw all sense of normal physics into question. It’s a realm that’s at once old beyond measure and alive with frantic births and breakdowns—a dim, gray-lit domain where reality itself occasionally slips its rails, letting the weirdness peek out in bursts of thunder and swirling clouds of luminous insects. It is a final place—a place shaped by time and reclaimed by vegetation’s unseen machines, humming and pounding away beneath the wet highlands, in a never-ending storm of growth and decay.
