It is an expansive lowland of mineral and water: a place of rivulets winding through rocky channels, condensation beading on cold stone, and darkness pressing in from every side. There is no tallest cliff or proud ridge to mark your bearings—just subtle undulations in the earth, where broken, timeworn rock emerges to form natural gutters that glisten with pools of collected moisture. Water seeps up from unseen cracks, then drips and slides away in narrow runnels, dappled by pale reflections of a light that never seems to reveal itself. The air is perpetually cool, clinging to your skin and to the craggy surfaces around you with a damp hush.

At first, you think the place is empty. No sign of green, no leaves or stems or fronds. Nothing that might be called a plant. Instead, a thin, lichen-like crust clings to the stone, but closer inspection shows it isn’t vegetation at all. Some of it is spongy rock or ever-so-faintly bioluminescent mineral. Some is the residue of something else—exudates from the countless life-forms that dwell in this dark realm. You can smell a faint tang, as of wet copper or iron. The ground is riddled with cracks, and the centuries of gentle, persistent erosion have softened the edges into time-smoothed grooves.

The emptiness doesn’t last. A surge of motion or a flicker of eyes in the gloom cues you to the truth: this land crawls and thrums with overwhelming fauna. They are layered upon each other—tiny chittering insects with translucent carapaces bob in rivulets next to damp-skinned amphibious creatures that lounge among the shallows. Above, at some points, swarms of small flying shapes swoop, drawn by chemical signals drifting on the cold air. Larger silhouettes lurk in muddy hollows, rippling out of the darkness on slick, scaled limbs. Even the stone walls seem alive with creeping shapes that scuttle sideways on too many legs or slither with improbable speed.

In this nearly unlit realm, the animals have adapted with fantastic strangeness. Some creatures emit low-frequency croaks or pulses that echo across the dripping rock, forming a constant, resonating thunder. Others click or hiss in rhythmic cycles, a press of insectile voices that crash together into a deafening wall of sound. Touch the ground with a fingertip, and you’ll feel the vibrations of a thousand footfalls, a seething tapestry of motion beneath your hand.

The darkness is so profound it seems to swallow all but the simplest reflections: the gleam of pale water surfaces, the glint of eyes flicking open, the erratic bioluminescent flickers from certain wormlike things that snake across the rock. Every now and then, a cold draught sweeps through, stirring the condensation and sending droplets to patter onto the ground. The climate is in constant motion: breezes that carry faint chemical scents from one den of creatures to another, swirling drifts of moisture-laden air that condense on any surface they touch.

Despite the heavy darkness, the land stretches on and on, seemingly without boundaries. You can sense it continues well beyond what your eyes can see, a warren of lowland flats and shallow ravines weaving into each other. In the distance, you can sometimes detect a slight slope or a basin where water has pooled. If you peer out, you’ll see countless animals crossing each other’s paths, an ecosystem with no recognizable plants to anchor it—somehow sustaining itself on mineral leachings, chemical processes, and relentless predation. On every stony outcrop, things cluster in layers, jostling for space, clinging to wet surfaces.

No humans have left their mark here: there are no ruins, no cut stones, no signs of machinery. Just a natural world so densely alive that you feel you’ve stepped into another planet’s version of a food chain. Strange, savage life forms pursue each other across the glistening rock, singing in croaks, shrieks, and drumming carapaces, the sound so loud it rattles in your chest. The environment feels ancient, as though it has quietly existed for eons, wearing down rock into silt, forging and unmaking alliances among the creatures through every damp, throbbing generation.

The intensity of the place grows by the second: the chorus of sounds swells into a ceaseless roar. The fauna surges and shifts as one species hunts another, small forms scattering in a frantic scuttle when something larger glides into view. It is a world of ceaseless competition and bizarre adaptation—where phosphorescent secretions, echolocating croaks, and chemical-lure tongues stand in for any sunlight or greenery that a more typical ecosystem might rely upon. Here, you are surrounded on all sides by living, breathing strangeness, a density of life that hums against your skin and drowns your ears in a cacophony that never rests.

No single vantage point can capture it all. You only ever see one part of the vast, archaic realm at a time, scanning shadows in hopes of glimpsing the next revelation. Step carefully—you might place a foot upon a creature that has quietly settled on the damp stone, or brush against a colony of a thousand eyes embedded in a living mat of mineral-flesh. Every small movement, every breath of chill, each distant shift of water, merges into the unending cycle of hunt and hide, of birth and decay. Here in the darkness, life continues relentlessly without respite, unbothered by the absence of sunlight or greenery—an endless, active hush of drips and echoes and chitinous thunder in a place that stands outside human notions of day or night.
