High above any known horizon—though it’s far too dark to see it—there lies a cramped and searing realm, its boundaries so tight as to feel like a sealed crucible. The air (or what passes for air here) tastes of minerals and acrid chemicals. There is no water anywhere: no pools, no streams, no sense of moisture at all. Surfaces appear etched from dry crystals, newly formed and not yet worn down by erosion. In truth, the entire place seems unfinished, the rock and mineral only just taking shape as if the world were still deciding what it wants to be.

The heat is intense: it presses in on all sides, radiating out of the coarse walls and floors. High elevation typically suggests thin air, but here every inch of space is packed—made dense by swirling fumes and crowds of living things. Wherever you step, you feel a shifting clutter of bodies beneath your feet. Still, there is no vegetation of any recognizable sort: not even the barest sign of fungus or lichen. Instead, life takes other forms. Hard-shelled creatures with jagged limbs scuttle in the dimness, their movements generating a perpetual rasping chorus. Faint, pulsing lumps cling to protrusions in the rock. Some appear like throbbing sacks or pods, occasionally emitting steam or bursts of sparks. Others, more mobile, grunt and drone as they push past each other, forming a continuous mass of living noise.

The darkness is nearly absolute, broken only by the dull glow of molten seams or the spark-flashes of creatures that channel the heat. The rock underfoot crackles as though new veins of minerals are cooling only moments after being born. No breezes blow, and yet the air roils with a heavy commotion—the stirring of countless bodies, the hiss of chemical releases, the low mechanical thunder of something deeper. It feels industrial by nature, not because there are factories or pumps (there are none) but because the processes here mimic the grind and churn of vast machinery. Pressurized gases whistle through veins in the stone, and the entire environment hums with a metallic resonance, as though the hillside itself is exhaling.

Despite the craggy expanse, you never see far. The space is claustrophobic and strangely pocketed, as if it all fits into one tight chamber that extends upward more than outward. Rising shapes of rock surge up around you with minimal definition—as if whatever force formed them had only just begun sketching out a mountain range. This embryonic geology reveals smooth expanses that suddenly taper into spires and scalding ridges. Some segments appear so new that they flake away under the slightest pressure, while others are sturdy enough to support entire swarms of the local fauna.

Though the place teems with life, it is a slow realm in terms of change. The creatures move constantly as a crowd, but their movements follow a timeless pattern—subtle flows of shifting positions. One swarm peels back, another slides in, a slow and patient cycle as the entire horde exchanges places. Every now and then, the largest of these creatures, with segmented legs and bulbous abdomens, release low rumbles so loud they vibrate the rock walls. Their calls mix with the incessant clack of exoskeletons colliding underfoot, making the entire space roar with unrelenting sound. Even so, the overall transformations here—rocks forming, ridges sharpening, gullies deepening—take place at a near-glacial pace.

No structures interrupt the ragged slopes or cluster in the various pockets; there is no hint of civilization. But the environment still echoes of heavy industry: each hiss, scraping whir, and deep rumble could be mistaken for machinery turning over on a production line. Gaseous vents open and close in slow intervals, releasing clouds of biting fumes that swirl together in swirling curtains. It’s as if the planet itself, locked in darkness, powers an enormous engine of life without needing water, sunlight, or any plant to mediate. Everything is mineral and heat, the chorus of creatures, and the earth’s interior forging new shapes out of scalding potential.

In the end, this small, high, punishingly hot domain seems both impossibly full—teeming with half-seen swarms—and also strangely minimal. No water flows, no tree or tendril grows, no tower or pipe stands anywhere. It is raw, forging itself, a hidden chamber where life swarms in dryness, whirs in the echoing dark, and reshapes hot rock through gritty, unhurried cycles. Over it all, that relentless din: the great factory of breathing creatures and newborn stone, all under high pressure, locked away, inching forward at the pace of an ancient forging process that never ends.
