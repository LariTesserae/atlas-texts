From a distance, the place appears as a series of broad terraces hewn into a vast basalt plateau, high enough that the horizon sometimes dissolves into low-hanging clouds. There is water everywhere, though it seldom gathers into true rivers or standing lagoons; instead it trickles along countless channels cut into the black stone, weeps from cracks in cliff faces, and condenses on every surface until all is slick and faintly reflective. By midday, the humid light has a gentle brightness to it, illuminating the winding structures that sprawl over the plateau like a puzzle of bristling ramps, arches, and arc-walled enclosures. 

Though it is warm here—no breath of biting cold—there is surprisingly little greenery to be found. Instead, a faint sheen of slick algae or grayish fungus might coat the undersides of walkway railings, and rare patches of spongy lichen cling to the basalt. But full-fledged plants are absent at these elevations. This near-lifeless mineral scape, however, is anything but empty. Life rummages constantly here, a fervent and overwhelming fauna that has found ways to thrive without the usual greenery. Creatures the size of cats slip between drainage pipes, seeking moisture. Others, sporting bizarre exoskeletal forms, clamber across the terraces, nibbling at hidden fungal blooms. Overhead, large winged beasts glide in broad loops, riding unseen thermals in the warm air. Their presence, and their keening calls, fill the sky with a perpetual, echoing chorus.

It is a city—industrial in feel, but older than its clanking, steam-wreathed factories suggest. Pipes and conduits loop around monolithic lumps of basalt, anchored by thick rivets hammered into shelves of rock that show centuries of weathering. The architecture seems grown more by necessity than aesthetic vision. Tall, rust-streaked towers loom, connected by high catwalks and metal lattices. Some overspill with mechanical apparatuses that churn and hiss with a slow, methodical rhythm, as if the city as a whole breathes in time with its machines. The noise is prevalent: engines rumble in the background, flocks of creatures cry out, and wind moans through the labyrinth of narrow vents and corridors. It rarely rises to a deafening roar, but it never slackens to true quiet either—just a resonant, omnipresent loudness that marks every hour.

And yet for all its bustle, change comes slowly. The city has stood too long and become too comfortable in its precarious balance. The basalt beneath it is worn into shapes that speak of ancient floods and soft, meticulous erosion. Oddly symmetrical channels run parallel to natural fissures in the rock, indicating some grand design from a prior age. Occasional scaffolds cling to steep chasms, offering vantage points over the watery rivulets that vanish into the hazy depths. Workers come and go—part human, part something else, dwarfed by the scale of the massive columns and ramparts they tread. Their days blur in the endless maintenance the city requires: clearing clogs in water channels, patching up leaks within the pipeworks, or safeguarding hatcheries in the nooks of half-collapsed towers. 

The atmosphere is damp but not stifling. Morning hours bring swirling fog that collects along the terrace edges, gently beaded with the flapping wings of scavenging fliers. In the midday light, a mild glow seems to make everything shimmer, the rock surfaces shining with condensed moisture. By dusk, the same clouds settle in, and the city’s lights shimmer across wet stone, gleaming like thousands of scattered embers. Industry continues at all hours, but labor routines extend in long, slow cycles—here, things rarely change overnight. Generations of the local fauna have established migratory routes through the network of terraces, some bounding across pipes with practiced ease or gliding on expansive membranes across chasms. Their diversity is startling: many-limbed amphibian-like dwellers in the puddles below, thick-plated creatures that rasp at the fungal coverings on pipes, and an endless swirl of winged forms overhead. Too many to name, too many to easily count—a layered zoo teeming with life.

Despite its grand scale and hum of factory work, there’s a stalled quality to the city’s development, as though it reached a complacent apex centuries before. The patina of rust and soot across the structural beams hints at generations of the same routine. While the industrial hum never stops altogether, it seems to produce less of a drive forward and more a sense of perpetual, cyclical toil. The result is a balance, a slow dance of survival: water seeps in, is routed where it must go; the basalt weathers but remains strong; the machines churn, forging the ingots and components the city always needs for repair. 

And threading through it all is that faint whiff of something unusual: a place of warm stone and ceaseless condensation, of mechanical invention anchored to an ancient, dripping plateau, where fauna looms in staggering numbers despite the absence of flourishing vegetation. It is a world that unsettles visitors with its countless chittering faces, echoing calls, and alien normalcy. And yet, within the slow pulse of its factories and the timeless sweep of clouds along the ridges, the city endures—patient, colossal, and half-forgotten in the mists of tomorrow.
