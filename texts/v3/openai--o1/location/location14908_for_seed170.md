No one expects so much life in a place so devoid of water and plant growth. At first glance, this high-rising plateau of mineral shards seems empty: a vast expanse of pale, sun-blanched ground that has cracked and splintered under waves of heat. There are no streams, no glimmer of moisture. The bare stone and gravel seem to have endured incalculable ages of wind-carving and slow chemical processes, their surfaces worn to soft curves and sharp edges in equal measure. From a distance, it looks inert—like the dusty shell of a world where nothing grows. But stay awhile and the deeper truth begins to hum through the air.

Close your eyes, and you’ll hear it: a low, pounding drone that swells in the silence like a far-off machinery line. You wonder if there might be factories hidden behind those jagged ridges, belching steam or black smoke into the open horizon. Yet there are no smokestacks, no pipelines or walls to be found—only an echoing industrial clamor that resonates invisibly, as if the environment itself is both engine and factory. The land is undeniably hot, the harsh sun awarding no mercy, and the mind easily wavers in the shimmering mirages. But this hum is not an illusion. It is the chorus of the creatures.

They emerge from burrows in the rock, from hidden crannies in the labyrinth cliffs. They are too numerous to count and yet strangely camouflaged; you almost miss them outright if you don’t look carefully. They fill the plateau with ceaseless movement: leathery, chitinous forms that clatter and click, each limb joint sounding faintly like metal rods striking stone. Others are ephemeral, flickering shapes of energy that flick across the mineral surfaces in arcs of heat lightning, turning corners then merging back into the underlying substrate. Some are squat, lined with ridged plates, while others loom tall—a confusion of bristling spines and rigid flanges. Many have eyes set in cluster formations or fractal patterns across their carapaces. Thick columns of them scuttle across the open ground with mechanical single-mindedness, heedless of the blazing temperature and the emptiness around them. Whatever fuels them isn’t water or leaf—it’s more akin to some unknown alchemy, an alien circuit of life fueling itself in a realm of dryness.

Their presence is overwhelming, not because they press in on you—if anything, they keep their distance—but because they number in the thousands, perhaps millions, saturating the region with motion and unearthly sound. Now and then, a deeper roar echoes from high above. It could be monstrous wings beating the scorching air, or perhaps an immense gear turning in some invisible skybound engine. If you look up, you might catch the silhouette of a gargantuan shape flickering against the normal daylight. You cannot be certain if these are truly living beasts or some breakdown of reality’s stable rules: they vanish and reappear in stuttering intervals, making it hard to judge size and distance. Everything about this place has a gentle fissure in reason—objects shift and distort in peripheral vision; sounds echo in repeating loops, as though the land wants to replicate and amplify every clank, buzz, or hiss.

Despite the frenetic bustle of the creatures, the environment itself hardly changes on human timescales. It has been this way for eons and will remain for eons more. Windblown minerals pile into ephemeral dunes and then degrade back into the exposed stone. There is no sense of progress or development—merely a slow wearing away, eroding in cycles older than memory. Even the fauna seem locked into grand patterns: a season-long migration from ridge to hollow, a centuries-long metamorphosis that recurs and leaves behind only faint outlines in the dust. The dryness ensures no footprints last for long. The scuttling horde might vanish at twilight, but their presence endures in the mind.

Perhaps the strangest aspect of all is that intangible hum, that incessant industrial chorus. It radiates from the teeming life forms, but also from cracks in the plateau floor itself—an ever-throbbing pulse like a million hidden turbines spinning in slow unison. You detect neither motors nor boilers. There is no tangible sign of a foundry or a power grid. Yet the hush between each rhythmic wave of sound bristles with mechanical intention, as if the place is itself one vast engine, using living creatures as pistons or relays in some cosmic mechanism. It is loud, yes, but you become oddly accustomed to it with time—like the constant rumble of a waterfall or an avalanche that never completely finishes. The plateau resonates in your bones.

Impossible as it might sound, this is a land of scorching dryness, no water and no greenery, yet brimming with a life born of raw heat and mineral. A place of emptiness, unpopulated by anything built or civilized, yet shaking with an industrial ferocity that knows no human blueprint. The reality here warps at the edges. An overabundance of cryptic creatures glide and crawl, humming in unison as geological ages pass in the blink of an eye. And as you stand there in the normal brightness of midday, dust swirling around your feet, it feels like the plateau might shift under your weight, as if the world’s crust is perilously thin—ready at any moment to open and disgorge new impossible shapes from beneath. This is a realm locked in a slow dance of living industry, an untamed engine of stone and scorching air where time itself corrodes away, leaving behind inexorable, reality-testing life forms to churn and swirl in an unending cycle of uncertain origin.
