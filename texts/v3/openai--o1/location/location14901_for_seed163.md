Roughly halfway between clouds and solid ground, a plateau rises high into a dazzling, sunlit sky. The air is cool against the skin, though not icy, made all the more refreshing by the constant presence of moisture: night-time mist lingers late into day, creeping along the ground, and thin streams of condensation trickle over sheer, rocky faces. Mosses and slick carpets of green have taken hold in every crevice, so that living matter seems to be the default “floor” rather than mere ground. Vines snake upslope, weaving in and out of sandstone ridges that still look half-formed, as if the earth hasn’t quite made up its mind about becoming stone. Where the stone does resolve, its surfaces weep with tiny rivulets that catch in pockets of soft, loamy mud.

Everything feels as though it’s in the early stages of becoming: raw, embryonic geology is visible in the newly fractured rock edges, still unweathered, still crystalline in places, not yet worn smooth by time. Sprawling tangles of ferns, enormous succulent-like growths, and bizarre spherical blooms cluster around watery depressions. And between these tufts of vegetation is emptiness, open space, the sense that you can look for miles without finding interruption. The plateau extends in a wide sweep—valley-like in scale—though it sits atop the world.

When the humidity condenses into sheets of unbroken drizzle, the plateau resonates with a deafening vibration. Some of it is the thunder of falling water—temporary rain-fed cascades dropping into hidden subterranean hollows below—yet much of that roar is mechanical. Clusters of towering structures, scattered across the horizon, belch clouds of steam into the bright air. Rust-streaked scaffolds stand amid treelike columns of living bark, bridging between trunk and trunk. Constructed walkways, suspended by steel cables, glisten with condensation. Some of these installations seem abandoned or half-built: skeletal frames with loops of piping that fail to connect to anything. Others hum with life, lit by high-voltage arcs that flicker under the glaring sun. Hissing valves and clanking conveyer belts unify with the crashing water, creating a continuous wall of noise that swallows lesser sounds.

Between metal beams and tangled roots, a persistent hush of life stirs in faint moments when the machinery lulls. Small insects with translucent wings flit past glowing blossoms. You might see a darting shape of an animal vanish into thick undergrowth–a rare rodent or a glimmering avian creature outfitted by nature to dwell in these high, humid realms. Yet fauna is elusive, as though the plateau’s primal conditions and clamorous engines keep larger creatures at a distance.

Despite the overwhelming presence of plants, everything about this plateau feels balanced in its minimalism: pockets of dense growth alternate with wide stretches of nearly bare stone. The mechanical apparatuses stand at respectful distances from one another, as if uncertain how—if at all—to expand into these fertile pockets. Even the brightness brings an otherworldly clarity, making every stem of moss and every rivet on the scaffolding stand out in sharp detail. Over time, you realize how slowly this place changes, how patiently the rock, water, plant, and churning machinery negotiate their roles. The thick moss creeps over the machine parts by millimeters each season. The new rock layers push up almost imperceptibly every decade, forging expansions of the plateau. Broken valves get replaced only after they have thoroughly rusted away. Growth, decay, renewal—these cycles stretch across years.

In some areas, moss-encrusted turbines spin methodically, driving unknown processes with industrial calm. In others, pipelines trace ephemeral paths through vegetation clogged with wet vines. Long ranges of half-complete conduits run from one ridge to another, disappearing abruptly as if waiting for the next construction campaign that never quite begins. The deafening soundtrack—an ongoing symphony of water-driven thunder, mechanical rumble, and perhaps the faint calls of those rare creatures—envelops it all.

And so, this plateau stands high and wet, bright and cool, both teeming and sparse—the earth still forming at its edges, the green growth pressing in from every angle, the steady background churn of industry humming a slow, patient tune of creation amid the newly minted stone.
