A crackling ocean stretches across the sky. It is a region of water so broad and far-reaching that it seems to have come loose from the planet below, hovering in partial suspension high above any known terrain. The temperature is cool in this strange half-world—somewhere between the crisp bite of early dawn and an unearthly hush of fresh vapors. And it all feels new, raw, like its very matter is still arranging itself into reality. 

At times, the watery expanse appears as a vast dome overhead, a turbulent, roiling sea turned upside-down. Luminous gray-blue currents swirl inside it, forming pockets of emptiness between great arcs of liquid. If you try to look deeply into the water, you'd realize it’s not truly an ocean in the conventional sense: it is more like a fluid tapestry of swirling potential, coalescing in parts and drifting apart in others—an embryonic ocean of cosmic proportions, lit by an otherwise normal, clear daylight that filters through from somewhere behind. The light casts off the currents in silver streaks and cerulean flashes. 

Despite its aquatic nature, there is hardly any life here. No creatures—and indeed no sign that creatures ever lived in these waters. In the near-void spaces between the currents, ridges of new matter are forming—bare, embryonic rock rising into existence. These ridges skirt the edges of enormous watery passages. Sometimes bits of stone extend and immediately collapse, as if the geology snaps in and out of being before it can set into stable form. 

In the rarer spots where the water drips down toward the fledgling earth, one finds a sparse suggestion of green—tiny scrubby vines or slender blades that might be the earliest vegetation trying to get a foothold. But the overall sense is one of emptiness: there is not enough solid ground here, not enough nutrients or time for more robust growth. The vegetation remains minimal, almost timid, as though unsure whether this floating oceanic realm will remain. 

An almost deafening noise reverberates through and around you. It comes from the dynamic surges of liquid against newly forming stone. It is a thunderous roar that never settles, punctuated by crashes that shake the fluid sky and the half-formed ground. The sound grows in intensity, an ever-present wall of motion. It is as if the water wants to become something more—tremendous pressure condensed into these thunderous collisions. In many ways, the noise hints at the world’s next steps—like an ongoing creation cry that marks each new development in the under-construction landscape.

As the vast water mass stirs and shifts, swirling eddies of incomprehensible size form, pulling entire sections of proto-rock upward until they disintegrate back into the swirling water. Sometimes you catch glimpses of technology that defies current physics: an interplay of energies, faint silvery filaments that seem to guide or shape the watery expanses, but there are no machines or visible structures. It’s more like subtle catalysts weaving through the fluid, reminding you that this place is beyond simple oceanic logic. Large arcs of current fluctuate in ways that suggest advanced forces at work behind the scenes—gravity or magnetism twisted into new shapes. 

The emptiness is so large that even the swirling, chaotic ocean can appear sparse. You feel like you could drift for miles between clusters of water, passing through a near-vacuum of calm before hitting another raging torrent. The scale is mind-boggling; it feels as though you have stepped into a planet in its earliest upheaval, or perhaps some cosmic tide pool belonging to an alien dimension. 

The atmosphere—though saturated—still has pockets of breathable air, cooled by the swirling surges, creating brief corridors of chill, vapor-laden currents. At every turn, there’s a sense that you are inside a massive living storm, yet nothing about it seems fully alive in the biological sense. It is more like a dynamic, embryonic tapestry of water, potential rock, and strange energies. 

Despite how overwhelmingly fluid-filled, windy, and loud it is, the place also has an eeriness. That stillness within the turbulence is the emptiness’s signature: no fauna, no watchers, no structures, no landmarks you can truly orient yourself by. Just the swirling ocean overhead, the occasional flicker of advanced energies working outside normal physics, and the echo of the universal roar. 

And so you stand, or drift, in this new world still forging itself. You witness the raw geology shudder into being in ephemeral bursts, watch stony plates quiver as they rise, tremble, and sometimes vanish. It is an event in transit, an environment with no final shape, a realm dancing on the boundary of becoming. Whether it is a cradle of life that might come to exist in some distant age, or a plane of endless turbulence that is its own final state—nobody knows. All you can do is listen to the crashing water, the loud roar of cosmic tide and wind, and marvel at the strangeness of this embryonic ocean-sky.
