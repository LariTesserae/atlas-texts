They call this region the Eave of Vertices, a place where gravity itself seems uncertain and the air is perpetually cool with half-tangible mists drifting in and out of stone spires. At first glance, it appears as a vast mountain range carved with near-vertical cliffs, plunging ravines, and narrow, serpentine ledges. Much of the exposed rock face is still in the process of forming—there are jagged edges that look like they’ve erupted just recently, raw veins of mineral shining wetly in the dim ambient light, as if the terrain is freshly baked out of the world’s crust.

Despite this rocky extremity, moisture clings in surprising corners. Small rivulets drip from unexpected cracks in the stone, forming shallow pools in the hollows. The air tastes faintly metallic, like condensation collecting on cold iron. Where water gathers, faint green mosses and larger vine-like plants take hold, bending across the craggy surfaces with an almost defiant vigor. Here and there, thicker clusters of vegetation flourish, especially around natural basins and along narrow gullies. Lush in patches, yet separated by stark expanses of raw rock, the greenery forms an uneven festival of color—leading you from one pocket of life to the next.

Your eyes are pulled to the city that inhabits these precarious slopes. Its towers and walkways grip the rock in a deeply industrial style—massive vertical factories that belch sporadic bursts of steam into the air, their structures lit only by feeble bulbs and the occasional scatter of neon piping. Clusters of metal scaffoldings crisscross over near-bottomless chasms. Some of these walkways look newly erected, rivets still shining. Others are older, half-rusted, their purpose unclear. All of it hints at ongoing expansion, as if the city is an organism gradually pushing deeper into the mountainous folds.

Puzzlingly, the place doesn’t seem to obey conventional geometry. The lines of the architecture bend at strange angles. Rampways that should intersect simply pass through each other like optical illusions in slow motion. High above, silhouettes of enormous machines grind away nearly soundlessly, though every so often, a deep throbbing boom echoes off the canyon walls—an industrial heartbeat that resonates in the chest. Steam pipes and large ducts curl against the cliffs, chewing into the stone and exhaling thick plumes of heated mist, which then condenses on every available surface in a perpetual damp sheen.

Even with the minimal light, you see an abundance of creatures scuttling, crawling, swooping. They occupy improbable niches: some hitch rides on the shifting scaffolds, others nest in hidden terraces behind waterfalls of thick, almost viscous water. You sense that not all of them are entirely natural—some move with smooth, mechanical joints, yet are covered in living fur or leaves. Others flicker at the edges of vision, as though drifting in and out of this reality. Birds with fractal-patterned plumage nest on ledges illuminated only by the faint glow from the factories. You hear them calling out, shrill notes mingling with the hiss of steam and the clang of metal.

The city has many levels, galleries of half-finished chambers carved or blasted out of the stone. You notice frequent expansions: new corridors being cut into the rock at a pace so glacial it’s barely perceptible. In the hush between mechanical pulses, you might hear water droplets hitting hollow steel or the heavy shuffle of some large beast crossing a catwalk in the distance. But mostly there is a steady hum, an undercurrent of loud, throbbing energy that suffuses every alley and arch.

Around the corner of one terrace, there’s the faint outline of a bizarre phenomenon—like ripples of impossible geometry hanging in midair. Walls open onto corridors that loop back into themselves. You catch glimpses of motion through those thresholds: silhouettes of a different place or time, drifting through. Yet though this city is layered with weird dimensional pockets, you sense its changes occur with a slow, patient inevitability—creeping almost like a glacier, forging new passages and rearranging its mechanical guts over centuries.

Lights remain dim; the entire region is cast in a subdued twilight, as if noon never quite arrives. Metal surfaces glisten with condensation, and the huge vertical planes of naked rock exhale a cold that seeps through your clothes. Still, the communal areas where people or creatures gather glow with a low industrial haze—and it’s there the sound of life intensifies. Clanking, rustling, laughter, the drone of machinery spinning up. When the factories work at full tilt, a thunderous roar rolls through the canyons, swelling for an hour, then receding into a quiet hiss of steam.

Yet there remains a greed of space: so many yawning chasms, plateaus only half-occupied by the city’s metal outgrowth, and cliff faces still untouched. The city is large enough to wander between boroughs without meeting another soul, if you choose carefully. And beyond its outskirts, virgin rock surfaces loom, barely scratched, as though waiting for the city’s next expansion or perhaps some new, stranger happening.

Uncertainty clings here, yet it’s a cradle of strange life, a place with the faint odor of new-formed stone and the hush of endless time. You can feel how the city and the mountains are fused into a single unfinished tapestry—wet and cool to the touch, humming with metal and the echoes of vast, slow processes that pay no heed to human timescales. And underlying it all is the subtle crackle of reality’s edges fraying, as if the city has pushed too far and begun to unravel the world’s foundations with each mechanized breath.
