By the time you set foot here, you’ve already climbed higher than you realized. The air is thin and shocking cold, but alive with the smell of damp loam and the faint electricity of tumbling clouds. Whole continents of living greenery expand in every direction, an ocean of foliage. At first, it seems too dark for anything to grow—mists drift in thick curtains, and the pale, watery sun rarely cuts through—but that’s the paradox of this place: the plant life isn’t just clinging to survival, it’s everywhere, forming knots of trunks and vines and high canopy arches that disappear into blackness above.

These mountains are young—rising in jagged spires that haven’t yet had time to wear smooth or gather much soil. The stone is exposed in knife-sharp cliffs, with cracks and fissures that vent sudden gusts of wind or plumes of water vapor. At certain cliff faces, half-frozen waterfalls hang like tongues of ice, dripping cold rivulets that feed the labyrinth of roots and creepers below. The entire terrain feels new, unsettled, as if it was thrust into being only a few centuries ago. You can see long fissures in the bedrock—fault lines, raw and pale—threading through green tangles of vine and moss.

Above these ridges and cliffs, the vegetation forms its own architecture. Not trees in the usual sense: they’ve developed towering columns with filament-bridges swinging between them, leaf-lattices that have grown thicker and stronger than any mundane building. Some leaves are massive, broader than a person, and funnel condensation down into small pools and trickling channels. Other plants glow with faint phosphorescence, lighting pockets in the gloom in ghostly greens and blues. Where they press close together, it’s eerily silent—until the winds surge and the vast canopies groan and rub their limbs together, creating a roar that rolls down the slopes.

Yet despite the density of foliage, the space most travelers move through is strangely empty. Between the trunks and creepers lies an immense airy gulf—there is so much biomass forming canopy and undergrowth that there’s little in between. It can feel like being inside the hollow skeleton of some colossal creature: a near-void beneath the living roof, except for drifting leaf litter and dangling roots overhead. The contours of the land vanish quickly in the gloom, so you navigate by sound, scent, and the faint luminescence of the plants themselves.

The wildlife abounds, filling the darkness with shrieks, clicks, and howls. Heavy wings beat against the thin air as large flighted creatures crisscross the canopy. Below, you hear scurrying in the thick mats of moss, unseen teeth gnawing on fibrous stems. Every once in a while, a deeper roar cascades off distant rock walls, something large chasing prey along the ridgelines. And then the plants themselves: they rustle with restless motion—growing, curling, releasing spores that hang like dust motes lit by the glow-plants.

Intriguing remnants of a primitive culture dot the steeper terraces: small huts built of woven vines, partial walls of stones fitted together by hand. Sometimes you find broken tools—simple chisels of stone, thick-bladed knives hammered from a soft metal, wooden clubs bound with leather. Their makers must have moved on or died out, leaving behind these faint footprints. The huts are slowly being overtaken by the unstoppable vegetation, walls dissolving into living frameworks of creepers.

Cloudbanks roll continuously across the sharp peaks, sometimes roaring in storms that flush the ravines with sudden rains and hail. The lightning up here forks and branches in the near-perpetual darkness, briefly illuminating the scale of it all: canopies spreading into black distance, unseen valleys far below. Thunder reverberates, magnified among the towering stone needles until you feel it in your ribcage. When these tempests pass, the forest resonates with drips, splashes, and trickling streams, and the creatures that had hidden burst into urgent activity—feeding, mating, hunting in the glistening gloom.

It’s a land of paradoxes—heavy with growth yet open and hollow in its middle regions, cold enough to freeze but so biologically exuberant it never feels truly barren. The high, steep spires are newly ripped from the planet’s crust but so covered in vines and creepers you forget there’s rock underneath. In the gloom, the world is lit by the sporadic flicker of bioluminescence, by flickers of lightning in muffling clouds, and by the echoing roars and howls of beasts you might never see outside of lightning’s brief glare. Everything is in constant motion—whether it’s the swirl of new weather systems forming above these heights or the surge of roots, leaves, and creatures battling for space and survival. Even your own footsteps echo strangely, swallowed by that vast, living emptiness, reminding you that this entire towering, cold wilderness is still evolving—and you’re just passing through its emerging heart.
